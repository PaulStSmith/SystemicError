Key Trump advisor denied position over alleged affair with Kristi Noem: report

U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security (DHS) Kristi Noem testifies before the House Appropriations Homeland Security Subcommittee hearing on oversight of the Department of Homeland Security, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 6, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

A top advisor to President Donald Trump was denied a role as Homeland Security chief of staff out of “concern” of his relationship with DHS Secretary Kristi Noem amid longstanding rumors of the two having an affair, Axios reported Thursday.

That top advisor is Corey Lewandowski, a special government employee in the Trump administration currently under investigation by the White House for undercounting work hours and skirting federal law.

“Concerns about their relationship led President Trump to nix the idea of allowing Noem to make Lewandowski her chief of staff and instead have him become an (special government employee),” Axios’ Brittany Gibson and Marc Caputo wrote Thursday, citing reports from The Atlantic and the Wall Street Journal verified by the publication.

By all accounts, however, Lewandowski has acted much like Noem’s chief of staff, even introducing himself as such in April before DHS issued a correcting statement, saying that he was merely an advisor to DHS.

Lewandowski is frequently seen accompanying Noem, and has been alleged to have gotten “handsy” with Noem in public, and on multiple occasions. This follows another allegation of the Trump advisor having gotten “handsy” with someone in public, though a previous instance landed him a misdemeanor battery charge for allegedly groping a GOP donor’s wife during a charity dinner in 2021.

Both Lewandowski and Noem have denied the allegations of having an affair.

Like many Trump appointees, Lewandowski has proved controversial, but perhaps uniquely so,even with Trump himself. Lewandowski had initially sought a senior position in Trump’s cabinet, but was denied that by Trump, though the two still remain close, according toreporting from The Atlantic, with Lewandowski still maintaining an outsized influence at the White House.

Our Analysis:

The Lewandowski-Noem Saga: A Symptom of the Trump Administration's Chronic Ills

The recent revelations surrounding Corey Lewandowski, a top advisor to President Donald Trump, and his supposed affair with DHS Secretary Kristi Noem serve as yet another glaring symptom of the chronic dysfunction and scandal that seem to perpetually orbit the Trump administration. According to a report by Axios, concerns over Lewandowski's relationship with Noem led to Trump nixing the idea of Lewandowski assuming the role of Homeland Security chief of staff, relegating him instead to the nebulous position of a special government employee (SGE).

The Underlying Issues

Firstly, the very fact that Lewandowski's potential appointment was derailed by "concerns" over personal relationships rather than professional qualifications or ethical considerations speaks volumes. It highlights a troubling pattern within the Trump administration of personal loyalties and entanglements often trumping (pun intended) meritocracy and ethical governance. Lewandowski's track record is marred by allegations of misconduct, including a misdemeanor battery charge for allegedly groping a GOP donor’s wife in 2021. Yet, these serious allegations seem to have been sidelined in the decision-making process, overshadowed by the sensationalism of rumored affairs.

The Role of a Special Government Employee

Lewandowski's designation as a special government employee is a curious workaround. Ostensibly, it allows him to skirt the full measure of accountability and transparency expected of more permanent officials. This status, coupled with allegations that he has been undercounting work hours and skirting federal law, paints a disconcerting picture of how the Trump administration manages its staff and adheres to federal guidelines. It's worth noting that despite the supposed downgrade in his official capacity, Lewandowski has been acting in capacities that far exceed his title, even going so far as to introduce himself as the chief of staff before being corrected by DHS. This blurring of lines between official titles and actual roles is symptomatic of a larger issue of accountability and transparency within the administration.

The Echoes of Past Controversies

Both Lewandowski and Noem have denied the allegations of an affair, yet this scenario feels eerily reminiscent of past controversies that have plagued Trump's tenure. From allegations of sexual misconduct to questions about ethical governance, the administration seems to be in a constant state of damage control. Lewandowski's continued influence in the White House, despite being denied a senior cabinet position and facing serious allegations, underscores a persistent theme in Trump's governance: loyalty over integrity.

Conclusion

The Lewandowski-Noem debacle is not just a tabloid scandal; it's a glaring indicator of the systemic issues within the Trump administration. It underscores a governance style that prioritizes personal relationships and loyalty over professionalism, ethics, and the law. As the administration continues to navigate these choppy waters, one can't help but wonder: how many more Lewandowskis are lurking in the shadows, and what does this mean for the future of American governance?

In essence, the saga is less about the salacious details of rumored affairs and more about the profound implications of such governance on the fabric of American democracy. It's a stark reminder that in the corridors of power, ethical governance and accountability should never be sacrificed on the altar of personal loyalty and political expediency.
