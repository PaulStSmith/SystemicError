Body language expert exposes signs Trump is 'breaking down'

The Diaper Clip Is Not the Story

The Source Material

A viral clip shared by White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt has fueled speculation about a bulge in Donald Trump’s pants, while clinical psychologist John Paul Garrison used the moment to argue that Trump may be showing signs of medical and neurological decline. He also pointed to Trump’s repeated use of “young, beautiful girls” as possible perseveration and lowered inhibition.

Power, Not Pantomime

The political fact here is not whether a body-language analyst can read Trump’s gait, phrasing, or wardrobe. The fact is that Trump holds the presidency, his press shop is actively circulating the footage, and the entire apparatus around him is normalizing a leader who is increasingly discussed as a medical curiosity instead of an accountable decision-maker.

That is the central failure of the coverage. It turns public power into a guessing game about symptoms. It invites the audience to stare at the man’s body instead of the machinery he controls.

Who Enables the Drift

If Trump is declining, that is not a private matter floating outside politics. It is a governance problem. The people closest to him, including the White House staff packaging and amplifying the clip, are not accidentally revealing chaos. They are managing it, exploiting it, and betting that spectacle is enough to drown out scrutiny.

Leavitt’s role matters because it shows how this White House works: provoke the internet, feed the feed, and let distraction do the rest. The point is not clarity. The point is to keep everyone chasing the wrong object.

The Article’s Real Weakness

The framing leans hard on medical speculation, which is inherently limited and easy to overread. A psychologist can describe possible signs of decline. That does not make any diagnosis established fact. But the larger problem is that this kind of commentary can become a substitute for political analysis.

Trump’s possible incontinence, repetition, or lowered inhibition are not the issue by themselves. The issue is that a serious political order has been reduced to a system where the public is pushed to infer the state of the presidency from body language, viral clips, and rumor instead of from coherent governance, transparent accountability, or institutional restraint.

Decay as Administration

This is what authoritarian politics looks like when it gets old and sloppy: the leader’s fragility becomes part of the branding, and the regime treats confusion as insulation. The public is handed a circus about diapers and phrases while the real question is left untouched: who is exercising power, with what competence, and on behalf of whom?

The answer in this story is ugly and plain. Trump remains the focal point of power. His aides help stage the optics. Commentators are pushed toward trivia. And the country is supposed to pretend that the degradation of presidential function is just another internet moment.

The Larger Pattern

The pattern is not merely one man aging in public. It is a political culture that keeps substituting spectacle for accountability and personality for institutional judgment. When the center of power is allowed to deteriorate in plain sight, the cover-up is rarely silence. More often, it is noise.

This is not governance. It is managed decline wrapped in content.
