'Wow': Trump DOJ shocks with use of 'nuclear option' as FBI investigates admin officials

Expanding on his own report for MS NOW, reporter Ken Dilanian admitted to host Chris Jansing on Thursday that he was legitimately surprised that the FBI is taking part in a grand jury investigation of two high-profile Donald Trump administration officials.

Moments after MS NOW posted the report from Dilanian and Carol Leonnig that explained that a federal grand jury in Maryland is looking into the actions of FHA Director Bill Pulte and DOJ official Ed Martin for improperly putting in place unauthorized people to assist mortgage fraud investigations of President Donald Trump’s critics, he went on air to fill in more details and express his surprise.

Calling Martin one of Donald Trump’s “darlings,” he added, “We’re hearing from some people [Attorney General] Pam Bondi and [Deputy Attorney General] Todd Blanche were never thrilled with these marginal mortgage fraud cases, and they know that career prosecutors said over and over again that they weren’t worth bringing.”

“And look, Pam Bondi was the attorney general of Florida. Todd Blanche is a former Justice Department prosecutor,” he pointed out. “Whatever else you think of them, they’re real lawyers and they’re running the Justice Department, and they know that things will happen in courtrooms that will blow up in their face, as we saw yesterday and they don’t want that to happen. So this could be an attempt for them to sort of get their house in order.”

“But wow, if it is, it’s sort of the nuclear option here,” he exclaimed. “A criminal investigation of some of Donald Trump’s most favored allies who are doing his bidding. They’re playing with fire. It will be really interesting to see how this plays out.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of MS NOW Report on FBI Investigation into Trump Administration Officials

In an era where sensationalism often trumps substance, it’s refreshing to see a report that not only dives deep into the intricate web of political maneuverings but also manages to express genuine surprise at its findings. Ken Dilanian’s report for MS NOW, followed by his on-air discussion with Chris Jansing, sheds light on a grand jury investigation in Maryland probing the actions of two high-profile officials from the Donald Trump administration: FHA Director Bill Pulte and DOJ official Ed Martin. Their alleged crime? The unauthorized placement of individuals to assist in mortgage fraud investigations targeting critics of President Donald Trump.

The Alleged Scheme

Dilanian’s reporting, co-authored with Carol Leonnig, uncovers what appears to be a blatant misuse of power designed to target political adversaries under the guise of rooting out mortgage fraud. The involvement of Pulte and Martin, described by Dilanian as one of Trump’s “darlings,” raises significant concerns about the politicization of federal investigative powers. The fact that this scheme is now under the microscope of a federal grand jury signals a serious escalation in the scrutiny of practices that, if proven true, could border on authoritarian misuse of government resources for political vendettas.

The Reaction from Within

What stands out in Dilanian’s revelation is not just the audacity of the alleged actions but also the internal disapproval it garnered. The mention of Attorney General Pam Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s skepticism toward the merit of these mortgage fraud cases is particularly telling. Dilanian points out their legal acumen and positions of authority, hinting at a discord within the Trump administration itself regarding the prudence and legality of pursuing these cases. Their apprehension, as noted by Dilanian, stems from a recognition of the potential for these cases to “blow up in their face” in court, a scenario they were evidently keen to avoid.

The Bigger Picture

Dilanian’s commentary underscores a broader issue at play: the weaponization of federal agencies against political opponents. The involvement of a grand jury suggests that there is enough concern about the legality of these actions to warrant a serious investigation, a move Dilanian describes as the “nuclear option.” This phrase aptly captures the gravity of the situation—a criminal investigation into the conduct of close allies of a sitting president, for actions taken at his behest, is no small matter. It speaks volumes about the lengths to which political battles are being fought and the potential legal and ethical lines being crossed.

Conclusion

The MS NOW report, and Dilanian’s subsequent on-air discussion, peel back another layer of the complex and often murky world of political machinations within the Trump administration. The investigation into Pulte and Martin is not just a story about potential legal missteps; it’s a window into the systemic challenges facing the United States’ democratic institutions and the ongoing battle to maintain integrity within the halls of power. Dilanian’s surprise at the FBI’s involvement is a sentiment likely shared by many observers—a mix of shock at the audacity of such actions and a sobering reminder of the fragility of the rule of law in the face of political pressures. This report serves as a critical piece of journalism, highlighting the need for vigilance, transparency, and accountability in government actions.
