'Something I've never forgotten': Scaramucci reveals what Trump told him about MAGA voters

Based on an article by David McAfee

Trump’s Instinct Is Not a Mystery. It Is the Method.

The Real Power

Anthony Scaramucci’s post is trying to do two things at once: warn critics not to underestimate Donald Trump, and explain why Trump keeps winning fights over culture and class. The useful part is not the Napoleon metaphor. It is the admission that the president knows exactly which voters matter to him, and which issues he can exploit without paying a political price.

What the Source Actually Shows

The reporting points to a simple power structure: Trump sits at the center of the decision-making, and his coalition is built around working-class voters who want government benefits protected while they are fed a steady diet of culture-war conflict. Scaramucci says Trump once described his base as “socially conservative and fiscally liberal,” and that he leaves Social Security and Medicare alone because he knows who put him in office. That is the whole machine in one sentence: protect the entitlements, weaponize the resentments.

The Safety-Net Red Line

This is not compassion. It is transaction. If Trump avoids touching Social Security and Medicare, the source suggests that is a calculated refusal to alienate the people who sustain him. The real decision is not whether he believes in the social safety net. It is that he understands it as political infrastructure. That is colder, and more revealing, than any false story about his generosity.

The Puppet Story Is a Comforting Lie

One reply in the piece says Trump is merely being “fed” information and acting like a puppet. That instinct is understandable, but it also serves a purpose: it lets opponents avoid the harder truth that deliberate political skill can coexist with chaos, vulgarity, and obvious corruption. If critics convince themselves he is only a vessel, they miss the more durable fact that the vessel keeps working.

Culture War as Coalition Management

Scaramucci’s real warning is not that Trump is brilliant in some grand historical sense. It is that he understands how to turn cultural panic into political loyalty. Transgender athletes, anti-woke backlash, Bible Belt sensibilities: these are not random obsessions. They are tools for binding a coalition together while keeping material harm away from the voters Trump cannot afford to lose.

The Pattern

The larger pattern here is not misunderstanding. It is misdirection. Trump and his orbit can be crude, erratic, and opportunistic while still making disciplined choices about who gets protected, who gets blamed, and which fights are useful distractions. That is how contemporary power often works: not through coherent ideology, but through selective cruelty, benefit protection for the loyal, and a permanent culture war that keeps attention away from the class bargain underneath.
