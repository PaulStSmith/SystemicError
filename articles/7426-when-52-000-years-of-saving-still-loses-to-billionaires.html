When 52,000 Years of Saving Still Loses to Billionaires

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 20, 20251 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

I just saw this meme:

The math is brutally unforgiving.

Let’s do it cleanly, no theatrics:

  • $10,000 per day

  • 52,024 years

  • ≈ 365.2422 days/year

That’s roughly 19 million days.

$10,000 × 19,001,360 ≈ $190 billion.

That’s it. No compounding. No risk. No “bootstraps.” Just immortal discipline across an ice age, the rise and fall of empires, the Black Death, colonialism, industrial capitalism, two world wars, and the internet.

And you still barely graze the lower edge of Elon Musk’s net worth on a good year.

Here’s the punchline people try very hard to dodge:
This is not about effort, thrift, intelligence, or time.
It’s about ownership structures that allow wealth to extract value faster than labor can accumulate it.

Billionaire wealth is not “a lot of money.”
It’s a different category of economic power, one that breaks intuition, arithmetic, and democracy alike.

When defenders say “don’t punish success,” they’re lying to themselves. No one is talking about confiscation. We’re talking about acknowledging that:

  • No human produces hundreds of billions in value alone

  • Markets don’t magically justify outcomes

  • Extreme wealth concentration is a policy choice, not a law of nature

If an immortal saver can’t catch up, the system isn’t rewarding productivity—it’s compounding privilege.

So yes.
Tax. The. Fucking. Billionaires.
