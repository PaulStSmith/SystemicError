That squirrel on Trump's head

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

Trump’s Machine Runs on Grift, Secrecy, and Disposable People

The source article jumps across several scandals, but the structure is consistent: Trump and the Republican apparatus use state power to move money upward, hide evidence, and blame weaker targets for the damage. A $150 million sole-source contract for immigrant children’s defense, ICE’s refusal to release bodycam footage except when convenient, manipulated jobs numbers, public-lands lies, oil-reserve profiteering, and donor-driven election games all point to the same system. This is not drift. It is governance as extraction.

The Real Power Is Administrative

The contract story tells you who holds the levers. A Texas law firm with little immigration experience and Trump-adjacent ties is not being rewarded for competence. It is being handed a lucrative channel into the machinery that cages immigrant children. That is what corruption looks like when it learns to wear procurement language.

ICE fits the same pattern. Body cameras mean nothing if the agency gets to decide when footage is released and when it is buried. “Transparency” controlled by the police force itself is not transparency. It is evidence management.

Secrecy Is the Product

The article’s language is blunt because the underlying arrangement is blunt: the state arrests, confines, intimidates, and then curates the record. When armed federal agents can act behind masks and then decide whether the public gets to see the footage, accountability has already been defeated.

That is why ICE cannot be treated as a reform project. Its role is not some accidental deviation from humane enforcement. Its role is coercion. Abolish ICE. Anything less preserves the same machinery with fresh branding.

The Economy Is Being Rewritten Upward

The jobs-number episode shows how propaganda works when it inherits a bureaucracy. Fire the statistics official, install a loyalist, revise the numbers after the headlines have already done their work, and then insist the public is confused. The confusion is the point.

Meanwhile, the economic reality is plain enough: higher costs, lower wages, and a political class that blocks a minimum-wage increase and union rights while billionaires and the Trump family get richer. The regime does not need the public to believe the numbers forever. It only needs enough fog to keep power in place while the wealth moves.

Public Goods Are the Target

The public-lands section is especially revealing because it shows how Republican politics works when it is not performing cultural warfare. It lies about comment periods, invents fake scarcity, and sells off shared assets under the banner of “energy independence.” That phrase is propaganda for resource capture. The same is true of the National Oil Reserve scheme described in the source: socialize the risk, privatize the spread, hand the profit to donor industries.

This is the governing philosophy underneath the noise. Public institutions are treated as ATMs for the well-connected. The rest is theater.

Auctions, Threats, and Discipline

The article also notes how money and fear discipline the political class. Former senators are valuable because access can be monetized; a Trump-aligned party keeps members in line by making defection expensive. Even when an industry wants a different outcome, it does not appeal to democratic principle. It buys a PAC, flips a primary, and replaces one Republican with another more useful to its interests.

That is the oligarchic bargain in plain view: elections remain, but they are increasingly priced, managed, and threatened into submission.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is not misunderstanding, incompetence, or excess. It is the deliberate conversion of government into a shield for theft and brutality. Immigrant children, workers, public lands, tax revenues, federal statistics, and even the record of state violence all become raw material for a political class that treats accountability as an inconvenience.

The democratic answer is not to pretend this is normal governance. It is to name the institutions doing the damage, strip away their cover stories, and rebuild power around public accountability instead of private extraction.
