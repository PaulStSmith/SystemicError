Karoline Leavitt hit with brutal X community note for 'misleading' claim on food prices

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt received a fact check in the form of a community note on the X social media platform after she made “misleading” claims about a DoorDash report on food prices.

“Prices are steadying and wages are climbing, new DoorDash report shows,” Leavitt wrote on Tuesday, echoing a Fox Business report.

Readers soon added context, calling Leavitt’s claim “misleading.”

“The report actually shows rising food prices. Avg price of a cheeseburger, a soda & fries increased 3.8% YoY in Sep25 while typical restaurant meals rose 3.2%,” the community note below Leavitt’s post said. “This echoes latest (Aug) USDA food cost data: 2.9% more overall, 2.7% (at home) & 3.9% (in restaurants).”

Earlier this month, President Donald Trump insisted that “groceries are down except for the beef.”

“The president’s claims about grocery prices don’t align with the government’s own data,” Michigan State University professor David Ortega told CNN. “The reality is that prices for many everyday grocery items have increased in recent months, and tariff-related cost increases are now feeding through to consumers.”

Our Analysis:

Fact vs. Fiction: Unpacking the White House’s Misleading Claims on Food Prices

In a recent display of selective information dissemination, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt took to the X social media platform to tout what appeared to be a silver lining in the economy. Her post, claiming that “Prices are steadying and wages are climbing, new DoorDash report shows,” was quickly met with skepticism and fact-checking by the platform’s users. This incident shines a spotlight on the administration’s ongoing struggle with factual accuracy and its implications for public trust.

The Misleading Optimism

Leavitt’s optimistic interpretation of the DoorDash report was not just a stretch; it was a leap. Echoing a Fox Business report, she attempted to paint a rosy picture of the economic situation. However, the reality, as noted by the community on X, paints a starkly different picture. According to the community note added to Leavitt’s post:

“The report actually shows rising food prices. Avg price of a cheeseburger, a soda & fries increased 3.8% YoY in Sep25 while typical restaurant meals rose 3.2%,”

Furthermore, this data is in line with the latest USDA food cost data, which indicates a general rise in food prices – 2.9% more overall, with a 2.7% increase for at-home consumption and a 3.9% increase in restaurants.

The Dissonance of Official Statements

This incident is not isolated. Earlier in the month, President Donald Trump made claims that “groceries are down except for the beef,” a statement that directly contradicts government data. David Ortega, a professor at Michigan State University, pointed out to CNN that:

“The president’s claims about grocery prices don’t align with the government’s own data,”

Ortega emphasizes that not only have prices for many everyday grocery items increased but tariff-related cost increases are also beginning to impact consumers directly.

The Bigger Picture

The discrepancy between the administration’s narrative and the empirical data available to the public is troubling. It’s a reflection of a broader strategy of misinformation that aims to paint a rosier picture of the economy than the facts warrant. This tactic, while potentially beneficial in the short term for managing public perception, erodes trust in the long term.

Furthermore, this situation underscores the importance of fact-checking and critical analysis. The quick response from the X community in adding context to Leavitt’s claim demonstrates a public that is increasingly vigilant and unwilling to accept official statements at face value.

Conclusion

The White House’s attempts to manipulate economic narratives through selective data sharing and outright misleading statements do a disservice to the American public. As the gap between official narratives and lived realities continues to widen, the need for critical journalism and public skepticism has never been more apparent. It’s essential that these discrepancies are not just noted but challenged with the tenacity that the truth deserves.

In the end, the integrity of public discourse hinges on our collective ability to discern fact from fiction. As we navigate these complex economic times, let’s commit to holding our leaders accountable for the accuracy of their statements. The health of our democracy quite literally depends on it.
