'Sweep up everyone': Lawmakers say DOJ spying part of much larger — and darker — scheme

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 13, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi speaks with an aide as she attends a House Judiciary Committee hearing on oversight of the Justice Department to testify, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 11, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

WASHINGTON — More lawmakers are coming out against Attorney General Pam Bondi’s secret surveillance of members of Congress to counter their investigation into how the Justice Department is handling the release of the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files.

In an exclusive conversation with Raw Story, several expressed discomfort with having their work spied on — and warned it’s part of something much larger and darker.

“It’s clear they’re doing it,” said Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee. “You can tell when you go over there, I mean you’re on a floor with 15 empty offices, and then you go in and you’re surrounded by people ... it’s so obvious.”

Rep. Zoe Lofgren (D-CA) concurred and said it was a failed attempt to scare lawmakers into backing off oversight.

“I think it’s weird,” she said. “Their M.O. is intimidation, and when you’re not intimidated, they’ve got nothing else.”

Rep. Mary Gay Scanlon (D-PA), however, told Raw Story she thinks this goes deeper.

“It’s not the only place” such surveillance is happening, said Scanlon, referencing a recent DOJ memo that orders anti-Trump protesters to be investigated as domestic terrorists. They are trying to create a “list of entities” to accuse of “left-wing terrorism,” Scanlon said, including for such innocuous reasons as opposing “traditional family values.”

The goal here is clear, she said: “Sweep up everyone who’s the president’s enemy.”

“We’re seeing this whole-of-government approach to attacking people this White House views as enemies, whether it’s arresting members of Congress, trying to have them indicted, going after attorneys general, going after FTC chairs,” said Scanlon. “Anything they can do to turn the government on people that the White House wants to silence. It’s hugely dangerous.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Shadowy Surveillance of Congress

In a revelation that sounds like it’s ripped straight from the pages of a dystopian novel, reports have surfaced about Attorney General Pam Bondi’s clandestine surveillance of members of Congress. This isn’t just a minor blip in the radar of political scandals; it’s a full-blown storm signaling a terrifying abuse of power at the highest levels of government. Let’s dissect the implications of this egregious overreach and what it means for democracy as we know it.

The Unsettling Reality of Being Watched

Imagine walking into a building, surrounded by vacant offices, only to realize you’re being closely monitored. This isn’t the plot of a new spy movie; it’s the reality described by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD). The fact that lawmakers can physically feel the weight of surveillance upon them as they conduct their duties is not only disturbing but signals a profound breach of trust and protocol. Surveillance, in this context, isn’t just about gathering information; it’s about exerting power and control.

Intimidation as a Tactic of Governance

Rep. Zoe Lofgren (D-CA) hits the nail on the head: intimidation. When the tools of governance are wielded like weapons to scare lawmakers into submission, democracy is in dire straits. The goal here isn’t just information gathering; it’s about creating an atmosphere of fear and compliance. This tactic, however, seems to have backfired, according to Lofgren, revealing a desperation in the administration’s attempts to silence its critics.

The Bigger, Darker Picture

Rep. Mary Gay Scanlon (D-PA) provides a chilling perspective on the scope and scale of these operations. This isn’t an isolated incident but part of a broader, more sinister strategy to label dissent as “left-wing terrorism.” This approach isn’t just about surveilling lawmakers; it’s about fundamentally altering the landscape of lawful protest and opposition in the United States. By creating a so-called “list of entities” for opposing “traditional family values,” the administration is attempting to criminalize dissent and redefine patriotism as unwavering support for its agenda.

The Danger of a Whole-of-Government Approach to Silencing Opposition

Scanlon’s observations about the “whole-of-government” strategy to target the administration’s perceived enemies are perhaps the most harrowing. This isn’t about one agency overstepping its bounds; it’s a coordinated effort across government bodies to silence, intimidate, and criminalize opposition. The implications for civil liberties, freedom of speech, and the very fabric of democracy are profound and deeply unsettling.

Conclusion: A Call to Arms for Democracy

What we’re witnessing is not just an overreach of power; it’s an existential threat to democratic principles. When government agencies are used as tools of intimidation against elected officials, the very foundation of democracy is under attack. This situation demands more than passive concern; it requires an active defense of our democratic institutions and principles.

As citizens, we must remain vigilant, informed, and engaged. We must demand accountability from our leaders and ensure that abuses of power are met with the full force of the law. The surveillance of members of Congress in an attempt to stifle oversight and dissent is a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy—and the constant vigilance required to protect it.
