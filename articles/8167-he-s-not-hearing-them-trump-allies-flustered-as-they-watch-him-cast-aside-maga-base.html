'He's not hearing them!' Trump allies flustered as they watch him cast aside MAGA base

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 2, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Gilded RetreatThe Stark ContrastThe International DimensionThe Political Antenna Gone Askew

U.S. President Donald Trump waves as he walks after arriving at the White House following a weekend trip to Florida, in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 2, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump has all but abandoned the political rallies that defined his 2016 campaign and first presidency according to a new analysis in The Atlantic Monday, opting instead for his golf clubs, sporting events and international trips that have left some of his allies worried he’s lost touch with voters.

“People voted for him to lower prices, to bring manufacturing back, to stand up to those taking advantage of them,” said someone described as a “close Trump ally,” speaking with The Atlantic’s Jonathan Lemire on the condition of anonymity. “They didn’t vote for him to build a damn gilded ballroom. He’s not hearing them.”

Lemire compared Trump’s travel schedule this fall with his outings from the fall of his first year in office during his first term in 2017, and came away “surprised by the drop-off.”

In 2017, Trump traveled domestically more than a dozen times between September and November, The Atlantic’s analysis found — once to rally support for Senate candidate Roy Moore in Alabama, and again to speak with energy workers in North Dakota.

But in the same time period this year, Trump had left the Washington, D.C. metro area “only five times,” with destinations such as his Mar-a-Lago resort in Palm Beach, Florida, another to venture to his golf club in New Jersey, and three times to attend sporting events with wealthy friends.

Trump has also increased his international travel when compared with his previous first year of his term, trips that some of his most die-hard supporters had even urged him to tone down, including far-right influencer Laura Loomer, former White House deputy chief of staff Steve Bannon, and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who has since announced her resignation from Congress amid a public spat with the president.

“Trump’s lack of travel across the United States, some allies fear, has knocked his political antenna askew,” Lemire wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Disconnection: A Deep Dive into Political Apathy

In a recent piece by The Atlantic, Jonathan Lemire unveils an intriguing shift in Donald Trump’s behavior, marking a stark departure from the fervent rally-driven energy that catapulted him into the presidency in 2016. The analysis paints a picture of a former president retreating into the comforts of luxury, seemingly aloof from the grassroots fervor that once defined his political persona. This development isn’t just noteworthy—it’s a glaring spotlight on a broader issue of political disconnect.

The Gilded Retreat

Trump’s choice to eschew the political rallies that were the hallmark of his earlier political career in favor of golf clubs, sporting events, and international jaunts is more than a mere change of scenery. It’s symbolic of a deeper estrangement from the very base that propelled him to the White House. As the article points out through a “close Trump ally,” the electorate didn’t cast their votes for Trump with the expectation of him prioritizing the construction of “a damn gilded ballroom” over their material concerns like lowering prices, reviving manufacturing, and tackling exploitation.

The Stark Contrast

Lemire’s comparison of Trump’s travel schedules between the fall of 2017 and the current period is particularly telling. The dramatic drop-off in domestic travel from more than a dozen times to a mere five is not just a deviation; it’s a retreat. And not to places where he might reconnect with the working-class voters of his base, but to luxury resorts and elite gatherings, further entrenching the image of a president disconnected from the plight of his supporters.

The International Dimension

Even Trump’s increased international travel does little to assuage concerns, especially considering the criticism from figures like Laura Loomer, Steve Bannon, and Marjorie Taylor Greene—individuals who themselves are no strangers to controversy but have been instrumental in shaping the far-right’s narrative. Their advice to Trump to limit such trips underscores a growing unease within his own ranks about his wavering focus on domestic issues.

The Political Antenna Gone Askew

The analysis concludes with a poignant observation: Trump’s lack of travel across the United States could signify a misalignment with his political base. This disconnection might not just be a temporary lapse; it could signify a profound misunderstanding of what his voters expect of him, or worse, an indifference to those expectations.

In all, The Atlantic’s piece serves as a critical examination of a figure who, once seen as a populist hero, now risks becoming an emblem of political detachment. Trump’s apparent preference for opulence over the campaign trail is a stark reminder of how quickly the tides of political fortune can turn when a leader’s actions betray a fundamental disconnect from their base. As this narrative unfolds, it begs the question: Can Trump recalibrate his political compass, or has the gulf between him and his supporters grown too wide to bridge?

In the grand chessboard of political strategy, Trump’s current moves seem less like those of a savvy player and more akin to someone who has forgotten the game they’re playing. The ramifications of this shift could extend far beyond his own political future, potentially reshaping the landscape of American politics in ways yet to be fully understood.
