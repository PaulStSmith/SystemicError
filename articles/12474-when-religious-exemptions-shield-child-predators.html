When Religious Exemptions Shield Child Predators

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There Is No Divine Permission Slip for CrimeFaith Is Not FactReligious Institutions Have a Long, Filthy HistoryWhat About the Victims?No More Sacred SilencesFinal Thought

In Washington state, a legal and moral line was drawn in blood—the blood of children who suffer abuse, silenced behind the holy veil of confessional secrecy. In June 2025, the Trump administration, under the banner of "religious freedom," intervened to block a law requiring clergy to report child sexual abuse. Yes, you read that right: a federal government fighting tooth and nail to defend the right of priests to not report crimes against children, so long as the abuse is confessed inside a wooden booth and whispered through a screen to a man wearing a collar.

Let that sink in.

The law in question, Washington Senate Bill 5375, sought to include clergy as mandatory reporters of child sexual abuse—a category that already includes teachers, doctors, and social workers. But the real flashpoint was its refusal to carve out an exemption for religious confession. That sacred space, say religious apologists, is inviolable. Apparently more so than a child’s right to live free from abuse.

The Department of Justice, with Trump-appointed zealots at the helm, claimed this requirement violated the First Amendment’s guarantee of religious freedom. A judge, in predictable fashion, agreed—at least temporarily—and issued an injunction. The confessional seal, it seems, is more sacrosanct than a child’s safety.

This is where we call bullshit.

There Is No Divine Permission Slip for Crime

Religious freedom is not—should not be—a get-out-of-jail-free card. And it sure as hell should not be a shield behind which child rapists hide, protected by men who claim to speak for a god no one has ever seen. The United States does not recognize divine law. Our system is, at least on paper, secular. So why in the name of reason are we giving clergy a loophole no other profession gets?

Imagine a therapist who hears a confession of child rape. They’re required by law to report it. A teacher? Report it. A nurse? Report it. But a priest? Oh no, he gets to sit back, whisper an Our Father, and send the monster on their way with a clean conscience.

We cannot continue to accept this grotesque double standard. We cannot tolerate a system where the fantasy of divine authority trumps the very real suffering of children.

Faith Is Not Fact

Let’s confront the core lie head-on: there is no evidence for any god. None. Zero. Every claim of divine authority is backed by circular reasoning, personal feeling, or ancient hearsay. Courts that bend over backwards to accommodate these fantasies are not defending freedom—they are enshrining delusion.

Religious exemptions depend on a collective willingness to pretend that these beliefs deserve special treatment. But why? If someone claimed their belief in unicorns prevented them from reporting a rape, would we nod solemnly and say, "Ah yes, well, religious freedom"?

Of course not. Because that would be insane.

But swap out unicorns for Jesus, or Yahweh, or Allah, and suddenly we have to show deference. We have to compromise justice. We have to accommodate the sacred.

This is not just cowardice. It is complicity.

Religious Institutions Have a Long, Filthy History

This isn’t a one-off. The Catholic Church alone has paid out billions in settlements for decades of systematic sexual abuse across the globe. Entire dioceses have gone bankrupt trying to hush the screams of the molested. And still, the institution fights tooth and nail against reforms that might limit their secrecy or power.

These are not isolated monsters. They are symptoms of a structure that rewards silence and punishes transparency. And now, that very structure is being defended by the U.S. government.

That’s not religious freedom. That’s state-sanctioned protection of predators.

What About the Victims?

Where in this legal debate is the voice of the child who prays each night to be believed? Where is the freedom of the child whose trauma is buried under centuries of dogma?

This law was meant to give them a voice. To stop the cycle of abuse perpetuated by men in robes with pulpits and power. And it was derailed by men in suits with gavels and agendas.

The Trump administration’s intervention here is not about freedom. It’s about control. About appeasing a religious base that views child safety as a negotiable inconvenience next to the sanctity of ritual.

No More Sacred Silences

We demand a legal system rooted in reality, not ritual. We demand that all adults—regardless of title, collar, or imaginary hotline to the divine—be held equally accountable. If a priest hears a confession of child rape and says nothing, that priest is an accomplice. If the law allows him to remain silent, the law is broken.

Religious freedom ends where someone else’s body begins. Especially when that body belongs to a child.

There is no god in that confessional booth. Just a man, and a choice. And if the law lets him choose silence, then justice is deaf.

Final Thought

This isn’t complicated. If you claim your god requires you to protect a rapist, then your god is not worth protecting. And if your government agrees, then it’s not worth trusting.

No gods. No sacred silences. No more excuses.
