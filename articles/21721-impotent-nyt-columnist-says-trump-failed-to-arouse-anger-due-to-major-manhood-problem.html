'Impotent': NYT columnist says Trump failed to arouse anger due to major 'manhood' problem

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Trump’s Real Weakness Is Not Virility. It’s Dependence.

The Speech Was the Cover Story

Maureen Dowd’s column zeroes in on Trump’s Thursday-night address and the flatness around it: the Republican Party barely moved, the major networks were the target of his threats, and Fox News acted oddly cautious after paying $787.5 million in the Dominion settlement. That is the context. The real story is not whether Trump sounded “low-T.” It is that the machinery around him is so experienced at laundering his conduct that even his tantrums can be treated as another episode of personality theater.

Power Was Not Missing. It Was Withholding

Trump still holds the highest institutional office in the country. Republicans on Capitol Hill also hold political power, and they chose not to spend it. Dowd’s note that there was “barely a peep” is the point, not a side detail. The governing class that could restrain him declined to do so, because Trump’s value to them is not discipline or coherence. It is permission. He supplies the spectacle; they supply the silence that makes the spectacle usable.

The Media Problem Is Not Confusion

Trump threatened ABC and NBC for not airing his rant live, then complained into the void as if broadcast access were a birthright. That is not a misunderstanding of media power. It is a demand for submission. The article’s framing gets close to this, but the deeper issue is simpler: Trump’s strategy depends on institutions treating obvious coercion as just another weird outburst. That is how deliberate pressure gets recoded as instability instead of abuse.

Fox Learned the Cost of Lying

Fox News’ hesitation is not moral growth. It is risk management after the Dominion settlement, which came after the network echoed claims that voting machines switched votes from Trump to Biden. That history matters. Fox is not a passive observer discovering standards; it is a repeat offender adjusting to the price of open fraud support. Its silence on “Fox & Friends” was not courage. It was damage control.

Masculinity Talk Is a Decoy

Dowd leans on the spectacle of testosterone language, but that is mostly garnish. Trump’s problem is not that he lacks the right hormone. It is that his politics require constant humiliation, manufactured grievance, and a captive audience willing to mistake both for strength. Pete Hegseth’s macho nonsense only helps obscure the actual arrangement: performative virility at the top, institutional cowardice below, and a media ecosystem trained to convert both into brand maintenance.

The Pattern Is Impunity, Not Noise

This story is another reminder that authoritarian politics rarely looks like command at every moment. More often it looks like a degraded system where no one in power wants to be the one who says no. Trump rants, Republicans retreat, Fox calibrates, and the public is asked to focus on the decibel level instead of the structure that makes the damage possible. The issue is not whether his speech landed. The issue is how many institutions still exist mainly to absorb the blast and keep moving.
