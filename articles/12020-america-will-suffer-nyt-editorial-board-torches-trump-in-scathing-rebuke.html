'America will suffer': NYT editorial board torches Trump in scathing rebuke

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 6, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump's decision to fire the Bureau of Labor Statistics director over weak jobs report numbers was torn to shreds by The New York Times editorial board on Tuesday.

The decision, which Trump claims with no evidence is because the statistics were somehow rigged or falsified by Democrats to make him look bad, will only ensure that "America will suffer" from the collapse of trust among federal statistics gatherers and the people who use them, the board wrote.

"Mr. Trump’s allegations against Erika McEntarfer, the longtime public servant whom he summarily fired, have no foundation in reality," wrote the board. "The government’s monthly report on the labor market is produced by the bureau’s nonpartisan staff. Estimates for different parts of the economy are compiled separately and then pulled together into a national report, which is completed before it reaches the desk of whichever political appointee happens to be running the bureau. Experts, including past leaders of the agency nominated by presidents from both parties, said that it was effectively impossible for the bureau’s leader to manipulate those numbers."

The bigger problem, the board wrote, is that Trump sent a clear message to McEntarfer's successor, and other nonpartisan agencies, that they must please him or risk their jobs, even if the actual cold, hard numbers are bad news.

"Public servants must now do their work while fearing that they may be fired merely for producing information that displeases the president. Mr. Trump is also making it harder for the government to obtain information, as people and businesses asked to respond to questions now have reason to doubt whether the answers will be accurately reported."

Trump claims to be concerned about the integrity of federal data, the board wrote, but he's actually undermining it.

"Instead of improving the quality of information gathered and reported by the government, he is sowing doubts about the ability of federal agencies to produce reliable data," the board warned.

A number of countries have gone down this road, with devastating consequences, the editorial noted, with countries like Greece and Argentina spiraling into crisis because investors couldn't trust the accuracy of statistics fudged to please the government.

"The original meaning of the word 'statistics' is 'information about the state.' Without statistics, a state cannot operate effectively. It cannot establish good rules. It cannot make good decisions," wrote the board.

There are real challenges BLS faces in gathering accurate data — although ironically, noted a recent report from the conservative National Review, the Trump administration axed a volunteer commission of experts who were working on improving that accuracy.

"[Trump] is not interested in how many jobs the American economy will produce this month," the board concluded. "In firing Dr. McEntarfer, Mr. Trump made clear that he doesn’t want to know the answer."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Dismissal of BLS Director: A Dangerous Game with Data Integrity

In what can only be described as a brazen attack on the integrity of federal statistics—and, by extension, the very fabric of informed policymaking—President Donald Trump's decision to fire the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) director, Erika McEntarfer, over what he baselessly claims to be falsified job report numbers, is nothing short of catastrophic. This move, as dissected by The New York Times editorial board, not only undermines the credibility of one of the nation's most vital institutions but also sets a perilous precedent for the treatment of nonpartisan agencies under his administration.

Trump's Allegations: Baseless and Dangerous

Let's be crystal clear: Trump's allegations against McEntarfer are devoid of any factual basis. The process of compiling the government's monthly labor market report is rigorously nonpartisan, involving a comprehensive and compartmentalized approach to ensure its accuracy and integrity. Experts, including those who have led the BLS under administrations from both sides of the aisle, have confirmed the near impossibility of manipulating these statistics. Trump's claims are not only unfounded but a gross misrepresentation of the bureau's operations.

A Clear and Present Danger to Data Integrity

Trump's dismissal of McEntarfer sends a chilling message to all nonpartisan agencies: align with the president's views or face termination. This is not merely an attack on an individual but an assault on the principle of impartial public service. The implication that data must be pleasing to the president, irrespective of its veracity, is a direct threat to the objective reporting and analysis critical to informed governance.

Furthermore, Trump's actions have broader repercussions for the collection and perception of federal data. By casting doubt on the accuracy and integrity of government statistics, he undermines public and international trust in U.S. data. This is not a trivial matter. As the editorial rightly points out, countries that have manipulated statistics to curry favor with their governments have often spiraled into crisis. The reliability of state information is not just an academic concern but a cornerstone of economic and political stability.

Irony and Hypocrisy

In a twist of irony, the Trump administration's purported concern for the accuracy of federal data comes after it axed a volunteer commission of experts focused on improving the very same. This act of hypocrisy underscores a disinterest in the factual accuracy of job reports and a preference for data that serves political ends over the public good.

Statistics: The Lifeline of Effective Governance

The editorial board's reminder of the original meaning of "statistics" is poignant. Without reliable information about the state, effective governance is impossible. Decisions must be based on solid data, not the whims of political leaders. Trump's dismissal of McEntarfer, therefore, is not just an attack on a public servant but an attack on the very foundation of informed decision-making in government.

Conclusion: A Call to Defend Data Integrity

Trump's decision to fire the BLS director over unfounded claims of data manipulation is a stark reminder of the fragility of institutional integrity under political pressure. It is imperative that we recognize this act for what it is: a dangerous precedent that jeopardizes the objectivity and reliability of federal statistics. As citizens, we must demand accountability and safeguard the integrity of our public institutions against such wanton attacks. The health of our democracy and the effectiveness of our governance depend on it.
