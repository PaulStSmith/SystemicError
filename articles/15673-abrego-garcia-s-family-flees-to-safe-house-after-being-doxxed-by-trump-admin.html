Abrego Garcia's family flees to safe house after being doxxed by Trump admin

By Paulo Santos

The Trump administration has not only sent Kilmar Abrego Garcia to a Salvadoran megaprison due to an "administrative error" and so far refused to comply with a U.S. Supreme Court order to facilitate his return to the United States, but also shared on social media the home address of his family in Maryland, forcing them to relocate.

The news that Abrego Garcia's wife, Jennifer Vasquez Sura, and her children were "moved to a safe house by supporters" after the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt posted to X a 2021 order of protection petition that Vasquez Sura filed but soon abandoned was reported early Tuesday by The Washington Post.

"I don't feel safe when the government posts my address, the house where my family lives, for everyone to see, especially when this case has gone viral and people have all sorts of opinions," said Vasquez Sura. "So, this is definitely a bit terrifying. I'm scared for my kids."

A DHS spokesperson did not respond Monday to a request for a comment about not redacting the family's address, according to the newspaper's lengthy story about Vasquez Sura—who shares a 5-year-old nonverbal, autistic son with Abrego Garcia and has a 9-year-old son and 10-year-old daughter from a previous relationship that was abusive.

On Wednesday, The New Republic published a short article highlighting the safe house detail and noting that "the government has not commented on the decision to leave the family's address in the document it posted online," sparking a fresh wave of outrage over the Trump administration endangering the family.

"The Trump administration doxxed an American citizen, endangering her and her children," MSNBC contributor Rotimi Adeoye wrote on X Wednesday. "This is completely unacceptable and flat-out wrong."

Several others responded on the social media platform Bluesky.

"These fascists didn't stop at abducting Abrego Garcia, they've now doxxed his wife, forcing her into hiding," said Dean Preston, the leader of a renters' rights organization. "The Trump administration is terrorizing this family. Speak up, show up, resist."

Jonathan Cohn, political director for the group Progressive Mass, similarly declared, "The Trump administration is terrorizing this woman."

Katherine Hawkins, senior legal analyst for the Project On Government Oversight's Constitution Project, openly wondered "if publishing Abrego Garcia and his wife's home address violates federal or (particularly) Maryland laws."

"Definitely unconscionable and further demonstration of bad faith/intimidation," Hawkins added.

While Abrego Garcia's family seeks refuge in a U.S. safe house, he remains behind bars in his native El Salvador—despite the Supreme Court order from earlier this month and an immigration judge's 2019 decision that was supposed to prevent his deportation. Multiple congressional Democrats have flown to the country in recent days to support demands for his freedom.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Unforgivable: The Trump Administration's Dangerous Missteps

In an egregious display of governmental negligence and potential malfeasance, the Trump administration has not only mishandled the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia but has escalated its actions to a level that endangers the safety of an American citizen and her children. This situation is not just a systemic failure; it's a stark illustration of the administration's cavalier disregard for individual rights and privacy. Let's dissect the layers of incompetence and potential illegality that have been reported.

The Unwarranted Deportation and Detention: At the heart of this debacle is the administrative error that led to Kilmar Abrego Garcia's deportation to a Salvadoran megaprison. It's a diplomatic and bureaucratic blunder of the highest order, especially given the refusal to comply with a U.S. Supreme Court order to facilitate his return. This alone would be sufficient to question the administration's competence and respect for the rule of law. Yet, the story descends further into the realm of the unconscionable.

Doxxing by Government Officials: The revelation that the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) and White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt shared the home address of Abrego Garcia's family is not merely irresponsible; it's potentially criminal. The act of doxxing—publishing private or identifying information about an individual on the internet, typically with malicious intent—by government officials is nothing short of appalling. Jennifer Vasquez Sura's statement, "I don't feel safe when the government posts my address...for everyone to see," echoes the sentiments of any reasonable person suddenly thrust into the public eye without consent, and in this context, by those in power.

The consequences of this action are far-reaching. Vasquez Sura and her children were forced to relocate to a safe house, an upheaval caused directly by the administration's recklessness. This act has not only endangered the physical safety of an American family but has also inflicted emotional and psychological distress.

The Response: The administration's silence in the wake of these actions is deafening. A DHS spokesperson's failure to comment on the decision to leave the family's address in the publicly posted document is indicative of a broader culture of unaccountability. Meanwhile, public outrage and condemnation from various quarters—including MSNBC contributor Rotimi Adeoye and political director for Progressive Mass, Jonathan Cohn—underscore the gravity of the administration's actions.

Legal and Ethical Implications: Katherine Hawkins, senior legal analyst for the Project On Government Oversight, raises a critical question regarding the legality of publishing Abrego Garcia and his wife's home address. Beyond the potential violation of federal or Maryland laws, this act represents a flagrant disregard for basic ethical standards of privacy and safety. It's unconscionable and a glaring demonstration of bad faith and intimidation tactics by those in power.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's handling of Kilmar Abrego Garcia's case, compounded by the doxxing of his wife, is not just a failure; it's a dangerous precedent. It reflects a disturbing trend of disregard for individual rights, privacy, and safety by government officials. This situation demands not just public outcry but stringent legal scrutiny and accountability. The administration's actions should not and cannot be brushed aside as mere policy missteps or bureaucratic blunders. They are, as described, acts of terror against an American family. It's a chilling reminder of the power wielded by those in office and the catastrophic consequences when it is abused.
