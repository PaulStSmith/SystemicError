'Are you kidding?' Stunned CNN expert scolds Dems as Trump soars despite turmoil

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Harry Enten on which party has the better economic plan (CNN Screenshot) 2025-06-02

The polls are in, and Americans have a clear favorite party to handle the economy. CNN Chief Data Analyst Harry Enten is asking how, after the first four months of the Trump presidency, are Democrats not polling better?

“You would think after all of the waves, after the last few months, the first four months of the Donald Trump presidency, you'd expect that Democrats would have this massive lead on the economy," he said.

"It ain't so. It ain't so.”

“The party that is closest to your economic views in November of 2023, it was the Republicans by 11 points.” Enten went on to say, “Now it's still within that range, still within that margin of error. Plus eight-point advantage for the Republican Party. How is that possible, Democrats?”

He added, “How is that possible? After all the recession fears, after the stock market's been doing all of this, after all the tariffs that Americans are against, and Republicans still hold an eight-point lead on the economy. Are you kidding me?”

The new CNN poll is not the only set of data showing this trend. Enten then pointed to a Reuters-Ipsos poll, which asked voters who is the party with the better economic plan?

“What do we see here?” Enten asked rhetorically, “Party with a better economic plan. Well, in May of 2024, just before Donald Trump was reelected president, Republicans had a nine-point advantage. Look at where we are now. In May of 2025, the advantage actually went up by three points.”

“Now, Republicans have a 12-point advantage when it comes to the party with a better economic plan,” Enten said, “And again, this is after months of economic uncertainty in which the stock market's been going bonkers, in which the tariff wars that Americans are against have been going on, and yet, despite all of that, the Democrats are down by 12 points on the economy.”

Enten added, “This speaks to Democratic problems on the economy better than basically anything that you could possibly look at.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing America's Economic Party Preference: A Scathing Review

Let's dissect the bewildering reality that despite the economic turbulence under the Trump presidency, including the fears of recession, volatile stock markets, and unpopular tariffs, the Republican Party still holds a significant lead over Democrats when it comes to handling the economy. How can this be?

"You would think... It ain't so." These words from CNN Chief Data Analyst Harry Enten encapsulate a befuddling scenario. In a rational world, the cocktail of economic instability and controversial policies during the first four months of the Trump presidency would see the Democrats soaring in polls regarding economic trust. Yet, here we are, in an apparent paradox where the Republicans not only maintain a lead but have it by 11 points in November of 2023, slightly narrowing to an 8-point advantage thereafter.

How Is This Possible, Democrats? Indeed, how? The Democrats seem to be facing an image crisis of monumental proportions. Despite the Republicans' enactment of policies that have directly contradicted the economic well-being and preferences of many Americans, the Democratic Party struggles to be perceived as the better economic steward. This isn't just a wake-up call; it's an air raid siren for the Democratic leadership.

The Polls Speak Volumes, and not in the way Democrats would hope. The shift from a 9-point to a 12-point advantage in favor of Republicans regarding who has the better economic plan, as revealed by Reuters-Ipsos, is more than just a statistical anomaly. It's a damning indictment of the Democrats' inability to effectively communicate and position themselves as the preferable alternative.

Economic Uncertainty and Public Perception: It's baffling that amid "economic uncertainty" and policies that have rattled the stock market and ignited tariff wars — actions that have tangible, negative impacts on Americans — the Republican lead has not just persisted but increased. This situation highlights a profound disconnect between policy outcomes and public perception, one that Democrats have failed to bridge.

The Democratic Dilemma

This data points to a deeper issue within the Democratic Party: a failure to resonate. It's not just about policies but about the narrative, the messaging, and the connection with the economic sentiments of the American populace. When tariffs that Americans oppose and stock market volatility do not sway economic trust away from Republicans, it signifies a monumental failure on the part of Democrats to craft and sell a compelling economic vision.

Conclusion: A Call to Action for Democrats

The numbers don't lie. They scream for a dramatic reevaluation of strategy by the Democratic Party. If ever there was a moment for introspection and a strategic overhaul, it's now. The party must find a way to effectively communicate its economic policies and vision to the American public. Failure to do so doesn't just spell trouble for the next election cycle; it questions the party's relevance in the economic discourse of the nation.

In essence, this isn't just a wake-up call; it's a rallying cry. The Democrats have a serious perception problem, and if they don't address it with the urgency and seriousness it deserves, they risk being sidelined in the crucial debates that shape America's economic future.
