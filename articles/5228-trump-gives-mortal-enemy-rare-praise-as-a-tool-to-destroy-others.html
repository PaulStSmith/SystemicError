Trump gives mortal enemy rare praise — as a tool to destroy others

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 18, 20264 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as he speaks to reporters onboard Air Force One, on travel from West Palm Beach, Florida, to Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., February 16, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

President Donald Trump issued one of his longstanding political rivals a rare compliment Monday while aboard Air Force One, though only in an effort to attack two other political rivals of his.

Unprompted, Trump told reporters Monday night that he had watched footage of the annual Munich Security Conference in Germany, and had a few choice words for two of its speakers in particular: Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and California Gov. Gavin Newsom.

“I watched AOC answering questions in Munich – this was not a good look for the United States,” Trump said. “I watched Gavin ‘Newscum’ answering questions in Munich, and this was a bad look for our country!”

Both Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom spoke at the conference, and both took harsh digs at Trump and his administration. Ocasio-Cortez warned that Trump’s policies were designed to usher in an “age of authoritarianism,” while Newsom offered a backhanded compliment crediting Trump with uniting Europe against his foreign policy.

Their remarks apparently didn’t sit well with Trump, who felt compelled to offer his own backhanded compliment to Hillary Clinton, invoking his former rival to cast the two in an unflattering light.

“These two people are incompetent! At least Hillary’s competent. She’s just Trump deranged, she was so deranged, she is an angry woman,” Trump said.

“But I watched the other two speaking and answering basic questions… [AOC] had no idea what was happening, she had no idea how to answer very important questions concerning the world. She can’t answer questions concerning New York City either, because New York City is ... got some problems.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Munich Critique: A Masterclass in Political Deflection

In a maneuver as predictable as it is tiresome, President Donald Trump has once again launched an attack against his political adversaries, this time setting his sights on Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) and California Gov. Gavin Newsom during an impromptu commentary aboard Air Force One. The incident, which unfolded on the backdrop of the annual Munich Security Conference in Germany, serves as a pristine example of Trump’s modus operandi: divert, denigrate, and deflect.

The Art of Unwarranted Criticism

Trump’s critique of Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom’s performance at the conference was, according to his own account, based on their “bad look for the United States.” This statement, dripping with irony, comes from a president whose own international appearances have often been marred by gaffes, misstatements, and a general disregard for diplomatic norms. The claim that Ocasio-Cortez “had no idea what was happening” and was unable to answer “very important questions concerning the world” is not just a baseless assertion but a stark reflection of Trump’s own insecurities projected onto those who dare to criticize him.

Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom, in their speeches, took aim at the Trump administration’s policies, suggesting they were steering the U.S. toward an “age of authoritarianism” and ironically crediting Trump with uniting Europe—against his foreign policy. These critiques, grounded in the tangible outcomes of Trump’s policies, were met not with counterarguments but with personal attacks, a testament to Trump’s inability to engage in substantive policy discussion.

A Rare Compliment as a Vehicle for Insult

In an odd twist, Trump invoked Hillary Clinton—his former rival and frequent target of his ire—as a tool to further disparage Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom. By calling Clinton “competent” in comparison, Trump aimed not to praise Clinton but to underscore his disdain for the other two Democrats. This backhanded compliment is a classic Trump tactic: elevating one adversary to knock down others, all while ensuring the conversation remains squarely about him and his viewpoints.

Trump’s Real Fear: Competent Criticism

The heart of Trump’s tirade lies not in a genuine concern for the United States’ image but in a fear of potent criticism. Ocasio-Cortez’s warning of an “age of authoritarianism” and Newsom’s pointed remarks about Trump’s foreign policy are not mere political jabs; they are incisive critiques that resonate with a significant portion of the American populace and beyond. Trump’s response—reducing these criticisms to incompetency and personal attacks—reveals a president more concerned with maintaining a facade of unassailability than with addressing the substantive issues at hand.

In essence, Trump’s comments aboard Air Force One were less about the performances of Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom and more about reasserting his dominance in the political narrative. By attacking his critics, Trump seeks to divert attention from the content of their critiques, focusing instead on a manufactured narrative of incompetence and derangement. This tactic, while transparent, remains a cornerstone of Trump’s political strategy, one that prioritizes personal vendettas over policy discourse.

In conclusion, Trump’s rare compliment to Hillary Clinton, served in a cocktail of criticism towards Ocasio-Cortez and Newsom, is a strategic deflection designed not to elevate political discourse but to mire it in the muck of personal attack. It’s a reminder that in Trump’s political playbook, the art of the insult often takes precedence over the substance of the argument.
