'Anguished' troops say Trump just put them in an 'impossible situation': ex-Republican vet

“Anguished” active duty military troops are feeling “moral doubt” and “profound legal confusion” as a result of Donald Trump’s actions, according to a conservative veteran of Operation Iraqi Freedom.

New York Times columnist David French, a former writer for the conservative National Review and Iraq War vet, wrote an article on Sunday called, “Trump Has Put the Military in an Impossible Situation,” in which he argues that soldiers are required to reject illegal orders. According to French, individual soldiers can’t be expected to have the knowledge to make that analysis for themselves every time.

But that’s what’s happening, according to the columnist.

“Trump has put the military in an impossible situation. He’s making its most senior leaders complicit in his unlawful acts, and he’s burdening the consciences of soldiers who serve under his command. One of the great moral values of congressional declarations of war is that they provide soldiers with the assurance that the conflict has been debated and that their deployment is a matter of national will,” French wrote. “When the decision rests with the president alone, it puts members of the military in the position of trusting the judgment of a person who may not deserve that trust. I have heard from several anguished members of the active duty military. They feel real moral doubt and are experiencing profound legal confusion.”

He then adds, “So here’s the bottom line: No legal opinion can compel any member of the military to commit ‘manifestly unlawful’ acts during a war.”

“But when it comes to the decision to begin an armed conflict, the responsibility doesn’t rest with individual soldiers, sailors, airmen or marines; it rests with Trump and his most senior military and political advisers — the men and women who ordered them to fight,” he wrote in the article.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Quandary: Trump’s Military Morass

In a recent piece for the New York Times, David French, a conservative voice and veteran of Operation Iraqi Freedom, casts a stark light on the turmoil within the ranks of the United States military under the leadership—or, more aptly, the misleadership—of Donald Trump. The article, “Trump Has Put the Military in an Impossible Situation,” serves not just as a critique but as an alarm bell regarding the ethical and legal predicaments facing active duty troops.

The Ethical Labyrinth

French articulates a scenario where soldiers, trained for combat and not jurisprudence, find themselves ensnared in a web of “moral doubt” and “profound legal confusion.” This isn’t merely an issue of battlefield ethics; it’s a profound crisis of faith in the chain of command. When the highest authority in the land, the Commander in Chief, issues directives that tread the murky waters of legality, it places an unbearable weight on the shoulders of those sworn to protect that very nation.

The core of the matter here is trust—or the catastrophic lack thereof. Trust in the president’s judgment becomes the linchpin of military obedience and morale. Yet, as French deftly argues, Trump’s actions have eroded this foundation, leaving troops in a quagmire of doubt. The assurance traditionally granted by congressional declarations of war has been supplanted by a solitary decision-maker whose compass, many fear, points nowhere near north.

The Legal Conundrum

French does not leave the reader with the impression that soldiers are expected to blindly follow orders into the abyss of unlawfulness. Indeed, he underscores a critical tenet of military service: the obligation to reject manifestly unlawful orders. However, the expectation that individual service members can—or should—be able to navigate these legal minefields independently is both unreasonable and unrealistic.

What French brings to the fore is a disturbing picture of a military caught in a vice between legality and loyalty. The “impossible situation” Trump has engineered does not merely ask soldiers to evaluate the legality of orders in real-time; it asks them to question the very legitimacy of their deployment. This is not a matter of typical military obedience but a profound crisis of legal and moral judgment foisted upon them by those at the apex of power.

The Bottom Line

The imperative takeaway from French’s analysis is clear: while soldiers bear the brunt of following—or defying—orders, the ultimate responsibility for the decision to engage in armed conflict rests with Trump and his cadre of senior advisors. This is a critical distinction that shifts the narrative from individual culpability to systemic failure.

French’s article, therefore, is not just an indictment of Trump’s handling of military affairs; it’s a clarion call for a reevaluation of how decisions of war and peace are made. The anguished cries of active duty members reflect not just personal turmoil but a profound institutional crisis that threatens the very fabric of military cohesion and morale.

In sum, David French’s piece is a scathing, yet meticulously argued, critique of the Trump administration’s cavalier attitude towards military ethics and legality. It highlights a dangerous precedent where soldiers are left to grapple with the moral and legal implications of their orders, a scenario that is as unsustainable as it is unconscionable. The message is unequivocal: this is not merely a failure of leadership, but a systemic erosion of trust and legality that puts the entire military institution—and, by extension, the nation—in jeopardy.
