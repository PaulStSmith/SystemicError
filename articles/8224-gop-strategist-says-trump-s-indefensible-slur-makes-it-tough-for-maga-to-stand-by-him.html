GOP strategist says Trump's 'indefensible' slur makes it 'tough' for MAGA to stand by him

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unabashed Hypocrisy of Trump’s MAGA Following

President Donald Trump’s use of a slur for intellectually disabled people to describe one of his political opponents may be costing him some MAGA support, according to one Republican strategist.

Mediaite reported Friday that during a segment on CNN, Joseph Moreno — a military veteran and former federal prosecutor — lamented Trump called Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz (D) “seriously retarded” on Thanksgiving Day. Trump insulted the Minnesota governor in the middle of a rant about Somali refugees in the Gopher State, as part of his comments in the wake of National Guard member Sarah Beckstrom being fatally shot in Washington D.C. this week.

When host Boris Sanchez quipped: “What happened to ‘Happy Thanksgiving,’” Moreno didn’t hold back.

“Boris, you’re putting your finger, honestly, on what’s so difficult to be a conservative-leaning citizen, who, my whole life has cherished vigorous, but respectful debate,” Moreno said. “And so when you see something like this, which is absolutely indefensible, and you’re sitting at the Thanksgiving table, and your family and friends say, ‘How can you possibly support an administration that comes out with messages like this?’ It’s not easy.”

“And you have to take the big picture view. Do you think that we’re better off as a country now than we would be under a Biden or Harris administration? And is it worth putting up with a president that puts out messaging like this?” He continued. “It’s a tough one, and I’m not gonna pretend I know the answer to that.”

Moreno wasn’t the only conservative who was out off by Trump’s comment. Earlier on Friday, Indiana state senator Michael Bohacek announced that he would not be supporting the president’s push for mid-decade redrawing of U.S. House district boundaries due to his “insulting and derogatory” remarks. Bohacek noted that he was particularly sensitive to the intellectually disabled community given that his daughter has Down Syndrome.

The Trump administration has announced it is halting all asylum applications and immigration from underdeveloped countries in the wake of the Washington D.C. shooting. 29 year-old Afghan national Rahmanullah Lakanwal was arrested and charged with carrying out the attack. Trump attributed Lakanwal’s alleged murder of Beckstrom to former President Joe Biden’s immigration and refugee policies, though the Trump administration’s Department of Homeland Security granted his asylum application earlier this year.

Our Analysis:

The Unabashed Hypocrisy of Trump’s MAGA Following

In a recent media spectacle, former President Donald Trump’s derogatory outburst, referring to Minnesota Governor Tim Walz as “seriously retarded,” has seemingly ruffled some feathers within his own MAGA camp. This incident, reported by Mediaite and discussed on CNN, showcases not just Trump’s penchant for offensive remarks but also the profound dilemma it poses for his supporters, especially those who have long championed the virtues of “vigorous, but respectful debate.”

The Irony of Moreno’s Defense

Joseph Moreno, a military veteran and former federal prosecutor, represents the internal conflict faced by conservative-leaning citizens who are now forced to confront Trump’s indefensible language. Moreno’s lamentation on CNN about the difficulty of supporting an administration that spews such vitriol, especially during a time meant for gratitude and reflection like Thanksgiving, is telling. His struggle to justify support for Trump, weighed against the potential horrors of a Biden or Harris administration, embodies the cognitive dissonance plaguing some segments of the MAGA base. Yet, one cannot help but detect the irony in Moreno’s angst-ridden defense. The question isn’t just about being “better off” under one administration or another; it’s about the moral and ethical standards we demand from our leaders. Trump’s remarks are not just a departure from decency but a headlong plunge into abhorrence.

Michael Bohacek’s Stand: A Rare Glimmer of Integrity

Indiana state senator Michael Bohacek’s decision to withdraw support for Trump’s redistricting ambitions over the offensive remarks provides a rare, albeit small, glimmer of integrity within the Republican ranks. Bohacek’s sensitivity to the intellectually disabled community, given his personal connection through his daughter with Down Syndrome, underscores a fundamental truth: political allegiance should never eclipse basic human decency. It’s a stark reminder that the lines we draw in the political sand are, after all, drawn in sand, not stone, and must be reassessed when the tide of decency recedes.

Trump’s Blame Game and Policy Hypocrisy

The article also touches on Trump’s dangerous rhetoric in the wake of the tragic shooting of National Guard member Sarah Beckstrom, attributing the act to Rahmanullah Lakanwal, an Afghan national, and by extension, to Joe Biden’s immigration policies. This, despite the glaring fact that it was Trump’s own administration that granted Lakanwal’s asylum application. The hypocrisy is as blatant as it is appalling, revealing a man and an administration willing to distort facts and manipulate tragedies for political gain. The decision to halt all asylum applications and immigration from underdeveloped countries in response to the shooting exemplifies a reactionary and xenophobic policy stance that does nothing to address the complexities of immigration, national security, and human decency.

In sum, the reactions to Trump’s derogatory comments about Governor Walz, from Moreno’s conflicted support to Bohacek’s principled stand, unveil the deep fractures within the MAGA movement and the broader Republican Party. They expose the moral quandaries and ethical gymnastics required to remain loyal to a leader whose behavior is indefensible. The real question for those still clinging to Trump’s coattails is not whether the country is better off under one administration or another, but whether their conscience can bear the weight of their continued support.
