'It is driving them nuts!' CNN panel warns latest revelations spell midterm doom for Trump

A CNN panel painted a grim picture Tuesday for President Donald Trump and the Republican Party headed into the 2026 midterm elections as evidence mounts against the president regarding his past ties with Jeffrey Epstein, new details of which were revealed Monday by a House Committee.

“This is the story that will not go away and it is driving them nuts!” said Democratic strategist Chuck Rocha, a former union organizer and campaign advisor to Sen. Bernie Sanders (D-VT).

“Every day that we're not talking about immigration, law enforcement and all the things that Trump wants to talk about, is losing. And it's his own fault because, as a political consultant, we all remembered we covered this: how the right is just rampant over this issue because they fed this machine, and now this machine wants something in return, and they're like 'not our Donald!'”

New details of Trump and Epstein’s relationship were revealed Monday by Democrats on the House Oversight Committee after having issued a subpoena to the Justice Department, compelling the agency to release more files that it holds on Epstein, who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex trafficking charges and is alleged to have run a blackmail operation targeting powerful figures.

Such details include a copy of the letter Trump allegedly sent Epstein for his 50th birthday, in which the president supposedly writes to the disgraced financier “may every day be another wonderful secret.” Another new revelation is that Epstein once joked about selling a “fully depreciated” woman to Trump for $22,500.

Trump has called the birthday letter a hoax and denied any wrongdoing as it relates to his history with Epstein. Participants on the CNN panel Tuesday, however, noted that Trump’s persistent denials were only digging him a deeper whole.

“The more he denies it – if he really did do it – the longer the story is going to last,” said Ashley David, a former White House official under President George W. Bush.

Susan Page, political commentator, journalist and author, came to the same conclusion that Trump and the White House were only making things worse with their outright denials.

“The White House has been, I think, stunned by the inability to put the Epstein issue behind them, and it just keeps getting bigger,” Page said. “[It’s adding] more fuel to the fire.”

Trump’s ties to Epstein go much further than the details revealed Monday. Trump once called Epstein a “terrific guy” who “likes beautiful women as much as I do, and many of them are on the younger side.”

It’s the trove of evidence that suggests the two enjoyed more than a passing relationship, Rocha argued, that could even create a major liability for Trump and the GOP in the upcoming midterm elections.

“Midterm elections are not presidential elections, not everybody votes, and it's motivation; he needs his base to show up in a big way to win back Congress for the Republicans,” Rocha said.

“There's people on the right – I'm not saying they'll vote Democrat, I'm just saying that there's enough people that could be frustrated by this because they fed this machine that they could be aggravated enough to stay home.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Ties with Epstein: A Political Quagmire

In an era where political scandals seem to be as common as weather changes, the recent revelations linking President Donald Trump to Jeffrey Epstein, the disgraced financier and convicted sex offender, have opened a new chapter of controversy that refuses to be shelved. A CNN panel discussion highlighted the growing unease within the Republican Party as more evidence surfaces, painting a grim forecast for the 2026 midterm elections. Let's dissect the elements of this unfolding drama with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned investigator.

The Unrelenting Shadow of Epstein

Jeffrey Epstein's death in 2019 did little to curtain the litany of accusations and investigations surrounding his life, particularly his connections with powerful figures, including President Trump. The House Oversight Committee's recent subpoena to the Justice Department, compelling the release of more documents related to Epstein, has unearthed unsettling details. Among these, a letter Trump allegedly sent to Epstein for his 50th birthday, filled with cryptic praise, and a distasteful joke about selling a "fully depreciated" woman, add layers of sordid intrigue to Trump's already controversial persona.

Denials and Digging Deeper

Trump's vehement denials and the White House's attempts to dismiss the Epstein connection have not quelled the storm but, according to commentators like Ashley David and Susan Page, have only exacerbated the situation. The strategy of denial, a common refuge for the embattled, seems to be failing Trump spectacularly. The more he denies, the deeper the hole he digs for himself and, by extension, the Republican Party. In politics, as in life, perception often trumps reality, and the growing perception is one of a president deeply mired in unsavory associations.

Political Repercussions

Chuck Rocha's analysis brings to light a critical aspect of this saga - the potential political fallout. Midterm elections, often seen as a referendum on the sitting president's performance, could be severely impacted by the Epstein revelations. The Republican base, already fed on a steady diet of controversies, might find this latest serving too distasteful to swallow. The concern isn't that traditional Republican voters will switch allegiance to the Democrats but that their disillusionment might lead to apathy, affecting voter turnout.

A Damning Association

The crux of the matter lies in Trump's past praises of Epstein, notably calling him a "terrific guy" who "likes beautiful women as much as I do, and many of them are on the younger side." This admission, more than any subpoenaed document, speaks volumes about the character and judgment of the president. It's a damning association, one that transcends political spin and enters the realm of moral bankruptcy.

Conclusion

In the grand theater of political scandals, the Trump-Epstein saga is a play that has yet to reach its final act. The revelations and ensuing controversy serve as a stark reminder of the perils of power unchecked and associations unvetted. For the Republican Party, the 2026 midterms loom as a potential storm, one that could wash away any remaining credibility unless they can convincingly distance themselves from the mire. For Trump, the path forward is fraught with peril, a path that could lead to political oblivion if he's not careful. As the story unfolds, one thing is clear: the truth, however uncomfortable, cannot be denied indefinitely.
