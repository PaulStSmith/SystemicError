Bread at $1,000 a Loaf: Trump, the Fed, and the Real Threat of Hyperinflation

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20255 min read

Kevin O’Leary, a man hardly known for progressive economics, stumbled into an uncomfortable truth when he warned that Trump’s threats against the Federal Reserve could push America into Venezuelan-style hyperinflation. It’s rare that a “Shark Tank” investor utters something so starkly obvious yet so widely ignored: the U.S. Federal Reserve, for all its flaws, exists to protect monetary policy from the whims of politicians. Allow Donald Trump—or any would-be autocrat—to dictate interest rates, and you are essentially handing the nuclear codes of the economy to a pyromaniac.

And make no mistake: Trump’s ongoing crusade against Jerome Powell and his attempts to purge independent members of the Fed board is not about sound policy. It is about control. It is about eliminating one of the last institutional barriers between his political ambitions and economic catastrophe.

Americans Have No Concept of Hyperinflation

Here is the problem: no American alive has ever truly lived through hyperinflation. The U.S. endured the Great Depression, recessions, financial crises, and the stagflation of the 1970s, but never the collapse of money’s very meaning. Even in the toughest times, a dollar remained a dollar. Families tightened belts, businesses closed, banks failed, but currency retained a baseline of stability.

Contrast that with Weimar Germany, where workers were paid twice daily because their wages would be worthless by sunset. Or Zimbabwe in the 2000s, where the government printed trillion-dollar bills that couldn’t buy a loaf of bread. Or Venezuela, where price tags rose faster than people could shop. Hyperinflation is not just rising prices—it is the total annihilation of trust in money itself.

Americans have never pushed wheelbarrows of cash to buy food, only to be told it wasn’t enough. They have never seen pensions and savings evaporate overnight into worthless paper. And precisely because they’ve never experienced it, they cannot fathom that Trump’s reckless meddling with the Fed makes such a nightmare conceivable.

The Fed: An Imperfect But Essential Dam

The Federal Reserve is not a saintly institution. It has bailed out banks, failed to foresee crises, and often catered to Wall Street more than Main Street. But its independence—the idea that it sets monetary policy free from the president’s tantrums—remains one of the cornerstones of America’s economic stability.

That independence is what prevents populist strongmen from juicing the economy before elections, printing cash, and flooding markets until inflation devours everything. It is what reassures global investors that the U.S. dollar will remain the world’s reserve currency. It is the silent guarantee that American wages, pensions, and contracts are denominated in something real.

Trump’s desire to fire Lisa Cook over a flimsy “mortgage fraud” accusation, his ongoing pressure campaign against Powell, and his installation of loyalists onto the board are not policy debates. They are authoritarian encroachments. They are steps in a broader strategy to bend monetary policy to his will, no matter the consequences.

The Banana Republic Playbook

This is how it starts: dismantle independent institutions, vilify experts, replace them with loyal sycophants, then dictate outcomes. It has been done in Turkey, where Erdogan’s meddling with the central bank destroyed the lira. It has been done in Venezuela, where Chavez and Maduro used the central bank as a political tool until the bolívar became toilet paper. It has been done in countless authoritarian regimes where “leaders” thought they could bend math to politics.

Trump’s obsession with controlling the Fed fits the same pattern. He wants to lower rates regardless of inflationary pressure, regardless of global consequences, because it juices markets in the short term. It lets him claim credit for growth while planting the seeds of collapse. It is the most dangerous combination imaginable: dictatorial impulse plus staggering economic ignorance.

The Dollar as the World’s Anchor

Since World War II, America has been the anchor of the global economy. The dollar became the reserve currency because the United States was stable, trusted, and unmatched in industrial capacity. Europe was rubble, Japan was shattered, and the Soviet Union was an opaque command economy. The U.S. leveraged this unique position into global dominance—not only militarily, but economically.

Every major transaction on earth—from oil to steel to soybeans—is denominated in dollars. This is not just a privilege, it is the foundation of U.S. power. It allows the government to run deficits others cannot, to enforce sanctions globally, and to live with a level of economic comfort unmatched in human history.

Hyperinflation in the United States would not be just another recession. It would be the collapse of this entire system. If the dollar loses its credibility, the world scrambles for alternatives. China, Russia, the EU, or even decentralized cryptocurrencies step into the void. The U.S. loses its global economic throne, not through foreign invasion, but through self-inflicted sabotage.

Tariffs, Debt, and Wall Street Sophistry

Trump’s economic team cloaks its schemes in the language of fiscal prudence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent brags that tariff revenue will pay down the debt. Kevin O’Leary fantasizes about using windfalls to balance the books. This is smoke and mirrors. Tariffs are not “revenue”—they are consumer taxes. They bleed households, raise prices, and slow growth.

Even if tariffs filled federal coffers, it would not offset the devastation of undermining Fed independence. You cannot pay down debt in a currency that no one trusts. You cannot balance books when inflation eats digits faster than they can be printed. And you certainly cannot maintain global hegemony when markets flee the dollar for safer shores.

The Stakes: Beyond Groceries

The cost of Trump’s interference will not be measured in abstract GDP points. It will be measured in grocery bills that double weekly, rents that triple monthly, retirement accounts that vanish, and paychecks that become worthless before they clear. It will be measured in civil unrest when ordinary Americans realize the paper in their wallets is no longer money but kindling.

And the rest of the world will not wait. Once hyperinflation looms, investors flee, trade contracts shift, and alternative currencies gain ground. The dollar will no longer be the safe harbor. It will be the sinking ship.

Conclusion: Ignore the Canary at Your Peril

Kevin O’Leary’s warning should not be dismissed. It should terrify. If even a capitalist shark can see the abyss Trump is dragging America toward, the danger is real. The independence of the Federal Reserve is not some arcane technocratic detail. It is the bedrock of modern prosperity. Undermine it, and the result is not mere inflation. It is collapse.

Americans believe “it can’t happen here.” They believe their wealth, their pensions, their grocery bills are insulated by history and geography. But history is not insulation—it is a warning. And the warning is clear: once a nation allows a strongman to seize control of money itself, the slide into hyperinflation is swift, brutal, and irreversible.

If Trump gets his way, Americans won’t just read about wheelbarrows of cash in history books. They’ll be pushing them down the aisles of Walmart, praying that what’s inside still buys bread before the register updates the price.
