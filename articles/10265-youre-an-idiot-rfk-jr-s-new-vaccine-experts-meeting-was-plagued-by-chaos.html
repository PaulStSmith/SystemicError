‘You’re an idiot’: RFK Jr.’s new vaccine ‘experts’ meeting was plagued by chaos

By Paulo Santos

Bethesda, MD, USA 11/21/2020: Vaccine Research Center of National Institutes of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) at NIH a world leader in vaccine development including COVID-19 vaccine. (Photo credit: grandbrothers / Shutterstock)

A panel designed to shape national vaccine policy made up entirely of new appointments by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy held a series of chaotic first meetings Thursday and Friday that was plagued with distractions, missteps and misinformation in what health experts decried as a “shipwreck.”

That panel, the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices within the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention – part of HHS – had its 17 former members removed by RFK Jr. and replaced, with its new members holding their first meetings Thursday and Friday. Health experts who observed the meetings said the panel’s discussions were “troubling.”

“It’s troubling to see the erosion of the committee’s integrity,” said Sandra Fryhofer, a physician with nearly two decades of experience working as a liaison for the American Medical Association, speaking to the panel according to a report Saturday from the Washington Post.

“We’re concerned about how vaccine recommendations are being developed by this new panel. Data is being selectively used to justify specific conclusions rather than considering all of the available evidence.”

Members of the panel floated several concerns over COVID-19 vaccines during the meetings, including concerns that the vaccines may have links to increased rates of cancer and autism, or that they could contaminate a person’s DNA, all claims that have either been thoroughly debunked or lack any scientific evidence.

Panelists also voted to stop recommending a combination of vaccines to toddlers during their Thursday meeting – measles, mumps, rubella and chicken pox vaccines – but voted the next morning to provide the same combination of vaccines to toddlers in a confusing, contradictory vote, the Washington Times reported.

One panelist blamed her mother’s cancer on a COVID-19 vaccine booster shot, while other panel members were largely distracted, such as panelist Robert Malone, who during the meetings posted on social media repeatedly about transgender people, Turning Point USA, and about his impending appearance on a podcast with Roseanne Barr.

During the chaos, one meeting attendee could be heard uttering “you’re an idiot.”

“What we're seeing is what happens when individuals who don't have a basic understanding about how vaccines are delivered are making these crucial policy decisions for the American public,” said Sean T. O’Leary, the chair of the American Academy of Pediatrics committee on infectious diseases, speaking with the Washington Post.

Our Analysis:

The Shipwreck of Science: RFK Jr.'s Advisory Committee Catastrophe

In an alarming display of scientific ignorance and chaotic decision-making, the newly appointed Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices, under the auspices of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., has initiated its tenure with what can only be described as a carnival of misinformation. The recent meetings held by this committee, now entirely composed of Kennedy's appointees, have been a spectacle of confusion, distractions, and most concerningly, the propagation of debunked myths regarding vaccines.

Misinformation Masquerading as Policy

The Advisory Committee, a cornerstone in the formulation of national vaccine policy, witnessed its entire roster overhauled by RFK Jr., a move that has swiftly culminated in a shipwreck of its former scientific integrity. The first gatherings of this newly minted panel were marred by discussions that veered into the realm of the absurd, with claims linking COVID-19 vaccines to cancer, autism, and even DNA contamination. These assertions are not just unfounded but dangerously misleading, and they represent a flagrant disregard for the overwhelming body of scientific evidence that supports the safety and efficacy of vaccines.

The Erosion of Committee Integrity

The observations made by health experts like Sandra Fryhofer, a seasoned physician and liaison for the American Medical Association, spotlight a troubling erosion of the committee's integrity. The selective use of data to justify preconceived conclusions, rather than a comprehensive examination of available evidence, marks a significant departure from the committee's vital role in safeguarding public health through informed vaccine recommendations.

A Whirlwind of Contradictions

In a bewildering turn of events, the committee voted to halt the recommendation of a combination of vaccines for toddlers - a decision that stands in stark contrast to established health guidelines aimed at protecting children from preventable diseases. This vote was perplexingly followed by a contradictory decision the next morning to endorse the same combination of vaccines. Such flip-flopping not only undermines the committee's credibility but also sows confusion among the public about vaccine safety and efficacy.

Distractions and Divisiveness

The meetings were further derailed by distractions that had little to do with the committee's mandate. Notably, panelist Robert Malone's social media activities during the meetings, which included posts on contentious topics unrelated to vaccines, underscored a startling lack of focus and professionalism. These actions detract from the critical task at hand - evaluating and recommending vaccine policies based on sound science.

Concluding Thoughts

What has unfolded under RFK Jr.'s newly appointed Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices is a stark reminder of the perils of allowing political and ideological agendas to infiltrate scientific discourse and policy-making. The committee's first meetings have not only been a shipwreck but a dire warning. The path being charted by this panel is one that veers dangerously away from evidence-based medicine towards a turbulent sea of misinformation and pseudoscience. It is incumbent upon those who value public health and scientific integrity to voice their opposition to this alarming departure from reason and to advocate for the restoration of a committee grounded in credible, evidence-based science.
