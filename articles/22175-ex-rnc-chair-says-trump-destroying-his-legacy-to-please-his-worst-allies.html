Ex-RNC chair says Trump destroying his legacy to please his worst allies

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Is Burning a Proven Record to Feed the Anti-Vax Mob

The Setup

The report is simple enough: Donald Trump is now using presidential authority to endorse anti-vaccine conspiracy politics, even if it means contradicting one of his administration’s few real achievements. He has pushed an executive order that would alter the childhood vaccination schedule, including spacing out the MMR shots, while repeating the lie that vaccines are linked to autism.

Who Actually Holds Power

This is not a culture-war accident. Trump holds the institutional power, and he is choosing to use it to elevate fringe paranoia into the language of government. That matters because when a president speaks, the machinery of the state echoes him. The White House is not a podcast set. It is an amplifier with consequences.

The Decision Was Deliberate

The source makes clear this is not confusion or drift. Trump once touted Operation Warp Speed as a major achievement of his first term, and even his political opponents acknowledged the speed and scale of that effort. Now he is discarding that record to satisfy the most conspiracy-soaked parts of his base, including the anti-vaccine and “Make America Healthy Again” factions. That is not principle. It is transactional sabotage.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The article’s framing is strongest when it names the lie outright: vaccine-autism propaganda is false and dangerous. Too much political coverage still treats this kind of behavior as if it were merely erratic or “controversial.” It is neither. It is a president laundering internet poison through federal authority, turning fringe chatter into official posture and forcing doctors, parents, and historians to spend time cleaning up a mess he deliberately created.

What This Reveals About MAGA Politics

The pattern is larger than one vaccine order. Trump’s coalition is increasingly organized around grievance, paranoia, and hostility to expertise, and he is willing to feed all three if it buys loyalty. That is why a former Republican chair can describe the move as Trump trampling his own legacy: the legacy is being sacrificed to maintain control over a shrinking, more radicalized base. The country gets the bill.

The Democratic Lesson

The deeper lesson is not about Trump’s hypocrisy, which is already exhausted as a category. It is about what happens when a president uses state power to legitimize conspiracy culture. Democratic politics cannot treat this as a side issue or a messaging problem. It is institutional vandalism. A government that lends its authority to anti-science propaganda is not merely mistaken; it is helping metastasize public harm.
