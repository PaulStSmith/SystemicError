'Womp womp': MSNBC's Rachel Maddow mocks Trump as forgery scandal blows up massive deal

MSNBC's Rachel Maddow took to her eponymous show on Monday night to poke fun at Donald Trump's family over a project they have overseas that is now embroiled in scandal, as a key document in the deal was forged.

In opening her show, Maddow discussed large-scale protests in Serbia sparked by a deadly train station roof collapse, which exposed government corruption under the country's authoritarian, Trump-like president.

Amid broad protests, Donald Trump Jr. made multiple trips to the country to support the embattled leader, partly due to a controversial development plan, in which Donald Trump wants to tear down a monument and build a luxury hotel and apartment tower there.

"Literally on that site," remarked Maddow.

"Somehow, the very Trumpy, authoritarian president of that country—somehow he was able to come up with some kind of waiver, some kind of permission slip that said, actually, that site is no longer a protected national heritage site," a dubious Maddow added.

The president said he received an opinion from an expert that authorized tearing down the memorial.

But now, the "whole mess is all now falling apart," she said, sharing a headline that the Trump-Kushner hotel project had hit a "snag" due to alleged forgery.

"Forgery. What?" she exclaimed. "The Trump family’s $500 million luxury hotel project in Serbia—slated to be built on the site of a bombed-out defense ministry building—has run into an embarrassing complication. A key document the Serbian government has relied on to deliver this deal was forged."

Officials said the expert opinion was fabricated to justify the government’s decision to revoke the site's historic status, and the official involved now faces criminal charges.

"Darn that rule of law," Maddow quipped. "If it wasn’t for the remaining rule of law... and for those meddling kids."

Maddow noted there may never be a Trump Tower in Europe.

"Womp womp. Sad trombone," she chided.

Our Analysis:

Maddow's Scathing Take on the Trump Family's Serbian Misadventure

In a striking display of investigative journalism mixed with a dash of sarcasm, Rachel Maddow dissected a burgeoning scandal involving none other than the Trump family, specifically zeroing in on their overseas ventures that have now hit a legal and ethical snag. The focus of Maddow's ire? A $500 million luxury hotel project in Serbia, touted by the Trumps, which has found itself mired in controversy over a forged document purportedly justifying the demolition of a historic site.

The Plot Thickens in Serbia

Maddow's narrative began against the backdrop of Serbia, a nation grappling with its own set of problems, from large-scale protests sparked by a tragic train station roof collapse to a government rife with corruption. The country's leadership, likened to an authoritarian regime mirroring Trump's own political stylings, has been under fire. Amid this turmoil, Donald Trump Jr.'s visits to support the embattled Serbian leader, especially at a time when the Trump family had vested interests in the country, reek of opportunism and a blatant disregard for ethical boundaries.

A Monumental Controversy

At the heart of the scandal is a plan to demolish a monument to erect a luxury hotel and apartment tower—a plan that screams of both historical and ethical insensitivity. Maddow's disdain for the project is palpable, particularly her skepticism over the conveniently obtained waiver that declared the site, previously protected as a national heritage site, open for development. The revelation that the critical document underpinning this waiver was forged only adds fuel to the fire of controversy surrounding the Trump family's international dealings.

The Irony of Law and Order

Maddow's quip, "Darn that rule of law," delivered with a hefty dose of irony, underlines a significant point—despite the Trump family's attempts to navigate or even bypass the legal and ethical frameworks that govern such projects, they find themselves thwarted by the very principles of law and order they often espouse. The involvement of criminal charges against the official who fabricated the expert opinion is a testament to the ongoing battle against corruption and malfeasance, a battle that even the Trump family cannot sidestep.

A Schadenfreude-Inducing Conclusion

Maddow's commentary, "Womp womp. Sad trombone," while humorous, encapsulates a broader sentiment of schadenfreude felt by those who view the Trump family's failed venture as a comeuppance for their repeated ethical transgressions. The potential collapse of the Trump Tower project in Europe is not just a blow to their business ambitions but also a stark reminder of the consequences of overreaching in foreign nations with little regard for legal, ethical, or historical considerations.

In conclusion, Maddow's analysis of the Trump family's Serbian scandal offers a piercing look into the confluence of political power, business interests, and ethical boundaries. Her blend of factual reporting with sarcastic commentary not only informs but also entertains, providing a compelling narrative on the perils of hubris and the inevitable clash between personal ambitions and the rule of law.
