'Isn't going to work': Georgia gov tells GOP congressmen their brand is too toxic to win

By Paulo Santos

Brian Kemp taking the oath of office becoming the 83rd Governor of the state of Georgia. (Georgia National Guard photo by Maj. William Carraway)

Gov. Brian Kemp (R-GA) delivered a private warning to the state's Republican members of Congress who are running for Senate: your agenda in Congress has been too unpopular for you to be electable.

According to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Kemp made the admission during a call with Republican donors, while arguing they should support former football coach Derek Dooley as opposed to Reps. Buddy Carter and Mike Collins, who have also declared their candidacies.

“Another congressman from a heavily Republican district with a congressional voting record isn’t going to work,” said Kemp in audio obtained from the call. “I know and respect both Congressmen Carter and Collins. This is not an attack on them. It’s just the way that I feel. Ossoff will have hundreds of millions of dollars to define anyone’s voting record in the worst possible light.”

Both lawmakers signed on to President Donald Trump's "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," a piece of legislation that cut over $1 trillion from Medicaid, food assistance, and green energy in order to partially offset massive tax cut extensions for the ultra-wealthy.

As for Dooley, he added, “He’s a hard worker who can connect with people and will bring common sense to Washington. While he may not have a political background, he has been tested more than most.”

Republicans in recent years have looked to multiple other people from sports to run for political office, and while former football coach Tommy Tuberville was successfully elected to the Senate from Alabama, the model hasn't worked as well in Georgia.

In 2022, the GOP recruited former NFL star Herschel Walker to run against Democratic Sen. Raphael Warnock, and the campaign fell apart near the end, from Walker whipping out a prop police badge onstage at a debate when challenged on his exaggerated claims to have worked as a police officer, to allegations about a history of domestic abuse.

Our Analysis:

Kemp's Candid Warning: A Strategy or Desperation?

In a move that reeks more of desperation than strategic brilliance, Gov. Brian Kemp (R-GA) has thrown a sharp elbow at the current political playbook of Georgia's Republican Congressional delegation, suggesting their approach is too toxic for the electorate. During a call with Republican donors, as reported by The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, Kemp's message was clear: the status quo won't cut it against Democrats, especially not against the likes of Sen. Ossoff, who, according to Kemp, will have a hefty war chest to demonize any Republican contender.

Kemp's Choice: Derek Dooley Over Proven Politicians

Kemp's endorsement of Derek Dooley, a former football coach with zero political experience, over seasoned politicians like Reps. Buddy Carter and Mike Collins, is both a bold gamble and a stark admission of the Republican Party's current predicament. His rationale? A fresh face might fare better against the Democratic fundraising juggernaut, a notion that reeks of wishful thinking given the mixed success sports figures have had transitioning into politics in Georgia.

The Unpopular Agenda: A Liability Too Big to Ignore

Kemp's criticism of Carter and Collins isn't just a personal or political jab; it's a reflection of a broader issue within the Republican Party. Both congressmen's support for President Donald Trump's "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" — a legislation that proposed slashing over $1 trillion from essential services like Medicaid and food assistance to finance tax cuts for the ultra-wealthy — epitomizes the kind of policies Kemp deems electoral poison.

This isn't just about policy; it's about perception. Kemp is effectively arguing that the Republican agenda, as championed by Carter and Collins, is so out of step with the needs of ordinary Americans that it renders them unelectable. It's a damning indictment not just of two politicians but of a whole swath of the Republican platform.

The Sports Figure Gambit: Hope or Hype?

Kemp's pivot towards Dooley, a man "tested" in the fields of sports rather than the political arena, is telling. It suggests a belief that charisma and an outsider status might trump (no pun intended) political experience and a voting record. However, the debacle of Herschel Walker's campaign against Sen. Raphael Warnock — complete with embarrassing gaffes and troubling allegations — serves as a cautionary tale that star power and good intentions aren't enough to secure electoral victory.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

Gov. Brian Kemp's warning is more than just advice; it's a clarion call for the GOP to reassess its strategy, message, and candidates. The reliance on unpopular policies and controversial figures has not only tarnished the party's brand but has also made the path to victory in competitive states like Georgia increasingly precarious. Kemp's push for a political novice like Derek Dooley over established, albeit polarizing, figures like Carter and Collins is a risky bet — one that underscores the GOP's urgent search for a winning formula in an evolving electoral landscape.
