As NBC News reported, “The president posted or reposted more than 100 times on his Truth Social website, in many cases amplifying messages disparaging perceived political foes.”

Brought on MS NOW to discuss the president’s photo-op speaking with children on Christmas Eve, Bulwark Editor Sam Stein laughed off the Trump talk with kids before noting the flurry of confrontational Truth Social posts from a clearly aggrieved president.

“I mean, this is what, year 5 or 6 of this routine?” a smiling Stein stated when asked about the phone calls. “There’s something very, I don’t want to say, charming, but it is, it’s like Trump at his, like, childish best, I suppose, because there’s some innocence to it. Except when he’s telling the kid that Santa might not be real. I feel like that might — he probably wanted to take that one back. That was a classic, but probably a problematic one. “

“But if you want me to psychoanalyze, I’ll do it,” he continued and elaborated, I suppose every single answer is told through the prism of Trump’s own personal and political experiences. “I think anyone who’s posting over 100 times on Christmas probably needs to get out and touch some grass at Mar-a-Lago, and I know he did play golf, so maybe he needs to just go out and play another round. But that’s just a lot of posting for anybody, let alone the president,” he suggested.

Our Analysis:

The Never-Ending Spectacle of Trump’s Social Media Antics

Donald Trump’s relentless barrage on Truth Social during the holiday season is a spectacle that veers between bewildering and deeply concerning. On Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, he transformed what could have been a silent night into a cacophony of digital noise, posting or reposting more than 100 times. This desperate clamor for attention is not just a testament to Trump’s insatiable need to dominate the media landscape but also a glaring example of how he manipulates social media platforms to disparage opponents and rewrite narratives.

Trump’s Distorted Christmas Message

His “Merry Christmas” message was anything but merry. Instead of spreading holiday cheer, Trump chose to disseminate a message laden with vitriol aimed at “Sleazebags,” a thinly veiled jab at his political adversaries linked to Jeffrey Epstein. This maneuver is classic Trump—mixing holiday wishes with conspiracy theories and self-aggrandizement. His claim of being the “only one” who disavowed Epstein “before it became fashionable to do so” is both laughable and misleading, given the well-documented social connections between Trump, Epstein, and various high-profile figures across the political spectrum.

Furthermore, Trump’s tirade against the “Radical Left Witch Hunt” and the “Russia, Russia, Russia Hoax” is a rehashing of his greatest hits of grievances. It’s a tired playbook aimed at rallying his base by invoking perceived injustices and conspiracies against him. The mention of the “Failing New York Times” and other media outlets as “losers” peddling “FAKE!” news is yet another example of Trump’s attempts to undermine confidence in the free press, a cornerstone of a healthy democracy.

A Disturbing Holiday Reflection

The juxtaposition of Trump’s confrontational posts with his participation in a seemingly innocent Christmas Eve photo-op with children is disturbing. As Bulwark Editor Sam Stein points out, there’s a twisted sort of charm to Trump’s interactions, marred by moments like questioning the existence of Santa Claus—a move that could be seen as emblematic of Trump’s penchant for shattering norms and injecting his personal grievances into any situation.

Stein’s suggestion that Trump “needs to get out and touch some grass” is a polite euphemism for the former president’s apparent detachment from reality. The sheer volume of posts—over 100 on Christmas alone—paints a picture of a man obsessed, not just with the platform but with the act of constant, unfiltered communication with his followers, regardless of the day’s significance.

A Wider Perspective

What does this say about the current state of American politics? Trump’s Christmas tirade is a symptom of a broader problem: the weaponization of social media by political figures to bypass traditional media, spread misinformation, and foster division. It’s a potent reminder of the power of these platforms and the responsibility that comes with them—a responsibility Trump consistently abdicates in favor of stoking the fires of discord and disinformation.

As we move forward, it’s crucial for both the media and the public to remain vigilant, to question and critically assess the barrage of information that comes from figures like Trump. It’s not just about one man’s social media habits but about the broader implications for democratic discourse and the health of public debate. The spectacle of Trump’s Truth Social frenzy is a warning sign of the dangers of unchecked, unfiltered communication from those in power—a lesson we would do well to heed.
