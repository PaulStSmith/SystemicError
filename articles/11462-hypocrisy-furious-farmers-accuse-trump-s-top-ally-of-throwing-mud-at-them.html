'Hypocrisy': Furious farmers accuse Trump's top ally of 'throwing mud' at them

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Hollow Promises of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again"

Farmer walking in corn field. (Photo credit: Zoran Zeremski / Shutterstock)

American farmers are growing disillusioned with Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again" agenda, according to a new analysis.

President Donald Trump won the support of small farmers in 2024 with his promises to improve wages and lower inflation, and many were optimistic about Kennedy's messaging around improving diet and nutrition. But he has instead cast doubt on established industrial farming practices as Health and Human Services secretary, reported the New York Times.

"As someone who lives in a rural part of the country among many who share the goals of the MAHA movement, I’m keenly aware of the frustration some in the agricultural community are feeling," wrote journalist Eoin Higgins for the Times. "I talked to many farmers over the past three months, and they all found Mr. Kennedy’s hypocrisy troubling. His unwillingness to stand up to policies within his own administration that undermine American farmers suggests, to them, that he is either too meek to push back or disingenuous about his commitment to his goals."

"Either way, Mr. Kennedy’s response to farmers thus far indicates his MAHA agenda isn’t really about meaningful impact," Higgins added, "it’s a rallying cry aimed at keeping himself in power."

Kennedy has made nutrition a core component of his MAHA agenda, and the Food and Drug Administration and the National Institutes of Health are looking into what harms might be caused by ultraprocessed foods and food additives, but the administration's policies have actually made life harder for farmers.

"Look no further than the cancellation in March of the Local Food Purchase Assistance Cooperative Agreement and Local Food for Schools federal programs," Higgins wrote. "Those cuts saved the government about $1 billion but came at the expense of providing schools, child care centers and food banks with fresh food from local farmers. Access to these foods would presumably be part of the solution for reducing diet-related chronic diseases, which MAHA contends are on the rise in underserved communities. The administration killed the program anyway."

Kennedy has remained silent on those moves, which has frustrated farmers, and farmers are also alarmed by his anti-science rhetoric, particularly the MAHA demonization of pesticide use.

“If we want to make America healthy again, can we please start with America and the people that are already doing that?” said Michelle Miller, a former farmer who writes about modern farming practices and policies under the name Farm Babe. “Can we stop throwing mud at the very people that are breaking their backs to feed us? And can we let them do their jobs and can we listen to them?”

Our Analysis:

The Hollow Promises of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again"

In what reads like a cautionary tale of political disillusionment, American farmers are reportedly growing weary of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s "Make America Healthy Again" (MAHA) agenda. The New York Times' analysis, penned by journalist Eoin Higgins, paints a picture of a policy initiative that, while ambitious in its health and nutrition goals, seemingly falls short in its understanding and support of the very backbone of America's food supply: its farmers.

The Disillusionment of American Farmers

Farmers, who once harbored optimism for Kennedy's vision, now find themselves at odds with policies that not only fail to support them but actively undermine their livelihoods. Higgins’ report is a testament to the growing frustration within the agricultural community, a sentiment that echoes far beyond the fields and into the heart of rural America.

Hypocrisy and Hollow Commitments

Kennedy's apparent reluctance to defend American farmers against detrimental policies within his own administration has not gone unnoticed. This has led to accusations of hypocrisy, with some suggesting Kennedy's agenda is more about consolidating power than making meaningful change. It's a damning indictment of a political figure whose rhetoric once promised to elevate the concerns of small farmers and improve the nation's health through better diet and nutrition.

The Irony of Nutrition-focused Policies

At the core of Kennedy's MAHA agenda is a focus on nutrition, particularly the harms caused by ultraprocessed foods and additives. Yet, the cancellation of federal programs designed to provide schools, child care centers, and food banks with fresh food from local farmers strikes a bewildering contrast to these goals. This move, purportedly saving the government $1 billion, not only deprives underserved communities of healthier food options but directly contradicts the essence of MAHA's mission. Kennedy's silence on these cancellations only adds insult to injury, leaving one to question the genuine priorities of his health initiative.

Anti-Science Rhetoric and the Demonization of Pesticide Use

Further complicating Kennedy's relationship with the agricultural community is his administration's stance on pesticide use. The MAHA movement's demonization of such practices not only alienates farmers but also ventures into dangerous anti-science territory. By ignoring the nuanced realities of modern farming, including the responsible use of pesticides, Kennedy's agenda risks exacerbating the very health issues it aims to combat.

A Call for Genuine Support and Understanding

Michelle Miller's poignant question, "Can we please start with America and the people that are already doing that?” sums up the sentiment of many within the farming community. It's a call for recognition, respect, and, most importantly, a plea for policies that truly support those who feed the nation. It's time for Kennedy's MAHA agenda to move beyond rhetoric and take meaningful steps toward understanding and addressing the needs of American farmers.

In essence, the disillusionment among American farmers with Kennedy's MAHA initiative serves as a stark reminder of the complex relationship between health policy and agriculture. For any health-focused agenda to succeed, it must not only embrace the latest nutritional science but also understand and support the intricacies of modern farming. Anything less is a disservice to those who work tirelessly to sustain and nourish the nation.
