‘Very similar to Russia’: Filmmaker issues chilling warning about authoritarian 'slide'

Documentary filmmaker and producer Alex Gibney issued a chilling warning Saturday over what he described as the United States’ “slow slide” into authoritarianism, drawing a telling comparison between Trump administration policies and those of the Russian government under President Vladimir Putin.

Appearing on MSNBC’s “The Weekend,” Gibney was promoting his new film, “Orwell: 2+2=5,” which follows the life of English novelist George Orwell, and draws comparisons to his warnings of authoritarianism with the American government under President Donald Trump.

Gibney was asked what “feels different” in the United States when compared with other governments that have seen a rise in authoritarianism, to which he said that “the similarities are maybe more important than the differences.”

“I think what you see is a slow slide. In other words, things begin to change,” Gibney said. “People say ‘well, is it that bad, it’ll probably be okay,’ until you realize you’ve begun to accept things that you never would have accepted three, four months earlier.”

To illustrate his point, Gibney recalled a protest he observed while in Russia in 2018, a protest led by the late Alexei Navalny, a Russian lawyer and opposition leader who died while serving a 19-year prison sentence, with human rights activists labeling him as a political prisoner of the Russian government.

“What’s going on in the United States is very similar to what I experienced in Russia when I went there in 2018,” Gibney said.

“I remember photographing a protest that was led by Navalny, but all the protesters were forced to [be in compliance by] security officers, and the security officers had their badges taped over and they were wearing face masks so that they couldn’t be identified. I thought to myself at that time ‘wow, that’s something that could never happen in the United States!’ Yet, here we are.”

Gibney’s anecdote of the Russian protest draws clear comparisons to federal law enforcement agency policies under the Trump administration, who have been frequently spotted operating in unmarked vehicles, wearing masks and sometimes in plain clothes, many of whom have frequently refused to identify themselves while making arrests.

Some states have tried to push back against federal officers performing their duties anonymously, such as inCalifornia, where Gov.Gavin Newsomsigned into lawa ban on federal officers wearing masks and concealing their identities, a law the Trump administration hasoutright rejectedand instructed federal officers to ignore.

Our Analysis:

Gibney’s Warning: A Dive into the “Slow Slide” into Authoritarianism

The chilling warning issued by Alex Gibney, a renowned documentary filmmaker, about the United States’ descent into authoritarianism under the guise of the Trump administration, demands an uncompromising analysis. His observations, articulated during an MSNBC interview while promoting his film, “Orwell: 2+2=5,” draw a harrowing parallel between the U.S. and Russian government tactics under President Vladimir Putin. This comparison not only serves as a stark warning but also as a call to action against the creeping normalization of authoritarian practices.

The Gradual Descent into Acceptance

Gibney’s assertion that the U.S. is experiencing a “slow slide” into authoritarianism is a critical observation. The notion that the populace begins to accept previously unthinkable governmental practices over time is not just alarming but a textbook strategy of authoritarian regimes. This gradual desensitization to the erosion of liberties and democratic norms is precisely what George Orwell warned against. The filmmaker’s comparison to Orwell’s narratives is not just apt but a dire reminder of the consequences of complacency and the failure to recognize and resist such shifts.

The Unsettling Parallels to Russia

Gibney’s firsthand account of the protest in Russia, led by Alexei Navalny, serves as a poignant illustration of his argument. The tactics employed by Russian security forces—concealing their identities and employing measures to quash dissent—mirror those increasingly observed in the United States under Trump’s tenure. The use of unmarked vehicles, masked and sometimes plainclothes officers refusing to identify themselves, is a tactic chillingly reminiscent of authoritarian regimes. It’s a stark violation of the principles of transparency and accountability that are cornerstones of democratic governance.

The Resistance Against Authoritarian Tactics

The response by states like California to such authoritarian tactics—specifically, the ban on federal officers concealing their identities—highlights a growing resistance against the encroachment of authoritarian practices. However, the Trump administration’s outright rejection of such measures is a testament to its disregard for democratic norms and state rights. This clash between state and federal policies underscores a broader struggle for the soul of American democracy.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

What Gibney’s observations and the parallels he draws underscore is the fragility of democracy and the constant vigilance required to protect it from authoritarian tendencies. The comparison between the Trump administration’s policies and those of Putin’s Russia is not just an academic exercise but a wake-up call. It’s a reminder that the price of freedom is eternal vigilance and that the “slow slide” into authoritarianism can only be halted by an informed, engaged, and resistant citizenry.

The normalization of tactics that undermine democratic principles and human rights under the guise of law and order is a slippery slope towards authoritarianism. Gibney’s warning is clear: what once seemed unimaginable in the United States is now a reality that must be confronted with urgency. The battle for democracy is ongoing, and complacency is its greatest enemy.
