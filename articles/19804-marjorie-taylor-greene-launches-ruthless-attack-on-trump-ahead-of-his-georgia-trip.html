Marjorie Taylor Greene launches ruthless attack on Trump ahead of his Georgia trip

Former Republican U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene is sharply criticizing President Donald Trump ahead of his Thursday trip to her former district, where he made — and then apparently forgot — an endorsement in the race to fill her old House seat.

“Well, we have a lot of people that want to take Marjorie ‘Traitor’ Greene’s place,” Trump said on Monday, as The Daily Beast reported. “Many, many candidates, and I have to choose one.”

Greene ignored Trump’s gaffe, but hit him, his administration, and her former Republican colleagues head-on in a post on X where she accused them all of trying to gaslight the American people.

“If you had put America FIRST from the start, instead of your rich donor class and foreign policy, you wouldn’t have to strategize on how to gaslight Americans,” wrote Greene, a former top Trump ally.

“If you had not called the Epstein files a hoax and treated the Epstein survivors (rape and trafficking victims) like they didn’t exist and if you would release all the files and put your rich powerful friends in prison then Americans might actually listen to your ‘messaging,’” she charged.

Mocking them all as on the “struggle bus,” Greene explained the situation her former constituents now face.

“Approximately 75,000 households in my former district had their health insurance double or more on January 1st of this year because the ACA tax credits expired and Republicans have absolutely failed to fix our health insurance system that was destroyed by Obamacare,” she said.

Republicans have blocked Democrats’ efforts — including a federal government shutdown over the expiring Affordable Care Act subsidies — to prevent the health care premiums crisis.

Greene said that hundreds of thousands of people in her former district saw their health insurance premiums double last month.

“Many dropped their policies and no longer have health insurance,” she wrote. “And that’s on top of EVERYONE ELSE who complains DAILY about the absurdly high cost of health insurance!!!”

She said the billionaires running the White House, the Trump administration, and Congress aren’t affected by the high health insurance premiums, noting that all of them have “very nice affordable government health insurance plans.”

“I’m talking about younger healthier people and families not on meds who can’t afford to pay $1500 to more than $2000 per month just for their monthly health insurance premiums,” she wrote, “not including $7-10,000 for a deductible before their ridiculously expensive health insurance policy kicks in.”

Greene also took a shot at House Speaker Mike Johnson, who, she said, “claimed he had the Republican plan during the 8 week shutdown in the fall, then carried on and has done nothing proving he lied once again.”

She also blasted Trump’s “messaging” efforts.

“Trump RX doesn’t fix this so that’s not your messaging answer,” she wrote. “A Truth Social Post or Trump video isn’t fixing this either.”

“Messaging won’t fix this,” she added.

Greene then moved on to foreign policy, warning Trump not to go to war with Iran. She also urged him to release the Epstein files, and told him to “stop the bullying, harassment, and name calling.”

“It’s immature, childish, and turning so many people away. Real leaders don’t act this way and it’s a horrible example set on the world’s stage. This isn’t the behavior we want to teach our children.”

“Deliver real results for the regular American people because respect is earned not given,” she said.

Our Analysis:

The Greene-Trump Saga: A Sharp Turn in Political Allegiances

In an unexpected twist that feels ripped straight from the pages of a political thriller, former Republican U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene has launched a scathing critique against none other than President Donald Trump, the very figure she once championed as an unwavering ally. This dramatic pivot underscores the volatile nature of political loyalties and the sharp divides within the Republican Party itself.

Trump’s Forgettable Endorsement

Trump’s recent faux pas — referring to Greene as Marjorie “Traitor” Greene and admitting his uncertainty in choosing a successor for her former House seat — serves as the spark igniting Greene’s fiery response. It’s almost comedic, if not for the grave implications it has on political endorsements and the seeming frivolity with which they are now treated by some within the party’s echelons.

Gaslighting Accusations

Greene’s accusations against Trump, his administration, and her former Republican colleagues are brutally frank. She charges them with attempting to gaslight the American populace, prioritizing the interests of the “rich donor class” over the country’s well-being. Greene’s critique extends to the handling of the Epstein scandal, suggesting a blatant disregard for victims in favor of protecting powerful allies. This allegation, if true, casts a long, dark shadow over the ethical standing of those implicated.

The Healthcare Debacle

Perhaps the most scathing part of Greene’s critique revolves around the healthcare crisis facing her former constituents — a crisis that has seen insurance premiums skyrocket, forcing many to forgo coverage. Greene lays this at the feet of the Republicans, accusing them of failing to rectify a system “destroyed by Obamacare.” The irony is palpable; the party that has long criticized the Affordable Care Act has, according to Greene, done little to propose a viable alternative that doesn’t leave thousands in financial peril.

An Attack on Inaction

Greene doesn’t stop there. She calls out House Speaker Mike Johnson for his alleged inaction during the government shutdown, challenging the sincerity of his claims to have had a Republican plan to address the healthcare fiasco. This accusation of political theater without substance is a damning indictment of the current state of political leadership, suggesting a gap between promises made and actions taken.

Beyond Messaging

In what might be her most valid point, Greene asserts that the issues plaguing Americans — from healthcare to foreign policy — cannot be resolved through mere “messaging.” This is a direct jab at Trump’s preferred communication strategies, highlighting a fundamental disconnect between political rhetoric and tangible outcomes.

The Call for Mature Leadership

Greene’s plea for a cessation of “bullying, harassment, and name calling” is a refreshing call for maturity in political discourse. It reflects a broader desire among the public for leaders who can rise above petty squabbles to address the pressing needs of their constituents.

Conclusion: A Mirror to the GOP’s Soul?

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s public rebuke of Donald Trump is not just a personal vendetta; it is a mirror reflecting the deep fissures within the Republican Party. It calls into question the party’s direction, its values, and its commitment to the American people. Whether her criticisms will inspire introspection or further division remains to be seen, but one thing is clear: the drama unfolding within the GOP is a symptom of a party at a crossroads, struggling to define its identity in the post-Trump era.
