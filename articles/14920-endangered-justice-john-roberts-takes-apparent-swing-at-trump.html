'Endangered': Justice John Roberts takes apparent swing at Trump

Chief Justice John Roberts took an apparent swing at President Donald Trump Monday as he warned that America's justice system was "endangered" — and took particular aim at attacks on judges.

Though Trump wasn't named by the justice, he has openly targeted judges in the multiple criminal and civil cases he has faced, as well as judges ruling on parts of his agenda. Some of this attacks have contributed to death threats.

Roberts, an extremely conservative justice, was speaking to Georgetown Law students Monday, according to Politico.

He made clear the public perception of the judiciary is not always as “toxic” as it may seem.

“We have to work together on these things that are difficult. You find a way to get along,” Roberts said. “It’s a long job. If you’re sort of really at each other’s throats with bad feelings and stuff like that, it just — it’s not a good way to function. … We do have to address some very, very difficult things, and we’re the only people who are doing it.”

He added, the fact that the justices “disagree a lot” does not mean there is personal animosity within the Supreme Court.

Politico reported, “Roberts described the rule of law as ‘endangered’” and he admonished “trashing the justices.”

“The notion that [the] rule of law governs is the basic proposition,” Roberts said. “Certainly as a matter of theory, but also as a matter of practice, we need to stop and reflect every now and then how rare that is, certainly rare throughout history, and rare in the world today.”

Roberts did not “point fingers” directly at Trump for publicly denouncing judges.

Roberts did however, point the finger at schools for “shortchanging civics education.”

“We’re developing a situation where a whole group of young people is growing up having no real sense about how our system of justice works,” the justice said.

According to Politico, during the event, “Roberts signaled that he has no plans to step down from the court,” for the second time in a week.

Our Analysis:

The Thinly Veiled Rebuke: Chief Justice Roberts vs. The Trumpian Assault on Judiciary

In what can only be described as a tacit condemnation of former President Donald Trump, Chief Justice John Roberts recently delivered remarks that, while stopping short of naming names, left little to the imagination regarding his target. Speaking to Georgetown Law students, Roberts lamented the endangered state of America's justice system, a system beleaguered by relentless attacks that have not only undermined public trust but have also escalated to the point of inciting death threats against judges.

Roberts' critique, though veiled, is a scathing indictment of Trump's notorious habit of lambasting judges and the judiciary at large—actions that have become as predictable as they are dangerous. Trump, with his unparalleled platform, has frequently taken to disparaging judges overseeing his plethora of legal troubles or those daring to rule against his agenda. This behavior, as Roberts subtly implies, is not only unbecoming of a leader but perilous to the very fabric of the rule of law.

The Chief Justice, described by Politico as extremely conservative, endeavors to cast the judiciary not as a battleground of personal animosities but as a solemn forum where disagreements, no matter how profound, are navigated with respect and adherence to legal principles. His assertion that the justices manage to work together despite frequent disagreements is a testament to the professionalism that he suggests is lacking in Trump's approach to the judiciary.

Roberts' speech goes beyond mere rebuke; it serves as a clarion call for a return to civility and respect for the rule of law. His warning that the rule of law is endangered is a sobering reminder of the stakes involved. It's not just about the reputation of individual judges or the outcomes of specific cases; it's about the integrity and viability of judicial independence.

Moreover, Roberts' criticism extends to the educational system, which he accuses of shortchanging civics education. This is a crucial point, as it underscores the long-term dangers of a populace uninformed about the workings of their government and judicial system. In a way, Roberts is implicating not just Trump but a broader failure to cultivate an informed citizenry capable of discerning the significance of an independent judiciary.

Yet, there's an elephant in the room that Roberts' remarks fail to address directly: the role of the Supreme Court itself. While he decries the toxic public perception of the judiciary, one cannot ignore the growing skepticism towards the Supreme Court, fueled by decisions and behaviors that many view as increasingly politicized. Roberts' call for reflection and civility, though noble, skirts the hard questions about the Court's own contributions to the very toxicity he bemoans.

In his assertion of steadfastness, signaling no plans to step down, Roberts seems to be bracing for continued turbulence. However, one must wonder whether his appeals for unity and respect for the rule of law will resonate in the current climate, or whether they will be dismissed as mere platitudes by those who see the judiciary, rightly or wrongly, as just another arena of partisan warfare.

In the final analysis, Roberts' speech, while eloquent and necessary, may be too little, too late. It's a band-aid on a gushing wound, a polite request for decorum in a political landscape that has long since abandoned such niceties. The Chief Justice's words, though commendable, feel like a whisper in a hurricane, unlikely to be heard over the din of an ongoing assault on the institutions designed to safeguard American democracy.
