'Is that a joke?' Kristi Noem hammered by Dem lawmaker for 'out of touch' crime claim

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem speaks to employees at the Department of Homeland Security, Tuesday, Jan. 28, 2025, in Washington, U.S. Manuel Balce Ceneta/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo

Reacting to comments made by Homeland Security Secretary Kirsti Noem during an appearance on CBS Sunday, the ranking Democratic member of the House Oversight Committee called her out for lying and then proceeded to rake her over the coals.

Appearing on MSNBC with fill-in host Erielle Resheff, Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) was shown a clip of Noem stating Donald Trump saved Los Angeles from being destroyed by a crime wave.

Specifically, she claimed that without the president’s deployment of the National Guard to LA, “That city would have burned down if left to the devices of the mayor and the governor of that state.”

“Your reaction? I see you smirking there,” the MSNBC host prompted her guest.

”I mean, it's hard not to. I mean, is that a joke?” he responded. “I mean, it's just completely outrageous and out of touch. I mean, obviously Los Angeles, besides having seen decreases over the last 10, 20, 30 years overall in crime, the fact that LA wouldn't have been standing had Donald Trump not intervened is just is a fabrication.”

“All they do is they look at the TV and they lie,” he accused. “And I think people know across here and across the country that the National Guard and the military shouldn't be sent into cities. What cities want is support. They want additional support to hire more police officers, for more park space, for libraries.”

"And certainly at this moment, right now, with these ICE raids that are happening across cities, across the country, everyone agrees that, yes, we don't want, of course, criminals,” he elaborated. “If you're here and you're a criminal, you shouldn't be in this country –– we understand that. But that's not what's happening. Most of the people being right now detained or abducted don't have criminal histories, they are people that are working at our car washes, at our restaurants, at our hotels. These are hardworking people.”

Our Analysis:

The Dance of Distortion: Analyzing the Clash Over LA's Crime Narrative

In a recent spectacle that could only be spawned from the depths of our current political theater, Homeland Security Secretary Kirsti Noem's comments on CBS Sunday have ignited a firestorm of controversy, leading to a fierce rebuttal from Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) on MSNBC. The fabric of reality seems to warp as political figures spin tales that range from the exaggerated to the patently false. Let's dissect the claims, the counterclaims, and what lies between the lines.

The Claim That Trump "Saved" Los Angeles

Noem's assertion that Donald Trump's deployment of the National Guard prevented Los Angeles from burning to the ground is nothing short of theatrical. It paints a picture of a city on the brink of destruction, saved only by the heroic intervention of the federal government. This narrative not only overstates the impact of the National Guard's presence but also grossly misrepresents the state of crime in Los Angeles. Contrary to the apocalyptic vision conjured by Noem, Los Angeles has seen a general decrease in crime rates over the past few decades. The idea that LA would have ceased to exist without Trump's intervention is, as Garcia rightly points out, a "fabrication."

The Reality of Los Angeles' Crime Rate

Rep. Garcia's response highlights a crucial point: the overall trend in Los Angeles (and indeed, in many parts of the United States) is one of decreasing crime rates. This reality starkly contrasts with the dystopian landscape suggested by Noem. Garcia's smirk is not just a reaction to a single exaggerated claim but to the broader pattern of fear-mongering that often characterizes political discourse on crime and safety.

The Misuse of the National Guard and Military in Cities

Garcia also touches on a contentious topic: the deployment of the National Guard and the military in American cities. This practice, fraught with concerns about civil liberties and the militarization of law enforcement, is a far cry from the support communities are actually requesting. Cities are seeking additional resources to enhance public services, not soldiers patrolling their streets. The call for more police officers, park spaces, and libraries reflects a desire for a holistic approach to community well-being, rather than a militarized response to complex social issues.

The ICE Raids: A Disturbing Trend

The discussion of ICE raids introduces another layer of complexity. Garcia's comments shed light on the reality that many of those detained or "abducted" are not the "criminals" the administration claims to target. Instead, they are often hardworking individuals contributing to their communities. This discrepancy between the official justification for the raids and the actual impact on communities reveals a troubling disregard for the lives and rights of immigrants.

Conclusion: Between Lies and Reality

In the end, the clash between Noem and Garcia is more than just a political disagreement; it's a reflection of a broader struggle over the narrative of crime, safety, and the role of government in addressing these issues. Noem's comments, and Garcia's rebuttal, expose the chasm between political rhetoric and the lived realities of communities. As observers, we must navigate these turbulent waters with a critical eye, recognizing the difference between fear-mongering and genuine concern for public safety. The dance of distortion continues, but it is our responsibility to discern the truth amidst the political performance.
