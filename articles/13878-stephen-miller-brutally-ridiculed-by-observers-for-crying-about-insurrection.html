After it was reported on social media that, "Protesters have surrounded the federal detention center in Los Angeles. California isn’t backing down, this is a full-blown standoff with the feds," Miller sought to define what is happening.

"An insurrection against the laws and sovereignty of the United States," Miller wrote.

Podcaster Brian Allen responded, saying, "Stephen Miller is now calling the anti-ICE uprising in Los Angeles 'an insurrection against U.S. sovereignty.' Let’s be clear: over 800 protesters cornered federal agents in a garage, breached a government building, and caused widespread damage."

"This isn’t just chaos, it’s a full-blown standoff. Miller is demanding National Guard deployment and federal arrests. The stakes just got raised," Allen added. "Stephen Miller; the guy who architected family separation, is now crying 'insurrection' because Californians dared to protest ICE raids. You don’t get to weaponize the government, then act shocked when the people fight back. California isn’t backing down."

Internet personality Leah McElrath also chimed in:

"To give people an idea of where the Trump administration is wanting to take things, White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller is calling the anti-ICE protests in large cities in blue states 'an insurrection against the laws and sovereignty of the United States.'"

Self-described "political junkie" Richard Angwin told his hundreds of thousands of followers:

"Calling peaceful protest an 'insurrection' disrespects our democracy. Protesting unjust policies is a constitutional right, not sedition. Miller's rhetoric endangers civil liberties and distorts the meaning of real threats."

U.S. Customs and Border Patrol echoed Miller's language, resulting in similar feedback.

"Let this be clear: Anyone who assaults or impedes a federal law enforcement officer or agent in the performance of their duties will be arrested and swiftly prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law. Attack a cop, and life long consequences will follow!" CBP wrote.

To that, speech writer Mark Salter replied, "Unless you’re breaking into the Capitol and trying to hang the VP."

Our Analysis:

The Rhetorical Insurrection: Stephen Miller and the California Protests

In a move that is as predictable as it is disheartening, Stephen Miller, the U.S. White House Deputy Chief of Staff, has labeled the California protests against ICE as an "insurrection against the laws and sovereignty of the United States." This proclamation is not just an overreaction; it's a dangerous manipulation of language intended to delegitimize valid public dissent. Let's dissect the layers of hypocrisy and fear-mongering embedded in Miller's statement and the broader implications it carries for democracy and civil liberties.

The Misuse of "Insurrection"

First and foremost, the use of the term insurrection by Stephen Miller to describe protests in California is a gross misrepresentation. Insurrection implies a violent uprising against an authority or government, but reporting and social media posts suggest a scenario far from a coup or armed rebellion. Instead, what we see are citizens engaging in protest — a fundamental right enshrined in the U.S. Constitution. By calling this an insurrection, Miller is not just stretching the truth; he's snapping it in half.

Brian Allen's response sheds light on the severity of the situation, indicating that while the protests have indeed become intense, labeling them as an insurrection is a leap designed to justify extreme countermeasures. Demanding National Guard deployment and federal arrests over protests is a tactic aimed at intimidation and suppression, not maintaining public safety.

The Irony of Stephen Miller's Outrage

The irony of Stephen Miller, infamous for his role in architecting the family separation policy at the border, decrying protests against ICE as an attack on U.S. sovereignty is rich to the point of absurdity. Miller's career is a testament to weaponizing government power against vulnerable populations. His shock at public resistance is either disingenuous or delusional. To paraphrase the critique, you don't get to abuse power and then play the victim when the populace objects.

The Dangerous Path of Rhetorical Escalation

What's particularly alarming about Miller's and the CBP's rhetoric is the pathway it paves for escalating confrontations. By framing the situation as an insurrection, they're not just mislabeling protests; they're setting the stage for potential violence and justifying any punitive measures taken. It's a self-fulfilling prophecy that could lead to unnecessary harm to protesters and a further erosion of public trust in law enforcement and government.

Moreover, the response from U.S. Customs and Border Patrol echoes Miller's dangerous sentiment, threatening "life long consequences" for those who resist. This not only exacerbates tensions but also blatantly disregards the right to protest and express dissent. Mark Salter's quip about the disparity in treatment between these protesters and those involved in the January 6 Capitol riot underscores a glaring double standard in how threats are perceived and addressed based on the political leanings of the protesters.

Conclusion: The Real Threat to Democracy

In labeling the California protests an "insurrection," Stephen Miller and the Trump administration's allies are not just misusing language; they're attacking the very foundations of democracy. Peaceful protest and civil disobedience are cornerstones of a healthy democracy, not sedition. The real insurrection isn’t the one happening on the streets of California; it's the one being waged against the American people's right to dissent.

Richard Angwin's point that calling peaceful protest an insurrection "disrespects our democracy" cannot be overstated. The rhetoric used by Miller and echoed by the CBP doesn't just endanger civil liberties; it distorts the meaning of real threats to the nation. In their quest to quash dissent, they risk undermining the democratic principles they claim to defend.

In the end, the situation in California is a stark reminder of the power of language in framing political discourse and the importance of vigilantly defending the right to protest. The real insurrection is the assault on these fundamental freedoms, not the voices of dissent rising in opposition to injustice.
