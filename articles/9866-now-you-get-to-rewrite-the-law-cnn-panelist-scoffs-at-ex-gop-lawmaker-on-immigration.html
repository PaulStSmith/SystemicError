'Now you get to rewrite the law?': CNN panelist scoffs at ex-GOP lawmaker on immigration

CNN screenshot

The panel on CNN’s “NewsNight with Abby Phillip” erupted on Thursday after a former GOP lawmaker claimed that people who immigrated to the U.S. with temporary protected status are “basically illegals.”

Betsy McCaughey, the former REpublican Lt. Gov. of New York, joined NewsNight to defend claims made by Republicans that Democrats want to provide free health care to illegal immigrants in the next funding bill. Experts have said that Democrats are really trying to reinstate cuts to Medicaid for people with temporary protected status like refugees and asylum seekers.

“Remember, under Joe Biden, several million people were brought in under temporary protected status, and they were equivalent to illegal in the sense that they didn’t wait the five years like other newcomers have to wait to be eligible for Medicaid,” McCaughey said.

Her comments were met with fierce pushback from other panelists.

“Or equivalent,” journalist Terry Moran scoffed. “So, now you get to rewrite the law because you don’t like it?”

Republicans have consistently claimed, without evidence, that Democrats are trying to provide free health care benefits to immigrants to justify the government shutdown. However, some have speculated that the shutdown serves a different purpose.

Fox News host Jesse Watters has suggested Republicans could use it to take away Temporary Protected Status from millions of immigrants. Others have said Republicans could use it to reduce the federal workforce.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Misinformation Machine in Full Swing

In a recent eruption on CNN’s “NewsNight with Abby Phillip,” the discourse veered into dangerous territory, thanks to former GOP lawmaker Betsy McCaughey’s comments. Her assertion that people with temporary protected status (TPS) are “basically illegals” not only showcases a profound misunderstanding of immigration law but also fuels the misinformation fire that has become all too common in political discourse today.

The Misinterpretation of Temporary Protected Status

McCaughey’s argument hinges on a gross mischaracterization of what TPS actually is. To be clear, individuals granted TPS are legally allowed to live and work in the United States because they are unable to return safely to their home countries due to ongoing armed conflict, environmental disasters, or other extraordinary and temporary conditions. Labeling them as “equivalent to illegal” is not just incorrect—it’s irresponsible.

The Health Care Straw Man

The heart of McCaughey’s appearance was to defend Republican claims that Democrats are intent on providing free health care to illegal immigrants through the next funding bill. However, this is a classic example of creating a straw man to knock down. As experts have clarified, Democrats are actually focused on reinstating Medicaid cuts for people with TPS, such as refugees and asylum seekers. This isn’t about handing out free health care to illegal immigrants; it’s about ensuring that those legally in the country under dire circumstances have access to basic health services.

The False Equivalency and Its Implications

McCaughey’s assertion that TPS recipients didn’t “wait the five years like other newcomers have to wait to be eligible for Medicaid” draws a false equivalency between various immigration statuses and the rights and benefits associated with them. TPS is granted based on immediate needs arising from crises, not a place in a queue that can be bypassed. This kind of rhetoric not only muddies the waters of public understanding but also vilifies vulnerable populations for political gain.

The Broader GOP Strategy?

The article hints at a broader GOP strategy, with references to Republicans possibly using the government shutdown as leverage to strip away TPS or reduce the federal workforce. While McCaughey’s comments might seem isolated, they feed into a larger narrative where immigration is weaponized for political ends. This isn’t just about policy disagreements; it’s a concerted effort to reshape the narrative around immigration and welfare in ways that could have real and detrimental impacts on millions of lives.

In Conclusion: The Need for Fact-Based Discourse

The exchange on “NewsNight with Abby Phillip” is a microcosm of the larger issues plaguing American political discourse. When former lawmakers like McCaughey spout misinformation on national television, they contribute to a landscape where facts are malleable and truth is what you make of it. It’s not just about one erroneous claim; it’s about how these claims proliferate and affect policy and public opinion.

In a world where misinformation can spread faster than ever, it’s crucial that public figures and media outlets prioritize accuracy and provide context. As viewers and readers, we must demand more than just sensational sound bites—we need a commitment to truth that transcends political affiliations and speaks to the core values of democracy.
