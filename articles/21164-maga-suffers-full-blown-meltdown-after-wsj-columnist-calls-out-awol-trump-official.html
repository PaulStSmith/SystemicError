MAGA suffers full-blown meltdown after WSJ columnist calls out 'AWOL' Trump official

The Vice President as a Promotional Object

A Small Fight That Exposes a Bigger Collapse

Kimberley Strassel’s column on JD Vance is not important because one conservative columnist got under the skin of Trump-world. It matters because it shows how little the MAGA right expects from power now. The source dispute is simple: Strassel mocked Vance for promoting a book during a GOP crisis; Vance’s defenders, including White House and MAGA figures, rushed to frame the criticism as unfair, especially since his wife had just given birth. The fight quickly became less about governance than about excuse-making.

Power Is Not Where the Apologists Pretend

The institutional power here sits with the Trump White House and the vice president’s office, not with Strassel, not with the podcasters, and not with the grievance merchants on X. Vance is not a neutral observer of the chaos. He is part of the governing apparatus. If congressional wrangling needs an “ambassador,” as Strassel put it, the problem is not that the vice president is misunderstood. The problem is that the office is being treated as a platform for image management instead of a vehicle for governing.

That is the real indictment buried inside the squabble: the MAGA movement has built a state that is always campaigning, always performing, and rarely doing the work of power.

The Misfire on Responsibility

Vance’s defenders immediately tried to convert criticism of his absence into a culture-war complaint about parental leave, husbandhood, and media hypocrisy. That is a familiar dodge. It shifts attention from a political actor’s choices to the emotional optics around his personal life. But Strassel’s point, whether one likes it or not, was about timing and obligation. Her argument was not that a father should be punished for having a child. It was that a vice president claiming to help marshal the GOP should not be off promoting a book while the party and White House are in disarray.

The attempt to blur those two things is deliberate. It replaces accountability with sentiment. It asks the public to treat a strategic failure as a family values story.

The Go-To Excuse Machine

The White House spokesperson insisted the criticism was “factually inaccurate,” pointing to Vance’s visits to the Capitol. That response tells you everything. Instead of defending an actual governing role, the office falls back on attendance counts and procedural trivia. Showing up “over two dozen times” is not a governing philosophy. It is a defensive talking point.

Then Strassel sharpened the point herself: Vance chose to go on Joe Rogan while his wife was still expecting, and that choice matters differently from a hospital admission. That distinction is basic. One is necessity. The other is preference. MAGA-world keeps collapsing those categories because it wants all consequences to disappear inside a cloud of grievance.

The Real Target

The loudest defenders of Vance were not really defending him. They were defending the right to avoid standards. Katie Miller, Sean Davis, and other MAGA voices attacked Strassel because criticism from inside the right is more dangerous than criticism from the left. It punctures the fantasy that this movement is disciplined, heroic, and always on message. It shows a governing coalition that cannot even agree on whether its second-in-command is supposed to be working or posturing.

This is why the fight is revealing. It is not about one book promotion. It is about a political culture that treats visible responsibility as optional and then lashes out when anyone notices.

What This Story Actually Shows

The pattern is simple: power is concentrated, accountability is dissolved, and criticism is redirected into identity theater. The vice president is supposed to be a governing instrument. Instead, he is being marketed, defended, and rebranded in real time while allies scramble to explain why the standards should not apply to him.

That is not just hypocrisy. It is institutional decay with a public relations budget. The MAGA right cannot govern cleanly because it cannot even describe power honestly. It prefers a system where loyalty counts more than performance, where excuses replace duty, and where every failure is laundered through performance politics.
