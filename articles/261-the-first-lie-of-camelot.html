The First Lie of Camelot

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 23, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

This story grew out of an idea that would not leave me alone.

It began as one of those insomnia thoughts that keeps circling long after it should have gone quiet: what would I do if I could travel back in time with enough money, enough preparation, and enough stubbornness to try to change the path of the United States after Nixon?

Not to rule it. Not to become king of some private empire. That was never the fantasy.

The question was different, and more dangerous. Could one person, arriving early enough and armed with impossible resources, build institutions strong enough to resist the derailment before it became permanent? Could the press be fortified before it was captured? Could civil rights be defended with banks, clinics, schools, lawyers, and newspapers instead of speeches alone? Could the rise of Reagan, the hardening of cruelty into policy, the worship of money, the laundering of racism into respectable politics, and all the machinery that followed be interrupted before it became the shape of ordinary life?

The more I thought about it, the less it felt like a story about time travel.

It became a story about institutions. About money and legitimacy. About newspapers, courts, banks, hearings, campaigns, hospitals, schools, factories, rockets, and all the slow machinery by which a country decides what it is willing to become. It became a story about the temptation to do good with unaccountable power, and the need to build guardrails before noble purpose becomes just another excuse.

The United States, in this imagined 1970, still carries the memory of Camelot: wounded, compromised, unfinished, but not yet surrendered. Nixon has not yet finished teaching the country how much damage a frightened man can do from the highest office. Reagan has not yet become the face of a movement that would turn public cruelty into common sense. The future is not saved. It is only still reachable.

This story is the result of asking what might happen if someone tried to reach it.

Not cleanly. Not innocently. Not without lies, costs, failures, shadows, and people strong enough to tell the would-be savior no.

But tried.
