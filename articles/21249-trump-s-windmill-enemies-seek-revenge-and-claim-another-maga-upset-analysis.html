Trump's windmill enemies seek revenge – and claim another MAGA upset: Analysis

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What HappenedWho Actually Had PowerThe Misdirection Is the StoryTrump’s Hand Is VisibleThis Is Political Warfare Because Policy Has Been MilitarizedThe Real Pattern

Clean Energy Is Not Hiding. Republican Cowardice Is.

What Happened

A solar-backed PAC spent $2 million to help defeat Rep. Andy Ogles in a Tennessee Republican primary, even after Donald Trump personally intervened for him. The same group has now helped push out or punish multiple Republicans who wanted a faster phaseout of renewable energy incentives, while also rewarding at least one Republican who cooperates with the clean-energy camp. The reporting matters because the ads did not sell clean energy openly; they attacked votes, flirted with conservative branding, and tried to win without triggering Trump’s anti-renewable baggage.

Who Actually Had Power

The power in this story was not the glossy language of “energy policy.” It was institutional leverage, money, and the ability to decide whether a candidate survives a primary. Trump had symbolic and practical power inside the GOP, and he used it. Republican members like Ogles, Chip Roy, and Ralph Norman had enough agency to push for a rapid rollback of renewable incentives, and that is the point: they were not trapped by events. They were actively trying to bend federal policy toward fossil-fuel politics and against clean energy.

The PAC’s power was different but real. It could spend more than the candidates combined and shape the outcome in a primary where the formal parties often pretend to be powerless spectators. That is not democracy at work in some idealized sense. It is a fight over who gets to command the party label and the policy pipeline.

The Misdirection Is the Story

The ads never mentioned clean energy directly. That is not a stylistic quirk; it is an admission of the political environment Republicans have built. Trump has spent years turning renewable energy into a culture-war target, so even solar executives feel compelled to hide the substance of their own cause behind generic attacks on missed votes and made-up conservative respectability.

That is what misdirection looks like in a degraded political system. The issue is not confusion. The issue is strategic cowardice on one side and deliberate poisoning on the other. Republicans pushed anti-renewable politics, Trump amplified it, and the PAC responded by laundering the energy fight through coded messaging because the Republican base has been trained to treat clean energy as tribal betrayal.

Trump’s Hand Is Visible

Trump’s intervention for Ogles was not incidental. He called Ogles “a friend” and “a terrific guy” in a tele-rally, which is another way of saying the presidency had been converted into a personal protection racket for loyalists. He stayed out of the Roy and Norman races, which suggests the PAC’s strategy had already carved out where Trump would and would not spend political capital.

That is the deeper pattern: Trump does not merely influence Republican politics. He disciplines it. Republicans who push the wrong way on renewable energy are not debating policy in good faith; they are negotiating their survival under a leader who treats ideology as a loyalty test and policy as a permission slip for grievance.

This Is Political Warfare Because Policy Has Been Militarized

Chris Larsen called the effort “political warfare,” and Michael Brune was even blunter about taking out members who voted the wrong way. The language is ugly, but the underlying fact is uglier: American policy has been reduced to factional combat because one party has made governance inseparable from sabotage. When a coalition wants to preserve renewable incentives, it does not operate through normal persuasion. It funds retaliation.

That should not be read as a triumphal story about smart messaging. It is evidence that the political system has become so distorted that even a growth industry like clean energy has to mimic the hard-edged tactics of the crypto super PAC world just to defend its existence.

The Real Pattern

The recurring fact here is not one lost primary. It is that Republicans keep trying to punish the future and call it conservatism. Then money enters to punish them back, not because the system is healthy, but because the formal institutions have failed to defend even basic material investment in the country’s energy transition.

One Republican, Mariannette Miller-Meeks, got $125,000 in support for leading the Conservative Climate Caucus. That detail matters because it reveals the transaction underneath the rhetoric: cooperate with decarbonization and you may survive; obstruct it and you may be targeted. The clean-energy camp is learning the same lesson the fossil-fuel camp learned long ago. In a corrupted system, policy is not argued into existence. It is forced through the machinery of money, primaries, and disciplined party punishment.

The larger meaning is simple: renewable energy is no longer just an environmental issue. It is a test of whether American politics can still reward reality over tribal obedience. Right now, too many Republicans are choosing obedience, and too many institutions are built to let them.
