'Sulfurous odors' wafting from White House explained in new report

Fermented Power

A Cabinet in the Wellness Bazaar

The source story is about a cluster of Trump officials and allies, including Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Howard Lutnick, Sean Duffy, JD Vance, and even Kevin McCarthy, embracing a fermented-food regimen sold by physician Sean O’Mara. That is the surface. The political fact underneath is simpler: some of the most powerful people in the country are treating a private diet pitch, and an $18,000 consultation model, as worthy of attention inside the upper ranks of government.

Who Has Power

The people holding actual institutional power here are not the physician, not the spouses carrying the bags, and not the reporters laughing at the smell. It is the vice president, cabinet secretaries, and other political operators who have already made themselves consumers of this wellness culture. They are the decision-makers. They chose the regimen. They normalized it around themselves. The rest is atmosphere.

Misdirection by Distraction

The reporting invites readers to laugh at the odor, the travel inconvenience, and the domestic friction. That framing is convenient because it turns a story about elite self-absorption into a harmless cabinet joke. But the joke points to something more revealing: these officials are not just governing; they are curating bodies, appearances, and lifestyles through a private market of expensive advice and pseudo-scientific certainty.

Private Certainty, Public Office

O’Mara’s pitch is not political in the formal sense, but it attaches itself to political power through access and prestige. A consultant who can charge up to $18,000 for the promise of better energy and appearance is not operating on the margins. He is feeding a class of officials who already have the power to confuse personal preference with institutional significance. When public authority starts to absorb private wellness rituals, the line between governance and self-branding gets thinner than the smell of the sauerkraut.

The Real Pattern

This is a familiar pattern: elite insecurity laundering itself through discipline, health, and optimization. The people who run the state are not immune to the basic vanity economy. They just have more money, more status, and more access to turn it into a lifestyle. The deeper story is not that they eat fermented food. It is that power in this ecosystem keeps drifting toward private gurus, expensive fixes, and symbolic self-management while the actual machinery of government remains in their hands.

What This Says About Rule

The cabinet’s cabbage cult is not a scandal by itself. It is a portrait of a governing class that treats authority as personal branding and seriousness as a consumable product. That is the structural lesson: when power becomes this insulated, it stops looking for expertise in public institutions and starts buying confidence from anyone who can make it feel healthier, sharper, or more righteous.
