Trump's all-out assault on reality won't end well

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The State Wants the Right to Lie, and Calls That Freedom

Power Is the Point

This story is not about education policy drifting off course. It is about state power being used to discipline speech. According to the source, Trump-era anti-WOKE laws, backed by Republican governors and state legislatures, have reached classrooms, universities, corporations, and even national parks. That is not confusion. That is institutional force aimed at controlling what can be said, taught, and remembered.

Who Chose This

The decision-makers are not the teachers, professors, parents, or park staff now placed under threat. The people with leverage are the officials writing vague statutes, directing agencies, and setting the penalties. Judges in the source correctly identify the mechanism: one rule for approved speech, punishment for disfavored speech. The point is not neutrality. It is a government-enforced worldview with a legal cudgel behind it.

The Misdirection

The framing of “freedom” is the tell. These laws do not expand free expression; they ration it. They bar discussion of race, gender, sexuality, slavery, Jim Crow, and dissenting viewpoints while pretending that censorship is liberty. That is propaganda by legislative branding. It tries to make suppression sound civic-minded and makes the target look like the aggressor.

The source also leans hard into outrage, but its central fact is stronger than its rhetoric: these policies create investigations, lawsuits, and terminations for speech that the state disapproves of. That is not an abstract culture war. It is bureaucratic intimidation.

What Gets Erased

The national park example strips the disguise bare. When officials remove or alter signs and materials that do not fit the preferred story, they are not “balancing” history. They are editing the record to flatten women, Native Americans, enslaved people, and minorities into background noise. The source calls this censorship, and that is the correct word. A state that selects which histories may be visible is not protecting truth. It is managing memory.

The Real Pattern

The broader pattern is authoritarian cruelty dressed as administrative common sense. The same machinery that bans classroom discussion of race or gender can also force a false historical narrative, chill corporate training, and threaten educators for acknowledging reality. The recurring move is simple: use law to punish complexity, then claim the damage is merely sensitivity or order.

That is the political meaning here. These laws are not about protecting children, families, or freedom. They are about who gets to define reality, who gets silenced for resisting it, and how far state power can go while calling itself the victim.
