'Deeply shameful': GOP senator skewered after 'wildly irresponsible' shooting remarks

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 9, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Traffic enters Fort Stewart at the main entrance gate following an active shooter incident on the U.S. Army base located in Hinesville, Georgia, U.S. August 6, 2025. Richard Burkhart/USA Today Network via REUTERS.

About two hours before the suspect in Wednesday’s Fort Stewart, Georgia, mass shooting was identified, U.S. Sen. Tommy Tuberville (R-AL) was quick to blame the “radical left” for allegedly demonizing law enforcement and the military. The suspect has since been identified as a U.S. Army Sergeant at Fort Stewart. No motive is currently known.

“Praying for the safety of everyone in Fort Stewart,” Tuberville wrote on social media at 1:25 p.m. “This is the DIRECT RESULT of the Radical Left’s constant demonization of law enforcement and our military service members.”

Five soldiers were shot, according to CNN. All were being treated for their injuries.

“Soldiers in the area that witnessed the shooting immediately and without hesitation tackled the soldier, subdued him. That allowed law enforcement to then take him into custody,” Brig. Gen. John Lubas, commander of the 3rd Infantry Division and Fort Stewart-Hunter Army Airfield, told reporters at a news conference.

Critics blasted the Alabama Republican senator who is currently running for governor.

“Fact check,” wrote The Washington Post’s Dan Lamothe minutes after Tuberville’s remarks. “There is NO clarity yet in the cause for this shooting.”

“The immediate and unfounded politicization of a tragic and rapidly evolving situation by a sitting senator is deeply shameful. This is not a time to score political points,” wrote Barratt Dewey, defense and policy reporter for Tectonic Defense.

“You’re a sitting senator. It is wildly irresponsible of you to ascribe a motive when we don’t know one yet,” said public relations executive Neal Urwitz, a former advisor to the Secretary of the Navy.

“Wildly irresponsible defines Tuberville,” wrote The Atlantic’s Dr. Norman Ornstein, a political scientist. “Along with dumb as a post. And let’s add, since this pathetic excuse for a senator disrupted hundreds or more military leaders by blocking their promotions for months, a world class hypocrite.”

Our Analysis:

The Rash Judgment of Sen. Tuberville: A Case Study in Political Opportunism

In a display that can be characterized as nothing short of reckless, U.S. Sen. Tommy Tuberville (R-AL) decided to exploit a tragedy for political gain even before the dust settled on the recent mass shooting at Fort Stewart, Georgia. Before the suspect was identified as a U.S. Army Sergeant stationed at Fort Stewart, Tuberville had already cast blame on the "radical left" for supposedly demonizing law enforcement and the military. This leap to conclusions is not just premature; it's a glaring example of political opportunism at its most distasteful.

Unfounded Accusations and the Need for Responsibility

Tuberville's accusation came with a boldness only matched by its baselessness. His claim, made via social media, that the shooting was the "DIRECT RESULT of the Radical Left’s constant demonization of law enforcement and our military service members," is a stark reminder of how political narratives are weaponized at the expense of truth and decency. The fact that the suspect was later identified as a member of the very institution Tuberville claimed was under attack by the left only adds a layer of irony to the senator's premature outburst.

Criticism from All Corners

The response to Tuberville's comment was swift and sharp. From The Washington Post's Dan Lamothe pointing out the lack of clarity regarding the shooting's cause, to defense and policy reporter Barratt Dewey's condemnation of the politicization of the tragedy, the chorus of disapproval highlighted a broader consensus: Tuberville's actions were not just inappropriate; they were irresponsible. Public relations executive Neal Urwitz's labeling of the senator's behavior as "wildly irresponsible" and Dr. Norman Ornstein's critique, painting Tuberville as both ignorant and hypocritical, underscore the gravity of the senator's misstep.

The Real Heroes of Fort Stewart

In stark contrast to Tuberville's rush to judgment, the real story of heroism at Fort Stewart was unfolding. Soldiers, in the face of danger, acted with bravery and decisiveness. They tackled and subdued the shooter, preventing further harm and demonstrating the true spirit of the military community. This act of courage stands in opposition to Tuberville's narrative, showcasing that the values of the military and law enforcement are not so easily undermined by political discourse as he suggests.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Responsibility

Sen. Tommy Tuberville's premature politicization of the Fort Stewart shooting serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing tragedy and the responsibility public officials have to avoid such recklessness. In a world where facts often fall victim to political narratives, Tuberville's actions are a reminder of the need for diligence, respect, and a commitment to truth in our discourse, especially in times of crisis. It's a lesson Tuberville, and indeed all political figures, would do well to learn.
