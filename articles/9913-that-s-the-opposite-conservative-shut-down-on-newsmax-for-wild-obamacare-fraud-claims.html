'That's the opposite!' Conservative shut down on Newsmax for wild Obamacare fraud claims

Congress failed to adopt a spending bill by the fiscal year deadline Tuesday night, and thereby kicked off the first government shutdown since 2019. The top sticking point for lawmakers was Republicans’ refusal to extend subsidies for Obamacare, which assists Americans without employer-based health insurance, with Republican-led states leading enrollment in the program in recent years.

Now, with 22 million Americans’ health insurance costs in limbo, Giordano attempted to dismiss the program out of hand by suggesting it was fraught with fraud and abuse.

“About 60% of the people on this extended benefit for COVID with Obamacare apparently have never filed a claim,” Giordano claimed. “You know what that points to with me? It points to fraud and abuse that’s here.”

Whether or not an American enrolled in Obamacare has filed a claim or not is not indicative of fraud and abuse, according to experts, with prominent Republican Sen. Bernie Moreno (OH) recently touting the same 60% figure to dismiss the program’s viability.

Appearing on Newsmax as well was Grant Stern, executive director of Occupy Democrats, who immediately challenged Giordano’s assertion.

“Never filed a claim? You can’t have fraud with no claim!” Stern said. “People with Obamacare subsidies, these are working people. In fact, most of the people that are getting these subsidies are probably self-employed working people, the people that we need to support the most, the entrepreneurs; they are the backbone of our economy.”

Giordano fired back at Stern, questioning how he knew that most Obamacare enrollees were self-employed, to which Stern said his assertion came from personal experience. About half of Obamacare enrollees are either small business owners, employees or self-employed, with the subsidies designed to help Americans without employer-based insurance afford health insurance on the private market.

Giordano would again bring up the 60% figure, to which, again, he was shut down by Stern.

“That means that they’re not abusing it, that’s the opposite!” Stern said. “If they’re filing claims, you could say, ‘well, maybe they’re filing some sort of false claim.’ There’s no fraud that’s happening in the Obamacare system.”

Furthermore, Giordano characterized the government shutdown as the “Schumer shutdown,” so named after Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY). Stern instead dubbed the shutdown as wholly belonging to President Donald Trump.

“This is Donald Trump’s shutdown! He doesn’t want to give health care to American people who are working class, people who need health care,” Stern said. “...This is the Republican strategy: they will shut the government down rather than fund Americans’ health care, and prices are rising right now, people need lower health care costs, lower health care premiums.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Misinformation on Obamacare and the Government Shutdown

In an era where facts are often drowned out by loud, baseless claims, the recent shutdown spectacle provides a textbook example of how misinformation can fuel politically charged narratives. Conservative radio host Dom Giordano’s appearance on Newsmax, where he attempted to discredit Obamacare by tying it to fraud, serves as a case in point. Let’s unpack the layers of misinformation and provide a fact-based analysis.

The Faulty Foundation of Giordano’s Claims

Giordano’s assertion that Obamacare is “fraught with fraud” because “about 60% of the people on this extended benefit for COVID with Obamacare apparently have never filed a claim” is a masterclass in misunderstanding or, more likely, deliberately misinterpreting how health insurance works. The leap from individuals not filing a claim to presumptions of widespread fraud is not just a logical fallacy; it’s a deliberate ignorance of insurance principles. Health insurance, by its very design, is a safety net, not a service utilized by submitting claims as proof of its necessity.

The Misrepresentation of the Government Shutdown

Giordano’s attempt to label the government shutdown as the “Schumer shutdown” is a blatant misdirection. The shutdown came about due to Congress’s failure to pass a spending bill, primarily because of Republican refusal to extend subsidies for Obamacare. These subsidies are crucial for Americans without employer-based health insurance—a demographic that has increasingly included Republican-led states. This isn’t a partisan squabble over trivialities but a fundamental disagreement on the role of government in healthcare, with millions of Americans caught in the middle.

The Real Faces of Obamacare

Grant Stern’s response to Giordano’s baseless claims highlights the reality of who benefits from Obamacare. Contrary to the image painted by its detractors, the program supports working people, small business owners, employees, and the self-employed—in short, the backbone of the American economy. These are individuals and families who otherwise might not afford health insurance, directly contradicting the narrative that the program is a hotbed of fraud.

The Irony of Enrollment Figures

It’s worth noting the irony in Republican-led states leading enrollment in Obamacare, a program their representatives often criticize. This fact underlines a glaring disconnect between political rhetoric and the healthcare needs of constituents. It’s an example of how ideological opposition to a policy can stand in stark contrast to its practical, often life-saving benefits for the public.

Conclusion: The Need for Fact-Based Discourse

The debate around Obamacare and the government shutdown exemplifies the dire need for a return to fact-based discourse. Misinformation, like that spread by Giordano, only serves to muddy the waters and impede meaningful progress. It’s time to cut through the noise and focus on the realities of healthcare in America—realities that affect millions of lives across the political spectrum.

In closing, let’s remember that at the heart of these political skirmishes are real people whose health and well-being hang in the balance. Healthcare should not be a partisan issue but a universal one, approached with the seriousness and fact-based analysis it deserves. As the dust settles on this latest government shutdown, one can only hope for a shift towards more informed, less divisive discussions on healthcare policy in the future.
