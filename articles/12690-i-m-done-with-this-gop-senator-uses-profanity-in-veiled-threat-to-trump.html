'I'm done with this': GOP senator uses profanity in veiled threat to Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a picnic with military families to mark Independence Day, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 4, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

One member of the Senate Republican Conference who has officially ruled out another term said that while he doesn't plan on sabotaging President Donald Trump's agenda, he also won't put up with "disrespectful" behavior during his last 18 months in Washington.

On Tuesday, Semafor published an interview with outgoing Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.), who announced that he was not seeking a third term in the U.S. Senate after officially coming out against the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act." The North Carolina Republican attributed his decision to leave the Senate to a post from Trump on his Truth Social platform attacking him. One Senate Republican anonymously told The Hill that they viewed Trump's post as "totally unnecessary."

"If somebody wants to know why I’m not running, it’s because some bonehead told him to post something and pretend like that was going to affect me," Tillis told Semafor. “It affected me in a way that said: ‘I’m done with this bulls---.’"

Several times during the interview, Tillis warned that he returned behavior he was presented with, and cited one example of a Trump supporter who confronted him at the Washington D.C. airport over his opposition to confirming former interim U.S. Attorney Ed Martin. The two-term Senate Republican told Semafor's Burgess Everett that he was a "former trailer park resident."

"There’s no human being on the face of the planet whose behavior I wouldn’t mirror if it’s disrespectful," he said.

Tillis also bristled at the comparisons to former Sen. Mitt Romney (R-UT), who became a thorn in the side of Trump's first administration by often voting against his legislative agenda. He cautioned Trump against comparing him to the one-time Republican presidential nominee, and implied that there would be consequences if the president attacked him in a similar fashion as he did Romney.

“If the president were to say something about me the way he did about Mitt Romney, people could only hope that I would behave like Mitt Romney," Tillis said. "Let’s just put it that way. I don’t take a pass on anybody flexing with me.”

Our Analysis:

The Saga of Thom Tillis: A Tale of Tumultuous Trump Ties

In an era where political allegiance often trumps (pun intended) principle, the story of Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) presents a curious case of political defiance, one that merits a closer examination. Tillis, in his swan song phase, has chosen to distance himself from the tumultuous Trumpian tactics that have come to define the Republican modus operandi of late. His recent interview with Semafor sheds light on this decision, revealing a tale of frustration, disrespect, and the refusal to bend the knee to former President Donald Trump's whims.

The Catalyst of Contempt

At the heart of Tillis' departure is a disdain for disrespect, a sentiment triggered by Trump's attack on Tillis via the Truth Social platform. This attack, deemed "totally unnecessary" by an anonymous Senate Republican, was the straw that broke the camel's back for Tillis, who told Semafor, “It affected me in a way that said: ‘I’m done with this bulls---.’” This moment of revelation underscores a broader issue within the GOP: the toxic environment fostered by Trump's penchant for public shaming and personal attacks on those who dare to dissent.

Mirror, Mirror on the Wall

Tillis' vow to mirror disrespectful behavior is both a defense mechanism and a warning. His encounter with a Trump supporter at the Washington D.C. airport, where he proudly claimed his roots as a "former trailer park resident," underscores his readiness to reflect any disrespect directed at him. This stance, while noble in its defense of personal dignity, raises questions about the efficacy of such a tit-for-tat approach in the political arena. Is mirroring disrespect truly the best way to elevate the discourse, or does it merely perpetuate a cycle of pettiness?

A Cautionary Tale: The Romney Comparison

The shadow of Sen. Mitt Romney (R-UT), another Republican who famously clashed with Trump, looms large over Tillis' narrative. Tillis' caution against being compared to Romney — and his veiled threat of unspecified consequences should Trump attack him similarly — is telling. It speaks to the precarious position of Republicans who challenge Trump's influence: too similar to Romney, and one risks the ire of Trump's base; too compliant, and one loses their integrity.

Conclusion: A Lonely Stand or a New Path?

Tillis' decision to not seek re-election and his public rebuke of Trump's tactics mark a significant, albeit solitary, stand against the prevailing currents in his party. His departure poses a critical question: Will others follow in his footsteps, or will his exit merely be a blip in the relentless march of Trumpism within the GOP?

As the Republican Party grapples with its identity in the post-Trump era, Tillis' saga serves as a reminder of the cost of political courage in a time of unyielding loyalty tests. While some may view his defiance as a futile gesture, others will see it as a necessary step toward reclaiming a party lost to the whims of one man's ego. Only time will tell which perspective will prevail.
