Trump repeatedly struggles to stay awake in Cabinet meeting video

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 3, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Somnolent Saga of President Trump: A Critical Examination

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting with U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Once again, President Donald Trump appeared to struggle to stay awake, this time during his mid-Tuesday televised Cabinet meeting. At several points, the president was filmed with his eyes closed, occasionally reopening them while seeming disengaged.

In one 30-second clip, the president’s eyes close numerous times, then Trump nods when he is mentioned. In a shorter clip, Trump also struggles to keep his eyes open, as his hand holds up his head.

In a 23-second clip, the president is hunched over, slouching in his chair, his eyes closed in what could be described as appearing to nod off.

Trump slouches and appears to try to listen as HUD Secretary Scott Turner speaks, in this 79-second video.

In a 17-second clip, journalist Aaron Rupar wrote, “Trump’s face is becoming contorted as he desperately tries to cling to consciousness.” In another, he called the president “Dozy Don.”

But in perhaps the most extreme capture of the president appearing to doze off, as Secretary of State Marco Rubio speaks, Trump is totally hunched over, his eyes closed, his head then falls forward, and he appears to try to wake up before seemingly falling back asleep.

While this is not the first time the president has appeared to fall asleep on camera, it comes after a massive late-night social media spree, in which Trump posted or reposted over 150 times, as Alternet reported.

Axios’ Marc Caputo noted that “Trump went on a Truth Social bender last night, posting 158 times from 9pm Monday to 12am Tuesday Just before 5:30 am, he started hitting social media again.”

The media is beginning to notice.

Last week, The New York Times published an in-depth look at Trump’s “signs of fatigue.”

“Mr. Trump appeared to doze off during an event in the Oval Office,” one week after Halloween, the Times noted.

The president “has fewer public events on his schedule and is traveling domestically much less than he did by this point during his first year in office, in 2017, although he is taking more foreign trips,” according to the Times. “He also keeps a shorter public schedule than he used to. Most of his public appearances fall between noon and 5 p.m., on average.”

“During an Oval Office event that began around noon on Nov. 6,” the Times added, “Mr. Trump sat behind his desk for about 20 minutes as executives standing around him talked about weight-loss drugs.”

“At one point, Mr. Trump’s eyelids drooped until his eyes were almost closed, and he appeared to doze on and off for several seconds. At another point, he opened his eyes and looked toward a line of journalists watching him. He stood up only after a guest who was standing near him fainted and collapsed.”

Our Analysis:

The Somnolent Saga of President Trump: A Critical Examination

In an era where political spectacle often overshadows substantive policy debate, the latest episode in President Donald Trump’s tenure adds a peculiar, albeit concerning, layer to the discourse. The president, known for his brash and unorthodox approach to both governance and communication, appears to now grapple with a more mundane adversary: the urge to sleep. This isn’t just a minor footnote in the annals of presidential quirks; it’s a glaring spotlight on a series of systemic issues, from the personal health of a nation’s leader to the broader implications of his behavior on the perception of leadership in the 21st century.

The Evidence Mounts

Reports indicate that during a mid-Tuesday televised Cabinet meeting, Trump displayed a marked struggle to remain conscious. With eyes frequently closed and head occasionally supported by hand, the president seemed less engaged in governance and more in a battle against somnolence. This wasn’t a fleeting moment but a series of instances captured on camera, showing a leader not at the helm but adrift in drowsiness.

Critics, seizing upon this imagery, have not held back. Journalist Aaron Rupar’s commentary, branding the president as “Dozy Don,” encapsulates the blend of concern and mockery this behavior has sparked. Trump’s apparent struggle to stay awake, especially as Cabinet members spoke, paints a picture not just of fatigue but of disinterest or incapacity.

The Late-Night Frenzy: A Prelude to Daytime Drowsiness

Adding context to these daytime lapses is Trump’s reported nocturnal activity on social media platforms. A “Truth Social bender,” as Marc Caputo of Axios described it, saw Trump posting a staggering 158 times between late evening and midnight, followed by an early morning resurgence. This isn’t merely a tale of digital addiction; it’s a testament to a disturbing disregard for the physical demands of the presidency. The nocturnal spree raises significant questions about self-discipline, prioritization, and the physical toll such habits may exact on an individual tasked with leading a nation.

Media Attention and Public Perception

The media’s growing focus on Trump’s tiredness, from The New York Times’ in-depth analysis to Alternet’s reportage, signifies a shift from mere curiosity to genuine concern. The implications are twofold: First, there’s the immediate question of Trump’s health and whether his lifestyle choices are compatible with the demands of his office. Second, and perhaps more critically, is the issue of public perception. In an age where image often translates to influence, the sight of a dozing president does little to inspire confidence or respect on the global stage.

The Schedule: A Symptom or a Cause?

Notably, Trump’s public schedule has seen a reduction in both the number and scope of events. This scaling back, as some reports suggest, could be an attempt to manage the president’s energy levels, a strategy that might alleviate symptoms but does little to address underlying causes. The emphasis on fewer, midday engagements points to a troubling adjustment of presidential duties around personal limitations, rather than the pursuit of a vigorous, proactive agenda.

Conclusion: Beyond the Doze

The saga of Trump’s struggles with sleep is more than a series of embarrassing clips; it’s a stark reminder of the human limitations even world leaders face. Yet, in an office that demands constant vigilance and unyielding energy, these limitations become liabilities. The presidency, in its ideal form, is a beacon of leadership, resilience, and dedication. When these qualities are overshadowed by physical fatigue, the systemic error isn’t just in one man’s sleep habits but in a political culture that somehow makes this scenario possible.

In the end, the issue isn’t merely about Trump’s need for rest; it’s about a nation’s need for robust, energetic leadership. As the world watches, one can’t help but wonder: In the relentless pace of modern politics, is there room for a president who needs a nap? Or is it time to wake up to the demands of contemporary governance and expect more from those who seek to lead?
