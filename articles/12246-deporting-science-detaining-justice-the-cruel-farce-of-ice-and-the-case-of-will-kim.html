Deporting Science, Detaining Justice: The Cruel Farce of ICE and the Case of Will Kim

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In a country that prides itself on innovation, justice, and the rule of law, the detention of Will Kim—an American in everything but paperwork—serves as a grim reminder that those ideals are often hollow slogans used to paper over cruelty.

Tae Heung "Will" Kim is a 40-year-old legal permanent resident of the United States. He has lived here since the age of five, built his life here, and is currently pursuing a Ph.D. at Texas A&M University, working on a vaccine for Lyme disease—a project that could save lives. And yet, in July 2025, he was held in ICE custody at San Francisco International Airport, caught in the gnarled web of an immigration enforcement system that is as merciless as it is mindless.

His supposed crime? A misdemeanor marijuana possession charge from fourteen years ago. A sealed case. A long-resolved issue. A slap-on-the-wrist offense that countless white Americans have laughed off or spun into tech-bro anecdotes about entrepreneurial spirit. Will Kim, however, is not afforded such privilege. Because ICE doesn't see him as a neighbor, a scholar, or a contributor to public health. They see a line item: "not a citizen." And that's all it takes.

The absurdity is staggering. Kim had completed his sentence—community service. He never reoffended. He was transparently returning from a family event, his brother's wedding. Yet CBP flagged him, detained him, and handed him over to ICE. Since then, he has been held in what can only be described as extrajudicial purgatory: no daylight, lights on 24/7, sleeping in a chair, limited food, and reportedly, no access to his asthma medication.

His legal team has been allowed just one brief phone call, and claims that CBP told them—brazenly—that constitutional rights don’t apply to green card holders. Let that sink in. The U.S. government is now openly asserting that legal residents have no claim to the Constitution. Not the Fifth Amendment. Not the Sixth. Not even the bare minimum of habeas corpus.

All of this is happening in the shadow of a federal government led once again by Donald Trump, under whose first term ICE became an unhinged, terror-inducing force. A rogue agency whose tactics included separating children from their parents, conducting warrantless raids, and maintaining private detention centers riddled with human rights abuses. Instead of reigning them in, Trump’s second term has escalated their powers, stripping away oversight and codifying cruelty as policy.

Will Kim’s case is not a bureaucratic oversight—it’s a calculated feature of a system designed to dehumanize. When ICE agents tear apart lives over petty, ancient offenses, they are not making America safer; they are feeding a xenophobic machinery that criminalizes presence, not behavior.

Ask yourself this: if Kim were white, would this be happening? If he were born in Canada or Britain instead of Korea? If his research were for a private biotech startup with connections instead of a public university? Would a 14-year-old minor possession charge be treated as a death sentence to one’s legal status? Of course not. Because this isn’t about justice. It’s about power.

ICE operates like a weapon, not an institution of order. Its agents, protected by the veil of national security, have become enforcers of racialized punishment. They cherry-pick who gets to stay and who gets disappeared into a system that increasingly resembles the Kafkaesque bureaucracies of authoritarian regimes.

And let’s be blunt: this is why ICE must be abolished.

You don’t reform a cancer. You excise it. ICE has proven time and again that its mission is not law and order but fear and removal. It’s an institution that cannot coexist with democratic values because it actively undermines them.

The chilling message sent by Will Kim’s case is this: No matter how long you’ve lived here, no matter how much good you do, no matter if you’ve done your time and walked the straight and narrow—you are disposable. One old mistake is all it takes for the government to erase your life here and ship you to a country you haven’t known since childhood.

This isn’t law enforcement. It’s ideological cleansing.

If you are an American and you are not appalled by this, then you have to ask yourself what it is you’re truly defending when you wave the flag. Because it’s not freedom. It’s not fairness. And it damn sure isn’t justice.

We cannot keep looking away. This isn’t a glitch in the system. It is the system. And until we tear it down and build something humane in its place, there will be more Will Kims. More scientists, nurses, fathers, neighbors, gone without warning. Locked away in windowless rooms, denied sunlight, medicine, and legal counsel—all because they weren't born in the right place.

This is not a country of laws. It is a country of cruelty by design.

And it needs to stop.
