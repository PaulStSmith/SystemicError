'Trump is worried': Experts say FCC move to kill Colbert interview backfired big time

President Donald Trump’s Federal Communications Commission began its long-threatened crackdown on political late-night talk show interviews by pressuring CBS to pull an already-filmed interview on “The Late Show with Stephen Colbert” with Texas U.S. Senate candidate James Talarico.

But that move appears to have blown up in their faces — and some political observers are saying this could be a “boon” for Talarico’s campaign.

Colbert responded to the legal threats by instead uploading the interview to “The Late Show” YouTube page, where it has since spread to every corner of the internet and likely earned far more viewership than it would have airing on CBS in the first place — an excellent development for Talarico, who has trailed most primary polls in recent weeks against Dallas-area Rep. Jasmine Crockett, partly due to his lesser name recognition among Texas voters.

Talarico posted the interview to X, saying, “This is the interview Donald Trump didn’t want you to see. His FCC refused to air my interview with Stephen Colbert. Trump is worried we’re about to flip Texas.”

Commentators noticed.

“James Talarico is having quite a moment in Texas after the Colbert interview — and according to Google Trends, Texans are now searching his name at a very high rate just in time for early voting,” said election forecaster Logan Phillips in a post on X.

“As of 12 PM ET, view counts on the most-watched Colbert clips on his FCC-censored Talarico interview: YouTube: 1.3M; Instagram: 1.6M; TikTok: 2.4M; Twitter: 5.1M,” wrote energy policy analyst Jessy Han. “For context, Colbert’s show averages 2.3 million viewers on linear TV.”

“This incredible stat says a lot about why this @colbertlateshow interview w/@jamestalarico was such a boon for him,” wrote former Barack Obama campaign strategist David Axelrod. “He gets the benefit of being banned from the airwaves by CBS under fire from Trump’s FCC — a badge of honor among Dem primary voters — and the interview already has been seen by more viewers digitally than it would have been had it simply aired.”

Longtime Bernie Sanders strategist and Hispanic voter outreach expert Chuck Rocha agreed, simply writing, “CONFIRMED.”

Our Analysis:

The FCC’s Attempted Censorship: A Case Study in the Streisand Effect

In a bold move that seems ripped straight from the playbook of political farce, President Donald Trump’s Federal Communications Commission (FCC) embarked on a crusade against free speech, targeting The Late Show with Stephen Colbert for its decision to air an interview with Texas U.S. Senate candidate James Talarico. However, this act of censorship backfired spectacularly, serving as a prime example of the Streisand Effect, wherein an attempt to suppress information only amplifies its reach and public interest.

The Boomerang Effect

The FCC’s pressure on CBS to pull the Colbert interview with Talarico was not just an assault on the media’s freedom but a blatant misuse of power to influence political discourse. Yet, in the age of digital media, such attempts at suppression are not just futile but counterproductive. Stephen Colbert’s strategic pivot to upload the interview to YouTube and other social media platforms turned a potentially silenced segment into a viral sensation. This move did not just sidestep the censorship attempt but catapulted Talarico from a candidate struggling with name recognition into the national spotlight.

The Numbers Don’t Lie

The statistics are a damning indictment of the FCC’s misguided endeavor. The interview’s view counts across platforms—YouTube: 1.3M; Instagram: 1.6M; TikTok: 2.4M; Twitter: 5.1M—dwarf the average viewership of Colbert’s show on traditional TV, which stands at 2.3 million. These numbers not only highlight the changing landscape of media consumption but also underscore the futility of trying to contain digital media within the archaic framework of broadcast regulation.

A Badge of Honor

For Talarico, the FCC’s censorship attempt has inadvertently provided a campaign gift, transforming him into a martyr for free speech and the democratic process. Being banned by Trump’s FCC has become a “badge of honor” among Democratic primary voters, as noted by David Axelrod, a sentiment echoed across the political spectrum. The incident has galvanized support for Talarico, showcasing the power of adversity to rally people around a cause or a candidate perceived to be unjustly targeted.

The Streisand Effect in Full Swing

The entire episode is a textbook case of the Streisand Effect, named after Barbra Streisand’s infamous attempt to suppress photographs of her home, which only led to widespread dissemination of those images. Similarly, the FCC’s actions have catapulted Talarico into a level of public awareness and support that he might not have achieved otherwise. This incident vividly illustrates the limitations of traditional media control in a digital age and the potential for backlash against attempts to curtail freedom of expression.

Conclusion

The FCC’s ill-conceived intervention in the Colbert-Talarico interview is a stark reminder of the power dynamics at play within the media landscape and the potential for government overreach to backfire. In the digital era, attempts to suppress information can lead to unforeseen consequences, amplifying the very messages authorities seek to silence. This episode should serve as a cautionary tale for those in power: in the age of the internet, censorship is not just unethical—it’s impractical and can have the opposite effect of what was intended, turning suppressive actions into a megaphone for the censored.
