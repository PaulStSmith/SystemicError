The Trump effect: Florida race turned on head as voters told what president wants

That’s from a new poll conducted for the James Madison Institute (JMI) in the middle of May among 516 Republican voters.

That’s after those Republicans were informed that Donalds has already been endorsed by President Donald Trump for the governor’s race. The poll says initial results from GOP voters surveyed before learning of the Trump backing had Ms. DeSantis narrowly leading Donalds by a single point, 29%-28%.

Ms. DeSantis is not a declared candidate yet for governor, but she has not dismissed the possibility of running next year to succeed her husband, Gov. Ron DeSantis, who is term-limited from running again.

She has faced more media attention over the past month than at any other time as First Lady over the past six years. That’s because of reports about potential misappropriation of funds involving Hope Florida and the Hope Florida Foundation, the social welfare charity that she has been associated with.

Gov. DeSantis has accused House Republicans, the “liberal media,” and Democrats of launching “baseless smears against Hope Florida and by extension myself and the First Lady,” he said at a press conference in Kissimmee last month.

Donalds announced his candidacy for governor on Feb. 26, five days after Trump endorsed him. Since his announcement, Donalds has reported raising more than $12 million as of the end of March, with most of that money flowing into his affiliated political committee.

The James Madison Institute commissioned two public polls, one in April and one in early May. The April poll results were taken when South Florida Sen. Jason Pizzo was still in the Democratic Party and a potential nominee for that party’s gubernatorial nomination next year.

The May poll shows former Northwest Florida U.S. Rep. and now cable news pundit Matt Gaetz in third place at 10%; Jeanette Nuñez, the former lieutenant governor and now interim president of Florida International University, in fourth place with 9% support. Miami Mayor Francis Suarez is next with 5%.

Democratic field

As mentioned above, the JMI poll surveyed voters in both April and May. In April, it surveyed 464 Democratic voters, who gave Pizzo 41% support.

In distant second place at that time was Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava with 15%. Former Republican U.S. Rep. turned Democrat David Jolly was next with 7%, followed by former U.S. Rep. Gwen Graham at 5%.

Jacksonville state Rep. Angie Nixon and former South Florida state Sen. Lauren Book were next at 4%. South Florida state Sen. Shevrin Jones was at 2% and House Leader Fentrice Driskell was at 1%.

But with Pizzo’s departure, a follow-up survey in May of 396 Democratic voters showed Mayor Levine Cava now leads the Democrats with 32%. Gwen Graham is next at 13%, followed by Jolly at 10% and former state Sen. Lauren Book at 7%. However, 20% were undecided.

The survey in both months was conducted by Targoz Market Research of 1,200 voters, 43% of whom said they were Republicans, 33% Democrats, and 23% independent voters. It has a +/- margin of error of 2.77%.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Winds of Political Favor: The Florida Governor's Race

In the swirling tempest that is Florida politics, a new public opinion survey has cast revealing light on the Republican race for governor in 2026. The survey, conducted by the James Madison Institute (JMI), places U.S. Rep. Byron Donalds firmly ahead of Florida First Lady Casey DeSantis, with a commanding lead of 44% to 25% among Republican voters. This leap in Donalds' favorability came after respondents were informed of his endorsement by President Donald Trump. The Trump Effect in Republican primaries, it seems, remains as potent as ever.

The Trump Endorsement: A Political Game-Changer

Before the revelation of Trump's backing, Casey DeSantis was narrowly leading Donalds by a mere point (29%-28%). The stark contrast in numbers pre and post-endorsement revelation underscores the significant sway Trump continues to hold within the Republican base. It's a testament to the power of high-profile endorsements in shaping political races, particularly in primaries where party loyalty and ideological alignment are at the forefront.

Casey DeSantis: The Unannounced Candidate

Casey DeSantis, despite not officially throwing her hat in the ring for the governor's race, has been under the political spotlight more than ever. The attention isn't solely due to her potential candidacy but also because of controversies surrounding the Hope Florida and the Hope Florida Foundation. Accusations of fund misappropriation have marred the charity's reputation, prompting Gov. Ron DeSantis to decry what he perceives as "baseless smears" from various quarters. The cloud of scandal, whether justified or not, could be contributing to Casey DeSantis' lag in the polls, illustrating the impact of negative media attention on political prospects.

The Fundraising Machine: Byron Donalds

Byron Donalds' campaign seems to be in an enviable position, with a reported $12 million fundraising haul. The significance of this cannot be overstated. In the high-stakes game of political campaigning, war chests matter. They translate into advertising, outreach, and the nuts and bolts of building a winning campaign. Donalds' financial advantage, coupled with Trump's endorsement, positions him as a formidable contender for the governorship.

The Democratic Contenders: A Shifting Landscape

On the Democratic side, the race appears more fluid. The departure of South Florida Sen. Jason Pizzo from the race has reshuffled the deck, propelling Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava into the lead with 32% support among Democratic voters. However, a notable 20% remain undecided, indicating a significant level of uncertainty and openness within the Democratic electorate.

Conclusion: The Long Road to 2026

While these polls offer a fascinating snapshot of the current political landscape in Florida, they also serve as a reminder of the volatility and unpredictability inherent in electoral politics. With the race for the Florida governorship still years away, there's ample time for shifts in public opinion, unexpected developments, and new contenders to emerge.

The enduring influence of Donald Trump within the Republican Party, the impact of financial resources on campaign viability, and the role of media in shaping political narratives are all on full display in this unfolding drama. As we march closer to 2026, one thing is clear: the road to the governor's mansion in Florida is anything but predictable.
