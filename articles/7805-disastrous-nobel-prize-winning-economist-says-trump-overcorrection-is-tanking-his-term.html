'Disastrous': Nobel Prize-winning economist says Trump overcorrection is tanking his term

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump needed to make “cosmetic changes” to the economy rather than grand and sweeping new policies, a Nobel Prize winner has claimed.

Paul Krugman suggested that only a few minor changes were needed to level out the economy left to Trump by Joe Biden’s administration. Instead, Krugman suggested Trump had tried and failed to “gaslight” the American people with a slate of large changes and tariff policies which have seen the president’s approval rating slump.

Krugman explained in his Substack, “Trump would be in much better political shape right now if he had basically continued Biden’s policies, with only a few cosmetic changes. When he took office inflation was on a declining trajectory. Consumer sentiment was relatively favorable at the start of 2025.”

“Americans were still angry about high prices, but the inflation surge of 2021-3 had happened on Biden’s watch and was receding into the past. My guess is that many voters would have accepted Trump’s claims that high prices were Democrats’ fault and given him the benefit of the doubt about the economy’s future if he had simply done nothing drastic and left policies mostly as they were.”

Instead, Krugman says the president tried to “gaslight the public” with recent speeches rejecting the idea there was an affordability crisis across the country. The Nobel Prize winner says that in order for Trump to criticise economic policies from the Democrats, he would have to admit the economy now is suffering, something the president has refused to do.

Krugman wrote, “A number of news analyses suggested that he would use the occasion to blame Democrats for the economy’s troubles. That was never going to happen.”

“Trump did, of course, take many swipes at Joe Biden, as well as attacking immigrants, women and windmills. But to blame Democrats for the economy’s problems he would have to admit that the Trump economy has problems. And the speech was important because it revealed that he won’t make any such admission, and will continue to gaslight the public.”

The so-called “affordability tour” was deemed to have gotten off to a “disastrous start” by Krugman, who added, “And it won’t get better, because while Trump insists that the problem is you, it’s actually him. And he isn’t going to change.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Krugman’s Critique of Trump’s Economic Policies

In a recent assertion that could only stir the pot further in the already simmering cauldron of American politics, Nobel Prize winner Paul Krugman has laid bare his critique of Donald Trump’s approach to the U.S. economy. According to Krugman, Trump, in his quest for a transformative presidency, eschewed the path of minor adjustments in favor of grandiose policies and tariff impositions. This strategy, Krugman argues, not only failed to resonate with the American populace but also contributed to a decline in the president’s approval ratings.

The Illusion of Grandeur: Trump’s Economic Missteps

Krugman’s analysis, shared via his Substack, underscores a pivotal misjudgment by the Trump administration: the belief that the economy required radical overhauls rather than tactical, cosmetic changes. The irony here is palpable. Trump, inheriting an economy on a recovery trajectory from the Biden administration, decided to fix what wasn’t broken. In doing so, he inadvertently anchored his ship in turbulent waters.

The inflation surge of 2021-2023, a significant point of contention, was already receding as Trump took office. Krugman posits that had Trump maintained the course set by Biden, merely applying superficial modifications, the narrative surrounding his economic stewardship might have been markedly different. Instead, Trump’s attempts to “gaslight” the American public by denying an affordability crisis only exacerbated his challenges.

The Rhetoric Versus Reality Conundrum

Trump’s refusal to acknowledge the economic pinch felt by Americans is a classic example of political gaslighting. By painting a rosy picture of the economy, Trump aimed to deflect blame and responsibility. Yet, Krugman argues, this strategy is fundamentally flawed. You cannot address problems you refuse to acknowledge. Trump’s speeches, laden with critiques of Biden, immigrants, women, and windmills, sidestepped the critical issue: the economy’s health under his watch.

Krugman’s analysis suggests that Trump’s economic policies and his reluctance to admit their shortcomings have led to a disconnect with the electorate’s realities. The so-called “affordability tour”, as per Krugman, is off to a disastrous start, not due to external factors, but because of Trump’s intransigence and his inability to pivot from policies that have evidently not yielded the desired outcomes.

Conclusion: The Unchanging Stance of Trump

The crux of Krugman’s critique lies not just in the policies themselves but in Trump’s steadfast refusal to adapt or acknowledge their pitfalls. This, according to Krugman, is a recipe for continued disappointment. The inability to admit fault or consider alternative strategies is not just a political misstep; it’s a failure of leadership.

Krugman’s assessment, sharp and scathing, offers a stark reminder of the complexities inherent in economic policy and the dangers of oversimplification. In politics, as in life, admitting a problem is the first step toward solving it. Trump’s refusal to do so, as Krugman rightly points out, is not just a personal failing, but one that has tangible repercussions for the American economy and its citizens.
