The Great Unraveling: How GOP Economic Sabotage Is Fueling 2025's Layoff Crisis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Layoff Surge: A Symptom of Ideological RotGaslight Nation: The GOP Narrative vs. RealityEconomic Sabotage by DesignA Tale of Two FuturesThe Bottom Line: Don't Get Fooled Again

In a jarring but unsurprising development, the United States is careening toward a labor crisis unseen since the early pandemic years. The July jobs report has landed with a thud: job growth well below expectations, May and June figures quietly revised downward, and the unemployment rate creeping back up to 4.2%. But the most damning figure comes from CBS News, citing data from Challenger, Gray & Christmas: in the first half of 2025 alone, over 744,000 jobs have been cut across the country.

This isn’t just an economic hiccup. It’s a full-blown red flag that demands urgent political accountability—and make no mistake, the fingerprints of Donald Trump and the Republican economic agenda are all over it.

The Layoff Surge: A Symptom of Ideological Rot

Let’s be clear: layoffs happen. But they don’t happen in a vacuum. When nearly three-quarters of a million jobs vanish in six months, it's not because the market randomly decided to contract. It’s because bad policy, short-sighted governance, and a fetishization of corporate deregulation have converged to gut the American workforce.

Under Trump and his GOP enablers, the economy was rigged to serve shareholders and megadonors at the expense of workers. The 2017 tax cuts gave massive handouts to corporations, many of whom used the windfall to buy back stocks and inflate executive pay while outsourcing jobs and automating labor. Trump’s war on labor unions and OSHA protections weakened workers' ability to collectively bargain or even feel safe at work. And his administration's bungled pandemic response left lasting scars on the economy, particularly in sectors like healthcare, hospitality, and manufacturing.

Fast-forward to 2025: these structural weaknesses have metastasized.

Gaslight Nation: The GOP Narrative vs. Reality

Cue the gaslighting. As Gavin Newsom rightly called out in his tweet, Trump and his defenders are already spinning this crisis as someone else’s failure. According to them, it’s Bidenomics, blue states, or, predictably, immigrants. But the data doesn’t lie:

  • The layoffs are heaviest in tech, logistics, and finance—sectors that thrived under artificial pandemic-era bubbles and were artificially propped up by Trump-era deregulation.

  • The so-called "red state recovery" has turned into a hollow PR slogan. States like Texas and Florida, governed by GOP hardliners, are facing rising unemployment, surging housing insecurity, and healthcare systems in freefall.

  • Meanwhile, the federal government is hamstrung by Republican obstruction in Congress, where every proposal to expand worker protections or extend safety nets dies under the boot of deficit hawks who had no problem adding trillions to the national debt with tax cuts for the wealthy.

Trump isn’t just failing Americans—he’s sabotaging the foundation beneath them.

Economic Sabotage by Design

It would be one thing if this were simple incompetence. But it's not. It's calculated.

Trump and the modern GOP aren’t interested in stabilizing the economy for the working class. Their economic model thrives on precarity. When people are desperate, they're easier to exploit, easier to radicalize, and less likely to demand accountability.

A weakened middle class isn’t a byproduct. It's a feature.

Under the guise of "fiscal responsibility," Trump and his allies are already calling for cuts to Social Security, Medicare, and Medicaid—programs that become lifelines during downturns like the one we’re experiencing now. Meanwhile, they continue to defend corporate subsidies, fossil fuel bailouts, and Wall Street deregulation. It's redistribution, all right—from the poor to the rich.

A Tale of Two Futures

This crisis could have been averted—or at the very least, softened.

Imagine an alternative where union power is respected, where pandemic-era investments in child care and healthcare weren’t left to rot under GOP-led court challenges. Imagine an America where infrastructure spending wasn't used as a talking point but actually executed. Where universal basic income pilots weren't demonized but studied and scaled. Imagine if the full economic power of the federal government were mobilized to protect workers instead of coddling CEOs.

Instead, we got Trump.

And now we get headlines announcing the worst layoffs since COVID.

The Bottom Line: Don't Get Fooled Again

The right will try to spin this as a problem of "woke capitalism," of liberal overreach, or of immigrants "stealing jobs."

Don't fall for it.

What we are witnessing is the predictable result of decades of Republican economic sabotage, accelerated and magnified under Trump. This is what happens when you let billionaires write tax policy, when you gut regulatory agencies, and when you treat workers as expendable.

So yes, Gavin Newsom is right to raise the alarm.

But the rest of us need to answer it.

The question now isn't whether the layoffs will continue. The question is whether Americans will connect the dots, hold the arsonists accountable, and stop letting the same party that lit the match convince us the firefighters are the problem.

Because the smoke is getting thicker.

And the fire is still burning.
