'Pretty much gone': Poll underscores 'gut punch' reactions to White House destruction

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A woman holds her phone camera up high to try to get a view of the ongoing demolition of the East Wing of the White House, where U.S. President Donald Trump’s proposed ballroom is being built, in Washington, D.C., U.S. October 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Reactions mounted Thursday as footage showed the White House’s East Wing “pretty much gone” as a damning poll shows Americans are appalled by the demolition, viewing it as a “gut punch.”

“A YouGov America survey shows that 50 percent disapprove of the overall White House refurb, as opposed to just 33 percent who declare that they are in favor,” The Daily Beast reports. “Some 53 percent are against the East Wing being torn down, while just 24 percent of the 2,000 people polled favor Trump’s redesign of the extension, originally constructed in 1902.”

Trump has said that the changes were a long time coming and claimed the construction was “music to my ears” on Monday.

“We’re building a ballroom. They’ve wanted a ballroom for 150 years, and I’m giving that honor to this wonderful place,” Trump said.

Social media users responded to the startling images and videos of the building:

“It looks like the East Wing is pretty much gone,” reporter Matt Novak wrote on Bluesky.

“East wing carnage,” reporter and analyst Molly Jong-Fast wrote on Bluesky.

“This image of the East Wing of the White House is a metaphor for broader, reckless destruction. The ballroom that will go up there will be a gaudy monument to vanity, corruption and excess.For anyone who has worked at the WH, or cares about its rich history, it’s a gut punch,” David Axelrod, political analyst and former senior advisor to President Barack Obama, wrote on X.

“Trump just unilaterally destroyed the entire East Wing after claiming ‘modest renovations.’ Asked no one. No study done. No public input. No historical preservation efforts. All that history gone on the whims of one man,” analyst and strategist Rachel Bitecofer wrote on X.

“It’s not his house. It’s your house. And he’s destroying it,” former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton wrote on X.

“Three problems with the destruction of the East Wing of the White House: 1. Dubious legality. 2. High likelihood of corruption in funding. 3. Grotesque deformation of an American house, built in an harmonious and republican ethos, soon to be bloated by an emiratic carbuncle,” journalist Terry Moran wrote on X.

“Guess what, America? Your favorite president PROMISED that the consruction of his bullshit $250M ballroom would not cause any damage to The White House. Now he’s demolishing its ENTIRE EAST WING! And he’s doing the same to our entire country!” Producer Morgan J. Freeman wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The East Wing Demolition: A Monument to Vanity and Recklessness

In a move that can only be described as a brazen affront to American history and sensibilities, the demolition of the White House’s East Wing has sparked rightful outrage and disbelief across the nation. As captured in various reports, notably by The Daily Beast, the reaction to this demolition, underpinned by a YouGov America survey, is overwhelmingly negative. 50 percent disapprove of the overall White House refurbishment, with a mere 33 percent in favor. The figures are even more damning when focusing on the East Wing’s destruction: 53 percent are against, compared to a paltry 24 percent showing support for Trump’s redesign.

Trump’s Tone-Deaf Celebration

Trump’s comments, claiming that the construction noise was “music to my ears” and justifying the addition of a ballroom as fulfilling a 150-year-old desire, reek of a disconnection from the public sentiment and historical reverence. The self-congratulatory tone, dubbing the ballroom a “wonderful place,” starkly contrasts with the public’s view of the demolition as a gut punch.

Social Media Erupts

The reaction on social media platforms from respected figures paints a vivid picture of widespread condemnation. From Matt Novak’s observation that the East Wing is “pretty much gone” to Molly Jong-Fast’s succinct “East wing carnage,” the sentiment is clear. However, it is David Axelrod’s comment that encapsulates the broader implications, viewing the demolition as a metaphor for “broader, reckless destruction,” a “gaudy monument to vanity, corruption, and excess.”

Analysts and Politicians Weigh In

Critique doesn’t just stop at observations. Analysts and strategists, including Rachel Bitecofer, point out the unilateral nature of Trump’s decision, emphasizing the lack of consultation, study, public input, or historical preservation efforts. Hillary Clinton’s poignant reminder that “It’s not his house. It’s your house. And he’s destroying it,” should resonate with every American, highlighting the personal stake and collective heritage being bulldozed.

Journalist Terry Moran outlines three critical issues with the East Wing’s destruction: its dubious legality, the high likelihood of corruption in funding, and the grotesque deformation of a symbol of American ethos. Morgan J. Freeman’s exasperated accusation captures the betrayal felt by many: despite promises of no damage, the entire East Wing has been demolished, serving as a metaphor for the broader damage inflicted on the country.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The East Wing’s demolition, under the guise of a long-desired ballroom, is not just an architectural loss but a symbolic devastation of American heritage and values. The overwhelming disapproval mirrored in the YouGov America survey and the scathing commentary from various quarters underscore the recklessness of this act. This is not merely a matter of aesthetic disagreement but a profound disrespect for the nation’s historical legacy and democratic ethos.

In a democracy, significant changes to national symbols should involve public consultation and respect for historical integrity. Trump’s unilateral decision-making and the subsequent outcry should serve as a wake-up call for the importance of preserving our collective history, not as a relic of the past, but as a beacon for future generations. The demolition of the East Wing stands as a stark reminder of what is at stake when vanity and caprice overshadow respect for history and democratic principles.
