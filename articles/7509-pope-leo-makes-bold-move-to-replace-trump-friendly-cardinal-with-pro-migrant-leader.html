Pope Leo makes bold move to replace Trump-friendly cardinal with pro-migrant leader

Pope Leo XIV has replaced President Donald Trump‘s ally and conservative Cardinal Timothy Dolan with a pro-migrant leader and fellow Chicagoan.

Ronald Hicks, 52, who is the current bishop of Joliet, Illinois, will replace Dolan as the leader of New York’s Roman Catholic archdiocese, The Guardian reported. Dolan, who is retiring, has selected for the role by Pope Benedict XVI in 2012.

Hicks and Pope Leo grew up a few blocks away from each other in Chicago’s south suburbs, according to The New York Times. The move is “one of the most anticipated decisions of Pope Leo’s young papacy, his first major move indicating the direction he wants the church to take in his home country.”

Catholic leaders last month issued a rare rebuke of Trump’s immigration policies. Dolan has been criticized by other clergy members for his public ties to Trump, frequent appearances on Fox News and refusal to speak out against the harsh treatment of immigrants.

Leo, the first American-born pope, has issued harsh criticism of Trump and his administration, condemning lethal boat strikes in the Caribbean Sea, just off the coast of Venezuela.

“The choice suggests a significant shift in leadership style for New York’s archdiocese, the country’s second largest and one of its most influential, serving 2.5 million Catholics across the Bronx, Manhattan and Staten Island and several areas north of the city, according to statistics provided by the archdiocese,” The Times reported. “The selection also comes at a pivotal moment in the city’s political and religious life.”

Hicks will be installed as archbishop on Feb. 6 at St. Patrick’s Cathedral in New York City.

Our Analysis:

A Shift in the Vatican’s American Chessboard: Analyzing the Replacement of Cardinal Dolan with Bishop Hicks

In what can only be described as a strategic and ideologically charged move, Pope Leo XIV has decisively steered the direction of the Roman Catholic Church in the United States by appointing Ronald Hicks, a pro-migrant leader, to replace the conservative and Trump-ally Cardinal Timothy Dolan as the leader of New York’s archdiocese. This decision, as reported by The Guardian, is not just a routine change of guard; it is a clear signal of the Vatican’s shifting priorities and an unmistakable repudiation of the policies and political alliances cultivated by Dolan during his tenure.

The Dolan Era: A Controversial Alignment

Cardinal Timothy Dolan, selected by Pope Benedict XVI in 2012, has been a figure of significant controversy within the church. His close ties with President Donald Trump, frequent appearances on Fox News, and his apparent indifference towards the harsh treatment of immigrants have not only raised eyebrows but have also drawn criticism from within the clergy. It’s telling that a leader of a faith rooted in compassion and support for the marginalized opted to remain silent—or worse, complicit—in the face of policies that have caused untold suffering to vulnerable migrant communities.

The Hicks Promise: A Shift Towards Compassion

Ronald Hicks, on the other hand, represents a departure from the Dolan doctrine. At 52, the bishop of Joliet, Illinois, is not just a fellow Chicagoan of Pope Leo but also a symbol of the Pope’s vision for a more inclusive and socially responsible church. The Guardian reports Hicks grew up mere blocks away from Leo, suggesting a deep-rooted alignment in values and perspectives that go beyond mere geographical coincidence.

The move is described as “one of the most anticipated decisions of Pope Leo’s young papacy,” indicative of a broader intent to realign the church’s stance on critical issues within his home country. This choice is particularly poignant given the rare rebuke issued by Catholic leaders last month against Trump’s immigration policies—a clear demarcation of where Hicks and, by extension, Pope Leo, stand on the matter.

Strategic Implications and the Road Ahead

The installation of Hicks as archbishop on February 6 at St. Patrick’s Cathedral in New York City is more than a ceremonial transition; it is a pivotal moment in the city’s—and arguably the country’s—political and religious life. With an archdiocese serving 2.5 million Catholics across some of the most diverse and dynamic areas of New York, the leadership style and priorities of its head can significantly influence the social and political discourse.

The selection of Hicks signals a hopeful shift towards a leadership style that prioritizes the church’s foundational values of compassion, support for the marginalized, and a commitment to social justice. This is a rebuke of the Dolan era’s controversial alignment with divisive political figures and policies. It also raises intriguing questions about the future of the Roman Catholic Church’s engagement with social and political issues in the United States, especially in an era marked by deep divisions and unprecedented challenges.

In conclusion, Pope Leo XIV’s appointment of Ronald Hicks over Cardinal Timothy Dolan is not just a change in personnel; it is a bold statement of intent, a declaration of ideological realignment, and a beacon of hope for those who advocate for a more compassionate and inclusive church. As this new chapter unfolds, it will be fascinating to see how Hicks navigates the complex waters of New York’s religious and political landscape, and how his leadership influences the broader conversation around faith, politics, and social justice in America.
