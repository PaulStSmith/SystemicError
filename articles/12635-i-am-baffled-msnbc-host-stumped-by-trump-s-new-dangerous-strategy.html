'I am baffled': MSNBC host stumped by Trump's new 'dangerous strategy'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 18, 20254 min read

Donald Trump's attempt to flip the script as the White House deals with a revolt from his MAGA supporters is both confusing and possibly making the situation worse.

That is according to MSNBC host Joe Scarborough who spent the first part of "Morning Joe" trying to understand why Trump is now pushing a narrative that Jeffrey Epstein files that the Department of Justice is sitting on are fraudulent and were cooked up by Democrats.

As the Trump administration continues to reel from blowback after the DOJ issued a memo saying there is nothing to be learned from the investigation of the well-connected convicted pedophile, the president fired off a rambling post on Truth Social floating the Epstein fraud story .

He later doubled down in multiple appearances throughout the day.

On Thursday morning, Scarborough shared clips of major MAGA influencers not buying Trump's story, with the MSNBC host explaining that Trump's claim of it being a hoax may make his followers question his other hoax claims.

"You just can't say halfway through '25, oh, nothing to see here, move along, move along and if you don't move along after you, said this for five years and believe this for five years, and this was the foundational belief of the MAGA movement, you can't tell those same people that they're stupid that they're excommunicated, and that somehow Joe Biden and Merrick Garland did this," he explained.

"I will also say to you, start throwing hoax around with a Epstein investigation, one of the worst pedophiles in American history, and I don't think anybody doubts that; you start calling that a hoax then people start looking at other things." he elaborated. "So wait a second he's called all these other things hoaxes and we just fell for it hook, line and sinker. If he's now calling the fact that Jeffrey Epstein was a pedophile who got the richest and most powerful men in the world on his plane going to his island, if he's calling that a hoax –– now, when we know it's not a hoax, what else is he not being straight with us about?"

"So it's a very it's a very dangerous strategy and one that I must say that I am baffled by," he concluded.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tangled Web: The Epstein Conundrum

In what reads like a script from a political thriller too convoluted to be believable, Donald Trump's latest maneuvers have plunged his MAGA base into a sea of confusion and skepticism. The centerpiece of this bewildering saga is Trump's recent claims regarding the Jeffrey Epstein files, which he alleges are fraudulent and concocted by Democrats. This narrative, as MSNBC host Joe Scarborough points out, not only adds layers of confusion but potentially exacerbates an already volatile situation.

The Misdirection Play

Trump's attempt to flip the script—asserting that the Department of Justice is harboring bogus Epstein files—reeks of desperation and misdirection. In the shadow of a DOJ memo stating the Epstein investigation would yield no significant findings, Trump's volley of claims on Truth Social and subsequent media appearances seem less like a strategic pivot and more like the flailing of a man grasping at straws.

The Irony of Hoax Accusations

Scarborough's observation that Trump's newfound hoax narrative might lead his followers to question the veracity of his past hoax claims is particularly poignant. Trump's presidency and post-presidency have been marked by a barrage of accusations against others, often labeling unfavorable stories or investigations as hoaxes. Yet, when the script is flipped, and Trump himself calls into question an investigation—especially one as serious and sordid as that of Jeffrey Epstein—it begs the question of consistency and truthfulness.

The MAGA Conundrum

The real kicker here is the quandary in which Trump's MAGA supporters find themselves. Having been fed a steady diet of conspiracy and claims of deep-state manipulation, they're now faced with a perplexing pivot: Trump, their standard-bearer of anti-establishment sentiment, is now seemingly dismissing a narrative that was once part of the MAGA lore. This isn't just about Epstein; it's about the foundation of beliefs that have been built over years.

The Dangers of a Fractured Base

Scarborough rightly points out the danger in Trump's strategy. By undermining a once-central pillar of the MAGA movement's belief system, Trump risks not only alienating his base but also inciting them to question other "truths" they've been led to believe. This isn't merely a political misstep; it's a potential unraveling of the MAGA movement's cohesive narrative.

Conclusion: A Strategy or a Misstep?

Is this a calculated strategy by Trump, or a misstep born from a lack of alternatives? The confusion and backlash from his own supporters suggest the latter. In trying to distance himself from the Epstein saga, Trump may have inadvertently opened a Pandora's box of skepticism among his followers. This isn't just about the legitimacy of the Epstein files; it's a reflection of the broader credibility crisis facing Trump and his movement.

As this drama unfolds, one thing remains clear: the Trump political saga, with its twists and turns, continues to be a source of fascination and frustration. However, this latest episode may prove to be one of its most defining moments, challenging the very core of MAGA's belief system and potentially altering the movement's trajectory in unforeseen ways.
