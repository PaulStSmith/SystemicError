'Shocking': Reagan-appointed judge condemns Trump’s attacks on 'due process' and 'liberty'

President Donald Trump's administration was just handed another stinging loss in court. This time, his defeat came at the hands of a Republican-appointed judge who openly challenged the administration's regard for the Constitution.

On Thursday, U.S. Circuit Court J. Harvie Wilkinson III — who was appointed to the 4th Circuit Court of Appeals by former President Ronald Reagan — shot down an appeal by the administration to impose an emergency stay on an order pertaining to the wrongful deportation of Maryland resident Kilmar Abrego Garcia. The Trump Department of Justice sought to halt an order put in place by U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis (a Barack Obama appointee) to "facilitate" the return of Abrego Garcia from the notorious maximum security prison in El Salvador where he has been incarcerated for more than a month without trial.

Wilkinson's opinion, which was joined by the other two judges on the 4th Circuit panel, tore into the administration for its assertion that Abrego Garcia didn't deserve to have his day in court despite the allegations against him. He added that the 4th Circuit panel's ruling was in line with the 9-0 decision the Supreme Court of the United States (SCOTUS) had already handed down.

"It is difficult in some cases to get to the very heart of the matter. But in this case, it is not hard at all," Wilkinson wrote. "The government is asserting a right to stash away residents of this country in foreign prisons without the semblance of due process that is the foundation of our constitutional order."

"Further, it claims in essence that because it has rid itself of custody that there is nothing that can be done," he continued. "This should be shocking not only to judges, but to the intuitive sense of liberty that Americans far removed from courthouses still hold dear."

The conservative jurist acknowledged that the DOJ asserted that Abrego Garcia was a member of the MS-13 gang, and used that claim to further underscore the need for due process in his case. Wilkinson also noted in his opinion that the administration had already admitted that it deported Abrego Garcia to El Salvador by mistake, and asked: "Why then should it not make what was wrong, right?"

Following its defeat at the hands of the 4th Circuit panel, the administration now has the choice to either comply with Xinis' prior ruling, or appeal to the full 4th Circuit in what's known as an en banc petition. Should the full 4th Circuit decline to take the case, the Trump administration's last avenue would be SCOTUS. However, SCOTUS intervening is unlikely, given its prior 9-0 ruling that the administration bring Abrego Garcia back to U.S. soil.

Our Analysis:

Trump Administration's Legal Defeat: A Testament to Constitutional Disregard

In what can only be described as a resounding defeat for President Donald Trump's administration, the U.S. Circuit Court has delivered a ruling that not only challenges the administration's actions but also its apparent disregard for the Constitution. The administration's attempt to impose an emergency stay on an order concerning the wrongful deportation of Kilmar Abrego Garcia was decisively shot down by J. Harvie Wilkinson III, a judge appointed by none other than former President Ronald Reagan. This decision is a stark reminder of the administration's failure to uphold constitutional values, a failure pointed out by a judge from its own political lineage.

The Core of the Matter

Wilkinson's ruling gets to the very heart of what it means to respect the constitutional rights of individuals. The Trump administration sought to keep Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident wrongfully deported, in a maximum security prison in El Salvador without trial. Wilkinson, in his opinion, underscored a fundamental principle: "The government is asserting a right to stash away residents of this country in foreign prisons without the semblance of due process that is the foundation of our constitutional order." This sentence alone should raise alarm bells about the administration's understanding and commitment to due process—a cornerstone of the American legal system.

Due Process: A Non-Negotiable Principle

The administration's actions, as highlighted by the conservative jurist Wilkinson, demonstrate a shocking disregard for due process. Despite acknowledging the wrongful deportation of Abrego Garcia, the administration's reluctance to rectify its mistake is telling. It’s a clear testament to a governance philosophy that seems to believe in the ends justifying the means, irrespective of the legal and moral costs. Wilkinson's poignant question, "Why then should it not make what was wrong, right?" reflects not only legal wisdom but a moral compass seemingly absent in the current administration's decision-making processes.

A Victory for Constitutional Order

The ruling by Wilkinson, supported by the other judges on the 4th Circuit panel, is not just a legal victory for Kilmar Abrego Garcia; it's a victory for the constitutional order and the principle of due process in the United States. It sends a strong message to the administration: disregard for the Constitution will not be tolerated, even by those within its political sphere. The administration's loss in court serves as a critical reminder of the judiciary's role as a bulwark against the erosion of constitutional rights.

The Path Forward

The administration now faces a choice: comply with the previous ruling by U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis to facilitate Abrego Garcia's return, or escalate the matter further, potentially to the Supreme Court of the United States (SCOTUS). However, given SCOTUS's prior unanimous decision in line with ensuring Abrego Garcia's return to U.S. soil, the administration's chances of a different outcome appear slim.

This court ruling is more than a legal setback for the Trump administration; it's an indictment of its approach to governance and legal affairs. In their quest for policy victories, they have too often skirted the edges of constitutional propriety, only to be rebuked by the very framework they swear to uphold. Wilkinson's ruling is a clarion call for a return to constitutional fidelity, and a reminder that in the United States, due process is not just a legal requirement—it's a fundamental right.
