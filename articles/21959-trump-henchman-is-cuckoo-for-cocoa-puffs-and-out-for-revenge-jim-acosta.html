Trump henchman is ‘cuckoo for cocoa puffs’ and out for revenge: Jim Acosta

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Didn’t Appoint an Attorney General. He Installed an Enforcer.

Todd Blanche’s confirmation is not a normal staffing decision. It is Donald Trump putting his own lawyer in charge of the Justice Department while Republican senators pretended they were voting on something else. The same political class that mouths about law and order is protecting a president who wants taxpayer money for January 6 rioters and a Justice Department tuned to his personal revenge list.

The Real Center of Power

The source text makes the hierarchy plain: Trump is the decision-maker, Blanche is the instrument, and Senate Republicans are the enablers. Blanche’s confirmation matters because he is not being sent in to constrain Trump. He is there to carry out Trump’s agenda inside the one department that can still be used to punish enemies, reward loyalists, and launder political cruelty through official procedure.

That is the real story. The attorney general is supposed to serve the law. Here, the office is being converted into a loyalty assignment.

The Slush Fund Is the Point

Republicans are treating the so-called “weaponization fund” as a side issue, a weird little scandal to be managed with vague assurances and procedural smoke. That is the misdirection. The source says Trump has repeatedly said he wants the fund and believes January 6 rioters “deserve taxpayer money.” That is not confusion. That is political patronage for insurrectionists.

The function is simple: keep the base on the hook by promising government largesse for people who attacked the Capitol on Trump’s behalf. It tells supporters that violence in service of the leader gets rewarded, not punished. That is not a policy dispute. It is moral rot with an appropriations angle.

Senate Republicans Chose This

Michael Steele’s point is the one the media should not soften: Republican senators know this is alive, and they confirmed Blanche anyway. They are not fooled. They are complicit. The source explicitly says they know the fund is still in play and have no real objection because it serves Trump and his base.

That matters because it strips away the favorite excuse of institutional cowardice: that everyone is trapped, confused, or waiting for clearer evidence. No. They see exactly what this is. They voted for it anyway.

Justice as Retaliation

Acosta’s warning is the sharper one: a Trump personal attorney now sits atop the Justice Department for the next two years, and the likely use case is political retaliation. The source points to renewed pressure on “perceived political enemies” and even a possible misdemeanor charge in the Reflecting Pool case against David Hearn. The message is unmistakable: law enforcement under Trump is being refitted as a vendetta machine.

This is how authoritarian politics works in practice. Not just rallies and slogans, but paperwork, indictments, and selective enforcement. The cruelty is bureaucratized so it can wear a badge and claim legitimacy.

Democrats Should Hit This Harder

If Democrats have any political instinct left, they should not treat this as one item among many. The source is right that they have a clean contrast: Trump protecting January 6 criminals, Trump’s attorney running Justice, Trump using the state for loyalists and enemies alike. That is the material. Use it.

But the deeper failure would be to frame this only as a messaging opportunity. It is also a test of whether Democrats understand that democracy is defended by naming power correctly. The danger is not “polarization.” The danger is a president using Republican institutions to convert insurrection into entitlement and justice into punishment.

The Pattern

This story is bigger than Blanche. It is the normalization of open corruption through official forms. Trump demands loyalty, Republicans supply cover, and the state is bent toward rewarding violence committed on his behalf while threatening opponents who stand in the way. The packaging changes; the structure stays the same.

That is the pattern: authoritarian politics does not always arrive with a coup. Sometimes it arrives with a Senate vote, a press appearance, and a government paycheck for the people who tried to overturn an election.
