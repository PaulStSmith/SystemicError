Blanche's loyalty to Trump sends him to a dark place as judge issues stunning rebuke

Todd Blanche Is Not Serving the Justice Department. He Is Serving Trump.

The Real Defendant

Judge Kathleen Williams did not discover a complicated legal misunderstanding. She found a sham. Trump’s supposed $10 billion IRS settlement was collusive because Trump controlled the executive branch and therefore controlled both sides of the case. The government raised none of its available defenses, the court saw no real adversary, and the judge sanctioned the lawyers who helped dress up a private protection racket as litigation.

That is the basic fact pattern. Everything else is ornament.

Power Was Never Disputed

The only institution with actual power here was the Trump White House, including the IRS and the DOJ. That is why the “settlement” could not function like a normal dispute. A president cannot sue the government he controls, then pretend the government independently negotiated against him, then use that fake bargain to launder away scrutiny.

The source makes the central issue plain: this was not a legal disagreement resolved by law. It was executive power being used to erase executive accountability. The release order, which purported to block audits and investigations, is the kind of move that only makes sense in a system where the people in charge think the law is just another instrument for self-protection.

Blanche’s Job Description Is Corruption

Todd Blanche is not drifting into ethical gray areas. He is acting like a man whose assignment is loyalty, not law. The source lays out the record: he interviewed Ghislaine Maxwell while she was imprisoned, helped manage the incomplete release of the Epstein files, led prosecutions of Trump’s enemies, and publicly defended ICE agents after they killed innocent Americans.

That last detail matters. ICE is not a problem of a few bad officers or bad training. It is an apparatus built for coercion, terror, and political enforcement. If Blanche is the kind of lawyer who defends that machinery after it kills innocent people, the answer is not reform theater. The answer is abolition.

His signature on the IRS release order is the sharper point. Blanche was not a neutral lawyer around the edges of a bad deal. Judge Williams found that his role showed he could speak for both sides of the case, which is another way of saying there was only one side: Trump’s.

The Court Saw Through the Theater

The judge’s ruling stripped away the usual legal fog. She said Blanche’s congressional testimony was “misleading” at best and “disingenuous” at worst. She also noted that, despite his prior representation of Trump, he did not recuse himself. That is not a technical lapse. It is a declaration of allegiance.

The paper trail is the story. Alejandro Brito got referred to the Florida Bar. Daniel Z. Epstein was blocked from pro hac vice admission in the district for a year or until further order. Stanley Woodward Jr., who signed for DOJ, is already facing disciplinary proceedings in Washington, and the court sent its ruling to the D.C. bar. These are not isolated clerical scoldings. They are judicial recognition that the lawyers handling this matter treated the court as a prop in a political protection scheme.

The release order’s promised immunity for Trump, his family, and related parties makes the motive impossible to miss. This was not about resolving a tax matter. It was about clearing the president and his circle from long-running scrutiny and making the state pretend it had never existed.

Republicans Are Enabling the Same Old Crime

Sen. John Kennedy’s exchange with Blanche was revealing because Blanche’s first answer was the honest one: “I’m his lawyer.” The correction to “was” did not improve anything. It only exposed the performance. Blanche’s political career is built on the fiction that personal loyalty to Trump can be repackaged as public service.

And the Republican Senate will probably do what it usually does when faced with outright disqualification: fold. The source is right to call Blanche blatantly unqualified for attorney general. A party serious about law would not even entertain a nominee who has turned government offices into instruments of one man’s immunity project. But the modern Republican conference is not organized around law. It is organized around permission.

The Systemic Error

This story is not mainly about one lawyer’s disgrace, though Blanche has earned plenty of it. It is about how authoritarian politics recruits legal professionals to convert abuse into procedure. The trick is always the same: seize institutional power, erase the adversary, fabricate a record of legitimacy, and then ask the public to respect the result.

That is the broader lesson here. Trump does not merely attract unethical lawyers. He depends on them. Blanche’s rise shows what happens when legal talent is put in service of personal rule instead of public obligation: the law stops restraining power and starts manufacturing cover for it. That is how a republic decays, not in a single dramatic break, but through a steady chain of lawyers, senators, and officials willing to call a rigged outcome normal.
