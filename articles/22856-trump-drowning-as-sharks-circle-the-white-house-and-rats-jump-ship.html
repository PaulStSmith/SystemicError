Trump drowning as sharks circle the White House and rats jump ship

Based on an article by Roxanne Cooper

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20263 min read

The West Wing Is Not Empty. It Is Being Looted.

The Real Story

Semafor’s report says the West Wing is shedding staff, that key aides are leaving, and that Markwayne Mullin used Susie Wiles’s absence to slip an ICE nominee directly to Trump, who then announced it on Truth Social without the usual internal filter. That is the context. The political meaning is sharper: the problem is not that Trump has been “left exposed.” The problem is that power in his orbit has been deliberately collapsed into a smaller, more pliable circle that rewards personal access over institutional process.

Power Did Not Disappear

Institutional power still sits with Trump and the people closest enough to manage him: Wiles, Stephen Miller, Steven Cheung, JD Vance, Marco Rubio. That is the operating center. The departures of Karoline Leavitt, James Blair, James Braid, and David Warrington matter, but not because they reveal a vacuum. They matter because every departure narrows the gate, and narrowing the gate increases the value of being inside it.

The article’s framing treats this as a stability crisis caused by turnover. That is too gentle. This is not a bureaucracy losing capacity by accident. It is a presidency organized around personal loyalty, constant access, and the conversion of proximity into leverage.

The ICE Example Tells the Truth

Mullin did not have to convince a process. He waited for a gap and went around the process. He pitched Trump directly on a nominee for ICE who was tied to Mullin’s own security detail in Oklahoma. That detail is the point. The nomination was not emerging from public need, policy design, or even ordinary administrative procedure. It was an inside move, a patronage play dressed up as personnel management.

If this story has a villain, it is not confusion. It is opportunism. Mullin found the opening. Trump rewarded the approach. The system is built to make that possible.

Gatekeeping Was Never Neutral

The report describes the inner circle as “carefully managed,” as if the gatekeepers were protecting the presidency from chaos. Maybe. But gatekeeping in this White House is not democratic accountability. It is a private filtration system for a private regime. When the guardrails are weaker, the danger is not simply that “rogue influencers” get in. The danger is that the presidency becomes even more dependent on whoever can stay physically close, flatter effectively, and exploit the president’s attention span.

The source’s reference to Trump’s supposed inability to keep track of people he does not see regularly may be colorful, but the deeper point is institutional, not psychological. A president who operates through constant visibility and shrinking trust circles is easy to manipulate because the office has been stripped down to a loyalty machine.

ICE Means Cruelty, Not Reform

The fact that this scramble centers on ICE should remove any illusion about what kind of government this is trying to be. ICE is not a normal agency that merely needs better staffing or cleaner lines of communication. It is a coercive arm of the state that has become central to punitive immigration politics. If a White House can casually turn an ICE appointment into a personal favor chain, the answer is not “better vetting.” The answer is abolition. An agency built for surveillance, detention, and terror should not be polished into legitimacy by personnel reform theater.

This is exactly how authoritarian systems operate: they do not only rely on lawlessness. They rely on normalized cruelty, then distribute access to that cruelty through loyalists, patrons, and aides.

The Pattern Is Capture

The larger pattern here is not disorder. It is capture. Trump’s orbit is shrinking, but shrinking does not mean weakening. It means the remaining players have more power, fewer checks, and more incentive to use the president as an instrument for their own aims. The article is right to sense danger, but it misidentifies the source. The threat is not mainly that outside sharks are circling. The threat is that the office itself has been redesigned so that sharks can thrive whenever the staff thins out.

That is the governing logic of this era: fewer rules, fewer intermediaries, more personal leverage, and more punishment for anyone outside the favored circle. The country does not need to wonder whether this is stable. It needs to recognize that instability is part of the method.
