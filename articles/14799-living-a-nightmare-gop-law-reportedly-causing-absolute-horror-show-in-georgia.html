'Living a nightmare': GOP law reportedly causing 'absolute horror show' in Georgia

The body of a legally dead woman is being forced to carry her baby to term, and Washington Post columnist Monica Hesse wants everyone to know “the body of Adriana Smith is being put through hell.”

Adriana Smith was a 30-year-old Atlanta nurse who died after an unsuccessful surgery to remove blood clots in her brain. She had one young child, but Hesse believes “what happened next turned the situation from a tragedy into an absolute horror show.”

According to local reports, her family was told she was not allowed to actually die because she was nine weeks pregnant at the time.

The hospital believes removing Smith’s life support would violate Georgia’s antiabortion laws.

“Georgia’s law does have an exception to save the life of the mother,” Hesse said, “but it apparently doesn’t apply to Smith’s scenario because her life cannot be saved. It is already over.”

“This is torture for me,” Smith's mom, April Newkirk told the local television station. “I see my daughter breathing by the ventilator but she’s not there.”

The baby is now 21 weeks along and has fluid in his brain.

Republican state Senator Ed Setzler told the Associated Press, “The hospital is acting appropriately.” He later added, “I think there’s a valuable human life that we have an opportunity to save, and I think it’s the right thing to save it.”

Setzler sponsored Georgia’s 2019 abortion law.

“This is hideous,” Hesse said. “This is hideous. The person who is not currently being treated as human is Adriana Smith.”

She went on to say, “This family is living out a nightmare in which they must watch her belly swell, as their own emotions rise and the hell they are in becomes more fraught and complicated with each passing hour.”

“Sweet Lord, it turns out I can still be shocked. It turns out I can still be stunned and infuriated by the audacity with which some lawmakers will decide that the lives of women do not matter,” Hesse added. “By the absolutely perverse definitions of life and dignity and religion that they use to justify this grotesquerie.”

She ended her column saying, “Gilead. Gilead. Gilead," in reference to the popular Handmaid's Tale.

Our Analysis:

The Grim Reality of Georgia's Abortion Laws: Adriana Smith's Tragedy

In a distressing turn of events that sounds more like a dystopian novel than reality, Adriana Smith, a 30-year-old Atlanta nurse, has become the posthumous centerpiece of a deeply troubling legal and ethical debate. Following an unsuccessful surgery to remove blood clots in her brain, Smith was declared legally dead. However, the nightmare for her family began when they were informed that Smith's body could not be allowed to "die" in the traditional sense because she was nine weeks pregnant.

A Legal Quagmire

Georgia's stringent antiabortion laws have thrust Smith's family into an unimaginable situation. Despite the law having an exception to save the life of the mother, this provision grotesquely does not apply to Smith, as her life is considered already over. This leaves us to question: When did the rights of the unborn eclipse the dignity and autonomy of the dead?

State Senator Ed Setzler, a sponsor of the 2019 abortion law, defends the hospital's actions, suggesting that preserving the unborn child's life is the "right thing" to do. This stance not only disregards the trauma of Smith's family but also raises profound ethical concerns about the use of a deceased individual's body against their family's wishes.

The Human Cost

Monica Hesse of the Washington Post rightfully labels the situation a "horror show." Smith's family is subjected to the daily torment of watching her body being used as an incubator, a scenario that strips Smith of her humanity and reduces her to a vessel. The baby, now 21 weeks along and suffering from fluid in the brain, is at the center of a heart-wrenching medical and ethical dilemma.

The chilling reality here is not just the application of the law but the cold, calculated manner in which lawmakers like Setzler defend it. The disregard for the psychological torture Smith's family endures daily is appalling. The insistence on prioritizing potential life over the clear and present suffering of the living is a perverse interpretation of justice and morality.

A Reflection of Societal Values?

Hesse's comparison to The Handmaid's Tale is apt, not for its dramatic flair but for the uncomfortable mirror it holds up to our society. How far are we from Gilead, really, when a woman's autonomy over her body is posthumously stripped away by the state? This isn't just about abortion; it's about dignity in death, the rights of the deceased, and the trauma inflicted on the living by draconian laws.

Smith's story is a tragic intersection of flawed healthcare, rigid laws, and a lack of empathy for the grieving. It's a stark reminder of the implications of legislating women's bodies and the ripple effects on their families.

In Conclusion

We must question the morality of a society that forces a family to endure such a nightmare. The grotesque spectacle of a body being kept alive solely for the continuation of a pregnancy is a scenario that should give any reasonable person pause. It's a glaring indication that our laws and, by extension, our societal values, are profoundly out of step with the principles of compassion, autonomy, and dignity.

Adriana Smith's story is not just tragic; it's a resounding alarm about the direction in which we're headed. It's time to reassess not just our laws but the underlying values they represent.
