'It's just not right': Lawmakers sound the alarm on sneaky Pentagon move

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

The Pentagon Wants AI, the Public Gets the Bill

The Decision Is Already Made

The Pentagon is already leasing land to private companies so they can build data centers on military bases. That is the real story. Not the hearings. Not the amendments. The decision-making machinery is moving first, and Congress is being invited in afterward, once the contracts, siting choices, and resource demands are already taking shape.

Water, Power, Silence

Lawmakers are focused on the obvious material cost: these facilities devour water and energy. Rep. John Garamendi is right to name them as extraordinary consumers. Rep. Mike Levin is right to say communities should not be forced to absorb the burden as a fait accompli. But the deeper scandal is not merely consumption. It is the normalizing of a model where public land and public infrastructure are converted into private AI capacity while the surrounding public is treated as collateral.

The Briefing Problem Is the Power Problem

Levin said Congress has not received an appropriate brief from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth or the Pentagon. That is not a procedural footnote. It is the governing method. The Pentagon is pushing ahead quietly, and oversight is being reduced to after-the-fact irritation. When an agency with military authority and massive procurement power withholds basic information while partnering with private companies, that is not confusion. It is controlled opacity.

Private Gain, Public Scarcity

Military bases are not being repurposed to solve a public need. They are being used to house energy-hungry infrastructure for a technology sector that wants scale, speed, and insulation from local resistance. The communities near these bases are left to deal with water use, grid stress, and land-use consequences. The profits accrue upward; the costs stay local. That is the pattern. The language of innovation is just the varnish.

Congress Is Catching Up to a Machine It Still Lets Run

Garamendi’s proposed amendments would force briefings before construction starts and annual reports afterward. That is a minimal floor for democratic control, not a solution. It is Congress trying to regain visibility after the Pentagon and its private partners have already started building the future in the dark. Marcy Kaptur is right: this Congress has an obligation to defend people, not behave as if it is still 1946. The problem is that the Pentagon is already acting like oversight is optional.

The Larger Pattern

This is what state power looks like when it is fused to private technological extraction: secrecy at the top, resource loss at the bottom, and democratic review treated as an inconvenience. The public is told the issue is whether there is enough water and energy. The real issue is who gets to decide that those resources should be diverted, on whose land, for whose profit, and without telling Congress in time to matter.
