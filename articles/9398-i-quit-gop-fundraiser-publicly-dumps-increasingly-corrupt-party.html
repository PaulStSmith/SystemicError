Taking a parting shot in a column for The Bulwark Monday, Miles Bruner, who served as a senior fundraising strategist for leading Republican digital fundraising firm Campaign Solutions for the past five years, announced he is stepping away from helping the Republican Party — and urged others to follow him.

Admitting his walking away won’t shake the GOP to its core, he began by stating, “Since Donald Trump descended that golden escalator in 2015, the Republican Party has devolved into a cult of personality that mirrors the worst authoritarian regimes of the last 100 years.”

He added the GOP has become “increasingly corrupt.”

“While Trump and his supporters in Congress have been the driving force behind the right’s descent into despotism,” he wrote, it has been possible because people like himself — and he can no longer be party to that.

“I worked inside GOP circles through Trump’s takeover of the party, his initial downfall, and his resurgence in 2023–2024. At every step along the way, I rationalized, compartmentalized, and found excuses to stay tethered to the party, even as I grew to believe it was undermining the foundations of our constitutional republic,” he wrote, before adding, “I have decided I have finally had enough.”

“My ego is not so large that I believe I played a significant role in putting Trump into office,” he admitted before suggesting that others like him need “to follow their consciences and leave with their integrity still intact.”

“I’m fully aware that any excuses at this point won’t justify why I stayed so long and will only sound like thin attempts at rationalization to alleviate my personal guilt. Maybe they are. I know that the fact that I’ve finally taken action doesn’t on its own bring me absolution. That’s not what I’m looking for here. My intention rather is to try to explain how it is that people who know better don’t act on that belief—to illuminate how we justify our contributions to the degradation of our politics in real time,” he wrote, before making his plea to his colleagues.

“For those who remain and who harbor doubts about what the Republican Party has become, this is my message to you: I know the thought of walking away from your career and your familiar social network is terrifying. I urge you to recognize that our nation is heading down a very dark path. But it’s not too late to change direction,” he wrote. “If you believe in this country, now is the time to refuse to ferry its destruction for a tainted livelihood. Take a stand. Speak out. Show your pride as an American who believes in the Constitution and the values we grew up with.”

Our Analysis:

The Great GOP Exodus: A Strategist’s Conscience Awakens

In what can only be described as a thunderous revelation of conscience, Miles Bruner, a long-time Republican strategist and fundraising maestro, has dramatically declared his departure from the GOP. His public denouncement, wrapped in a cloak of disillusionment and moral awakening, was aired in a column for The Bulwark. But let’s dissect this, shall we? Because while Bruner’s exit might seem like a heroic leap into integrity for some, it raises eyebrows and questions about the timing, the motivations, and the broader implications for the GOP and the political landscape at large.

The Cult of Personality

Bruner’s assertion that the Republican Party has devolved into a cult of personality under Donald Trump is neither groundbreaking nor revelatory. The transformation of the GOP into a vehicle for Trumpism has been a glaringly obvious shift, marked by unwavering loyalty to the individual over longstanding party principles. Yet, Bruner’s comparison of the GOP to “the worst authoritarian regimes of the last 100 years” is a bold statement that underscores the severity of the party’s metamorphosis under Trump’s dominion.

A Complicit Silence

One of the most biting revelations from Bruner’s departure is his admission of complicity. For years, he remained tethered to the GOP, rationalizing and compartmentalizing as the party veered off the rails. His candid acknowledgment that he, and others like him, were instrumental in enabling the party’s descent into what he now deems despotism, is a stinging indictment of the moral compromises made by party insiders.

However, while Bruner’s introspection might be commendable, it also begs the question: Why now? His epiphany comes after years of benefiting from the very machine he now criticizes. This delayed reaction to a long-evident crisis within the GOP speaks volumes about the political calculus that often trumps moral clarity in the high stakes world of political strategy.

The Call to Arms

Perhaps the most intriguing aspect of Bruner’s departure is his plea to his former colleagues. His call for others to follow his lead, to abandon ship before it’s too late, is a dramatic stand against what he perceives as the GOP’s dark trajectory. But is this rallying cry too little, too late? The GOP’s embrace of Trumpism isn’t a new development; it’s a well-entrenched reality. Bruner’s appeal, while noble, may fall on deaf ears, especially among those who’ve grown accustomed to the power dynamics and financial rewards the Trump era has afforded them.

The Bigger Picture

At its core, Bruner’s exit and public denouncement of the GOP signal a deeper, systemic issue within American politics: the erosion of ideological integrity in favor of personal ambition and party loyalty. While Bruner’s move is a significant personal stand, the impact on the GOP’s trajectory remains to be seen.

In the grand scheme of things, one strategist’s departure might not shake the foundations of the Republican Party. However, it does shine a light on the internal conflicts and moral reckonings taking place within. Whether Bruner’s exit will inspire a wave of similar defections or merely serve as a footnote in the saga of the GOP’s transformation is an open question.

Conclusion

Miles Bruner’s departure from the GOP is a fascinating development, rich with implications for the party’s future and the broader political discourse. While his newfound moral clarity is laudable, it also underscores the complexities of navigating a political landscape where personal integrity often clashes with professional allegiance. As the GOP continues to grapple with its identity in the post-Trump era, Bruner’s exit serves as a reminder of the personal toll exacted by political complicity.
