Mike Johnson is playing a 'silly mind game' with spending bill: former GOP chair

By Paulo Santos

Former RNC chairman Michael Steele railed against House Speaker Mike Johnson, saying the speaker is playing a “silly mind game” with his defense of “the big, beautiful bill.”

The comment came Friday afternoon after anchor Chris Jansing played a sound of Johnson replying to criticism from Elon Musk.

“I sent my good friend Elon a long text message last night,” the House speaker said on Fox News Thursday, “explaining that it can be both big and beautiful. Okay?”

He added, “So I wanted to make sure that he understands that the projection that he's referring to ... is from the CBO, the congressional budget office, they are historically totally unreliable.”

Jansing said, “NBC News reports that Musk's comments have served to embolden conservative critics of that multitrillion-dollar package as it gets taken up in the Senate.”

The anchor went on to ask, “Do you think that really has any influence? I mean, when you consider the influence Donald Trump has?”

“What is the speaker talking about? The CBO is totally historically unreliable,” Steele rhetorically asked. “Why do you have them? Defund the operation. You want to save some money, get rid of an institution that doesn't provide you with good information. What are you talking about?”

The former RNC chair then got animated, “I mean, this is all part of the silly mind game that's being played here. Look, the Senate will eventually capitulate and give Donald Trump probably most of, if not all, of what he wants.”

“The cuts are going to be made, and then they're going to have to go out to the country and explain to our poor and those who are in dire need of health care.” Steele added the GOP will also need to answer, “Why those services that they rely on, like food and SNAP programs, health care programs, have been cut. That's the Republicans' reality right now.”

“You can talk about a big, beautiful bill all day, but at the end of the day, ... the weight of that bill is going to land on the back of the very constituents that you clearly don't care about.”

Steele later said the House is “wholly feckless in stopping any of this.” He then criticized, “Musk, who's got the chainsaw, and doing his thing. And Trump, who's sitting back, letting those two pieces go without any of the dirt falling on him.”

“That's the drama in front of us as I see it,” Steele said.

Our Analysis:

The Great Pretense: Unmasking the Farce Behind the "Big, Beautiful Bill"

In a political landscape that often resembles a tragicomedy, the latest act involves former RNC chairman Michael Steele's vehement critique of House Speaker Mike Johnson and the contentious multitrillion-dollar package that has become the center of a peculiar tug-of-war. Steele's comments, delivered with the kind of fervor one might reserve for a Shakespearean betrayal, rip through the veil of political doublespeak to reveal the stark realities and mind games at play. Let's dissect the layers of irony, hypocrisy, and outright delusion that permeate this narrative.

The Irony of Dismissing the CBO

At the heart of Steele's critique is Johnson's dismissal of the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) as "historically totally unreliable." This claim is not just a slap in the face to an institution designed to provide nonpartisan analyses to Congress but a glaring testament to the selective hearing that plagues our political system. Why, indeed, maintain an institution only to disregard its findings when they inconvenience you? The audacity to suggest defunding the CBO as a cost-saving measure, while championing a multitrillion-dollar package, is a hypocrisy that would be hilarious if it weren't so deeply tragic.

Musk, Trump, and the Theater of the Absurd

The article also highlights the peculiar role of Elon Musk, whose comments against the package have apparently emboldened conservative critics. This circus becomes even more bizarre when considering the influence of Donald Trump, who, despite not holding office, seems to wield considerable power over the package's fate. Steele's assertion that the Senate will eventually give Trump "probably most of, if not all, of what he wants" is a damning indictment of the current state of American politics, where the whims of a few overshadow the needs of the many.

The Real Victims of Political Gamesmanship

Perhaps most significant in Steele's commentary is the spotlight on those who will bear the brunt of these political machinations: the poor and those in dire need of healthcare. The discussion of cutting services such as food and SNAP programs and healthcare is a sobering reminder of the human cost of political posturing. Steele's scathing critique of the GOP's willingness to sacrifice the most vulnerable on the altar of political gain is a powerful call to action for those who believe in a government that serves all its citizens, not just the wealthy or influential.

Feckless House and Teflon Trump

Steele's characterization of the House as "wholly feckless in stopping any of this" and his depiction of Musk and Trump as somehow being above the fray, shielded from the consequences of their actions, paints a bleak picture of a political leadership disconnected from the realities of those they serve. The drama, as Steele aptly puts it, is not just in the actions of these individuals but in the collective failure to address the systemic issues that allow such farcical situations to arise in the first place.

Conclusion: The Weight of the "Big, Beautiful Bill"

In conclusion, Steele's commentary is a scathing indictment of the current political moment, marked by gamesmanship, hypocrisy, and a troubling disregard for the well-being of the American people. The "big, beautiful bill" is revealed to be nothing more than a mirage, a promise of prosperity that, in reality, will "land on the back of the very constituents that [politicians] clearly don't care about." This analysis is not just a critique but a clarion call for accountability, transparency, and a recommitment to the principles of democracy and equity. The drama unfolds, and the audience— the American people— must decide whether to applaud or demand a new script.
