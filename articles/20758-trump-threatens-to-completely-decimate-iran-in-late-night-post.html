Trump threatens to 'completely decimate' Iran in late-night post

Based on an article by Roxanne Cooper

The State as Personal Vendetta

Trump Is Not Just Ranting

Late Friday night, Trump used Truth Social to threaten Iran with military destruction, claiming orders had already been given and casting the conflict as a personal matter about attempts on his life. That is the surface event. The real story is that a sitting president, commanding the U.S. war machine, is using state power as a megaphone for retaliation, grievance, and escalation.

Power Was Not Hiding

The institutional power here sits with the U.S. presidency and the military command structure beneath it. The source says Trump claimed orders were already issued, which means this is not a loose online tantrum detached from policy. It is a threat backed by the machinery of war. When the head of state speaks this way, the line between propaganda and command is not rhetorical. It is operational.

The Earlier Crime Matters More

The article gestures at Iranian retaliation while burying the fact that this conflict traces back through Trump’s own killing of Qassem Soleimani in 2020 and the U.S. strikes that launched the current war in early 2026. That sequence matters. It is not “both sides” instability. It is American initiation, American escalation, and then American outrage when the damage returns. The source’s framing risks turning deliberate state violence into a cycle of vague hostility.

Expediency Killed Children

The most damning detail is not Trump’s posturing. It is the CNN-reported strike that killed 168 children in an Iranian school after senior U.S. commanders allegedly ignored warnings that target data was badly outdated. If that account is accurate, this was not a tragic mistake in the abstract. It was an institutional decision to privilege expediency over intelligence and civilian life. That is not confusion. That is command-level recklessness with a body count.

Blame Is Being Managed

The article points to Iranian denials and U.S. accusations, but the stronger pattern is easier to see: powerful actors frame their own escalation as defense and their own violence as necessity. Israeli intelligence becomes a convenient prop for the latest threat. The Trump administration gets to pose as the aggrieved victim while the U.S. military presence expands, cities are destroyed, and civilians pay the price. The misdirection is structural. It shifts attention from the people with launch authority to the people meant to absorb the consequences.

Imperial Logic, Personalized

This is what authoritarian politics looks like when it is fused to empire: personal grievance presented as national security, retaliatory war sold as prudence, civilian death reduced to collateral noise. Trump’s rhetoric is obscene, but it is not an anomaly. It is a distilled expression of a state that treats violence as administration and accountability as an inconvenience.
