'America is gone': Tiny act of kindness illuminates stark new reality of Trump's DC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 15, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

'Crime, bloodshed, bedlam and squalor'‘Just doing my part’Our Analysis:The Stark Contrast Between Human Compassion and Political Indifference

An unhoused man sleeps just outside police tape near the White House on Wednesday. Photo: Matt Laslo/Raw Story

WASHINGTON — Just outside the newly unrolled yellow police tape that encloses Lafayette Square, the green seven-acre public park just north of the White House, a graying African American man bent down to leave a hot Potbelly sandwich and tall store-bought water for a younger white man who lay there, sleeping in the sun.

“Sir, I don’t mean to disturb you,” Michael — a 61-year-old Houston native — said as the man was roused.

“I brought you a sandwich and a drink.”

Michael walked away. Moments later, as Raw Story asked what prompted his act of kindness, the unhoused man sat up and enjoyed the sandwich.

"We're all human," Michael said. "It's a human lying on the street, hungry. It's terrible."

Hunger may remain “terrible” to many Americans, but to President Donald Trump, the homeless themselves need to be, at the very least, hidden far from the public eye.

“The Homeless have to move out, IMMEDIATELY,” the president thumbed on social media over the weekend. “We will give you places to stay, but FAR from the Capital.”

'Crime, bloodshed, bedlam and squalor'

Throughout much of the nation’s capital — especially the hot spots frequented by lobbyists, politicians and tourists — you can hardly tell that earlier this week Trump took control of the Washington Metropolitan Police Department and deployed National Guard troops.

Trump’s promise to “rescue” the nation's capital from “crime, bloodshed, bedlam and squalor” is far from the reality of the city streets.

Murders have dropped 34% this year, compared to 2024.

In May, homelessness data from the District’s Department of Human Services (DHS) showed a “9% decrease from 2024, including an 18.1% decrease among families and a 4.5% decrease among unaccompanied individuals.”

District officials say the latest numbers reveal a 19% decrease from its last count in 2020, before COVID-19 shuttered much of the nation.

Community Partnership, a local advocacy group, says an estimated 798 individuals sleep on D.C. streets any given evening.

Michael, the man who helped the sleeping man in Lafayette Square, said he was in town from Houston, visiting his daughter who just left the Navy and started "one of the coolest jobs" in national security.

Raw Story asked: "What do you think about President Trump coming through and saying, like, 'You don't have a home but you can't stay here'?"

"President Trump is a dictator,” Michael said. “I think that America is gone the way we knew it. It will never come back.

"I think this is martial law with a different name on it. What's the definition of a martial law? It's when the government takes over the functions of the state, and that's exactly what happened yesterday. In detail.”

Eight months into his second term, Trump deployed the National Guard in Los Angeles — ostensibly to deal with protests — and now Washington, despite howls from local and state leaders.

To Michael, it’s just the beginning.

"They're not just here. It's going to be in every state,” he said. “It's clear to me. It's clear to me."

‘Just doing my part’

Washington may be getting its Trumpian makeover, but Michael says his goal will remain to never forget the least of us, especially those suffering homelessness.

"Just doing my part, man,” Michael said. “Nothing special."

"That is special," Raw Story pressed.

"Nothing special.“

Michael shared that he'd had experiences with homelessness himself. “That's why I didn't want to wake him up," he said.

“But when you wake up, there you go. [Meal] right there."

Our Analysis:

The Stark Contrast Between Human Compassion and Political Indifference

In an age where the cacophony of political discord drowns out the soft hum of humanity, a simple act of kindness outside the newly unrolled yellow police tape in Lafayette Square starkly contrasts the cold, hard stance of President Donald Trump towards the homeless population. A 61-year-old man from Houston, known only as Michael, becomes the protagonist in this narrative, offering a hot Potbelly sandwich and a store-bought water to a younger man sleeping in the sun. This gesture of basic human decency, "We're all human," as Michael puts it, becomes a profound statement against the backdrop of Trump's recent declaration: “The Homeless have to move out, IMMEDIATELY.”

Trump's Vision: Out of Sight, Out of Mind

Trump's approach to homelessness — essentially sweeping it under the rug in an attempt to sanitize the view from the Capitol — speaks volumes about his administration's disconnect from the realities of societal issues. The president's directive to relocate the homeless "FAR from the Capital" not only lacks compassion but also practicality and understanding of the root causes of homelessness. It is a cosmetic solution to a systemic problem, one that requires far more than just relocation — it demands comprehensive social, economic, and health support systems.

A Dictatorship Veiled as Public Safety?

Michael's labeling of Trump as a "dictator" and his interpretation of the deployment of the National Guard as "martial law with a different name" might seem hyperbolic at first glance. However, these statements underscore a growing concern among citizens about the erosion of civil liberties and the militarization of public spaces. The blurring lines between national security and authoritarianism are worrying, with Trump's actions in Los Angeles and now Washington serving as a testament to a potentially overreaching executive power.

A Decrease in Homelessness: The Untold Story

Amidst the political maneuvering, it's critical to highlight the factual inaccuracies—or perhaps selective omissions—by the Trump administration. Contrary to the implied lawlessness and decay, the article sheds light on a significant decrease in homelessness in the nation's capital. A 9% decrease from 2024, including an 18.1% decrease among families and a 4.5% decrease among unaccompanied individuals, suggests progress that contradicts Trump's narrative of "crime, bloodshed, bedlam, and squalor." This progress, a result of the efforts of local agencies and advocacy groups, is a narrative inconvenient for an administration thriving on the portrayal of chaos and disorder.

The True Essence of Leadership

At the heart of this article is a tale of two leaders: one, a man with no political power but with a profound understanding of empathy and social responsibility; the other, a man with immense power but seemingly devoid of the fundamental qualities of compassion and leadership. Michael, through his simple act of feeding a hungry man, exemplifies what true leadership looks like. It's not about grandiose gestures or authoritarian dictates but about recognizing the humanity in each other and doing our part to support the least among us.

Conclusion: A Call for Empathy and Action

As we navigate through these tumultuous times, the narrative presented in this article serves as a powerful reminder of the importance of empathy, compassion, and community. While political leaders may choose the path of indifference and exclusion, it is the collective actions of individuals like Michael that weave the fabric of a compassionate society. The contrast couldn't be starker, nor the choice clearer. Will we follow the path laid by those who seek to divide and hide away the uncomfortable truths, or will we choose to face these challenges head-on, with empathy and resolve? The answer to this question will define the soul of our nation for generations to come.
