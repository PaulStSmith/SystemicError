Trump admin cancels jobs report for first time in 12 years

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The BLS Debacle: Trump’s Playbook on Economic Data Manipulation

President Donald Trump’s Bureau of Labor Statistics has announced there will not be an October jobs report — the first time the monthly data has not been released in 12 years.

According to Axios, “The agency’s closure during the record long shutdown prevented officials from collecting all of the key data needed during the month. It comes at a vulnerable moment for the economy, when data like the unemployment rate, which is are based on surveying American households, can be particularly affected.”

BLS says it will include the data from October in the November jobs report, which will be delayed to allow the publication of both, citing precedent from how the agency handled similar challenges during the government shutdown of October 2013.

However, this move comes after the BLS also postponed the September jobs report.

It also comes after months of controversy over how Trump has leaned on the BLS with political demands to make his numbers look as good as possible.

In the summer, Trump fired the agency’s director after routine revisions that showed certain job numbers weaker than expected, and initially tried to install as a replacement E.J. Antoni, a far-right economist who works for the think tank behind the Project 2025 agenda.

However, Trump ultimately backed off this nomination following a bipartisan backlash over what appeared to be an effort to chill any publication of bad news about the economy under the president’s policies.

Our Analysis:

The BLS Debacle: Trump’s Playbook on Economic Data Manipulation

In an unprecedented move that reeks of political interference and sets a dangerous precedent, President Donald Trump’s Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) has announced that it will not release the October jobs report. This marks the first time in 12 years that the monthly data, crucial for gauging the health of the economy, has been withheld from the public. This decision comes on the heels of the agency’s failure to publish the September jobs report, underlining a disturbing pattern of delay and obfuscation.

According to Axios, the reason cited for this omission is the agency’s closure during the “record long shutdown,” which purportedly prevented officials from collecting all the necessary data. While logistical challenges are understandable, the timing and the context in which these delays occur cannot be ignored. They come at a vulnerable moment for the economy, a time when accurate and timely data is indispensable for policymaking and public confidence.

The BLS’s commitment to include the October data in the November report, while citing precedent from the 2013 government shutdown, does little to assuage concerns. This move raises serious questions about transparency and the integrity of economic reporting under the Trump administration. It is essential to recognize that the health of the American economy cannot be measured solely by cherry-picked data or delayed reports that may serve political agendas.

Furthermore, the controversy surrounding Trump’s handling of the BLS adds another layer of concern. The firing of the agency’s director following routine revisions that showed job numbers weaker than expected, and the initial attempt to install E.J. Antoni, a far-right economist, as his replacement, speaks volumes. It reveals a blatant attempt to politicize economic data, which should remain impartial to maintain trust in governmental institutions. The bipartisan backlash that followed Trump’s nomination of Antoni is a rare beacon of hope, suggesting that there are limits to the politicization of economic data that Congress is willing to tolerate.

However, this episode is a stark reminder of the lengths to which the Trump administration is willing to go to manipulate public perception of the economy. The absence of the October jobs report is not just a bureaucratic hiccup; it is emblematic of a broader strategy to control the narrative at the expense of transparency and accountability.

In conclusion, the decision to delay the publication of crucial economic data, coupled with attempts to install politically aligned figures in key statistical roles, undermines the credibility of the Bureau of Labor Statistics. It is a disservice to the American public, who rely on accurate and timely information to understand the state of their economy. As we move forward, it is imperative that the integrity of economic reporting be preserved against political interference, ensuring that data, not spin, guides our understanding and decision-making.
