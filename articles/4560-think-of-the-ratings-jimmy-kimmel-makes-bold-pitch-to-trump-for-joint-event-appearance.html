'Think of the ratings': Jimmy Kimmel makes bold pitch to Trump for joint event appearance

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: Show host Jimmy Kimmel delivers his opening monologue at the 96th Academy Awards in Hollywood, Los Angeles, California, U.S., March 10, 2024. REUTERS/Mike Blake/File Photo

Jimmy Kimmel has made a bold pitch to Donald Trump during the opening monologue of his talk show.

The 58-year-old comedian, who has faced off with the president on a number of occasions since Trump took office, has spoken directly to the 47th POTUS and suggested they have dinner together. Trump earlier this week confirmed he would attend the White House Correspondents Dinner, a gathering he has refused to attend in the past.

Kimmel pitched himself as the host of the dinner, though it does not appear likely that the president will accept his request. The talk show host said, “You know the dinner is usually hosted by a comedian. Al Franken hosted it, Ray Romano, Seth Meyers, Jon Stewart, Conan, Wanda Sykes - I Googled it this morning, turns out I even hosted it back in 2012.

“This year, I guess Trump didn’t want to be made fun of by a comedian, so the host is a mentalist. We’ll have a mentalist and a mental case on stage together.” Oz Pearlman has been confirmed as the host of the White House Correspondents Dinner for 2026.

Kimmel did not appear moved by the decision to hire Pearlman as host, and instead offered his services as an entertainer for the night.

Looking down the barrel of the camera, he said, “This feels like a cop out to me, and I want to address the president directly on this. Mr. President, please, let me host this dinner. I’ve never asked you for anything.

“Can you imagine? You, me, the commissioner of the FCC [Federal Communications Commission], all at a table together? Think of the ratings. I’ll even throw in an award. You like awards. I’ll give you a brand new award. The Corresponding.

“No one will ever get it besides you. Please, give the people what they want for once. This is our destiny together, you and me, let’s make it the greatest, hottest, most spectacular dinner of any kind ever.”

Our Analysis:

The Theatrics of Political Satire: A Closer Look at Kimmel’s Gambit

In an era where the lines between entertainment and politics blur more with each passing day, Jimmy Kimmel’s latest overture to Donald Trump exemplifies this cultural confluence with a dose of irony so thick, you could cut it with a knife. The seasoned comedian, no stranger to political commentary, has extended an olive branch—or perhaps a thorny rose—to the 47th POTUS, suggesting an unlikely dinner rendezvous that sparks more than just culinary curiosity.

The Bold Pitch: Kimmel’s proposition, made during his talk show’s opening monologue, is nothing short of audacious. Directly addressing Trump, he offers to host the White House Correspondents Dinner, an event Trump has famously shunned in the past. The inclusion of a mentalist, Oz Pearlman, as the actual host for 2026, serves only to heighten the absurdity of the situation, with Kimmel quipping about the pairing of a “mentalist and a mental case” on stage.

The Underlying Message: What might at first glance appear as a mere comedic stunt, upon closer examination, reveals layers of critique and societal commentary. Kimmel’s comparison of past comedians who have hosted the dinner—figures like Jon Stewart and Conan O’Brien—to the choice of a mentalist underscores a shift away from direct political satire towards a more oblique, perhaps even sanitized, form of entertainment. This change, subtle yet significant, mirrors broader trends in how political figures engage with the media and, by extension, the public.

The Satirical Edge: Kimmel’s humor, sharp and unapologetic, cuts to the core of contemporary political discourse. By openly mocking the decision to opt for a mentalist over a comedian as a “cop out,” he not only critiques the choice but also the current administration’s apparent aversion to being the butt of jokes. His offer to host, complete with promises of high ratings and a bespoke award for Trump, serves as a cleverly veiled challenge: Can Trump, known for his sensitivity to criticism, withstand the heat of comedic scrutiny?

The Ratings Game: Beyond the jest and jabs lies a poignant reflection on the commodification of political events. Kimmel’s mention of “ratings” and the spectacle of the dinner speaks volumes about the entertainment value now ascribed to political gatherings. In a society where viewership and viral moments often overshadow substantive discourse, Kimmel’s pitch is a reminder of the power dynamics at play, where politicians and entertainers vie for the spotlight, sometimes at the expense of deeper engagement with policy and governance.

Conclusion: Jimmy Kimmel’s bold pitch to Donald Trump is more than just a bid to host a dinner; it’s a satirical masterpiece that holds a mirror up to the state of American politics and media. In a world where the lines between satire and reality are increasingly blurred, Kimmel’s monologue serves as both a critique and a call to action—a reminder that beneath the laughter and ratings, the stakes are profoundly serious. Whether or not this dinner materializes, the conversation it sparks is a testament to the enduring power of satire to challenge, provoke, and ultimately, enlighten.
