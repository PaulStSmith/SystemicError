'Oh, come on': Trump's DOJ hamstrung by loss of credibility with judges

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Erosion of Judicial Deference Under Trump's DOJIn Conclusion

Attorney General Pam Bondi (Photo via Reuters)

The deference that judges have traditionally extended to lawyers representing the Department of Justice (DOJ) has quickly evaporated under Donald Trump and his Attorney General Pam Bondi.

According to a report from the Washington Post, it has become a common occurrence for DOJ lawyers to be admonished byjudges over specious claims, "shoddy work" and their inability to answer simple questions from the bench.

As former federal Judge John E. Jones III, appointed to the bench by President George W. Bush, put it the “deference that judges would give to attorneys from Main Justice is evaporating,” and they have “lost a fair measure of their credibility.”

ALSO READ: 'Sad white boys': Fear as Trump terror adviser shrugs off threat from 'inside the house'

According to the Post's Mark Berman and Jeremy Roebuck, Lawyers defending Trump administration policies are "encountering mounting criticism and frustration from federal judges, a sign of deepening tension between the executive branch and courts weighing its aggressive uses of power," adding the example of U.S. District Judge John D. Bates berating a DOJ lawyer with "Oh, come on," during a hearing this past week.

Noting that Trump told ABC News this past week, “We’re not being treated fairly by all judges,” the Post report pointed out that "the pushback from the bench has come from judges appointed by Republican as well as Democratic presidents — including by Trump himself — suggesting the issue is more about the Justice Department’s evidence and court arguments than judicial activism."

The report goes on to note that frustrated judges "have criticized the statements and behavior of administration officials, accusing them of defying court orders, submitting flimsy evidence, providing inadequate answers to questions and even acting like toddlers."

Our Analysis:

The Erosion of Judicial Deference Under Trump's DOJ

The Washington Post’s recent exposé on the rapidly diminishing respect for Department of Justice (DOJ) lawyers in the courtroom is more than a mere observation; it's a screaming siren for the erosion of legal integrity under the Trump administration. The tenure of Donald Trump and his Attorney General, Pam Bondi, has seemingly transformed the DOJ into an entity that judges view with increasing skepticism and diminishing deference. This is not just a subtle shift in courtroom dynamics; it's a damning indictment of the quality of legal work and the politicization of justice.

"Evaporating" Credibility

The phrase from former federal Judge John E. Jones III that deference for DOJ attorneys is "evaporating" should send chills down the spine of anyone who values the sanctity of the legal process. This isn't about a singular bad day in court; it's a systemic failure. When judges, regardless of their appointing president, express frustration towards DOJ lawyers for what amounts to shoddy work, inability to answer basic questions, and reliance on specious claims, we're not just talking about a decline in professional standards. We are witnessing the crumbling of the very foundation upon which trust in governmental legal proceedings is built.

Bipartisan Bench Backlash

It's crucial to note, as the article does, that the frustration from the bench is not a narrative of partisan judicial activism. The criticism comes from judges appointed by both Republican and Democratic presidents, indicating a universal standard of legal integrity that the current DOJ is failing to meet. Trump's lamentation that “We’re not being treated fairly by all judges,” in light of this, comes off as nothing short of petulant. It's not the fairness of the judges that's in question; it's the competency and credibility of the DOJ's legal work.

Judicial Exasperation

The article paints a vivid picture of exasperation from the judiciary, with instances like U.S. District Judge John D. Bates' incredulous "Oh, come on," serving as a microcosm of the broader judicial sentiment. This isn't merely about judges being difficult; it's about a systemic disrespect for the legal process. When the judiciary accuses administration officials of acting like toddlers, defying court orders, and submitting flimsy evidence, it underscores a disturbing trend towards the erosion of legal norms and the expectation of rigorous, respectful engagement with the courts.

In Conclusion

The Washington Post article does more than just report on a troubling trend; it holds up a mirror to the state of the DOJ under Trump and Bondi, reflecting a department that appears to have strayed far from its mission of upholding justice with integrity and professionalism. This isn't just about a loss of deference; it's about a loss of credibility and trust. The implications of this shift are profound, affecting not just the outcomes of specific cases but the very fabric of American legal and democratic principles. To restore faith in the DOJ, a return to rigorous legal standards and respect for the judiciary is not just advisable; it's imperative.
