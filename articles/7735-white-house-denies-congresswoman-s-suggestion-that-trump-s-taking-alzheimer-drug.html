White House denies congresswoman's suggestion that Trump's taking Alzheimer drug

A Democratic lawmaker’s “curious” theory about President Donald Trump’s health forced an official White House denial.

Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove (D-CA) tweeted last week that the 79-year-old president is showing signs he’s taking the Alzheimer’s disease drug Leqembi, and The Daily Beast’s Farrah Tomazin asked White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt to comment on the congresswoman’s evidence.

“The Alzheimer’s drug Leqembi: Is administered through an infusion (for example, through the hand); Can cause swelling, bleeding, or fluid leakage in the brain, requiring regular MRIs; Can cause tiredness,” Kamlager-Dove had posted on Dec. 4. “Curious.”

When pressed on the topic Thursday, Leavitt claimed the president “is constantly shaking hands” because “the Oval Office is like Grand Central Terminal,” and added that he takes aspirin daily, but the press secretary declined to answer specific questions about Trump’s disclosure that he had undergone three cognitive tests.

“I’m not a doctor, but I do think it’s curious that [Trump’s] drowsiness and visible bruising on his hands are consistent with known symptoms and side effects of Alzheimer’s medications like Leqembi,” Kamlager-Dove told the Daily Beast after the press briefing.

She also reposted a screenshot of her previous post speculating about the president taking Leqembi, which is listed at $26,500 a year and promises to slow down progression of the disease but does not reverse it.

“Hey, I’m just asking questions,” Kamlager-Dove wrote, adding another screenshot of Trump’s rambling Truth Social post boasting that he had aced three dementia tests and warned it was “treasonous” to question his health.

The White House issued an official denial of the congresswoman’s claims.

“It seems as if the Congresswoman has not been taking her medication because she is hallucinating, distorting reality in a clearly troublesome state that requires urgent medical care,” White House spokesperson Steven Cheung told the Daily Beast.

Trump’s father, Fred Trump, suffered from Alzheimer’s disease, which puts the president at risk, and, as Kamlager-Dove pointed out, he has displayed some of the known side effects of Leqembi, which can delay the onset of severe cognitive decline by five months.

The president’s right hand has consistently shown bruising, which is conspicuously covered by makeup, and he has acknowledged MRI exams, which patients would undergo regularly to check for brain swelling and microhemorrhages.

“Leqembi patients may also experience fatigue, particularly after an injection,” The Daily Beast reported. “The president has noticeably struggled to stay awake during public appearances and meetings for weeks. The Alzheimer’s Association says Alzheimer’s patients may experience difficulty sleeping and take daytime naps.”

“Those close to Trump have tried to praise his fitness by boasting about how he rarely sleeps,” the report added.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Allegations and Responses Surrounding President Trump’s Health

In a recent whirlwind of speculation, Rep. Sydney Kamlager-Dove’s comments regarding President Donald Trump’s health have sparked a fiery exchange between the lawmaker and the White House. The congresswoman’s “curious” theory, suggesting that Trump may be taking the Alzheimer’s disease drug Leqembi, has thrust the topic into the public discourse, eliciting a denial that is as fascinating as it is vehement from the White House.

The Speculation

Kamlager-Dove’s observations hinge on a combination of Trump’s physical symptoms (notably bruising on his hands and drowsiness) and his familial history (his father, Fred Trump, suffered from Alzheimer’s). These, coupled with the president’s own admissions of undergoing MRI exams, form the backbone of her speculation. The cost of Leqembi, a staggering $26,500 a year, and its effects, which include the potential for swelling, bleeding, or fluid leakage in the brain, add a layer of intrigue to her claims.

The White House’s Rebuttal

The White House’s response to these allegations was nothing short of theatrical. Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt’s comparison of the Oval Office to “Grand Central Terminal” in a bid to explain Trump’s hand-shaking routine, and the lack of a direct response to the president’s cognitive tests, raise more questions than answers. Meanwhile, spokesperson Steven Cheung’s retort that Kamlager-Dove might be “hallucinating” and in a “troublesome state” is not just a defense but a counterattack, aiming to discredit the congresswoman’s claims by questioning her mental state.

The Underlying Issues

This saga underscores several critical issues in the political and public health spectrum:

  • Transparency and Health in Leadership: The opacity surrounding the health of political leaders isn’t new, but it’s always concerning. The public’s right to know is often at odds with the privacy and strategic interests of political figures.

  • The Stigmatization of Health Conditions: The discourse here dangerously flirts with stigmatizing not just Alzheimer’s but any health condition. The insinuation that having Alzheimer’s or being under its medication disqualifies a leader, without a nuanced understanding of the condition or the individual’s capacity, is problematic.

  • The Politicization of Health Speculations: Kamlager-Dove’s remarks and the White House’s response are emblematic of a broader trend where health speculations are weaponized for political gain. The congresswoman’s “just asking questions” stance and the White House’s aggressive dismissal exemplify how health-related discourse can be manipulated for political ends.

Conclusion

While it’s undeniable that the health of a president is of public concern, the manner in which this concern is addressed by both parties leaves much to be desired. The leap from speculation to accusation and the subsequent leap to disparagement do not contribute to a constructive dialogue.

Both the allegations and the responses serve as a reminder of the intricate dance between privacy, public interest, and political strategy. In the end, the truth about President Trump’s health remains obscured by a fog of political rhetoric, leaving the public in a state of uncertainty and speculation. What is clear, however, is the need for a more respectful and fact-based discourse on such matters, free from the sensationalism and stigmatization that currently pervade it.
