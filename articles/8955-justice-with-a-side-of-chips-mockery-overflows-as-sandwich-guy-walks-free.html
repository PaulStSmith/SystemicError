Dunn had been accused of assault after he threw a Subway sandwich at a federal agent during the President Donald Trump-led occupation of the city.

The case grew increasingly difficult for the Justice Department, which failed to garner the support of a grand jury to indict Dunn on felony assault charges. Ultimately, they dropped the charges to a lower-level misdemeanor — and they still couldn’t get a conviction.

Speaking to MSNBC after the verdict, legal analyst Lisa Rubin said that Dunn’s lawyers were very smart about pointing out the incident became a joke among federal agents. At one point, the victim had a plush sandwich displayed at his desk and a “felony footlong” patch on his lunchbox.

National security analyst Marcy Wheeler pointed to Dunn’s lawyer, saying, “Sabrina Shroff is a hero.”

Dunn was celebrated by legal analysts like Bradley P. Moss, who cheered, “What do we want? Onions and mustard! When do we want it? Now!”

“After the U.S. government refused to give the jury in the sandwich trial any lunch -- not even a few slices of baloney -- they reached a verdict,” lawyer and writer David R. Lurie wrote.

“Give me your wheat bread, your onions and mustard, your sandwich meat …. Yearning to be tossed,” punned Moss in a later post.

“I love DC and DC juries so so so so much,” Slate’s legal analyst Mark Joseph Stern wrote on Bluesky. “Sorry not sorry, we are the best.”

“All hail the Sandwich Guy! Justice has been served with a side of chips,” cheered legal analyst Jen Taub.

“The grand jury refused to indict on felony charges so DOJ brought a misdemeanor. The trial jury acquitted—note: that means that every single person on the jury voted for acquittal. There wasn’t even a single vote to convict,” former prosecutor Joyce Vance said.

“People yakking about Pirro, and other far reaching implications of the Dunn verdict are, in sandwich terms, baloney. The jury acquitted Dunn via a not guilty vote. That’s it. Period. All the clackers imprinting their own thoughts on it are bogus,” lawyer Bill Moore quipped.

“The taxpayers deserve to know how much this colossal waste of time & resources cost us. That includes Jeanne “Liquid Lunch” Pirro’s salary. Trump seems to think we’ll happily pay for everything. Tariffs, legal fees, golf trips, this, housing staff on military bases. Endless waste,” said artist and editor Scott Cohen.

Our Analysis:

The “Sandwich Guy” Verdict: A Mockery of Justice or a Victory for Common Sense?

In the latest episode of what could only be described as the absurd theatre of modern American politics and legal system, Sean Dunn, dubbed Washington, D.C.’s “Sandwich Guy,” has been acquitted of assault charges. The charges stemmed from an incident where Dunn, in an act that reads more like a satirical sketch, threw a Subway sandwich at a federal agent during the tumultuous President Donald Trump-led occupation of the city. This case, which could easily be mistaken for a plotline in a late-night comedy show, has ended not with a bang, but with the whimper of a jury’s laughter.

The Prosecution Fumbles: A Misdemeanor Too Far

Let’s dissect the farce that unfolded. The Justice Department, in a move that reeks of desperation or perhaps a lack of more pressing concerns, attempted to indict Dunn on felony assault charges. Yet, they found themselves unable to convince a grand jury — a collective presumably with a modicum of common sense — to indict Dunn. Reduced to pursuing a misdemeanor charge, one can imagine the prosecutors’ embarrassment as they were forced to argue the grave dangers of airborne sandwiches in a court of law. And still, they failed to secure a conviction.

The Defense’s Winning Strategy: Making a Mockery of a Mockery

Dunn’s defense lawyers, particularly Sabrina Shroff, emerged as the heroes of this narrative — or the heroines of this farce, depending on one’s perspective. They adeptly highlighted the absurdity of the case, pointing out that even the alleged victim seemed to find humor in the situation, sporting a “felony footlong” patch and keeping a plush sandwich at his desk. If the victim can find humor in the incident, what does that say about the severity of the crime?

The Public Reaction: From Legal Analysts to the Twitterati

The public and legal analyst reactions have been a mix of mockery and jubilation, further underscoring the absurdity of the case. Bradley P. Moss’s calls for “Onions and mustard!” and David R. Lurie’s quip about the jury’s lack of a “few slices of baloney” lunch, exemplify the broader mockery. This isn’t just about a sandwich or even about Sean Dunn; it’s a reflection of public sentiment towards what many perceive as a bloated and out-of-touch legal system.

The True Cost: A Waste of Taxpayer Money

Beyond the jokes and social media banter lies a sobering reality highlighted by Scott Cohen — the colossal waste of time and resources spent on prosecuting a case about a thrown sandwich. At a time when the American public grapples with pressing issues such as healthcare, education, and infrastructure, the focus on such a trivial matter is, frankly, an insult to the taxpayer.

Conclusion: The Sandwich Guy and the State of American Justice

The acquittal of Sean Dunn is not just a victory for him or a defeat for the Justice Department; it’s a glaring spotlight on the absurdity that sometimes pervades our legal system. It underscores a growing public perception of the judicial process as occasionally being disconnected from the principles of common sense and proportionality. While legal analysts and the Twitterati may celebrate or mock, the underlying question remains: Are we, as a society, content with our resources being used in such a manner? The “Sandwich Guy” saga may be over, but the taste it leaves behind is one of skepticism and, for many, a yearning for a more sensible allocation of our collective focus and funds.
