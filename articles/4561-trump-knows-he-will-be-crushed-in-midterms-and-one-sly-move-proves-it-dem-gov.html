A pre-midterm move from Donald Trump proves the president knows he will not fare well in the elections, Gavin Newsom has claimed.

During an appearance on Jimmy Kimmel’s talk show, the Governor of California once more repeated his assessment of the midterms - that the GOP will lose massively because of Trump. But the projected wins of the Democratic Party, while not guaranteed, will be aided by a move Trump pulled last year.

Newsom said the party and representatives must “fight fire with fire” when it comes to Trump, and a redistricting success under the Governor of California came as a result of the president’s attempt to rezone Texas ahead of the midterm elections.

This move, Newsom believes, highlights the shortcomings of the Trump administration and his fear of losing the Senate majority. He told Kimmel, “Trump is a historic president. Historically unpopular. He’s going to get crushed, shellacked, in the midterms. He is. He’s toast. And he knows it.

“Why else did he call Greg Abbot, saying he’s ‘entitled’ to five seats in a mid-decade redistricting? They, of course, obliged in Texas. What Trump thought would follow was maybe a conversation about writing an op-ed in California to try to win the argument as they’re consolidating power.

“Instead, we decided to redraw the lines and over a 90-day period raised $120million, and we kicked his a-- in November and redrew the lines. I know we’re all prone to a little hyperbole. This is not hyperbolic.

“You saw what happened on January 6, tried to wreck the country, light democracy on fire, dialed up for 11,000, 12,000 votes in Fulton County. That’s exactly what he’s doing all across the United States.

“Not just as it relates to redistricting, trying to nationalize the election, trying to take those voting roles and purge voters, decide who can vote, and who cannot. Ask the folks in Fulton County what he’s just done. It’s happening in plain sight.”

It comes after Kimmel pitched Trump an appearance at the upcoming White House Correspondents Dinner, which the talk show veteran has hosted in the past.

He said, “Can you imagine? You, me, the commissioner of the FCC [Federal Communications Commission], all at a table together? Think of the ratings. I’ll even throw in an award. You like awards. I’ll give you a brand new award. The Corresponding.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Pre-Midterm Moves: Politics as Usual or Desperation?

In a recent appearance on Jimmy Kimmel’s talk show, California Governor Gavin Newsom took a bold swipe at Donald Trump, suggesting the former president’s actions ahead of the midterm elections stem from a deep-seated fear of losing. Newsom’s critique, wrapped in the typical bravado of late-night television, deserves a dissecting look, not just for its surface-level entertainment but for the deeper political strategies at play.

Trump’s Redistricting Gambit: A Fear-Driven Maneuver?

Newsom claims that Trump’s push for redistricting in Texas, a move aimed at securing five additional seats for the GOP, is a tell-tale sign of his apprehension about the midterms. This action, according to Newsom, underscores Trump’s “historic” unpopularity and his desperate clutch at retaining Senate majority. But let’s not kid ourselves—while it’s easy to cast Trump as the villain of democracy, redistricting is a tool frequently wielded by both sides of the aisle, albeit with varying degrees of shamelessness.

California’s Countermove: Fighting Fire with Fire

The response from Newsom and the Democratic Party in California is particularly noteworthy. Raising $120 million to redraw district lines and “kick Trump’s a--” is framed as a heroic counterstrike against GOP manipulation. However, this narrative conveniently glosses over the fact that such tactics are part of the same political playbook—one that prioritizes power over the principle of fair representation. It’s a stark reminder that in the high-stakes game of politics, moral high ground is often claimed but seldom inhabited.

The Bigger Picture: Democracy in the Crosshairs

Beyond the specifics of redistricting fights, Newsom’s comments touch on a more sinister aspect of Trump’s legacy: the attack on the electoral process. The reference to January 6 and the attempts to “purge voters” illuminates a broader strategy to undermine democracy. While Newsom’s indignation is palpable—and in many ways justified—it’s crucial to recognize that the erosion of democratic norms is not the monopoly of any one party or individual.

Ratings Over Reality: The Kimmel-Trump Proposition

The article concludes with an anecdote about Kimmel pitching Trump an appearance at the White House Correspondents Dinner, a move that feels eerily reminiscent of the media’s role in elevating Trump’s profile in the lead-up to his presidency. This proposition, likely made in jest, inadvertently highlights the dangerous dance between media and politics, where ratings can sometimes eclipse the importance of substantive political discourse.

In conclusion, while Newsom’s critique of Trump provides ample fodder for late-night laughs, it also serves as a mirror reflecting the broader dysfunctions within American politics. Both parties engage in tactics that undermine the democratic process, and the media often plays along, prioritizing spectacle over substance. As observers and participants in this democracy, it’s crucial to look beyond the partisan jabs and recognize the systemic issues at play. Only then can we begin to address the root causes of our political malaise.
