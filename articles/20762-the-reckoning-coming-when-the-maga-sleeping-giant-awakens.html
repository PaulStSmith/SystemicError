The reckoning coming when the MAGA sleeping giant awakens

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

The Economy Was Looted, Then Blame Was Redirected

Midnight Exposes the Con

At the same moment Zohran Mamdani was sworn in on a promise that New York should be a city people can afford, Republicans let enhanced Obamacare subsidies expire for more than twenty million Americans. That contrast is the whole story in miniature: one political camp is trying to make life cheaper, while the other is making it more expensive and then daring voters to confuse cruelty for policy.

The source is right to treat this as a power story, not a personality story. The relevant actors are not abstractions. Republicans refused the subsidy extension. Republican politicians protected donors. Republican governance delivered the bill.

Scapegoats Are the Product

The article’s clearest point is also the ugliest: the right does not answer economic pain with solutions, it monetizes that pain with scapegoats. Immigrants, Black and brown people, queer people, women, “communists,” whoever can be made to stand in front of the camera while the oligarchs empty the vaults. That is not confusion. It is an organized misdirection campaign.

The source cites Scott Bessent’s claim that immigration “artificially suppressed wages,” then shows the damage from that lie. A Brookings analysis found ICE operations wiped out roughly 668,000 jobs, including tens of thousands held by U.S.-born workers. The promised wage boom never arrived. What arrived was disruption, job loss, and another round of propaganda telling workers to blame the nearest vulnerable person instead of the people designing the system.

The Wound Is Real

What makes this politics effective is not that the story is true. It is that the injury is real. The source is blunt about the collapse of the ordinary middle-class life that a union job once made possible: one paycheck, a house, a car, a pension, college for the kids. Reagan-era union destruction, wage freezes, outsourcing, and privatized essentials did that damage.

That matters because it means the base condition here is not ideological hysteria. It is economic dispossession. People are not inventing the fact that rent is crushing them, health care is unaffordable, and the old bargain has been broken. The fraud is the explanation they are handed for who broke it.

The Machine That Keeps the Lie Alive

The article correctly identifies the machinery: billionaires, Fox, social media algorithms, consultants, and donor-driven politics all work to keep workers split into hostile tribes. This is not a side effect of polarization. It is the business model.

The source’s polling numbers make the point even harder to evade. Working-class Trump voters and college-educated Democrats overlap heavily on populist economics, billionaire power, and the need for a system that actually works. The public is not divided on the diagnosis as much as elites would like. It is divided on who gets blamed, because the blame has been professionally rerouted for decades.

Democrats Have a Job to Do

The article is strongest when it treats progressives as the faction naming the real enemy: oligarchic extraction. That is the democratic coalition’s assignment now. Not vibe management. Not donor-friendly triangulation. Not the tired performance of competence while everyday life keeps getting more expensive.

But the source also hints at the Democratic problem without fully saying it: if Democrats are going to be the institutional vehicle against authoritarian politics, they have to behave like one. That means using power to lower costs, strengthen labor, defend public goods, and stop treating affordability politics as a branding exercise. Mamdani’s promise works because it names a material injury. Democrats lose when they replace that clarity with consultant speech and half-measures.

What This Story Really Shows

This is not a tale of two extremes. It is a tale of one governing class and one fighting coalition. On one side, oligarchs and their political servants have spent forty-five years transferring wealth upward and then selling the public a culture war to hide the theft. On the other, there is a democratic politics trying to recover the conditions of a normal life.

The larger pattern is simple: when wages collapse and institutions fail, authoritarian movements do not solve the crisis. They weaponize it. They hand working people enemies who are easier to hate than the people who actually took the money. The task for anyone serious about democracy is to stop accepting the bait and name the theft directly.
