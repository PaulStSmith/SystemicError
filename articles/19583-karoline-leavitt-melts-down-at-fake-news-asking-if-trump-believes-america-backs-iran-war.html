White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt delivered an attack on the media on Wednesday during the first press briefing since the U.S. launched its military operation against Iran five days ago.

NBC News senior White House correspondent Garrett Haake asked Leavitt why President Donald Trump, just days after his State of the Union address, had not laid out the case for why the U.S. had planned with its ally Israel to launch military strikes on Iran. Leavitt blamed the press for conflicting reports over the Trump administration’s objectives in the military action, and what prompted the attacks.

“Does the president believe the country supports the actions that he’s taken so far in Iran?” Haake asked.

Leavitt responded to the question with a swipe at the media, including the reporters at the briefing.

“I think he does. And I think the president knows the country is smart enough to read past many of the fake news headlines produced by people in this room, that this action was unjustifiable,” Leavitt said.

“Again, this is a rogue terrorist regime that has been threatening the United States, our allies and our people for 47 years,” Leavitt argued. “And the American people are smart enough to know that. And they’ve also been smart enough to listen to the president himself, not just over the past year in this second term, but during his first term as president and also for the past 40 years of his life. This is a president who has been remarkably consistent on this issue, that Iran can never obtain a nuclear weapon. And the president tried peace through diplomacy exhaustively and extensively. He and his team gave it their best go.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of White House Press Secretary’s Remarks on Military Operation Against Iran

In a spectacle that has become all too familiar in the corridors of power, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt launched a scathing attack on the media during a press briefing, a mere five days into the U.S. military operation against Iran. This briefing, meant to shed light on the administration’s actions, instead turned into a stage for deflecting criticism and blaming the press for the administration’s communication failures.

The Art of Deflection: Blaming the Media

Leavitt’s response to NBC News senior White House correspondent Garrett Haake’s legitimate query was a textbook example of political deflection. Haake’s question, which pressed for clarity on why President Donald Trump had not provided a detailed justification for the military strikes on Iran, especially so soon after his State of the Union address, was met with outright evasion. Instead of addressing the core of the question, Leavitt accused the media of producing “fake news headlines” and suggested that these were responsible for any public confusion regarding the administration’s objectives in Iran.

This move to shift blame onto the media is a dangerous game that undermines the critical role of the press in a democracy. It’s a tactic designed to divert attention from the administration’s lack of transparency and accountability.

The Rhetoric of Justification: Iran as a “Rogue Terrorist Regime”

Leavitt’s characterization of Iran as a “rogue terrorist regime” that has been threatening the U.S., its allies, and its people for 47 years is a significant rhetorical choice. It serves to paint the military operation not as an aggressive unilateral action but as a defensive measure against a perennial threat. This framing is crucial for the administration, as it seeks to justify its actions in the court of public opinion.

However, the assertion that the American people are “smart enough” to see past media reports and understand the necessity of the operation is both patronizing and overly simplistic. It assumes a unanimous public alignment with the administration’s viewpoint, ignoring the diversity of opinion and the legitimate concerns many Americans have regarding the escalation of military conflict.

Consistency or Stubbornness? Trump’s Stance on Iran

Leavitt praises President Trump for his “remarkable consistency” on the issue of Iran, particularly his stance that “Iran can never obtain a nuclear weapon.” While consistency in policy can be a virtue, it’s essential to distinguish between steadfastness and inflexibility. The claim that Trump and his team have tried “peace through diplomacy exhaustively and extensively” is a contentious one, especially considering the administration’s withdrawal from the Iran nuclear deal in 2018—a move that many experts argue has led to increased tensions and diminished opportunities for diplomatic resolution.

Conclusion: Between Transparency and Transgression

The press briefing serves as a stark reminder of the current administration’s approach to both the media and military action. By attacking the press and offering vague justifications for serious military engagements, the White House is engaging in a dangerous game of obfuscation and blame-shifting. This strategy not only threatens the essential democratic role of the press but also risks alienating the very public it claims to understand and represent.

In the final analysis, the American people deserve clear, honest communication about the actions taken in their name—especially when it comes to matters of war and peace. The administration’s current strategy of evasion and attack does a disservice to the public discourse and to the principles of accountability and transparency that underpin American democracy.
