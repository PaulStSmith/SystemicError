Scott Jennings' repulsive blabber will leave his GOP pals wide open for a knockout punch

The Press Secretary Is Not the Story

The Real Boss

Karoline Leavitt’s rumored exit is being treated like personnel theater, but the actual power structure is unchanged. Donald Trump still sits at the center, choosing who speaks, who gets humiliated, who gets access, and which lie becomes the day’s official version of reality. The White House press secretary is not an independent actor in this setup. She is a disposable interface for a presidency built on domination, loyalty tests, and spectacle.

That is the first thing the coverage obscures when it turns the story into gossip about plane assignments, burnout, or who got to stand closest to Trump. The decision-maker is Trump. The enablers are the people around him who keep the machine moving and the institutions that keep pretending this is normal governance instead of organized degradation.

Spectacle as Cover

The article gestures at a swirl of rumors about a “decoy plane,” doctors, cabinet members, and media choreography. Whether any of that is true is beside the point. The deeper fact is that the Trump operation thrives on confusion. It floods the zone with stories that make access itself seem like news, while the real content of power stays hidden in plain sight: who is protected, who is promoted, who is sacrificed, and how much official machinery is being repurposed for ego management.

That is not accidental miscommunication. It is a method. When the press corps spends its energy parsing travel gossip and staff logistics, it is easier to ignore the larger vandalism: the normalization of a presidency that treats public institutions like props.

Media’s Complicity

The most revealing part of the piece is not the Leavitt gossip. It is the suggestion that Scott Jennings, a CNN commentator described in the source as a loyalist and bully, would be a fitting replacement. That is not a joke about one cable personality. It is a portrait of the media class as an accessory market for authoritarian politics.

CNN does not merely “platform” compromised figures; it trains the audience to accept them as legitimate interpreters of democracy while they recycle right-wing propaganda and perform contempt as expertise. The result is a degraded public square where the line between journalist, pundit, and enabler is so blurred that failure can be rebranded as balance.

Democrats Need a Counterbriefing

The source lands on one useful political instinct: Democrats need their own press operation. That is not a vanity project. It is infrastructure. If Republicans are using the White House podium as a loyalty theater, Democrats should not respond with scattered quotes and reactive press releases. They should build a daily briefing apparatus that speaks plainly, competes for attention, and names the abuse of power without apology.

But this cannot be another consultants-only exercise or an audition for 2028. If Democrats want to be the institutional vehicle against authoritarian politics, they need disciplined communication that treats the public like adults and the opposition like a threat, not a branding opportunity. A serious democratic press operation would not mimic Trump’s circus. It would puncture it.

What This Story Actually Shows

The pattern here is familiar: authoritarian politics depends on a press ecosystem that confuses proximity with relevance, loyalty with competence, and scandal fatigue with inevitability. Trump’s people are interchangeable because the system does not reward skill. It rewards submission. The media then launders that submission into commentary, and the public is left with personality churn instead of accountability.

Leavitt, Jennings, and the rest are not the center of the story. They are symptoms of a political order that converts institutions into stage sets and then dares everyone to call it governance. The real issue is not who holds the podium for a week. It is who controls the machinery behind it, who benefits from the noise, and how long the rest of the country is expected to mistake humiliation for politics.
