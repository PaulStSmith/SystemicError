'More chaos' as Trump ramps up targeting and purging his own appointees: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 31, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Turbulent Tenure: A Legacy of Leadership Chaos

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to reporters ahed of boarding Marine One to depart for New Jersey, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 1, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Among the Trump administration's defining characteristics has been the president’s ire for those appointed to positions of power by past administrations, with President Donald Trump declaring early on in his second term that he would “clean house immediately” of most officials appointed under the Biden administration.

Now, however, Trump has redirected his angst toward his own appointed officials in a pattern of firings that one expert noted surpassed any previous administration in history.

“No administration has seen more chaos in its leadership ranks than the Trump administration other than Trump one,” said Max Stier, an attorney and president of the nonprofit Partnership for Public Service, which tracks government hiring and firing, speaking with the Wall Street Journal Friday. “He begins by being right, and if anyone challenges his worldview, they need to go away.”

The observation of Trump’s chaotic second tenure comes just days after Health and Human Services Secretary RFK Jr. fired Susan Monarez, the Trump-appointed director for the Centers for Disease Control, in a decision that was backed up by the White House. Trump also fired former Rep. Bill Long (R-MO) this month, whom he appointed to head the Internal Revenue Service, just two months into the job.

A number of other high-profile firings of Trump-appointed officials, by Trump, have also taken place within the past seven months; the acting FEMA administrator was removed, the Department of Treasury’s second-in-command was replaced, and two senior Justice Department officials were axed earlier this month.

Trump and his supporters have largely defended the firings, with GOP strategist Brad Todd telling the Wall Street Journal Friday that the president’s leadership shakeups – even when it’s directed at his own appointees – came with Trump’s 2024 election victory.

“The voters pick one person to lead the executive branch and that means that person gets to choose every other person,” Todd said.

The turnover rate during Trump’s first term in office wasas high as 92%as of Jan. 20, 2021, according to extensive tracking by the nonprofit organization the Brookings Institution. And his second term, while only seven months in, has also beenplagued with rising numbers of departures and high-profile firings

Trump's Turbulent Tenure: A Legacy of Leadership Chaos

The Trump administration's hallmark seems to be an unparalleled level of instability and turmoil, particularly when it comes to the president's own appointees. In a move that has become characteristic of his leadership style, President Donald Trump's second term has been marked by a series of high-profile firings that exceed anything witnessed in previous administrations. This pattern not only underscores a deep-seated issue within the administration but also raises questions about the efficacy and stability of Trump's governance.

Firstly, it's crucial to highlight the ironic twist in Trump's administrative strategy. Initially, Trump set his sights on purging officials appointed under the Biden administration, under the guise of "cleaning house." However, the president's focus rapidly shifted towards his own appointees, leading to a series of dismissals that can only be described as self-sabotage. This erratic behavior is not just a reflection of Trump's impulsiveness but a glaring indication of a lack of foresight and strategy in his appointments.

Max Stier's observation that "No administration has seen more chaos in its leadership ranks than the Trump administration" is a damning indictment of the current state of affairs. Stier, an attorney and president of the nonprofit Partnership for Public Service, brings to light the unprecedented level of instability that has come to define Trump's tenure. The fact that this chaos is self-inflicted, with Trump removing his own appointees, speaks volumes about the president's inability to foster a stable and effective team.

The recent firings of high-profile officials, including Health and Human Services Secretary RFK Jr., the director for the Centers for Disease Control, the head of the Internal Revenue Service, and two senior Justice Department officials, are not just personnel changes; they are symptomatic of a deeper issue. These dismissals, backed by the White House, reveal a pattern of governance based on loyalty and agreement with Trump's worldview rather than competence and performance. This approach not only undermines the integrity of the administration but also jeopardizes the functionality of the government.

GOP strategist Brad Todd's defense of the firings, attributing them to Trump's mandate from the 2024 election victory, is a precarious justification for the upheaval. While it's true that the president has the authority to appoint individuals to the executive branch, the constant turnover and firing of high-profile officials suggest a leadership style that is erratic and counterproductive. The argument that "the voters pick one person to lead the executive branch" fails to account for the need for stability, consistency, and expertise in governance.

The staggering turnover rate during Trump's first term, which reached as high as 92%, is not just a statistic; it's a testament to the chaotic and unstable nature of his administration. The continuation of this trend into his second term, with rising numbers of departures and firings, is alarming. This level of instability has profound implications for the administration's ability to govern effectively and respond to the challenges facing the nation.

In conclusion, the pattern of firings and leadership chaos within the Trump administration is not a mere quirk of Trump's leadership style. It is a deeply concerning trend that undermines the effectiveness and stability of the U.S. government. The focus on loyalty over competence, the lack of strategic foresight in appointments, and the unprecedented turnover rate are all indicative of a governance style that is fundamentally flawed. As the Trump administration continues to navigate its second term, the question remains: Can effective governance emerge from chaos? The evidence, thus far, suggests not.
