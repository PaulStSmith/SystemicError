'A lot of uncertainty': How Trump is aggravating a troubled housing market

President Donald Trump's policies appear to be exacerbating high home prices, NPR reports.

“Home prices are out of reach for many who would like to buy. And the tariff drama under President Trump has both made it more expensive to build new homes and made the future more unpredictable for would-be homebuyers,” reports NPR. “The result is a country where builders want to build, and buyers want to buy — but the future is too much in doubt.”

A May survey of builder confidence conducted by Wells Fargo and the National Association of Home Builders, rates home builder sentiment as dropping to a level not seen since November 2023. High home prices were one of the issues then candidate Trump blamed his predecessor’s for.

“Under this administration, our current administration, groceries are up 57 percent, gasoline is up 60 and 70 percent, mortgage rates have quadrupled, and the fact is it doesn’t matter what they are because you can’t get the money anyway,” Trump said in his RNC speech last year.

A PBS fact-check of the claim revealed home prices had actually only doubled, but Trump had vowed in the same speech: “Starting on Day 1, we will drive down prices and make America affordable again. We have to make it affordable. It’s not affordable. People can’t live like this.”

Despite that, numbers show the median existing home sales price continued to climb nationally, to $414,000, which is an all-time high for the month of April and the 22nd consecutive month of year-over-year price increases, NPR reports. While home prices are up nearly 50 percent since before the pandemic, mortgage interest rates crept up to 6.86 percent last week for a 30-year fixed-rate loan.

"The tariff debate has created a lot of uncertainty," says Robert Dietz, chief economist at the National Association of Home Builders. “… Wood framed homes represent about 90% of single-family home building and about a quarter of softwood lumber that we use comes from Canada.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tumult: A Blow to Affordable Housing

In an era where the American Dream seems increasingly like a fantasy, President Donald Trump's policies have thrown yet another wrench into the works. The latest report from NPR sheds light on a grim reality: Trump's economic maneuvers, particularly his penchant for tariffs, are exacerbating the crisis of sky-high home prices. Let's dissect this piece, shall we?

Tariffs and Turmoil: The Trump Effect

The crux of the issue lies in Trump's trade policies—specifically, the dramatic increase in tariffs on Canadian lumber, which has jumped from a little over 8% last August to a whopping 14.5%. This move has not only made it more costly to build new homes but has also injected a hefty dose of uncertainty into the housing market. Robert Dietz, chief economist at the National Association of Home Builders, quantifies this impact, stating that these tariffs add nearly $11,000 to the average price of building a single-family home. In a market where affordability is already out of reach for many Americans, this is no small matter.

The Builder-Buyer Conundrum

NPR's report highlights a peculiar paradox: builders are eager to build, and buyers are eager to buy, yet the future is shrouded in doubt. This sentiment is echoed in the Wells Fargo and National Association of Home Builders' May survey, which shows builder confidence plummeting to levels not seen since November 2023. The irony? High home prices were a point of contention for Trump as a candidate, blaming them on his predecessor. Yet, under his administration, the situation has only deteriorated.

Promises vs. Reality

Trump's campaign trail was littered with promises to make America "affordable again." However, the harsh reality is that, despite these promises, the median existing home sales price has surged to an all-time high of $414,000 in April, marking the 22nd consecutive month of year-over-year price increases. Mortgage interest rates have also soared, reaching 6.86% last week for a 30-year fixed-rate loan. This stark contrast between Trump's promises and the actual outcomes under his administration highlights a disconnect that cannot be ignored.

The Bottom Line

The NPR report paints a bleak picture of the current state of the U.S. housing market, one where Trump's policies seem to be doing more harm than good. The tariff on Canadian lumber is a prime example of how short-sighted economic policies can have long-lasting impacts on everyday Americans. The dream of homeownership is becoming increasingly elusive, and for many, Trump's promise of an affordable America remains just that—a promise.

In conclusion, as we sift through the rhetoric and examine the facts, it becomes clear that Trump's approach to addressing the housing crisis is, at best, misguided. The consequences of his tariffs and economic policies serve as a testament to the complexity of the housing market—a market that requires nuanced understanding and thoughtful policy-making, not blunt instruments that exacerbate existing problems. The narrative unfolding is one of missed opportunities and unfulfilled promises, leaving American families to navigate an ever-toughening landscape in pursuit of their slice of the American Dream.
