'Washed up has-been loser!' White House erupts at famed rocker for criticizing Trump decor

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20254 min read

The White House clapped back at the White Stripes front man Jack White after the famed rocker criticized President Donald Trump’s Oval Office makeover on Tuesday, calling the Grammy Award-winning musician a “washed up” and “has-been loser.”

Long a vocal critic of Trump, White took to social media Tuesday to criticize Trump’s redesign of the Oval Office, which under his administration has been adorned with gold moldings, fireplace mantels and figurines, calling it “vulgar" and “gaudy.” In response, White House spokesperson Steven Cheung took aim at White and his career in a comment to The Daily Beast.

“Jack White is a washed up, has-been loser posting drivel on social media because he clearly has ample time on his hands due to his stalled career,” Cheung said.

“It’s apparent he’s been masquerading as a real artist, because he fails to appreciate, and quite frankly disrespects, the splendor and significance of the Oval Office inside of ‘The People’s House.’”

White is not alone in his criticisms of Trump’s Oval Office makeover, with some critics viewing it as excessive and symbolic of autocratic tendencies, and others likening it to a lookalike of the Palace of Versailles or the palace of Iraqi President Saddam Hussein.

“A gold plated trump bible would look perfect up on that mantle with a pair of trump shoes on either side wouldn't it?” White wrote Tuesday. “What an embarrassment to American history.”

Despite the White House’s assertion of White having a “stalled career,” the 12-time Grammy Award winning musician is set to be inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame later this year as a member of the White Stripes, the Detroit-based rock duo whose albums have gone multi-platinum, a signifier that an album has sold more than two million units.

Trump’s redesign at the White House is now extending beyond the walls of the Oval Office, with plans underway to demolish the East Wing to make way for a$200 million ballroom.

Our Analysis:

The White House's Petty Counterpunch to Jack White's Critique: A Symptom of Deeper Issues

In a move that could easily be mistaken for a subplot in a farcical political drama, the White House has stooped to engaging in a war of words with Jack White, a musician whose main crime seems to be possessing an aesthetic sensibility and a platform from which to express it. Let's dissect the layers of irony, hypocrisy, and misplaced priorities this incident lays bare, shall we?

First, the accusation against Jack White by White House spokesperson Steven Cheung as a “washed up” and “has-been loser” reeks of desperation and ad hominem attack, a tactic often employed when substantial counterarguments are lacking. Cheung's comments are a textbook example of trying to shoot the messenger when you can't refute the message. It’s noteworthy that this insult is hurled at someone who is about to be inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame and whose albums have gone multi-platinum. If this is what a stalled career looks like, the standards for success must have shifted dramatically.

Jack White's criticism of President Trump's Oval Office makeover as “vulgar" and “gaudy” may be subjective, but it is hardly without merit, especially considering the context. The Oval Office, symbolizing the seat of power in a democracy, being adorned in a manner that critics liken to autocratic regimes or palatial excess, sends a concerning message about the administration's values and priorities.

The defense of the makeover by Cheung, asserting that White “fails to appreciate, and quite frankly disrespects, the splendor and significance of the Oval Office inside of ‘The People’s House,’” is an astonishing display of cognitive dissonance. The critique was not of the Oval Office's inherent dignity, but of the specific choices made in its redesign, choices that arguably detract from its dignity and symbolism.

Moreover, the plan to demolish the East Wing to make way for a $200 million ballroom is emblematic of a broader issue: an administration seemingly more concerned with opulence and grandeur than with the practical needs and concerns of its citizens. This is not just a matter of taste but of governance philosophy, where the aesthetics chosen by those in power reflect their attitude towards the institutions they steward.

In this debacle, what's truly “embarrassing to American history” is not Jack White’s remarks but the pettiness of the White House’s response. It is a distraction from more pressing issues and a stark reminder of the current administration's penchant for personal vendettas over policy substance. The response to White's criticism could have been an opportunity for thoughtful dialogue about the role of art, architecture, and aesthetics in public life. Instead, it was reduced to a schoolyard taunt, unworthy of the office from which it emanated.

In conclusion, this incident is not merely about clashing tastes in interior design but about the values an administration chooses to project and the lengths it will go to silence or belittle its critics. It's a microcosm of a larger, systemic problem, where instead of elevating the discourse, those in power too often choose to wallow in the mud of personal attacks. The real question we should be asking is not whether Jack White or Donald Trump has better taste but what this pettiness and focus on surface-level grandeur reveal about the state of American democracy.
