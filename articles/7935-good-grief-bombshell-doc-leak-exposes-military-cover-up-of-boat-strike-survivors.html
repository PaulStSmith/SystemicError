A new report about a military cover up had one former GOP lawmaker calling the debacle “absolutely incredible and wrong.”

Melissa Corrigan and David Shuster, the latter having previously exposed GOP corruption as an Emmy-winning anchor at MSNBC and CNN, reported in an article called “EXCLUSIVE: US Navy Deleted Video of October Boat Attack Survivors,” that a “leaked document reveals effort to coverup aftermath of Oct 17 strike.”

According to the report, “two detainees were brought on board a US Navy vessel, one in apparent medical distress having survived an explosive boat strike. Shipboard personnel followed standing protocol and code to capture video and photo documentation of the onboarding of these individuals, and then were given the dubious order to delete that footage.”

Shuster wrote about the story on social media, “US Navy deleted video of October 17 boat strike survivors. In this attack, two men were rescued and detained on USS Iwo Jima. Navy was ordered to delete photos/videos, ship document reveals.”

“This story proves the U.S. military has engaged in a coverup and destruction of documents/materials related to these boat attacks. Good grief,” he then added.

Ex-GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger shared the report and added, “This is absolutely incredible and wrong.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Veil: The Alleged Military Cover-Up

In a stark revelation that pierces through the often opaque veil of military operations, a new report has come to light, shedding disturbing illumination on what appears to be a deliberate cover-up within the U.S. Navy. The report, spearheaded by Melissa Corrigan and David Shuster, a journalist with a decorated history of exposing corruption, brings forth allegations that could rattle the foundational ethics of military conduct.

The Heart of Darkness: A Deleted Truth

The core of the exposé revolves around an incident on October 17, where, following an explosive boat strike, two individuals were rescued and taken aboard the USS Iwo Jima. What follows is a narrative not unfamiliar in the annals of power corridors, yet always jarring in the bluntness of its audacity. The ship’s personnel, adhering to protocol, documented the rescue operation, only to be ordered to delete the footage subsequently.

“US Navy deleted video of October 17 boat strike survivors.” These words by Shuster on social media encapsulate a chilling decision to obliterate evidence. By doing so, the military not only undermines its own protocols but also raises grave questions about the transparency and accountability of its operations.

The Cover-Up: A Symptom of a Greater Malaise?

This incident, as reported, does not exist in isolation. It is a symptom, a manifestation of a possible systemic inclination towards obfuscating truth when it becomes inconvenient. When the U.S. military, an institution held to the highest standards of honor and integrity, is implicated in a cover-up and destruction of documents/materials, it erodes the bedrock of trust and credibility.

David Shuster’s assertion that “the U.S. military has engaged in a coverup and destruction of documents/materials related to these boat attacks” is not just an indictment of a singular event. It is a mirror held up to the potential systemic rot within the institution, a rot that, if left unchecked, could corrode the very essence of military ethos.

Political Echoes and Reverberations

The reaction from ex-GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger, calling the debacle “absolutely incredible and wrong,” reflects a bipartisan concern over the allegations. It underscores the universal expectation of transparency, accountability, and moral rectitude from the armed forces, irrespective of political affiliations.

The Unanswered Questions

This report leaves us grappling with questions far beyond the immediate implications of the alleged cover-up. What were the motivations behind the deletion of the footage? Was it an isolated incident, or does it point to a broader pattern of behavior? And perhaps most importantly, what does this say about the ethical landscape of military operations?

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Accountability

As this story unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the perpetual need for vigilance, transparency, and accountability in all sectors of government, including the military. The allegations, if proven true, demand not just outrage but action. It is imperative to ensure that such incidents are thoroughly investigated and, if necessary, that reforms are instituted to prevent their recurrence.

This is not merely about one deleted video or two survivors of a boat attack. It’s about the integrity of the U.S. military and the democratic values it is sworn to uphold. In the quest for national security, let us not forsake the principles of transparency and ethical conduct. For in the end, it is these values that truly secure a nation.
