'In new territory': Experts staggered by new revelations about Trump's court contempt

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A new analysis has found that President Donald Trump and his appointees have flouted a third of the court rulings against them

The Washington Post determined that Trump and his appointees have not complied with about one in three of the 160 legal challenges against the administration in which a judge has issued a substantive ruling, and plaintiffs have complained that Department of Justice lawyers have provided false information, failed to turn over evidence, worked around court orders and cooked up excuses to carry out actions that have been blocked.

“The pattern of stuff we have … I haven’t seen before,” said Andrew McCarthy, a columnist for the conservative National Review and a former federal prosecutor. “The rules of the road are supposed to be you can tell a judge, ‘I can’t answer that for constitutional reasons,’ or you can tell the judge the truth.”

Courts are hesitant to begin contempt proceedings, especially when their rulings are under appeal, and analysts say judges might be reluctant to call on the U.S. Marshals, whose director is appointed by the president, to enforce their orders and risk a standoff that could ultimately undermine their own authority.

“The courts can’t enforce their own rulings — that has to be done by the executive branch,” said Paul Michel, a retired federal judge and former Watergate special prosecutor.

A whistleblower filed a complaint last month accusing DOJ officials of ignoring court orders in immigration cases and presenting legal arguments with no basis in the law, and Supreme Court justice Sonia Sotomayor has blasted the administration, saying Trump officials have “openly flouted” court orders against deporting migrants to a country where they did not hold citizenship.

"The Post examined 337 lawsuits filed against the administration since Trump returned to the White House and began a rapid-fire effort to reshape government programs and policy," the newspaper reported. "As of mid-July, courts had ruled against the administration in 165 of the lawsuits. The Post found that the administration is accused of defying or frustrating court oversight in 57 of those cases — almost 35 percent."

That pattern is unprecedented and threatens the judiciary's constitutional authority, legal experts say, and while most of those cases have involved immigration, the administration has also flouted rulings on cuts to federal funding and the workforce.

“The current challenge is even bigger and more complicated because it involves hundreds of actions, not one subpoena for a set of tapes,” Michel said. “We’re in new territory.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Defiance: Trump's Administration and the Judiciary

In an era where the rule of law is ostensibly the bedrock of American democracy, the findings by The Washington Post reveal a troubling defiance of legal authority by the highest echelons of power. President Donald Trump and his appointees, as per the analysis, have embarked on a path that not only questions the very foundation of judicial oversight but also blatantly ignores it.

The Stark Numbers

The investigation by The Washington Post lays bare the numbers: about one in three of the 160 legal challenges against Trump's administration, where a judge issued a substantive ruling, were not complied with. This isn't just a few isolated incidents; this is a pattern, a systematic disregard for judicial authority that raises alarm bells about the state of constitutional checks and balances in the United States.

The Methods of Defiance

The administration's tactics to circumvent court orders are as varied as they are concerning. From providing false information to courts, failing to turn over evidence, fabricating excuses, to outright ignoring court orders, the methods of defiance are not just underhanded but a direct assault on the judiciary's role in American governance. When plaintiffs allege that Department of Justice lawyers have cooked up excuses to carry out actions blocked by the courts, it's a sign of an administration that believes itself above the law.

The Judicial Reluctance

A key aspect of this troubling scenario is the judiciary's hesitance to enforce its rulings, particularly through contempt proceedings. This reluctance, fundamentally, stems from a fear of sparking a constitutional crisis. Yet, this hesitance inadvertently emboldens an administration that perceives judicial orders as mere suggestions rather than binding directives. The notion that "courts can’t enforce their own rulings" speaks volumes about the erosion of judicial authority in the face of executive obstinance.

The Systemic Issue

What we're witnessing isn't just a series of legal battles; it's a systemic issue that threatens the very core of the judiciary's constitutional authority. When legal experts describe this pattern as unprecedented, it's not hyperbole. It's a reflection of a reality where the administration, tasked with enforcing the law, becomes the very entity undermining it. This isn't just about immigration policies or cuts to federal funding; it's about the fundamental principle that no one, not even the President or his appointees, is above the law.

A Bigger Challenge

This defiance isn't just a challenge to individual judges or courts; it's a challenge to the judiciary as a whole. Comparisons to historical moments like Watergate, where the issue was a single subpoena, underscore the magnitude of the current challenge. We're in uncharted territory, dealing with an administration that views judicial oversight not as a constitutional safeguard, but as an obstacle to be navigated or ignored.

Conclusion

The findings by The Washington Post are more than just a compilation of legal disputes; they're a blaring alarm about the state of the rule of law in the United States. This isn't just about the Trump administration's disdain for judicial oversight; it's about the potential erosion of one of the most fundamental pillars of democracy. If the judiciary's authority can be so blatantly disregarded by the executive, what does that say about the health of our democracy? It's a question that doesn't just demand reflection but immediate action to reaffirm the principle that no individual or administration is above the law.
