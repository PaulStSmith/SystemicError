'Not gonna happen!' Trump official says Thanksgiving travel about to 'slow to a trickle'

U.S. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy speaks during a press conference to discuss the impact of the government shutdown on air safety workers and travel at Philadelphia International Airport in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S., October 24, 2025. REUTERS/Rachel Wisniewski

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy took to Fox News Sunday to warn that for many of the millions of Americans hoping to fly for the upcoming Thanksgiving holiday, their plans were “not gonna happen,” predicting air traffic would “slow to a trickle.”

“As I look two weeks out as we get closer to Thanksgiving travel, I think what’s going to happen is you’re going to have air travel slow to a trickle,” Duffy told Fox News.

More than 4,200 flights have been delayed and 1,520 have been canceled as of Sunday as shortages of air traffic controllers continue to increase amid the ongoing government shutdown, which has left them without any pay since mid-October.

Duffy was quick to immediately blame Democrats for the flight disruptions, who have refused to back any spending measure that doesn’t include funding for Obamacare subsidies, which, if allowed to expire, would see health insurance premiums skyrocket for millions of Americans and cause an estimated 4 million to lose coverage.

“As everyone wants to travel to see their families, I think we’re going to see very few air traffic controllers coming to work, which means you’ll have a few flights taking off, but the thousands of flights that happen every day that move people around the country for this great American holiday? It’s not gonna happen!” Duffy continued.

“You’re going to have massive disruption, a lot of angry Americans, and I think we have to be honest about where this is going: it doesn’t get better, it gets worse until these air traffic controllers are going to get paid.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s Statements on Thanksgiving Air Travel

In an appearance on Fox News Sunday, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy painted a bleak picture for millions of Americans planning to fly during the Thanksgiving holiday. His prediction that air travel will “slow to a trickle” due to the ongoing government shutdown is a dire warning worth dissecting for its implications, underlying causes, and the political blame game at play.

The Stark Prediction

Duffy’s forecast for the Thanksgiving travel season is nothing short of apocalyptic for holiday travelers. With more than 4,200 flights already delayed and 1,520 canceled as of his statement, the stress and chaos for millions of Americans are not just hypotheticals—they are imminent. The reason? A shortage of air traffic controllers, who have been working without pay since mid-October due to the shutdown. Duffy’s dramatic language suggests a scenario where the American tradition of Thanksgiving travel could effectively come to a standstill.

The Blame Game

Without missing a beat, Duffy immediately casts blame on Democrats for the anticipated travel disruptions, specifically targeting their refusal to back any spending measure that doesn’t include funding for Obamacare subsidies. This is a classic example of political football, where the real-life consequences of policy stalemates—such as potential massive disruptions in air travel—are used as leverage in broader political battles.

However, Duffy’s framing oversimplifies and politicizes what is fundamentally an issue of governance and public service. By focusing solely on the Democrats’ stance on Obamacare subsidies, Duffy conveniently omits a broader discussion about the responsibilities of government to ensure the functioning of critical services, like air traffic control, especially during peak travel times.

The Real Victims

The most glaring omission in Duffy’s narrative is the plight of the unpaid air traffic controllers. These individuals are expected to ensure the safety and efficiency of air travel, a job that requires high levels of skill, concentration, and responsibility, yet they are doing so without compensation due to the shutdown. The human cost of the shutdown, including the financial strain on these essential workers and their families, is lost in Duffy’s political point-scoring.

Moreover, the millions of Americans whose holiday plans are jeopardized become collateral damage in this political standoff. The disruption to family gatherings, the financial losses from non-refundable travel bookings, and the overall impact on the holiday season’s spirit and economy are profound, yet they are reduced to mere talking points in Duffy’s commentary.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s statements reflect a systemic failure to prioritize the needs and well-being of the American public and the workers who serve them. The looming chaos for Thanksgiving travelers is a symptom of a larger issue: a government that is increasingly dysfunctional, held hostage by political brinkmanship.

While Duffy attempts to lay the blame solely at the feet of the Democrats, the reality is that governing effectively requires collaboration, compromise, and a focus on the public good—all of which are notably absent in this narrative. As Thanksgiving approaches, instead of looking forward to a time of gratitude and family reunion, many Americans are faced with uncertainty, frustration, and the consequences of a government that has lost sight of its purpose.
