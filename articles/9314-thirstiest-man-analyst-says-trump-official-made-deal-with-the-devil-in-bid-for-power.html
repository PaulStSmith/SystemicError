Secretary of State Marco Rubio has developed a “reputation as the thirstiest man” and “happily made a deal with the devil,” according to a new analysis.

Rubio has his eyes on a 2028 presidential run and has gone full MAGA, Salon columnist Heather Digby Parton writes Thursday.

“Most of America first took notice of Marco Rubio when he gave one of the most-cringe prime-time performances of all time, the official Republican rebuttal to Barack Obama’s 2013 State of the Union address,” Parton writes. “No one who watched is likely to forget Rubio’s awkward stare as he furtively reached for a water bottle, cementing his reputation as the thirstiest man in the U.S. Senate. It’s a testament to his limitless ambition that he came back from that and is now one of the most powerful people in the world.”

Rubio, dubbed “Little Marco” by President Donald Trump during the 2016 campaign, has typically focused on national security and foreign policy, and although he has broken with the GOP in the past, including finding evidence that Russia interfered in the 2016 elections — something Republicans now claim to be a “hoax” — Trump appears to have overlooked or ignored that. Not to mention, the former Florida senator’s comment about Trump’s “small hands” that played out during a 2016 debate on national television.

In his current role, he drives the illegal war in Venezuela, Parton explains.

“According to recent reports in the Wall Street Journal, the extrajudicial killings, CIA covert actions and pending war plans against Venezuela are all being driven by Rubio,” she writes.

And although he doesn’t always take the lead on each top issue for Trump, “that has just given him time to pursue his own special interests.”

“The political establishment, including many Democrats, were relieved when Rubio was chosen for the State Department job, reassured that a supposedly serious fellow with Senate credentials would keep Trump foreign policy from going off the rails,” she writes. “The hope was that Rubio might stop the president from doing something silly, like bailing out of NATO or invading Greenland. Little did they know that Rubio had happily made a deal with the devil and now seems to relish the idea of ripping up the world order in Trump’s image.”

His main goal has been to fit in to the president’s inner circle and carve out a space for his own political future.

“Marco Rubio almost certainly intends to run for president in 2028 and sees his service in that cause as the best way to fulfill his own agenda and expand both his power and his political profile. He’s certainly not the only person in the Trump administration with that idea, but he stands out in that many observers still view him as an ‘adult in the room’ with establishment credibility. That’s entirely wrong. He’s a fully paid-up MAGA fanatic now, and no one should think otherwise,” Parton writes.

Our Analysis:

The Metamorphosis of Marco Rubio: From Water Bottle Mishaps to MAGA Crusader

In an era where political flip-flops are often more acrobatic than Olympic gymnasts, Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s transformation is not just another tale of ideological gymnastics. It is a vivid testament to ambition’s ability to overshadow principles, making Rubio’s journey from a “thirsty” senator to a MAGA devotee a case study in political metamorphosis.

The Thirst for Power

Remembering Rubio’s infamous water bottle fiasco during his rebuttal to Obama’s State of the Union address, one might have predicted a future of meme obscurity rather than political ascendancy. Yet, here we are, dissecting his rise to one of the “most powerful people in the world.” This episode, initially seen as a career faux pas, now serves as a mere footnote in Rubio’s relentless pursuit of power. It’s a sharp reminder that in politics, yesterday’s embarrassment can be tomorrow’s trivia, provided one’s ambition is limitless enough.

A Pact with the Devil

Rubio’s transformation is marked by a series of ideological contortions that would make even the most seasoned politico dizzy. Once dubbed “Little Marco” by Trump, Rubio’s journey from a critic of Trump’s “small hands” to a MAGA enthusiast is nothing short of remarkable. It’s a classic tale of if you can’t beat them, join them – or rather, if you can’t beat them, morph into their most ardent supporter.

His alleged role in driving the illegal war in Venezuela, as outlined by Salon columnist Heather Digby Parton, paints a picture of a man who has not only embraced the Trumpian world view but is actively shaping it. Rubio’s penchant for national security and foreign policy has seemingly found a new outlet in advancing a controversial agenda that includes covert actions and extrajudicial killings.

The Illusion of the Adult in the Room

What’s most alarming about Rubio’s transformation is the shattered illusion that he could serve as the “adult in the room” within the Trump administration. The hope that he might temper Trump’s more erratic impulses now seems like wishful thinking. Instead of pulling the administration towards a more conventional path, Rubio has embraced the chaos, becoming a key player in tearing up the “world order in Trump’s image.”

The political establishment’s initial relief at Rubio’s appointment to the State Department underscores a grave misjudgment. Assuming that Rubio’s Senate credentials and serious demeanor could counterbalance Trump’s unpredictability was a miscalculation. Rubio, it turns out, is not the stabilizing force many had hoped for but a “fully paid-up MAGA fanatic.”

The Future is MAGA?

With eyes set on a 2028 presidential run, Rubio’s current trajectory is clear. He has chosen to align himself closely with Trump’s inner circle, seeing it as the best launchpad for his own political aspirations. This alignment raises questions about the future of the Republican Party and the extent to which Trump’s influence will shape its direction and its candidates.

Rubio’s transformation is emblematic of a broader trend within the party: the willingness to forsake long-held principles for political expediency and power. It’s a cautionary tale of how ambition can lead to ideological surrender, making Rubio not an outlier but a harbinger of what’s to come.

In conclusion, Marco Rubio’s political evolution from a so-called establishment figure to a MAGA adherent is not just a personal saga but a reflection of the current state of American politics. It serves as a stark reminder that in the quest for power, convictions can be as fluid as the policies one is willing to support or oppose. As Rubio continues to carve out his place in Trump’s America, let’s not forget the lessons learned from watching his transformation: in politics, today’s principles are tomorrow’s compromises.
