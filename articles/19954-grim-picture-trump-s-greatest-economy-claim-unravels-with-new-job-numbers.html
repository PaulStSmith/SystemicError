'Grim picture': Trump's 'greatest economy' claim unravels with new job numbers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20265 min read

Revised federal data released Wednesday shows that the US economy under the stewardship of President Donald Trump added hundreds of thousands fewer jobs in 2025 than previously reported, further undercutting the president’s claim to have ushered in the “greatest” economy in history.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said Wednesday that US employers added just 181,000 jobs last year, an average of roughly 15,000 per month. That’s roughly 69% fewer than the previous estimate of 584,000 jobs created in 2025.

Groundwork Collaborative, a progressive advocacy group, said the updated figures paint “a grim picture” of the job market under Trump, who has repeatedly promised—and taken credit for bringing about—an economic boom.

“Today’s numbers show that the economy spent 2025 treading water while costs surged and families fell further behind,” said Alex Jacquez, chief of policy and advocacy at Groundwork. “Job growth was dramatically weaker than advertised and concentrated nearly entirely in healthcare, leaving the rest of the labor market to stall. Opportunities are drying up outside a handful of sectors, and more and more workers are settling for part-time hours or have stopped looking for work entirely. 2025 was a lost year for American workers.”

Daniel Zhao, chief economist at the employment site Glassdoor, told the New York Times in response to the revised numbers that “we’ve been hearing from workers that the job market is not working for them for some time.”

“The anecdotes are starting to align with the data,” Zhao added.

A separate analysis released Wednesday by Democrats on the Joint Economic Committee (JEC) found that the US lost 108,000 manufacturing jobs during the first year of Trump’s second term in the White House, despite the president’s pledge to revive American industry through his tariff regime.

“While President Trump promised us a manufacturing boom, the reality of his first year has been a bust,” said Sen. Maggie Hassan (D-NH), the JEC’s ranking member. “It is critical for both our national security and our economic future that we grow our manufacturing sector. The president has instead spent his first year burdening manufacturers with reckless tariffs, and this loss of jobs is the result.”

Our Analysis:

The Mirage of Trump’s “Greatest” Economy

In what can only be described as a stark unveiling of truth, revised federal data has mercilessly shredded President Donald Trump’s oft-repeated boast of having engineered the “greatest” economy in history. Wednesday’s revelations from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) illuminate a reality far removed from the rosy picture painted by an administration that thrives on hyperbole. Last year, the US economy added a mere 181,000 jobs, a figure that falls astonishingly short of the previously estimated 584,000 jobs. This adjustment is not merely a statistical footnote; it is a glaring testament to the fact that the economy under Trump was, contrary to his claims, staggering rather than soaring.

The Devil is in the Details

The BLS’s recalibration reveals a job market that was not burgeoning but barely breathing. At an average of approximately 15,000 jobs per month, the pace of job creation was a mere shadow of what was once proclaimed. This 69% downward revision is not a mere mathematical mishap; it is a damning indictment of the Trump administration’s economic stewardship.

It’s particularly telling that the Groundwork Collaborative, a progressive advocacy group, has characterized 2025 as a “lost year” for American workers. The analysis that job growth was “dramatically weaker than advertised” and concentrated almost entirely in healthcare is a sobering reminder of an economy that was far from inclusive or robust. The fact that “opportunities are drying up outside a handful of sectors” paints a picture of an economy where the deck is stacked against the average worker, contradicting the Trump administration’s narrative of widespread prosperity.

Manufacturing: The Broken Promise

Trump’s promise to revive American manufacturing and bring back jobs through his tariff regime has proven to be hollow. The loss of 108,000 manufacturing jobs during the first year of his second term is a stark contrast to the manufacturing boom promised. Sen. Maggie Hassan’s (D-NH) comments underscore the reality of a sector not revitalized but ravaged by what she terms “reckless tariffs.” Trump’s economic policies, far from fostering growth in manufacturing, have instead contributed to its decline—a bitter pill for workers who placed their trust in his promises.

The Bitter Reality

What we are witnessing, as the revised job figures and the manufacturing job losses illustrate, is the unraveling of the myth of Trump’s economic genius. The chorus of criticism from advocacy groups and economic analysts, backed by hard data, signals a growing acknowledgment that the emperor has no clothes. The job market, rather than being a bastion of Trump’s success, has been revealed as a realm of missed opportunities and broken promises.

Daniel Zhao, Glassdoor’s chief economist, notes that workers have felt the job market failing them “for some time.” This alignment of anecdotal evidence with hard data is a potent reminder that behind the veneer of economic success often touted by the Trump administration lies a reality of struggle and disillusionment for many American workers.

Conclusion

In the final analysis, the revised job figures serve as a reality check for those who have been swayed by the narrative of unparalleled economic success under Trump. The stark truth is that the “greatest” economy, as it turns out, was not so great for everyone. As we move forward, it is imperative that we scrutinize the narratives spun by those in power and confront the systemic issues that prevent many Americans from sharing in the nation’s prosperity. The myth of Trump’s economic miracle has been debunked, leaving us with the task of rebuilding not just an economy, but the very fabric of truth in our political discourse.
