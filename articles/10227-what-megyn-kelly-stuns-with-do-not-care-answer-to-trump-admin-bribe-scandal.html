When ex-Fox News host Megyn Kelly was confronted with a report about a bribery scandal in Donald Trump's administration, she stunned observers when she proudly declared, "We do not care."

Raw Story reported on Saturday about a MSNBC exclusive in which the outlet claimed that border czar Tom Homan had previously been investigated for accepting $50,000 from undercover FBI agents. That caused U.S. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) to issue a stinging four-word response to Homan.

It also caused a senator to declare that Attorney General Pam Bondi "knew" about the reported bribery operation.

But for Kelly, she had a different type of response.

"We DO NOT CARE. Don’t bother [Homan] he’s a national treasure," she wrote on X.

Journalist Sam Stein replied, "For me, this just raises the question: is there ANYTHING Homan (and, I guess, Trump) could do that Megyn or others WOULD care about?"

"The question of whether there are any lines left is a valid one," Stein added.

Conservative strategist Tim Miller asked Kelly, "You don’t care that government officials are taking cash bribes? What?"

Kelly responded in part, "No, we don’t care what you say about Tom Homan. We do not trust you. We only care about defeating you."

Ex-GOP pollster Sarah Longwell also responded to Kelly's response to the scandal.

"Yes, we’re aware you don’t care about the rampant corruption in this administration," Longwell wrote on Saturday. "That’s been clear."

Our Analysis:

The Unwavering Loyalty of Megyn Kelly: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

The recent commentary by Megyn Kelly, former Fox News host, on the bribery scandal involving Tom Homan, Trump's border czar, is not just shocking; it's a damning indictment of the current state of political discourse in the United States. Kelly's blithe dismissal of serious allegations of corruption—"We do not care"—is a bold-faced reflection of a political climate where loyalty trumps integrity, and facts are malleable.

The Scandal Unfolds

According to a report by Raw Story, based on an MSNBC exclusive, Tom Homan was allegedly investigated for accepting a $50,000 bribe from undercover FBI agents. This revelation should have been enough to spark outrage, trigger investigations, and demand accountability. Instead, it was met with a disturbingly cavalier attitude by some, most notably Megyn Kelly, who went so far as to call Homan a "national treasure."

The Reaction: A Study in Partisan Blindness

Kelly's response to the scandal is emblematic of a deep-seated problem within American politics: the willful ignorance and outright dismissal of corruption when it emanates from "our side." Her statement, "We DO NOT CARE. Don’t bother [Homan] he’s a national treasure," is not just a personal opinion; it's a public declaration that integrity, ethics, and accountability no longer matter to a significant segment of the political spectrum.

Journalist Sam Stein raises an essential question in response to Kelly's remarks: "is there ANYTHING Homan (and, I guess, Trump) could do that Megyn or others WOULD care about?" This question goes to the heart of the issue. It's not just about Homan or even Trump; it's about the erosion of ethical standards and the normalization of corruption.

The Bigger Picture

The reactions from various quarters to Kelly's statement reveal the bifurcation of American politics into two camps: those who are appalled by the rampant corruption and those who, either out of loyalty or disillusionment, simply "do not care."

Conservative strategist Tim Miller and ex-GOP pollster Sarah Longwell both express incredulity and concern over Kelly's stance. Yet, the most telling aspect of this saga is Kelly's assertion: "We do not trust you. We only care about defeating you." This zero-sum mentality, where winning matters more than the means by which victory is achieved, is a cancer eating away at the democratic norms and ethical standards that have long underpinned American governance.

Conclusion: A Call for Renewed Vigilance

Megyn Kelly's unabashed declaration that she and, by extension, her cohort "do not care" about allegations of corruption is a stark reminder of the challenges facing American democracy. It underscores the urgent need for the electorate to demand higher standards from its leaders and to hold them accountable for their actions.

The reaction to the bribery scandal involving Tom Homan is not just about partisan politics; it's a reflection of how far some are willing to go in their unconditional support for their political idols. As the line between right and wrong becomes increasingly blurred, the responsibility falls on every citizen to stand up for integrity, accountability, and the rule of law.

In the end, the question isn't just whether Megyn Kelly or others will ever care about corruption within their ranks. The more pressing question is: will the American public?
