That someone is likely to be Attorney General Pam Bondi, according to longtime DC insider Elanor Clift in a new column.

To date, since the files on the convicted pedophile became the central focus of far-right conservatives after the AG said in an interview that she had a list of Epstein clients sitting in her desk, which she later attempted to retract, Bondi has had to scramble to deal with almost daily revelations that have only exacerbated the White House crisis.

According to Clift, unlike Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, whose bungling at the Pentagon doesn't implicate Trump, Bondi may not be the recipient of the patience the embattled defense chief has been given.

With that in mind, Clift claimed the impatient and short-tempered Trump may throw Bondi to the wolves to give himself some relief from the MAGA horde which feels it has been betrayed over transparency on Epstein.

Suggesting that Bondi "is in a particularly precarious position, as the official most directly in the line of fire over the Epstein debacle," the journalist wrote the "MAGA base doesn’t want to turn on Trump, but they want someone to pay for the switcheroo they’ve been served."

That, in turn, makes Bondi the "likely fall guy."

Michael Sozan at the Center for American Politics agrees.

“It’s like ‘The Apprentice’ taking place in the White House with Cabinet secretaries and top officials knowing that in order to survive they better do anything they can to please him, even when it’s not in the best interest of the American people,” he suggested before adding, "She still sits at the apex of the decision-making. I don’t see how she successfully gets out of this.”

Our Analysis:

The Scapegoat Strategy: Unpacking the Bondi-Epstein Controversy

In the latest episode of political drama that could only be rivaled by the most convoluted of soap operas, we find ourselves dissecting a narrative so riddled with desperation and misdirection it would be hilarious if it weren't so deeply troubling. The article in question casts a spotlight on the tumultuous situation surrounding the Jeffrey Epstein file controversy and its potential fallout on Attorney General Pam Bondi, under the scrutinizing gaze of President Donald Trump's administration. Let's dive into the mire, shall we?

The Epstein File Fiasco: At the heart of this debacle is the explosive implication that far-right conservatives, a significant portion of Trump's MAGA base, have been betrayed. Betrayed by whom? By none other than AG Pam Bondi, who allegedly claimed to have a list of Epstein's clients, a claim she later tried to walk back. This isn't just a minor slip-up; it's a political landmine that has been detonated in the middle of an already beleaguered administration.

The Disposable Pawn: Elanor Clift's analysis suggests that Bondi is on the chopping block, poised to be the administration's sacrificial lamb. This wouldn't be the first time Trump's administration has thrown someone under the bus to save face, but the choice of Bondi as the fall guy (or fall woman, as it were) is particularly poignant. Unlike Pete Hegseth, whose Pentagon mishaps have somehow left Trump unscathed, Bondi's direct link to the Epstein controversy makes her an ideal target for a president known for his "you're fired" catchphrase, now turned governing strategy.

A MAGA Mutiny?: The article posits that the MAGA base feels "betrayed" over the lack of transparency regarding Epstein, a sentiment that cannot be understated in its potential impact. Trump's base is not just any political constituency; it's a fervently loyal group that has stood by him through countless scandals. To risk alienating this base is to flirt with political disaster.

Survival of the Fittest: Michael Sozan's comparison of the situation to 'The Apprentice' is both apt and chilling. The notion that Cabinet secretaries and top officials must do anything to please Trump "even when it’s not in the best interest of the American people" is a damning indictment of the current state of affairs. It's a stark reminder that in the quest for power and survival within Trump's orbit, the American populace is but an afterthought.

The Underlying Issue: What this article lays bare, beyond the immediate controversy, is a systemic problem within the Trump administration: a culture of disposability and a lack of accountability. The readiness to sacrifice Bondi on the altar of political expediency is symptomatic of a broader issue where loyalty is a one-way street, and the truth is malleable.

In conclusion, the Bondi-Epstein controversy is not just about the potential fall of another Trump administration official. It's a glaring example of the chaotic, self-serving nature of a presidency that treats governance like a reality TV show, where ratings (or approval ratings, in this case) trump principles, and the quest for truth is a road less traveled. As the MAGA base grapples with the possibility of betrayal, one must wonder: at what point does loyalty to an individual override the commitment to broader democratic principles and the pursuit of truth? In Trump's America, that question remains painfully rhetorical.
