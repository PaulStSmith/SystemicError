Trump heads to Federal Reserve building — with sworn enemy and an axe to grind

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as Jerome Powell, his nominee to become chairman of the U.S. Federal Reserve, speaks at the White House in Washington, U.S., November 2, 2017. REUTERS/Carlos Barria/File Photo

President Donald Trump was headed to the Federal Reserve building on Thursday afternoon with an avowed enemy.

Trump claimed that the Federal Reserve renovation budget, which was under $2.5 billion, has now increased to over $3.1 billion. He brought it up in a Truth Social post announcing that he was headed to tour the building, along with several lawmakers, including Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC) and Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC).

He noted Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell would also attend, a frequent target of his attacks, along with several construction experts.

The Wall Street Journal published an exposé on the cost of the renovations, noting that among the problems are asbestos remediation and lead paint removal, but the centuries-old building also lacks some of the requirements in the Americans with Disabilities Act, such as wheelchair accessible ramps in all locations.

Trump has implied that he could use the overspend on the project to fire Powell, who refuses to cater to Trump's demand to drop interest rates.

Trump nominated Powell to be the chair in 2017.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Crusade Against the Federal Reserve: A Cloaked Vendetta?

In a recent spectacle that could only be conceived in the fevered dreams of political theater, President Donald Trump has once again positioned himself in the eye of a storm, this time targeting the Federal Reserve and its chairman, Jerome Powell. Trump's latest escapade, a tour of the Federal Reserve building accompanied by a coterie of lawmakers and experts, is not merely a benign visitation. It's a calculated move, cloaked in the guise of fiscal responsibility but deeply rooted in personal vendetta and political maneuvering.

The Alleged Budget Overrun: A Convenient Excuse?

Trump's assertion that the Federal Reserve renovation budget has ballooned from under $2.5 billion to over $3.1 billion serves as the ostensible reason for his visit. However, the subtext is impossible to ignore. By focusing on this budget overrun, Trump seeks to undermine the Federal Reserve's credibility and, by extension, that of Jerome Powell. It's essential to scrutinize the figures presented: a budget increase of $600 million for a project of this magnitude, especially considering the complexities of asbestos remediation, lead paint removal, and ADA compliance, is not as scandalous as it's made out to be. These are not mere aesthetic upgrades but necessary measures to ensure the building's safety and accessibility.

Powell in the Crosshairs: A Battle Beyond Budgets

Trump's antagonism towards Powell is no secret. Since nominating him in 2017, Trump has repeatedly lambasted Powell for not bending to his will, particularly regarding interest rate policies. The current situation reeks of Trump using the renovation budget as a pretext to escalate his campaign against Powell. The suggestion that this budget issue could be grounds to fire Powell is not only legally dubious but also a gross simplification of how Federal Reserve governance works. It's a dangerous conflation of fiscal oversight with personal vendettas, setting a worrying precedent for the politicization of monetary policy.

The Wall Street Journal's Role: Exposé or Fuel on the Fire?

The Wall Street Journal's exposé on the Federal Reserve's renovation costs plays a pivotal role in this narrative. While investigative journalism is crucial in holding institutions accountable, one must question the timing and focus of this report. By zeroing in on the renovation costs without offering a nuanced understanding of the necessary expenses involved in such a massive project, the article inadvertently provides ammunition for Trump's assault on Powell and the Federal Reserve's independence.

Conclusion: A Distraction from Bigger Issues?

Trump's tour of the Federal Reserve with lawmakers and construction experts, under the banner of fiscal scrutiny, is a theatric exercise designed to distract from more pressing issues. It's a tactic pulled straight from the populist playbook: create a spectacle, stoke outrage over perceived financial imprudence, and direct that anger towards convenient scapegoats. In this case, the scapegoat is Jerome Powell, a figure who represents a rare bastion of independence in an era increasingly defined by political loyalty over principled governance.

As observers, we must cut through the theatrics and recognize this maneuver for what it is: a dangerous game of political chess that threatens to undermine the Federal Reserve's autonomy and, with it, the stability of the United States' monetary policy.
