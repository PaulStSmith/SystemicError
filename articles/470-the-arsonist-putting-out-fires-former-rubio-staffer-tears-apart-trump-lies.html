'The arsonist putting out fires': Former Rubio staffer tears apart Trump lies

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

Trump Lit the Match, Then Praised the Smoke Clearing

The Power Behind the Price

The White House tried to claim credit for falling gas prices after the market had been jolted by Donald Trump’s decision to launch war with Iran. That is the basic fact pattern: the president used state power to create the disruption, and then his own account posted a victory lap when the immediate panic eased.

The Damage Was the Decision

This was not an accident, and it was not a mystery. The source text says gas prices surged after Trump’s war decision, peaking around $5 a gallon nationally, the highest in four years. That is the consequence of executive power being used recklessly, then rebranded as management once the worst spike began to soften.

The Spin Machine Does What It Always Does

The White House framing is doing political work, not informing anyone. It converts a self-inflicted crisis into a story about competent recovery. That is the old trick: create the harm, wait for the market to partially adjust, then claim the credit for the adjustment while the public is still paying for the damage.

What the Critics Actually Exposed

Gregg Nunziata’s “arsonist putting out fires” line lands because it names the real structure of the story. The administration is not being misunderstood; it is being caught trying to launder responsibility. Even the article’s own details undercut the boast: experts warn that oil flows, shipping, refinery pricing, and domestic retail prices all lag behind the headline drop, meaning the pain does not vanish because the account holder says so.

Scapegoats and Cover

The source also shows how this kind of messaging shifts blame downward. Gas station owners, global shipping delays, refinery contracts, and market inertia become convenient fog. Those are real factors, but they are being used here to blur the central fact that one man, holding the office with the most coercive power in the system, made the initiating decision that set the whole chain in motion.

The Pattern

This is not just bad messaging. It is a governing style built on manufactured crisis, delayed accountability, and propaganda as consolation prize. The public pays for the escalation, then gets told to applaud the partial cleanup. That is how institutional cowardice works when power refuses to own the consequences of its own violence.
