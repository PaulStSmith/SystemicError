The National Cattlemen’s Beef Association, the Ranchers-Cattlemen Action Legal Fund United Stockgrowers of America and other farming groups condemned the president’s casual remark about importing beef from Argentina, and Trump lashed out at them in a Wednesday afternoon Truth Social post.

“The Cattle Ranchers, who I love, don’t understand that the only reason they are doing so well, for the first time in decades, is because I put Tariffs on cattle coming into the United States, including a 50% Tariff on Brazil,” Trump posted. “If it weren’t for me, they would be doing just as they’ve done for the past 20 years — Terrible!”

Trump suggested ranchers were insufficiently grateful and blamed them for higher prices for consumers.

“It would be nice if they would understand that, but they also have to get their prices down, because the consumer is a very big factor in my thinking, also!” Trump posted.

U.S. ranchers have been enjoying some rare profitable years in an industry that ebbs and flows, but agricultural economists say Argentine beef accounts for only about 2 percent of beef imports, so even doubling that amount would not change consumer prices by much but could create uncertainty for ranchers and make them less likely to invest in raising more cattle.

“I love ‘Make America Great Again’ rhetoric. I love ‘America First’ rhetoric,” South Dakota rancher Brett Kenzy told the Associated Press. “But to me this feels a lot like the failed policies of the past — the free trade sourcing cheap global goods.”

Trump’s tariffs on Brazil, which is a much bigger beef importer than Argentina, and limits on Mexico due to concerns about a parasitic infection have driven down beef imports and spiked prices for meat.

“In addition to everything else, Tariffs on other Countries SAVED our Cattle Ranchers!” Trump insisted.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Beef with Ranchers: A Policy Mis-steak?

In a move that could only originate from the chaotic epicenter of former President Donald Trump’s policy playbook, we find ourselves amidst a beef about beef. Trump’s recent tirade against American ranchers, following their pushback against his proposal to import beef from Argentina, is a masterclass in political contradiction and economic misunderstanding. Let’s carve through the fat of this situation.

Trump’s Misguided Attack on Ranchers

Firstly, Trump’s attack on the ranchers is as misguided as it is misinformed. His Truth Social post lambasting these hardworking Americans for their lack of gratitude towards his tariffs reveals a fundamental disconnect. Trump claims, “The Cattle Ranchers, who I love, don’t understand that the only reason they are doing so well, for the first time in decades, is because I put Tariffs on cattle coming into the United States, including a 50% Tariff on Brazil.” This statement is a prime cut of irony, given his proposal to import beef from Argentina—a move that directly undermines the interests of these very ranchers.

The Tariff Tango: A Step Backwards

Trump’s tariffs, while ostensibly protective, have had a mixed impact at best. While he asserts that tariffs on Brazil and limits on Mexico have “SAVED our Cattle Ranchers!”, the reality is more complex. Tariffs are a double-edged sword, often resulting in retaliatory measures and increased costs for consumers and producers alike. The notion that these tariffs have been an unqualified success is, at best, a selective reading of economic outcomes.

Argentine Beef Import: A Recipe for Uncertainty

The proposal to import beef from Argentina to ease record prices is a band-aid solution to a deeper economic wound. As agricultural economists have pointed out, Argentine beef accounts for a mere 2 percent of beef imports. Doubling this amount would hardly make a dent in consumer prices but could inject significant uncertainty into the market. This uncertainty could discourage investment in the cattle industry, potentially leading to long-term instability. Brett Kenzy, a rancher from South Dakota, encapsulated this sentiment perfectly, noting that this move feels like a reversion to “the failed policies of the past.”

Consumer Prices vs. Producer Livelihoods

Trump’s emphasis on lowering consumer prices, while ignoring the broader implications for ranchers, is a narrow approach to a complex issue. Yes, consumer interests are important, but not at the expense of the producers who form the backbone of the American economy. The balance between affordable food prices and a sustainable, domestically thriving agricultural sector is delicate and requires nuanced, informed policy-making—not blunt force trade maneuvers.

In Conclusion: A Call for Policy That Supports All Stakeholders

Trump’s approach, as evidenced by this latest controversy, lacks the finesse and understanding necessary to navigate the complex interplay of trade, agriculture, and economics. His broadside against ranchers for their lack of gratitude is not just a political misstep; it’s a policy mis-steak that could have lasting consequences for American agriculture.

In the grand scheme of things, this issue may seem like small potatoes (or small steaks, if you will). However, it is emblematic of a larger pattern of impulsive policy decisions that fail to consider long-term impacts. As we move forward, let’s hope for leadership that values informed decision-making and prioritizes the well-being of all sectors of the American economy—not just the interests of a select few or the whims of political rhetoric.
