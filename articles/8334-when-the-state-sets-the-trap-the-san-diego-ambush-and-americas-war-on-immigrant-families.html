When the State Sets the Trap: The San Diego Ambush and America’s War on Immigrant Families

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 26, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The New York Times didn’t break a story this week; it uncovered a crime scene.

What unfolded inside the fluorescent-lit hallways of the San Diego U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services office was not law enforcement. It was not procedure. It was not public safety. It was a state-designed ambush, a bureaucratic sting operation targeting the very people federal law says must be protected. Couples walked in expecting to finalize their green cards, carrying photo albums, joint bank statements, and diaper bags. They walked out with their spouses in handcuffs.

This wasn’t an accident. It wasn’t a misunderstanding. It wasn’t even overreach.

It was cruelty by design.

The Honeypot Strategy: Turn the USCIS Office Into a Snare

The married couples who came to these interviews had done everything the government demanded. They filed the forms. They paid the hundreds of dollars in fees. They were fingerprinted, medically examined, background-checked, and work-authorized.

Most importantly, they entered legally, which—per the 1986 congressional statute—makes them fully eligible to adjust their status even if their prior visas expired while the case was pending.

Congress was explicit: spouses of U.S. citizens, who entered lawfully, can receive green cards despite overstays.

For decades, administrations from both parties honored that directive. These cases weren’t controversial. They weren’t deportation priorities. They weren’t even news.

Until Trump decided to weaponize them.

Instead of adjudicating green cards, ICE waited until the final interview—often the moment a family breathes relief for the first time in months—and then stormed in, arrested the foreign spouse, and dragged them to detention centers.

No warnings. No policy change announcements. No memos. Just a quiet switch from rule of law to rule by trap.

And the numbers aren’t small. Immigration lawyers in San Diego estimate dozens of spouses have been detained since mid-November, with no transparency about how many more across the country.

USCIS—the agency designed to welcome new Americans—has been turned into a hunting ground.

Lives Upended in the Interview Room

The stories, as reported by the Times, are not “edge cases” or hypotheticals. They are families devastated in real time:

  • A British mother of a 4-month-old baby, arrested while holding her infant.

  • A robotics engineer from Germany, handcuffed by masked agents in bulletproof vests.

  • A young Mexican woman, sobbing as agents took her away while even the USCIS interviewer cried.

These aren’t criminals. These aren’t fugitives. These aren’t threats.

These are spouses of U.S. citizens, most with clean records, all following the process to the letter. Their “crime” is a lapsed visa—something Congress explicitly said must not disqualify them.

Yet the Trump administration has chosen to treat these families as high-value targets. Because nothing says “law and order” like terrorizing a nurse and her husband of one year. Nothing says “America First” like throwing a young mother into detention while her sheriff’s-deputy husband scrambles to take parental leave.

This is the new American immigration doctrine: make the process so terrifying that people abandon their legal rights.

ICE: The Cruelty Engine That Must Be Shut Down

ICE has long been the enforcement arm of a failed immigration system, but this is a new frontier. It’s not just over-policing. It’s not just overreach.

It’s entrapment.

ICE agents are being deployed inside a civilian agency—USCIS—to snatch people who were invited by the government to be there. Officers reportedly told one U.S. citizen husband they didn’t agree with the orders but had to follow them.

When even the people doing the arresting know the operation is rotten, the rot is complete.

ICE is beyond reform. You cannot salvage an agency whose mission has metastasized into state-sanctioned terror. It must be dismantled. Its functions must be redistributed to agencies bound by due process, not political sadism.

This is what happens when an administration views immigrants not as humans with rights but as objects—deterrents, symbols, warnings.

ICE’s raids inside USCIS offices turn American citizens into collateral damage. They turn marriages into liabilities. They turn lawful procedure into a pipeline to detention centers.

This isn’t immigration enforcement. It’s bureaucratic violence.

The Goal Is Not Deportation — It’s Surrender

Immigration lawyer Andrew Nietor said it plainly: the government’s strategy appears to be to break these couples into giving up.

Fight your case in immigration court? Good luck. Backlogs stretch for years.

Try to post bond? It will cost thousands.

Try to maintain your household on a single income while the other spouse is locked up? Impossible for many.

So families buckle. They “voluntarily depart.” They abandon their petitions. They give up.

And that’s the plan.

The state doesn’t need to deport everyone. It just needs to make the path so filled with terror and grief that people walk away on their own.

This is cruelty as administrative strategy.

Ambush Governance: The Trump Doctrine in Practice

The Trump administration didn’t announce this policy because they didn’t want the backlash. They wanted the shock. They wanted the panic. They wanted the fear to spread faster than the news cycle could keep up.

That’s the point of an ambush.

By the time families realize what’s happening, the net has already closed.

This is the same doctrine that brought you family separation. The same doctrine that erected the Muslim ban. The same doctrine that has turned ICE detention centers into overcrowded warehouses of human misery.

The law doesn’t matter. The regulation doesn’t matter. Congressional intent doesn’t matter.

Only the message matters: We can take you whenever we want.

America Must Decide What It Is

If a U.S. citizen cannot safely bring their spouse to a government office without risking an ambush, then we are no longer dealing with an immigration system. We are dealing with a state apparatus that has abandoned any pretense of humanity.

The San Diego interviews were supposed to be a celebration. A transition to stability, legality, and permanence.

Instead, they became scenes of horror.

Families ripped apart in front of their children. Couples left in limbo. Lawyers stunned. Even civil servants crying.

This is not “enforcement.” It is sadism wrapped in procedure.

And it is the direct consequence of a government that sees immigrants not as future Americans but as political props.

You can tell a lot about a nation by where it places its traps.

In San Diego, the traps were laid in the immigration office—in the very place designed to help people become Americans.

Because as always with Trump’s immigration agenda, the cruelty is not incidental. It’s not accidental. It’s not a flaw.

The cruelty is the point.
