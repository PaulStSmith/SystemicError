Biden family grift vs. Trump family grift: Let's do the numbers

Based on an article by Dean Baker

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real StoryWho Holds PowerThe Misdirection MachineTrump’s Presidency as Revenue StreamThe Numbers Tell the TruthWhat the Framing Still MissesThe Pattern Beneath the Headlines

Trump’s Open-Rack Graft, Biden’s Phantom Scandal

The Real Story

The source article does one useful thing: it puts numbers, however crude, next to a basic political fact that Washington keeps trying to blur. Hunter Biden’s behavior was tacky, unethical, and in some cases plainly exploitative. Donald Trump’s operation is something else entirely: a presidency run as a personal extraction machine, with state power converted into family revenue streams. That is the story. Everything else is camouflage.

Who Holds Power

The decisive power is not held by the adult children, the spouses, or the hangers-on. It sits with Donald Trump, with the offices he controls, and with the Republican machinery that protects him. Trump has the White House, Republicans control Congress and the Supreme Court, and the article makes clear that this is the condition that turns corruption from rumor into practice. Power is what makes the graft scalable. Power is what makes the cover possible.

The Misdirection Machine

Republicans spent years inflating the Biden family into a national corruption drama because it served a political purpose: muddy the water until ordinary theft looks like normal politics. The article is blunt about the asymmetry. It does not claim Joe Biden personally directed his relatives’ business dealings, and it notes that Republicans investigated aggressively while Trump was in office and still found no direct proof of presidential benefit. That matters. The point of the Biden story was never to build a serious theory of state capture. It was to train the public to accept the phrase “everyone does it” and stop asking who is actually in charge.

Trump’s Presidency as Revenue Stream

Trump’s operation is different because it is built around office, access, and institutional leverage. The article lays out a pattern: a reported $13 billion from Venezuelan oil with no clear accounting, a Trump family crypto empire, a Qatar plane gift the Pentagon is making expensive to retrofit, a pay-to-know Truth Social scheme, pardons wrapped around campaign money, Jared Kushner’s Gulf-funded hedge fund, and Trump sons benefiting from military contracts. Each item points to the same structure. The presidency is not being used to govern. It is being used to route value upward and outward to the Trump family.

The Numbers Tell the Truth

The article’s comparisons are crude by necessity, but the contrast is still decisive. Hunter Biden’s Burisma work, Chinese payments, and art sales are counted in millions. Trump-world corruption is counted in hundreds of millions and billions, and the article rightly treats those numbers as lower bounds, not ceilings. That is the critical political fact: one family’s misconduct was opportunistic and embarrassing; the other family’s conduct is embedded in the levers of government. One is a scandal. The other is a business model.

What the Framing Still Misses

The source article is strongest when it refuses false equivalence, but it still leaves one thing slightly too soft: this is not just “corruption” in the abstract. It is a regime habit. Trump does not merely tolerate graft around him; he normalizes a state where public office is a private asset, loyalists are enablers, and oversight is treated as a nuisance to be crushed. Republicans in Congress and the Court are not bystanders. They are the insulation. They make the theft durable.

The Pattern Beneath the Headlines

This is what contemporary American authoritarianism looks like in practice: deny the scale, inflate the counterexample, saturate the media with noise, and let institutional power do the rest. The public is told to compare a few unethical family transactions with a presidency organized around extraction. That comparison is not confusion. It is political design. The purpose is to make looting feel ordinary, make accountability feel partisan, and make the country accept a ruling family as if it were just another campaign scandal.
