'Are you kidding?' Texas GOP senators scoff at challenge from Jasmine Crockett

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 10, 20255 min read

Alexander Willis / Raw Story

WASHINGTON — Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) finally settled weeks of speculation on Monday by announcing her candidacy for U.S. Senate — but for the time being, at least, Texas’ two Republican senators do not feel threatened by her.

Asked for comment by Raw Story, Sen. John Cornyn, the senator currently seeking reelection, said, “I welcome her to the race.”

Cornyn, who faces a serious threat in his own primary by Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, added that he doesn’t consider her a substantive candidate.

“No, are you kidding? Has she actually passed anything?” said Cornyn. “I think that’s where the Democratic Party is today. Getting a guy like Colin Allred to bow out because he’s afraid of losing the primary is all you really need to see.”

Sen. Ted Cruz had a similar assessment of his colleague’s competition.

“The Democrat Party has decided to be the party of crazy, and I can’t think of a better frontman for the job,” Cruz told Raw Story. “I don’t think Texas is terribly eager to be represented by a hard-left radical. And [Zohran] Mamdani may be elected in Manhattan, but I don’t think that Texas is going down the same road.”

Crockett, one of President Donald Trump’s most outspoken detractors in Congress, has said she wants to focus on driving Democratic base voter enthusiasm, but has also speculated in a recent interview she could compete for some lower-propensity Trump voters: “We are going to be able to get people that potentially have voted for Trump, even though I obviously am one of his loudest opponents, because at the end of the day, they vote for who they believe is fighting for them. It’s about moving people, and I’ve got a track record of doing that.”

With former Rep. Colin Allred (D-TX) bowing out of the Senate race to run for a congressional district in Dallas, Crockett’s main primary opponent will be James Talarico, an Austin-area state representative and Presbyterian seminarian.

Our Analysis:

The Political Arena: A Closer Look at Texas’ Senate Race Dynamics

In the latest twist in Texas politics, Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) has thrown her hat into the ring for the U.S. Senate, setting the stage for what promises to be an intriguing electoral battle. However, the reactions from Texas’ current Republican senators, John Cornyn and Ted Cruz, reveal a deeper narrative about political posturing and strategic dismissal that merits a scathing analysis.

Cornyn’s Condescension and Cruz’s Contempt

Firstly, Sen. John Cornyn’s response to Crockett’s candidacy is a masterpiece of political patronization. “I welcome her to the race,” he states, with a tone that barely conceals his derision. His follow-up comment, questioning Crockett’s legislative achievements, is not just a dig at her but a broader swipe at the Democratic Party. Cornyn’s remarks are a classic example of a political strategy designed to undermine an opponent’s credibility from the get-go. Yet, it’s essential to highlight the irony in Cornyn’s own precarious situation, facing a “serious threat” from Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton in his primary. This scenario begs the question: is Cornyn’s bravado a mask for his vulnerability?

Sen. Ted Cruz’s assessment drips with even more disdain. Describing the Democratic Party as “the party of crazy” and casting Crockett as a “hard-left radical” are not just dismissive but strategically crafted to stigmatize her candidacy. Cruz’s attempt to alienate Crockett from the Texas electorate by comparing her unfavorably to Zohran Mamdani, presumably more palatable in Manhattan than in Texas, is a classic fearmongering tactic. It’s an old playbook move: label your opponent as an extremist to scare off moderate voters.

Crockett’s Counter-Narrative

Amidst the Republican senators’ scornful remarks, Rep. Jasmine Crockett’s stance shines as a beacon of resilience and strategic insight. Her focus on driving Democratic base voter enthusiasm, coupled with her ambition to appeal to “lower-propensity Trump voters,” showcases a nuanced understanding of the complex Texan electorate. Crockett is not just playing the game; she’s attempting to redefine it.

Her assertion that she can move people, even those who may have supported Trump, by highlighting who is genuinely fighting for their interests, is a powerful narrative. It speaks to a broader political wisdom: voters are more than their party affiliations. They are individuals with concerns that transcend partisan boundaries. Crockett’s strategy, aiming to bridge these divides, contrasts sharply with the divisive rhetoric from her Republican counterparts.

The Democratic Primary: A Prelude to Battle

With Colin Allred stepping out of the Senate race for a congressional run in Dallas, Crockett’s primary battle against James Talarico, an Austin-area state representative and Presbyterian seminarian, becomes a focal point of interest. This primary presents an opportunity for Crockett to solidify her base and refine her message before taking on the Republican nominee.

Conclusion: Underestimation at One’s Peril

The reactions from Cornyn and Cruz are not just examples of political posturing; they reflect a broader trend of underestimating opponents based on ideological grounds. However, history is littered with instances where such underestimation has led to shocking upsets. Crockett’s candidacy, backed by a strategic approach to voter engagement and a message of unity over division, may yet surprise those who write her off too quickly.

In the end, the Texas Senate race is shaping up to be more than a mere electoral contest; it’s a battleground for the soul of Texan politics. And if there’s one thing that’s certain, it’s that underestimating Jasmine Crockett could be a grave mistake for the Republican establishment. The political arena is unpredictable, and as the race heats up, all eyes will be on Texas.
