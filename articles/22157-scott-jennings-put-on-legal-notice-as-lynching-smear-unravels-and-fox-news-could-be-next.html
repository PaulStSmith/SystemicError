Scott Jennings put on legal notice as lynching smear unravels — and Fox News could be next

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Right-Wing Media Doesn’t Misunderstand Context. It Destroys It

A Manufactured Smear

Scott Jennings aired a stitched-together montage of Hasan Piker’s Twitch streams on his radio show and used it to accuse Piker of endorsing violence against Black voters. Snopes found the clip had been spliced from separate streams and stripped of context; the underlying footage was real, but the meaning had been manufactured. Piker says a defamation suit is coming, and the damage has already expanded beyond reputational harm into death threats and security costs.

That is the story. Everything else is camouflage.

Who Actually Had Power

Jennings had the platform. Salem Media had the distribution. Fox News helped launder the same clip when Harris Faulkner repeated it on air. That is institutional power: the ability to take a distorted fragment, wrap it in authority, and blast it across television and radio until the lie feels like a fact with a microphone.

Piker is an influencer with a large audience. Jennings is a syndicated pundit with CNN credentials and access to conservative media machinery. Those are not equivalent positions. One side manufactures a narrative; the other side gets forced into legal defense against it.

The Smear Was the Point

Jennings did not stumble into confusion. He claimed Piker’s “best friend” was “calling for Black voters to be lynched,” then doubled down online and told people to “listen for yourself.” That is not a misunderstanding. It is a premeditated editorial choice: cut the clip, erase the context, name the target, and dare the audience to accept the lie because it arrived through familiar partisan channels.

Piker’s point about clip culture matters here, but only as a description of the weapon, not an excuse. Live-streamed political conversation is easy to splice because that is what the format is. The people amplifying these snippets know exactly how the medium works. They are not victims of the medium. They are exploiting its weaknesses for partisan attack.

CNN’s Name Is Part of the Problem

Jennings’ CNN role matters because it gives the smear more weight than a generic radio hit ever could. CNN is not being deceived by a random troll account. It is choosing to keep a contributor whose public conduct includes airing a manipulated compilation and pushing it as evidence. That is not neutrality. It is reputational laundering.

Piker also pointed to Jonah Goldberg’s July claim on CNN that he “abuses his dog on camera,” later walked back by Kasie Hunt. That pattern is the point. CNN keeps treating defamatory smear artists as usable panelists and then acts surprised when the audience concludes the network’s standards are decorative. If a network will not draw a line when its contributors traffic in lies, then it is not protecting discourse. It is monetizing degradation.

The Real Political Work Is Misdirection

The article’s framing is too polite for what is happening. This is not a culture-war misunderstanding between loud personalities. It is a right-wing media ecosystem using fabricated context to turn a political commentator into a target, then passing the result through national outlets to lend it legitimacy.

Piker’s suggestion that Republican operatives fed Jennings the footage is an allegation, not a proven fact. But it fits the larger pattern: the GOP does not need to win arguments when it can flood the zone with lies, outrage, and decontextualized video. In that environment, the function of the lie is not just persuasion. It is exhaustion. It buries actual political accountability under performative scandal.

And the target matters. The claim was linked to Abdul El-Sayed, a Michigan Senate nominee. That means the smear is doing campaign work. It is not random cruelty. It is electoral sabotage dressed up as commentary.

The Pattern Behind the Noise

This is what authoritarian media culture looks like in practice: invent the offense, amplify it through credentialed outlets, force the target into defensive survival mode, and call the whole thing a debate. The harm is real. The threats are real. The asymmetry is real.

Democracy does not survive by pretending these actors are confused or merely overheated. It survives when institutions stop laundering lies for people who know exactly what they are doing. Jennings’ conduct is not a lapse in civility. It is propaganda work. Salem Media and Fox helped carry it. CNN’s continued association with him shows how much establishment media still tolerates from the right as long as it can package the abuse as commentary.

The broader lesson is simple: when media institutions reward deception because it is commercially useful and politically aligned, they become part of the machinery of intimidation. The problem is not that bad actors occasionally break the rules. The problem is that the rules themselves are being hollowed out by people with platforms who treat truth as a negotiable inconvenience.
