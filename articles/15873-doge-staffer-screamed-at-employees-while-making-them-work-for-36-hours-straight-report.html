DOGE staffer screamed at employees while making them work 'for 36 hours straight': report

Employees at one federal agency were reportedly kept up for more than a day and a half straight due to the demands of a staffer from South African centibillionaire's Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE).

That's according to a Friday article from CNN, which reported on DOGE staffer Gavin Kliger's alleged treatment of workers at the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB). The allegations stem from a sworn declaration a CFPB employee made in U.S. District judge Amy Berman Jackson's court anonymously out of fear of retaliation.

In the declaration, the worker stated that Kliger "kept CFPB staffers working for 36 hours straight to send out mass layoff notices" and was apparently "screaming at those he thought weren’t working fast enough." Kliger — who Reuters reported had amplified content from white supremacist Nick Fuentes — has since been ordered to appear at a hearing scheduled for April 28 pertaining to the mass firing of CFPB employees.

On Friday, Judge Jackson ruled that the roughly 1,500 fired CFPB workers (out of approximately 1,700) would have to be reinstated, as she weighed whether their firings were a violation of a prior court ordered preventing DOGE from effectively shutting down the agency aimed at protecting American consumers from financial scams.

That prior decision ruled that the mass firings of workers at the agency were illegal, as it would prevent the CFPB from meeting its statutory requirements that Congress legislated at the time of its creation. Because the CFPB is a Congressionally authorized agency, it would take an act of Congress to dismantle it.

The Trump administration argued that the mass firings were legitimate, asserting that the CFPB had "pushed well beyond the limits of the law" in its work while "engag[ing] in intrusive and wasteful fishing expedition. Mark Paoletta, who the administration named as the CFPB's chief legal officer, insisted that leaving the CFPB with just 200 employees was sufficient for the agency to fulfull its statutory obligations.

"An approximately 200 person agency allows the Bureau to fulfill its statutory duties and better aligns with the new leadership’s priorities and management philosophy," he said.

The Erosion of Consumer Protection: A Critical Analysis of the CFPB Mass Layoffs

In a recent report from CNN, a harrowing account emerged from the depths of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), painting a picture of a federal agency under siege by the whims of a South African centibillionaire's Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE). This story, however, is not just a tale of overworked government employees; it's a glaring symptom of a much larger disease eroding the foundations of consumer rights and protections in the United States.

Excessive Demands and Questionable Ethics

At the heart of this debacle is Gavin Kliger, a DOGE staffer whose methods and motivations have sparked significant controversy. According to a sworn declaration made anonymously by a CFPB employee, Kliger allegedly forced staffers to work for 36 hours straight to issue mass layoff notices, all while reportedly "screaming at those he thought weren’t working fast enough." The severity of these accusations cannot be overstated. Not only do they raise serious concerns about workplace abuse and harassment, but they also spotlight the reckless disregard for the well-being of employees tasked with protecting American consumers from financial scams.

The Shadow of White Supremacy

Adding a more sinister layer to Kliger's profile is his reported amplification of content from white supremacist Nick Fuentes. This connection is not just a personal failing of Kliger but a reflection of the toxic ideologies infiltrating spaces of power and influence. When individuals who endorse or promote hate are placed in positions that can shape policies and practices, the consequences can be devastatingly far-reaching.

Judicial Intervention and the Battle for the CFPB's Soul

In a move that underscores the gravity of the situation, Judge Amy Berman Jackson has ruled that the approximately 1,500 dismissed CFPB workers must be reinstated. This decision not only challenges the mass firings but also calls into question the Trump administration's assertion that reducing the CFPB to a skeleton crew of 200 employees would suffice for the agency to fulfill its statutory duties. The administration's argument, as voiced by the CFPB's chief legal officer Mark Paoletta, dangerously misconstrues the essence of the bureau's mission and its importance in safeguarding consumer rights.

The Bigger Picture: A System Under Threat

This episode is symptomatic of a broader assault on regulatory agencies and the principles of consumer protection. The audacity to dismantle or debilitate an agency through mass layoffs, under the guise of efficiency and alignment with new leadership priorities, is a stark reminder of the ongoing efforts to undermine the checks and balances designed to protect the public from corporate overreach and malfeasance.

The Trump administration's characterization of the CFPB's work as "pushing well beyond the limits of the law" and engaging in "intrusive and wasteful fishing expeditions" is not just a gross misrepresentation; it's an attempt to vilify the very mechanisms that ensure corporate accountability and transparency. By framing consumer protection efforts as overreaching, those in power pave the way for deregulation that disproportionately benefits the wealthy and powerful at the expense of the average American.

Conclusion: The Need for Vigilance and Resistance

The saga of the CFPB's mass layoffs is a cautionary tale of what happens when government agencies, designed to serve the public good, become battlegrounds for ideological warfare and corporate interests. It is a clear indication that the fight for consumer rights is far from over and that vigilance and resistance are more crucial than ever.

This incident should serve as a rallying cry for policymakers, advocates, and the public to stand firm in defense of regulatory agencies like the CFPB. It's not just about protecting jobs; it's about safeguarding the mechanisms that protect us all from financial exploitation and abuse. The reinstatement of the CFPB workers is a temporary reprieve, but the underlying struggle for the soul of consumer protection continues.
