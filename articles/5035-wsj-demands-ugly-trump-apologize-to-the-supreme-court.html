WSJ demands 'ugly' Trump apologize to the Supreme Court

The conservative learning Wall Street Journal blasted President Donald Trump for “smearing” members of the Supreme Court who overruled his unilateral tariff policy Friday.

“President Trump owes the Supreme Court an apology — to the individual Justices he smeared on Friday and the institution itself,” WSJ reported. “Mr. Trump doubtless won’t offer one, but his rant in response to his tariff defeat at the Court was arguably the worst moment of his Presidency.”

Where other presidents have criticized SCOTUS when they didn’t like a ruling, WSJ said Trump “lit into the Justices who voted against him as traitors bought by foreign interests.”

Trump called the liberals on the court a “disgrace to our nation,” but reserved even more hate for friendly justices he’d expected to side with him due to their nearly rubberstamp approval in past cases.

“[They] think they’re being ‘politically correct,’ which has happened before, far too often, with certain members of this Court, when, in fact, they’re just being fools and lapdogs for the RINOs and the radical left Democrats—and . . . they’re very unpatriotic and disloyal to our Constitution,” Trump spat. “It’s my opinion that the Court has been swayed by foreign interests.”

“This is ugly even by Mr. Trump’s standards,” said the WSJ. “He’s accusing them of betraying the U.S. at the behest of nefarious interests he didn’t identify, no doubt because they don’t exist. Asked about Justices Gorsuch and Barrett, whom he appointed, Mr. Trump called them ‘an embarrassment to their families.’”

The WSJ editorial board pointed out that “this is the same Court that ruled Mr. Trump’s way on presidential immunity, which was more personally consequential for this President.”

“Mr. Trump shouldn’t have been surprised by the Court. We warned from the start that this would be the result of his unlawful resort to IEEPA. The fault doesn’t lie with the Justices but with his own tariff obsessions,” WSJ said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Supreme Court Tirade: A New Low in Presidential Decorum

The Wall Street Journal’s recent editorial blasts President Donald Trump’s ferocious attack on the Supreme Court, setting a new precedent for presidential contempt towards the judiciary. Trump’s incendiary remarks came in response to the Court’s rejection of his unilateral tariff policy, a decision that led him to accuse the justices of betrayal and allegiance to foreign interests. This episode not only highlights Trump’s disregard for the separation of powers but also underscores a troubling trend of undermining judicial independence.

The Assault on Judicial Integrity

Trump’s rhetoric against the Supreme Court justices who ruled against him was nothing short of a smear campaign. Labeling the liberal justices a “disgrace to our nation” and accusing the Court of being swayed by foreign interests are baseless allegations that serve no purpose other than to sow discord and undermine public trust in one of the pillars of American democracy. The President’s comments were not just an attack on the individuals he singled out but an affront to the institution of the Supreme Court itself.

A Misguided Expectation of Loyalty

Trump’s disappointment with Justices Gorsuch and Barrett, whom he nominated, for not ruling in his favor, reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of the judiciary. The expectation that justices should act as “lapdogs” for the administration that appointed them is a dangerous precedent. It suggests a preference for a judiciary that serves the whims of the executive rather than upholding the Constitution and the law. Trump’s characterization of Gorsuch and Barrett as an “embarrassment to their families” for their perceived disloyalty is not only inappropriate but also deeply personal and unwarranted.

The Irony of Presidential Immunity

It’s particularly ironic that Trump would lash out at the same Court that ruled in his favor on matters of presidential immunity. This previous decision was significantly more personal and beneficial to Trump, yet he was quick to turn on the Court when the decision did not align with his interests. This fickleness underscores a transactional view of the judiciary, where loyalty is expected, and decisions are only respected when they serve Trump’s agenda.

The Real Culprit: Trump’s Tariff Obsession

The Wall Street Journal rightly points out that the blame for the Supreme Court’s decision lies not with the justices but with Trump’s own “tariff obsessions.” His reliance on the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) as a basis for imposing tariffs was a legally questionable move that the Court ultimately found untenable. Trump’s inability to accept this outcome betrays a deeper issue: an obsession with pursuing policies by any means necessary, regardless of their legality or the consequences for democratic institutions.

Conclusion: A Destructive Path

Trump’s response to the Supreme Court’s ruling is not just “ugly even by Mr. Trump’s standards,” as the Wall Street Journal puts it, but a dangerous escalation in the erosion of democratic norms. By attacking the judiciary and undermining its independence, Trump is treading a destructive path that threatens the very foundation of American democracy. An apology to the Supreme Court is the least that Trump owes, but the damage to the institution and its reputation will not be so easily repaired.
