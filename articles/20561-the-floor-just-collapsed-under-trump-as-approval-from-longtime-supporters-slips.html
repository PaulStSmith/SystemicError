The floor just collapsed under Trump as approval from longtime supporters slips

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20263 min read

Trump Is Bleeding the Coalition That Powered Him

The Poll Is the Story

A new Focaldata/Financial Times survey shows Donald Trump’s net approval among men falling from -13 to -19 in one month, with his overall approval at 34 percent and disapproval at 57 percent. That matters because men were not an accessory to his coalition in 2024; they were one of its load-bearing walls. The same poll says his support is collapsing in the very areas where he claimed competence: immigration, inflation, jobs, and the economy.

This is not a communications problem. It is a governing problem showing up in the numbers.

Power Has a Name

Trump holds the institutional power. He ran on strength, singular authority, and the fantasy that one man could impose order on a broken economy and a chaotic state. He owns the results. If inflation remains punishing, if the cost of living keeps grinding people down, if jobs and economic confidence are weak, that is not an abstract drift in sentiment. That is the public judging the administration’s actual performance.

The poll does not describe confusion among voters. It describes a ruling coalition losing faith in the ruler.

The Useful Myth of Loyalty

The press often treats Trump’s base as if it were a durable political law, something beyond accountability. The source article correctly notes erosion among men and the “Reluctant Right,” but the deeper pattern is that these voters were never a permanent ideological bloc. They were assembled through grievance, negative partisanship, and low-grade authoritarian promise: hurt the people they hate, and ignore the damage everywhere else.

Now that bargain is fraying. Younger voters in the Reluctant Right are apparently reacting to the Epstein scandal, the disastrous war with Iran, and the collapsing economy. That is not a mystery. It is what happens when a coalition built on resentment collides with reality and starts costing its members too much.

No More Fantasy of Competence

Trump’s worst numbers in the poll are exactly where his brand was supposed to be strongest. On immigration and border security, approval is underwater. On inflation and the cost of living, the gap is catastrophic. On jobs and the economy, he is not merely unpopular; he is failing the core test that presidents set for themselves every day.

That exposes the central fraud of his politics. He does not deliver order. He markets domination. He does not solve material problems. He repackages them as enemies, culture-war spectacle, and permanent emergency. Once voters stop buying the performance, there is not much left.

The Midterm Warning

The article gestures toward 2026, but the more immediate lesson is that Trump’s coalition is becoming less reliable under pressure. When a president loses men, younger voters, and the reluctant edge of his own support, the issue is not merely approval. It is mobilization. Voters who came in half-convinced can leave quickly, and they are often the first to stop showing up.

That does not mean the political system corrects itself automatically. It means Trump is entering a phase where disappointment can harden into abstention, defection, or cynicism. None of those outcomes is democratic renewal by itself. They are openings. What matters is whether the opposition can turn that opening into durable power instead of just hoping collapse does the work for it.

The Bigger Pattern

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics always depends on a coalition that mistakes cruelty for competence. It runs on the promise that humiliation of enemies will substitute for governing. Then the bills come due, the coalition gets smaller, and the public notices that the strongman was never strong, only loud.

Trump’s men are slipping because the deal is failing. The republic does not need to admire that fact. It needs to understand what it means: when performance propaganda can no longer cover material failure, the people who bought the performance start looking for the exit.
