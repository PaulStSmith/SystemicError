Humiliation for Trump's hatchet woman is a win for everyone else

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Poetic Justice of Sandwich Rebellion: A Critique of Jeanine Pirro's Prosecutorial Zeal

It’s a case of poetic justice made for the Internet.

Self-adulating U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro just received a large dose of humiliation, not mitigated in the slightest by her own inability to feel shame.

You know the famous adage that “you can get a grand jury to indict a ham sandwich”?

Well, a Washington, D.C. grand jury sided Tuesday not only with the sandwich but with the young man who slung it at the chest of a Customs and Border Protection agent who Donald Trump wants to appropriate as a storm trooper.

The grand jury took the extraordinary step of declining felony charges against Sean Charles Dunn, 37, a former Justice Department paralegal, who had been enraged by the presence of agents in a peaceful D.C. restaurant-and-bar neighborhood.

Dunn has become something of an Internet folk hero since video captured him hurling his protest sub.

Undoubtedly, the grand jurors had to be pushing back against what they saw as a dramatic overcharging of Dunn with a felony. Good for them.

Why? Because ever since her broom landed in the U.S. Attorney’s office, Pirro has cackled that every Trumpian case on her watch must be charged up to the max. Just because she can.

There should be another adage for that: Bad people make bad prosecutors.

Get ready to be tired of losing.

And I’m enjoying the online backup that Dunn has been receiving, with my personal favorite being this one from poster Tim Massie raising the perfect question:

"What did Pirro want him charged with? Assault with a deli weapon?”

Let us not forget that this same Pirro was just fine with Trump pardoning as patriots 1,500 January 6 insurrectionists — including 14 commutations for full-out violent criminals — for their role in trying to overturn his election defeat on January 6, 2021.

Four policemen lost their lives, and at least 174 were injured as a result of the riot at the U.S. Capitol.

Trump’s patriots were not known to attack police with a single sandwich. No — their weapons included firearms, tasers, knives, crowbars, flagpoles bearing the American flag, fire extinguishers, and pepper spray.

So we don’t need a single lecture from the former Fox News ghoul — nor the First Felon — about protecting police.

Allow me to share the insults Dunn hurled at Trump’s police-state platoon along with the sandwich, straight from the police report:

“F––– you! You f–––––– fascists! Why are you here? I don’t want you in my city!”

Maybe Dunn shouldn’t have wasted his sandwich on this, but I’m fine with him speaking his piece as an American who doesn’t wish to live in a police state. Even better were the grand jurors who sent “Judge Jeanine” a loud-and-clear sandwich message Tuesday.

Now, let the good memes roll.

The Poetic Justice of Sandwich Rebellion: A Critique of Jeanine Pirro's Prosecutorial Zeal

In a saga that reads like a script straight out of a satirical playbook, U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro finds herself at the center of a delicious controversy. The case involves one Sean Charles Dunn, a 37-year-old ex-Justice Department paralegal, who, in a moment of politically charged fervor, launched his sandwich at a Customs and Border Protection agent. This act, emblematic of protest rather than violence, has spiraled into an unexpected referendum on prosecutorial overreach and the absurdity of legal discretion under Pirro's reign.

The Sandwich Incident: A Misguided Felony Charge

It's a narrative befitting the digital age: a sandwich, wielded not as a weapon of harm but as an instrument of protest, becomes the centerpiece of a legal battle that underscores the chasm between public sentiment and prosecutorial zeal. The grand jury's decision to decline felony charges against Dunn is a clear rebuke of the overzealous approach favored by Pirro. It's a moment of sanity in a justice system that seems increasingly detached from the principles of proportionality and fairness.

Pirro's Prosecutorial Philosophy: Charging to the Max

Jeanine Pirro's tenure as U.S. Attorney has been marked by a relentless pursuit of maximal charges, a strategy that reeks of vindictiveness rather than justice. This "charge to the max" philosophy, as the article aptly points out, not only undermines the integrity of the legal system but also betrays a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of a prosecutor. Prosecutors wield immense power over the lives of individuals and the fabric of communities; when wielded with such reckless abandon, this power can erode trust in legal institutions.

The Stark Contrast: January 6 Insurrectionists vs. A Sandwich Thrower

The article draws a stark, biting contrast between the treatment of the January 6 insurrectionists and Dunn, the sandwich assailant. While Pirro seems all too comfortable with the idea of pardoning violent criminals involved in a deadly siege on the Capitol, she appears equally enthusiastic about pursuing disproportionate charges against a man whose "weapon" was a deli sandwich. This juxtaposition lays bare the hypocrisy and selective indignation that plagues Pirro's prosecutorial judgment.

The Verdict of Public Opinion and the Role of Memes

Public reaction to the case, particularly online, underscores a collective incredulity towards the prosecutorial priorities displayed by Pirro's office. Memes, often the currency of internet discourse, serve as a conduit for a broader societal critique, encapsulating the absurdity of the situation while highlighting the democratic value of dissent—even when it's sandwich-armed.

Conclusion: A Sandwich Message to Judicial Overreach

The refusal of the grand jury to indict Dunn on felony charges is more than just a legal outcome; it's a societal statement against the misuse of prosecutorial power. It sends a clear message to Pirro and her ilk: bad people do indeed make bad prosecutors, and the law should not be a weapon wielded in service of political vendettas or personal whims. In the end, this case is not just about a sandwich thrown in protest; it's about the enduring American values of justice, proportionality, and the right to dissent. The grand jury, in its wisdom, chose to side with these principles over the prosecutorial overreach that has come to define Pirro's approach to justice. Let the good memes roll, indeed, but let's also ensure that the lessons of this case inform a broader conversation about justice in America.
