'Putin and his cronies laugh at Trump': Ex-Swedish PM says US being 'played' by Russia

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Administration's Ukrainian Quagmire: A Dissection

U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin hold a bilateral meeting at the G20 leaders summit in Osaka, Japan June 28, 2019. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

Former Swedish Prime Minister Carl Bildt has criticized President Donald Trump for his failure to restore peace in Ukraine despite his pledge to end the war on "day one."

"It has been nearly 100 days since Donald Trump returned to the U.S. presidency and Russian missiles continue to rain down on Ukrainian civilians. Despite Trump’s pledge to end the war on 'day one,' peace is nowhere in sight. When will the administration acknowledge that it is failing?" Bildt wrote in an op-ed published in the Ukrainian newspaper The Kyiv Independent on Monday.

He added that there has been no ceasefire, nor are there any signs that Russian President Vladimir Putin is willing to agree to one. "He has continued to launch attacks on civilian targets in Ukrainian cities with impunity," wrote the former Swedish PM.

Bildt noted that since Trump's first conversation with Putin, the Russian president and his team have been reluctant to show flexibility and make progress in the negotiations. The Swedish politician went on to call Trump's Special Envoy Steve Witkoff an "inexperienced negotiator," saying that he has been dragged down "a rabbit hole of complex conditionalities and impossible demands" by the Russian side.

"After many weeks of this, even the most dimwitted of negotiators should have realized that Putin has no intention of agreeing to a ceasefire or accepting Trump’s plan or timetable," Bildt said of Witkoff.

"In theory, Trump himself could change course by applying serious pressure on Putin and stepping up support for Ukraine. If that were to happen, he might then achieve the ceasefire that he seeks. Otherwise, he will continue to fail — as Putin and his cronies laugh at him behind his back," he added.

Bildt said Putin was "playing" Trump by deploying "all the resources at his disposal."

"Everyone knows that the U.S. president is highly susceptible to flattery, and Putin has gone overboard in arguing that the 2020 U.S. presidential election was 'stolen,' and that the war never would have happened with Trump in office. He wants us to believe that he went to his private chapel to pray for Trump after the assassination attempt in 2024, and he has even had the Kremlin court artist paint a portrait of Trump as a gift," he said.

Meanwhile, Witkoff is slated to visit Moscow this week, according to Russian presidential aide Yuri Ushakov who confirmed the planned visit to Russian state-controlled media outlet Interfax.

Witkoff, who serves as the Trump administration's representative to the Middle East, has traveled to Russia multiple times this year, engaging in three meetings with Putin. His actions have faced backlash from officials in both the U.S. and Ukraine for advocating positions that align with the Kremlin, such as the proposal of exchanging Ukrainian land for peace.

AlterNet reached out to the State Department for comment.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's Ukrainian Quagmire: A Dissection

Former Swedish Prime Minister Carl Bildt's recent op-ed in The Kyiv Independent doesn't just throw a spotlight on President Donald Trump's unfulfilled promise to end the war in Ukraine; it rips open the festering wound of international diplomacy under the Trump administration. Bildt's critique, grounded in the stark reality of continued Russian aggression against Ukraine, serves as a damning indictment of Trump's foreign policy prowess—or the lack thereof.

The Unkept Promise

Let's start with the elephant in the room: Trump's bold pledge to end the Ukrainian conflict "on day one" of his presidency. This promise has aged about as well as milk in the sun, with Russian missiles still "raining down on Ukrainian civilians" nearly 100 days into Trump's term. Bildt's question, "When will the administration acknowledge that it is failing?" is rhetorical but piercing. It underscores a glaring gap between Trump's campaign bravado and his administration's tangible achievements—or in this case, the total absence thereof.

Putin's Unyielding Stance

Bildt correctly highlights the lack of a ceasefire and the apparent disinterest from Russian President Vladimir Putin in reaching one. This isn't just an oversight; it's a strategic maneuver. Putin's continued attacks on civilian targets without repercussion underscore a grim reality: Russia acts with impunity, emboldened by a lack of decisive pushback from the Trump administration.

The Negotiation Debacle

Turning our gaze to the negotiation table, where Trump's Special Envoy Steve Witkoff finds himself outmatched and outplayed. Described by Bildt as an "inexperienced negotiator," Witkoff's portrayal is less a personal attack and more a symptom of a broader issue: the Trump administration's apparent disregard for the complexity of international diplomacy. Being "dragged down a rabbit hole of complex conditionalities and impossible demands" by Russia isn't just a negotiation failure; it's a strategic blunder of epic proportions.

Trump's Misplaced Optimism

Bildt's analysis doesn't let Trump off the hook, suggesting that the President could theoretically pivot by applying "serious pressure on Putin" and increasing support for Ukraine. Yet, this notion feels almost whimsical in the context of Trump's track record, where bravado often supersedes strategy.

Flattery and Manipulation

Perhaps the most scathing part of Bildt's critique is the assertion that Putin is "playing" Trump, exploiting the latter's vulnerability to flattery. The absurdity of Putin's overtures to Trump—praying in a private chapel, commissioning a Kremlin court artist for a portrait—would be laughable if the stakes weren't so tragically high. This manipulation tactic not only undermines U.S. authority but also exposes a dangerous flaw in Trump's foreign policy approach: susceptibility to ego-stroking.

The Kremlin-Aligned Special Envoy

Steve Witkoff's upcoming visit to Moscow, confirmed by Russian presidential aide Yuri Ushakov, adds another layer of concern. Witkoff's engagements in Russia, especially advocating positions that align with the Kremlin, such as land exchanges, betray a worrying trend of U.S. foreign policy seemingly capitulating to Russian interests.

Conclusion: A Foreign Policy in Shambles

Carl Bildt's op-ed doesn't just criticize; it eviscerates the notion that Trump's administration is effective in handling the Ukrainian crisis. From unkept promises to ineffective negotiations and susceptibility to flattery, the Trump administration's approach to Ukraine is depicted as a case study in how not to conduct foreign policy. As Bildt's analysis suggests, the real losers in this diplomatic debacle are the Ukrainian civilians, who continue to suffer under the shadow of Russian aggression, while U.S. leadership, under Trump's guidance, flounders on the international stage.
