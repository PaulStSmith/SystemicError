Trump says he doesn't want to 'frighten off' foreign companies after raid on Hyundai

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks with the media on the day of the signing of an executive order to rename the Department of Defense the “Department of War”, in the Oval Office, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 5, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump tried to do damage control after the Department of Homeland Security raided a Hyundai plant in Georgia and detained hundreds of workers with legal visas.

"When Foreign Companies who are building extremely complex products, machines, and various other 'things,' come into the United States with massive Investments, I want them to bring their people of expertise for a period of time to teach and train our people how to make these very unique and complex products, as they phase out of our Country, and back into their land," Trump wrote Sunday on Truth Social.

"If we didn't do this, all of that massive Investment will never come in the first place — Chips, Semiconductors, Computers, Ships, Trains, and so many other products that we have to learn from others how to make, or, in many cases, relearn, because we used to be great at it, but not anymore," the president acknowledged.

"I don't want to frighten off or disincentivize Investment into America by outside Countries or Companies."

Despite placing the South Korean employees in shackles, Trump insisted on Sunday that "we welcome" foreign workers.

"We are willing to proudly say we will learn from them, and do even better than them at their own 'game,' sometime into the not too distant future!" he remarked.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Contradiction: Trump's Immigration Tango

In a display of political gymnastics that could only be choreographed by President Donald Trump himself, we find ourselves dissecting a recent episode that strikes at the very heart of contradiction and confusion. The scene? A Hyundai plant in Georgia, where the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) decided it was high time to raid and detain hundreds of workers. The twist? These workers were not hiding in the shadows with forged paperwork; they were in the United States on legal visas.

Trump's Attempt at Damage Control

In what can only be described as a frantic attempt at damage control, Trump took to Truth Social to express his views. His message was a dizzying mix of welcoming foreign investment and expertise while simultaneously overseeing an administration that shackles the very embodiment of that foreign know-how. Trump writes, "When Foreign Companies who are building extremely complex products, machines, and various other 'things,' come into the United States with massive Investments, I want them to bring their people of expertise for a period of time to teach and train our people."

This statement, while seemingly reasonable, clashes spectacularly with the reality orchestrated by his DHS. The optics of welcoming foreign experts with one hand while detaining them with the other is a masterpiece of contradiction.

Investment vs. Intimidation

Trump's concern over frightening off foreign investment by being too harsh on immigration is valid. Yet, the irony is so thick you could cut it with a knife. The president acknowledges the importance of foreign investment and the need to learn, or relearn, from these international partners. He states, "Chips, Semiconductors, Computers, Ships, Trains, and so many other products that we have to learn from others how to make, or, in many cases, relearn, because we used to be great at it, but not anymore."

The acknowledgment here is a rare moment of clarity — America's industrial and technological prowess has slipped, and foreign expertise is not just welcome; it's necessary. However, detaining those who come to share this expertise under legal auspices sends a conflicting message that undermines the very investment Trump claims to covet.

"We Welcome" Foreign Workers

Perhaps the most jarring part of this saga is Trump's insistence that "we welcome" foreign workers, a statement made even as South Korean employees were placed in shackles. The cognitive dissonance needed to reconcile these actions with his words would be commendable if it weren't so damaging.

Trump's vision of learning from others and then surpassing them "at their own 'game'" is a lofty and admirable goal. But it's a goal that seems fantastical when those very teachers are met with handcuffs rather than handshakes.

Conclusion

In this latest chapter of Trump's administration, the message is as clear as mud. The raid at the Hyundai plant in Georgia, followed by Trump's bewildering attempt at reassurance, encapsulates the ongoing struggle within his presidency: the desire for foreign investment and expertise at loggerheads with a harsh, often contradictory immigration stance.

It's a juggling act of epic proportions, trying to attract and detain at the same time. This episode not only highlights the systemic error in Trump's approach to immigration and foreign investment but also casts a long shadow over America's ability to truly learn and relearn from the world.
