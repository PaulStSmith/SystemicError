Trump yanks nominee accused of saying resigning official should be 'shot'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Unpacking the Drama in the Department of the Treasury Nominee Withdrawal

President Donald Trump speaks to members of the media as he arrives at Palm Beach International Airport, in West Palm Beach, Florida, U.S. on Oct. 17, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

President Donald Trump has dropped his support for a nominee for the Department of the Treasury who had apparently suggested a former IRS official should be shot.

Trump made the announcement Friday afternoon via his Truth Social platform.

“Please be advised that I am withdrawing the nomination of Donald Korb to be Assistant General Counsel in the Department of the Treasury. Thank you for your attention to this matter!”

Trump did not elaborate any further, Reuters reports.

It’s unclear exactly which factors led to Trump’s withdrawal.

In September, Sens. Mike Crapo (R-ID) and Ron Wyden (D-OR) questioned Korb during a Senate Finance Committee hearing about his negative comments about former IRS staffers, including Doug O’Donnell, according to Reuters. O’Donnell most recently served as an acting commissioner before he resigned in late February.

Wyden asserted that Korb apparently was talking about “the recent resignation of a career IRS official who served as acting commissioner,” and apparently told Senate Finance staffers that “the individual should have been shot for his decision to resign.”

MAGA influencer Laura Loomer this week wrote on X, accusing Korb of “praising” Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY) and claimed he donated to Democrats, including Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse.

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Drama in the Department of the Treasury Nominee Withdrawal

In the latest episode of political theater that could only unfold in the era of President Donald Trump, the abrupt withdrawal of a nominee for the Department of the Treasury encapsulates the chaos and impulsiveness that has become a hallmark of Trump’s leadership style. The nominee in question, Donald Korb, found himself out of favor for remarks that veered into the realm of the violent and absurd, suggesting that a former IRS official “should have been shot” for his decision to resign. This nomination saga is a microcosm of the broader dysfunctions within Trump’s administration, where loyalty and controversy often outweigh competence and stability.

The Withdrawal Announcement

Trump’s method of communication, a post on Truth Social, is emblematic of his preference for direct and unfiltered communication with the public, bypassing traditional media outlets. The terse announcement, devoid of explanation or context, leaves much to the imagination and speculation. It’s a classic Trump move: stir the pot, provide no clarity, and let the chaos unfold.

The Controversial Comments

The comments made by Korb, as reported, are not just distasteful; they are dangerous. In a political climate increasingly marred by violent rhetoric and actual violence, suggestions of shooting a public official over a resignation cross a line from the merely inappropriate to the potentially incendiary. It’s concerning that such comments would not immediately disqualify a nominee in the eyes of any administration. However, the delay in acting until these remarks were scrutinized by Senators Mike Crapo and Ron Wyden during a Senate Finance Committee hearing suggests a reactive rather than proactive stance on vetting nominees.

Political Infighting and MAGA Influences

Adding another layer of intrigue to the saga is the involvement of MAGA influencer Laura Loomer, who took to X (formerly Twitter) to accuse Korb of “praising” Democratic Senator Chuck Schumer and donating to Democrats. In the Trumpian universe, where loyalty often trumps (no pun intended) all else, such accusations can be damning. It’s a vivid illustration of how political purity tests are increasingly being used to vet nominees, sidelining qualifications and professional conduct.

The Larger Implications

This episode raises serious questions about the criteria and processes used to select nominees for crucial government positions. When suggestions of violence are not immediate deal-breakers, what message does that send about the administration’s values? Moreover, the influence of social media influencers in shaping political decisions is a worrying trend, reflecting the outsized impact of online echo chambers on real-world governance.

Conclusion

In withdrawing Korb’s nomination, Trump has once again shown that his administration’s decision-making is erratic, influenced by partisan loyalty and online controversies, rather than grounded in a thorough evaluation of a nominee’s qualifications and conduct. This incident is a stark reminder of the importance of vetting and the need for a return to a more deliberate and principled approach to governance. As the nation grapples with increasing polarization and the threat of political violence, the last thing it needs is for those in power to treat such serious matters with anything less than the gravity they deserve.
