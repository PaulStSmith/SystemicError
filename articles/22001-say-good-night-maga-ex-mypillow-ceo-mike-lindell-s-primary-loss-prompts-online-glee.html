'Say good night': MAGA ex-MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell's primary loss prompts online glee

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Mike Lindell Was Never the Point

A Loss Does Not Wash Away the Damage

Mike Lindell losing a race in Minnesota is not the story. The story is that a man who built his political brand by laundering election lies into a public persona was ever treated as a serious candidate in the first place. The source wants the reader to enjoy the embarrassment, especially Trump’s embarrassment. That is a sideshow.

What matters is that Lindell rose through the Republican ecosystem by repeating false claims about the 2020 election, then translated that attention into another campaign. That is not confusion. That is a career built on deliberate disinformation.

Power Sat With Trump and the Party

The actual institutional power here was not Lindell’s. It was Trump’s endorsement machinery and the Republican apparatus that keeps turning propagandists into contenders. Lindell stepped down from MyPillow to chase office, but he did so under the protection of a political culture that rewards loyalty to Trump over reality.

That is why the result should not be read as some healthy self-correction. It is a sign that even after years of election sabotage rhetoric, the GOP still makes room for figures like Lindell until they become embarrassing liabilities. The problem is the pipeline, not the punchline.

The Article Misdirects by Treating Corruption as Spectacle

The source spends a lot of energy on commentary posts, mockery, and Trump’s image damage. That is cheap framing. It turns a political operation into a personality story and encourages readers to focus on humiliation instead of responsibility.

The more serious fact is the one buried in the paragraph: a federal judge ruled last year that Lindell defamed Smartmatic by falsely claiming its machines helped rig the 2020 election. That is the record. He spread lies, got sued over them, and kept moving. The harm is not that he looked foolish on Election Day. The harm is that Republican politics kept giving him oxygen after he helped poison trust in elections.

Election Denial Is a Career Path

Lindell is useful because he shows how the modern right monetizes grievance. He was not punished by his party for lying about an election. He was promoted by it, endorsed by Trump, and folded into the broader ecosystem of anti-democratic propaganda.

That is the pattern. Facts become optional. Loyalty becomes a credential. Public office becomes a reward structure for people willing to degrade the basic legitimacy of elections. When that machine produces losers, it still leaves behind the damage they did on the way up.

The Minnesota Detail Matters Less Than the Republican Habit

The source frames this as an upset and a humiliation for Trump. Fine. But the deeper issue is what it says about Republican recruitment: even absurd, litigation-burdened election deniers can be elevated if they perform the right obedience.

That is not a mistake at the margins. It is how the party metabolizes extremism. It normalizes the lie, cashes in the outrage, then asks for credit when the lie eventually fails at the ballot box.

The System, Not the Punchline

Lindell is a symptom of a political order that treats fraud as identity politics and institutional vandalism as strategy. The relevant question is not whether he got laughed at. It is why the Republican Party keeps producing men like him and why Trump’s endorsement still functions as a credential in that world.

That is the larger pattern: authoritarian politics does not only rely on the loudest liar. It depends on the institutions, donors, candidates, and media habits that keep converting lies into campaigns long after the damage is obvious.
