Terror move backfires as 'loser' Proud Boys' socialist hate collides with love for donuts

Based on an article by Jordan Green, Investigative Reporter

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20262 min read

The Proud Boys Came for a Meeting. That Was the Point.

Not a Debate

The reported event is simple enough: about 51 Proud Boys showed up at a Democratic Socialists of America meeting in Winston-Salem, tried to film attendees, and then amplified the encounter online with threats and doxxing-style posts. They did not need to throw a punch to make the message clear. The performance was the message.

Who Actually Had Power

The Proud Boys do not have institutional authority. That is exactly why they lean on spectacle, numbers, and fear. Their power here came from choosing the terms of contact: entering a public space, targeting people in a membership meeting, and turning the presence of cameras into a weapon. The article makes clear this was organized, not accidental. Cape Fear Proud Boys appear to have led it, with members traveling in from Virginia and Georgia. That is not spontaneous agitation. That is coordination.

The Story Is Not “Socialism”

The framing around a “backlash against socialism” is doing a lot of damage for very little truth. What happened here was not a policy dispute. It was an intimidation campaign against a political organization that was trying to meet, recruit, and operate in public. The Proud Boys then escalated online, focusing on people who appeared LGBTQ and threatening sexual violence. That is not discourse. That is authoritarian harassment dressed up as street politics.

Fading Brand, Still Useful Violence

The source notes the Proud Boys are splintered, declining, and less appealing to younger far-right activists. Fine. But a dying organization can still do real harm when it retains a talent for spectacle and a willingness to menace ordinary political life. Their relevance no longer depends on coherence. It depends on intimidation, media oxygen, and the hope that enough people will self-censor before they ever organize.

The Weak Point Is Civic Space

The DSA chapter is now reassessing whether public libraries are workable meeting spaces because public access can also mean open exposure to hostile actors. That is the practical lesson here. Democratic participation is supposed to happen in ordinary civic spaces, but those spaces become vulnerable when far-right groups treat them as hunting grounds. The problem is not that the DSA failed to be “tough” enough. The problem is that organized intimidation can exploit the openness that democracy requires.

What This Reveals

This is how authoritarian politics operates at the local level: not only through elections and laws, but through pressure, stalking, filming, threats, and the normalization of political fear. The Proud Boys are not misunderstood enforcers of order. They are a recurring instrument of anti-democratic coercion, and the system keeps letting them convert social cowardice into leverage. The deeper pattern is not fringe extremism on the margins. It is the persistent attempt to make public organizing feel unsafe, so that power can move quietly back to the people who prefer silence.
