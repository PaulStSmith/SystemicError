CNN panelists burst into laughter at 'head-scratching moment' in Pam Bondi hearing

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi responds to a question from Senator Peter Welch (D-VT) as she testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Panelists on “CNN This Morning” burst into laughter when host Audie Cornish brought up a startling response by Attorney General Pam Bondi during her contentious congressional hearing.

Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) asked the attorney general Wednesday how many of Jeffrey Epstein’s co-conspirators she had indicted, which set off an eruption from her, and Bondi loudly changed the subject when he and Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) attempted to redirect her.

“The DOW is over 50,000 dollars,” Bondi fumed. “I don’t know why you’re laughing. You’re a great stock trader, as I hear, Raskin.”

Cornish brought up that exchange Friday morning in the context of President Donald Trump’s economic record.

“I want to talk about the price of Trump’s prosperity, which is turning out to come at a cost to Americans because we have been paying the tariffs, not foreign countries,” Cornish said. “That’s according to a new report from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, which finds we paid a whopping 90 percent of import taxes last year. It cost U.S. households an average tax increase of about $1,000. The National Bureau of Economic Research and CBO had similar numbers. I wanted to talk about this because there was this kind of head scratching moment in the Pam Bondi hearings, where I think it was Jerry Nadler was trying to ask her question about the things she was there for, something legal, and the Epstein files.”

She played a video clip of Bondi’s outburst, and New York Times podcaster Lulu Garcia-Navarro and Vox podcaster Noel King started laughing.

“Why are you laughing?” Cornish said.

“Because she’s head of the Justice Department,” Garcia-Navarro replied, shrugging.

“So the internet responded hilariously,” Cornish continued. “There were people that are like, ‘Oh, your girl is cheating on you, say why are we talking about this? The Dow.’ One joke hit me where it was like, ‘My rent is late, but what about the Dow? Why are we talking about this?’”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Spectacle: A Glimpse into Media and Political Absurdity

In an era where the line between political discourse and daytime television drama blurs, the recent exchange involving Attorney General Pam Bondi takes center stage—or should we say, screen? The episode, as highlighted on “CNN This Morning,” encapsulates more than just a fleeting moment of absurdity; it reveals a systemic rot festering within the halls of power and the channels of our news media.

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s Evasion: A Masterclass in Deflection

At the heart of this circus lies Attorney General Pam Bondi’s startling response during a congressional hearing. When confronted by Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) on the critical issue of Jeffrey Epstein’s co-conspirators—a question of immense public interest and concern—Bondi’s strategy was to pivot, and loudly so, to the Dow Jones Industrial Average. “The DOW is over 50,000 dollars,” Bondi declared, as if the stock market’s performance was a magical incantation capable of banishing all legal and moral inquiries.

This maneuver is emblematic of a broader, deeply troubling trend among public officials: the inclination to dodge accountability and weave economic indicators into non-sequitur arguments. It’s a tactic that insults the intelligence of the public and undermines the very essence of democratic oversight.

The Media’s Role: Laughter in the Face of Evasion

The reaction from “CNN This Morning” hosts and panelists to Bondi’s deflection was laughter—a natural response, perhaps, to the sheer absurdity on display. Yet, this moment of mirth masks a somber reality. When the guardians of our public discourse respond to evasion and obfuscation with chuckles and guffaws, they inadvertently signal that such behavior is acceptable, or at least, not worth the effort of a rigorous challenge.

Lulu Garcia-Navarro’s comment, “Because she’s head of the Justice Department,” while intended as a critique, also underscores the gravity of the situation. The head of the Justice Department, of all people, should be held to the highest standard of transparency and accountability. The laughter, while understandable, distracts from the pressing need for a more assertive media willing to hold power to account.

The Cost of Trump’s Prosperity: An Uncomfortable Truth

Audie Cornish’s pivot to discussing the economic report from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York serves as a sobering reminder of the tangible consequences of political decisions. The report’s findings—that Americans bore 90 percent of the cost of import taxes, resulting in an average tax increase of about $1,000 per household—paint a stark picture of the real price of so-called prosperity under President Donald Trump’s administration.

This economic analysis should serve as a wake-up call, not only about the direct impact of Trump’s policies on American households but also about the need for a more informed and critical discourse on economic matters. Instead of getting lost in the spectacle of political evasion, there’s a dire need for the media and the public to focus on the substantive issues affecting people’s lives.

Conclusion: Beyond Laughter, Towards Accountability

The episode on “CNN This Morning” is a microcosm of the broader challenges facing our political and media landscapes. While laughter is a natural human response to the absurd, it should not be the end of the story. As we navigate these tumultuous times, let us demand more from our public officials and media institutions. Let us push for a discourse grounded in fact, accountability, and a relentless pursuit of the truth. Only then can we hope to address the systemic errors eroding the foundations of our democracy.
