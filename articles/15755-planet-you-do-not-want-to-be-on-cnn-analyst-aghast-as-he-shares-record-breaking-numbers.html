'Planet you do not want to be on!' CNN analyst aghast as he shares record-breaking numbers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Trade War: A Stock Market Catastrophe?

CNN’s Harry Enten. (CNN)

President Donald Trump's trade war has led to a collapse of the stock market that eclipses any other presidency in modern history, CNN forecaster Harry Enten told CNN's Kate Bolduan on Tuesday morning.

"The stock market was down in a big, big, big way yesterday," Bolduan asked him. "How poorly has the stock market done since Trump took office?"

"Sometimes you look at the day-to-day and I feel like we can get lost," said Enten, a frequent analyst of Trump's polling numbers who has flagged precipitous declines this year.

"So let's take a look over the whole stretch of the Trump presidency as we approach day 100. The S&P has dropped the most under Trump for any president at this point in their presidency since the S&P 500 was in fact created back in 1957. And indeed, it is not anywhere close, folks. It's not anywhere close under Trump. It's dropped — get this — 14 percent."

The second biggest drop, he noted, was the 7 percent decrease under former President George W. Bush as a result of the September 11 attacks.

"So Donald Trump is on a planet all by himself, a planet you do not want to be on, the American people don't want to be on, in terms of the drop in the S&P 500 approaching 15 percent already in his presidency. No bueno," Enten added.

Bolduan then questioned him about the risk of a recession on the horizon, something consumers are increasingly afraid of per consumer confidence data. "Where are the numbers on that?"

"At this particular point, there's a decent chance that it will cascade across the economy," said Enten. "Chance of a recession, you see I have four different little metrics. We can look at JPMorgan, 60 percent. Polymarket, 57 percent. Reuters, 45 percent. Goldman Sachs, 45 percent. That's a bunch of numbers on your screen, but they're all basically telling the same exact story that is on either side of 50 percent.

"There's basically a 50/50 chance of a recession at this point, and that is way up from where we were at the beginning of the Trump presidency, when the numbers, simply put, weren't anywhere near this."

Our Analysis:

The Trump Trade War: A Stock Market Catastrophe?

In an era where sensationalism often trumps (pun intended) substance, it's refreshing, albeit grim, to see data-driven analysis cutting through the cacophony. The recent CNN segment featuring Harry Enten elucidates the dire consequences of President Donald Trump's trade policies on the stock market. But let's not stop at mere regurgitation of facts; let's dissect the implications and the somewhat apocalyptic forecasting that seems to be on the horizon.

The Stock Market's Freefall: A Historical Perspective

Enten's assertion that the S&P 500 has suffered its most significant decline under Trump since its inception in 1957 is not just a statistic; it's a testament to the volatility and uncertainty injected into the market by erratic trade policies. A 14 percent drop might seem like a mere number, but in the grand scheme of things, it represents billions of dollars in lost wealth, diminished retirement savings for the average American, and a shaken confidence in the U.S. economy's stability.

Comparing this to the 7 percent decrease under George W. Bush post-9/11 puts things into stark perspective. One was a response to an unprecedented terrorist attack; the other, seemingly, a self-inflicted wound born out of a misguided attempt to recalibrate trade imbalances.

The Specter of Recession: More Than Just a Shadow

Talking heads and market analysts have long debated the potential for a recession, but Enten's presentation of the odds—citing sources like JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs—paints a rather bleak picture. When the likelihood of a recession hovers around the 50 percent mark, it's no longer a matter of if but when.

This isn't just economic jargon being thrown around; it's a looming threat to the livelihood of millions of Americans. The potential cascading effects across the economy could mean job losses, further declines in the stock market, and an exacerbation of the wealth gap.

Rhetoric vs. Reality: The Trump Economic Paradox

Throughout his presidency, Trump has boasted about the strength of the economy, using the stock market as a barometer for success. However, the numbers and analysis presented by Enten highlight a glaring contradiction. How can an administration claim economic prosperity when its policies have led to a historic stock market plunge and a coin toss's chance of a recession?

It's crucial to differentiate between short-term gains and long-term stability. While certain policies may inflate the market temporarily, the underlying volatility and risk introduced can have devastating effects, as evidenced by the current state of affairs.

No Bueno, Indeed

Harry Enten's colloquial sign-off, "No bueno," might have been intended to add a touch of levity, but it underscores a grave reality. The economic policies and trade wars initiated under Trump's watch have not only destabilized the stock market but have also set the stage for a potential economic downturn that could take years, if not decades, to recover from.

As we dissect the numbers and forecasts, let's not lose sight of the broader implications: the erosion of economic stability, the amplification of uncertainty, and the very real consequences for everyday Americans. The stock market isn't just a number on a screen; it's a mirror reflecting the health of the nation's economy. And right now, that reflection is far from flattering.
