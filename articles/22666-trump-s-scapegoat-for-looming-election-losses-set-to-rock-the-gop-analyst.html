Trump's scapegoat for looming election losses set to 'rock the GOP': analyst

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Trump Is Preparing the Scapegoat, Not the Answer

Blame Before Ballots

The reporting here is straightforward: Trump is already positioning congressional Republicans to take the fall if the midterms go badly. He reposted a MAGA warning that the party is “sleepwalking” into defeat, while simultaneously trying to make the election a referendum on himself. That contradiction is the story. He wants credit for the movement and insulation from the wreckage.

Power Still Sits at the Top

The key fact is institutional power. Trump is not a commentator outside the system. He is the president, the central figure in the party, and the person whose agenda Republicans are expected to carry whether it helps them or not. When he tells voters to “pretend” he is on the ballot, he is not merely energizing supporters. He is claiming ownership over the party’s fortunes while leaving its congressional members to absorb the damage.

That is not confusion. It is command.

The GOP’s Consent Problem

Republicans did not stumble into this trap. They built it. For years, they let Trump escape consequence by absorbing his lies, his failures, and his demand for permanent loyalty. They treated political survival as a substitute for judgment. Now the same party may discover that a movement organized around one man’s grievance does not produce a durable majority. It produces dependency.

The article’s framing is too soft when it describes a possible “intraparty clash” as though this were an ordinary strategic dispute. It is more accurate to say the party is confronting the cost of surrendering its own institutional identity.

No Accountability, Just Rotation of Targets

Trump’s pattern is stable: lose, then blame fraud; mismanage, then blame aides; weaken the coalition, then blame the people beneath him. If Republicans lose seats, he will not treat that as evidence that his politics failed. He will search for a smaller target to punish. Congressional Republicans are convenient because they are powerful enough to be useful and weak enough to sacrifice.

That is how authoritarian politics preserves itself inside a nominally democratic party: not by solving problems, but by endlessly relocating responsibility away from the leader who created them.

The Coalition Was Always Fragile

The reporting notes that Trump has disillusioned Republican voters with the war in Iran and his trade wars, and that his base turns out less reliably when he is not personally on the ballot. That is the structural weakness beneath all the noise. The GOP has spent years converting itself from a governing party into a vehicle for Trump’s personal brand. Brand politics is not a durable substitute for legitimacy, policy coherence, or democratic accountability.

So when losses come, the blame will move downward before it moves inward. That is the mechanism.

The Pattern

This story is not really about an election slump. It is about a ruling figure preparing to externalize failure before the votes are even counted. Republicans are not being misled by Trump at this point; they are being used. And if they lose, the lesson will not be that Trump misunderstood the party. It will be that the party mistook submission for strength and called it strategy.

That is the larger political pattern: a movement that demands total loyalty, delivers shrinking legitimacy, and then tries to preserve itself by sacrificing the people still foolish enough to stand beneath it.
