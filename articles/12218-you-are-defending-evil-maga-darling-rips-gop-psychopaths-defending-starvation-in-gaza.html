'You are defending evil!' MAGA darling rips GOP 'psychopaths' defending starvation in Gaza

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 31, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Candace Owens. (Shutterstock)

A darling of the MAGA movement slammed the GOP on Tuesday for the party's hesitation to address starvation in Gaza.

Candace Owens, who is currently ensnared in a defamation lawsuit with French President Emmanuel Macron and his wife Brigitte, made the comments Tuesday on her podcast, "Candace." She claimed that GOP lawmakers, like Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL), who defended Israel's efforts to withhold aid from innocent Gazans, are indefensible.

"The issue that Zionists are having, the broader issue that you are having, is that you are defending evil," Owens said. "You are behaving like psychopaths, and it's caught up."

Since the war in Gaza began in 2023, Israel has largely prevented Gazans from receiving foreign aid. At times, Israel's political leaders have shot at and killed innocent Gazans as they lined up to receive food and other aid.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has defended these actions by saying Hamas is stealing aid from Gazans. U.S. foreign aid workers have said that the claim is untrue.

Meanwhile, images continue to surface of children facing malnutrition in Gaza. World health leaders have also said Gaza is on the brink of famine because of Israel's actions.

Owens pushed back against lawmakers who she said have called on people protesting Israel to lose their jobs. President Donald Trump signed an executive order in January prohibiting anti-Israel protests on college campuses.

"You are pretending that people are not starving in Gaza," Owens said. "You are mocking images of children who are starving, saying they deserve that. And, you can only behave like a sadistic, psychopathic, murderous individual for so long before the world takes notice."

Our Analysis:

Candace Owens' Critique of GOP's Stance on Gaza: A Closer Look

In a recent episode of her podcast "Candace," Candace Owens, a figure often celebrated within the MAGA movement, delivers a scathing rebuke of the GOP's apparent indifference towards the humanitarian crisis in Gaza. This marks a significant, if not shocking, deviation from the typical narratives espoused by her political cohort. Owens, embroiled in a defamation lawsuit with French President Emmanuel Macron, does not hold back in her criticism, particularly targeting GOP lawmakers who have defended Israel's withholding of aid from Gazans amidst ongoing conflict.

Unpacking Owens' Claims

Owens directs her ire at GOP lawmakers, such as Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL), who have supported Israel's efforts to restrict aid to Gaza, labeling their defense as "indefensible." She accuses them of defending what she sees as "evil" actions and behaving like "psychopaths." It's a stark accusation, one that underscores the severity of the situation in Gaza, where Israeli measures have reportedly led to a dire humanitarian crisis.

Since the war in Gaza began in 2023, Israel has largely prevented Gazans from receiving foreign aid, with political leaders even accused of using lethal force against innocent civilians attempting to receive food and aid. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's justification—that Hamas is stealing aid—has been contested by U.S. foreign aid workers, who claim such assertions are unfounded. Meanwhile, distressing images and reports signal that Gaza is on the brink of famine, a crisis exacerbated by Israel's actions according to world health leaders.

Owens also condemns the broader GOP and, by extension, the U.S. government's stance on protests against Israel. She references an executive order signed by President Donald Trump, which prohibits anti-Israel protests on college campuses, as an example of how criticism of Israel is being stifaciously curtailed in public discourse.

Analysis: The GOP, Israel, and the Humanitarian Crisis

Owens' commentary brings to light several critical issues worth dissecting:

  • The GOP's Alleged Indifference: Owens' critique suggests a troubling indifference within the GOP towards the suffering of Gazans. If accurate, this stance not only undermines the party's moral standing but also contradicts the fundamental human rights principles that the United States purports to champion on the global stage.

  • The Politicization of Human Suffering: The defense of Israel's actions by some GOP lawmakers, as Owens describes, illustrates the dangerous politicization of human suffering. When access to basic necessities and humanitarian aid becomes a bargaining chip in political and military strategies, it is the innocent who suffer the most.

  • Freedom of Speech Concerns: Owens' denunciation of the executive order against anti-Israel protests highlights a significant tension between freedom of speech and political policies. The move to silence protests under the guise of combating anti-Semitism raises questions about the balance between protecting individuals from hate speech and ensuring the preservation of free discourse in academic and public spheres.

Conclusion: A Call for Empathy and Policy Reevaluation

Owens' unexpected critique of the GOP's stance on Gaza is a reminder of the complex and often contentious nature of U.S.-Israel relations, and the impact of these politics on human lives. It serves as a call for a more empathetic approach to foreign policy, one that prioritizes human rights and humanitarian aid over political allegiances or strategic advantages.

While Owens' commentary may not shift the GOP's policies or the U.S.'s stance on Israel overnight, it certainly lays bare the urgent need for a reevaluation of these policies in light of the humanitarian crisis in Gaza. It's a stark reminder that, in the arena of global politics, the cost of indifference can be measured in human lives.
