Vietnam vets tear apart Trump in court over 'disrespectful' DC arch

Triumphal Ego: Trump’s Arch and the Manipulation of Memory

Unilateral Power Moves

The U.S. Commission on Fine Arts recently approved a “triumphal arch” proposed by President Donald Trump, set to dominate the landscape across the Potomac River from Washington, DC. This decision, while seemingly procedural, underscores a stark wielding of power by the Trump administration. The arch, intended to tower over the historical sightline from the Lincoln Memorial to the Robert E. Lee Memorial, is not just an architectural endeavor; it’s a political statement, reshaping a national memorial space into a personal legacy project.

Bypassing Democratic Processes

Critics, including Vietnam War veterans Shaun Byrnes and Jon Gundersen, have raised alarms not just about the symbolic implications of the arch but about the process through which it has been rushed. The project has reportedly skipped essential steps such as proper congressional approval, sidelining democratic oversight in favor of executive imposition. This act of bypassing elected representatives to push through personal projects underlines a disturbing trend of governance by decree rather than by democratic consensus.

Symbolic Disruption

The proposed arch, according to its detractors, threatens to disrupt the “symbolic connection” between key historical memorials, intruding upon a visual and thematic narrative of unity and reconciliation post-Civil War. The planned structure, dwarfing the Lincoln Memorial in height, shifts focus from collective national memory to an individualistic display. This architectural intervention in a space dedicated to national memory and mourning is seen by many, including the veterans leading the opposition, as a disrespectful act that prioritizes individual aggrandizement over communal honor and remembrance.

Veterans’ Resistance as Patriotic Assertion

The resistance led by veterans like Gundersen and Byrnes is not just about preserving historical aesthetics but is a broader defense of democratic values and respect for the nation’s war dead. Their lawsuit and public opposition frame their actions as loyalty to the country, challenging what they perceive as an autocratic maneuver. Their stance highlights a critical viewpoint where loyalty involves active participation and sometimes resistance against what is perceived as overreach by those in power.

Monument to Authoritarianism?

Gundersen’s comparison of the arch to monuments in authoritarian regimes is particularly pointed. This critique reflects deep unease about the trajectory of American democracy and the role of public monuments in it. In his view, the arch would symbolize not the nation’s 250th birthday, as officially stated, but rather the glorification of Trump himself, mirroring practices common in dictatorships where monuments often reinforce personal power rather than collective heritage.

Conclusion: Guarding Against the Cult of Personality

The controversy surrounding the triumphal arch proposed by Trump is emblematic of a larger issue in American politics: the rise of personal power at the expense of institutional integrity and respect for democratic norms. It also serves as a reminder of the need for vigilance and resistance when public symbols and spaces are co-opted for personal glorification. As the resistance by Byrnes, Gundersen, and their fellow veterans shows, true loyalty to democratic ideals often requires standing up to the powerful, especially when they seek to rewrite the landscapes of history in their own images.
