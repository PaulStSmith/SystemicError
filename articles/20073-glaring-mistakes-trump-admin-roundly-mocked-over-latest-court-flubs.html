'Glaring mistakes': Trump admin roundly mocked over latest court flubs

(Photo: Dan Fleckner / Shutterstock)

President Donald Trump's administration filed its response to the lawsuit from California on Wednesday, but the 32-page document contained some notable omissions.

Lawfare's Anna Bower pointed out that there was no table of contents, only a title page saying, "Table of Contents." In another section, the "Table of Authorities" at the top of the page was also blank.

The flubs prompted mockery from other lawyers on Bluesky.

"I do love poetry," mocked civil rights lawyer Josh Erlich.

"OMG I have 40 years experience in litigation and have NEVER filed anything this sloppy," another person remarked.

"That's nothing, the lawyer who hit the button on this filing probably has over 40 WEEKS experience, most of that within the last 90 days (it counts as multiple weeks if you file and lose multiple cases a week, right?) and they make bigger mistakes than this before breakfast," remarked Joe Gordon, another lawyer.

Evolutionary biologist Joshua G. Schraiber responded, "What losing all of your competent career employees does to an mf."

West Virginia University College of Law incoming Professor Jerry Edwards was more empathetic.

"Mistakes happen. I've made some errors in my own filings (always very frustrating), but the Trump admin keeps making super glaring mistakes. 'I didn't bother to look over my documents a final time before I filed them' mistakes. Indicates they lack attorney resources or they're incompetent (or both)," said Edwards.

"Not an auspicious beginning. Bondi Justice Department submits response in Newsom v Trump, responding to California's request for a Temporary Restraining Order, and yes, this is page 3," quipped Just Security's Ryan Goodman about the Table of Contents' absence.

Civil litigator Owen Barcala confessed that he also does the "bracket and highlighting thing," that the filing does on the "Table of Authorities." However, he added, "it's a little ridiculous when it's the only thing on a blank page."

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump Administration's Legal Follies

In what can only be described as a farcical display of legal ineptitude, President Trump's administration has once again found itself the subject of ridicule and mockery, this time for its bewildering blunders in a recent court filing. The 32-page document, ostensibly a response to a lawsuit from California, seemed more akin to a hastily assembled draft than a serious legal document, conspicuously lacking both a table of contents and a table of authorities. These are not mere typos but glaring omissions that speak volumes about the administration's approach to legal proceedings.

Amateur Hour at the Justice Department

First, let's address the elephant in the room: how does a document, which is supposed to be the administration's formal response in a legal dispute, omit foundational elements like a table of contents and a table of authorities? These are not decorative flourishes; they are essential components that lend clarity and legitimacy to legal documents. The absence of these elements is not just an oversight; it's a testament to a profound lack of professionalism and, possibly, competence.

Mockery from the Legal Community

The legal community's response ranged from disbelief to outright mockery. Civil rights lawyer Josh Erlich's quip about poetry and the sarcastic remarks from seasoned litigator Joe Gordon underscore the disbelief and amusement such errors elicit among professionals. Even more telling is the comment from Joshua G. Schraiber, pointing to the loss of competent career employees as a root cause of such blunders. This is not just an isolated incident; it's symptomatic of a broader trend of deterioration within the administration's legal ranks.

A Symptom of Deeper Dysfunction

Jerry Edwards, an incoming professor at West Virginia University College of Law, offers a more measured, yet equally damning, perspective. Acknowledging that errors can occur, Edwards points out the recurring nature of these "super glaring mistakes" as indicative of a deeper malaise. Whether it's a lack of attorney resources, incompetence, or both, the pattern is clear: this administration's legal filings reflect a chronic disregard for diligence and professional standards.

Civil litigator Owen Barcala’s confession about using brackets and highlighting underscores a key issue: even the most basic, arguably excusable practices in legal drafting are rendered ridiculous when they stand alone on an otherwise blank page. It's a fitting metaphor for the administration's legal strategy – a facade of competence masking a void of substance.

Conclusion: A Farce with Real Consequences

While it's easy to mock these errors and the administration's apparent legal ineptitude, it's critical to remember the stakes involved. Legal proceedings, especially those involving the administration, have far-reaching implications. When the government's legal work is riddled with errors, it's not just a matter of embarrassment; it undermines the integrity of the legal process and, by extension, the rule of law itself.

In sum, the Trump administration's latest legal blunder is more than an isolated incident of sloppiness; it's a glaring symptom of a systemic problem. As we continue to observe this administration's legal escapades, one can't help but wonder: how low can the bar go?
