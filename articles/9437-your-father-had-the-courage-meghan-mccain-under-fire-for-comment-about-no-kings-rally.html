Some analysts have hailed this weekend’s pro-democracy protests as the largest on record, but political dynasty McCain, the daughter of the late GOP presidential candidate John McCain, does not understand the point.

“I don’t understand how Trump is a King when he won every single swing state, the electoral college and popular vote in a democratic election,” McCain wrote on X Saturday.

That led to immediate responses from various high-profile political insiders.

Atlantic writer Jonathan Chait asked the commentator, “Is it your view that literally nothing a freely elected president does in office can be anti democratic?”

GOP pollster Mike Madrid said, “Your father had the courage to speak out against this.”

The popular Nerds for Humanity account told McCain that “winning fair and square doesn’t make overreach okay.”

The account added, “Critics aren’t calling him king for the vote, they’re worried about power grabs like these: -Issuing an EO to end birthright citizenship, defying the 14th Amendment. -Firing 17+ inspectors general without the required 30-day notice to Congress, gutting independent oversight. -Invoking the Alien Enemies Act for mass deportations, a wartime power twisted for peacetime crackdowns. -Freezing congressionally approved federal funds, usurping lawmakers’ purse strings. -Directing agencies to rewrite election rules with voter barriers, invading Congress’s turf. Make sense?”

Former GOP staffer Drew McDowell said, “Meghan McCain is doing her best to blow up her father’s legacy.”

“John McCain would be speaking at a No Kings rally today,” he added on Saturday.

One attorney, Christine Jones, pushed back on those bringing up Meghan McCain’s father.

“Why does every Meghan post bring out the ‘your dad would have…’ crowd, mostly people who never even met him?” she asked Saturday. “I knew John McCain fairly well, and I’d never presume to know how he’d react to any of Meghan’s posts. The compulsion to drag him into every conversation is just bizarre.”

Our Analysis:

Meghan McCain’s Misguided Commentary on “No Kings” Protests: A Critical Analysis

Meghan McCain’s recent remarks on the “No Kings” protests have ignited a firestorm of criticism, and rightfully so. Her comments, as reported, betray a fundamental misunderstanding of the democratic principles at stake and the nature of the criticisms being levitated against former President Trump’s actions. Let’s dissect the layers of irony, misinformation, and missed opportunities in McCain’s statements.

McCain’s Misunderstanding of Democratic Principles

At the heart of Meghan McCain’s controversial tweet is a glaring misconception: that electoral victory equates to carte blanche authority. McCain’s assertion, “I don’t understand how Trump is a King when he won every single swing state, the electoral college and popular vote in a democratic election,” fundamentally misunderstands the essence of democratic governance. It’s not merely about how one attains power but how one exercises it. Jonathan Chait’s response slices through this confusion brilliantly, highlighting the absurdity of the notion that a democratically elected leader is incapable of anti-democratic actions.

The Irony of Political Dynasty

The irony of Meghan McCain, a beneficiary of a political dynasty, failing to grasp the nuances of democracy and the dangers of autocratic overreach cannot be overstated. Her position as the daughter of the late Senator John McCain, a figure often celebrated for his maverick stance and commitment to democratic ideals, adds a layer of tragic irony to her missteps. Mike Madrid and Drew McDowell’s comments poignantly underscore this, lamenting the perceived betrayal of John McCain’s legacy.

A Catalog of Concerns

The critique offered by the Nerds for Humanity account provides a succinct and damning catalog of the actions that have fueled the “No Kings” protests. From the attempted erosion of birthright citizenship to the undermining of independent oversight and the manipulation of election rules, these actions represent a systematic assault on the checks and balances that underpin American democracy. McCain’s failure to engage with these substantive issues, instead focusing on the legitimacy of Trump’s election, is a missed opportunity to contribute meaningfully to the discourse.

The Unhelpful Invocation of John McCain’s Legacy

While Christine Jones’ defense of Meghan McCain’s autonomy in her viewpoints is fair, it sidesteps the broader issue at hand. The frequent invocation of John McCain’s legacy in discussions about Meghan’s public statements is not merely about familial legacy but a reflection of the stark contrast between his often principled stand on democratic values and the positions Meghan appears to advocate. This contrast is not “bizarre” but a natural point of comparison given the context.

Conclusion

Meghan McCain’s comments on the “No Kings” protests do more than reveal a personal misunderstanding of democratic principles; they highlight a broader issue within segments of the political elite—a failure to recognize or acknowledge the grave implications of undemocratic actions by those in power. As critiques from figures like Jonathan Chait and the Nerds for Humanity account illustrate, the heart of the matter is not Trump’s electoral victory but his governance’s adherence to democratic norms and the rule of law.

McCain’s remarks are a missed opportunity to affirm a commitment to democracy over partisanship. Instead, they serve as a stark reminder of the vigilance required to guard against the erosion of democratic principles, even, and especially, when it comes from those who have previously been champions of those very ideals.
