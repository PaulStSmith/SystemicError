Trump just told the drug industry to end RFK Jr's career for him: Morning Joe

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Blatant Ironies and Confusions in Trump's Demand for Pharmaceutical Clarity

U.S. Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., attends a cabinet meeting with President Donald Trump, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 26, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough parsed President Donald Trump's social media statement demanding that pharmaceutical companies "justify the success" of their COVID medications.

Nine former directors and acting directors of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention condemned Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s leadership as dangerous in a scathing op-ed Monday in the New York Times, and later that morning Trump issued a Truth Social post calling on drug companies to put to rest concerns about coronavirus vaccines and therapies.

"Many people think they are a miracle that saved Millions of lives. Others disagree!" Trump posted. "With CDC being ripped apart over this question, I want the answer, and I want it NOW. I have been shown information from Pfizer, and others, that is extraordinary, but they never seem to show those results to the public. Why not???"

"They go off to the next 'hunt' and let everyone rip themselves apart, including Bobby Kennedy Jr. and CDC, trying to figure out the success or failure of the Drug Companies Covid work," he added. "They show me GREAT numbers and results, but they don’t seem to be showing them to many others. I want them to show them NOW, to CDC and the public, and clear up this MESS, one way or the other!!! I hope OPERATION WARP SPEED was as 'BRILLIANT' as many say it was. If not, we all want to know about it, and why???"

The "Morning Joe" host tried to read between the lines in the post, which came amid speculation about the president's own health after ducking out of public appearances for days.

"President Trump seems to be playing to his base, questioning the effectiveness of the Covid vaccine," Scarborough said. "It comes amid chaos at the CDC with several high-profile resignations in response to the Trump administration's vaccine skepticism. You know, what's interesting here is ... Trump tells drugmakers to 'justify the success' of Covid meds after the FDA limits vaccine approval."

"I do wonder if this isn't actually him saying to the drugmakers, tell them what I did right, like how wrong they are and what, because he's not going to sacrifice Operation Warp Speed, will he?" Scarborough added. "I mean, he knows, he hears, you know, even his Democratic friends, he hears scientists, he hears people in media saying this was an extraordinary success. So it's kind of like, yeah, 'God, please tell me, was this a success or not?'"

The development of mRNA vaccines in a public-private partnership to protect against Covid-19 has been widely praised as one of Trump's greatest achievements, although many of his supporters have been suspicious of them since they became available, and Kennedy is reportedly considering pulling them off the market.

"I mean, the crazy thing is, are you really going to let this guy who's been engaging in pseudoscience for the past 20 years undermine what really should be seen as his greatest accomplishment?" Scarborough said. "I mean, in terms of opening up business, opening up society, opening up the world, saving millions of lives, saving billions and billions of dollars, I mean, and he's going to, I find it hard to believe he's going to let Bobby Kennedy Jr. trash the legacy as much as Bobby Kennedy Jr. is trying to trash that legacy right now."

Our Analysis:

The Blatant Ironies and Confusions in Trump's Demand for Pharmaceutical Clarity

In a spectacle that can only be described as both bewildering and alarmingly ironic, Donald Trump has emerged from the shadows of his social media banishment to challenge pharmaceutical companies to "justify the success" of their COVID medications. This demand, as MSNBC's Joe Scarborough astutely points out, reeks of political manipulation and a desperate attempt to cling onto any remnants of a legacy, amidst the chaos he sowed.

Firstly, let's dissect the sensationalist rhetoric Trump employs. His Truth Social post is a masterclass in contradictions and unfounded implications. "Many people think they are a miracle that saved Millions of lives. Others disagree!" he proclaims, without specifying who these "many people" or "others" are, leaving a gaping hole where evidence should be. This is a classic Trump maneuver—vague statements that inflame rather than inform, casting doubt without basis and demanding answers without posing a coherent question.

The timing of Trump's outcry is also suspiciously convenient, coming as it does in the wake of a scathing op-ed by nine former CDC directors condemning the leadership of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.. This op-ed, which should have sparked a serious examination of Kennedy's leadership, is instead overshadowed by Trump's demands for pharmaceutical transparency. It’s a clever diversion, but one that Scarborough sees through. Trump is not genuinely concerned about the efficacy of COVID medications; he's concerned about his image and the legacy of Operation Warp Speed.

Scarborough’s analysis suggests Trump is essentially pleading with pharmaceutical companies to vindicate his administration's efforts, masked as a call for transparency. This is a man who is, at his core, desperate for validation, even if it means undermining the public's trust in vaccines during a global health crisis. It's a dangerous game of ego over evidence, where the stakes are public health and safety.

Furthermore, Trump's fixation on Kennedy—a figure who has been widely criticized for his anti-vaccine stance—adds another layer of irony to the situation. Scarborough rightly questions whether Trump is willing to let Kennedy, a proponent of "pseudoscience," tarnish what is arguably one of his administration's few universally acknowledged successes. It's a rhetorical question, of course, but one that highlights the absurdity of Trump's position. He is willing to risk the public’s health and the global economy, not to mention his own legacy, on the altar of political expediency.

In essence, Trump's demand for pharmaceutical companies to "justify the success" of COVID medications is not a call for transparency or accountability. It's a thinly veiled attempt to control the narrative around his administration's response to the pandemic, at a time when his leadership has been widely criticized. It's an act of political theater, designed to distract and divide.

Scarborough’s insights shed light on the dangerous implications of Trump's statements. As the former president plays to his base, stoking vaccine skepticism and sowing discord, the real issues at hand—public health, scientific integrity, and the effective leadership of crucial institutions like the CDC—are pushed to the sidelines. It's a disturbing spectacle of misinformation and manipulation, one that the public must see through if we are to navigate out of the mess that has been left in the wake of such erratic leadership.
