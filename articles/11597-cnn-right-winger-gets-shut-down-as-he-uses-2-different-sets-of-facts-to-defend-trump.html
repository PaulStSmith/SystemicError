CNN right-winger gets shut down as he uses '2 different sets of facts' to defend Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20255 min read

Rob Bluey and Sabrina Singh (CNN)

A conservative commentator was hit with a quick fact check on CNN after excusing President Donald Trump's interference in the Texas redistricting mess.

Border Patrol agents poured into downtown Los Angeles as California Gov. Gavin Newsom and other Democrats outlined plans to redraw California's congressional map in response to a Republican push to do the same in Texas to their own advantage, and conservative journalist Rob Bluey told "CNN This Morning" that the situations in the two states were completely different.

"It's important also to point out that we're talking about two different sets of facts," Bluey said. "The whole situation in Texas stemmed from a lawsuit and a decision by the Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals."

Fellow panelist Sabrina Singh, a former deputy Pentagon press secretary under Joe Biden, quickly pushed back.

"That's still caught up in the courts," she said. "What Gov. [Greg] Abbott is taking a Sharpie pen and just redoing the maps. That's not from the lawsuit that's been brought."

Bluey argued the GOP governor's actions were justified by changes to the state's population since the last census was conducted five years ago.

"Texas has also had 2 million new residents move into the state since the last census," he said. "The census made errors. Texas was cheated out of a seat and an electoral vote and Florida was cheated out of two because the census made errors. There were a number of problems that have happened over the last couple of years that could lead people to that conclusion that [host Audie Cornish] just made, that their vote is in some ways not [being counted]."

Cornish expressed skepticism, asking whether states should simply call for a new census and redraw their congressional maps if they didn't like the results of the head count conducted under constitutional authority, and Bluey eagerly took the bait.

"I think states should do their own census," Bluey said. "Maybe each state and the federal government can do this in collaboration. By the way, in the 1970s they amended the law and they said that you could do a mid-decade census, so it's not that Donald Trump's doing anything unusual, it's just that the federal government hasn't done it before."

Singh poured cold water on Bluey's argument, saying the president's insistence on changing a state's congressional map to favor his own party was indeed unusual.

"Each state has their own different constitution, but the lawsuit that you're referring to is not why Gov. Abbott decided to draw the map, redraw the maps," Singh said. "He decided to do that because Donald Trump put pressure on him. The lawsuit is still in the Texas courts and has not risen to the state level to redraw the map."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Spin: The Texas Redistricting Fiasco

Let's dissect this article with the precision of a scalpel, shall we? At the heart of the discussion is the contentious issue of redistricting in Texas, a move that smells more of political manipulation than any genuine concern for the so-called "errors" in the census.

The False Equivalence

Conservative commentator Rob Bluey's attempt to draw a distinction between the situations in Texas and California is a classic case of false equivalence. While he's right that the circumstances originate from different sparks—lawsuits in Texas versus a Democratic response in California—the underlying fire is the same: partisan gerrymandering. Bluey's argument is akin to comparing a controlled burn to a wildfire and then claiming they're entirely different because one was lit with a match and the other with a lighter.

The Census Excuse

Bluey's rationale that Texas' redistricting efforts were justified by a population increase and alleged census errors is a thinly veiled excuse for partisan gerrymandering. The census, conducted every ten years, is indeed not without its faults. However, the suggestion that Texas was somehow "cheated" out of representation because of these errors is a leap of logic so vast it would require warp speed to cross. The idea that these supposed errors justify the unilateral redrawing of congressional maps by a GOP governor, under pressure from Trump, no less, is not just dubious—it's a brazen attempt to manipulate electoral outcomes.

The Mid-Decade Census Proposition

Bluey's proposition that states should conduct their own censuses, with a nod to a legal amendment from the 1970s, is a Pandora's box of potential abuse. The notion that states, particularly those under the thumb of whichever party is thirsting for more power, should undertake their own population counts is riddled with conflicts of interest. It's the equivalent of letting students grade their own exams and then wondering why everyone suddenly aced the test.

Sabrina Singh's Cold Water

Sabrina Singh, in her rebuttal, doesn't just pour cold water on Bluey's argument; she douses it with the entire Arctic. By highlighting the ongoing nature of the lawsuit and the direct pressure from Trump on Governor Abbott to redraw the maps, Singh exposes the redistricting efforts for what they truly are: a power grab. The lawsuit, still tangled in the courts, is a convenient fig leaf for the real motive—ensuring GOP dominance by any means necessary.

The Bigger Picture

What this article, and the debate it encapsulates, really underscores is the perilous state of American democracy. When electoral maps can be redrawn at the whim of political pressure, rather than fair and impartial processes, the very foundation of representative government is shaken. It's not just about Texas or California; it's a systemic issue that threatens the integrity of every vote and every voice in the nation.

Conclusion

Rob Bluey's arguments, while wrapped in the guise of concern for fairness and accuracy, are little more than a smokescreen for partisan interests. The real issue at hand is not census errors or population shifts; it's about who gets to wield power and how they're allowed to seize it. In the end, democracy is the loser in this game of political chess, and it's the American people who are left in checkmate.
