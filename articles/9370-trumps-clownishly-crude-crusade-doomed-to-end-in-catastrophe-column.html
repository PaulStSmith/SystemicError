Trump’s ‘clownishly crude’ crusade doomed to end in catastrophe: column

An analyst warned Tuesday that President Donald Trump‘s war on drug cartels in the Caribbean and blowing up alleged drug boats is “clownishly crude” and will end in “human misery.”

America has a history of meddling in South America, and if that indicates anything, the current Navy attacks could be destined for disaster, Salon columnist Heather Digby Parton writes Tuesday.

“Reporting over the last week has made it clear: The danger of this situation going sideways becomes greater every day. And considering America’s history in the region, such an outcome almost seems pre-ordained,” Digby Parton writes.

At least 32 people have died, and multiple high-ranking military officials have questioned the administration’s strikes and raised concerns over whether the attacks are lawful.

“The president, though, does not seem to feel any moral obligation — or pressure — to produce any evidence, and over the weekend he inadvertently revealed the vacuity of the administration’s arguments,” Digby Parton writes.

The writer points to the 1950s with the U.S.-backed coup in Guatemala, military coup in Brazil, the assassination of the leader of the Dominican Republic, the Iran-Contra scandal, and the failed Bay of Pigs, plus the 1970s “dirty wars” in Argentina and Chile, as just some examples of how the U.S. has interfered in South American politics in the past. She also explains that “the apparent template for Trump’s obsession with Venezuela was America’s invasion of Panama in 1989.”

“If all of this weren’t enough, last week Trump declared that he had approved covert operations in Venezuela, which certainly challenges the meaning of the word covert. The CIA has a long and checkered history in the region over many years, but I don’t think any president has been dim enough to announce it in advance. American interference in Latin American affairs has almost always led to total disaster. It’s hard to imagine that this crazy scheme won’t end up being the worst of all,” Digby Parton writes.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s War on Drug Cartels: A Recipe for Disaster?

In an article that cuts to the heart of America’s turbulent history with Latin America, Heather Digby Parton from Salon lays bare the glaringly evident truths about President Trump’s war on drug cartels in the Caribbean. This strategy, as Parton rightly describes, is not just “clownishly crude” but is dangerously poised on the precipice of causing “human misery.” The audacity of blowing up alleged drug boats and conducting covert operations in Venezuela shines a light on a path we’ve trodden before—a path littered with the debris of failed interventions and the wreckage of countries left in turmoil.

The Folly of Ignoring History

Let’s dissect the argument laid forth by Parton, who, with surgical precision, enumerates the historical blunders of the U.S. in Latin America. From the 1950s U.S.-backed coup in Guatemala, the military coup in Brazil, to the Iran-Contra scandal, and the failed Bay of Pigs invasion, each instance serves as a stark reminder of the consequences of America’s meddling. The reference to the 1970s “dirty wars” in Argentina and Chile further cements the argument that U.S. interference has seldom, if ever, left countries better off.

Trump’s Misguided Crusade

The parallels drawn between Trump’s current vendetta against drug cartels and past U.S. misadventures in Latin America are both apt and alarming. Parton’s critique of Trump’s “obsession with Venezuela” as mirroring the invasion of Panama in 1989 is particularly noteworthy. It underscores a cycle of interventionist policies that have historically ended in disaster. This is further compounded by Trump’s brazen announcement of covert operations in Venezuela, an act that not only defies logic but also the very definition of “covert.”

Are we truly doomed to repeat the mistakes of the past? Trump’s approach, as highlighted by the article, indicates a glaring disregard for the lessons history has attempted to teach us. The death toll from these actions, already at 32, is a harrowing testament to the human cost of such recklessness.

The Vacuity of Administration’s Arguments

Parton’s critique of the Trump administration’s lack of moral obligation or pressure to produce evidence supporting their attacks is a scathing indictment of the current state of affairs. The mention of high-ranking military officials questioning the legality and efficacy of these strikes adds a layer of institutional concern to the mix. It’s not just a matter of misguided policies, but also of the potential unlawfulness of these actions.

Conclusion

In essence, Parton’s article serves as a clarion call to acknowledge and learn from the past. The “clownishly crude” approach taken by Trump in dealing with drug cartels, coupled with the ill-conceived covert operations in Venezuela, does not just risk international condemnation but also the lives of countless innocents. As history has shown, American interference in Latin America has been a quagmire of moral and ethical dilemmas, leading to outcomes that are anything but desirable.

In closing, the question then becomes: When will lessons from the past illuminate the folly of current endeavors? Trump’s war on drug cartels, as highlighted by Parton, is not just a misguided adventure—it’s a ticking time bomb of “human misery” waiting to explode. It’s high time this administration, and indeed future ones, reevaluate the implications of such interventionist policies. Only by doing so can we hope to avoid the pitfalls that have plagued our history with Latin America.
