'Narcissist' Trump proves he’s 'hearing the footsteps of the Grim Reaper': analyst

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

A President Building His Own Shrine

The source in brief

The reporting here is about Trump’s recent AI-generated self-mythology: images placing him beside John F. Kennedy, standing with George Washington, and recasting himself as a figure of historical destiny. It also notes reporting from Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan that Trump is thinking about mortality in his final term, alongside public discussion of his visible health issues. The White House response was its usual sludge of insult and deflection.

The real story is not vanity

This is not a quirky episode of presidential insecurity. It is a display of power operating through image control. Trump is not simply admiring himself; he is trying to overwrite the public record with a personal mythology in which history bends around him.

That matters because the presidency is not a personality contest. It is an institutional office with enormous coercive reach. When the person holding it turns the office into a branding project, the damage is not symbolic only. It normalizes the idea that state power exists to validate the ruler.

Mortality makes the narcissism louder

The reporting points to something more revealing than the usual Trump ego theater: a man who appears to be thinking about his own end. The AI images are not random. They are compensation. They are an attempt to place himself among dead presidents, as though proximity to the canon can rescue him from the fact that he is temporary.

That is the political meaning of the stunt. Trump is not trying to persuade the country of policy competence. He is trying to defeat time through propaganda. The cape, the time travel, the Kennedy imagery, the Washington fantasy: it is the visual language of a man who cannot tolerate limits, so he manufactures immortality in pixels.

The White House answer is pure misdirection

The White House response, calling Rothkopf a “far-left loser” with “Trump Derangement Syndrome,” does not rebut anything. It performs the standard authoritarian dodge: when the facts are embarrassing, attack the messenger, invent pathology, and make the argument about the critic instead of the conduct.

That tactic is not accidental. It is how power avoids accountability. The administration does not need to explain why the president is staging his own hagiography if it can reframe the question as elite hatred. It is a familiar trick: convert scrutiny into persecution, and then sell victimhood from the seat of power.

What the Kennedy Center detail shows

The Kennedy Center detail matters because it exposes how Trump treats cultural institutions: not as public goods, but as surfaces for his name. The source says he has “upended” the place, with tarps still hanging over the front because he cannot bear the building without his branding attached.

That is the governing logic in miniature. He does not inherit institutions; he occupies them, strips them of civic purpose, and repurposes them for self-worship. The result is an “empty disaster” because emptiness is what remains when public life is subordinated to personal vanity.

The larger pattern

The deeper pattern here is not one man’s fear of death. It is the collision between democratic institutions and personal rule. Trump uses the office to manufacture permanence, while his defenders use outrage management to smear away the evidence. That is how democratic decay works in practice: not only through overt lawbreaking, but through the conversion of public power into a mirror.

The political lesson is simple. When a president needs myth more than governance, the country is already paying the price.
