Legal experts floored as Trump DOJ botches complaint against judge — providing no evidence

The Justice Department waged a legal battle against the chief U.S. District Court judge for the District of Columbia, but appeared to have messed up the paperwork.

Legal analyst Liz Dye, who writes at “Law and Chaos” and also co-hosts the site’s podcast, pointed out that the DOJ’s effort to claim Judge James Boasberg committed some form of “misconduct” failed to attach any proof of that misconduct.

“The complaint, addressed to Chief Judge Sri Srinivasan of the DC Circuit, alleged that Judge Boasberg attempted to intimidate Chief Justice John Roberts at the March meeting of the Judicial Conference and made ‘improper public comments’ about pending cases in violation of the Judicial Canon,” Dye wrote along with co-author Andrew Torrez.

Categorizing it as a “nastygram,” the legal analysts explained that the filing was assigned by Attorney General Pam Bondi’s chief of staff, Chad Mizelle.

He referenced a “vague” source of “information, simply dropping a footnote to ‘Attachment A at 16.’”

The problem, however, is that there was no “Attachment A.”

“Law and Chaos” filed a Freedom of Information Act request to find this mystery attachment, only to find out that it doesn’t exist.

“This would suggest that the complaint was purely performative, lodged solely to discredit a jurist who has issued rulings adverse to the Trump administration. Under the guise of protecting the ‘integrity and impartiality of the judiciary,’ the Trump administration is in fact working to undermine it,” the analysts suggested.

Legal analyst Anna Bower noted that the story in “Attachment A” states that the only source of the misconduct was that attachment.

“DOJ never even bothered to file the attachment,” Bower needled.

The legal analysts said that it’s clearly a matter of public interest, given Attorney General Pam Bondi posted about it on social media.

“And yet,” the authors said, “the DOJ’s Office of Information Policy refused our request to expedite, claiming that it ‘cannot identify a particular urgency to inform the public about an actual or alleged federal government activity beyond the public’s right to know about government activities general.’”

Lawyer Kel McClanahan, who represents the site, said that the court doesn’t need to accept the allegations about Boasberg. Rather, all the court must do is “accept that DOJ has stated them in a formal judicial filing and cannot retreat from them now when it is inconvenient. According to DOJ’s own words, the document requested by Law and Chaos clearly raises ‘possible questions about the government’s integrity which affect public confidence.”

Our Analysis:

The DOJ’s Phantom Misconduct Allegation: A Farce Unveiled

In what can only be described as a spectacular failure of bureaucratic competence, the Justice Department’s recent legal skirmish with Chief U.S. District Court Judge James Boasberg reads more like a satirical sketch than a legitimate legal proceeding. The DOJ, under the ostensible guardianship of the law by Attorney General Pam Bondi, embarked on what was meant to be a damning crusade against Judge Boasberg, only to fall flat due to an elementary clerical error: the missing “Attachment A.”

The Case of the Missing Proof

Liz Dye and Andrew Torrez of “Law and Chaos” have unraveled this farcical attempt by the DOJ to besmirch Judge Boasberg’s reputation. The Department aimed its cannons at Boasberg, accusing him of misconduct that ranged from intimidation tactics against Chief Justice John Roberts to making improper public comments about pending cases. Yet, when it came time to present evidence, the DOJ’s case evaporated into thin air—because their so-called evidence, referenced as “Attachment A,” was nowhere to be found. Not just missing in action, but, as subsequent Freedom of Information Act requests have revealed, non-existent.

A Performative Attack on Judicial Integrity

This debacle transcends mere incompetence; it signifies a pernicious attempt to undermine the judiciary. The DOJ’s actions, mistakenly or deliberately, have painted a picture of an administration more interested in discrediting those who oppose it than in upholding the integrity of the judicial system. The accusation against Judge Boasberg, devoid of any substantiating evidence, was nothing more than a nastygram—a term fittingly used by Dye and Torrez. It was an effort to tarnish the reputation of a jurist who has not shied away from ruling against the Trump administration’s interests.

The DOJ’s Response: A Study in Evasion

The Justice Department’s handling of the aftermath is equally troubling. Despite the public interest in the allegations against Judge Boasberg—fueled further by Attorney General Bondi’s social media posts—the DOJ’s Office of Information Policy refused to expedite the FOIA request filed by “Law and Chaos.” Their justification? A lackluster claim that they “cannot identify a particular urgency to inform the public about an actual or alleged federal government activity beyond the public’s right to know about government activities general.” This response is a masterclass in obfuscation, demonstrating a blatant disregard for public accountability and transparency.

Conclusion: A Mockery of Justice

What the DOJ has achieved through its botched legal battle against Judge Boasberg is a mockery of the justice system. By engaging in what appears to be a baseless character assassination attempt, the Department has not only exposed its own incompetence but also its willingness to manipulate judicial proceedings for political ends. The real casualty in this farce is the public’s confidence in the integrity and impartiality of the judiciary.

As legal analysts and commentators continue to dissect this incident, one thing is abundantly clear: the DOJ’s actions have raised “possible questions about the government’s integrity which affect public confidence.” It’s a sad day for justice when those tasked with upholding it are the ones undermining it through petty vendettas and performative legal filings. This episode should serve as a cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing the judiciary and the imperative of maintaining a vigilant and informed citizenry.
