During a Thursday interview on Real America’s Voice, Posobiec spoke with conservative host Megyn Kelly about her remarks from earlier in the week, revealing that Erika Kirk had been “genuinely rattled“ after feminist website Jezebel paid witches to place a hex on her husband.

“We should talk about whether or not the demonic had some influence here,” Posobiec said.

Kelly acknowledged that Jezebel’s article “didn’t ask for actual bodily harm.”

“And they did this 18 days before Charlie died,” she noted. “But the day before he was assassinated, Erika and Charlie saw the story because Jezebel wrote it up the Monday before Charlie was killed, which was a Wednesday.”

“And they asked a friend of theirs, and I think Erika told me that it was a Catholic priest, but I can’t totally remember, but it was a Catholic, just a friend of theirs, and they asked him for a blessing over Charlie the night before he was killed,” she continued. “But my own takeaway as a friend of hers and as a Christian was this was evil what they did. This was an evil act.”

Kelly suggested the witchcraft could have led to Charlie Kirk’s murder.

“And I also happen to feel that there is a possibility they opened a very dark door,” she explained. “I don’t know how things work.”

“Like you’re calling up things you might not want to call up in your home or in this world. So who are we to say, as mere mortals, that doing that kind of ritual is utterly powerless, is the joke that Jezebel seemed to think it was. I don’t know that. I have no idea what doing that is capable of.”

Posobiec revealed some of the steps he had taken to keep his family safe from supernatural forces.

“I, as everyone knows, I always have my rosary right here, very handy,” he pointed out. “I’ve got crucifixes up in my bedroom and the bedrooms of my children, the same way that my parents had for me. We have holy water in the house.”

“So yes, they could have an agent of evil fire a bullet at Charlie,” he added. “And I know that that spell, that hex could never break Charlie’s bond with Christ.”

Our Analysis:

The Twisted Intersection of Politics, Witchcraft, and Media Sensationalism

In a recent melodramatic turn of events that sounds more like the plot of a poorly scripted horror movie than real life, MAGA influencer Jack Posobiec has taken to the airwaves with claims that smack of medieval superstition rather than 21st-century reasoning. During an interview on Real America’s Voice, Posobiec, along with conservative host Megyn Kelly, delved into a narrative that not only blurs the lines between reality and fantasy but also dangerously amplifies the rhetoric of demonization (quite literally) in political discourse.

The Core of the Absurdity

Here we have an article that propels us back to the days of witch trials and superstition, rather than forward into rational and enlightened debate. Posobiec’s revelation that he keeps a supply of “holy water” on hand, following an admittedly bizarre act by the feminist website Jezebel—paying witches to hex Charlie Kirk—is a testament to how far down the rabbit hole of irrationality political discourse has fallen.

The Witchcraft Element

Jezebel’s decision to engage in a stunt that involved paying witches to place a hex on Kirk is, without question, a provocative act designed to elicit a reaction. However, it’s the leap to attributing actual, physical consequences to these actions — such as suggesting a link to Kirk’s tragic murder — that catapults this situation from the realm of provocative satire into dangerous and irresponsible territory. Kelly’s insinuation that these acts of witchcraft could have led to Kirk’s murder is not only baseless but borders on the absurd.

The Supernatural Defense

Posobiec’s measures to protect his family from “supernatural forces” with rosaries, crucifixes, and holy water might be his personal prerogative, but presenting these actions as a serious countermeasure on a news platform is bewildering. It perpetuates a narrative where political and personal safety are intertwined with the supernatural, a notion that has historically led to witch hunts and fearmongering.

The Misplaced Focus

Amidst this sensationalist narrative, what is glaringly absent is a rational discussion on the real issues at hand. The focus on witchcraft and hexes as a political tool is not only a distraction from substantive political discourse but also a dangerous game that trivializes genuine concerns and issues facing society. The murder of Charlie Kirk is a tragedy that deserves serious investigation and discourse, not speculative connections to witchcraft and hexes.

Conclusion: A Call for Rationality

The real “evil act” here is not the supposed hex but the willingness to exploit tragedy for sensationalist coverage and to entertain notions of witchcraft as a legitimate influence on political and personal events. This story is a stark reminder of the need for a return to rational discourse, where facts and logic prevail over superstition and sensationalism. As tempting as it may be to engage in or entertain these narratives, it’s crucial to remember the destructive power of irrational fear and the importance of grounding political discourse in reality, not superstition.
