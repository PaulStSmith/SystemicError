Pirro signs DOJ motion to toss Bannon indictment in 'interests of justice'

Jeanine Pirro, United States Attorney for the District of Columbia, walks out of the White House to be interviewed in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Longtime Trump ally and former White House senior counselor to the president, Steve Bannon, is trying to get his 2022 conviction on two counts of contempt of Congress overturned — and now he has the support of Trump’s Department of Justice to back him up.

“DOJ is trying to help Steve Bannon erase his conviction for defying a subpoena from the Jan. 6 committee,” reports Politico’s Kyle Cheney.

Cheney noted that the DOJ’s motion “has no career prosecutor” listed, only U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro. He also reported that the case had been headed to the U.S. Supreme Court.

“The government has determined in its prosecutorial discretion that dismissal of this criminal case is in the interests of justice,” the motion reads.

Bannon served four months in prison and was released in October 2024. He was found guilty by a jury on two counts of contempt of Congress, the Associated Press reported, “one for refusing to sit for a deposition with the Jan. 6 House Committee and a second for refusing to provide documents related to his involvement Trump’s efforts to overturn his loss to Joe Biden in the 2020 presidential race.”

He referred to himself as a “political prisoner” when he began serving his sentence.

Our Analysis:

Bannon’s Bid for Legal Redemption: A Tale of Judicial Favoritism?

In an audacious move that reeks of cronyism at its most blatant, Steve Bannon, the former White House senior counselor known for his controversial stances and unabashed loyalty to Donald Trump, is angling to have his 2022 conviction for contempt of Congress obliterated. What’s more alarming, however, is the backing he’s garnered from a most unexpected quarter: Trump’s Department of Justice (DOJ). This development isn’t just a footnote in the annals of legal proceedings; it’s a glaring spotlight on the politicization of justice under Trump’s administration.

The DOJ’s Unusual Advocacy

According to Politico’s Kyle Cheney, the DOJ’s motion to dismiss Bannon’s conviction conspicuously lacks the signature of any career prosecutor. Instead, it’s U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, a figure known more for her political affiliations than her prosecutorial discretion, who’s leading the charge. This absence of a career prosecutor’s involvement raises serious questions about the impartiality of the justice system, suggesting that Bannon’s political connections, rather than the merits of his case, are driving the DOJ’s actions.

The Charges Against Bannon

Let’s not forget the gravity of Bannon’s defiance. Convicted on two counts of contempt of Congress, Bannon’s refusal to cooperate with the Jan. 6 House Committee wasn’t just an act of political theater; it was a direct challenge to the oversight authority of Congress. His actions—or lack thereof—obstructed an investigation into one of the most tumultuous events in recent American history: the storming of the Capitol in an attempt to overturn the 2020 presidential election results.

Bannon’s self-styling as a “political prisoner” upon his incarceration is nothing short of ludicrous. It’s a manipulative narrative, attempting to recast blatant disregard for legal obligations as some form of martyrdom for the cause. The reality is far simpler and less noble: Bannon was found guilty by a jury of his peers for acts that undermine the very foundations of democratic governance.

The Implications of DOJ’s Motion

The DOJ’s motion, stating that the dismissal is “in the interests of justice,” is a jarring assertion that seems to fly in the face of accountability and transparency. If Bannon’s conviction is overturned with such a blatant display of political backing, it sets a dangerous precedent that could embolden others to flout congressional subpoenas with impunity, provided they have the right connections.

This move by the DOJ under Trump’s influence is not just a single case of legal maneuvering; it’s emblematic of a broader trend of politicizing the justice system to protect allies and undermine the checks and balances that are fundamental to the American political system. It is a stark reminder of the lengths to which this administration is willing to go to shield its loyalists from legal repercussions, even at the cost of eroding public trust in the institutions meant to serve justice impartially.

In summary, Bannon’s bid to have his conviction overturned, with the help of Trump’s DOJ, is not just an individual legal battle; it’s a litmus test for the integrity of the U.S. judicial system. The outcome of this case could either reinforce the notion that political connections trump accountability or serve as a rallying cry for those who believe in the sanctity of the rule of law, regardless of political affiliation. As it stands, the scales of justice seem perilously tilted towards the former.
