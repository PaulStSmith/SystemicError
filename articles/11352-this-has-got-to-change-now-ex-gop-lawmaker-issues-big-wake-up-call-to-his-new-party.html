Joe Walsh, a former GOP congressman who challenged Donald Trump for the 2020 presidential nomination, on his Social Contract Substack page pointed to new reporting that shows that Democrats lost up to 5 million voters in all 30 states where party registration is required – which he said was unacceptable.

"This is during the age of Trump — an insurrectionist, a fascist, a pathological liar," Walsh said. "Somebody who is cruel and ignorant and un-American is the leader of the political party that gained voters, according to voter registration in all 30 of these states. And Democrats lost voters in every single one of those states."

"My new political party, the Democratic Party, for the last four to five years has been living in some MSNBC studio, utterly out of touch with where regular folks are," he wrote. "Big-tent progressives: we’re all together, but the national party has been too far-left — or been perceived to be too far-left. They’ve ceded the middle to an authoritarian-embracing cult. They’ve ceded the middle to the Republican Party."

"This has got to change now — as in NOW — for the very preservation of democracy," Walsh added. "This must change."

The newly minted Democrat offered some advice to party leaders.

"Democrats must fight," Walsh wrote. "They must get off of their high horse and out of their ivory towers and out of their MSNBC studio and get with regular folk. Listen to and talk about what regular folk care about. And doggone it, you can’t be so freaking far-left and win nationally."

"This is a wake-up call," he added. "And if this doesn’t wake up the Democratic Party, nothing will. In every single state, Democrats have lost voters. Republicans have gained voters over the last four to five years."

Our Analysis:

The Alarm Sounds: A Former Republican's Warning to Democrats

Joe Walsh, once a staunch Republican turned 2020 anti-Trump presidential candidate, has now taken on the mantle of a Democratic harbinger of doom, or so he styles himself on his Social Contract Substack page. His dramatic proclamation centers on the Democratic Party's significant voter registration slump, a staggering loss of up to 5 million voters across 30 states. Walsh's commentary, while laced with hyperbole, raises critical points about the current state of American politics, especially for Democrats. But let's dissect the nuances and the broader implications of his argument with the sharpness it deserves.

The Irony of Joe Walsh's Transformation

First off, the irony of Joe Walsh, a former Republican, sounding the alarm on Democratic strategies (or the lack thereof) cannot be overstated. His transformation from a GOP congressman to a Trump challenger and now a self-proclaimed Democrat highlights a political journey that mirrors the broader existential crisis within American political identities. But what makes Walsh's critique particularly fascinating is its source: a voice that has been on both sides of the fence, or perhaps, has jumped from one sinking ship to another, hoping to patch leaks he's all too familiar with.

The Uncomfortable Truth About Voter Registration

Walsh points to an "unacceptable" trend: Democrats losing voters in all 30 states that require party registration. This fact alone should serve as a blaring siren for Democratic leadership. The loss of up to 5 million voters is not a mere statistical blip; it's a hemorrhage. In the "age of Trump" — whom Walsh spares no adjectives to describe as an "insurrectionist, a fascist, a pathological liar," among other things — the expectation was presumably that the Democratic Party would capitalize on the widespread disenchantment with Trump's brand of politics. Instead, the opposite has happened.

The Disconnect: Democrats & 'Regular Folk'

Walsh's critique of the Democratic Party's disconnect with "regular folk" hits a nerve. His depiction of Democrats as residents of an "MSNBC studio" encapsulates a broader perception problem that the party is too ensconced in elite, far-left echo chambers, disconnected from the bread-and-butter issues facing everyday Americans. This perception, whether fully accurate or not, has evidently been costly. His advice? Democrats need to "get off of their high horse" and engage directly with the concerns of "regular folk."

The Big Tent vs. Far-Left Dilemma

The crux of Walsh's argument lies in the Democratic Party's struggle to maintain its "big-tent" identity while being perceived as veering too far left for the average American voter's comfort. This perception has ostensibly allowed the Republican Party, despite its embrace of Trumpian "authoritarian" tendencies, to position itself as the refuge for the politically homeless middle. The irony, of course, is that Walsh himself symbolizes this political homelessness, having transitioned from a critic of Trump within the GOP to a critic of the Democratic Party's strategy.

A Wake-Up Call?

Walsh's proclamation serves as a wake-up call, but one must wonder if it's the kind that leads to hitting the snooze button or to an actual, galvanized response. His advice for Democrats to "fight" and to recalibrate their engagement with the electorate is sound, but it's also advice that's been echoed by numerous political pundits and strategists ad nauseam.

Conclusion: The Walsh Warning — Heed or Dismiss?

Joe Walsh's transformation and subsequent warning to the Democratic Party encapsulates the current tumult within American politics — an era defined by shifting allegiances, ideological realignments, and the search for a coherent identity amidst the chaos. Whether his advice will fall on receptive ears or be dismissed as the rantings of a political chameleon remains to be seen. What is clear, however, is that the Democratic Party faces a significant challenge in reconnecting with the American electorate — a challenge that will require more than just alarm bells to overcome.
