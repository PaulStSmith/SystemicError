'Don't text me': GOP lawmaker rages at reporter over talk of her goal to take Trump's spot

The Georgia Republican has publicly criticized her party and its leadership over the past year, and NOTUS reported that four sources familiar with the matter say Greene has been working to reinvent herself with an eye on seeking the GOP nomination in 2028.

“One source says her conversations have centered around her belief she is ‘real MAGA and that the others have strayed,’ adding that she believes she has ‘the national donor network to win the primary,’” NOTUS reported.

Greene promptly texted NOTUS after the report’s publication to deny what she called “a baseless article,” and she asked why the outlet had not attempted to contact an individual in her office instead of sending an inquiry to her communications director, which reporter Reese Gorman said he had done.

“Who is your source? Laura Loomer?” Greene said in the text, and she posted a screen shot of the message on her X account. “She claimed I was running for governor last week and announcing it in the view. Once again you publish baseless gossip. Very disappointing. Don’t text me again.”

Greene sneered at the outlet’s reliance on “sources,” one of which was described as having spoken with her directly about the matter, and emphasized that she was focused on her legislative duties.

“Congress needs to go back in session because DC reporters are bored and desperate,” she said. “And I would like to pass bills and appropriations for my current job.”

Her boyfriend Brian Glenn, chief White House correspondent for the right-wing Real America’s Voice, chimed in with a post mimicking President Donald Trump’s social media style.

“MTG has NO plans to run for President in 2028,” Glenn posted. “I AM YOUR SOURCE. Thank you for your attention to this matter. BG.”

NOTUS stood by its reporting, saying its sources said Greene had spoken about a presidential run numerous times.

“While the conversations lack specifics and she hasn’t officially decided anything, she has brought up on multiple occasions that she is interested in launching a campaign,” NOTUS reported. “Sources point to her recent media run on CNN, ‘The View’ and ‘Real Time with Bill Maher’ as evidence that she is trying to connect with a more national audience outside of the hardcore MAGA base.”

Our Analysis:

The Denial Dance: Greene’s Presidential Ambitions in Question

In a classic display of political evasion, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene has vehemently denied reports of her eyeing the presidential seat for 2028, a denial as predictable as it is questionable. The report by NOTUS, which claims four sources familiar with the matter, suggests Greene is not only considering a run but believes she embodies the “real MAGA,” a claim that reeks of the usual hubris associated with political figures of her ilk. Yet, Greene and her broadcaster boyfriend’s denials do little to quash the suspicions that arise from such a report. Instead, they add fuel to the speculative fire, making one wonder what lies beneath the surface of these vehement denials.

The Art of the Denial

Greene’s response to the NOTUS report is a textbook example of political deflection. By attacking the publication for relying on anonymous sources—a common and necessary practice in journalism—she attempts to undermine the credibility of the report without directly addressing its contents. Her sarcastic query, “Who is your source? Laura Loomer?“, and the dismissive remark about reporters being bored, serve as nothing more than distractions from the issue at hand.

Her boyfriend, Brian Glenn’s Trump-esque declaration of being the definitive source on Greene’s intentions, adds an almost comedic layer to the denial. It’s as if the couple believes that a strong enough denial can erase any traces of ambition Greene might harbor for the presidency.

Between the Lines

Let’s dissect the undercurrents of this political drama. Firstly, the notion that Greene sees herself as the “real MAGA” leader suggests a profound sense of self-importance and a belief that she is the true heir to Trump’s legacy. This is not just an ambition; it’s a declaration of war against those in the GOP who she believes have strayed from the MAGA path.

Secondly, the mention of her “national donor network” is a clear indication that conversations about a potential run have taken place, despite her denials. In politics, where there’s smoke, there’s usually a fire. And in this case, the smoke is billowing.

The Attempted Image Rehab

NOTUS points out Greene’s recent media appearances on platforms like CNN, ‘The View,’ and ‘Real Time with Bill Maher’ as attempts to connect with a national audience beyond her hardcore MAGA base. This is particularly telling. Why would Greene, a figure known for her far-right stances, seek to broaden her appeal unless she had higher political aspirations?

Conclusion

In the end, whether Greene plans to run for president in 2028 remains to be seen. However, the dance of denials and deflections she and her boyfriend have engaged in does little to convince the informed observer. Their reactions, steeped in sarcasm and indignation, seem to betray a deeper truth about Greene’s ambitions.

The reality is that in politics, denials are often just the prelude to confirmations. And with Greene, a figure who thrives on controversy and attention, the prospect of a presidential run is not just plausible—it’s almost expected. Keep your eyes peeled, folks. This saga is far from over.
