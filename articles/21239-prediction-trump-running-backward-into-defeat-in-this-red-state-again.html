Prediction: Trump 'running backward' into defeat in this red state — again

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20263 min read

Trump’s Georgia Obsession Is Not a Gaffe. It’s a Governing Style.

Losing on Purpose

The reporting here is simple enough: Donald Trump is not helping Georgia Republicans win; he is dragging them back into the graveyard of his own 2020 defeat. Instead of talking about the cost of living, GOP nominees, or the actual ballot in front of voters, he is reviving the same disproven fraud claims that helped sink his own candidates before and is now threatening to do the same again.

That is the story. The bigger point is what it reveals: Trump treats electoral loss not as a fact to process, but as an injury to avenge through institutions he can still bend.

Who Holds the Levers

The actor with real institutional power here is Trump, even when he is not on the ballot. He has the microphone, the party gravity, and access to federal machinery that can be used to re-litigate a dead election. The source notes that the FBI seized hundreds of boxes of 2020 materials from a Fulton County warehouse and that a federal judge appointed by Trump rejected a subpoena from Trump’s politicized Justice Department seeking personal information on Fulton County election workers.

That is not random noise. That is the state being pulled into a fantasy of stolen victory.

The Scapegoat Is the Point

The intended target is not evidence. It is accountability. Fulton County election workers, Georgia officials, and the legal record of the 2020 vote are being used as props in a political con. Trump lost Georgia. The 2020 Senate runoffs were lost too. The source is blunt that the damage came from Trump’s own performances, including the Dalton rally where he would not stop talking about the “rigged election” instead of helping Kelly Loeffler and David Perdue.

This is how authoritarian politics works in practice: not by persuading people of a lie once, but by keeping the lie alive long enough to punish the people who maintained the system that defeated it.

Republican Candidates Pay the Bill

The immediate casualties are not abstract. They are Mike Collins and Rick Jackson. Collins is running against Jon Ossoff, and Jackson is trying to win a governor’s race where, as the source notes, his chances depend heavily on Senate margins and on whether Trump decides to torch the ticket again.

That is the cruelty of Trumpism for his own party. He demands loyalty, then spends it like gasoline. Georgia Republicans are not being asked to govern. They are being asked to carry the cost of a grudge that was settled years ago.

The Framing Problem

The weakest part of the political conversation around this story is the lingering temptation to treat Trump’s behavior as obsession, confusion, or an inability to move on. That is too gentle. He is not confused. He is choosing to use his remaining institutional leverage to keep the grievance machine running.

And the apparatus around him is participating: the Justice Department, the FBI surge, the campaign of intimidation aimed at election workers. Even when the judge blocks one piece of it, the larger effort has already done its work. The point is not always to win in court. Sometimes the point is to keep the country stuck inside the accusation.

The Larger Pattern

Georgia is not just a state Trump keeps revisiting. It is a case study in how defeated power tries to survive: by rewriting loss into victimhood, by turning public institutions into instruments of revenge, and by forcing local officials and election workers to absorb the consequences of national delusion.

That is the real political meaning here. Trump is not merely refusing to accept 2020. He is demonstrating how far a movement will go to convert defeat into permanent sabotage.
