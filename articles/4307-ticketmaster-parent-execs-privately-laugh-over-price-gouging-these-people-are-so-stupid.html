Ticketmaster parent execs privately laugh over price-gouging: 'These people are so stupid'

Newly revealed internal communications show a pair of executives at entertainment venue giant Live Nation laughing about how much they are able to gouge people for concert tickets.

“In a series of chats from 2022, Ben Baker and Jeff Weinhold, two regional directors of ticketing for Live Nation amphitheaters, boasted about their ability to raise so-called ‘ancillary fees’ – like parking, lawn chair rentals and VIP access – and still get concertgoers to pay for them,” reported Bloomber News. “In one exchange, Weinhold gloated about raising VIP parking costs at a Virginia concert venue to $250. ‘These people are so stupid. I almost feel bad taking advantage of them,’ Baker wrote, adding later, ‘I gouge them on ancil prices.’ In another exchange, he bragged about charging ‘$50 to park in the grass’ and ‘$60 for closer grass.’”

“Robbing them blind, baby, that’s how we do it,” Baker wrote.

In a statement provided to Raw Story, Live Nation disavowed these remarks.

“The Slack exchange from one junior staffer to a friend absolutely doesn’t reflect our values or how we operate,” said the statement. “Because this was a private Slack message, leadership learned of this when the public did, and will be looking into the matter promptly. Our business only works when fans have great experiences, which is why we’ve capped amphitheater venue fees at 15% and have invested $1 billion in the last 18 months into U.S. venues and fan amenities.”

Live Nation has been accused in a series of lawsuits of holding a monopoly over venues, that squeezes both performers and ticketholders alike — resulting in people being charged hundreds or thousands of dollars more than reasonable to see concerts, shows, and performances around the country. They also own the booking platform Ticketmaster, which has infamously hiked booking fees to higher and higher levels over the years, and can often be the only way to book tickets for Live Nation owned venues. The fiasco surrounding tickets for Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour brought many of these issues into national focus.

The company has also been accused in litigation of stonewalling congressional investigators.

This comes as the Trump administration Justice Department’s antitrust division reached a settlement with Live Nation, which requires them to pay $200 million to several states, allow third-party sellers access to Ticketmaster, limit their exclusivity agreements, divest 10 of its amphitheaters, and cap service fees for amphitheater tickets to 15 percent of ticket price.

This settlement has been rejected by over two dozen state attorneys general as inadequate to resolve Live Nation’s monopoly power, since it doesn’t require Ticketmaster to be divested altogether, and state-level litigation is expected to continue.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Live Nation’s Pricing Practices and Corporate Accountability

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

In dissecting the situation presented by the Bloomberg News report regarding Live Nation’s internal communications, it’s crucial to pinpoint where institutional power resides and who made the decisions that led to the consumer price gouging. Live Nation, as a corporate entity, holds significant institutional power in this scenario. It operates a vast network of entertainment venues and owns Ticketmaster, a dominant player in the ticketing industry. The decision to charge exorbitant fees for concert-related amenities, as bragged about by Ben Baker and Jeff Weinhold, reflects a broader corporate strategy rather than isolated incidents of individual malfeasance.

Who Made the Decision?

While the report focuses on exchanges between two regional directors, the ability to set and enforce pricing strategies at Live Nation venues does not rest solely with these individuals. Such pricing decisions are indicative of a corporate culture that prioritizes profit maximization over consumer experience. Therefore, while Baker and Weinhold are directly responsible for the comments, the environment that allows or even encourages such attitudes is a product of decisions made at higher levels of corporate governance.

Misdirection of Responsibility

Live Nation’s response to the controversy attempts to frame the issue as an isolated incident involving “one junior staffer” and a friend. This framing grossly underestimates the systemic nature of the problem and seeks to redirect responsibility away from those who hold actual power. By focusing on the private nature of the Slack messages and claiming ignorance until the public revelation, Live Nation’s leadership deflects accountability.

The article’s framing, on the other hand, correctly identifies the broader issues at play, including allegations of monopoly power and the impact on consumers and performers. It is not misled by Live Nation’s attempt to minimize the situation.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating

Rejecting false equivalence and scapegoating is essential in this analysis. Live Nation’s attempt to scapegoat two employees for what is clearly a systemic issue within the company and the industry at large is a prime example of such tactics. The problems with exorbitant fees and anti-competitive behavior are not merely the result of actions taken by low-level employees but are indicative of broader corporate strategies and practices.

Furthermore, the settlement reached with the Trump administration’s Justice Department, criticized by state attorneys general as inadequate, highlights the inadequacy of current regulatory measures to curb Live Nation’s monopolistic tendencies. This situation transcends individual misbehavior, implicating regulatory, and institutional failures at multiple levels.

Conclusion

The controversy surrounding Live Nation’s pricing practices cannot be adequately addressed by focusing solely on individual employees or isolated incidents. This is a systemic issue that reflects the priorities and practices of the corporate entity and the industry at large. Criticism should be squarely aimed at the corporate governance of Live Nation and the regulatory environment that allows such practices to flourish. Any narrative that seeks to divert attention from these fundamental issues is not only misleading but also complicit in the ongoing exploitation of consumers and artists in the entertainment industry.
