The Ellis Act: A Realistic Blueprint for Humane and Accountable Immigration

For decades, the U.S. immigration system has teetered between chaos and cruelty. Bureaucratic backlogs, impossible wait times, exploitative labor structures, and the moral stain of agencies like ICE have left us with a system that is expensive, ineffective, and fundamentally unjust.

It doesn't have to be this way.

We propose a model that combines the clarity and strength of rule-based systems with the compassion and dignity that any free society should extend to those seeking a better life. Call it what you like, but we call it The Ellis Act.

This is a carrot-and-stick immigration framework designed for the 21st century: structured, enforceable, and humane.

THE CARROT:
Legal, Streamlined Immigration with Dignity

Eligibility Requirements

  • Apply through a U.S. consulate or embassy

  • Clean background check

  • Basic medical screening

  • No felony convictions prior to entry

  • Proof of financial support to cover 12 months of living expenses:

    • $50,000 per single adult

    • $75,000 per couple

    • $25,000 per dependent (defined as individuals under 18)

Outcome:

  • One-year provisional visa with full legal protections

  • Right to live, work, study, and contribute

  • No fear of deportation, detention, or exploitation

An annual visa maintenance fee of no more than $500 per family unit will be required at each annual compliance checkpoint. This fee may be reduced or waived based on income.

MANDATORY CHECKPOINTS

Immigrants must attend three check-ins during their visa year:

  • At 6 months

  • At 9 months

  • At 12 months (final status review)

These reviews track progress, provide guidance, and identify those at risk of falling through the cracks early enough for intervention.

Visa holders may leave the country for up to 90 consecutive days under their national passport without losing their status.

THE STICK: Clear Rules, Real Enforcement

To renew or transition into a longer-term status, each adult in a family unit must meet at least one of the following by the 12-month mark:

1. Full-Time Employment

  • With a U.S. company or employer

2. Enrollment in Education or Training

  • Accredited university, trade school, apprenticeship, or certification program

3. Business Ownership

  • Own and operate a registered U.S. business for at least 6 months

  • Business must employ at least 3 full-time workers (target of 5), verified by W-2s or equivalent

4. Verified Self-Employment

  • Legally registered business (EIN or sole proprietor)

  • Must file taxes (Schedule C or equivalent)

  • Earn at least $36,000/year (or ~70% of local median wage)

  • Provide 1099s or client contracts

  • Continuous operations for at least 6 months

5. Financial Self-Sufficiency

  • Bank statements, investments, or verified income showing ability to live without public assistance

If any adult fails to meet these requirements:

  • They receive a 60-day grace period with monitored status (non-invasive ankle bracelet)

  • They can requalify or must voluntarily exit the U.S.

  • After grace period expiration without compliance, they are subject to mandatory removal

  • A two-year reapplication bar is imposed

FAMILY UNITS AND INDEPENDENT RESPONSIBILITY

Each adult in a family unit is individually responsible for meeting the renewal criteria. However, economic compliance (employment/self-sufficiency) can be satisfied collectively within the family.

Dependents turning 18 during the visa period must apply independently at the next checkpoint.

Elderly individuals are not eligible to be claimed as dependents.

MISCONDUCT AND CRIMINALITY

Felonies:

  • Immediate revocation of provisional visa and removal proceedings for the individual

  • DHS may evaluate whether to allow remaining family members to remain based on their own compliance

Misdemeanors:

  • 3 Class A misdemeanors within 12 months or 9 within 60 months triggers DHS review

  • Pattern of repeated lower misdemeanors may also trigger review if tied to public safety risk

EMERGENCY HARDSHIPS & ABUSE PROTECTIONS

Compliance waivers shall be granted in the following cases:

  • Medical Emergency: Diagnosed, non-preexisting condition requiring continuous in-patient care or long-term out-patient home care

  • Job Loss Near Checkpoint: If job loss occurs within 90 days of a checkpoint, a 90-day extension is granted

  • Employer Abuse: Documented wage theft, labor violations, or employer fraud triggers deferral of employment requirement and whistleblower protections

PATH TO PERMANENCE:
From Provisional to Citizenship

After a successful one-year provisional visa:

  • Continued check-ins at 24, 36, 48, and 60 months

  • Successful completion of all checkpoints leads to Green Card issuance

  • English proficiency test required for permanent residency

  • Failure to pass results in continued provisional status with annual reviews

  • After 5 years with a Green Card, eligible for citizenship

STEWARD CLASS VISAS FOR MIGRANT WORKERS

Tiered agricultural employment visa program:

Tier A (Large agribusiness):

  • $1,000/worker onboarding (non-refundable)

  • $500/year compliance fee

  • Must provide housing (HUD standard dorm-style or better)

Tier B (Mid-size farms):

  • $1,000/worker onboarding (refundable after 5 years)

  • $250/year compliance fee

  • Must provide housing

Tier C (Small/micro farms):

  • $500/worker onboarding (refundable after 2 years)

  • $50/year compliance fee

  • Housing optional, but encouraged

All tiers must:

  • Comply with wage, safety, and labor law

  • Allow worker mobility

  • Submit to audits and surprise inspections

  • Face escalating fines and bans for non-compliance:

    • 1st offense: 70% of 5-year average gross revenue

    • 2nd offense: 140%

    • 3rd offense: Permanent ban

LEGACY IMMIGRANT INTEGRATION

For undocumented residents present for 5+ years:

  • No felonies; no more than two misdemeanors (non-immigration)

  • Must register within 12 months

  • Must show work, school, or caregiving history

  • 5-year compliance pathway with Green Card eligibility

  • Failure to register = 10-year bar from benefits

FUNDING

  • Annual visa fees (max $500/family)

  • Steward compliance fees

  • Fines collected from non-compliance

  • Interest on refundable agri-fees

Funds used for:

  • Program administration

  • Housing/labor audits

  • Legal and language services

  • Outreach and fraud prevention

WHY THIS WORKS

  • Makes immigration predictable and legal

  • Kills the underground economy for coyotes and smugglers

  • Ends reliance on detention, ICE raids, and family separations

  • Replaces fear with structure and accountability

  • Rewards contribution, not exploitation

  • Restores moral legitimacy to immigration policy

We don’t need a wall. We need a door.

And this? This is the key.
