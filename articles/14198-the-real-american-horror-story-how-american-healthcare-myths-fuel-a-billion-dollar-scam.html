The Real American Horror Story: How American Healthcare Myths Fuel a Billion-Dollar Scam

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 31, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There’s a favorite bedtime story told to Americans about Canadian healthcare. It usually begins with, “I heard someone in Canada died waiting for care,” and ends with, “Thank God we have the best healthcare in the world.” But like all fairy tales designed to scare children into obedience, this one collapses under the weight of facts.

Let’s get one thing straight: the horror stories about Canadian healthcare aren’t just overblown—they’re often fabricated. Who’s behind them? Look no further than the web of American for-profit insurance companies, their hired PR agencies, and policy “think tanks” whose sole mission is to preserve the most lucrative, inefficient, and predatory system in the developed world.

The Great Insurance Shell Game

Both Canada and the U.S. rely on for-profit doctors. Both pay them via insurance. But here’s where the paths diverge:

  • In Canada, each province has one insurance plan. It’s public. It covers every resident, from birth to death. There are no co-pays, no deductibles, no coverage gaps, and no random denials.

  • In the U.S., there are thousands of insurance plans. They compete not by offering better care, but by denying coverage more creatively. Doctors must spend endless hours navigating this maze, and patients have to pray their procedure is deemed “medically necessary” by someone whose degree is in actuarial tables, not medicine.

This Byzantine complexity isn’t a bug—it’s a billion-dollar feature. Every layer of paperwork is an opportunity to deny, delay, or dodge responsibility. In Canada, doctors input a treatment, and the system pays. No argument. No appeal. No financial landmines.

Emergency? Canada Has You Covered

Ask any Canadian if they get help immediately during an emergency—a stroke, heart attack, major accident. The answer is a resounding yes. Because triage in Canada is based on medical urgency, not how much money you can cough up.

Contrast this with the American system, where showing up unconscious without insurance could get you basic stabilization and a bill that might cost more than your car. And if you’re conscious? Better bring a wallet fat enough to ward off financial trauma.

The Triage “Wait Time” Lie

One of the most insidious myths peddled by defenders of U.S. healthcare is that Canadian patients “wait forever” for care. This trope is a willful misrepresentation of how triage systems work.

Here’s the truth: wait times exist in every healthcare system. But in Canada, they’re calibrated around actual need. If you need emergency surgery, you’ll get it in hours. If you have mild arrhythmia, you might wait weeks. That’s not neglect—that’s prioritization. It’s also an application of queuing theory, a mathematical principle that ensures systems run efficiently and effectively.

In the U.S., you can jump the line if you have money. In Canada, you can’t. Your bank account doesn’t affect your spot in line—your health does.

That might sound “unfair” to Americans conditioned to think everything should be purchasable. But fairness, in medicine, isn’t about privilege—it’s about need.

Doctors in Canada: Free to Focus on Medicine

Let’s talk about doctors. In the U.S., they need entire billing departments to chase down payments, fight insurance denials, and navigate the endless spaghetti bowl of policies, providers, and pre-authorizations. In Canada? Most doctors don’t even employ billing clerks. They treat the patient, enter the data, and get paid—automatically.

A Canadian doctor doesn’t care if you’re unemployed, retired, a child, or a millionaire. Everyone’s covered. No moral calculus about whether the patient can afford to live. No paperwork jungle. Just medicine.

Anecdotes with Teeth

Americans love anecdotal evidence—so let’s use one. A Canadian woman feels unwell. Her husband takes her to the hospital. She’s diagnosed with a gallbladder attack and prepped for surgery within the hour. By that evening, she’s recovering at home. Cost to them? Zero. Not even parking fees. His biggest expense? A cup of tea.

Now imagine the same event in the U.S. Without insurance, they’d be buried under tens of thousands in medical debt. With insurance? Still probably thousands, plus an exhausting battle over what gets reimbursed.

The Real Feature: Efficiency

Those dreaded Canadian “wait times”? They’re a sign of smart resource allocation. In system design terms, they build in capacity for emergencies by not scheduling every hour of every day. It’s not chaos—it’s planning.

This is the kind of thinking that allows Canada to spend half of what the U.S. spends per person on healthcare and still deliver better outcomes across nearly every major metric: life expectancy, infant mortality, chronic disease management, and overall satisfaction.

The American Nightmare

So what’s the real horror story? It’s not a Canadian waiting three months for a routine orthopedic consult. It’s an American dying because they rationed insulin. It’s someone avoiding the ER with chest pain because they can’t afford the deductible. It’s cancer patients having their treatment interrupted because their insurer found a loophole.

That’s not medicine. That’s extortion.

Conclusion: Healthcare or Wealthcare?

The U.S. system isn’t broken. It’s built this way. Designed to enrich middlemen, nickel-and-dime patients, and turn human suffering into profit margins. And the biggest scam of all? Convincing millions of Americans that anything else would be worse.

Canada proves that’s a lie.

If you truly want healthcare, you need to stop defending the system that gives you wealthcare—for the few—and lets the rest suffer quietly.

In a world where every developed nation has figured out how to provide universal healthcare except the richest one, maybe it’s time to admit: the horror stories aren’t coming from Canada.

They’re coming from home.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
