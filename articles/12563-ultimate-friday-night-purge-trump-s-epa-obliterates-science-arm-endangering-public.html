‘Ultimate Friday night purge’: Trump's EPA obliterates science arm, endangering public

By Paulo Santos

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's decision Friday to eliminate its scientific research arm drew horrified responses from public health experts and climate advocates, who warned that the Trump administration is targeting the foundation of the department's work to shield Americans from hazardous chemicals, toxic pollution, and drinking water contaminants.

"This is grim news," said Adam Gaffney, an ICU doctor at the Cambridge Health Alliance. "For decades, the EPA's Office of Research and Development has produced the science that underlies the regulations and technologies that protect us from innumerable hazards."

"You can't put a number on the lives that it has saved. Now Trump and Zeldin are killing it," Gaffney added, referring to the president's handpicked EPA administrator.

Since taking charge at the EPA, Lee Zeldin has moved aggressively to implement President Donald Trump's executive orders aimed at gutting the agency's staff and freeing oil and gas corporations from regulatory restraints.

The agency will soon have 12,448 employees, after starting the year with more than 16,000. Staffers at the targeted research office—which had more than 800 employees as of earlier this week—reportedly learned of what one public health expert called "the ultimate Friday night purge" through the EPA's public press release.

In the statement, Zeldin said the elimination of the Office of Research and Development would help "ensure the agency is better equipped than ever to deliver on our core mission of protecting human health and the environment."

But scientists said the closure of the research office would have the opposite impact, leaving the agency's ability to protect the environment and public health badly compromised.

Gretchen Goldman, president of the Union of Concerned Scientists, said that "it is absolutely devastating that Trump officials would shut down this office in its entirety."

"Science, data, and research underpin all of EPA's work, from protections from harmful chemicals to air quality standards to safe drinking water. It's hard to see how EPA can fulfill its mission without its scientific research arm," said Goldman. "The nation enjoys a cleaner environment thanks to the decades of high-quality research coming out of this office. Our nation cannot let this stand. Members of Congress must act."

In his public messaging, Zeldin has deemphasized the EPA's fundamental responsibility to protect the environment, instead casting the agency as a promoter of "energy dominance"—the slogan Trump administration officials have used to describe the president's commitment to boosting fossil fuel drilling.

Earlier this year, Zeldin boasted about launching "the biggest deregulatory action in U.S. history," targeting power plant rules, Mercury and Air Toxics Standards, and other regulations.

"Out in the open, Zeldin's EPA has been dismantling protections against precisely the sorts of dangers that right-wingers warn are coming from alleged deep-state conspiracies: toxic, cancer-causing chemicals that corporations have lobbied to freely inject into our air, water, food, and bodies," The New Republic's Kate Aronoff wrote in a recent column.

"Among the broader suite of regulations Zeldin's EPA has promised to roll back," Aronoff wrote, "is one that would require coal-fired power plant operators to upgrade wastewater treatment facilities, limiting their ability to freely discharge toxins like mercury, arsenic, selenium, lead, and bromide and to threaten local drinking water supplies."

Our Analysis:

The EPA's Unscientific Shutdown: A Deep Dive into Environmental Recklessness

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's (EPA) recent decision to dismantle its Office of Research and Development (ORD) is not just an administrative reshuffle—it's a frontal assault on the very foundation of environmental protection and public health safeguarding in the United States. This move, announced under the guise of streamlining and efficiency, represents a catastrophic pivot away from science-based policy, towards a dangerous, deregulatory agenda fueled by the Trump administration's allegiance to oil and gas interests.

The Axe Falls on Science

The elimination of the EPA's scientific backbone is a move that reeks of disdain for empirical evidence and a deliberate attempt to blind the agency to the realities of environmental and health hazards. Adam Gaffney's horrified response encapsulates the sentiment of many: the ORD has been instrumental in saving lives by informing regulations and technologies that mitigate environmental risks. By axing this department, the administration is not just cutting costs; it's endangering lives.

Zeldin's Destructive Efficiency

Lee Zeldin, Trump's EPA administrator, embodies the administration's anti-environment ethos. Tasked with implementing Trump's executive orders to decimate EPA staffing and loosen the regulatory leash on polluters, Zeldin's actions speak volumes about priorities. The significant staff reductions—from over 16,000 employees to a projected 12,448—signal a retreat from the EPA's core mission. Zeldin's justification for closing the ORD, that it will somehow make the agency "better equipped" to protect human health and the environment, is not just paradoxical; it's patently false.

The Fallout of Forsaking Science

Gretchen Goldman, representing the Union of Concerned Scientists, rightly identifies the closure as devastating. The EPA's effectiveness hinges on its ability to conduct and utilize scientific research. Without the ORD, the agency's capacity to enforce laws that ensure clean air, water, and safe chemicals is severely compromised. This decision doesn't just undermine the EPA; it undercuts the nation's health and environmental safety.

Energy Dominance over Environmental Protection

Zeldin's and, by extension, the Trump administration's emphasis on "energy dominance" marks a dangerous shift in EPA policy. This slogan, code for ramping up fossil fuel extraction, signals an alarming departure from the agency's foundational goal of environmental stewardship. The touted "biggest deregulatory action in U.S. history" is not an achievement; it's a regressive move that prioritizes industry profits over people's health and the planet's welfare.

The Real Deep-State Conspiracy

Kate Aronoff's observations highlight a bitter irony: the administration is manifesting the very dangers it claims to combat through its conspiracy-laden rhetoric. The rollback of regulations protecting against toxic pollutants essentially grants corporations a license to pollute. Actions like weakening wastewater treatment standards for coal-fired power plants directly endanger public health and contradict any rational definition of public service.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The dissolution of the EPA's Office of Research and Development is a stark reminder of the Trump administration's environmental and health policy bankruptcy. It is a direct attack on the principles of scientific inquiry and evidence-based policymaking. This isn't just an issue for environmentalists; it's a dire concern for anyone who values public health, clean air, and water.

Congressional intervention is not just warranted; it's essential. To allow this decision to stand is to be complicit in the erosion of national and global environmental health standards. The time for outrage is now, but it must quickly transform into determined, collective action. Our environment, our health, and our future demand nothing less.
