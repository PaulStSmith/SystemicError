Stephen Miller 'ripped into' ICE for not targeting immigrants at '7-Eleven'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller looks on as he speaks to reporters at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

During a tense meeting with ICE officials, Trump aide Stephen Miller strongly suggested that agents target convenience stores for undocumented migrants in an effort to up their arrest tallies, according to a report in The Washington Examiner.

The outlet spoke with three current and former federal immigration officials who recounted how Miller upbraided about a dozen ICE agents for their low deportation numbers and suggested where they might find more potential marks.

“Miller came in there and eviscerated everyone. ‘You guys aren’t doing a good job. You’re horrible leaders.’ He just ripped into everybody. He had nothing positive to say about anybody, shot morale down,” an official told the news outlet.

“Stephen Miller wants everybody arrested. ‘Why aren’t you at Home Depot? Why aren’t you at 7-Eleven?'” the official recounted.

When one official spoke up to say they were targeting "criminals" and not the "general illegal immigration population," as "Tom Homan says every time he's on TV," Miller reportedly exploded, demanding, "What do you mean you’re going after criminals?"

Laszlo Baksay, ICE deputy assistant director of media affairs, disputed the report, telling The Examiner, “Stephen Miller did not say many of the things you state."

Nevertheless, an ICE official described the atmosphere at the Department of Homeland Security as "a fearful environment. Everybody in leadership is afraid. … There’s no morale. Everybody is demoralized.”

"ICE officers tasked with arresting and transporting illegal immigrants inside the U.S. have lamented internally about the massive task they have been assigned...at a time when the White House keeps moving the goal post, according to two officials," the report said.

Miller recently told Fox News's Sean Hannity, "Under President Trump’s leadership, we are looking to set a goal of a minimum of 3,000 arrests for ICE every day, and President Trump is going to keep pushing to get that number up higher each and every single day, so we can get all of the Biden illegals that were flooded into our country for four years out of our country."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Tactics of Fear and Intimidation in Trump's Immigration Policy

In a disturbing revelation reported by The Washington Examiner, Stephen Miller, a Trump aide known for his hardline stance on immigration, has been exposed for his attempts to coerce ICE agents into ramping up their arrest quotas by targeting convenience stores for undocumented migrants. This strategy, aside from being morally questionable, underscores the administration's desperate measures to inflate deportation numbers, regardless of the ethical or human cost involved.

Miller's Scathing Rebuke of ICE Agents

According to the report, Miller's approach during a meeting with ICE officials was nothing short of tyrannical. Describing the atmosphere, one official recounted Miller's words: “Miller came in there and eviscerated everyone. ‘You guys aren’t doing a good job. You’re horrible leaders.’” This kind of leadership—if one can call it that—is not only destructive to morale but is indicative of a broader culture of fear and intimidation that seems to permeate the Department of Homeland Security under Trump's regime.

The Questionable Targeting of Non-Criminals

Miller's suggested focus on convenience stores like Home Depot and 7-Eleven as hunting grounds for undocumented migrants reveals a troubling shift away from prioritizing individuals with criminal records. This approach blatantly disregards the nuanced realities of migration and the lives of those who, often out of sheer necessity, find themselves without documentation in the U.S. It's a stark departure from the already questionable rhetoric that the administration is only out to deport "criminals."

The Dispute and Denial

Laszlo Baksay, ICE's deputy assistant director of media affairs, disputed the claims made in the report. However, the denial seems weak in the face of detailed accounts from not one, but three current and former federal immigration officials. This discrepancy raises questions about the transparency and accountability of the administration's actions and statements regarding its immigration policy.

A Fearful and Demoralized Environment

The report paints a grim picture of the state of morale within the Department of Homeland Security, describing it as a "fearful environment" where everyone in leadership is afraid, and everybody is demoralized. This atmosphere is not conducive to ethical decision-making or the protection of basic human rights, and it certainly doesn't bode well for the effective and humane management of immigration issues.

Miller's Ambitious Arrest Quota

Miller's statement to Fox News's Sean Hannity about setting a goal of a minimum of 3,000 arrests for ICE every day is both alarming and revealing. It showcases a perverse prioritization of numbers over human lives, a tactic designed to stoke the fires of the administration's base by presenting inflated arrest figures as achievements, regardless of the societal or human cost.

Conclusion

The strategies and attitudes revealed in this report are indicative of a broader, systemic issue within the current administration's approach to immigration. The focus on intimidation, fear-mongering, and the dehumanization of undocumented migrants demonstrates a troubling disregard for basic human rights and dignity. It's imperative that these tactics be scrutinized, and the administration held accountable for its actions, which seem more aligned with scoring political points than addressing the complex realities of migration and human need.
