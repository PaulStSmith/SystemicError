How Trump's 'magical thinking' is destroying the economy

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The SetupWho Actually Has PowerThe Real Decision MakersMisdirection as StrategyThe Billionaire Fantasy MachineThe Pattern

The Market Doesn’t Mysteriously Worship Trump. It Prices Power.

The Setup

The source story points to a blunt contradiction: Trump is presiding over a war that has cost the U.S. billions, rattled oil markets, and caused global economic damage, while equities keep climbing and SpaceX is being valued at absurd heights. That is the surface event. The deeper fact is uglier: wealth is not acting like an independent judge of reality. It is responding to political force, elite protection, and the expectation that damage will be socialized while gains stay privatized.

Who Actually Has Power

Trump holds formal state power, and Elon Musk and his billionaire class hold the capital power that can convert instability into asset inflation. The source makes that relationship visible without naming it cleanly enough: the White House can move markets with announcements, and the market can reward the very actors benefiting from the chaos. That is not “confidence.” It is a political economy built around intimidation, access, and the privilege to profit from volatility.

The Real Decision Makers

The decisions at the center of this story were not made by ordinary investors or consumers. Trump chose the tariffs, the war posture, and the public messaging. Billionaire backers chose to keep funding and benefiting from a system in which spectacle is treated as policy and policy is treated as a trading signal. When the source notes that the market jumps on a Trump post and drops on a Trump announcement, that is not a mystery. It is a sign that power has become personalized enough to turn public governance into private market theater.

Misdirection as Strategy

The useful lie in stories like this is that the economy is “doing well” if a few indices are up. That framing launders harm. It shifts attention away from inflation, infrastructure damage, war costs, and disrupted industrial output, and toward the paper wealth of people already insulated from the damage. The article’s strongest point is also its most damning: the system can report prosperity while ordinary people absorb the fallout. That is not confusion. It is selective accounting.

The Billionaire Fantasy Machine

SpaceX’s looming valuation is the clearest symbol of the rot. A company priced at fantasies larger than a quarter of global annual output is not evidence of rational belief. It is evidence that capital markets can detach from material reality when enough prestige, access, and speculative hunger are stacked in the same direction. The source calls this “magical thinking,” and that is accurate as an inference. More precisely, it is a permission structure: elites are allowed to pretend scale is destiny because the losses, if they come, will not be theirs alone.

The Pattern

This story is not just about Trump or Musk. It is about a governing class that turns crisis into branding, policy into market manipulation, and public consequence into a side effect. When institutional power is fused with billionaire speculation, the result is not stability disguised as strength. It is a rigged order where the people making the decisions are shielded from the costs, and the rest of the country is told to call that success.
