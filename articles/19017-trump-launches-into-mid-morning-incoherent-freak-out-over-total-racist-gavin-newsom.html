Trump launches into mid-morning incoherent freak-out over ‘total racist’ Gavin Newsom

President Donald Trump lashed out at California Gov. Gavin Newsom Sunday as a “total racist” in a lengthy and confusing rant against several Democratic figures.

Trump’s attack on Newsom was in reference to remarks the California governor made last month to a predominantly Black audience in Atlanta, Georgia, telling them he was “like you” due to scoring below average on a standardized college admissions test.

“Gavin Newscum who admitted that he suffers from mental incapacity, is unable to read a speech, and is dumb but, in saying ‘the dumb comment,’ referred to his audience, and therefore became, in the eyes of African Americans, A TOTAL RACIST — which is probably true!” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

“Gavin admits that he’s a Low IQ individual, which typically is not good for winning the Presidency. His interview may have been the worst ever given by a ‘professional’ politician. I believe, it takes him OUT OF THE RACE. Kamala [Harris] is back!”

Trump also went on to attack James Talarico, the Democratic Texas state representative and Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate. Talarico defeated Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) in his bid for Senate.

“The Democrat running in Texas, James Talarico, turns out to be a FRAUD! It showed after the Election when he beat Low IQ Candidate Jasmine Crockett (No relation to Davy Crockett!),” Trump continued.

“He’s got six Genders, insults to Jesus, only vegan food, was wearing a mask in 2023 and 2024, and is a weak, ineffective guy who we ‘allowed’ to win prior to releasing the avalanche of information we had on him because, as bad as Crockett was as a Candidate, this guy is worse! I believe that any human being running against him, sick, incompetent, close to death or, even a child, would win.”

Our Analysis:

Unraveling Trump’s Tirade: A Critical Examination

In a recent outburst, former President Donald Trump launched a series of attacks against California Governor Gavin Newsom and Texas State Representative James Talarico. Trump’s accusations range from personal attacks on intelligence to unfounded claims of racism and fraudulence. To dissect this narrative, we must examine the institutional power dynamics, the veracity of the decision-making claims, and the accuracy of responsibility as framed by Trump’s statements.

Institutional Power Dynamics

Donald Trump, as a former president and influential figure within the Republican Party, wields significant political and social power. His platform enables him to shape public discourse and influence his base. Gavin Newsom, as the Governor of California, holds substantial institutional power within his state, responsible for state-level executive decisions. James Talarico, a state representative and Democratic nominee for the U.S. Senate, has power within his legislative domain but operates on a different scale compared to a state governor or a former president.

Decision-Making Claims

Trump’s rant includes several claims: 1. Newsom’s Alleged Racism: Referring to Newsom’s attempt to connect with an audience by discussing his own academic struggles, Trump labels him a “total racist.” This assertion seems to twist Newsom’s words beyond their original intent, which was arguably to foster a sense of shared experience rather than to demean.

  1. Talarico’s Alleged Fraudulence: Trump attacks Talarico, calling him a “FRAUD” and criticizing his personal choices and political stances without providing evidence of actual fraudulent behavior.

In both attacks, Trump’s accusations appear to be more about character assassination than about actual decisions made by Newsom or Talarico in their professional capacities.

Misdirection of Responsibility

Trump’s framing attempts to direct responsibility for societal and political issues toward individuals with limited control over the matters he implicitly references (e.g., racial tensions, political polarization). In doing so, he diverts attention from systemic issues and the role of broader institutional dynamics—and potentially his own contributions to those dynamics.

Newsom’s comments, while potentially clumsy, do not in themselves wield the power to significantly alter racial dynamics in America; they’re more reflective of an individual’s attempt to communicate empathy. Talarico’s personal attributes and political positions, similarly, are presented without context, exaggerating his individual influence on complex political and societal issues.

Conclusion

Trump’s comments reflect a strategic use of his platform to undermine political opponents through personal attacks and unfounded accusations rather than substantive critique of policy or governance. This approach not only misdirects responsibility but also obscures the real sources of power and influence in the situations he critiques.

The institutions of the presidency, state governorship, and legislative representation each come with significant but distinct powers. By conflating personal criticism with institutional critique, Trump’s narrative serves to distract from meaningful political discourse and analysis. It’s crucial to differentiate between personal animosity and legitimate institutional criticism to maintain a clear understanding of where power lies and how it is exercised.
