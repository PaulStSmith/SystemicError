Republicans may have won the battle over the government shutdown after a handful of Democrats and one independent caved because they thought they couldn’t outlast Donald Trump, but they now face losing the war over the Affordable Care Act.

That is the opinion of GOP political strategist Susen Del Percio, who told an MSNBC host that the Republican Party is now trapped by its promise to hold a vote on the ACA, also known as Obamacare, which has the potential to be a loser for them no matter how the vote turns out.

Speaking with host Anna Cabrera, Del Percio said she was surprised that the eight lawmakers rolled over, but that she also thinks the GOP didn’t get a win they can cash in on — and they know it.

“Susan, what about Republicans?” the MSNBC host prompted her guest. “I mean, what did they get out of all of this? Do they have anything, any win to really point to?”

“They don’t have a win,” Del Percio shot back. “And the Democrats — it just surprises me that they couldn’t take the win, the win last week that they had at the election box.”

“Like that was big and they could have owned it,” she added. “They could have done the shutdown even towards the end of last week and said, ‘Win, win, win, and we’re forcing the Republicans to take a vote on health care subsidies — that is huge.”

“And that’s what the Republicans have been afraid of all along, is this vote,” she elaborated. “They’re going to have to take in the Senate by, I think, the second week of December. Will they support those subsidies to the Affordable Healthcare Act from expiring at the end of December? That’s going to put them in a box.”

“So if they if they vote yes, then the Democrats [can] say ‘We won, we pushed it, we got it.’ And if they vote no, they have a great election issue come 2026. So I think that the Republicans are more or less scratching their heads, saying, ‘Why aren’t the Democrats, why are they not taking this as a big win?’ And it’s just more reflective of the intra-party fighting in the Democratic Party,” she concluded.

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s ACA Quagmire: A Political Analysis

In a recent MSNBC discussion, GOP political strategist Susen Del Percio laid bare the precarious position the Republican Party finds itself in concerning the Affordable Care Act (ACA), also known as Obamacare. The analysis provided by Del Percio offers a trenchant look into the strategic missteps and potential fallout for Republicans, stemming from their convoluted handling of the government shutdown and their promises regarding the ACA. Let’s dissect this with the unyielding clarity and biting commentary it deserves.

The Shutdown Fiasco: A Hollow ‘Victory’

At first glance, the Republicans’ ability to end the government shutdown, thanks to the capitulation of a few Democrats and one independent, might seem like a tactical win. However, as Del Percio astutely points out, this victory is anything but. The GOP, in its relentless quest to undermine the ACA, has painted itself into a corner. The party’s commitment to hold a vote on the ACA has set them up for a lose-lose situation, brilliantly highlighting the shortsightedness of their strategy.

The ACA Vote: A GOP Nightmare

The impending vote on health care subsidies related to the ACA is a ticking time bomb for the Republican Party. Here’s why:

  • If they vote yes, they risk alienating their base, which has been fed a steady diet of anti-Obamacare rhetoric for years. This would not only be a blatant contradiction of their long-standing position but could also lead to significant backlash from their most ardent supporters.

  • If they vote no, they hand Democrats a golden election issue on a silver platter. It would be a stark illustration of the GOP’s disregard for the health care needs of the American populace, providing ample ammunition for Democrats in future elections.

Democratic Disarray: A Missed Opportunity

Del Percio’s surprise at the Democrats’ inability to seize this opportunity is understandable. Following a significant electoral win, the Democrats had the momentum and public support to push the Republicans into an even tighter corner over the ACA vote. Yet, internal strife and a lack of strategic cohesion have left them squandering this advantage. The failure to capitalize on the Republicans’ ACA dilemma is a stark reminder of the perennial infighting that plagues the Democratic Party, often to its own detriment.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

The GOP’s current predicament over the ACA vote is emblematic of a broader issue within American politics: the tendency for parties to prioritize short-term victories over long-term strategy and the well-being of the American people. The Republicans’ maneuvering around the ACA and the government shutdown may have been politically expedient in the moment, but as Del Percio highlights, such tactics are unsustainable and ultimately self-defeating.

As we edge closer to the ACA vote, the Republican Party must confront the consequences of its actions and the untenable position it has placed itself in. Meanwhile, the Democrats must learn from their missteps and forge a more unified and strategic path forward. Only then can both parties hope to address the pressing needs of the American public effectively.

In the end, the saga of the ACA vote is yet another chapter in the ongoing narrative of political shortsightedness and inefficacy that defines much of Washington D.C. It is a stark reminder of the need for a more thoughtful, strategic approach to governance—one that prioritizes the long-term welfare of the nation over fleeting political gains.
