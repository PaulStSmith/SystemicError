War brews as GOP members clash over glut of fresh accusations: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 26, 20254 min read

Rep. Nancy Mace (R., S.C.) looks on from the O’Neill House Office Building in Washington, D.C., February 28, 2024. (Evelyn Hockstein/Reuters)

Already facing criticism for taking an extended vacation to avoid voting on the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, House Republicans returned to work — and have been spending their days pushing censure motions against both Democrats and their own colleagues.

That has some House Republicans furious as the see colleagues skip the House Ethics Committee to rush to judgment without due process.

According to a report from the Washington Post, there is a war brewing among House Republicans aimed at grandstanding members within the caucus who use their censure motions as a way to raise their profiles.

As a “bipartisan group of members is trying to address the growing frustration” after a week of censure motions, the Post reported that some members are going public with their frustrations.

Publicity-seeking Rep. Nancy Mace’s (R-SC) unsuccessful attempt to censure Rep. Cory Mills (R-FL) for “a range of offenses” ended up being forwarded to the Ethics Committee — and that is the way it should be handled, some House members asserted.

Rep. Lance Gooden (R-TX), who voted against the censure of Virgin Islands Del. Stacey Plaskett filed by Rep. Ralph Norman (R-SC), said, “Censures mean nothing here. Removing from committees does and should only be done after Ethics does its work, and I’m not participating in committee removal outside of the Ethics Committee process ever again.”

Rep. David Joyce (R-OH) agreed and added that the spate of motions has become “ridiculous.”

The Ohio Republican, who previously served on the Ethics Committee, complained, “We have the important business in this country to do, and we’re not doing it because we’re wasting time on events like this.”

“Even members who have introduced censures said the procedure is overused. Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA), who unsuccessfully tried to censure Rep. LaMonica McIver (D-NJ) after she was indicted for an incident at an ICE facility, said the House has gotten ‘carried away’ with censures,” the Post reported.

It added, “Higgins dodged his own censure last year after he made comments demeaning Haitians on social media as ‘thugs’ who eat pets. He walked back his comments, and the censure motion against him — pushed by the Congressional Black Caucus — never got a vote.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: House Republicans’ Censure Circus

In what reads like a script for a political satire, House Republicans, fresh off an extended vacation designed to dodge the difficult vote on the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, have returned to the Capitol only to engage in what can only be described as a petty squabble fest. The latest episodes in this farcical saga involve a rash of censure motions, both against Democrats and their own colleagues, signaling not just a breakdown in decorum but a complete abandonment of legislative priorities.

The Folly of Avoidance

First, let’s address the elephant in the room: the extended vacation. The audacity to take a prolonged break to sidestep accountability on matters as grave as the Jeffrey Epstein files is not just cowardly; it’s a dereliction of duty. The public deserves transparency and action, not evasion and cowardice.

Censure Overdrive

Upon their return, instead of tackling pressing issues, House Republicans have chosen to immerse themselves in what can only be described as a campaign of self-sabotage. The Washington Post’s report outlines a scenario where Republicans, led by figures like Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC), have opted for the theatrical over the substantive, pushing censure motions as a means to elevate their profiles rather than address the nation’s pressing concerns.

The Voice of Reason?

It’s somewhat refreshing, yet simultaneously depressing, that voices within the Republican caucus, such as Rep. Lance Gooden (R-TX) and Rep. David Joyce (R-OH), call out this nonsense for what it is: a monumental waste of time. Gooden’s assertion that “Censures mean nothing here” and Joyce’s lamentation of the abandonment of “important business” in favor of these stunts, shine a light on the internal conflict tearing at the fabric of the caucus.

Hypocrisy and Irony

The irony of Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) — who narrowly escaped censure himself after making derogatory remarks about Haitians — decrying the overuse of censure motions cannot be overstated. It’s a clear indication of the hypocritical and self-serving nature of these actions. When the tool of censure, which should be reserved for the most egregious breaches of conduct, becomes a weapon in petty partisan squabbles, its value is diminished, and the institution’s integrity is eroded.

Conclusion: A Call for Sanity

This ongoing spectacle within the House Republican caucus is more than just a series of misguided attempts at political point-scoring; it’s a symptom of a deeper malaise afflicting our political discourse. The prioritization of self-promotion and partisan bickering over governance and accountability is a betrayal of the public trust.

As the bipartisan group of members attempts to curb this descent into absurdity, one can only hope for a renaissance of reason and a return to the pressing issues facing the country. Until then, the censure circus rolls on, a testament to misplaced priorities and the tragic comedy of our times.
