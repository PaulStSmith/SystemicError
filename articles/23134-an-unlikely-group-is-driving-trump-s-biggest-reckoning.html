An unlikely group is driving Trump's biggest reckoning

Based on an article by John Stoehr

The Club That Governs by Resentment

Treasury’s Contempt

Scott Bessent did not speak like a neutral technocrat at the G20. He spoke like a man using state authority to sneer at the people whose economic insecurity is supposed to be beneath his notice. In the source, his line about “over-educated and under-earning” voters is not treated as a gaffe. It is treated correctly as class contempt wearing a governing title.

That matters because Bessent is not an outside commentator. He sits inside the machinery. When a Treasury Secretary mocks struggling people as “underachievers,” he is not just insulting them. He is announcing the social order his coalition believes it deserves to run.

The Welfare State for Mediocrity

The source is blunt about what MAGA really protects: not markets, not merit, not discipline, but a subsidized hierarchy for mediocre white men. That is the real fraud at the center of the movement. It screams about dependence while building a culture of exception for the men at the top of its tribal ladder.

This is why the article’s language lands hard. The problem is not that these men are “misunderstood.” The problem is that they have turned privilege into a moral identity. They call that merit. They call that work. They call that leadership. In practice, it is a system that shields them from scrutiny while demanding obedience from everyone below them.

Projection as Governance

The accusation of “socialism” in the source is not a policy argument. It is projection. The people in charge complain about the redistribution they claim their opponents want while presiding over a social arrangement that distributes dignity, leniency, and authority upward toward their own class and race.

That is the political trick: turn domination into virtue and call accountability persecution. The source says the white-power order socializes justice and decency for its own members, even after abuse and corruption. That is not confusion. That is a governing habit. The whole point is to make consequences disappear for the in-group and reappear as moral failure in everyone else.

Masculinity as Recruitment

The source also identifies something more useful than the usual pundit noise about “male voters.” MAGA did not only sell resentment; it sold a masculinity fantasy built for men who want the authority of leadership without the burden of character. That is why the movement can celebrate “strength” while elevating bullies, snobs, perverts, and criminals.

The real threat to that project is not a polished centrist apology. It is men who refuse the bargain. Men who value competence, honesty, brotherhood, and accountability are a problem for a movement built on cowardice and obedience. That is because MAGA does not need strong men. It needs compliant ones who will confuse domination for virtue.

Misdirection Is the Point

The source is right to reject soft labels that make this coalition sound more coherent than it is. “Authoritarianism” can become a fog machine if it lets the people involved hide behind abstract nouns. The harder truth is more direct: this is a coalition of liars, cheats, thieves, and enablers who use racial grievance and masculine theater to keep power while making life worse.

The article’s framing is strongest when it names the mechanism: power sits in the presidency, Treasury, Congress, the Court, and the elite institutions that normalize the whole arrangement. Its weakness is that “whiteness” does too much explanatory work by itself. Race is central here, but so are appointments, patronage, institutional cowardice, and the willingness of educated elites to launder obvious corruption as seriousness.

The System Itself

This is the larger pattern: American power still rewards people for being loyal to the club rather than worthy of the job. MAGA did not invent that arrangement. It exposed it, weaponized it, and made the ugliness more honest. The result is a politics that celebrates mediocrity, excuses cruelty, and calls the damage strength.

That is the real story. Not just that Trump and his men are small. It is that they have built a governing style large enough to reward smallness across the whole system.
