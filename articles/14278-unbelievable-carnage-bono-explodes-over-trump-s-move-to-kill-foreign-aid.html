'Unbelievable carnage': Bono explodes over Trump's move to kill foreign aid

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Irish singer-songwriter and U2 frontman Bono tore into President Donald Trump in an interview with Esquire over the administration's decimation of foreign aid programs.

Bono, who has been involved in social and political causes around the world for decades and frequently criticized Trump in the past, said the president stands to wreck progress on health and food security for the most vulnerable.

“The most unbelievable carnage imaginable is happening to our work,” Bono told Esquire. “These are the brightest, best people, who’ve given their lives trying to serve the poorest, most vulnerable communities, and they’ve just been thrown in the dumpster.”

Despite his current criticism of Trump and the Republican Party, historically he hasn't been afraid to work with whoever will advance his causes: For instance, noted the interview, "He controversially worked with George W. Bush to pass the President’s Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief program, even against criticism from the Left. But according to a recent report, the program states that it is responsible for saving over 26 million lives since its creation."

Trump recently moved to halt that program, triggering outcry.

“I describe myself as a radical centrist,” Bono continued. “And I am sure that that sounds absurd, but I am also sure that is how we get through the future. What’s being served up on the far left and on the far right is not where we need to be.”

Trump's effort to shut down the U.S. Agency for International Development, or USAID, has resulted in the suspension of critical programs around the world that benefit the U.S. in hundreds of ways, from giving early warning of pandemics to building up support for American values abroad.

Legal challenges have been filed to these cuts, with some federal judges issuing preliminary orders that prevent the agency from being dismantled. The Trump administration has sought to skirt these rulings whenever possible.

Our Analysis:

A Symphony of Hypocrisy and Short-sightedness: The Bono-Trump Duet

In a recent tirade that reads more like a tragic opera than a coherent critique, Irish singer-songwriter and U2 frontman Bono has unleashed a barrage of criticism against President Donald Trump, particularly targeting the administration's approach to foreign aid. Bono's remarks to Esquire paint a picture of a world teetering on the brink, thanks to what he perceives as Trump's catastrophic policies. Let's dissect this performance, note by note.

The Carnage of Compassion

Bono's main gripe centers on the alleged "decimation" of foreign aid programs under Trump's watch. He melodramatically claims, “The most unbelievable carnage imaginable is happening to our work,” referring to efforts by those who serve "the poorest, most vulnerable communities." This imagery of "carnage" and "dumpsters" is emotionally charged, sure, but it skirts around a crucial question: Is foreign aid the panacea he believes it to be? A deeper dive into the effectiveness of such programs is conspicuously absent from his lament.

The Bono Paradox

Historically, Bono has displayed a remarkable ability to collaborate with political figures across the spectrum, a trait that seems at odds with his current vilification of Trump. He proudly recounts his work with George W. Bush on the President’s Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief program, which has reportedly saved over 26 million lives. Yet, when Trump allegedly moves to halt this program, Bono cries foul. This inconsistency raises an eyebrow - is Bono's outrage selective, based on the occupant of the White House rather than the policy itself?

Radical Centrism or Convenient Label?

“I describe myself as a radical centrist,” Bono declares, positing this stance as the solution to the world's ills. However, one must wonder if this self-styling as a "radical centrist" is anything more than a convenient label, a way to sidestep the messy, uncomfortable realities of the political spectrum. His critique of the extremes of both the left and the right, while calling for a middle ground, feels less like a call to action and more like a retreat to safety.

The USAID Conundrum

Bono's alarm over Trump's effort to shut down the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID) and the suspension of critical programs worldwide is genuine and shared by many. However, his analysis lacks depth. The question isn't merely whether USAID should exist but how it can operate more effectively. Moreover, Bono's commentary fails to engage with the broader, more complex debates over America's role in the world, the effectiveness of its aid, and the accountability of such programs.

Skirting the Issue

The article mentions that the Trump administration has sought to skirt legal rulings preventing the dismantling of USAID. This is a serious accusation, implying a disregard for the rule of law. Yet, Bono's critique, as relayed in the article, doesn’t delve into the implications of such actions. It's a missed opportunity to explore the tension between executive authority and judicial oversight, a critical issue in any democracy.

In Conclusion: A Performance Falling Flat

While Bono's passion for social and political causes is undeniable, his latest critique of Trump regarding foreign aid and USAID feels more like a performance than a poignant political analysis. It's rich in emotion but poor in nuanced understanding of the complex interplay between global aid, political ideology, and effective governance. Bono's heart may be in the right place, but his arguments, as presented, are singing off-key.
