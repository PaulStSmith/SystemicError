GOP warned it's speeding towards 'serious mistake' with new act

House Republicans were blasted Monday for pushing forward a law opponents say is designed to hobble agencies responsible for keeping food and drugs safe.

The criticism came as the GOP looks to move forward with the “REINS Act,” according to a report from Politico.

Short for “Regulations from the Executive in Need of Scrutiny,” the act would require federal agencies to get Congressional approval before adopting new rules.

This would include rules overseeing public health, food safety, environmental protection, health care, and more.

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) believes that Republicans are “trying to handcuff the agencies that work to make sure that our food and drugs are safe and our air and water are clean.”

Raskin, who is also the ranking member on the House Judiciary Committee, slammed the provision that would allow Congress to repeal numerous regulations through the use of a single resolution.

He asserted that such action would be used to “hide the most destructive deregulatory votes among dozens of others, completely burying it in darkness.” He added, “This is not what the American people are looking for.”

House Speaker Mike Johnson told Politico’s E&E News he is expecting the REINS Act to be in the final budget package.

Democrats and progressive advocates argue that the REINS Act could empower congressional majorities to reject regulations they oppose.

This would allow partisan divisions to effectively sideline rules crafted by dedicated experts across federal agencies.

The House has passed the REINS Act several times, but it has been slapped down in the Senate each time.

This is because it threatens the Senate filibuster. “That’s why backers think the reconciliation package is their best shot for the foreseeable future,” Politico wrote.

“It would be a war on regulations,” said Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL), the minority whip. “To take that authority away from the executive branch would be a serious mistake.”

Supporters say lawmakers need to be able to sign off on certain agency regulations in order to check the executive branch’s broad powers.

They also believe that increased congressional oversight for rules that have significant impacts on individuals and industries is essential.

Our Analysis:

The REINS Act: A Cloistered Attempt to Derail Regulatory Protections

In an audacious move that could only be described as a brazen assault on regulatory oversight, House Republicans are advancing a law that opponents argue is a thinly veiled attempt to undermine the very agencies tasked with safeguarding public health and environmental integrity. The law in question, known as the "Regulations from the Executive in Need of Scrutiny" (REINS) Act, is, at its core, a masterclass in the art of obfuscation and bureaucratic sabotage.

The Mechanics of Disruption

By requiring federal agencies to obtain Congressional approval before implementing new rules, the REINS Act effectively places a stranglehold on the regulatory process. This isn’t just about oversight; it’s about creating a bottleneck so severe that it threatens to grind the machinery of public safety to a halt. The proposed legislation covers a wide swath of regulatory territory, including—but not limited to—public health, food safety, environmental protection, and healthcare.

A Chorus of Criticism

Critics, led by voices like Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), have not minced words, accusing Republicans of attempting to "handcuff" agencies dedicated to ensuring the safety of our food, drugs, and environment. The provision allowing Congress to repeal regulations en masse, with a single resolution, is particularly contentious. Raskin’s imagery of "burying [destructive deregulatory votes] in darkness" evokes a scenario where accountability is not just diminished but actively obfuscated.

A Strategy Rooted in Partisanship

House Speaker Mike Johnson's expectation to include the REINS Act in the final budget package reveals a strategic maneuver designed to circumvent traditional legislative barriers. This method, arguably, seeks to exploit partisan majorities to override the expertise of dedicated professionals within federal agencies, substituting political whims for informed decision-making.

The repeated failure of the House to pass the REINS Act, primarily due to its implications for the Senate filibuster, underscores the contentious nature of this legislative effort. Yet, the resolve to push it through via a reconciliation package speaks volumes about the lengths to which its proponents will go to wage their so-called "war on regulations."

The Dichotomy of Oversight

Proponents of the REINS Act cloak their arguments in the rhetoric of necessary oversight and checks on executive power. However, this stance wilfully ignores the essential nature of regulatory agencies as bodies of expertise, designed to operate with a degree of independence precisely to insulate critical safety and environmental standards from the ebbs and flows of political tides.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The REINS Act represents more than just a legislative proposal; it is a deliberate attempt to erode the foundational principles of regulatory oversight. By prioritizing political control over expert guidance, the Act threatens to compromise the very mechanisms designed to protect public health and the environment. As such, it is not a question of if the American people are looking for such measures, but rather, why their representatives are so determined to pursue them at the expense of public safety and environmental integrity.
