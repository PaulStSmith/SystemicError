GOP lawmakers in ruby red state privately rage against Trump plans: 'She prayed every day'

Donald Trump's economic policies are reportedly tearing apart a ruby red state.

GOP senators and representatives in the reliably red state of Utah are talking tough on Trump's policies in private, only to later bend to the president's will, sometimes the same day, according to the conservative Wall Street Journal.

In an article called How GOP Lawmakers Are Privately Distancing Themselves From Trump’s Tariffs, the outlet tells the story of GOP lawmakers willing to make assurances against tariffs but then can't back them up.

"In April, a group of Utah business leaders visited Washington to meet with lawmakers and petition for relief from tariffs that they said were raising their costs and making it difficult to plan investments," according to the report. "The messages they received from their elected officials in private were encouraging. Their follow-up actions in public, however, fell short."

The Journal goes on to describe a specific anecdote involving GOP senator, John Curtis.

"During an April 28 meeting at the Capitol Hill Club near Congress, the chief of staff for Sen. John Curtis (R., Utah) told the group of CEOs and other executives that the senator was sensitive to the costs of tariffs on businesses in the state, according to people familiar with the conversation, including attendees of the meeting. The chief of staff said the senator planned to support those businesses by voting for a bipartisan resolution that would have ended the emergency authority used for President Trump’s so-called reciprocal tariffs, the people said," the report states. "The morning of the April 30 vote, Curtis himself told business leaders that he would consider supporting measures to curtail Trump’s tariffs if they had a chance of passing in Congress, according to people familiar with that conversation, including attendees. But that afternoon, Curtis ended up casting what proved to be a decisive vote against the measure, which failed in a 49-49 vote, after pressure from Senate GOP leadership and a visit from U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, who joined a Senate Republican lunch the day before the vote."

The Journal goes on to call the Curtis incident "a vivid example of a widespread dynamic among congressional Republicans: many have grappled with the Trump administration’s tariff policies—even while falling in line."

"It is a tough spot for these Republican lawmakers to be in. Criticism of the president’s policies could earn a public rebuke from the White House. But embracing the new duties could be politically risky: Voters disapprove of the president’s handling of the economy, inflation, and tariffs, according to recent polling by The Wall Street Journal," according to the report, which notes that the tariff issue is hurting Utah, "a largely rural state with a number of trade-sensitive industries, from agriculture and lumber to mining, energy and small businesses that use foreign materials."

The Journal cites further anger at other local GOP lawmakers, including Rep. Celeste Maloy.

"On the same April trip, Rep. Celeste Maloy (R., Utah) told business leaders that she prayed every day that the turmoil surrounding Trump’s tariffs—which at that point were roiling global markets and angering trading partners—would subside, according to people familiar with the conversation," the report states, while noting that Maloy hasn't publicly spoken out about the tariffs.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Polices: A Ruby Red State's Unraveling

In a revealing expose by the conservative Wall Street Journal, the tumultuous impact of Donald Trump's economic policies on the steadfastly Republican state of Utah is laid bare. The article, titled How GOP Lawmakers Are Privately Distancing Themselves From Trump’s Tariffs, serves as a glaring spotlight on the dissonance between private concerns and public allegiance within the GOP ranks.

Utah's GOP Senators: A Case of Double Standards

Utah's GOP senators and representatives, it seems, are caught in a precarious dance of contradiction. They voice their apprehensions about Trump's tariffs in the shadows, only to capitulate to the former president's demands in the glaring light of day. This behavior is not just a testament to the political gymnastics these lawmakers are willing to perform; it's a vivid illustration of the broader GOP's struggle to reconcile its traditional economic principles with Trump's populist policies.

The Tariff Turmoil

April's account of Utah business leaders descending upon Washington seeking respite from crippling tariffs paints a picture of a political machinery in disarray. These tariffs, as reported, are not just abstract figures on a balance sheet but are actively eroding the operational foundations of businesses, making strategic planning a Herculean task.

Senator John Curtis, in particular, is spotlighted as a prime example of this political duplicity. Despite assurances to Utah's beleaguered business leaders, his decisive vote against a measure to curtail Trump's tariffs—under the looming shadow of GOP leadership and persuasive efforts from U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer—speaks volumes. Actions, in this case, speak louder than words, and Curtis's actions echo the unsettling reality of a party at odds with itself.

The Conundrum of Criticism and Compliance

The article astutely points out the quandary faced by Republican lawmakers. To criticize Trump's policies is to risk public censure from the White House—a daunting prospect for any elected official. Yet, the alternative, embracing policies that are palpably harming their constituents and falling out of favor with the electorate, is equally fraught with peril. This balancing act is unsustainable and indicative of a party in an identity crisis.

The Broader Implications

The economic fallout from Trump's tariffs in Utah—a state with a diverse economy reliant on trade-sensitive industries—cannot be overstated. From agriculture to mining and small businesses relying on foreign materials, the ramifications are widespread. The article's reference to the dissatisfaction among voters over Trump's handling of the economy, inflation, and tariffs, underscores a critical disconnect between the GOP's leadership and its base.

A Silent Struggle

Rep. Celeste Maloy's anecdote is particularly telling. Her daily prayers for an end to the tariff-induced turmoil, while touching, highlight a pervasive sense of powerlessness among Utah's GOP representatives. The silence on such a critical issue is deafening and indicative of a deeper reluctance to confront the party's current trajectory head-on.

Conclusion

The Wall Street Journal's report is more than a critique of Trump's economic policies; it's a mirror reflecting the GOP's internal strife and the toll it's taking on states like Utah. The juxtaposition of private disapproval and public acquiescence among Utah's GOP lawmakers is a microcosm of a party at a crossroads, grappling with its identity in the wake of Trump's presidency. The question that looms large is whether the GOP can reconcile these differences or if it will continue to navigate this unsustainable path of contradiction and compromise.
