'Do we have to read it?' Morning Joe buries GOP senator over 'pathetic' Trump defense

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks with members of the media on board Air Force One en route to Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., February 6, 2026. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

An attempt by a Republican lawmaker to provide cover for Donald Trump after he posted an extremely offensive video on his Truth Social account depicting former President Barack Obama and his wife Michelle as apes got a smackdown on MS NOW on Monday morning.

During a segment on “Morning Joe” looking back on the controversy that rocked the White House last week and had Trump administration officials scrambling and contradicting each other, co-host Jonathan Lemire began to read a social media post from Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA).

That, in turn, made Joe Scarborough loudly groan.

Lemire pointed out that Trump did get some major pushback, most notably from Sen. Tim Scott of South Carolina, the lone Black GOP senator.

He added, “So despite some Republicans taking a stand, there were others who attempted to shield the president from criticism. Senator Bill Cassidy, who is facing a tough reelection —.”

“Uggggh. This was so pathetic,” Scarborough blurted and then added, “Do we have to read it?”

Lemire then noted Cassidy wrote the post, “after President Trump endorsed his opponent; actually thanked Trump for removing the video.”

“Well, he thanked Trump for removing the video, and then Donald Trump said, ‘I did nothing wrong,’” Scarborough observed.

Trump endorsed Louisiana Rep. Julia Letlow (R) over Cassidy for the Louisiana seat last month. Cassidy had voted to convict Trump during his second impeachment trial in 2021.

Our Analysis:

The Unbearable Lightness of Political Expediency

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the tempestuous theater of American politics, Donald Trump once again found himself at the epicenter of a controversy so grotesque, it beggars belief. This time, it involved a video on his Truth Social account that not only scraped the bottom of the barrel of decency but seemingly tunneled through the barrel and into the depths of outright racism. The video depicted former President Barack Obama and his wife Michelle as apes, a vile nod to one of history’s most repugnant racist tropes.

Enter Senator Bill Cassidy (R-LA), who, in a move that could be generously described as political gymnastics, attempted to shield Trump from the backlash. This incident was dissected on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” where co-host Jonathan Lemire highlighted the response, or the lack thereof, from the Republican camp. Notably, Senator Tim Scott of South Carolina, the lone Black GOP senator, did offer some form of pushback. Yet, Cassidy’s response, which came after Trump endorsed his opponent and after Trump had removed the video, was to thank Trump. Yes, you read that right. In an utterly bewildering display of gratitude for the bare minimum, Cassidy thanked Trump for removing the deeply offensive video, a video that should have never seen the light of day.

Joe Scarborough’s reaction, a mix of disbelief and disgust, mirrored what any rational observer would feel. “Uggggh. This was so pathetic,” he said. And right he was. It’s a stark reminder of the moral gymnastics some politicians are willing to perform to stay in the good graces of their party’s power players, even when it involves cosigning on behavior that is indefensibly reprehensible.

Let’s be clear: Donald Trump’s actions were not just a mere faux pas that one can simply apologize for and move on. This was a deliberate dissemination of racist content, an act that, in any other era or under any other administration, would have been universally condemned without hesitation or the need for political calculus.

And yet, here we are, witnessing a political landscape where the lines between right and wrong have been blurred by allegiance to one man. Cassidy’s attempt to provide political cover for Trump is not just “pathetic”; it’s indicative of a deeper, systemic rot within the party—a party that once prided itself on moral fortitude and principled leadership.

The endorsement saga adds another layer of irony and opportunism to this sordid tale. Cassidy, who had the temerity to vote to convict Trump during his second impeachment trial, is now facing the wrath of Trump’s endorsement machine, with Trump throwing his weight behind Louisiana Rep. Julia Letlow (R) over Cassidy. This maneuver by Trump is nothing short of a political vendetta, a clear message that loyalty to him supersedes any semblance of moral or ethical considerations.

In the end, Cassidy’s “thank you” to Trump, and the broader GOP’s tepid response to the incident, underscores a troubling trend: the party’s continual capitulation to Trump, no matter the cost to its soul or to the country. It’s a dance of convenience and cowardice that prioritizes political survival over principled stances, leaving the rest of us to wonder: at what point does the cost of such allegiance become too high?

In this episode of political theater, there are no winners, only a glaring spotlight on the systemic error that plagues our current discourse. And for those who still hold on to the threadbare notion of political integrity, it’s a sobering reminder that in today’s climate, such ideals are as endangered as they are essential.
