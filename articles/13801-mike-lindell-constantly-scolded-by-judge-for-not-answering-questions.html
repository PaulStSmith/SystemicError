Mike Lindell 'constantly scolded' by judge for not answering questions

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Mike Lindell speaking with attendees at a “Save America” rally at Country Thunder Arizona. (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

A federal judge reportedly scolded MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell on Tuesday for repeatedly rambling with long monologues instead of answering questions on the stand.

According to Kyle Clark of 9 News, U.S. District Court Judge Nina Wang became upset with Lindell during his second day of testimony.

"Lindell’s counsel is trying to guide him through how he came to believe elections are rigged. Lindell is being constantly scolded by the judge for not directly answering questions and going on monologues," Clark reported on Tuesday.

Lindell and his MyPillow company were facing trial after being sued for defamation by former voting machine executive Eric Coomer, whom he called "disgusting" and a "traitor."

"Lindell testifies he has called 50-100 people a traitor, including journalists," Clark noted. "Now to his 'crimes against humanity' claim about Coomer. Says it also applies to two journalists in the room," Clark noted.

The pillow executive also stated that he was "devastated" after presenting voting machine evidence at the White House, but President Donald Trump chose not to take action.

On his first day of testimony, Lindell told the jury that he had spent all of his money on his crusade against voting machines.

“I went from a net worth of $50 million to $10 million in the hole, and I’ve lost everything,” Lindell complained. "I personally have nothing left in the world... We've been raising funds since we ran completely out of money."

Our Analysis:

The Courtroom Circus: Lindell's Legal Lament

In a spectacle that seems ripped straight from the pages of a Kafkaesque legal drama, MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell has transformed a federal courtroom into his personal soapbox. According to reports from 9 News' Kyle Clark, U.S. District Court Judge Nina Wang had to repeatedly reel in Lindell from his rambling monologues back to the matter at hand during his testimony. This wasn't just a mere deviation from courtroom decorum; it was a full-blown assault on the judicial process's sanctity.

The Allegations: At the heart of this circus lies a defamation lawsuit brought forth by Eric Coomer, a former voting machine executive whom Lindell defamed as "disgusting" and a "traitor." The case itself is straightforward, but Lindell's responses? Anything but. Instead of directly addressing the allegations, Lindell embarked on a quest to substantiate his unfounded claims that elections are rigged, painting himself as a martyr in the process.

The Ramblings of a CEO Turned Conspiracy Theorist: What's strikingly alarming is Lindell's admission that he has branded not one, not two, but 50-100 people as traitors, extending this egregious label even to journalists present in the courtroom. His audacity knows no bounds, as he further accused Coomer (and by extension, the journalists) of committing "crimes against humanity" for their alleged roles in this supposed election fraud.

Financial Ruin in the Name of a Crusade: Lindell's testimony took a turn towards the tragicomic when he revealed the financial toll of his Quixotic quest against voting machines. Once boasting a net worth of $50 million, Lindell now claims to be "$10 million in the hole," having poured every penny into proving his baseless claims. The irony? This self-proclaimed patriot has not only bankrupted himself but has also resorted to fundraising to continue his misguided mission.

Analysis

A Dangerous Precedent: Lindell's actions and the subsequent legal proceedings underscore a dangerous trend in American politics and society. When individuals with significant platforms and resources propagate unfounded claims, it not only undermines the democratic process but also erodes public trust in our institutions. Lindell's courtroom performance is not just an exhibition of a man grasping at straws; it's a manifestation of how deep the roots of misinformation have grown.

The Cost of Lies: Beyond the immediate legal implications for Lindell and MyPillow, this case serves as a somber reminder of the real-world consequences of spreading falsehoods. Defamation lawsuits, financial ruin, and a tarnished reputation are just the tip of the iceberg. The broader societal impact — increased polarization, skepticism, and erosion of civil discourse — is far more concerning.

A Call to Reason: While it's easy to dismiss Lindell's antics as the last stand of a conspiracy theorist, doing so misses the larger point. This is a wake-up call for society to critically evaluate the information we consume and the leaders we choose to follow. As this trial progresses, one can only hope that it serves not just as a legal reckoning for Lindell, but as a moment of reflection for all those who have been swept up in the maelstrom of misinformation.

In the end, the courtroom drama starring Mike Lindell may provide some with entertainment, but the underlying issues it exposes are no laughing matter. As we watch this saga unfold, let it be a lesson in the dangers of unchecked rhetoric and the responsibility we all share in safeguarding the truth.
