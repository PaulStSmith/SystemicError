AOC insiders reveal 2028 plans: 'Height of arrogance to assume she couldn't win'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) is gearing up to either launch a presidential bid or challenge Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) in 2028, multiple people “familiar with her operation” told Axios in a report published Friday.

"It would be the height of arrogance to assume she couldn't win the 2028 nomination,” said Ari- Rabin-Havt, former deputy campaign manager for Sen. Bernie Sanders’ 2020 presidential bid, speaking with Axios.

“...She has a supporter base that, in many ways, has a larger potential width than Bernie's. She has been in the glare of the spotlight from day one and has the national campaigning experience a lot of other potential candidates are now trying to get."

According to those familiar with AOC’s 2028 plans, who spoke with Axios under the condition of anonymity, AOC has recently hired former senior advisors to Sanders in an effort to “bolster her operation.”

Insiders said AOC had not made any final decisions as to whether to pursue the Oval Office or Schumer’s seat, the latter of which was described by Axios’ Alex Thompson as a potential “generational clash,” pitting the 35-year-old progressive against the 74-year-old Democratic Party powerhouse.

AOC traveled the nation this year alongside Sanders as part of the two’s “Fight Oligarchy” tour, a series of rallies highlighting the outweighed influence of wealthy elites in politics, drawing tens of thousands in attendance, including 34,000 to one rally in Las Vegas, Nevada, the single-largest event ever held by the Democratic Party at the time.

According to recent polling, AOC is viewed more favorably than either President Donald Trump or former President Joe Biden, and in another recent poll, remains one of only three American politicians with a net positive favorability.

Our Analysis:

The Ambitions of Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez: Between Presidential Aspirations and Senate Challenges

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY), or AOC as she's popularly known, is stirring the political pot with rumors of a possible presidential bid or a bold challenge against Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) in 2028. This information, sourced from people "familiar with her operation" according to an Axios report, isn't just gossip. It's a testament to the seismic shifts occurring within the Democratic Party and the broader American political landscape.

"It would be the height of arrogance to assume she couldn't win the 2028 nomination," Ari- Rabin-Havt, a former deputy campaign manager for Sen. Bernie Sanders’ 2020 presidential bid, told Axios. This statement isn't just bravado; it's a reflection of the reality that AOC possesses a unique political appeal. Comparing her potential to Bernie Sanders, Rabin-Havt highlights an essential truth: AOC's support base may indeed have "a larger potential width" than Bernie's, a significant acknowledgment given Sanders' formidable grassroots movement.

The strategic move to hire former senior advisors to Sanders is not to be underestimated. It signifies a consolidation of the progressive front, uniting under AOC's banner to possibly challenge the old guard represented by figures like Schumer. The potential clash with Schumer, 74, is described as a "generational clash," a battle not just of age but of ideology, approach, and vision for the future of the Democratic Party and the country.

AOC's "Fight Oligarchy" tour with Sanders, drawing unprecedented crowds, including a staggering 34,000 in Las Vegas, is a clear indicator of her mass appeal and the resonance of her message against the influence of wealthy elites in politics. These aren't mere rallies; they are a mobilization of a significant segment of the American populace tired of the status quo and yearning for substantive change.

Furthermore, the mention of AOC being viewed more favorably than either President Donald Trump or former President Joe Biden, and her status as one of only three American politicians with a net positive favorability, is a testament to her political acumen and the effectiveness of her messaging. This isn't just about personality; it's about policies that speak to the needs and concerns of a broad spectrum of voters.

In a political era often criticized for its lack of vision and bold leadership, AOC's potential candidacies, be it for the presidency or the Senate, represent a beacon of hope for many. They symbolize the possibility of a politics that dares to dream big and challenge the entrenched power structures that have long dictated the American political narrative.

The implications of AOC's potential bids are profound. Should she decide to run, either path she chooses will significantly impact the Democratic Party's future direction and the broader American political discourse. Her actions may well catalyze a long-overdue generational and ideological shift, ushering in a new era of progressive politics that prioritizes people over profit, equity over elitism, and justice over just getting by.

In conclusion, while the 2028 elections may seem distant, the groundwork being laid by AOC and her team is a clear indication that the future is being written now. The political establishment should take note: the winds of change are not just coming; they're already here. And Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez is at the helm, steering the ship towards uncharted but hopeful waters.
