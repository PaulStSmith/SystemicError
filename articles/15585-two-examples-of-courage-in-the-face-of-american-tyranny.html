Two examples of courage in the face of American tyranny

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The first is Maryland Senator Chris Van Hollen, who traveled to El Salvador to seek answers about the detention and safety of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland resident whom the Trump regime abducted and sent to El Salvador’s notorious CECOT prison, admittedly in “error” due to a 2019 court order prohibiting his deportation — yet refuses to do anything about. The Trump regime alleged Garcia was affiliated with the MS-13 gang — an accusation his attorneys and his family firmly deny. It is still just an allegation; there has been no arrest, no evidence, and no legal findings.

Yesterday, Van Hollen accused the Trump administration of “outright defying” the Supreme Court’s order to facilitate the return of Garcia.“They are flouting the courts as we speak,” he charged. “Facilitating his return means something more than doing nothing, and they are doing nothing.”

The second Joseph Welch Award goes to Senator Lisa Murkowski, the Alaska Republican who has often broken with the Republican Party to criticize Trump. She was the first Republican senator to announce she would vote against some of Trump’s Cabinet nominees, the only one to criticize his berating Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, and the only one to criticize the chaos that Elon Musk and his U.S. DOGE Service are causing.

Last week, Murkowski made a courageous admission about the reality of serving in public office at a time when a tyrant in the Oval Office is bent on retribution against his political foes. Speaking at a conference in Anchorage, she said, “We are all afraid.” And then, after pausing for about five seconds, she continued:

“It’s quite a statement. But we are in a time and a place where I certainly have not been here before. I’ll tell you, I’m oftentimes very anxious myself about using my voice, because retaliation is real. And that’s not right.”

(See clip below.)

A critical step in stopping Trump’s viciousness is for Republican lawmakers to stand up to it, speak up against it, and condemn it. Congratulations, Senator Murkowski.

By contrast, this week’s Neville Chamberlain Award for trying to appease the tyrant goes to two individuals who epitomize the efforts of Britain’s former prime minister to appease Hitler.

First is Justice Samuel Alito, who (joined by Justice Clarence Thomas) wrote that the Supreme Court’s decision to block the Trump administration from deporting Venezuelan migrants under a wartime law was “premature.”

Premature? It was clear to the seven other justices that the Trump regime couldn’t be trusted to live up to an earlier ruling that said detainees were entitled to be notified of government plans to deport them — in a way that would allow them to challenge the move.

The second Neville Chamberlain Award goes to House Majority Whip Tom Emmer (R-Minn.) who opined yesterday that Trump has the legal authority to deport U.S. citizens to El Salvador on the basis of unproven allegations.

“What he’s doing is absolutely legal, which is removing those that do not have legal status in this country, that have proven to be the worst of the worst.”

“Proven?” The whole point is that nothing has been proven.

My friends, we are coming ever closer to the point when the Trump regime is no longer a legitimate part of our democracy, but a dictatorship. Courage is our only real weapon against its vileness.

Our Analysis:

The Tyranny of Misinformation and the Courage to Confront It

In a chilling testament to the erosion of democratic norms under the Trump administration, Maryland Senator Chris Van Hollen's recent ordeal shines a damning light on the administration's flagrant disregard for the rule of law. Senator Van Hollen's journey to El Salvador, in a bid to seek answers about the wrongful detention of Maryland resident Kilmar Abrego Garcia, underscores an egregious error that morphs into an outright defiance of a Supreme Court order. The Trump regime's abduction and subsequent dispatch of Garcia to El Salvador's infamous CECOT prison, despite a clear 2019 court order prohibiting his deportation, is not just an error; it's a deliberate flouting of judicial authority.

It's appalling to note that the administration's justification for this draconian action hinges on the unsubstantiated accusation of Garcia's affiliation with the MS-13 gang. This accusation, vehemently denied by Garcia's attorneys and family, remains just that—an allegation without arrest, evidence, or legal findings. Senator Van Hollen's accusation that the Trump regime is "outright defying" the Supreme Court's directive not only speaks volumes about the contempt with which this administration views the judiciary but also highlights a dangerous precedent where allegations suffice to strip away an individual's rights and freedoms.

On the other side of the coin, Senator Lisa Murkowski's candid admission about the fear and anxiety pervading public office in these tyrannical times is both courageous and alarming. Her revelations about the real threat of retaliation from the Oval Office for dissenting voices within the Republican Party underscore the toxic atmosphere of intimidation and fear cultivated by the Trump administration. Murkowski's acknowledgment of this grim reality, and her willingness to speak out against it, is commendable and serves as a beacon of hope for preserving democratic integrity and accountability.

However, the awarding of the Neville Chamberlain Award to Justice Samuel Alito and House Majority Whip Tom Emmer for their efforts to appease what can only be described as tyrannical whims, is a stark reminder of the dangers of complacency and the misguided belief in appeasement. Alito and Emmer's rationalizations for the administration's actions—notably, the premature blocking of deportations and the legal authority to deport U.S. citizens on unproven allegations—reflect a disturbing alignment with authoritarian measures and a blatant disregard for the principles of justice and due process.

This article is not just a critique; it's an urgent call to action. The Trump regime's actions, as outlined, are not merely policy disagreements but a fundamental assault on the very fabric of our democracy. The courage of individuals like Senator Van Hollen and Senator Murkowski in standing up to such tyranny is commendable, but it's a battle that requires the collective will and courage of all who believe in democracy and the rule of law.

In the face of such vileness, courage is indeed our only real weapon. The relentless pursuit of justice, the unyielding defense of democratic norms, and the unwavering condemnation of tyranny must be the guiding principles for all who hold democracy dear. The fight against the Trump regime's authoritarian tendencies is not just political—it's a moral imperative for the preservation of our democratic institutions and the protection of individual freedoms.
