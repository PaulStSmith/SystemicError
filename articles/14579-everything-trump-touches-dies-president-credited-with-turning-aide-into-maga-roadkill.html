Rick Wilson, who helped found the anti-Trump Lincoln Project, which is consistently filled with current and former Republicans who oppose the President, commented Wednesday on the "humiliating exile" of richest man Elon Musk.

"If I’m remembered for nothing else—and there’s a nonzero chance I won’t be—it will be for a single ironclad political law that holds across time, space, and every variable in politics: Everything Trump touches dies," Wilson wrote. "What started as shorthand for the emotional, legal, and reputational annihilation of Trump’s cronies has metastasized into something bigger and bleaker. It now includes America’s global influence, our economy, our system of government, our rule of law, and the increasingly fragile scaffolding of liberty itself. Even Trump’s most obsequious sycophants—especially them—eventually get the knife. Some in public, many in private."

That same concept then came for the richest man in the world, and "first buddy," Elon Musk, according to Wilson's analysis.

"And now, the latest name added to the long, sad ledger of MAGA roadkill is none other than former Co-President and Viceroy of DOGE: Elon Musk," he said on Wednesday.

He continued:

"Remember Elon just a few short months ago? He wasn’t just powerful—he was the power behind the throne. The real Prime Minister of the United States. The Lord Regent of Dark Elite Ultra Gothic Hyper Cyber MAGA governance. He had Trump on speed dial, could make senators grovel with a tweet, and walked the White House halls like he owned the place."

Wilson added, "Because, for a while, he rather did."

"He became bigger than Trump," according to Wilson, who detailed Musk's rise to GOP fame before showing his quick downfall.

"Elon was the belle of the MAGA ball, fawned over by every frothing, red-hatted courtier in the palace. But the Trump curse is patient. And now?" Wilson asked. "Elon’s deep drink from the poisoned chalice has cost him billions. It’s left him on the outs with Trump’s White House (Susie Wiles, to her credit, wanted him gone before they even took office) and exiled from even Trump’s island of misfit toys. Right now, clearly in the hands of very, very, very expensive crisis communications professionals, he’s out chasing press hits and groveling before normies on what can only be described as a Humility Tour."

Our Analysis:

The Fallout of Associating with Trump: A Study in Political and Personal Ruin

Donald Trump, a name that resonates with controversy, chaos, and, according to Rick Wilson, a conservative strategist and co-founder of the anti-Trump Lincoln Project, a Midas touch in reverse. Unlike the mythic king whose touch turned everything to gold, Wilson posits, everything Trump touches "dies." This grim assessment isn't just a flippant remark but a stinging indictment of the toxic influence Trump wields over his allies, his party, and, by extension, the country.

Wilson's analysis, which extends Trump's reach of ruin to the "richest man in the world," Elon Musk, is not just about the financial or political fallout of being in Trump's orbit. It's a cautionary tale of the emotional, legal, and reputational annihilation that follows those who align too closely with the former president. Wilson's use of the term "MAGA roadkill" to describe Musk is both brutal and vivid, illustrating the sharp decline from grace that associates of Trump often face.

The Illusion of Power: Musk's Meteoric Rise and Fall

Elon Musk, once revered as the "real Prime Minister of the United States" and the "Lord Regent of Dark Elite Ultra Gothic Hyper Cyber MAGA governance," according to Wilson, is now on what is derogatorily described as a "Humility Tour." This dramatic reversal of fortune from power-behind-the-throne to seeking redemption in the public eye underscores the volatile nature of political favoritism and the precarious position of those who seek power through proximity to Trump.

The description of Musk's relationship with Trump and his administration reads like a cautionary fable. Musk's initial elevation to GOP fame and his subsequent downfall is a testament to the transient nature of political alliances and the danger of intertwining one's fate too closely with that of a polarizing figure like Trump. The term "Trump curse" is apt, signifying a seemingly inevitable downfall that awaits those who enter Trump's inner circle.

The Bigger Picture: A Nation Under Strain

Wilson's commentary transcends the personal plights of Trump's associates and touches upon a more profound and disturbing implication: the erosion of America's global influence, economy, system of government, rule of law, and the very scaffolding of liberty itself. This broader analysis is a searing critique of the Trump administration's impact on the foundational elements of American democracy and its standing in the world.

The mention of Trump's "obsequious sycophants" and their inevitable betrayal highlights a cycle of loyalty and discard that characterizes Trump's leadership style. This pattern not only destabilizes individual lives but also threatens the integrity and functionality of the government and its institutions.

Conclusion: A Path Forward or a Warning Unheeded?

Rick Wilson's analysis, while focused on the fall of Elon Musk, serves as a broader warning about the dangers of associating too closely with divisive political figures. The article is a scathing rebuke of Trump's Midas touch in reverse, where everything he touches doesn't turn to gold but withers away.

It remains to be seen whether Wilson's warning will be heeded by current and future political movers and shakers. However, the "long, sad ledger of MAGA roadkill" is a testament to the destructive path left in Trump's wake. The question for those in power, or seeking it, is whether the allure of proximity to Trump is worth the inevitable cost to their reputations, careers, and, potentially, their personal integrity. The cautionary tale of Elon Musk's rise and fall may just be the latest chapter in an ongoing saga of political and personal ruination.
