'Lost all hope': Trump policies are crushing food industry in Republican heartland

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 13, 20264 min read

Louisiana crawfish processors say it’s the height of spring season, but the Acadiana Advocate reports their crawfish processing tables are empty and money is vanishing out the door due to harsh new policies enacted by President Donald Trump.

“It’s a s——show,” said Charlie Johnson, owner of a crawfish processing plant in Breaux Bridge, Louisiana. “We’d like to be processing. We prepped all offseason to make this work, but you can’t do anything because you don’t have labor. And then also having to tell your farmers that you can’t move their product.”

“Nearly all of Louisiana’s 20 crawfish processors are unable to hire migrant labor because of the federal cap on temporary visas, Louisiana Agriculture and Forestry Commissioner Mike Strain told [Louisiana] legislators last week,” according to the Acadiana Advocate.

“Without those workers, the crawfish don’t get peeled,” said Strain. “The plants will shut down, and either, one, those crawfish are not consumed, or two, as has happened in the past, they will fill up tractor-trailer loads of these crawfish, send ‘em down to Mexico, get ‘em peeled and bring ‘em back.”

But now normally bustling crawfish prep tables sit empty at the peak of spring season thanks to Trump’s onerous new process for legally hiring migrant workers.

Louisiana Crowley processor Alan Lawson says he has very little expectation of recouping his loss this year after his 125 migrant helpers failed to arrive this season. The Advocate reports his workers normally start in December, and his company is not eligible for the additional visas reserved for returning workers in January or for those entering a February federal admissions lottery. As of now, Lawson’s nearby refrigerating units — which are usually packed tight this time of year — hold only a few crawfish tail packages from last season.

“I’ve lost all hope. We would be happy going from 125 people to just give us 50. At least we can salvage (this),” said Lawson, standing in front of a crawfish sorter. “This machine doesn’t make us a whole lot of money when it’s not making noise.”

He told the Advocate that he has contacted several Republican officials, including Rep. Mike Johnson, who is the U.S. House Speaker, Gov. Jeff Landry and other contacts in Washington, D.C. But all he’s received is the assurance that they’re “working on it.” Louisiana residents overwhelmingly voted for Donald Trump in the 2024 presidential election.

“This will cease to exist,” Lawson said about his processing plant if the issue isn’t resolved before next season.

In the meantime, the Advocate reports processing will likely shift to Mexican tables and push U.S. processors out of the picture.

Analysis: The Crawfish Crisis in Louisiana Under Trump’s Policies

Institutional Power and Decision-Makers

At the heart of Louisiana’s crawfish processing crisis lies a federal cap on temporary visas, a policy decision directly attributable to the administration of President Donald Trump. The president and his administration hold the institutional power to enact, amend, or repeal federal immigration policies, including those governing temporary work visas. In this scenario, the decision to impose harsher visa policies falls squarely on the executive branch of the United States government, led at the time by President Trump.

Impact on Local Businesses and Workers

The consequences of these policy decisions are vividly illustrated by the experiences of local crawfish processors like Charlie Johnson and Alan Lawson. Their inability to hire migrant labor due to the federal cap on temporary visas directly results from the policies implemented by the Trump administration. This has led to significant financial losses for these businesses and threatens the viability of Louisiana’s crawfish processing industry.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article suggests that local processors and the Louisiana Agriculture and Forestry Commissioner, Mike Strain, are victims of federal policy decisions. There is no indication that the article misdirects responsibility towards actors with limited control over the situation. Instead, it accurately places the blame on the federal policies enacted under President Trump’s administration, which have a direct and detrimental impact on the crawfish processing industry in Louisiana.

Conclusion: Accurate Attribution of Blame

The situation described in the article is a clear example where criticism of the Trump administration’s policies is well-placed. The administration’s decision to impose harsher visa policies directly affects the ability of Louisiana crawfish processors to hire the necessary labor to operate effectively. This has resulted in financial losses for local businesses and could potentially lead to the industry’s decline in the region.

Criticism is not misplaced in this instance; rather, it highlights the significant impact of federal immigration policies on local economies and industries. The Trump administration, holding the institutional power to enact and modify these policies, rightly bears the responsibility for the consequences of these decisions. This analysis rejects any attempt to shift blame away from federal policymakers who have the authority to alleviate the situation but have chosen not to act in a way that supports the local crawfish processing industry in Louisiana.
