‘He promised!’ CNN host drops the hammer on Trump over broken pledge on jobs

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Unpacking the Broken Promise: A Sharp Analysis

Screengrab / CNN

The latest jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics revealed, among other things, that Black unemployment reached its highest level since January 2022, a revelation that CNN highlighted Saturday as yet another broken promise from President Donald Trump.

“Who’s taking the Black jobs now?” asked CNN’s Victor Blackwell, referencing Trump’s past claim that “migrants” were taking “African-American jobs.”

“On the 2024 campaign trail, he promised millions of new jobs to Black voters, and July was the second consecutive month that the unemployment rate for Black Americans climbed, and that's relevant to every worker.”

The latest jobs report left experts “stunned” given the relatively low new jobs created in July of 73,000 – far below the projected 115,000 – as well as the significant revisions to new jobs created in May and June, with June’s job numbers revised from 147,000 to just 14,000. And while unemployment ticked up by just 0.1%, for Black Americans, that figure climbed from 6% to 6.8%, the highest rate in nearly four years.

“These numbers matter, not just because it affects Black people; the reality is that Black Americans may feel it first, but all Americans could feel this next,” Blackwell said. “The pitch to America was economic, specifically to Black voters and Black men, more jobs, lower prices, and now we see these numbers.”

Blackwell was joined by Michael Harriot, author and columnist at The Grio, who also laid into Trump for his broken promise on Black employment, and warned that the spike in Black unemployment was a strong indicator for future economic turmoil.

“I wonder what happened to all those Black jobs? Remember, economists always say the economic recession hits Black Americans first, and you can almost predict since the last 50 years a recession by the number of Black jobs,” Harriot said.

“It historically has always been about double the white unemployment rate, but what we're seeing now is going to get worse. So it's going to get worse for Black folk before it gets worse for Americans, but it's going to get worse for Americans.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Broken Promise: A Sharp Analysis

The recent jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics has unveiled a stark reality that contradicts the grandiose promises made by President Donald Trump, particularly to Black Americans. CNN's coverage, led by Victor Blackwell, spotlights this discrepancy, framing it as "yet another broken promise" from Trump. The report's findings are not just numbers; they are a reflection of lived experiences and failed policies.

Black Unemployment: A Rising Concern

The increase in Black unemployment to its highest level since January 2022 is alarming. This rise to 6.8% is not just a statistic; it's a clear indication of the widening economic disparities that continue to plague Black communities. Trump's 2024 campaign promises of millions of new jobs for Black voters stand in stark contrast to this reality. The fact that this is the second consecutive month of rising unemployment rates for Black Americans further punctuates the failure to deliver on these promises.

The Misleading Narrative on Migrants and Jobs

Trump's narrative that migrants are taking "African-American jobs" is a diversion, a tactic to shift the blame without addressing the systemic issues at hand. The problem isn't the migrants; it's the policies and the lack of targeted efforts to improve employment opportunities for Black Americans. The rhetoric serves only to divide and distract from the true economic challenges facing the nation.

Economic Indicators and Warnings

The jobs report's revelation of only 73,000 new jobs created in July, significantly below the expected 115,000, coupled with the drastic revision of June's job numbers, signals a troubling trend. Experts are "stunned," and rightly so. This isn't just an economic hiccup; it's a symptom of deeper issues within the economy. Michael Harriot's comments underscore this point, warning of the broader implications of rising Black unemployment as a precursor to potential economic turmoil.

The Disproportionate Impact

Harriot's observation that recessions hit Black Americans first is a critical insight. The persistent gap, where Black unemployment historically doubles that of white unemployment, is not just an economic disparity; it's an indictment of the systemic inequalities that continue to persist. The worsening condition for Black Americans is a harbinger of broader economic challenges that will eventually impact all Americans.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Action

This jobs report, and the coverage by CNN, serves as a stark reminder of the unfulfilled promises made to Black Americans. It's not enough to make campaign promises of economic prosperity if those promises are not met with concrete actions and policies that address the systemic barriers to employment faced by Black communities.

The narrative that migrants are to blame for the loss of "African-American jobs" is a misleading diversion from the real issues. The focus should be on creating equitable opportunities and addressing the systemic inequalities that continue to hinder economic progress for Black Americans.

As we move forward, it's imperative that these conversations lead to action. The economic well-being of Black Americans should not be used as a campaign talking point but should be at the forefront of economic policy-making. The rising unemployment rate among Black Americans is a warning sign that cannot be ignored. It's time for accountability and a recommitment to policies that genuinely address the economic disparities facing Black communities. Without this, the promises of economic prosperity will remain unfulfilled, leaving behind a legacy of broken promises and missed opportunities.
