Trump acting untouchable as biggest threat to his presidency looms: DC insider

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Trump Runs the Senate Like a Loyalty Shop

The Real Center of Power

The article’s most revealing sentence is also the most embarrassing for Senate Republicans: they have spent years training Donald Trump to believe that humiliation comes with rewards. That is not a personality quirk. It is a power relationship. Trump holds the leverage because GOP lawmakers keep handing it to him.

The Senate is supposed to be an institution with its own authority. In this story, it functions more like a compliance layer for Trump’s moods.

A Manufactured Crisis

The immediate trigger was Trump’s fury over Republicans refusing to eliminate the filibuster for his voter-restrictions bill. He responded by blowing up a housing affordability deal that even the White House was publicly praising as major legislation. That is the governing model here: if Trump does not get his way, he sabotages the party’s own accomplishments to remind everyone who is boss.

This is not confusion. It is deliberate coercion by one man and disciplined surrender by the people who know better.

The Cowardice Is Collective

The article correctly notes that individual senators sometimes show principle for a moment, then fold after Trump yells at them. That is the real pattern. Isolated resistance is tolerated only until Trump applies pressure. Then the party reverses itself and calls the result “process.”

That framing matters because it launders submission into normal politics. Senators who cave are not “caught off guard.” They are choosing self-preservation over institutional responsibility, and they are doing it in public.

The Blame Goes Up, Not Down

The weak, familiar trick in coverage like this is to make Trump’s tantrums the whole story, as if the problem were volatility rather than the structure that rewards it. But the decision-makers are not the people being yelled at. The decision-makers are the lawmakers who keep yielding, the party officials who keep rationalizing, and the caucus that keeps refusing to impose any cost for abuse.

Even the Axios framing lands on “crisis” without naming the architecture of that crisis: a party that has outsourced its judgment to a man who treats loyalty as a substitute for governance.

What the Senate Has Become

The line from Trump ally that “the Senate is now behaving like the Senate” is meant as reassurance, but it reads like a confession. A chamber that waits for permission to act is not independent. It is managed.

Trump’s reported pleasure in seeing Republicans lose when his name is not on the ballot only sharpens the picture. He does not need a healthy party. He needs dependents. And the party, terrified of losing access to him, keeps accepting that arrangement.

The Pattern Beneath the Noise

This is how institutional decay looks before it becomes open ruin: leadership by tantrum, obedience dressed up as strategy, and legislators who keep mistaking submission for influence. The scandal is not that Trump acts like a strongman. The scandal is that Senate Republicans keep building a system that lets him.
