'Dangerous': Ex-official raises alarm over Epstein file war ripping DOJ apart

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of the Absurd: DOJ vs. FBI

Radio host Daniel Bongino speaks during the House Judiciary Committee hearing on Policing Practices and Law Enforcement Accountability at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, DC, U.S. June 10, 2020. Michael Reynolds/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo

The growing war between Attorney General Pam Bondi and top FBI officials Kash Patel and Dan Bongino over the transparency of the Jeffrey Epstein files led one former top DOJ official to rip into the entire crew.

During an appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend," ex-acting United States Assistant Attorney General Mary McCord expressed outrage at the internal battle at the DOJ that has led the FBI's Kash Patel and Dan Bongino to reportedly call for Bondi's firing.

Speaking with the hosts, McCord expressed genuine alarm that the war between the officials is putting the country at risk.

"How serious a rift is this and what do you make of the deputy FBI director, according to sources ,demanding that the attorney general be fired and he didn't show up to work yesterday?" host Jonathan Capehart asked.

"He's not a serious person," McCord shot back." He came into this job with a serious mission, he has no relevant experience to be the FBI deputy director and I think that's now showing. "

"You know, there are certainly times in my long tenure at the Department of Justice when there are differences between leadership of the Department of Justice and leadership of the FBI," she added. "We have disagreed on various things, but you don't do this; you don't go out in public and demand resignations. You don't have public spats like this. You you deal with those things like adults who are doing serious work, which is law enforcement and protecting national security in this country."

"The conspiracies about it and the reason that this is so annoying to the MAGA base is all just such a sideshow. and then to have that boil over into this very public spat, I mean, I think it just shows us all how dangerous this crew that is leading the Department of Justice and the FBI," she charged.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: DOJ vs. FBI

In what can only be described as a circus of the highest order, the internal squabble within the Department of Justice (DOJ) and between the DOJ and the FBI has transcended the bounds of professional disagreement into a full-blown spectacle. The cast of characters in this drama includes Attorney General Pam Bondi, FBI officials Kash Patel and Dan Bongino, and the voice of reason (or so it seems), former acting United States Assistant Attorney General Mary McCord.

The Plot Thickens: At the heart of this debacle is the transparency of the Jeffrey Epstein files—a matter of significant public interest, given Epstein's notorious legacy and the powerful individuals connected to him. Yet, instead of focusing on the gravity of the issue, we have high-ranking officials engaging in what can only be described as a political brawl, complete with demands for firing and absenteeism from critical roles.

McCord's Lament: McCord's appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend" wasn't just a critique; it was a scathing indictment of the current state of affairs within these crucial law enforcement bodies. Her outrage is palpable and justified. The "war" between Bondi, Patel, and Bongino, as she puts it, isn't just an internal conflict—it's a spectacle that undermines the very mission of these agencies.

A Serious Rift: Jonathan Capehart's query about the severity of this rift and the astonishing demand by a deputy FBI director for the attorney general's firing (followed by a no-show at work) reveals the depth of dysfunction. To which McCord's response is both a dismissal and a diagnosis: a lack of seriousness and relevance from those who should be leading by example.

Adults in the Room: McCord reminisces about her time at the DOJ, acknowledging disagreements between DOJ and FBI leadership but emphasizing the adult manner in which these were traditionally handled. Her words echo the sentiment of anyone watching this unfold: You deal with these things like adults, especially when the stakes involve law enforcement and national security.

The Sideshow: Perhaps the most damning part of McCord's critique is her characterization of the entire fiasco as a sideshow, particularly annoying to the MAGA base and dangerously indicative of the caliber of individuals leading the DOJ and FBI. This isn't just about internal politics; it's about the security and integrity of the nation.

In Conclusion: As we peel back the layers of this sordid affair, it's clear that the real losers are the American people. The transparency of the Epstein files is a matter of public interest, yet it has been overshadowed by petty infighting and power plays. The DOJ and FBI, under the current leadership, appear more concerned with their internal battles than with serving the public's need for transparency and justice.

In the end, McCord's outrage is not just warranted; it's a wake-up call. The question remains: will anyone heed it, or will the circus continue unabated?
