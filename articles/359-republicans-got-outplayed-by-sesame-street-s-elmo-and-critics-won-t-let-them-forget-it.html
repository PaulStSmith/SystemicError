Republicans got outplayed by Sesame Street's Elmo — and critics won't let them forget it

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJune 22, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Elmo Was Never the Point. Control Was.

The Setup

House Republicans tried to turn an Elmo clip into a cheap patriotism prop, calling the puppet a “certified PATRIOT” and mocking Democrats as if a children’s character had suddenly joined their political tribe. The stunt collapsed almost immediately under its own hypocrisy, because the same movement has spent years attacking Sesame Street and PBS as “woke” and, in Trump’s case, moving to cut federal funding altogether.

Who Holds Power

The people with actual institutional power here are not the online users fact-checking a puppet clip. They are Trump and House Republicans, who control federal levers, set the tone of the party, and can punish public media through budgets and executive orders. Elmo has no agency in this fight; he is being drafted into it by politicians who do.

The Real Target

The source makes clear that this was never just about a World Cup greeting. Trump’s hostility toward PBS appears to be both ideological and personal, and the reporting points to a long memory: Sesame Street has mocked him for decades through Ronald Grump, while later episodes also took aim at The Apprentice. That is not random cultural snark. It is a sustained public humiliation of a man who cannot tolerate being lampooned by something so widely loved and so institutionally harmless.

The Misdirection

The Republican framing is doing what it always does: pretending the offender is the victim and the victim is the offender. They want the audience to focus on Elmo’s brief “Go Team USA” line and ignore the machinery behind the punchline. The real story is not that Democrats are “rooting for foreigners.” The real story is that Trump’s allies are trying to launder an old grievance against public media through a mascot and then call it patriotism.

The Pattern

This is a familiar political habit: authoritarian resentment dressed up as cultural warfare. First, attack an institution for being “woke.” Then defund it. Then mine its symbols for convenience when the optics suit you. The point is not consistency; it is domination. If a children’s show can be punished for mocking power, then the message is clear enough: in this politics, satire is tolerated only when it flatters the ruler.

What This Says

The Elmo episode is small, but the structure behind it is not. A movement with real governing power spends its time picking fights with a puppet, a public broadcaster, and the idea that institutions can exist outside its loyalty test. That is not confusion. It is a governing style built on grievance, revisionism, and the expectation that everyone else will forget who started the fire.
