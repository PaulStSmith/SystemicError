New unemployment claims jump to highest level in months as Trump economy teeters

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Unpacking the Economic Turmoil Under Trump’s Watch

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he arrives to deliver remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, U.S. December 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Federal data released Thursday shows that the number of Americans filing for unemployment benefits surged last week, another indication of growing instability in President Donald Trump’s economy as corporations lay off workers en masse and prices continue to rise.

For the week ending December 6, new unemployment claims jumped to 236,000—an increase of 44,000 from the previous week, according to figures from the US Labor Department.

Andrew Stettner, an unemployment insurance expert at The Century Foundation (TCF) noted that new unemployment claims are now at their highest level since early September.

“These totals don’t include an additional 12,732 former federal workers who are also now relying on unemployment benefits, as the number of federal workers on UI has stayed at levels not seen since the pandemic, even after the government shutdown has ended,” Stettner said.

“This disappointing news comes on the heels of other troubling labor market data,” he continued, pointing to private-sector payroll figures showing the US economy lost 32,000 jobs in November. “With hiring still so weak, it is no surprise that the percentage of workers feeling confident enough to quit their job dropped to its lowest level since the beginning of the pandemic in April 2020. In fact, our polling shows that 27% of Americans said they took on a ‘second job, side hustle, or gig work’ in the past year to help make ends meet.”

The updated unemployment numbers come as Trump is on an economic messaging tour during which he has dismissed the notion that his policies have worsened the country’s affordability crisis, calling such claims a Democratic “hoax” even as polling shows Americans—including a significant percentage of his own voters—increasingly blame the president for rising costs for groceries and other necessities.

“We inherited the highest prices ever, and we’re bringing them down,” Trump said, falsely, during a stop in Pennsylvania earlier this week.

“We’re crushing it, and you’re getting much higher wages,” the president added, another falsehood.

Survey data released Thursday by The Century Foundation shows that Americans are increasingly skipping meals and doctor visits as prices rise.

“Roughly three in 10 voters delayed or skipped medical care in the past year due to cost, while nearly two-thirds switched to cheaper groceries or bought less food altogether,” the group noted in a summary of its findings. “About half tapped into their savings to cover everyday expenses.”

Julie Margetta Morgan, president of The Century Foundation, said in a statement that “while President Trump calls affordability a ‘hoax,’ countless families are being forced into impossible tradeoffs every day as a result of Trump’s disastrous policies that are jacking up prices.”

“Working-class Americans are living in a different, harsher economy under Trump,” Morgan added, “and they feel the impacts of financialization—and the added risks and costs that come with it—most severely.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Economic Turmoil Under Trump’s Watch

The latest data from the Federal Government paints a grim picture of the American economy, one that starkly contrasts the rosy scenarios often peddled by President Donald Trump. A surge in unemployment claims—to the tune of 236,000 for the week ending December 6, marking an increase of 44,000 from the week prior—signals not just a blip but a worrying trend of growing instability. This is not merely a number; it’s a glaring indicator of the turbulent economic waters that the United States is currently navigating under Trump’s stewardship.

The Unvarnished Truth About Unemployment

Andrew Stettner from The Century Foundation brings us closer to the stark reality, pointing out that these numbers have reached their highest levels since early September. But here’s the kicker: these figures don’t even account for the 12,732 former federal workers now dependent on unemployment benefits, a number that remains alarmingly high post-pandemic and subsequent government shutdown. This fact alone should raise eyebrows and questions about the purported strength of the economic recovery narrative.

The labor market data further compounds the issue, with the private sector shedding 32,000 jobs in November. In an economy that Trump touts as booming, this job loss is a cold shower on the administration’s parade. It’s no wonder that the percentage of workers confident enough to quit their jobs has plummeted to its lowest since the pandemic’s onset in April 2020. The reality on the ground, underscored by 27% of Americans taking on additional work to make ends meet, starkly contradicts the administration’s victory lap over the economy.

Trump’s Economic Messaging: A Masterclass in Denial

In an almost Orwellian twist, Trump has embarked on an economic messaging tour, dismissing the glaring signs of economic distress as a Democratic “hoax.” This denial comes in the face of his own voters feeling the pinch and increasingly holding him accountable for the spiraling costs of living. Trump’s claims of inheriting the highest prices and supposedly bringing them down are not just misleading; they’re an affront to the millions struggling to afford basic necessities.

The president’s assertions of “crushing it” and the promise of “much higher wages” fly in the face of the harsh realities that The Century Foundation’s survey data reveals. With Americans increasingly skipping meals and medical care due to rising costs, the claim of economic prosperity under Trump’s policies is not just a falsehood—it’s a cruel joke on the working-class Americans bearing the brunt of his disastrous policies.

A Tale of Two Economies

Julie Margetta Morgan of The Century Foundation captures the essence of the Trump economy: a harsher reality for working-class Americans, forced into “impossible tradeoffs” by policies that exacerbate financial strain. The narrative of financialization under Trump’s watch reveals a tale of two economies: one where the rich continue to thrive and another where the average American struggles to survive.

In summary, the evidence is overwhelming and the verdict is clear. The Trump administration’s economic policies have not only failed to usher in the prosperity promised but have actively contributed to an environment of instability and hardship for countless American families. It’s time to call out the administration’s economic messaging for what it is: a masterclass in misinformation and denial, divorced from the lived realities of the people it claims to serve.
