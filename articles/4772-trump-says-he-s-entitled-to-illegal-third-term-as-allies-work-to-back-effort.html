Trump says he's 'entitled' to illegal third term as allies work to back effort

President Donald Trump raised eyebrows and angst among democracy defenders Friday for saying he deserves an unconstitutional third term in office, remarks that came a day after reporting that right-wing activists are drafting an executive order that could empower him to ban mail-in ballots and voting machines ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

“Maybe we do one more term. Should we do one more?” the 79-year-old Republican president asked attendees of an event at the Port of Corpus Christi in Texas on Friday to roaring applause. “Do one more term. Well, we are entitled to it.”

During his State of the Union address on Tuesday, Trump rehashed his thoroughly debunked claim that Democrats stole the 2020 election for former President Joe Biden, saying this “should be my third term.”

A third term would require a constitutional amendment, as the 22nd Amendment restricts US presidents to two terms in office.

Extensions of presidential terms or abolition of limits are hallmarks of dictators and backsliding leaders of erstwhile democracies. After Chinese President Xi Jinping lifted constitutional term limits in 2018, Trump marveled, “He’s great,” adding, “He’s now president for life.”

Trump has made cryptic allusions to a third term in office on multiple occasions.

While many Trump supporters believe he should also be president for life, his allies in actual positions of power—including Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) and political strategist and convicted fraudster Steve Bannon, whom Trump granted clemency—have backed a third term for his administration.

A constitutional amendment enabling a third Trump term is not under any consideration and is all but impossible by the 2028 election. So Trump and his allies are working on other ways for the president to remain in office, focusing heavily on voter suppression. The Washington Post reported Thursday that a group of right-wing activists is writing a draft decree that would give the president “extraordinary power over voting.” On Friday, Democracy Docket published an April 2025 version of the draft order provided by a Trump ally, which the outlet described as “riddled with errors.”

According to the Post, the draft executive order would cite the pretext of alleged Chinese interference in the 2020 election. The Office of the Director of National Intelligence concluded that there was no such interference.

MS NOW national security contributor Marc Polymeropoulos called the draft order “bats--- authoritarianism.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Unconstitutional Third Term Ambitions: A Dive into Desperation and Delusion

In a display of audacity that borders on the absurd, President Donald Trump has once again ignited controversy with his declaration of entitlement to an unconstitutional third term in office. This statement, made during an event in Texas, was not a novel outburst but a continuation of Trump’s dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism. The implications of such rhetoric are profound, not only for the erosion of democratic norms but also for the alarming support it receives within certain circles of power.

The Unconstitutional Yearning

Trump’s assertion that he “deserves” a third term, coupled with his unfounded claims of election theft in 2020, lays bare a stark disregard for the U.S. Constitution—specifically, the 22nd Amendment, which unequivocally limits presidents to two terms. This amendment is a safeguard against the very tyranny that the founders feared could emerge from prolonged tenure in power. Yet, Trump’s rhetoric, alarmingly cheered on by his supporters, echoes a dangerous precedent set by leaders like Chinese President Xi Jinping, whose term limits were abolished to extend his rule indefinitely. Trump’s admiration for such authoritarian maneuvers is not just troubling; it’s a red flag for the health of American democracy.

A Dangerous Path to Power Retention

What’s even more disturbing is the strategy emerging from Trump’s camp to circumvent the constitutional barrier. Reports of a draft executive order aimed at granting the president “extraordinary power over voting” reveal a sinister plot not just to cling to power, but to undermine the very foundation of democratic elections. By exploiting unfounded allegations of foreign interference, these efforts seek to justify draconian controls over voting mechanisms—a classic playbook move of autocrats seeking to manufacture their mandate.

The Farce of Fighting Fraud

The draft order, reportedly rife with errors, is not just a testament to the incompetence of its architects but also to their blatant disregard for the truth. The Office of the Director of National Intelligence’s findings that refute the claims of Chinese interference in the 2020 election expose this initiative for what it truly is: a baseless, desperate grab for power under the guise of national security. It’s a strategy that reeks of what national security contributor Marc Polymeropoulos aptly described as “bats--- authoritarianism.”

The Role of Enablers

It is imperative to scrutinize not just Trump’s authoritarian fantasies but also the enablers in positions of power who lend credibility to these dangerous ideas. Figures like Sen. Lindsey Graham and Steve Bannon, who have thrown their support behind the notion of a third Trump term, are complicit in this affront to democratic principles. Their backing raises serious concerns about the lengths to which Trump’s allies might go to subvert electoral integrity and democratic governance.

Conclusion: A Call to Defend Democracy

The discourse around a third Trump term, unconstitutional maneuvers to retain power, and the undermining of electoral integrity are not issues to be taken lightly. They represent a clear and present danger to the fabric of American democracy. As defenders of democratic values, it is our duty to remain vigilant, to call out these autocratic ambitions with the clarity and urgency they demand, and to ensure that the principles of democracy are upheld against all odds. The path forward must be guided by a steadfast commitment to constitutional norms, electoral integrity, and the unyielding defense of democratic values.
