Trump admin shoots itself in the foot by setting up Dem Epstein backfire: ex-prosecutor

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 12, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Epstein Files: A Masterclass in Obfuscation and Disappointment

Jeffrey Epstein in an undated photo released by the Department of Justice on Dec. 19, 2025. (DOJ)

The rollout of Jeffrey Epstein’s files could be headed for an unsatisfying conclusion, according to a political analyst who says people should temper their expectations.

The Department of Justice has slowly released the convicted sex offender’s documents, and, according to Politico writer Ankush Khardori, there may not be enough information in the remaining files to prosecute persons of interest. Being named or pictured in the files is not an indication of wrongdoing, and many of those identified in the files or in previous releases related to Epstein have expressly denied any wrongdoing.

Khardori wrote, “All of this is deeply unsatisfying, particularly for the women who were so horribly abused by Epstein and Maxwell.

“As other observers have noted, the desire for more legal accountability for Epstein’s sex crimes is perfectly understandable, but these are difficult cases to investigate and prosecute in the ordinary course, and the passage of time and the onslaught of political activity, political opportunism and public commentary do not help.”

Whether further details from the Epstein files can be gleaned at this point and investigated is another matter entirely, Khardori suggests, as it would mark a huge reversal for the DoJ and, by extension, Donald Trump’s administration.

Khardori wrote, “Even if the Justice Department were somehow to reverse course and charge someone new — perhaps some Democrats, as Trump has proposed — the defense would have an argument based on the department’s own prior statements that the case was not brought on the merits but was contrived in order to satisfy the public’s desire to see someone else go to prison for Epstein’s crimes.

“The argument would be particularly potent if the evidence in the case was based on information that was already in the government’s possession at the time of the release.

“Meanwhile, serious questions remain about whether the Trump administration is covering up information in the documents pertaining to the president.

“That was the focus of Washington last summer, but it has at times been overtaken by a more provocative claim — that there is a sprawling, bipartisan ‘Epstein class’ of people who, in the words of Khanna, traveled to a ‘rape island, where rich and powerful men were abusing young girls with impunity,’ and who would be revealed to the public once the documents became public.”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Masterclass in Obfuscation and Disappointment

The ongoing saga of Jeffrey Epstein’s documents and their slow trickle into the public domain is yet another episode in the never-ending series of elite impunity. According to Politico’s Ankush Khardori, expectations for a dramatic legal reckoning should be significantly lowered. This analysis, while pragmatic, underscores a fundamental flaw in our justice system: the rich and powerful often escape the consequences of their actions through a mixture of legal technicalities, political maneuvering, and, as it seems, time itself.

The Unfulfilled Promise of Justice

Khardori’s analysis brings to light a deeply unsatisfying reality, especially for the victims of Epstein and Maxwell’s horrendous crimes. The hope for more legal accountability seems to be dwindling as the challenges of investigating and prosecuting these cases become more apparent. The passage of time, coupled with political opportunism and a flood of public commentary, has muddied the waters further, complicating the path to justice.

A Twisted Irony

The suggestion that the Department of Justice (DoJ) might reverse its stance and pursue new charges under the Trump administration is laced with irony. The possibility that these actions could be dismissed as attempts to appease public outcry rather than genuine pursuits of justice is both cynical and telling of the current state of our legal system. This scenario hints at a grotesque politicization of justice, where decisions on whom to prosecute may hinge more on political calculations than on the merits of the evidence.

The Elephant in the Room: Political Cover-ups

The article also touches upon a more sinister angle: the potential cover-up by the Trump administration of information that might implicate the former president or his associates. This speculation is part of a broader, and more alarming, narrative suggesting the existence of a bipartisan “Epstein class.” This term conjures images of a shadowy network of powerful individuals who have engaged in abominable acts with impunity, shielded by their wealth, status, and political connections.

The Unsettling Reality

What’s particularly striking in Khardori’s piece is the acknowledgment of a sprawling, bipartisan network of the elite potentially involved in Epstein’s crimes. The notion of a “rape island,” where the rich and powerful abused young girls without consequence, is not just a scandal; it’s a profound indictment of societal and systemic failures at every level.

In Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

The slow and unsatisfying rollout of Epstein’s files is a stark reminder of the limitations and failures of our justice system when it comes to holding the powerful to account. It’s a story of delayed justice, political machinations, and a society that continues to grapple with the reality of an untouchable elite. As observers and critics, we must not allow the unsatisfying conclusion of the Epstein saga to dampen the demand for accountability. Instead, let it fuel a greater push for systemic reform, ensuring that no individual, regardless of their power or position, is above the law.

In an age where trust in institutions is at an all-time low, the handling of the Epstein files serves as a grim reminder of the work that remains to be done. The victims of Epstein and Maxwell deserve better. Society deserves better. It’s high time the scales of justice are balanced, not tipped in favor of those with the means to evade their reach.
