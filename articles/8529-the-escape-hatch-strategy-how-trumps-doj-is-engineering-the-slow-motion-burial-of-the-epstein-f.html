The Escape Hatch Strategy: How Trump’s DOJ is Engineering the Slow-Motion Burial of the Epstein Files

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The performance playing out in Washington would be laughable if it weren’t drenched in cynicism. The Epstein Files Transparency Act passed the House 427–1 and sailed through the Senate unanimously — a rare moment when both sides of the aisle agreed that transparency is non‑negotiable. But the moment the bill reached the doorstep of Donald Trump’s Department of Justice, we watched the same old shell game begin. The Orange Baboon and his handpicked attorney general, Pam Bondi, have been preparing their escape hatch for weeks, and the video makes that perfectly clear.

Bondi’s repeated mantra — “We will follow the law” — is the political version of a magician’s misdirection. She’s technically answering the question, but with the enthusiasm of a defense attorney who knows full well her client is guilty. Every mention of “maximum transparency” is followed immediately by the legal caveat big enough to hide a freight train: protecting victims, following procedure, respecting ongoing investigations. The DOJ could not have crafted a more convenient excuse if they had hired a Hollywood scriptwriter.

And that’s precisely the point.

When Trump floated his Truth Social edict demanding a brand‑new investigation into a list of Democrats he wants to smear — Clinton, Summers, Hoffman, and anyone else he can weaponize — Bondi didn’t just nod; she saluted. Within hours, she conjured an “ongoing investigation” out of thin air. Suddenly, the DOJ had the very pretext they needed to stall the release of the Epstein files indefinitely.

Because here’s the rub: transparency laws mean nothing if the executive branch claims it can’t release documents due to an ongoing probe. That’s the loophole. That’s the hatch. And Trump and Bondi sprinted toward it with all the grace of panicked rats.

The video shows Bondi dodging at Olympic level speed. Asked whether the new investigation would prevent the release of files, she refused to say no. Asked why the DOJ reversed its earlier position that no further investigations were warranted, she muttered something vague about “new information.” What new information? A presidential temper tantrum on Truth Social.

The DOJ’s performance reeks of coordinated obstruction. For ten straight months, Republicans dragged their feet on releasing the files. Bondi promised transparency… until she didn’t. Mike Johnson adjourned the House to avoid a vote. Trump hauled Lauren Boebert into the Situation Room to strong‑arm her out of supporting the discharge petition. This is not the behavior of people eager to let the truth breathe sunlight.

This is the behavior of people terrified of what sunlight will reveal.

The irony is thick enough to bottle. The very lawmakers who now swear they want “maximum transparency” have spent the better part of a year contorting themselves to avoid exactly that. They only flipped when the political cost of obstruction became untenable — when the margin in Congress signaled that transparency was coming whether they liked it or not.

Trump promised he’d sign the bill, but anyone paying attention understood that was nothing more than a last line of rhetorical defense. He couldn’t come out against transparency because that would be political suicide. But he could instruct his DOJ to engineer a backdoor blockade.

That’s the entire game.

Calling this predictable is an understatement. It was inevitable. Trump has always relied on delay tactics, weaponized confusion, and procedural foot‑dragging. The playbook never changes because it doesn’t have to. His base treats every obstruction as a noble act of defiance. His allies in Congress trip over themselves to justify whatever transparent nonsense he deploys. And his appointees — now including Bondi — execute his demands with a level of obsequious loyalty that would embarrass a medieval court.

But this time, the gears are grinding. Trump is cornered. His ability to stall depends on the rest of his party standing behind him, and increasingly, they aren’t. He’s facing pressure from all sides — Congress, the press, even factions within his own political machine. He’s being hit with Epstein questions everywhere: Air Force One, bilateral meetings, Oval Office briefings, press gaggles. The story is clinging to him like oil.

If he tries to stretch this new investigation into a perpetual shield, he’ll ignite a bipartisan uproar that he can’t control. And maybe that’s worth it for him. Maybe the contents of those files are so radioactive that eternal delay is preferable to truth. But the cost is rising daily.

The DOJ under Bondi has shown no good faith. They lied about files being ready, then suddenly not ready. They handed right‑wing influencers decorative binders full of already public material. They stalled on requests from the oversight committee. They offered excuses that collapse under the slightest scrutiny.

This is not the behavior of an institution acting in the public interest. This is an institution acting under orders.

And Trump? He’s doing what he always does: projecting. He calls the Epstein scandal a “Democrat hoax” despite years of photographic and testimonial evidence tying Epstein to Trump himself. He claims Democrats are weaponizing the issue even as he weaponizes it for his own vendettas.

It’s a pattern. It’s a reflex. It’s a tell.

The escape hatch may buy him time, but the clock is still ticking. Every stall exposes the desperation behind the curtain. Every dodge reveals a political apparatus scrambling to prevent the obvious conclusion: the Epstein files are a threat not just to reputations, but to power.

Trump’s crew is playing the same old game, but the board has changed. And this time, they may find that the hatch they’re so desperately trying to pry open leads straight into a furnace.
