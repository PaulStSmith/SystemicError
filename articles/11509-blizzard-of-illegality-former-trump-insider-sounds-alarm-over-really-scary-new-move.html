'Blizzard of illegality': Former Trump insider sounds alarm over 'really scary' new move

A former Donald Trump insider on Sunday blew the whistle on the president's border czar, saying Tom Homan knowingly told the media some false information.

Former Homeland Security official Miles Taylor, who has claimed his home was targeted by a break-in shortly after he published a scathing "Anonymous" op-ed about Trump's presidency, appeared on MSNBC over the weekend, where he purported to reveal Trump's true motives for ongoing power grabs.

After that, Taylor was asked about a recent comment by Homan, who said, "Trump doesn't have a limitation on his authority to make this country safe again."

When asked by the host about this comment, Taylor noted that, "There's a limitation: it's the United States Constitution."

Taylor went on to say, "And I know Tom Homan. I worked with Tom Homan. I saw Tom Homan on a weekly basis for two and a half years. And he knows that what he just said was wrong."

"And that's what's really scary here, is that you've got people who know better going to the sticks, going to the microphones with total conviction and saying exactly what this president wants to hear," he added. "And in the meantime, as you noted, the courts are ruling against him. There is a blizzard of illegality in this administration. But I say, blizzard, because you don't pay attention to each snowflake in the blizzard, right? And that's what's happening here. We're having these rulings ... against the administration. But they're flooding the zone so much that it's hard to keep up. But not just that, it's hard to make sure anyone is held accountable."

He also noted that, "If this was the Obama administration and one of Obama's lieutenants had said what Tom Homan just said, it would be a multi-month controversy, and people will have forgotten about it by tomorrow. That is the concern here."

Our Analysis:

Whistleblowing in the Eye of the Political Storm

In an era where the truth is often a casualty of political warfare, the revelations from Miles Taylor about the inner workings of the Trump administration serve as a chilling reminder of the lengths to which those in power may go to manipulate public perception. Taylor, a former Homeland Security official and the author of a scathing "Anonymous" op-ed, took to MSNBC to expose what he claims to be deliberate misinformation spread by Tom Homan, Trump's border czar.

The Constitution: A Limitation Forgotten or Ignored?

Homan's assertion that "Trump doesn't have a limitation on his authority to make this country safe again" was swiftly countered by Taylor, who rightly pointed out the glaringly obvious limitation: the United States Constitution. This exchange highlights a fundamental and frightening shift in the rhetoric emanating from the Trump administration — a belief in almost autocratic, unchecked presidential power. Taylor's intimate knowledge of Homan and his acknowledgment that Homan is aware of the constitutional limitations makes this misinformation not just a mistake, but a deliberate act of deception.

The Blizzard of Illegality

Taylor’s metaphor of a "blizzard" to describe the overwhelming wave of questionable legality within the Trump administration is as apt as it is alarming. The sheer volume of court rulings against the administration's actions creates a scenario where the public can scarcely keep track of each instance of overreach or illegality. More distressingly, this strategy seems to be by design, aimed at obfuscating the truth and avoiding accountability. The notion that the administration is "flooding the zone" with controversy and illegality to the point of public desensitization is a stark indictment of its modus operandi.

Double Standards and Short Memories

Perhaps one of the most critical points Taylor raises is the glaring double standard in public and media response to governmental overreach. He suggests that a similar statement from an Obama administration official would have resulted in a prolonged scandal. This comparison sheds light on the inconsistent standards of outrage and accountability that seem to be applied based on political affiliation rather than the objective severity of the offense.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Taylor's revelations, while not entirely surprising, serve as a necessary reminder of the importance of vigilance and accountability in an era of unprecedented political maneuvering. The audacity with which certain officials, according to Taylor, spread misinformation and test the boundaries of lawful authority is not just a challenge to the norms of democratic governance but a direct threat to the very fabric of the republic.

The question then becomes, how does one keep up with each "snowflake" in this blizzard of illegality? And more importantly, how does one ensure that those responsible are held accountable? While the answers to these questions are complex and multifaceted, one thing is clear: the role of whistleblowers like Miles Taylor, and the press in amplifying their voices, is more crucial than ever in ensuring that the truth does not become a casualty in the blizzard of political warfare.
