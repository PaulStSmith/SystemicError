There may be a surprising reason why President Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth called an in-person meeting of America’s top military brass this week, according to a former Trump-world insider.

Lev Parnas, who worked as a political operative during Trump’s first presidential campaign, wrote in a recent Substack essay that Trump and Hegseth were looking for signs of dissent during the meeting. That could help explain why there were so many obviously choreographed moments.

“After breaking fast tonight, while preparing for dinner and catching up on the flood of missed calls and messages, I heard from sources I trust,” Parnas wrote. “And what I heard stopped me cold.”

“The reason Trump and Pete Hegseth gathered all those generals in one room was not just about a loyalty speech or a pep rally,” he continued. “I’m hearing that the Trump team used artificial intelligence and facial-recognition technology during those briefings to monitor the generals’ reactions in real time.”

“Every eyebrow raise, every flicker of doubt, every moment of discomfort was scanned and analyzed by an algorithm designed to detect who would obey orders without question—and who might resist,” he continued.

Parnas added that the Trump administration appears to be using the technology on its own people as well.

“This same technology is being used as a tool inside Trump’s orbit — a quiet weapon of power, deployed by his loyalists to identify and weed out whistleblowers and anyone who isn’t completely obedient,” Parnas wrote. “This is how authoritarian power consolidates itself — by quietly monitoring every reaction, every hesitation, every private doubt, and turning it into a scorecard of allegiance.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Alleged Use of AI Against Military Brass

In an era where political maneuvers often sound like they’ve been ripped straight from a dystopian novel, the recent revelations by Lev Parnas, a former operative in Trump’s presidential campaign, add a chilling layer to the already complex tapestry of American politics. Parnas, in a Substack essay, uncovers what could be seen as a frightening misuse of technology by President Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The duo allegedly convened a meeting with America’s top military brass not as a benign gathering, but as a covert operation to scan for loyalty amidst dissent.

The Allegation: Parnas claims that the meeting wasn’t merely for pep talks but was a meticulously planned operation to use artificial intelligence (AI) and facial-recognition technology to analyze the generals’ reactions. This wasn’t about gauging the morale of the troops but about identifying who would “obey orders without question” and who might resist. It’s a scenario that sounds eerily like it’s been plucked from the pages of George Orwell’s 1984, where every gesture could be construed as dissent.

The Implications: If true, this strategy marks a disturbing pivot in how technology is utilized within the highest echelons of power. The use of AI for facial recognition to monitor loyalty is a stark testament to the lengths certain factions are willing to go to consolidate power. It transforms technology into a weapon of surveillance and control, extending the reach of authoritarian impulses into the very fabric of the military’s leadership.

The Bigger Picture: Beyond the immediate shock of such allegations, this move, as described by Parnas, underscores a broader trend of eroding democratic norms. It’s not just about the potential privacy violations or the ethical misuse of AI; it’s about the authoritarian underpinnings of such actions. When leaders resort to technologically enhanced surveillance to ensure loyalty, it signals a deep mistrust not only of the governed but of the governors themselves.

The Weaponization of Technology: The narrative that unfolds from Parnas’s essay is one where technology is not a tool for advancement but a mechanism for control. The irony is palpable — in a nation that prides itself on freedom and democracy, the very tools that could be used to protect liberty are turned against its defenders. It paints a grim picture of a future where power is maintained not through respect and mutual trust but through fear and surveillance.

The Response: As this story unfolds, one must wonder about the countermeasures that can or will be taken, both within the military and in the broader political landscape. The use of AI in this manner raises significant ethical questions, and the lack of transparency and potential for abuse calls for stringent oversight.

In Conclusion: Parnas’s revelations, if verified, are not just a cautionary tale but a red alert for the state of democracy in America. The alleged actions of Trump and Hegseth go beyond politics as usual — they venture into a realm where technology becomes a silent arbitrator of loyalty and dissent. This isn’t just about one administration or one meeting; it’s a warning about the path technology and power might take if left unchecked. In the end, the greatest defense against such dystopian scenarios is not more technology but more transparency, accountability, and a steadfast commitment to democratic principles.
