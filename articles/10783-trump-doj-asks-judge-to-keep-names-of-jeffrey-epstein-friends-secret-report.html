Trump DOJ asks judge to keep names of Jeffrey Epstein friends secret: report​

Donald Trump's administration has asked a judge to keep secret the names of individuals who had monetary transactions with disgraced financier and convicted child abuser Jeffrey Epstein, according to NBC.

Tom Winter, National Law Enforcement and Intelligence Correspondent for NBC, reported on Saturday that "the Justice Department on Friday asked a federal judge overseeing the case of deceased sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein to deny a request from NBC News to unseal the names of two associates who received large payments from him in 2018," citing court documents in the case.

According to the report, "The Justice Department cited privacy concerns expressed by the two individuals as the reason for not making their names public."

"The first associate received a payment of $100,000 from Epstein and the second associate received a payment of $250,000, both in 2018, days after the Miami Herald began publishing a series of investigative stories where victims criticized a plea deal he received in Florida in 2008," the outlet reported. "The payments became public after Epstein was indicted and arrested in New York in 2019 and asked to be released on bail."

The report goes on to describe the nature of the payments from Epstein.

"Federal prosecutors in New York filed a memorandum on July 16, 2019, that argued Epstein should remain in jail to prevent him from tampering with witnesses," NBC reported. "They cited the payments he made to the two individuals, which began two days after the Miami Herald began publishing its stories on Epstein’s plea deal, also known as a nonprosecution agreement, or NPA."

Prosecutors at the time reportedly said, "This individual is also one of the employees identified in the Indictment, which alleges that she and two other identified employees facilitated the defendant’s trafficking of minors by, among other things, contacting victims and scheduling their sexual encounters with the defendant at his residences in Manhattan and Palm Beach, Florida.”

"Prosecutors said in the filings that Epstein’s payments may be evidence of 'efforts to influence witnesses,'" the report states.

Our Analysis:

The Veil of Secrecy: Trump's DOJ and the Epstein Connection

In an era where transparency in government dealings is more of a slogan than a practice, the latest revelation about Donald Trump's administration's efforts to keep secrets is hardly surprising, yet deeply troubling. According to a report by NBC, the Justice Department, under Trump's administration, has actively sought to prevent the disclosure of individuals involved in monetary transactions with the infamous Jeffrey Epstein, a convicted child abuser and financier whose notoriety spans the globe. This move, ostensibly made to protect privacy, raises ominous questions about the nature of these transactions and the identities of those involved.

The Shadow of Epstein

Jeffrey Epstein's dark legacy is well-documented, with his connections reaching the upper echelons of global power. The request to keep the names of two associates who received substantial payments from Epstein in 2018 secret, as reported by NBC, comes off as a desperate attempt to keep the public in the dark. Why? The timing of these payments is particularly suspect, arriving days after the Miami Herald shed light on the sweetheart plea deal Epstein received in Florida in 2008, a deal that has since been critiqued for its glaring leniency given the severity of his crimes.

Privacy Concerns or Obfuscation?

The Justice Department's reasoning, citing "privacy concerns" for not disclosing these names, is a thinly veiled excuse that fails to hold water. In the context of a case as high-profile and as deeply unsettling as Epstein's, the public's right to know who might be complicit or connected to his activities surely outweighs the privacy concerns of the individuals involved. Especially when one considers that these payments were highlighted by prosecutors as potential efforts by Epstein to "influence witnesses" — a serious allegation that suggests these were not mere financial transactions but potentially part of a broader scheme to obstruct justice.

A Pattern of Protection

This isn't the first time Trump's administration has been accused of protecting those with questionable ties to Epstein. Recall that Alexander Acosta, Trump's former Secretary of Labor, was the U.S. Attorney in Miami who oversaw the 2008 plea deal allowing Epstein to serve a minimal sentence — a deal that has been widely criticized for its leniency and lack of transparency. The continuity of protection — from Acosta's plea deal to the DOJ's recent maneuvers — paints a disturbing picture of a systemic effort to shield powerful figures from the consequences of their association with Epstein.

The Bigger Picture

The real issue at hand goes beyond the specifics of these transactions or even Epstein himself. It's about a culture of secrecy and protectionism that seems to permeate the highest levels of government, where the interests of the few are placed above the pursuit of justice and transparency. When the government acts as a gatekeeper of secrets that have significant public interest implications, it undermines democracy and erodes public trust.

Conclusion

In the end, the Justice Department's request to keep these names under wraps is a stark reminder of the challenges facing those who seek to uncover the truth in the face of powerful interests. It is a call to action for journalists, activists, and the public to demand greater accountability and transparency from those in power. The connections between Epstein and influential figures must be thoroughly investigated and exposed, no matter where the evidence leads. Anything less is a disservice to the victims of Epstein's crimes and a grave threat to the principles of justice and transparency.
