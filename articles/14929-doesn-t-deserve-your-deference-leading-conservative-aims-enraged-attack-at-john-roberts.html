Iowa-based talk show host Steve Deace published an op-ed in the Conservative Review castigating U.S. Supreme Court chief Justice John Roberts for claiming the judiciary was “independent” from the other branches of government while also insisting the courts had authority to “strike down” both legislation and executive actions.

"So which is it? Are judges independent arbiters — or unaccountable gods?" wrote Deace, who had been an outspoke Donald Trump opponent during his first term.

"Roberts may not understand what 'independent' actually means," he added. "How can the judiciary call itself independent when it relies entirely on the other two branches for its power? Judges don’t appoint or confirm themselves. They don’t fund their own operations. They can’t enforce their own rulings or impose new policies. They act only through the political structures that created them."

Deace argued that the judiciary was dependent on the other branches of government for their power and were designed, as Alexander Hamilton wrote to possess “neither force nor will." He called for Republican leaders to ignore court rulings that went against them.

"Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier shows exactly what’s at stake," Deace wrote. "He’s openly defying a federal judge’s order on immigration. So why hasn’t anyone arrested him for contempt? Who would enforce the order? The U.S. Marshals? Not without Trump’s OK. Local sheriffs? Only if Gov. Ron DeSantis agrees."

The chief justice is hoping Americans don't notice the limits of the court's authority, Deace wrote, and the outspoken Covid-19 vaccine skeptic urged Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and his "Make America Healthy Again" movement to test those limits and openly defy them.

"That political will must now be exercised — boldly — against both the judiciary and the emerging fractures inside the 'Make America Healthy Again' movement," Deace wrote in his piece, titled "John Roberts doesn't deserve you deference."

"While it’s true that MAGA 2.0 wouldn’t exist without MAHA, the movement faces internal risks just as dangerous as external enemies. If MAHA lets infighting fester, it will rot from the inside — just as Anthony Fauci’s unchecked power eroded trust during COVID."

Kennedy's inner circle has been plagued by infighting during the early days of Trump's second presidency, and Deace urged them to set those differences aside and pull Covid vaccines from the market entirely and encouraged Republican lawmakers to impeach judges who stand in their way.

"Pulling the COVID shot off the market would take guts," Deace wrote. "So will getting a Republican Congress to accept its mandate from the people, rather than punting to unelected judges while cashing in on K Street."

"The next 100 days must restore order," the conservative writer added. "The path forward looks clear. What’s uncertain is whether we have the courage and conviction to walk it. Were we made to be ruled by John Roberts and Anthony Fauci? Or will we step up and govern like citizens? Yes, governing is hard. But letting medical and judicial 'experts' run our lives is far worse.

Our Analysis:

Deconstructing Deace: A MAGA Manifesto Against Judicial Independence

Steve Deace, an Iowa-based talk show host, recently penned an op-ed in the Conservative Review that cuts to the core of the MAGA movement's disdain for the judiciary's independence, a stance that is as troubling as it is misguided. Deace, who notably opposed Donald Trump in his first term, has now aligned with a radical call to erode the foundational principles of American democracy in favor of establishing unchecked Republican dominance. Let's dissect the dangerous underpinnings and outright fallacies of Deace's argument.

The Misconception of Judicial Independence

Deace's attack begins with a critique of Chief Justice John Roberts' assertion of the judiciary's independence, misconstruing it as a claim of omnipotence. "So which is it? Are judges independent arbiters — or unaccountable gods?" Deace questions, misunderstanding the very fabric of the U.S. Constitution. The judiciary's independence is not about being "unaccountable gods" but about safeguarding the rule of law from the whims of political tides. It's about ensuring that neither the executive nor the legislative branches can dictate judicial rulings, preserving a system of checks and balances that is fundamental to democratic governance.

A Dangerous Call to Arms

What is profoundly alarming in Deace's op-ed is the call for Republican leaders to simply ignore court rulings that do not align with their agenda. This is not just a challenge to judicial independence; it's a call for anarchy. By championing the defiance of legal authority — exemplified by Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier's refusal to comply with a federal judge's order — Deace is advocating for a breakdown of legal norms and protocols that keep the government's powers in balance.

The Misuse of Historical Context

Deace invokes Alexander Hamilton, suggesting the judiciary was designed to have “neither force nor will,” thereby implying it should be subservient to the other branches of government. This selective reading of Hamilton ignores the broader context of The Federalist Papers, where Hamilton argues for a judiciary that, while lacking the power of the purse or the sword, exercises judgment as its mechanism of influence. The independence of the judiciary is crucial for it to perform its role as a bulwark against tyranny — a concept Hamilton fiercely advocated for.

The False Dichotomy of Governance vs. Expertise

The op-ed further criticizes the reliance on "medical and judicial 'experts'" as being worse than governing. This presents a false dichotomy, suggesting that governance cannot coexist with expertise. The COVID-19 pandemic has underscored the importance of expert guidance in public health decisions, and to suggest that such expertise is antithetical to democratic governance is not only wrong but dangerous. The idea of pulling COVID vaccines off the market, as Deace advocates, flies in the face of scientific consensus and public health best practices, potentially endangering millions.

Conclusion: A Path to Autocracy?

Steve Deace's op-ed is more than a critique of the judiciary; it's a manifesto for MAGA's vision of America — one where checks and balances are discarded in favor of absolute power. This vision threatens the very principles of democracy and the rule of law. Governance requires a delicate balance of power, expertise, and accountability. To suggest otherwise, to call for the breaking of courts and the dismissal of judicial and expert authority, is not a step toward "restoring order" but a leap into the abyss of autocracy.

In a democracy, we are indeed governed by laws and principles, not by the whims of individuals, whether they be judges, presidents, or talk show hosts. The courage and conviction we need is not to walk the path Deace lays out, but to reject it outright and reaffirm our commitment to the principles of justice, independence, and democratic governance.
