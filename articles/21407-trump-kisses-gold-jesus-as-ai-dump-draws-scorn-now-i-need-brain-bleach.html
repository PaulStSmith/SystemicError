Trump kisses gold Jesus as AI dump draws scorn: 'Now I need brain bleach'

The State as Self-Portrait: Trump Turns Power Into Fetish

A President Performing Himself

Donald Trump spent Saturday flooding Truth Social with AI-generated images of himself kissing a golden Jesus, posing as Elvis, looming over Greenland, and recasting South America as a future “51st State.” That is the surface event. The substance is simpler: a man with institutional power using a propaganda feed to turn public office into a private hallucination.

This is not random weirdness. It is a deliberate visual regime. Trump is not communicating policy so much as staging dominion, nationalism, and religious spectacle in the same image grammar. The cross is gold. The flag is omnipresent. The man himself is the point.

Who Has Power Here

The actor with actual power is Trump. He controls the office, the megaphone, and the terms of attention. Everyone else in the story is reacting to his output after the fact. Journalists, social media users, and critics can mock the images, but mockery does not alter the basic asymmetry: Trump gets to define the spectacle because he holds the platform of state-backed authority.

That matters because political coverage often treats this kind of content as internet nonsense, a prank, or evidence of instability. That framing softens responsibility. The issue is not whether the posts are “bizarre.” The issue is that the president is using symbolic saturation to normalize domination as style.

The Misdirection Is the Message

The source article notes the scorn these posts drew, and the ridicule is easy enough to understand. But the ridicule can also become a trap. If the coverage stops at “Grandpa is hallucinating again,” it lets the deeper function slide past: these images are not accidental noise, they are messaging.

Trump’s repeated use of himself as sovereign, warrior, saint, and successor is an effort to collapse the boundary between personal vanity and state power. George Washington split in half with Trump’s face beside him is not merely self-flattery. It is an attempt to graft himself onto national origin, to make the republic look like an inheritance waiting for him to claim it. The point is not coherence. The point is occupation.

Religion, Nationalism, and Theft

The gold Jesus image is the clearest tell. Trump is not presenting faith; he is performing possession. The gold cross, the flag, the religious iconography, the sequined patriot costume: all of it reduces religion and patriotism to props in a one-man loyalty theater.

That is why the images feel so crude. They are not trying to persuade a serious audience. They are designed to reassure followers that every symbol can be emptied out and reused as branding. This is what propaganda looks like when it stops pretending to be argument and becomes pure costume. The country, the faith, the founding myth, the military pageant, even age itself are all remixed around the same figure.

The Real Story Is Institutional Cowardice

The deeper scandal is not that Trump posts absurd images. It is that the political system still allows the president to behave as though the office were a content factory for personal mythmaking. The behavior is treated as spectacle, then absorbed as background noise.

That is a failure of institutional response, not just media attention. Trump can keep posting because there is no meaningful penalty for converting presidential power into a vanity engine. The system keeps normalizing each escalation as another episode of the same personality disorder show, when what is actually being normalized is authoritarian pageantry.

The Pattern

This story is part of a larger pattern: power no longer needs to speak plainly when it can overwhelm. It can smear itself across flags, saints, founders, soldiers, and pop icons until the distinction between governance and narcissism breaks down. That is not harmless nonsense. It is how political domination gets wrapped in entertainment, religious reverence, and national symbolism before the damage becomes harder to name.

Trump is not confusing public life with private fantasy. He is showing how easily institutional power can be made to serve personal myth. The images are ridiculous. The arrangement of power behind them is not.
