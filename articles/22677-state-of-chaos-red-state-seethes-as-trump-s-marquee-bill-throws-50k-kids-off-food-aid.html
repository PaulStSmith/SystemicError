'State of chaos': Red state seethes as Trump's marquee bill throws 50K kids off food aid

Based on an article by Megan Henry, Ohio Capital Journal

Paulo SantosAugust 28, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Affordability Under Assault, Blame on the Victims

Ohio is being used as a case study in how conservative governing works: cut food, cut health care, raise costs, then talk about “affordability” as if the damage arrived by weather. The source article lays out the numbers plainly. More than 100,000 Ohioans have lost SNAP benefits, thousands more are at risk of losing Medicaid coverage, premiums have jumped, and housing is so warped that a full-time worker needs $23.82 an hour just to afford a modest two-bedroom apartment.

Power Wrote the Bill

The central fact is not confusion. It is power. Donald Trump and congressional Republicans made the policy choices that produced these losses. Ashley Bryant Bailey says it directly: these are consequences of policy choices, not some mysterious inflation force. That matters because the political system still loves to describe self-inflicted harm as if it were accidental.

This is not an economy that “got expensive” on its own. It is an economy made more brutal by people who can sign bills, set eligibility rules, and strip support from working families.

The Lie of “Making America Affordable”

Trump’s promise was affordability. The record in Ohio is the opposite: food assistance slashed, health coverage eroded, monthly marketplace premiums nearly doubled from $126 to $233, and gas prices up nearly 42% over the year. When policy makes daily life more expensive, the word “affordability” becomes propaganda.

That is the point of the language. Republicans keep the slogan and hand off the consequences. They want the branding without the bill.

Hunger, Health Care, Housing: One System

The story is not three separate crises. It is one political structure tightening around basic life. Food insecurity rises when SNAP is cut. Medical insecurity deepens when Medicaid work requirements and expired tax credits push people off coverage. Housing becomes a trap when wages do not match rents and the shortage of affordable units reaches 266,000.

Karen Brownlee’s point is sharper than the usual policy talk: when basic needs are unstable, people live in stress and chaos. That is not an abstract social mood. It is what happens when public policy treats survival as optional.

Misdirection as a Campaign Tactic

The Republican response in the article is not an argument. It is a distraction. Eric Conroy’s campaign manager accuses Greg Landsman of “fighting inflation” like an arsonist putting out a fire he set, then pivots to slogans about “opening up American energy” and “bringing back outsourced jobs.” That is standard evasive theater: change the subject, blur responsibility, dress ideology up as common sense.

This is how the right keeps economic pain politically useful. It causes the damage, then uses cheaper rhetoric to accuse Democrats of being the problem. The point is not to explain prices. The point is to immunize power from blame.

Democrats Need to Name the Culprit More Aggressively

The Democratic lawmakers in the article are identifying real harm, and they are right to focus on reversing the food and health care cuts. But the larger lesson is harsher: describing the damage is not the same as confronting the machinery that produced it. Voters do not need a softer version of austerity management. They need politicians willing to say that Republican governance is making life materially worse on purpose.

That means naming the bill, naming the party, and naming the class of choices behind it. Not “cost pressures.” Not “headwinds.” Deliberate redistribution upward.

The Pattern

This is the recurring formula of authoritarian politics in ordinary clothes: strip support from the public, transfer risk onto households, then pretend the resulting misery proves government does not work. It is a politics of engineered scarcity and rhetorical escape hatches. Ohio is not seeing a temporary affordability crisis. It is seeing what happens when elected power is used to make basic life harder, and then to lie about who did it.
