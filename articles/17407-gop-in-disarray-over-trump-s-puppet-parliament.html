Abdication of Duty: When Congress Cedes Power to the Executive

A Startling Shift in Governance

Amidst the cacophony of daily political discourse, a significant deviation in the functioning of the U.S. government under the Trump administration has unfolded with little of the scrutiny it merits. Historically, periods of unified government have been seen as golden opportunities for significant legislative achievements—from the New Deal to the Affordable Care Act. However, under President Trump, the Republican-controlled Congress has taken a starkly different path, opting to relinquish its legislative power in favor of bolstering the executive’s unilateral authority. This shift represents not merely a change in strategy but a fundamental disruption of the checks and balances that underpin American democracy.

The Power Surrendered

Instead of harnessing their majority to pass comprehensive legislation, Republican lawmakers have allowed—and at times enabled—President Trump to dictate policy through executive orders and administrative actions. This is not governance; it’s a dereliction of duty. The legislative successes that have occurred, such as the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act,” primarily serve to consolidate the President’s agenda, offering tax breaks and slashing funding to critical programs without substantive debate or opposition from Congress. This is not a partnership but a capitulation—a dangerous blurring of the lines between the legislative and executive branches.

Blocking and Tackling for the Executive

House Speaker Mike Johnson’s role in this dynamic is particularly troubling. By blocking votes on measures that challenge or even merely question Trump’s policies, Johnson is not acting as a leader of a co-equal branch of government but as a gatekeeper for the Presidency. This includes stifling attempts to force transparency regarding the Justice Department’s files on Jeffrey Epstein—a move that reeks of political protectionism rather than legislative prudence.

Internal Dissent and Broader Implications

The frustration within the ranks of the Republican party is palpable. Figures like Representative Max Miller openly criticize Johnson’s leadership, signaling a brewing storm not just over policy but over the very role and power of Congress. These internal conflicts hint at a broader issue: the potential erosion of institutional integrity and democratic norms. If Congress cannot or will not assert its authority, what checks remain on the President? This is not merely a procedural concern but a profound existential threat to the balance of power that safeguards American liberties.

A Pattern of Power Consolidation

This scenario is not occurring in a vacuum. It reflects a larger pattern of power consolidation by executive authorities seen globally. When legislative bodies weaken or willingly hand over their powers, democracies inch closer to autocracies. The implications are dire: when one branch of government accumulates too much power, the potential for abuse increases exponentially, and the mechanisms for accountability diminish.

Conclusion: A Call for Legislative Reassertion

The current trajectory of the Republican-controlled Congress under Trump is not just an anomaly; it’s a warning. If this pattern continues or, worse, becomes a template for future governance, the foundational democratic principle of checks and balances is at risk. It is incumbent upon Congress to reclaim its legislative authority and responsibility. Failure to do so not only undermines the institution’s role but also imperils the very structure of American democratic governance. The time for Congress to rectify this imbalance is not just now; it was yesterday.
