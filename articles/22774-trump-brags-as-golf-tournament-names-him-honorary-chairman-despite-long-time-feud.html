Trump brags as golf tournament names him honorary chairman despite long-time feud

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Trump Doesn’t Need the Presidency to Cash in on It

A Familiar Transaction

The reporting here is simple enough: Donald Trump says the PGA Tour asked him to serve as honorary chairman for the Presidents Cup in Chicago, and he announced the role on Truth Social just weeks before the midterms. That is the entire event. The political meaning is not the golf title. It is the access, the timing, and the normalizing of Trump as a permanent public fixture regardless of office, scandal, or schedule.

Who Actually Has Power

Trump still understands institutional gravity better than most of the media that covers him. He may be outside the formal chain of command on this tournament, but he remains the center of attention that others orbit. The PGA Tour, by contrast, is the institution doing the accommodating. It once shunned his courses for a decade after his presidential run and even moved an event from Doral to Mexico City. Now, according to Trump, its new commissioner Brian Rolapp has helped hand him a ceremonial perch.

That is not a footnote. It is the story. Institutions that once tried to draw a line around Trump are still capable of erasing it when prestige, access, or convenience beckon.

The Real Decision

The relevant decision is not whether Trump likes golf. It is whether the PGA Tour wanted to put him back in the frame. If the tour did ask him, that is a conscious choice to launder his image through a respected sports institution. If Trump is exaggerating or narrating the invitation in his own self-glorifying way, that is still the point: he can turn almost any platform into a stage for self-importance because too many organizations keep supplying the stage.

The source text hints at this with the way Trump’s post reads like a ceremonial state function. He presents a golf announcement as if he were accepting a diplomatic post. That is not accidental. He knows that prestige rubs off in both directions.

Timing Is the Point

The midterm calendar matters. This event lands at the end of September, only weeks before voters are asked to decide the balance of power in Congress. That does not make the tournament a campaign event in any formal sense. It does make it a reminder that Trump’s political brand never stops moving through elite spaces while governing continues to be treated as background noise.

The article lightly nods at this with outside commentary on the timing, but the deeper issue is not a “fun” golf appearance before election season. It is the ongoing conversion of democratic politics into a spectacle in which Trump remains the protagonist no matter what office he occupies.

Leisure as Cover

Trump’s golf obsession is not a side story. It is part of the political brand: leisure, grievance, vanity, and domination wrapped together so that indulgence looks like power. The source notes commentary that he once promised he would not be “playing much golf” if elected, and that one post claims this is already his 130th day golfing in his second term. Whether one treats that count as exact or rhetorical, the larger pattern is obvious enough: promises of discipline were always branding, not restraint.

This is how Trump operates. He turns contradiction into a feature. He can claim duty, accept honors, post photos, and stay in motion without ever being pinned to a single standard of conduct.

The System Behind the Spectacle

The weaker actors in this story are not the athletes or the fans. They are the institutions that keep mistaking Trump’s appetite for relevance as inevitability. That is the misdirection. The public is invited to see a quirky golf announcement. What’s actually being displayed is institutional surrender to a man who never stopped converting attention into authority.

The larger pattern is not about golf at all. It is about how elite organizations rehabilitate power once they think it is safe, useful, or unavoidable. Trump thrives because too many institutions would rather host him than reject him.
