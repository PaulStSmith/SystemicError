Republicans seethe at GOP governor accused of breaking deal with Trump in key Senate race

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 26, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republicans in Georgia are turning on Gov. Brian Kemp, accusing him of reneging on an agreement with President Donald Trump to reach a consensus on a GOP Senate nominee — and blowing up the party’s strategy to try to pick up a seat from Democrats.

This comes after Republicans were already disappointed in Kemp for not running for Senate himself, as party insiders considered him their best shot to defeat Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff.

According to Reese Gorman of NOTUS, “After Kemp decided last year against a Senate run, he ventured out to Trump National Golf Club in Virginia for a meeting with President Donald Trump. The two reached an agreement to work together — along with the National Republican Senatorial Committee and the Senate Leadership Fund — and find the right candidate to take on Ossoff the following November, as NOTUS previously reported.”

However, they never reached an agreement, and Kemp went on to endorse former Tennessee football coach Derek Dooley for the race, who is currently polling third — vastly increasing the odds the GOP primary will advance to a costly runoff, and infuriating Republicans who think Kemp went off and did his own thing rather than work in the best interests of the party.

The other two leaders in the race include U.S. Reps. Mike Collins and Buddy Carter.

“When Dooley met with the White House and Senate Republicans’ campaign arms about his run, he left a favorable impression on those he met with, sources familiar with the meetings told NOTUS. But, because Kemp pushed Dooley without the express backing of Trump, it didn’t do Dooley any favors,” said the report. One Georgia GOP official explained, “You don’t get to go past the Trump team and then turn around and come back to them and say, ‘Hey, can I have your endorsement now?’ Right? It was a strategic mistake.”

This comes at the same time as some of the other candidates in the race deal with their own issues, with Mike Collins potentially facing subpoenas for allegations of illegal behavior by a congressional staffer.

Our Analysis:

The Misplaced Blame in Georgia’s GOP Senate Nominee Drama

In the unfolding drama of Georgia’s GOP Senate nominee selection, the narrative has quickly turned into a saga of betrayal and infighting, with Governor Brian Kemp at the epicenter. However, a closer examination reveals a more nuanced power dynamic and decision-making process than the surface-level blame game suggests.

Who Holds the Power?

At first glance, the story paints Governor Kemp as the primary architect of the GOP’s predicament. Yet, this overlooks the broader institutional structure within which these decisions are made. The key players include:

  • Governor Brian Kemp, with substantial influence in Georgia’s political landscape.

  • President Donald Trump, whose endorsement has proven pivotal in GOP primaries.

  • The National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) and the Senate Leadership Fund, both of which play significant roles in shaping and supporting GOP Senate campaigns.

Decision-Making Dynamics

The crux of the controversy revolves around Kemp’s endorsement of Derek Dooley, a decision that allegedly contradicted an agreement with Trump and other GOP entities to collaboratively select a nominee. This decision, however, cannot be viewed in isolation:

  • Kemp made a strategic choice, likely based on his assessment of Dooley’s potential as a candidate.

  • Trump and GOP committees had envisioned a unified approach to selecting a nominee, seeking to consolidate support and resources.

Misdirected Responsibility?

The narrative suggests Kemp single-handedly derailed the GOP’s strategy. However, this simplification misses the complexity of political maneuvering and shared responsibility:

  • Institutional Power: While Kemp wields significant influence, the process of selecting and endorsing candidates is inherently collaborative, involving negotiations among various party leaders and entities.

  • Decision Outcome: Kemp’s endorsement of Dooley, despite the lack of consensus, was a move within his rights as governor, though politically contentious.

  • Framing Misdirection: Blaming Kemp alone for the GOP’s internal strife and the potential for a costly runoff overlooks the shared responsibility and the inherent unpredictability of political endorsements.

Conclusion: Beyond Scapegoating

The situation in Georgia is emblematic of the challenges facing the GOP as it navigates internal divisions and the influence of Trump’s endorsements. While Kemp’s decision to endorse Dooley without consensus has undoubtedly stirred discontent, it is a symptom of broader dynamics at play within the party, including the struggle for control and influence.

False Equivalence: Equating Kemp’s endorsement with sabotaging the party’s chances simplifies the intricate dance of political strategy and alliance-building.

Lazy Narratives: Casting Kemp as the villain overlooks the collective decision-making processes and the various power centers within the GOP.

Convenient Scapegoats: Blaming Kemp alone absolves other party leaders and entities of their roles in the nomination process and the ensuing fallout.

In sum, the real story behind Georgia’s GOP Senate nominee drama is not one of betrayal by a lone actor but a tale of strategic miscalculations, conflicting interests, and the complex interplay of power within party politics. The focus should shift from individual blame to a more profound reflection on the mechanisms of consensus-building and strategy formulation within the GOP.
