Trump's 'rogue actor' diplomat leaves US officials in the dark on Putin deals: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 30, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Russian President Vladimir Putin shows the way to U.S. President Donald Trump’s envoy Steve Witkoff during a meeting in Moscow, Russia, April 25, 2025. Sputnik/Kristina Kormilitsyna/Pool via REUTERS

Donald Trump's choice as his lead diplomat entrusted with dealing with Russian President Vladimir Putin, when he is not taking part in negotiations to end the war in Gaza, is getting poor marks with European allies as well as U.S. officials.

According to a report from Politico’s Felicia Schwartz, real estate investor-turned-international negotiator Steve Witkoff is leaving a trail of frustration and fury due to his secretive meetings with Putin, where he has failed to avail himself of the wealth of experts with experience in Russian negotiations and has refused to share the details being discussed.

As the report notes, under Witkoff, “promised follow-up meetings between Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy have not materialized, ceasefire demands were dropped, threats of tough action have disappeared,” and the White House seems directionless.

According to one U.S. official, “His inexperience shines through, he has the president’s ear, which is evident, but there has been some confusion about what has been said and agreed.”

“He’s kind of a rogue actor,” another administration official admitted. “He talks to all these people, but no one knows what he says in any of these meetings. He will say things publicly but then he changes his mind. It’s hard to operationalize that.”

The report points out that his U.S. is “sparsely staffed” and that he has refused help in understanding the differences between Russia and Ukraine from experts both inside and outside the government. Politico is reporting, “His staff, to the extent he has any, often doesn’t know where he is or what he is doing, according to four people familiar with the dynamics of the office. They said he spends most of his time at his office in the White House, while the rest of his team is at the State Department.”

That led one diplomatic expert to complain, “The thing is, Witkoff isn’t consistently engaged. He will pop in for a visit to Vladimir Putin, say a bunch of stuff, not tell anyone what really happened and then just f--- off to his life again. Meanwhile, the Russians are talking to you about how 'Witkoff says…’ and you don’t know whether they’re right or not, but you can’t get a readout from the Russians.”

The report adds, “Witkoff at times appears to struggle to focus on more than one task at a time, the U.S. official and another one of the people said, adding that when a flurry of developments occurs in one portfolio, say Gaza or Iran or Ukraine, other priorities take a backseat. He is not a voracious consumer of his intelligence briefing materials and doesn’t read them every day, the second person said.”

Our Analysis:

The Witkoff Wobble: Trump's Diplomatic Disaster

In an era where the art of diplomacy is more critical than ever, the appointment of Steve Witkoff as Donald Trump's lead diplomat for dealings with Vladimir Putin and the Gaza conflict is not just a curious choice but a blatantly reckless gamble. The recent report from Politico's Felicia Schwartz paints a picture so grim it borders on the farcical, were the stakes not so catastrophically high.

Witkoff, a real estate investor with no apparent background in diplomacy, let alone in the intricately volatile web of international relations, has been leaving a "trail of frustration and fury" across both European allies and U.S. officials. Secretive meetings with Putin, a refusal to consult with experts, and a lack of transparency about the discussions have all but characterized his tenure. This approach to diplomacy is not just unorthodox; it's dangerously naive.

The consequences of Witkoff's inexperience and secretive nature are not trivial. Promised follow-up meetings between Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy have vanished into thin air. Ceasefire demands and threats of tough action have dissipated like morning mist. The White House, under his guidance, appears to be as directionless as a ship without a compass in a stormy sea.

Officials have described Witkoff as a "rogue actor" and highlighted his inexperience. The critique that "His staff, to the extent he has any, often doesn’t know where he is or what he is doing" speaks volumes about the disarray and disconnect within his office. This isn't just about a lack of experience; it's about a fail at basic principles of leadership and management.

Moreover, the fact that Witkoff "is not a voracious consumer of his intelligence briefing materials and doesn’t read them every day" is alarming. In the complex world of international relations, where nuances matter and the devil often lies in the details, such negligence is not just irresponsible; it's potentially dangerous.

The critique from a diplomatic expert that Witkoff "will pop in for a visit to Vladimir Putin, say a bunch of stuff, not tell anyone what really happened and then just f--- off to his life again" encapsulates the absurdity of the situation. Diplomacy, especially in dealing with figures like Putin and in regions as volatile as Gaza, requires consistent engagement, strategic planning, and most importantly, a deep understanding of the geopolitical landscape.

Conclusion: A Call for Responsibility

The Politico report is not just an indictment of Steve Witkoff's capabilities as a diplomat; it's a glaring spotlight on the flawed decision-making process that led to his appointment. Entrusting critical diplomatic negotiations to an individual with no relevant experience or expertise is a gamble that the U.S. and its allies can ill afford.

The role of a diplomat, especially in negotiations involving major global powers and conflict zones, cannot be understated. It requires a blend of expertise, strategic thinking, and the ability to engage with and understand multiple perspectives. The complexity of modern international relations demands more than what Witkoff, as depicted in the report, can offer.

In a world fraught with geopolitical tensions, the stakes are too high for such gambles. The Trump administration’s approach, as reflected in this appointment, is a systemic error that undermines the very foundations of effective diplomacy. For the sake of global stability and security, one can only hope for a swift correction of this egregious misstep.
