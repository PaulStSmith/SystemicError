'I was a little surprised': Another Republican is leapfrogging Vance for 2028 nomination

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Behind the Scenes of GOP Maneuvering: Vance vs. Rubio for Trump's Successor

U.S. Vice President JD Vance stands in the House Chamber prior to U.S. President Donald Trump’s speech to a joint session of Congress at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 4, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

In a report on Republican lawmakers maneuvering behind the scenes to be the person who replaces Donald Trump as the 2028 presidential candidate –– providing the president agrees to step aside gracefully –– a CNN reporter specializing in campaign coverage was surprised to find the Vice President J.D. Vance is not a prohibitive favorite.

In an interview with CNN's Zachary B. Wolf, correspondent Eric Bradner said Vance "is obviously positioned as Trump’s understudy," but that he also not a lock to be the face of the part three and half years from now.

As he explained to Wolf, Secretary of State Marco Rubio has a legion of admirers among party activists.

"I was a little surprised during a recent visit to Iowa how frequently the name of Secretary of State Marco Rubio came up, often in the same breath as JD Vance," he recalled. "Both of them, despite their own very public criticism of Trump in the past, now seem to be viewed as team players; as closely aligned with Trump and with his current administration, obviously, as leading members of it."

He added that Rubio has certain advantages over Trump's veep.

"There’s interest in Rubio in part because he has run for president before, unlike Vance. A lot of people in the early voting states remember Rubio visiting them in 2016, when he finished third in Iowa in what were pretty competitive caucuses. So a lot of these early-state Republican voters have met Rubio before," he elaborated before saying of Vance who is still new on the scene as a GOP lawmaker, "They like Vance, but they don’t know him yet. They haven’t had a chance to go through the usual process with him."

He added, "He obviously starts with an advantage as Trump’s legacy, but based on the conversations I’ve had, it doesn’t appear to be a lock."

Our Analysis:

Behind the Scenes of GOP Maneuvering: Vance vs. Rubio for Trump's Successor

In the latest report revealing the undercurrents of the Republican party's quest for the 2028 presidential ticket, we're treated to a fascinating, albeit predictable, political drama. It appears that the GOP's backstage is crowded with figures positioning themselves to inherit Donald Trump's mantle, with Vice President J.D. Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio emerging as front-runners. However, the dynamics are anything but straightforward.

Vance: The Understudy Without a Guarantee

J.D. Vance, described as Trump’s "understudy," finds himself in a peculiar position. Despite being in the immediate orbit of Trump's power, his ascendancy to the role of the GOP's presidential nominee is far from assured. This revelation shouldn't surprise those who have followed Vance’s trajectory. His transition from a Trump critic to a Trump loyalist encapsulates the acrobatics many Republicans have performed in the Trump era, yet it seems insufficient to secure him the party's unequivocal backing for the presidency.

Rubio: The Familiar Face with an Edge

Enter Marco Rubio, the Secretary of State with a "legion of admirers" and a history of presidential ambition. Rubio's advantage in this political chess game is twofold: his prior run for president in 2016, which granted him visibility and familiarity among early-state Republican voters, and his perceived alignment with Trump's administration despite previous criticisms. The mention of Rubio's name alongside Vance's in discussions among Iowa Republicans highlights a significant aspect of presidential politics—memory and familiarity matter.

The Trump Factor: A Blessing or a Curse?

Both Vance and Rubio's closeness to Trump is painted as an asset in their quest to become the GOP's next presidential nominee. However, this affiliation raises critical questions about the future direction of the Republican Party. Aligning with Trump has become a litmus test for many within the GOP, yet it's a double-edged sword that can alienate as much as it endears.

The GOP's Identity Crisis

This behind-the-scenes jockeying for position underscores a broader identity crisis within the Republican Party. The struggle to find a successor to Trump who can unite the party's factions and appeal to a broader electorate is evident. The fact that Vance, despite his direct line to Trump, is not a shoo-in for the nomination speaks volumes about the party's uncertainty about its own future and the type of leader it seeks post-Trump.

Conclusion: The Road to 2028 Is Unpaved

As the GOP navigates its internal dynamics and external pressures, the road to the 2028 presidential nomination remains unpaved. Vance and Rubio's positioning as potential heirs to Trump's legacy is a narrative filled with irony, ambition, and a dash of unpredictability. Whether the party will opt for Vance's direct inheritance of Trump's mantle or Rubio's blend of past ambition and present alignment with Trump's administration remains an open question.

In the end, the GOP's choice will signal not just a personal victory for the nominee but a definitive statement about the party's identity and direction in the post-Trump era. For now, all we can do is watch as the drama unfolds, keeping in mind that in politics, as in life, there are no guarantees—only calculated gambles and the relentless pursuit of power.
