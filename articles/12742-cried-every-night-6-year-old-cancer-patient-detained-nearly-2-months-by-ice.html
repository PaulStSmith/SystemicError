'Cried every night': 6-year-old cancer patient detained nearly 2 months by ICE

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Inconvenient Truth: A Reflection on Immigration Policies and Human Rights

Protesters opposing possible Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) raids in public schools demonstrate on the steps of the State Department of Education during their monthly board meeting in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma, U.S. January 28, 2025. REUTERS/Nick Oxford

A 6-year-old Honduran boy battling leukemia was detained — along with his family — by President Donald Trump's ICE agents despite following every immigration rule, the boy's lawyer told Salon.

The family's nightmare began when they were seized by plainclothes ICE agents after a court hearing in May.

"The boy and his 9-year-old sister cried every night in detention," attorney Elora Mukherjee told Salon. The government pursued expedited removal while the cancer patient suffered in a Texas detention facility that Biden had shuttered but Trump reopened.

"The Trump administration's policy of detaining people at courthouses who are doing everything right, who are entirely law-abiding, who are trying to fulfill all the requirements that the U.S. government asks of them — it violates our Constitution, it violates our federal laws," Mukherjee said. "It also violates our sense of morality."

The family had fled Honduras after receiving death threats, applied for asylum through proper channels, and waited for permission to enter using a CBP appointment. They never crossed the border illegally, the lawyer said.

"So this particular family did everything right," Mukherjee emphasized.

During their month-long detention at the Dilley Immigration Processing Center, the boy experienced leukemia symptoms including easy bruising and bone pain. He missed a crucial June 5 cancer appointment. His sister barely ate.

Jeff Migliozzi from Freedom for Immigrants blasted Trump's "aggressive quota of 3,000 daily immigration arrests — a policy pushed by hardliners in the White House like known white nationalist Stephen Miller — is terrorizing communities."

The administration's "bait-and-switch tactics" increasingly target people at scheduled check-ins and courthouses, Migliozzi said. "Here you have people doing everything they can to follow the instructions given to them, and then the rug is pulled out from under them."

The family was released July 2 after public pressure and media coverage, but only after enduring traumatic detention that Mukherjee said "clearly violates both the Fourth Amendment and the Fifth Amendment."

"High-level officials in the Department of Homeland Security constantly say that we are targeting the 'worst of the worst,'" Mukherjee noted. "These are the people who are doing everything right."

Our Analysis:

The Inconvenient Truth: A Reflection on Immigration Policies and Human Rights

In an era where political rhetoric often clouds the harsh realities faced by individuals on the ground, the story of a 6-year-old Honduran boy battling leukemia while being detained by ICE agents under President Donald Trump's administration serves as a stark reminder of the human cost of stringent immigration policies. This incident, as reported by Salon, underscores not just a failure of policy but a profound moral crisis at the heart of America's immigration enforcement strategy.

The Facts: A Family's Nightmare

The boy, alongside his family, was detained despite following every immigration rule laid out by the U.S. government. Their detention began after a court hearing in May, where they were seized by plainclothes ICE agents, marking the start of a month-long nightmare. According to their attorney, Elora Mukherjee, the family had fled Honduras due to death threats, applied for asylum through the correct channels, and waited for permission to enter the U.S. legally. They never crossed the border illegally. Yet, they found themselves in the Dilley Immigration Processing Center in Texas—a facility previously shut down by the Biden administration but reopened by Trump. Here, the young boy's health deteriorated as he missed a crucial cancer treatment appointment.

Violation of Constitutional and Moral Principles

Mukherjee's assertion that the Trump administration's policy of detaining people at courthouses, who are doing everything right, "violates our Constitution, it violates our federal laws," and "it violates our sense of morality," rings with a truth that is hard to ignore. It is a chilling indictment of a system that purports to target the "worst of the worst" yet ensnares a vulnerable child and his family who were doing everything within their power to comply with U.S. immigration laws.

The Role of Public Pressure and Media Coverage

The family's release on July 2, after enduring traumatic detention, came after public pressure and media coverage. This outcome highlights the power and necessity of vigilance and advocacy in holding government policies to account. However, it also raises critical questions about the countless others who remain voiceless and faceless within the system—those who endure without the spotlight of media attention or the support of public outcry.

A Critique of Aggressive Enforcement Tactics

Jeff Migliozzi from Freedom for Immigrants condemned the Trump administration's aggressive quota of 3,000 daily immigration arrests as terrorizing communities. This approach, fueled by hardliners such as Stephen Miller, represents a bait-and-switch tactic that undermines the very principles of justice and fairness. The targeting of individuals at scheduled check-ins and courthouses, who are attempting to follow legal procedures, is a betrayal of trust and a clear abuse of power.

Conclusion: A Call for Compassion and Reform

The detention of a young boy battling leukemia and his family is not just a policy failure; it is a moral failing. It serves as a reminder of the urgent need for immigration reform that prioritizes human dignity and compassion over aggressive enforcement. As a nation, we must ask ourselves whether we will continue to allow such injustices to occur in our name or whether we will demand a system that truly reflects the values of justice, fairness, and humanity. The choice is ours, and the time for action is now.
