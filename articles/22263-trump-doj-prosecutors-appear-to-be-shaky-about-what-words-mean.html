Trump DOJ prosecutors 'appear to be shaky' about what words mean

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

The Justice Department Is Acting Like a Grievance Machine

Trump Holds the Levers

The source is about a Justice Department filing meant to “salvage” Donald Trump’s case against James Comey over a social-media post that read “86-47.” But the real center of gravity is not Comey’s post. It is Trump’s power over the machinery of prosecution and his public habit of turning personal grudges into state action.

This is not a dispute between equal actors. Comey is the target. Trump is the one with the institutional reach to demand action, publicly pressure officials, and convert his obsession into a criminal case.

The Filing Admits the Relationship

The defense says the indictment is the product of a “years-long campaign” by Trump to use criminal process against Comey for protected speech and personal animus. The prosecution’s answer does not dispel that picture; it deepens it. The DOJ is arguing that Comey has not shown “actual malice” in a way that supposedly defeats the defense claim.

That is the wrong frame to hide inside. The question is not whether Trump’s enemies can produce a courtroom-ready folder labeled “malice.” The question is whether Trump’s repeated public denunciations, including calling Comey a “corrupt piece of garbage” and demanding that he be prosecuted, make the prosecution look like retaliation rather than law enforcement.

What “Actual Malice” Is Doing Here

The source correctly notes that “actual malice” is a legal term with a legal definition: knowledge that a statement is false or reckless disregard for whether it is true. That matters because the DOJ’s motion, as described here, appears to blur legal language into political theater.

If prosecutors cannot distinguish the relevant standard from a partisan talking point, then the filing is not a serious answer to vindictive-prosecution claims. It is cover. It turns legal jargon into a smokescreen for a case that, by the defense’s account, exists because Trump wanted revenge and got federal lawyers to help manufacture it.

The Real Decision Makers

The article’s most revealing detail is Trump’s September 2025 Truth Social post to then-Attorney General Pamela Bondi: “Pam, Nothing is being done. What about Comey, Adam ‘Shifty’ Schiff, Leticia [James]??? ... JUSTICE MUST BE SERVED, NOW!” That is not subtlety. That is direct pressure on the attorney general to move against named political targets.

Whatever procedural decisions followed, the enabling structure is obvious. Trump sets the demand, the Justice Department supplies the legal costume, and the victim is told to argue in court that the prosecution is not “good-faith application of the law to the facts.” That is what weaponized prosecutorial power looks like when it stops pretending to be neutral.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The source spends time on whether Comey’s seashell post could be read as threatening Trump’s assassination. That is useful context, but it can also function as a decoy. It invites readers to focus on the weakest, most ambiguous element in the story while the stronger fact pattern sits in plain view: Trump’s long record of venom toward Comey and his explicit demand that the Justice Department act.

That is the move. Inflate the target’s alleged offense, then bury the stronger evidence of retaliatory motive under procedural noise. It is a classic power tactic: make the subordinate look dangerous, make the powerful look merely aggrieved, and call the whole thing “law.”

What This Story Really Shows

If this case collapses under its own vindictiveness, that will not be because Trump accidentally said the quiet part loud. It will be because he said exactly what he meant: he wanted punishment, and he wanted federal power to deliver it.

The deeper pattern is the normalization of personal rule through legal institutions. The department is not merely interpreting law here; it is being used to launder vengeance into procedure. That is the institutional cowardice at the heart of the story.
