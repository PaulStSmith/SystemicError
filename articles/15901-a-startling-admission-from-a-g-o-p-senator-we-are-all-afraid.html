A Startling Admission From a G.O.P. Senator: ‘We Are All Afraid’

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Senator Lisa Murkowski, Republican of Alaska, in the Capitol earlier this year.Credit...Haiyun Jiang for The New York Times

Senator Lisa Murkowski, the moderate Alaska Republican who has routinely broken with her party to criticize President Trump, has made a startling admission about the reality of serving in public office at a time when an unbound leader in the Oval Office is bent on retribution against his political foes.

“We are all afraid,” Ms. Murkowski said, speaking at a conference in Anchorage on Monday. After pausing for about five seconds, she acknowledged: “It’s quite a statement. But we are in a time and a place where I certainly have not been here before. I’ll tell you, I’m oftentimes very anxious myself about using my voice, because retaliation is real. And that’s not right.”

Ms. Murkowski’s comments were first reported by The Anchorage Daily News.

Ms. Murkowski, an independent voice in an increasingly tribal party, has been the rare Republican on Capitol Hill willing to criticize the Trump administration. After Mr. Trump in February berated President Volodymyr Zelensky of Ukraine in the Oval Office, she warned that the United States was “walking away from our allies.”

She has routinely criticized the Department of Government Efficiency for creating a “tremendous amount of unnecessary anxiety” and for firing federal workers in her state. And Ms. Murkowski was one of only three Republicans who voted not to confirm Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth.

Ms. Murkowski has become more outspoken in Mr. Trump’s second term, but she has long been part of a small minority in her party willing to stand up to its leader at a time when most G.O.P. lawmakers have chosen to fall in line. She was one of seven Republicans who voted in 2021 to convict Mr. Trump in his second impeachment trial, and the only one up for re-election at the time. She was also one of three Republicans who voted to support Judge Ketanji Brown Jackson’s confirmation to the Supreme Court.

Ms. Murkowski has been upfront about her discomfort in a party led by Mr. Trump, whom she said she refused to vote for last year; Ms. Murkowski endorsed former Gov. Nikki Haley of South Carolina for president. At the time, Ms. Murkowski even considered leaving the party altogether, although she has not done so.

“I just regret that our party is seemingly becoming a party of Donald Trump,” she told CNN last year. When asked if she would leave it and become an independent, she said that she was “navigating my way through some very interesting political times. Let’s just leave it at that.”

On Monday in Anchorage, Ms. Murkowski said she did not plan to stop speaking out, even if she was frightened of retaliation. “That’s what you’ve asked me to do,” she said, referring to her constituents. “I’m going to use my voice to the best of my ability.”

She added: “I have to figure out how I can do my best to help the many who are so anxious and are so afraid.”

Ms. Murkowski, a fourth-term senator, is up for re-election in 2028.

Our Analysis:

‘We Are All Afraid’: The GOP’s Public Confession of Cowardice

“We are all afraid.” — Senator Lisa Murkowski

When a sitting U.S. Senator openly admits that fear is driving her party, the story is no longer about politics — it’s about submission. Senator Lisa Murkowski’s remarks in Anchorage weren’t bold. They were bleak. They were a whispered cry from inside a party that’s been eaten alive by the man it was too afraid to stand up to.

This wasn’t an offhand remark. She paused — five full seconds — before saying it again. “We are all afraid.”

Afraid of what? Afraid of Donald Trump.

This is no longer the Grand Old Party. It’s the Gutted Out Party, where conviction goes to die and cowards go to hide.

The GOP: Hostages in Their Own Chamber

Let’s be very clear: Murkowski wasn’t describing a culture war or ideological divide. She was describing a climate of fear. Fear of political retaliation. Fear of threats. Fear of primary challengers, MAGA mobs, and maybe even violence.

That’s not democracy. That’s hostage governance.

The Republican Party is no longer a political party — it’s a franchise of fear, run by a man who’s made retribution the centerpiece of his platform. And the senators? They’re not leaders. They’re accomplices in their own intimidation.

The Theater of Resistance

Murkowski has voted against Trump before. She opposed his second impeachment. She supported Judge Ketanji Brown Jackson. She says she didn’t vote for him in 2024 and backed Nikki Haley instead.

But let’s not overstate her courage. This is not some great act of rebellion. It’s the minimum, dressed in moral ambiguity. Murkowski “considered leaving the party.” Considered. She speaks of anxiety. Of fear. Of knowing what’s right and struggling to act.

We don’t need senators to narrate their fear. We need them to legislate against it.

The Lesson of ‘We Are All Afraid’

When the people with the most power, most insulation, and most resources are too scared to speak, what happens to everyone else?

What happens to immigrants, to LGBTQ+ kids, to teachers, librarians, scientists, journalists, and voters when even senators feel like they’re one sentence away from political execution?

And worse — what happens when this fear becomes the new normal?

This Is Not Leadership. This Is a Warning.

Murkowski’s confession should shake the nation. It’s not just a quote. It’s evidence.

The Republican Party is no longer silent because of strategy. It’s silent because it is afraid of its own voters, its own media machine, and most of all, its de facto leader — a man who promises vengeance, not governance.

Lisa Murkowski didn’t give us bravery. She gave us proof.

And the rest of us? We should be afraid too. Not because fear is normal — but because in this political moment, truth is dangerous, and silence is rewarded.

And that is how democracies die.
