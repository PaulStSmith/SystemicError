The Men Who Mistake Power for Grammar

A Vice President Who Treats Lying as Policy

JD Vance says the U.S. “wins either way” in Iran negotiations, even as Iran answers with drone attacks on Bahrain and strikes in the Strait of Hormuz. He also insists Iran’s enrichment ability has been “destroyed,” despite the source noting that Iran still holds 60 percent-enriched uranium. The pattern is familiar: declare victory first, let facts catch up if they can.

This is not confusion. It is an exercise in political domination through false certainty.

The Real Power Is Not the Performance

Vance is not a commentator on the sidelines. He is the sitting vice president, a future presidential contender, and a man already being groomed for the next stage of the right-wing project. The article makes the core structure plain: billionaire Peter Thiel funded Vance, shaped his rise, and treated him as a “generational bet.” That is where the actual leverage sits.

Vance is the public face. Thiel is part of the machinery behind it. The point is not just to win elections. It is to build a political order in which wealth can appoint its enforcers.

Misdirection as Method

The article is right to call out Vance’s habit of manufacturing moral panic and then retreating into irony when caught. Haitian immigrants eating pets. Europeans supposedly inviting murder by “self-hatred.” Democrats reduced to “childless cat ladies.” Nixon recast as a victim of the “deep state.”

This is not random trolling. It is a system of blame transfer. Strong actors create a story in which weaker people, immigrants, liberals, bureaucrats, and “the media” become the visible target while the real architects of damage stay offstage. The lie is not a bug. It is the cover.

Nixon, Rebranded for the Grift Age

Vance’s praise for Nixon is revealing because it strips away the usual pretense. He does not admire restraint or statesmanship. He admires the idea that accountability is a temporary obstacle to be mocked after the fact. His claim that Watergate would be “a 12-hour news story” only makes sense in a political culture already saturated with corruption so brazen that scandal no longer functions as punishment.

That is the regression here: from scandal to impunity, from impunity to doctrine.

The Endgame Is Administrative Capture

The source quotes Vance outlining the real program: replace midlevel bureaucrats and civil servants with “our people,” then dare the courts to enforce their rulings. That is not populism. It is a blueprint for seizure.

It also exposes the fantasy at the center of this movement. The goal is not a leaner state or freer citizens. It is a state that serves patrons, punishes enemies, and removes the friction that democracy imposes on concentrated wealth. “Freedom,” in this arrangement, means freedom for oligarchs to rule without interruption.

What This Story Actually Shows

The article’s larger point is simple: Vance is dangerous not because he is loud, but because he is disciplined enough to make authoritarian ambition sound respectable. He packages bigotry, elite capture, and institutional sabotage in a Yale-inflected tone of seriousness. That makes the con easier to sell.

The pattern is older than Vance. Rich men fund the candidate, the candidate lies for the crowd, and the crowd is instructed to blame the wrong enemies. By the time the damage is visible, the people with power have already renamed it as strategy.
