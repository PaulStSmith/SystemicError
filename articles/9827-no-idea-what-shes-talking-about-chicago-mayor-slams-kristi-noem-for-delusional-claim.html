‘No idea what she’s talking about!’ Chicago mayor slams Kristi Noem for ‘delusional' claim

By Paulo Santos

Screengrab / MSNBC

Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson fired off at Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem Saturday in response to what he called her “delusional” claims that protesters in Illinois’ most-populated city were threatening to harm federal agents and their families.

On Friday, Kristi Noem was filmed in Chicago prepping federal agents for President Donald Trump’s crackdown on Chicago, telling them “we’re going to hammer these guys that are advocating for violence against the American people!” She went on to allege Chicago protesters of advocating to harm federal agents and their families, dox them online, and claimed that they “[victimize] people every day by the way that they’re talking.”

Johnson, however, dismissed Noem’s claims out of hand, and alleged Noem of having “no idea what she’s talking about” while appearing on MSNBC.

“First of all, she has no idea what she’s talking about,” Johnson said. “The Trump administration, they are completely delusional right now, and what they are promulgating in this particular moment are not just unconstitutional measures, but they’re dangerous and reckless. It’s antithetical to who we are as a nation… This president has worked hard to relitigate the Civil War, and we’re not going to stand for it!”

Johnson said that he’s received no communication whatsoever from the Trump administration regarding its deployment of federal officers and National Guard members to the city, a deployment he said he ordered to crackdown on crime, and over the objections of Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker.

But for Johnson, he argued that the deployment wasn’t about cracking down on crime, but rather, about “profit.”

“This not about deportation, this is not about community safety, this is, quite frankly, about for profit,” Johnson said, who highlighted the alleged acceptance of a $50,000 bribe by Tom Homan – Trump’s border czar – in exchange for a promise to award government contracts, potentially for private prisons seeking to secure lucrative contracts with Immigrations Customs and Enforcement.

“The President of the United States of America has declared war on poor people, and if there was ever a time for working people to stand up across this world, the time is now!” Johnson said.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Chicago Mayor’s Response to Homeland Security Secretary’s Claims

In a heated exchange that underscores the escalating tensions in Chicago, Mayor Brandon Johnson vehemently countered Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem’s allegations regarding protesters in the city. Noem, aligning with President Donald Trump’s aggressive stance, accused Chicago protesters of threatening federal agents and their families, an assertion Mayor Johnson branded as “delusional.”

The Allegations: Fearmongering or Reality?

Secretary Noem’s comments, made amidst preparations for a federal crackdown in Chicago, paint a picture of a city besieged by violence against government officials. Her claim that protesters are advocating for harm against federal agents and their families, and engaging in doxxing, is a serious one. However, without tangible evidence to back these assertions, they risk being perceived as fearmongering tactics designed to justify the deployment of federal forces.

Johnson’s Rebuttal: A Denunciation of Federal Intervention

Mayor Johnson’s response to Noem’s claims was swift and scathing. By dismissing the allegations outright, Johnson positions the Trump administration’s narrative as not just flawed, but “completely delusional.” His criticism extends beyond mere disagreement, framing the administration’s actions as “unconstitutional,” “dangerous,” and “reckless.” This language underscores a profound concern for the implications of federal intervention under the guise of law enforcement.

Moreover, Johnson accuses the administration of attempting to “relitigate the Civil War,” a statement that invokes historical tensions and suggests a deep-seated ideological conflict at play. This rhetoric elevates the debate from a policy disagreement to a clash over the very identity and values of the nation.

The Motive: Profit Over Safety?

Perhaps the most damning aspect of Johnson’s critique is the suggestion that the deployment of federal officers is motivated by profit rather than public safety. The allegation that Tom Homan, described as Trump’s border czar, accepted a $50,000 bribe in exchange for government contracts hints at corruption at the highest levels. If true, this would represent a significant ethical and legal breach, casting a shadow over the administration’s law enforcement actions in Chicago.

Johnson’s assertion that the president has “declared war on poor people” amplifies the narrative of exploitation and injustice. It frames the federal crackdown not as a measure to protect citizens but as an assault on vulnerable communities for financial gain.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

In essence, Mayor Johnson’s response to Secretary Noem is a vehement rejection of the Trump administration’s portrayal of Chicago and its protesters. By challenging the validity of Noem’s claims and highlighting potential ulterior motives, Johnson seeks to reframe the narrative around the federal presence in Chicago.

This episode is emblematic of the broader tensions between local and federal government, especially in cities led by officials who are politically opposed to the Trump administration. Johnson’s call for working people to “stand up” resonates as a rallying cry for resistance against what he perceives as authoritarian overreach and exploitation.

As the situation in Chicago unfolds, the crucial questions will revolve around the evidence supporting Noem’s allegations, the legality and ethics of the federal deployment, and the impact of these actions on the community’s safety and trust in its leaders. The discourse around these issues remains charged and deeply divided, reflecting the broader political and social chasms in contemporary America.
