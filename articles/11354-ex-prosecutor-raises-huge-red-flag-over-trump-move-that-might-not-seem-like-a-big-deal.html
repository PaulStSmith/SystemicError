Of all the things Donald Trump is doing, there is one that isn't getting as much attention, but should be, according to a former prosecutor.

Ex-federal prosecutor Joyce Vance wrote a piece on Substack called Investigating the Police, in which she covers the recent news that Trump's DOJ is investigating whether the D.C. Police Department manipulated data to make crime rates appear lower, a move the legal expert called as "performative as sending battleships to fight a cartel."

"Trump needed to save face. Hence the investigation," Vance wrote. "Trump, by the way, took to social media to post about the probe: 'D.C. gave Fake Crime numbers in order to create a false illusion of safety. This is a very bad and dangerous thing to do, and they are under serious investigation for so doing!' he wrote on Truth Social. Predictably, he’s tying this to his decision to send federal law enforcement officers and the D.C. National Guard onto the city’s streets."

She goes on to explain what she believes to be Trump's motive.

"Occam’s razor suggests that the simplest explanation is usually the best one. Trump is trying to justify his unpopular strongman tactic in D.C. This is a familiar refrain we’ve heard from him before—calling for a phony investigation to give him room to maneuver," Vance wrote. "Under the Biden, Obama, or Bush administrations, word of an investigation like this would have been viewed as one being conducted in good faith. But there is no more presumption of regularity with this administration, which has stretched the limits of the rule of law and even now has launched criminal investigations into Trump enemies like New York Attorney General Letitia James and Democratic Senator Adam Schiff, despite claims from DOJ officials during their confirmations that there was no revenge agenda. This is exactly what we warned about before the election. Using prosecutions as a political weapon is the hallmark of a would-be dictator in a banana republic."

According to Vance, "An investigation like this might not seem like a big deal given the broader scope of events this week. Sometimes it’s the details that let us see the forest."

Our Analysis:

Trump's DOJ and the Manipulation of Crime Data: A Closer Look

In a recent op-ed on Substack titled Investigating the Police, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance sheds light on a rather overshadowed maneuver by Donald Trump's Department of Justice (DOJ)—the investigation into the D.C. Police Department for allegedly manipulating crime data. Vance's analysis, ripe with insight, exposes this move as nothing short of performative politics, likening it to "sending battleships to fight a cartel." Let's dissect the layers of irony, hypocrisy, and strategy embedded in this action.

The Alleged Crime Data Manipulation

According to Vance, Trump's aim behind this probe is clear: to paint a narrative that justifies his heavy-handed tactics in Washington D.C. This is classic Trump—using the machinery of government to serve personal and political ends. His claim, broadcasted loudly on Truth Social, that D.C.'s "Fake Crime numbers" were an attempt to "create a false illusion of safety," is a strategic move to validate his deployment of federal law enforcement and the National Guard onto the city streets. This isn't just about crime rates; it's about controlling the narrative in a way that benefits him, a tactic we've seen all too often.

Occam’s Razor and Trump’s Motive

Vance invokes Occam’s razor to suggest that the simplest explanation for Trump's actions is likely the correct one: he's attempting to justify his "unpopular strongman tactic in D.C." This is not the first time Trump has called for what Vance terms a "phony investigation" to carve out political space for himself. It's a tired yet dangerous play from a playbook that prioritizes power over principle.

A Pattern of Political Weaponization

What's particularly alarming, as Vance highlights, is the erosion of trust in federal investigations under Trump's administration. During more stable times, under the leadership of Presidents Biden, Obama, or Bush, a probe like this might have been given the benefit of the doubt, seen as an act of due diligence. However, under Trump, such investigations are suspect, viewed through the lens of political vendettas and weaponization of the legal system against perceived enemies like New York Attorney General Letitia James and Senator Adam Schiff. Vance's point here is chilling and critical: using the DOJ as a political weapon is a "hallmark of a would-be dictator in a banana republic."

The Bigger Picture

Vance concludes with a poignant reminder that while this investigation might seem minor in the grand scheme, it's the accumulation of these "details" that reveals the full extent of the forest—a landscape of political manipulation, where government tools are repurposed for personal vendettas and political theater.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Joyce Vance's piece is a stark warning about the subtle yet significant ways in which democracy can be eroded from within. It's a reminder that the manipulation of data, the weaponization of legal institutions, and the blatant disregard for the principles of justice and fairness are not just abstract concerns—they're real tactics being employed in the here and now. As observers, critics, and citizens, it's our responsibility to remain vigilant, to call out these maneuvers for what they are, and to push back against the tide of autocracy that threatens to engulf the principles on which democratic governance stands.

In a world where facts are under siege and political expediency often trumps integrity, pieces like Vance's are not just commentary—they're a clarion call for accountability.
