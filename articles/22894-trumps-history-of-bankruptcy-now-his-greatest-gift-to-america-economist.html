Trump’s history of bankruptcy now his greatest gift to America: economist

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Trump Didn’t Inherit a Debt Crisis. He Manufactured One.

The Source Is Right About the Villain

The reporting is brief, but the point is clear: the debt story is not some natural disaster drifting in from the markets. Henrietta Treyz points directly at Donald Trump and the Republican Party for gutting revenue through tax cuts and then hollowing out the IRS, while the national debt keeps climbing past $40 trillion. That is the actual political object here: not “fiscal concern” in the abstract, but a governing coalition choosing to weaken the state and then pretending the damage is accidental.

Power Made the Problem

The people with real institutional power are the ones writing tax policy, controlling the agencies, and deciding whether the government can collect what is owed. Trump and congressional Republicans cut taxes, and Trump’s orbit helped slash agency jobs at the IRS. That is not incompetence in the passive sense. It is a political design: reduce the public’s revenue base, call the resulting hole a crisis, and leave everyone else to live with the bill.

The article’s strongest line is also its most revealing: they are not “cutting your taxes.” They are refusing to collect money that is already owed. That is the trick. It recasts deliberate revenue sabotage as a gift to ordinary people.

Debt Panic Works as Cover

The debt panic language is useful to power because it turns a policy choice into a weather report. Suddenly the problem is “borrowing rates,” “Treasurys,” and “deficit panic,” as if the United States stumbled into this on its own. But the source itself points to the mechanism: tax cuts that are “inordinately expensive,” plus an IRS too damaged to do basic collection. That is not confusion. That is governance in service of the wealthy and irresponsible in service of everyone else.

If the framing feels weak, it is because “fiscal alarm” is a timid way to describe a party engineering state failure while claiming to be the champion of efficiency.

Trump’s Record Explains the Method

The piece also drags in Trump’s long trail of business failures, bankruptcies, stiffed contractors, and the Trump University fraud settlement. That history matters not because it is tabloid color, but because it shows the governing style plainly: privatize gains, externalize losses, and rely on someone else’s money to cushion the collapse. A man like that does not treat public finance as a public trust. He treats it the way he treated his own firms: as a balance sheet to game and a set of creditors to outlast.

The larger point is not that Trump is uniquely shameless. It is that shamelessness is politically functional when the institutional system keeps absorbing the damage.

The Real Pattern

This story is about more than debt. It is about how authoritarian-adjacent politics launders extraction through patriotic language. Slash taxes for the powerful, weaken enforcement, explode the deficit, and then use the resulting chaos to argue that government cannot work. That sequence is the point.

The lesson is not austerity for its own sake. It is clarity about causation. When Republicans create the fiscal wreckage and call it a crisis of excess, they are not misinformed. They are laundering responsibility through spectacle.
