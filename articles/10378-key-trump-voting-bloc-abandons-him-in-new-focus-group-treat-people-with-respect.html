Key Trump voting bloc abandons him in new focus group: 'Treat people with respect'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Regretful Latino Voters: A Closer Look

Latinos for Trump at 2016 RNC. Image: VOA/WikimediaCommons

One of the new focus groups hosted by The Bulwark on Sept. 10 included Latino voters who had voted for President Donald Trump in 2024 and now regret their decision.

In a Wednesday report, the site said that the former Trump voters were angry about the economy and frustrated by the way that his administration was handling immigration and the mass deportations. Most gave Trump a "D" or "F" for his grade. One woman said she gave Trump a "D" because she was trying to be nice.

"The focus group included voters from swing state Nevada, blue state California, and red state Florida, as well as states, including New Jersey, that have traditionally been heavily Democratic but moved toward Republicans in the Trump era. New Jersey is one of two states with gubernatorial elections this November," the report said.

The focus group confirms polling data reported by Politico earlier this week. His approval from Latino voters dropped from a net-negative 12 points in February to a net-negative 20 in September.

A Virginia woman said that the country is headed in a "negative" direction.

"Grocery prices, we have the tariffs, we have the immigration going on, and also using the National Guard instead of police to fight crime," she cited.

“I think a lot of people are a lot more hateful," said a woman from Colorado. "They feel they can be a lot more open about it, because they see it everywhere so much that no one is really trying to be nice and get along and respect differences. They think it’s okay to pass their judgments and stereotypes willy-nilly because no one does anything about it, because they see the people in control doing it.”

The focus group was before the slaying of far-right activist Charlie Kirk.

Adrian Carrasquillo, who has covered immigration issues and Latino voters for the past decade, said that he was struck by the focus group's nuance around such issues.

Reed, from Nevada, said he liked Trump's moves on immigration, but then witnessed the suffering it was causing as "families [were] being ripped apart."

“I didn’t think it was going to be that dramatic, where it’s going to be hurtful in that sense. So, that kind of changed your mood from ‘Okay, the country is getting better on that immigration part.’ The borders are getting more strict, but at what cost?” he asked.

Beth said she also had full faith in the administration, but that quickly fell away when she witnessed the lack of a "humane" approach. She compared the willy-nilly deportations to the arbitrary firing of hundreds of thousands of federal workers by tech billionaire Elon Musk under the executive order to eliminate "waste, fraud, and abuse."

“It’s bullying at another level,” she said. “I live in America, I don’t want to be in a dictatorship. I still want to be in a democratic nation that has rules, has regulations. . . . This is America, we gotta treat people with respect and dignity and I think a lot of that has gone out the window.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Regretful Latino Voters: A Closer Look

In a revealing piece by The Bulwark, a focus group of Latino voters who once cast their ballots for Donald Trump in 2024 now express deep regret over their decision. Their grievances span from economic dissatisfaction to dismay at the administration's immigration policies and mass deportations. This scenario, albeit unsurprising to those who've been paying attention, sheds light on the shifting sands of political allegiance and the tangible impact of governance on voter sentiment.

Economic Woes and Immigration Woes: A Double Whammy

Firstly, let's dissect the economic frustration. The participants' grievances include soaring grocery prices and the impact of tariffs—issues that hit the wallets of everyday Americans, particularly those in working-class communities. Trump's economic policies, once hailed by supporters as the blueprint for American prosperity, seem to have lost their sheen. The use of the National Guard to combat crime, instead of addressing these economic pressures, further illustrates a government more interested in muscle-flexing than in the wellbeing of its citizens.

The Immigration Debacle

The handling of immigration, a cornerstone of Trump's agenda, has evidently backfired. The focus group's feedback underscores a critical misjudgment by the Trump administration: underestimating the human cost of stringent immigration policies. Reed from Nevada initially supported stricter border controls, only to recoil at the sight of families being torn apart. This sentiment is echoed by Beth, who condemns the "willy-nilly deportations" and the lack of humanity in the administration's approach, drawing a parallel to Elon Musk's infamous federal worker purge.

A Nation Divided

Perhaps the most poignant observation comes from a Colorado woman who highlights a surge in open hostility and judgment within the country—a direct reflection of the tone set by those in power. This toxic atmosphere, fueled by divisive rhetoric and policies, stands in stark contrast to the ideals of respect and dignity that America professes to uphold.

The Verdict

The Bulwark's report, while focusing on a specific demographic, touches on broader themes of disillusionment and disenchantment that transcend racial and ethnic lines. The Trump administration's failure to maintain the trust of these Latino voters—once supporters—is indicative of a larger pattern of political fallout resulting from unfulfilled promises and detrimental policies.

A Democratic Nation at Heart

In the end, the message from these regretful voters is clear: America's strength lies in its democratic values, its respect for diversity and human dignity. The administration's deviation from these principles has not only cost it the support of these individuals but has also stirred a longing for a return to a more respectful and humane governance.

In assessing the fallout from Trump's policies, one cannot ignore the broader implications for the Republican Party and the political landscape at large. The shift in sentiment among these Latino voters from support to regret is a cautionary tale of the consequences of prioritizing ideology over empathy and economic pragmatism. As we move forward, the voices of these voters serve as a critical reminder of the need for policies that uplift and unite, rather than divide and disenfranchise.
