Trump's National Guard move hit with block: 'Facts do not justify the President's actions'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 17, 20254 min read

National Guard members walk at the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) Broadview facility in Chicago, Illinois on Oct. 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jeenah Moon

A federal appeals court Thursday blocked the National Guard from deploying in Illinois, saying “the facts do not justify the President’s actions” and rejecting the Trump administration’s request to put a hold on a court ruling that temporarily blocks the troops’ deployment, according to reports.

This ruling allows a temporary restraining order against the guard deployment issued by U.S. District Judge April Perry in Chicago on Oct. 9 to remain in place, Reuters reports.

The restraining order is set to expire on Oct. 23; however, Perry has added a hearing the day before to see if it should be extended beyond that date.

“We conclude that the district court’s factual findings … were not clearly erroneous, and that the facts do not justify the President’s actions.”

Trump has sent National Guard troops to multiple cities, including Los Angeles, Washington, D.C., Memphis and Chicago. He has signaled plans to send them to other cities, including Portland, Oregon.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Federal Appeals Court Decision on National Guard Deployment in Illinois

The recent decision by a federal appeals court to block the National Guard from deploying in Illinois serves as a stark reminder of the constant tug-of-war between executive power and judicial oversight. The court’s assertion that “the facts do not justify the President’s actions” is not just a slap in the face to the Trump administration; it’s a blaring siren for the urgent need to scrutinize the justifications behind deploying military forces within the United States.

The Court’s Decision: A Judicial Check on Executive Power

The appeals court’s decision to uphold the temporary restraining order against the National Guard’s deployment, initially issued by U.S. District Judge April Perry, underscores the judiciary’s role as a critical check on executive power. The court’s reliance on factual findings to reach its decision highlights the importance of evidence-based governance—a principle seemingly lost on the Trump administration.

It’s noteworthy that the court’s judgment hinges on the assertion that the facts do not justify the President’s actions. This statement is not just a legal conclusion; it’s a damning critique of the Trump administration’s approach to handling domestic unrest. The implication here is clear: the deployment of the National Guard was not supported by a rational assessment of the situation but rather, it appears, by other, less savory motivations.

Trump’s Deployment of the National Guard: A Pattern of Controversy

President Trump’s decision to send National Guard troops to cities like Los Angeles, Washington, D.C., Memphis, Chicago, and potentially Portland, Oregon, has been mired in controversy. The administration’s rationale for these deployments—ostensibly to maintain order—has frequently been met with skepticism and accusations of political grandstanding.

The use of military personnel in these contexts raises profound questions about the militarization of the United States’ response to civil unrest. It’s a move that not only escalates tensions but also erodes the foundational principle of civilian governance. The federal appeals court’s ruling, therefore, is not just about the deployment of troops; it’s a critique of a governance style that leans too heavily on shows of force.

Looking Forward: The Implications of the Court’s Ruling

With U.S. District Judge April Perry’s hearing scheduled for the day before the restraining order’s expiration, the future of the National Guard’s deployment in Illinois—and potentially elsewhere—hangs in the balance. This upcoming hearing is not merely procedural; it represents a critical juncture in the ongoing debate over the limits of executive power and the role of the military in civilian life.

The court’s decision to block the deployment, at least temporarily, offers a glimmer of hope for those advocating for de-escalation and a return to civilian-led governance. However, it also serves as a reminder of the fragility of democratic norms under an administration willing to push the boundaries of its authority.

In the final analysis, this episode reflects a broader struggle over the soul of American democracy. It’s a struggle between those who see military might as a first resort and those who believe in the power of dialogue, diplomacy, and the rule of law. As this saga unfolds, it will undoubtedly continue to spark fierce debates over the balance between security and liberty, the executive’s reach, and the judiciary’s role in maintaining the equilibrium of power.

In conclusion, the federal appeals court’s decision is a significant moment of judicial pushback against what many see as an overreach of executive power. It’s a reminder that in a democracy, no single branch of government should have unfettered control, especially when it comes to decisions that could fundamentally alter the character of civilian-police relations. As we move forward, it’s imperative that we keep a vigilant eye on the balance of powers, lest we find ourselves on a slippery slope to authoritarianism.
