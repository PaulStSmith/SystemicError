The 100-year-old memoir that predicts exactly what will happen in November

Trump’s 33 Percent Is Not the Story

The Poll Is a Distraction

The source treats Trump’s 33 percent approval rating as the headline, then spends most of its energy gawking at how anyone could still support him. That reaction is understandable, but it is also evasive. Approval numbers are not power. Power is who controls the state, who sets the agenda, and who can translate grievance into coercion. Trump still holds the White House, and that is the fact with consequences.

The article briefly notes his 2024 win with roughly 49 percent of the vote. That matters more than the approval poll because it identifies the mechanism: a minority of the electorate, concentrated and mobilized, can still seize institutional control and use it to do damage.

Trump’s Base Is Not a Mystery

The piece wants to frame Trump’s support as a moral aberration, a refuge for “the most tasteless and racist people.” That may satisfy outrage, but it explains too little. His backing is not held together by confusion. It is held together by permission structures: racism, authoritarian appetite, backlash politics, and the willingness of institutions to normalize behavior that would have disqualified any previous president.

The article is right about one thing: Trump is not being propped up by accident. He is being carried by people who know exactly what he is and accept the bargain anyway.

Misdirection by Melodrama

The Blumenthal anecdote is meant to supply historical gravity, but it also functions as a kind of refuge. Once the writer reaches for Germany and Hitler, the analysis becomes easier and less precise. The comparison is emotionally legible, but it risks turning contemporary political failure into a morality play about evil people rather than a study of institutional collapse.

What actually matters in the source is not the theatrical invocation of dictatorship. It is the concrete claim that Republicans are using gerrymandering and voter suppression, while Trump attacks elections themselves. That is the real structure: manipulation at the institutional level, then a fog of rhetoric to make it seem like ordinary politics.

The Democratic Task Is Institutional, Not Therapeutic

The article predicts Democratic victories in the midterms, citing recent contests where Democrats have won and turnout has been strong. That is the only part of the piece that approaches strategy instead of venting. If Democrats are winning, it is because voters are reacting to an immediate threat and because the opposition still has enough institutional footholds to turn fear into ballots.

But the source still drifts into fantasy when it treats an election wave as a clean rescue. Defeating authoritarian politics requires more than waiting for the map to turn blue. It requires holding power, governing hard, and using institutional leverage against the machinery that produced the threat in the first place.

What the Story Really Shows

This is not a story about a shocking approval number or about whether Americans have finally “woken up.” It is a story about how authoritarian politics survives while still being unpopular, because institutions can be captured, elections can be manipulated, and damage can be normalized faster than public opinion can correct it.

That is the larger pattern: cruelty does not need majority enthusiasm. It only needs enough power, enough fear, and enough people willing to mistake outrage for resistance.
