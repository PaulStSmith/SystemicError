'I want the answer, and I want it now': Trump launches baffling attack at drug companies

Donald Trump attempted what appeared to be a defense of embattled Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr on Monday morning by making demands of drug companies to turn over information that the president claims he has already seen.

With Kennedy facing a firestorm over firings and ensuing resignations at the Centers for Disease Control just before the Labor Day holiday weekend, Trump waded into the chaos by telling drug companies that took part in halting the COVID pandemic they need to share data.

As part of his busy Labor Day morning on his Truth Social platform, the president admitted that he has already seen the data, before making the baffling demand.

“It is very important that the Drug Companies justify the success of their various Covid Drugs. Many people think they are a miracle that saved Millions of lives. Others disagree!” he wrote before adding, “With CDC being ripped apart over this question, I want the answer, and I want it NOW. I have been shown information from Pfizer, and others, that is extraordinary, but they never seem to show those results to the public. Why not??? They go off to the next ‘hunt’ and let everyone rip themselves apart, including Bobby Kennedy Jr. and CDC, trying to figure out the success or failure of the Drug Companies Covid work.”

He then admitted, “They show me GREAT numbers and results, but they don’t seem to be showing them to many others. I want them to show them NOW, to CDC and the public, and clear up this MESS, one way or the other!!! I hope OPERATION WARP SPEED was as ‘BRILLIANT’ as many say it was. If not, we all want to know about it, and why??? Thank you for your attention to this very important matter! President DJT.”

The president made no mention of why he has not, or refuses to, share information he has already seen with RFK Jr.

Our Analysis:

An Exercise in Absurdity: Trump's Baffling Defense of RFK Jr.

In what can only be described as a theatrical performance of political maneuvering, Donald Trump's recent attempt to defend Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. plunges us into a realm of confusion and contradiction. On a seemingly ordinary Labor Day morning, Trump transformed his social media platform into a stage for what appears to be a mix of a defense plea and a demand for transparency from drug companies, all while entangled in a web of his own contradictions.

The Context of Chaos

With RFK Jr. caught in a storm due to firings and resignations at the CDC, Trump's intervention could have been a moment of clarity. Instead, it morphs into a spectacle of confusion. Trump's assertion, “It is very important that the Drug Companies justify the success of their various Covid Drugs,” is laughable in its irony. One must wonder, if the president believes in the miraculous nature of these drugs, why the sudden demand for justification?

The Paradox of Knowledge

Trump's admission that he has already seen the data he demands makes one question the purpose behind his public outcry. It's almost as if he's performing a magic trick, waving one hand to distract the audience while the other conceals the truth. His statement, “I have been shown information from Pfizer, and others, that is extraordinary, but they never seem to show those results to the public. Why not???” begs a question in return: If the information is indeed extraordinary, why has Trump himself not shared it?

The Unanswered Question

The crux of the matter, and the most glaring omission in Trump's convoluted defense, is his failure to share the information he claims to possess with either RFK Jr. or the public. This omission is a glaring systemic error in the narrative he attempts to construct. It's a classic Trump tactic: claim to have solutions or knowledge he refuses to share, thereby creating a narrative where he is the only savior, without providing any tangible proof.

The Demand for Now

Trump's insistence on immediate action from drug companies, “I want them to show them NOW, to CDC and the public, and clear up this MESS, one way or the other!!!” is as ironic as it is futile. It's akin to a captain demanding that the sea calm itself during a storm he helped to brew. The urgency and desperation in his tone cannot mask the underlying inaction that characterized his own response to the crises.

OPERATION WARP SPEED: A Retrospective Glorification?

Ending on a note of self-congratulation, Trump hopes OPERATION WARP SPEED is as “BRILLIANT” as he's been led to believe. This self-applause, however, feels more like a desperate grasp at legacy than a genuine inquiry into the program's efficacy. In true Trump fashion, it's less about the truth and more about the perception of success.

Conclusion: The Theater of the Absurd

Trump's Labor Day performance is a masterclass in contradiction, diversion, and the art of saying much without saying anything of substance. It's a narrative filled with demands for transparency that he himself refuses to provide, wrapped in a defense that does little to defend. In the end, it leaves us with more questions than answers, a hallmark of the Trump era. The real question remains: When will the audience tire of the performance?
