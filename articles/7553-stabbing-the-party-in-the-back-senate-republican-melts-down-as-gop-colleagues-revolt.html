'Stabbing the party in the back!' Senate Republican melts down as GOP colleagues revolt

Republican lawmakers on Wednesday were enraged after four moderate Republicans revolted against their own party to push forward a health care petition to extend Affordable Care Act subsidies before they are set to expire.

The health care debate was a significant blow to House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), who has been losing control over his own party as Republicans in swing districts sided with the 214 Democrats — adding 218 total votes — and creating “enormous tension” within the GOP, CNN chief Congressional correspondent Manu Raju reported.

Several Republicans had heated responses, voicing their anger over the swing votes from Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick, Ryan Mackenzie, and Rob Bresnahan, all of Pennsylvania; and Rep. Mike Lawler of New York.

“To me, they’re stabbing the rest of the party in the back,” Rep. Eric Burlison (R-MO) said. “We took a bold stand and I think the president is absolutely right. Throwing money at insurance companies doesn’t solve the problem. This is, to me, a betrayal to the rest of us Republicans.”

Some Republicans were critical of Johnson’s leadership.

“He needs to do better,” Rep. Nick Lalola (R-NY) said. “He needs to allow for a vote that both addresses the short- and long-term issues of Obamacare.”

It’s unclear when the vote would happen, but lawmakers have pointed to Americans’ dissatisfaction as a primary reason for pushing forward the bill to the Senate, despite challenges that could await the legislation from the Republican Senate majority. If the legislation is not passed, millions of people in the U.S. could see their health care premiums skyrocket in 2026.

“People feel that they have counted on, or waited for, their Congress to act on an issue that they feel is a huge priority and they see no action — there’s consequences to that,” Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) said.

Our Analysis:

The GOP Health Care Rebellion: A Symptom of Deeper Issues

In a move that can only be described as a political earthquake, four moderate Republicans have defied the party line, inciting what amounts to an intra-party insurrection over health care policy. Wednesday’s events are not just a fleeting moment of defiance but a glaring symptom of the systemic dysfunction within the Republican Party. Let’s dissect the layers of this debacle with the precision of a surgeon and the sting of a bee.

The Rebels and the Reaction

At the heart of this rebellion are Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick, Ryan Mackenzie, Rob Bresnahan of Pennsylvania, and Mike Lawler of New York. These lawmakers, representing swing districts, have taken a stand that aligns more closely with the demands of their constituents than the party doctrine. This is a classic case of political survival over party loyalty, a concept that seems to have enraged their colleagues.

Rep. Eric Burlison’s statement that these four are “stabbing the rest of the party in the back” is a melodramatic portrayal that misses the mark. The real issue isn’t betrayal; it’s the refusal to acknowledge a health care system in crisis. The insistence that extending Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies is merely “throwing money at insurance companies” simplifies a complex issue to a dangerous degree. It’s not about insurance companies; it’s about preventing millions from facing exorbitant health care premiums.

Leadership Under Fire

House Speaker Mike Johnson’s leadership—or lack thereof—has come under scrutiny. Criticism from within his own ranks, including Rep. Nick Lalola’s call for better leadership, highlights a party at war with itself. The inability to marshal the party’s diverse factions under a unified strategy has led to public displays of discord, undermining the GOP’s ability to govern effectively.

The demand for a vote that addresses both the short- and long-term issues of Obamacare is a rational request that seems to be falling on deaf ears. The leadership’s failure to navigate this crisis speaks volumes about its inability to reconcile the party’s internal divisions.

The Bigger Picture

The extension of ACA subsidies isn’t just a policy debate; it’s a lifeline for millions of Americans. The potential for skyrocketing premiums in 2026 underscores the urgency of the matter. This rebellion, therefore, isn’t just political theater—it’s a desperate attempt to force action on an issue of national significance.

Senator Lisa Murkowski’s comments encapsulate the frustration felt by many: the American people are tired of waiting for Congress to act on critical issues. The consequences of inaction are real and immediate for countless individuals and families across the nation.

Conclusion: A Call for Real Leadership

What this episode reveals is a Republican Party at a crossroads, caught between ideological purity and practical governance. The moderate rebellion is a wake-up call that the status quo is untenable. It’s a demand for leadership that is responsive, pragmatic, and capable of addressing the needs of the American people.

The GOP’s internal strife over health care policy is a microcosm of a larger issue: a party struggling to define itself in the post-Trump era. As the 2026 deadline looms, the question becomes whether the Republican Party can overcome its internal divisions to govern effectively, or if it will continue to be consumed by them. The choice is clear, but whether the GOP can make the right decision remains to be seen.
