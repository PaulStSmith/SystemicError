'Unhinged': Spy chief alerted as Trump seen 'threatening Supreme Court justices'

Former U.S. Rep. Tulsi Gabbard attends a campaign rally of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump in Greensboro, North Carolina, U.S. October 22, 2024. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

"Trump is now threatening Supreme Court justices who ruled against him," Democratic influencer Harry Sisson said on Saturday.

That is just one of the notable people who over the weekend accused the president of lobbing indirect threats at the members of the mostly conservative Supreme Court, many of whom Trump himself appointed.

The allegations stem from a Trump Truth Social post on Saturday, when the president shared a comment made by a MAGA attorney.

Former GOP staffer Mike Davis has made headlines for his social media comments in the past, and was rumored to be on Trump's list for attorney general. Recently, he posted a plan to get back at the Supreme Court justices for not ruling in line with MAGA.

"The Supreme Court still has an illegal injunction on the President of the United States, preventing him from commanding military operations to expel these foreign terrorists," Davis wrote. "The President should house these terrorists near the Chevy Chase Country Club, with daytime release."

Chevy Chase Club is "an elite country club that counts Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. among its members," the Washington Post reports.

Sisson reiterated that fact when he said Trump had reposted "a message from one of his advisors calling for terrorists to be released near the Chevy Chase Country Club in Maryland, where Justices Kavanaugh and Roberts are members."

"Absolutely unhinged," the liberal influencer added.

Conservative political analyst Tim Miller also weighed in, saying, "Every right wing account on here had the vapors over random Dems threatening SCOTUS post Roe (in some cases rightly.) Here’s the president of the United States threatening SCOTUS justices."

"Will see if anyone even criticizes him for it on Fox. Not holding my breath," he then added.

Former Obama speechwriter Jon Favreau spoke directly to the source of the original plan, telling Davis that, "I think any threats to Supreme Court justices are wrong and dangerous - whether they come from the left or come from you, like in the post where you suggested releasing 'terrorists' where Justices Roberts and Kavanaugh live. Despicable."

Ex-GOP lawmaker Barbara Comstock alerted the director of national intelligence, saying, "Just noting for [Tulsi Gabbard] in case she’s concerned."

Our Analysis:

An Analysis of Trump's Alleged Threats to the Supreme Court

"Trump is now threatening Supreme Court justices who ruled against him," purports Democratic influencer Harry Sisson, catapulting us into a narrative that seemingly unfolds with all the subtlety of a Shakespearean tragedy, yet with the modern twist of social media intrigue. This article, detailing accusations of former President Donald Trump's indirect threats toward the Supreme Court—many of whose members he appointed—plunges us into a convoluted tale of political retribution and judicial intimidation.

The Allegations: A Closer Look

At the heart of these allegations is a post from Trump's Truth Social platform, echoing a statement by MAGA attorney Mike Davis. Davis, a former GOP staffer with a penchant for headline-grabbing social media comments, is rumored to have been considered for Trump's attorney general. His recent post outlines a plan to retaliate against Supreme Court justices for decisions not aligned with MAGA ideals, suggesting the President should "house these terrorists near the Chevy Chase Country Club," a place where Justices Kavanaugh and Roberts are members.

The implications of this statement are as dangerous as they are absurd. To suggest releasing 'terrorists' near the homes of Supreme Court justices as a form of retribution not only undermines the rule of law but also flirts dangerously with incitement to violence. It's an escalation from political disagreement to personal vendetta, crossing lines that should remain sacrosanct in a democracy.

The Reaction: A Spectrum of Discontent

The reactions to Trump's repost of Davis's plan span the political spectrum, yet all seem to converge on a single point: this is unacceptable. Conservative analyst Tim Miller and former Obama speechwriter Jon Favreau, among others, underscore the peril such threats pose, regardless of political affiliation. It's a rare moment of bipartisan agreement in an era where such consensus is as rare as a unicorn sighting.

Notably, ex-GOP lawmaker Barbara Comstock's decision to alert the director of national intelligence highlights the severity of the situation. This isn't merely political theater; it's a potential threat to the safety and independence of the judiciary.

The Media's Role: Fox News in the Spotlight

Tim Miller's skepticism regarding Fox News's potential criticism of Trump illuminates another critical facet of this saga: the role of media in holding power to account. The question isn't just whether Fox News will criticize Trump; it's whether any media platform can transcend partisan biases to condemn actions that threaten the bedrock of American democracy.

Conclusion: Beyond Partisan Politics

This article isn't just a tale of Trump's alleged threats; it's a wakeup call to all who cherish democratic principles. The independence of the judiciary is a cornerstone of democracy, and any attempt to undermine it—be it through direct threats or insinuations of violence—must be met with unequivocal condemnation.

In the end, the issue transcends Trump, Davis, or any individual. It's about preserving the sanctity of our democratic institutions against the corrosive tide of partisanship and personal vendettas. As this narrative unfolds, one can only hope that the collective voice of reason will prevail over the whispers of retribution and intimidation.
