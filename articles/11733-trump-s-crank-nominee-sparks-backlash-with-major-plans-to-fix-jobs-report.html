Trump's 'crank' nominee sparks backlash with major plans to 'fix' jobs report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 13, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Politicization of the Bureau of Labor Statistics: A Dangerous Precedent

President Donald Trump’s pick to head the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Heritage Foundation economist E.J. Antoni, is facing backlash after reports that he would consider suspending the crucial monthly jobs report, calling it flawed.

“How on earth are businesses supposed to plan – or how is the Fed supposed to conduct monetary policy – when they don’t know how many jobs are being added or lost in our economy? It’s a serious problem that needs to be fixed immediately,” Antoni told Fox Business.

His solution is to publish the data less often—a move that could send shockwaves through the business world.

“Until it is corrected, the BLS should suspend issuing the monthly job reports but keep publishing the more accurate, though less timely, quarterly data,” he said. “Major decision-makers from Wall Street to D.C. rely on these numbers, and a lack of confidence in the data has far-reaching consequences.”

President Trump fired the previous Commissioner of Labor Statistics, Erika McEntarfer, after the BLS released downward revisions of more than 250,000 for April and May.

“I was just informed that our Country’s ‘Jobs Numbers’ are being produced by a Biden Appointee, Dr. Erika McEntarfer,” Trump wrote in an August 1 social media post, baselessly accusing her of having “faked the Jobs Numbers before the Election to try and boost Kamala’s chances of Victory.”

The 141-year-old Bureau of Labor Statistics is an independent statistical agency and part of the U.S. Department of Labor. Installing what critics are calling a highly partisan commissioner would be unprecedented in modern times.

Former Secretary of Labor Robert Reich issued a warning: “The independence of the Bureau of Labor Statistics is vital. If politicized, it can’t be trusted as a source of information. Who did Trump just put in charge of the agency? E.J. Antoni — chief economist at the Heritage Foundation and contributor to Project 2025.”

“On top of being the Heritage Foundation’s Chief Economist, E.J. Antoni is literally the 5th Contributor named in Project 2025,” noted journalist Ahmed Baba. “You can’t make this up. Trump is seeking to put the BLS — and America’s economic data — in the hands of a Project 2025 extremist.”

READ MORE: Trump Melts Down Over Elizabeth Warren’s Accusations

Economics writer Joey Politano wrote: “An ‘economist’ so dumb I had to explain to him how the import price index works last month will now lead the BLS…”

Former Obama White House Chief Economist Jason Furman, a professor of economics remarked: “I don’t think I have ever publicly criticized any Presidential nominee before. But E.J. Antoni is completely unqualified to be BLS Commissioner. He is an extreme partisan and does not have any relevant expertise. He would be a break from decades of nonpartisan technocrats.”

The Atlantic’s Dr. Norman Ornstein, a political scientist warned: “Be sure to absorb the latest inflation and jobs reports from BLS. They are the last accurate ones you will see at least for the next 3+ years.”

U.S. Sen. Tina Smith (D-MN) wrote: “Installing a crank economist to only publish favorable or distorted data on the economy (inflation, jobs numbers, etc.) Textbook authoritarianism – mum from my GOP colleagues.”

“Can’t have bad jobs numbers if there are no jobs numbers,” snarked Aaron Fritschner, Deputy Chief of Staff for U.S. Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA).

Our Analysis:

The Politicization of the Bureau of Labor Statistics: A Dangerous Precedent

In a move that shakes the very foundations of economic reporting and analysis, President Donald Trump’s appointment of E.J. Antoni, a Heritage Foundation economist, to head the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) signals a disturbing pivot towards the politicization of government agencies that have historically been bastions of impartiality. Antoni's consideration to suspend the monthly jobs report, under the guise of addressing its supposed flaws, is nothing short of alarming.

E.J. Antoni’s Proposal: A Red Flag

Antoni’s proposal to suspend the monthly jobs report and instead opt for less frequent, quarterly publications is a blatant attempt to obscure real-time economic realities. This move, ostensibly to correct purported inaccuracies, raises significant questions about the motives at play. The monthly jobs report is a critical tool for economic forecasting and decision-making, utilized by businesses and policymakers alike. To suggest its suspension under the pretext of improving accuracy is to undermine the economic intelligence that guides everything from Federal Reserve policies to small business planning.

The Firing of Erika McEntarfer: A Baseless Act

The dismissal of Erika McEntarfer, the previous Commissioner of Labor Statistics, over downward revisions of job numbers further illustrates a troubling trend of attacking professional and apolitical civil servants. Trump’s accusations of data manipulation to influence election outcomes, without evidence, is a reckless and dangerous narrative that erodes trust in public institutions.

A Heritage Foundation Economist at the Helm: The Implications

The appointment of E.J. Antoni, a figure deeply embedded in conservative think-tank circles and a contributor to Project 2025, represents an unprecedented politicization of the BLS. Critics rightly point out that placing economic data in the hands of someone with a clear political agenda could compromise the integrity of information that millions of Americans and businesses rely on.

The Chorus of Criticism

The backlash from economists, former officials, and journalists is telling. From being labeled as unqualified and extreme partisan to warnings about the last accurate reports we might see for years, the consensus is clear: Antoni’s leadership threatens the credibility and reliability of vital economic data. The critique from figures such as Robert Reich, Ahmed Baba, and Norman Ornstein underscores the potential for long-term damage to the BLS’s reputation and, by extension, to public trust in government-produced economic indicators.

Conclusion: A Path Toward Authoritarianism?

The narrative unfolding around the BLS and E.J. Antoni is more than a mere personnel change; it is indicative of a broader assault on the apolitical nature of government agencies. This move towards favoring “favorable or distorted data,” as Senator Tina Smith points out, is emblematic of textbook authoritarianism—a strategy aimed at controlling the narrative by manipulating facts.

In times where economic data plays a critical role in shaping policies and business strategies, the integrity of institutions like the BLS is paramount. The potential suspension of the monthly jobs report and the installment of a highly partisan commissioner should be a call to action for anyone who values the independence and accuracy of public economic data. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant against attempts to undermine the foundations of our economy and society for political gain.
