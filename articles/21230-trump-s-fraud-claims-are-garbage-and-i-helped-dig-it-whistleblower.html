Trump's fraud claims are garbage — and 'I helped dig it': Whistleblower

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20263 min read

Trump Wants the Evidence, Not the Truth

The Setup

Donald Trump is preparing to release classified intelligence documents he says will prove “irregularities” in the 2020 election. Miles Taylor, who worked on election protection at DHS, says the whole thing is recycled material Trump cherry-picked years ago and will twist into a fraud story the intelligence community never made.

That is the basic shape of the scam. The source is not a revelation about election theft. It is a preview of how an incumbent-minded strongman weaponizes the state after the fact: not to clarify reality, but to launder a loser’s grievance into government-sounding propaganda.

Who Actually Had Power

The power in this story sits with Trump. He had the intelligence apparatus, the declassification authority, the personnel decisions, and the ability to set the narrative while in office. If he believed the documents proved his case, he could have released them before leaving office in January 2021. He did not.

That matters. It means the delay was not bureaucratic caution or hidden discovery. It was selection. Trump had the power to disclose what he wanted, when he wanted, and he kept the material in reserve because the point was never transparency. The point was political utility.

Selective Security, Selective Outrage

Taylor’s account is brutally simple: Trump cared about “election security” only when the story helped him. He was briefed that Russia, Iran, and China all tried to meddle in U.S. elections. He was also told, repeatedly, that none of that tampering changed votes in 2018 or 2020.

Trump’s reaction exposed the logic of his politics. He warned loudly about Chinese interference when it fit his messaging, barely acknowledged Russia when Russia was the country that had helped him in 2016, and fired Chris Krebs by tweet after Krebs called the 2020 election the most secure in American history. That is not confusion. It is governance by loyalty test, where the same evidence is treated as grave only when it can be turned against an enemy.

The Misdirection Machine

The framing here is weak because it asks readers to treat foreign interference and election legitimacy as adjacent concerns. They are not. Foreign states tried to meddle. That is real. But Trump and his allies are trying to convert that real fact into proof of a stolen election, which is a different claim entirely.

That distinction is the whole game. Authoritarians do not need to invent everything from scratch. They raid real facts, strip out context, and repackage them as evidence for the lie they already need. The source text describes exactly that process: old intelligence, cherry-picked and recast, then sold as hindsight that somehow rewrites the result of an election Trump lost.

A Loyalist State

Taylor says Trump has installed loyalists over the DNI, DOJ, and FBI while mounting a “whole-of-government campaign” to sow doubt about an election his side fears losing. That is the important part. The story is not just about one man lying. It is about how control of institutions turns lying into policy.

When the agencies that are supposed to protect democratic administration are staffed for obedience, the machinery of the state can be used to preemptively poison public trust. The target is not simply voters. It is the idea that elections can produce legitimate outcomes at all.

The Pattern

This is the familiar pattern of modern authoritarian politics in the United States: keep the power, reverse the facts, and call the reversal patriotism. Trump did not build his argument from evidence. He built it from grievance, then tried to retrofit the intelligence community into his storyline.

Democrats, and anyone serious about democracy, should read that plainly. The danger is not that Trump is confused about the record. The danger is that he understands power well enough to know that if he can control the agencies, he can turn defeated lies into official reality.
