'It is criminal': GOP lawmaker wants Gavin Newsom to be arrested for Stephen Miller insult

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 29, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

California Governor Gavin Newsom speaks after he and other lawmakers signed the “Election Rigging Response Act” in Sacramento, California, U.S., August 21, 2025. REUTERS/Fred Greaves

California Governor Gavin Newsom should be charged criminally for describing Stephen Miller as a “fascist,” according to a Republican lawmaker.

On Friday, Newsom’s press office posted this on X: “STEPHEN MILLER IS A FASCIST!” MAGA accused Newsom of “trying to get Miller killed” with the simple comment on social media. Miller is currently the White House deputy chief of staff under Trump.

Republican Rep. Derrick Van Orden (WI) went as far as to allege criminality on behalf of the governor.

“At this point, this reaches the threshold of domestic terrorism,” the lawmaker claimed. “This is no longer ‘inflammatory’, it is criminal.”

He went on to cite 18 U.S. Code § 2331, and say, “No one is above the law, @CAgovernor.”

Ex-prosecutor Ron Filipkowski flagged the comment, saying, “Congressman wants Gavin Newsom arrested for terrorism because he called Stephen Miller a fascist.”

“The hundreds of times Miller called Democrats fascist apparently doesn’t count for anything,” he then added Sunday.

Our Analysis:

The Absurdity of Criminalizing Political Discourse

In a bewildering leap of logic, a Republican lawmaker has declared that California Governor Gavin Newsom should face criminal charges for labeling Stephen Miller a “fascist” on social media. This accusation, teetering on the brink of the absurd, underscores a dangerous trend in American politics where the line between hyperbolic rhetoric and genuine threats is not just blurred but seemingly obliterated.

The Allegation

Republican Rep. Derrick Van Orden (WI) has taken umbrage with Newsom’s candid critique, escalating the matter to the heights of “domestic terrorism.” Yes, you read that correctly. A political comment, devoid of any call to violence, has been equated with an act that would traditionally involve significant threats to national security. Van Orden’s invocation of 18 U.S. Code § 2331 in this context is not only a monumental stretch but also a gross misinterpretation of the law concerning acts of terrorism.

The Double Standard

The irony of Van Orden’s statement cannot be overstated. The political arena is rife with instances of individuals on both sides of the aisle using “fascist” as a go-to insult. Yet, the selective outrage exhibited when the term is directed at Stephen Miller, a figure notorious for his hardline stances on immigration and policy advising under the Trump administration, is telling. Ron Filipkowski’s comment illuminates this hypocrisy brilliantly, pointing out the evident double standard in how political speech is being weaponized.

The Danger of This Precedent

What’s at stake here is far more significant than a political squabble. If the assertion that calling someone a “fascist” constitutes a form of domestic terrorism were to gain any legal traction, it would set a perilous precedent. The very fabric of free speech, especially within the political domain, relies on the ability to express dissenting opinions without fear of criminal prosecution. To label such discourse as criminal is to edge closer to authoritarianism, where political criticism is met with legal retribution.

A Critical Examination

Let’s be clear: the suggestion that Governor Newsom’s comment could or should be construed as “criminal” is not only baseless but laughably so. It is a testament to the current state of political hyperbole and the lengths to which some will go to silence or discredit their opponents. The allegation of domestic terrorism, in this case, does not stand up to scrutiny, serving only to dilute the gravity of true acts of terrorism and undermine the legal framework designed to combat them.

In the broader context, this incident reveals a troubling trajectory towards criminalizing political speech, one that all parties should be wary of. The ability to critique, to label, and to speak openly against political figures is a cornerstone of democracy. Efforts to label such speech as criminal are not just misguided; they are fundamentally anti-democratic.

Conclusion

The charge against Governor Newsom is not only unfounded but represents a dangerous flirtation with authoritarian tactics of suppressing dissent. It’s imperative that we recognize this incident for what it is: an overblown reaction that seeks to weaponize the legal system against political opponents. In defending the right to political discourse, we uphold the principles of democracy and free speech. Anything less is a systemic error we cannot afford.
