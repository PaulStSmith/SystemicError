ICE seizes 11-year-old to force dad’s deportation — despite torture risk

Aaron Reichlin-Melnick, a senior fellow at the American Immigration Council, posted an excerpt of a New York Times report revealing that the man fled to the United States after his wife was locked up for her political views.

Pavel Snegir and his 11-year-old son, Aleksandr, already passed the initial screening and confirmed that if Snegir is sent back to Russia, he will likely be tortured.

"But the Trump admin is still trying to deport him anyway, and has taken away his son until he agrees to be deported," wrote Reichlin-Melnick.

Snegir and his son had been in ICE custody, but in May he was taken to an airport in San Diego. He was told he could take his son to the court hearing in New York. But once they were at the airport, Snegir was scared to board the plane, convinced he was about to be deported back to Russia.

"Later that day, after the flight had left, an ICE official told him he would be separated from his son because he refused to be deported," the report said.

Snegir said he refused to give the government his child. ICE followed with threats he'd be thrown "to the ground, handcuffed and taken away if he did not relent."

He didn't move and "everything she promised happened," Snegir recalled.

His son witnessed the whole ordeal. He previously watched his mother be taken by the Russian government, too.

Now, ICE is telling Snegir that he can self-deport back to Russia, or they'll deport him anyway, without his son. They claimed he may never see his son again.

Snegir relented, but the following day, he was approved under the protection screening, which means ICE can deport him, but he can't be sent to Russia.

This week, the administration also published its guidance on birthright citizenship, which will allow ICE to enter maternity wards and demand papers from families after their infants are born. If the parents can't prove their citizenship, the government can take the newborn away from its parents and deport it to whatever country it wants, one legal analyst described.

Our Analysis:

The Cruel Chess Game: Trump's Administration and the Pawn of Human Rights

In a move that reeks of authoritarian overreach and a blatant disregard for human compassion, President Donald Trump's administration is making headlines for its ruthless attempts to deport Pavel Snegir, a Russian man who sought asylum in the United States after his wife was imprisoned for her political beliefs. This case is not just a bureaucratic misstep; it's a chilling illustration of how the mechanics of power can be manipulated to crush the spirit of those seeking refuge.

The Asylum Screening: A Hollow Promise

Pavel Snegir and his 11-year-old son, Aleksandr, underwent the rigorous U.S. asylum screening process, a procedure that ostensibly serves to protect those at risk. They passed, confirming the dire predictions that Snegir would likely face torture if returned to Russia. Yet, despite this clear danger, the Trump administration is hell-bent on deporting him. The irony is as thick as it is tragic: the very system that acknowledged his peril is now orchestrating his potential demise.

The Sinister Twist: Separation as a Tool of Coercion

In a move that could only be conceived by those with a profound deficit of empathy, the U.S. government has resorted to using Snegir’s son as leverage, essentially holding the child hostage to force Snegir's compliance with his own deportation. When Snegir was understandably terrified to board a plane to what he believed was his forced return to Russia, ICE officials coldly separated him from his son, employing threats and physical force. This is not law enforcement; it's psychological warfare against the vulnerable.

The ICE Threat: A Bargain with the Devil

ICE has presented Snegir with a grotesque ultimatum: self-deport back to Russia and face the dire consequences he fled from, or be deported without his son, with the ominous warning that they may never be reunited. This is not just a violation of human rights; it's a calculated terror tactic aimed at those who have already endured unimaginable fear and loss.

The Policy of Fear: Birthright Citizenship Under Siege

In a broader stroke of cruelty, the Trump administration’s recent guidance on birthright citizenship reveals a sinister plan to invade maternity wards, demanding papers from new parents. If unable to prove citizenship, their newborns could be snatched away and deported. This policy is not about immigration reform; it's about instilling fear and controlling bodies, a tactic ripped straight from the most oppressive regimes in history.

Conclusion: A Government's Moral Collapse

The case of Pavel Snegir is not an anomaly but a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend under the Trump administration. It reveals a government willing to exploit and exacerbate human suffering to advance its political agenda. This is not about border security or the rule of law; it's about using power to terrorize and dehumanize. We must ask ourselves, at what point does the pursuit of policy objectives justify a complete abandonment of morality and human decency? For the Trump administration, it seems, that line has long been crossed.
