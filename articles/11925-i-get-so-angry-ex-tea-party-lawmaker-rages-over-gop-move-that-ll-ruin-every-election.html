'I get so angry': Ex-Tea Party lawmaker rages over GOP move that'll ruin 'every election'

By Paulo Santos

Congressman Joe Walsh of Illinois speaking at CPAC 2011 in Washington, D.C. (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

Texas Republicans aren't just redistricting. They are doing so in a dangerous and unprecedented way, according to a former Tea Party Republican lawmaker.

Former GOP Congressman Joe Walsh, who recently accused Donald Trump's administration of a massive coverup, published an article called, America, Go Back to Civics Class, Please, in which he argues there isn't enough outrage about the GOP's new gerrymandering scheme.

"I’ve got to get something off my chest," he said, "and this is really directed at the American people."

He continued:

"I love you, American people, but we’ve got to be f------ better informed and better educated about our politics and civics in this country. Man, we stopped teaching American government and civics so damn long ago. We ought to be teaching it in our schools every single day."

Walsh went on to say, "This whole Texas redistricting/gerrymandering thing? Pay attention. I get so angry when people don’t understand what is so unprecedented about what Texas is trying to do."

According to the former lawmaker, the situation now in Texas is far different than any other redistricting.

"Yes, gerrymandering is wrong, but it happens. And both sides do it in whatever states they control. But it’s done every 10 years. It’s done at the end of every decade. It’s done with each and every census we have at the end of every decade. Every 10 years, we redistrict, and Republicans gerrymander, and Democrats gerrymander," he wrote on Substack. "What Texas is doing is different."

He continued, adding that, "They’re proposing mid-decade redistricting, not waiting until 2030. We just did this in 2020. The next redistricting is in 2030 and then 2040. Texas is saying, 'Screw that. I’m gonna redistrict now. I’m not waiting until 2030.'"

He then asked, "Do you understand how dangerous and unprecedented this is?"

"Because this will then make it possible for any state, and any party in any state, to say, 'The hell with it. I’m gonna redistrict this year, before this election'," the now-Democrat wrote. "We will get states gerrymandering before every single election in this country. Representatives will not know what districts they represent. People will not know who their representatives are. This will f--- up every single election in this country."

Our Analysis:

Texas Republicans: A New Frontier in Gerrymandering

In what can only be described as a bold maneuver, Texas Republicans are taking the art of gerrymandering to a whole new level—and not in a good way. According to Joe Walsh, a former Tea Party Republican lawmaker and a vocal critic of the Trump administration, the Texas GOP's latest redistricting efforts are not just controversial; they are unprecedentedly dangerous.

The Unprecedented Nature of Mid-Decade Redistricting

Walsh's primary contention—and it's a significant one—is that Texas is attempting to undertake redistricting mid-decade. This is not the usual, somewhat begrudgingly accepted, once-a-decade affair that follows the census. No, this is a blatant attempt to redraw the political map just two years after the last redistricting, completely disregarding the traditional census-based schedule.

"Screw that. I’m gonna redistrict now," seems to be the Texas GOP's battle cry, according to Walsh. This isn't just moving the goalposts; it's playing an entirely different game. The implications of such actions are far-reaching. Should Texas succeed, it opens the door for any state, regardless of the ruling party, to undertake redistricting whenever it suits them. This, as Walsh rightly points out, is a recipe for chaos.

The Dangers of Constant Redistricting

Imagine, if you will, a scenario where states decide to redraw their political maps before every single election. Representatives would be in a constant state of uncertainty about which districts they actually represent. Voters, on the other hand, would be equally confused about who their representatives are. The very foundation of representative democracy—the connection between elected officials and their constituents—would be severely undermined.

Walsh's warning is clear: this move by the Texas GOP could "f--- up every single election in this country." And it's hard to argue with his logic. The stability and predictability of the electoral process are crucial for the health of any democracy. Introducing such a level of volatility and manipulation could erode public trust in the electoral process, leading to disillusionment and disengagement from the political process.

A Call to Civics

Perhaps Walsh's most poignant point is his lamentation of the American public's lack of political and civic education. "I love you, American people, but we’ve got to be f------ better informed and better educated about our politics and civics in this country," he pleads. It's a fair critique. The complexities of redistricting and gerrymandering are lost on many, not due to apathy, but due to a lack of understanding.

His call for a return to robust civics education is not just about understanding what gerrymandering is, but about grasping the profound impact it has on democracy. Knowledge is power, and in the fight against political manipulation, it's our most potent weapon.

In Conclusion

Joe Walsh's critique of the Texas GOP's redistricting efforts is a wake-up call. It's a reminder of the lengths to which some will go to secure political power, and the importance of staying informed, engaged, and ready to defend the principles of democracy. Let Walsh's words be a rallying cry for all who care about the integrity of our elections. The battle for fair representation is ongoing, and ignorance is a luxury we can ill afford.
