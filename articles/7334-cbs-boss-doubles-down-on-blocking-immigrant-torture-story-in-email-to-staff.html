CBS boss doubles down on blocking immigrant torture story in email to staff

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 25, 20255 min read

Bari Weiss (Photo via Michael Blake for Reuters)

CBS News’ newly-appointed right-wing chief, Bari Weiss, doubled down in an email to staff on her decision to delay a “60 Minutes” investigation into the way migrants President Donald Trump has sent to the Salvadoran CECOT megaprison are being tortured, some without having any criminal record or violation of the law whatsoever.

Weiss, who founded The Free Press, was given control of CBS as part of a Trump administration-approved merger between the network’s parent company, Paramount, and Skydance.

“Right now, the majority of Americans say they do not trust the press. It isn’t because they’re crazy,” wrote Weiss in the email, obtained by Prem Thakker of Zeteo News. “To win back their trust, we have to work hard. Sometimes that means doing more legwork. Sometimes it means telling unexpected stories. Sometimes it means training our attention on topics that have been overlooked or misconstrued. And sometimes it means holding a piece about an important subject to make sure it is comprehensive and fair. In our upside-down moment, this may seem radical.”

“Such editorial decisions can cause a firestorm, particularly on a slow news week,” Weiss continued. “And the standards for fairness we are holding ourselves to, particularly on contentious subjects, will surely feel controversial to those used to doing things one way. But to fulfill our mission, it’s necessary. No amount of outrage — whether from activist organizations or the White House — will derail us. We are not out to score points with one side of the political spectrum or to win followers on social media. We are out to inform the American public and to get the story right.”

The email triggered a fresh wave of outrage on social media.

“Is that what Stephen Miller told her to say?” wrote attorney Brad Moss. “Bari has no educational background in journalism, nor has she ever done hard-hitting news beats. But ok,” wrote Georgia-based constitutional law professor Anthony Michael Kreis.

Despite Weiss’s efforts to keep the “60 Minutes” story under wraps, it has been leaked and posted online, after a Canadian broadcaster aired it apparently by mistake.

Our Analysis:

The Weiss Controversy: A Deep Dive into CBS News’ Questionable Editorial Decisions

In what can only be described as a bold move, CBS News’ newly-appointed chief, Bari Weiss, has ignited a firestorm of controversy. Her decision to delay a critical “60 Minutes” investigation into the harrowing conditions faced by migrants sent to the Salvadoran CECOT megaprison by the Trump administration is not just a mere editorial call; it’s a glaring testament to the problematic entanglement of media, politics, and the quest for ‘fairness.’

Weiss, a figure with a notable background for founding The Free Press but lacking in traditional journalistic experience, steps into her role amid a merger approved by the very administration her network now seemingly seeks to protect. This isn’t just a red flag; it’s a blaring siren warning of the risks of political interference in media integrity.

The Dubious Defense

Weiss’s defense, as outlined in her email to staff, is a masterclass in obfuscation. She argues that the delay is in pursuit of a “comprehensive and fair” story, a noble goal if it weren’t for the context. The investigation in question concerns allegations of torture — a subject that demands urgent public discourse, not delayed scrutiny under the guise of thoroughness.

Her email touts the need to rebuild trust in the press, a commendable aim indeed. Yet, how does withholding explosive revelations about human rights abuses align with such a mission? If anything, it suggests a disturbing prioritization of the network’s image over the imperative of informing the public about governmental overreach and atrocities.

The Reaction: A Litmus Test for Media Integrity

The backlash from the journalistic community and the public has been swift and severe. Critics, including attorney Brad Moss and constitutional law professor Anthony Michael Kreis, point out Weiss’s lack of traditional journalistic credentials and experience as particularly troubling given the gravity of the editorial decisions she’s making. This criticism isn’t just ad hominem attacks but a reflection of the broader concerns about the suitability and motivations behind Weiss’s leadership in a storied news organization.

The leak and subsequent airing of the investigation by a Canadian broadcaster reveal not only the newsworthy nature of the report but also the apparent underestimation by CBS and Weiss of the story’s significance and urgency. This episode serves as a damning indictment of the current state of editorial judgment at CBS News, suggesting a departure from journalistic instinct towards political calculus.

The Bigger Picture

What this debacle lays bare is the intricate dance between media, politics, and the public’s right to know. When media outlets, especially those with the reach and influence of CBS News, falter in their duty to hold power to account, it erodes the very foundation of democracy. The decision by Weiss and, by extension, CBS, to delay the “60 Minutes” investigation, is not merely an isolated case of poor judgment. It is indicative of a systemic error within the media landscape — a willingness to compromise on principles of immediacy and transparency for political and corporate considerations.

In the end, the saga of the delayed investigation is a stark reminder of the challenges and responsibilities facing the press in an era of dwindling public trust and rising authoritarianism. It’s a call to action for media outlets to recommit to the principles of journalism: to inform, to expose, and to hold the powerful to account, without fear or favor. Anything less is a disservice to the public and a dereliction of duty.
