Insider reveals Trump's 'absurd' Kennedy Center plan — that aides scrambled to kill

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 7, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Audacity of Ego: Trump’s Attempt to Erase JFK

Donald Trump, Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts (Photos by Yuri Gripas, Elizabeth Frantz for Reuters)

President Donald Trump’s advisers stopped him from ripping John F. Kennedy’s name off the Kennedy Center altogether, leaving the cultural hub simply called the Trump Center, his biographer said.

“Why does this have to be Kennedy?” the president apparently argued. “That was such a long time ago.”

Trump, 79, has substantially remade the Washington, D.C. performing arts institution in his image, taking control of its board, renaming it “The Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts” without congressional approval, and announcing plans to close it for extensive renovations.

But writer Michael Wolff, who spent months inside the first Trump White House, told the Daily Beast that Trump’s original proposal was far more ambitious. Wolff stated, “His first idea was to call this the Trump Center.”

“I was almost assassinated,” he said, according to Wolff. “Therefore, it should be me.” Trump experienced two assassination attempts in 2024.

Trump’s inner circle was able to persuade him away from the plan. Wolff said, “You can’t say, ‘This is a terrible idea. This is a megalomaniacal idea. This is not good politics.’ You just cannot say any of that stuff to Trump. So instead, they said, ‘Well, why don’t we call it the Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Center?’”

“It’s a perfect Trump setup. He goes for the absurd, and he settles for the outlandish.”

On December 18, Trump’s handpicked board voted to rename the center. Trump claimed he was “surprised” by the board’s vote, though he controlled its composition. While congressional action is required for formal changes, the center added Trump’s name to its signage and updated its website to read “The Trump Kennedy Center.”

The center has seen plunging ticket sales and cancelled performances since Trump’s takeover. Last weekend, the president announced it would close for two years, claiming it was for renovations.

Our Analysis:

The Audacity of Ego: Trump’s Attempt to Erase JFK

In a move that reads like a chapter ripped from a dystopian novel, former President Donald Trump once aspired to obliterate John F. Kennedy’s name from the iconic Kennedy Center, ambitiously seeking to rebrand it as the “Trump Center.” This revelation, courtesy of Michael Wolff’s latest dive into the Trumpian saga, is not merely a tale of egotistical bravado but a glaring emblem of Trump’s relentless pursuit to stamp his identity on the nation’s cultural and historical tapestry.

Why JFK, Indeed?

Trump’s rhetorical question, “Why does this have to be Kennedy?” not only underscores his profound disconnect with the nation’s history but also his insatiable appetite for self-aggrandizement. Kennedy, a figure who represents an era of hope, ambition, and a striving for societal progress, is relegated to a mere historical footnote in Trump’s revisionist agenda. The suggestion that JFK’s legacy is somehow outdated or irrelevant is not just an affront to the memory of a pivotal American leader but a display of Trump’s chronic narcissism.

The Art of the Absurd

Trump’s initial ambition to entirely rename the Kennedy Center in his own image, without a nod to Kennedy, borders on the megalomaniacal. His justification, citing near-assassination as a credential, is both absurd and alarming. The rationale that personal misfortune somehow equates to, or even surpasses, Kennedy’s enduring legacy is a twisted logic that only Trump could espouse. It’s a stark reminder of his tenure’s hallmarks: distortion and self-veneration above all else.

A Compromise of the Outlandish

The eventual renaming to “The Donald J. Trump and John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts,” though a step back from the brink, still reeks of Trump’s egocentricity. Wolff’s commentary that Trump’s advisers couldn’t outright condemn his idea but had to navigate around his ego is a testament to the dysfunctional advisory dynamics that plagued his administration. This “compromise” still elevates Trump to a stature he believes is on par with Kennedy, a delusion fueled by yes-men and sycophants.

The Fallout

Unsurprisingly, the Trump rebranding has had tangible negative impacts. The plummeting ticket sales and canceled performances are not mere coincidences but a direct reflection of the public’s aversion to Trump’s invasive branding. The decision to close the center for two years under the guise of renovations further alienates an already disillusioned audience. It’s a classic Trump maneuver: disrupt, rename, and claim victory amidst the ruins.

A Legacy Hijacked

Trump’s attempt to hijack the Kennedy Center’s legacy is a microcosm of his broader assault on American norms and institutions. It’s not just about renaming a building; it’s about reshaping history in his image. The audacity of such an endeavor speaks volumes about Trump’s character, revealing a man more concerned with his legacy than the cultural and historical significance of the institutions he seeks to commandeer.

In the end, this episode is another glaring example of Trump’s unbridled ego attempting to leave an indelible mark on the fabric of American society. It’s a stark reminder of the dangers posed by leaders who prioritize personal legacy over collective memory and cultural heritage.
