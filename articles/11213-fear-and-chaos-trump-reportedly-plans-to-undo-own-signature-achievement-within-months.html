'Fear and chaos': Trump reportedly plans to undo own signature achievement 'within months'

The Trump administration will pull the COVID vaccine off the market "within months," one of Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s closest associates told The Daily Beast.

“It could [happen] in a number of stages, including learning more about the data,” said Dr. Aseem Malhotra, a British cardiologist who claims the vaccines are more dangerous than the coronavirus.

Malhotra told the website that Kennedy's position on the vaccines was shared by “influential” members of the Trump family, and he claims Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) is reviewing so-called "vaccine injuries."

“Given the increased talk of vaccine injuries in the past few weeks among the administration," he said, "it could also come with one clean decision.”

The vaccine skeptic told The Daily Beast that Kennedy's closest advisers have said they "cannot understand" why the shots continue to be prescribed, and he said the Health and Human Services secretary would likely recommend removing it from the U.S. market even if the decision would likely cause "fear of chaos" and legal challenges.

Kennedy's skepticism about the shots is based on a 2022 paper that appeared in the journal Vaccine that found patients given the mRNA vaccines had a 16 percent higher risk of “excess serious adverse events” than those given a placebo, but that study has been widely dismissed within the medical community as underestimating the benefits and overstating risks due to biased data selection and other methodological flaws.

Malhotra did not take part in that study but presented its findings July 9 to controversial lobby group Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) Action, which he said triggered a "holy s---" reaction among Kennedy's senior aides in attendance.

Kennedy has already canceled $500 million in funding for mRNA vaccine research, and Malhotra says he will meet next month with the president, who authorized the Operation Warp Speed public-private partnership in May 2020 to develop a vaccine to protect against SARS-CoV-2 during the COVID-19 pandemic.

“I think [Trump will] get it, because it’s in his interest to,” Malhotra said. “This information is only going in one direction, and there’s something really terribly wrong with the system. I think once [he] understands the situation... that will encourage him to act to change the system. He’s in a position to fix it. He can create a lasting legacy by doing so.”

More than 750 current and former staff members from the HHS, Centers for Disease Control (CDC) and National Institutes of Health (NIH) called out Kennedy last week for spreading disinformation about vaccines, saying his comments and actions undermined public health.

“Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., is complicit in dismantling America’s public health infrastructure and endangering the nation’s health by repeatedly spreading inaccurate health information," they wrote in a letter to Congress.

The Daily Beast contacted the White House, the HHS, Pfizer, and Moderna for comment on Malhotra claims about Kennedy's plans to pull the vaccine from the U.S. market, but only the White House responded.

“The Administration is relying on Gold Standard Science and is committed to radical transparency to make decisions that affect all Americans. Unless announced by the Administration, however, any discussion about HHS policy should be dismissed as baseless speculation," said White House spokesman Kush Desai.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Potential Pullback of COVID Vaccines: A Tug of War Between Politics and Science

In a move that could only be described as audaciously controversial, President Donald Trump is reportedly considering the rollback of one of his hallmark achievements: the accelerated development and deployment of COVID-19 vaccines under Operation Warp Speed. Based on a report from The Daily Beast, this initiative is being spearheaded by none other than Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a figure who has long been embroiled in vaccine skepticism, alongside his associate, Dr. Aseem Malhotra—a cardiologist with a penchant for challenging the vaccine's safety profile.

The Claims and the Skeptics

Dr. Malhotra, citing supposed concerns over "vaccine injuries", suggests that influential members within the Trump administration, including those from the Trump family itself, are considering the drastic step of withdrawing the COVID vaccine from the U.S. market. This consideration is allegedly based on a study published in the journal Vaccine, which claims a 16 percent increase in "excess serious adverse events" in those receiving mRNA vaccines compared to a placebo. However, this study has not sailed smoothly in scientific circles, facing criticism for methodological flaws and a biased approach towards data selection—a critical detail that seems to be glossed over by those championing the vaccine's market withdrawal.

The Reaction: A Blend of Disbelief and Alarm

The response from the scientific community and over 750 health professionals, including staff from HHS, CDC, and NIH, has been one of disbelief and concern. Their collective outcry against Kennedy's stance on vaccines underscores a worrisome trend of politicizing public health issues. The letter to Congress, calling out Kennedy for disseminating disinformation, highlights a growing chasm between evidence-based science and political agenda—a chasm that threatens the very foundation of public health infrastructure.

The White House's Stance: A Beacon of Rationality?

In a sea of speculative and potentially damaging rhetoric, the White House's response shines as a beacon of rationality. The commitment to "Gold Standard Science" and "radical transparency" as mentioned by White House spokesman Kush Desai, underscores an essential principle: public health decisions should be anchored in robust scientific evidence and clear communication, not swayed by political winds or unfounded claims.

The Bigger Picture: A Legacy at Stake

The narrative woven around the potential withdrawal of COVID vaccines is not just about public health; it's a reflection of a broader struggle between science and misinformation, public welfare and political maneuvering. If President Trump indeed moves forward with this plan, influenced by Kennedy and Malhotra's assertions, the ramifications could be far-reaching. Beyond the immediate public health impact, such a decision would cast a long shadow over Trump's legacy, potentially positioning him as a leader who, in the face of a global pandemic, chose to sideline scientific consensus for political and personal gain.

Conclusion: Navigating the Crossroads

As we stand at this critical crossroads, it's imperative to remember the lessons of the past. The development and distribution of COVID vaccines represented a monumental leap forward in combating a pandemic that has claimed millions of lives worldwide. To roll back this achievement on the basis of disputable claims and political calculations would not only undermine the fight against COVID-19 but also erode trust in public health institutions and the very essence of scientific inquiry.

In the end, the decision to pull COVID vaccines from the market should not be a political chess move but a conclusion arrived at through rigorous scientific evaluation and transparent public discourse. Anything less would be a disservice to the principles of public health and an affront to the individuals and families affected by the pandemic.
