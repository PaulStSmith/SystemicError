Epstein investigation 'just getting started' despite Trump trying to move on: Dem lawmaker

The investigation and release of Jeffrey Epstein’s files is only just beginning according to a Democratic Party representative.

Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) says the prolonged release of the files warrants further investigation, despite Donald Trump calling for people to focus on other political matters. The president slammed the release of the files and called for the public and press to move on from discussing them in a presser in the Oval Office yesterday (February 4).

He said, “Nothing came out about me other than it was a conspiracy against me, literally, by Epstein and other people. But I think it’s time now for the country to maybe get on to something else like health care or something that people care about.”

Garcia disagrees and believes there is much more to come from the files. Speaking to Politico, he said, “I’ve said from day one that this has been a White House cover-up. That cover-up continues, and the difference between the DOJ’s position and ours is that the DOJ thinks this investigation is over. We think it’s just getting started.

“We have so much work to do ahead of us, and the fact that only 50 percent of the files have been released is not just against the law, it violates a subpoena that we’ve had in place since the summer. Of course, it’s been really harmful to the survivors, as they have said themselves.”

Garcia went on to claim the Department of Justice cannot be trusted with the handling of the files. Trump admin representatives claimed there is not enough information in the files to lend them to criminal conviction cases.

The Californian Dem Rep said, “What’s interesting has been the DOJ’s position that there’s not enough information for criminal convictions. As far as I’m concerned, the DOJ cannot be trusted. It’s corrupt. That’s just been the case from day one.

“I think it’s also true that the White House has been trying to hide the release of the files. Anyone can just look at the timeline. You campaign on releasing the files, then you’re president, you try to ignore them.

“Then your DOJ and your attorney general say, ‘I have them ready to be released.’ Then they release [something] to a bunch of influencers, which is really nothing. Then they say there’s nothing to release. In fact, the case is closed.”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files Fiasco: A Tale of Obfuscation and Political Maneuvering

In the labyrinthine corridors of power, the Epstein files have become a symbol of much more than the sordid secrets of the rich and powerful; they have morphed into a battleground for political narratives, with truth seemingly buried under layers of strategic obfuscation and outright denial. The recent comments by Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) and the dismissive retorts from President Donald Trump illuminate a glaring chasm between the pursuit of transparency and the inclination towards political convenience.

The Unending Saga

First, let’s dissect the assertion by Rep. Garcia that the investigation into Jeffrey Epstein’s files is “just getting started.” This statement alone underscores a fundamental and disturbing reality: the quest for accountability and justice in the Epstein saga is far from over. Despite the gravity of the accusations and the potential implications for numerous high-profile individuals, we find ourselves wading through a swamp of political posturing rather than making strides toward clarity and resolution.

Trump’s Call for Amnesia

The president’s plea for the country to “move on” to issues like healthcare, while dismissing the Epstein files as a “conspiracy against me,” is a masterclass in deflection. It’s a convenient tactic—redirecting public attention to more palatable topics rather than confronting the uncomfortable questions that the Epstein case raises about the intersections of wealth, power, and criminal justice.

The Allegations of a Cover-up

Rep. Garcia’s charge that the handling of the Epstein files represents a “White House cover-up” is a serious accusation. It’s one thing to argue over policy or political philosophy; it’s another to suggest that the highest office in the land is actively working to obscure the truth about potentially criminal activities. If only 50 percent of the files have been released, in violation of a subpoena, one must ask: what exactly is being concealed? And why?

The DOJ’s Dubious Stance

Garcia’s mistrust of the Department of Justice (DOJ) and his claim that it is “corrupt” strike at the heart of a deeply unsettling possibility: that the very institutions meant to uphold justice are instead prioritizing political allegiance over legal and moral obligations. The suggestion that the DOJ does not find sufficient information in the files for criminal convictions, juxtaposed with the insistence on the files’ incomplete release, raises more questions than answers. Is the issue truly a lack of evidence, or is it a reluctance to pursue cases that might ensnare the politically powerful?

A Pattern of Evasion

The timeline laid out by Garcia, from campaign promises to a series of half-releases and claims of “nothing to release,” paints a picture of deliberate evasion. It’s a narrative that, whether true or not, suggests a concerted effort to manage the fallout from the Epstein files rather than confront their contents head-on.

Conclusion: The Quest for Truth

In the end, the saga of the Epstein files is not just about the salacious details of one man’s crimes. It’s a litmus test for the integrity of our political and justice systems. As Rep. Garcia and President Trump spar over the path forward, the real losers are the survivors of Epstein’s alleged crimes and the public, whose faith in their leaders and institutions continues to erode.

The call to “get on to something else” is not just a diversion; it’s an insult to the notion that truth and justice should prevail over political convenience. The investigation into the Epstein files must not only continue but do so with a commitment to transparency, accountability, and, above all, the pursuit of justice, no matter where it leads.
