'The truth is obvious': NY Times obliterates Trump in no holds barred economy attack

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Broken Promise: A Tale of Economic Misadventure

Donald Trump gestures to reporters from the roof of the White House. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

When Donald Trump ran for president, he made a bold promise: “From the day I take the oath of office, we’ll rapidly drive prices down and make America affordable again. Prices will come down. You just watch. They’ll come down fast.”

But 10 months into his presidency, prices have stubbornly remained high.

On Friday, the New York Times had had enough. In a brutal column, the storied newspaper’s Editorial Board let fly.

“The president’s failure was predictable,” the editors wrote. “Prices almost never fall across the entire economy. When they do, it tends to be because of a crisis, such as the Great Depression.

“Mr. Trump is responsible for misleading the American people. But he has not merely failed to keep an impossible promise. Mr. Trump has pursued policies, starting with tariffs, that are making the problem worse by making life in America less affordable.”

To back up its attack, the newspaper’s board quoted economist Claudia Sahm. “In some ways, this administration could have come in and done nothing and made progress on inflation. But the policies they’ve chosen have created extra costs for businesses, and businesses tend to pass on costs.”

Trump’s approach to economic challenges has been notably tone-deaf, the newspaper’s column stated.

Earlier this year, he dismissively suggested Americans could handle tariffs by buying fewer toys, quipping, “Well, maybe the children will have two dolls instead of 30 dolls, you know? And maybe the two dolls will cost a couple of bucks more than they would normally.”

After recent Democratic electoral victories focused on affordability, Trump changed his tune entirely. He boldly claimed, “Every price is down,” despite clear evidence to the contrary. The White House even went so far as to declare that “inflation has been tamed” and “everyday prices are beginning to drop” - statements that are flatly untrue.

Goldman Sachs estimates that the share of tariff costs borne by consumers has dramatically increased from 22 percent in April to 55 percent in October, with projections suggesting this will rise to 67 percent by mid-next year.

The Yale Budget Lab estimates that the current tariffs will cost the average U.S. household about $1,800 per year - a significant burden for many families already struggling with rising prices.

“The truth is obvious to people who pay their own bills or shop for their own groceries,” the editors wrote.

The article concluded, “The president recently shared a photo of a White House bathroom that he had renovated by covering everything in marble.

“What Americans need is a president who is more focused on the cost of toilet paper. It’s up more than 3 percent so far this year.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Broken Promise: A Tale of Economic Misadventure

When Donald Trump vaulted onto the political stage with the audacity of a carnival barker, he made a bold promise that echoed in the ears of hopeful Americans: a swift, unfaltering reduction in prices to make America affordable again. Yet, here we are, 10 months into his presidency, witnessing the stark gap between grandiose promises and the harsh reality. The New York Times, in a brutal column, didn’t mince words when it highlighted this chasm, and neither will we.

Trump’s Economic Folly: Tariffs and Their Toll

The crux of Trump’s failure isn’t just rooted in his inability to fulfill an ostensibly impossible promise, but in the active deterioration of affordability through misguided policies. Starting with tariffs, Trump’s administration has inadvertently cranked up the cost of living. As Claudia Sahm eloquently put it, Trump’s policies have “created extra costs for businesses,” which, in turn, have been unceremoniously dumped onto consumers’ laps. This isn’t rocket science; it’s Economics 101: businesses facing higher costs will invariably pass these on to consumers.

Tone-Deaf and Out of Touch

Trump’s response to the economic challenges has been nothing short of tone-deaf. His suggestion that Americans could simply buy fewer toys epitomizes a gross misunderstanding (or perhaps a disdainful disregard) for the everyday struggles of the American people. The change in tune, post-Democratic electoral victories, where he claimed, “Every price is down,” only adds insult to injury. The audacity to declare inflation tamed, in the face of evidence to the contrary, is not just misleading; it’s a blatant falsehood.

The Cost of Tariffs: A Burden on American Households

Let’s talk numbers - because facts are stubborn things. The escalation of tariff costs borne by consumers, from 22 percent to a projected 67 percent, is not just alarming; it’s a direct hit to the wallets of American families. The Yale Budget Lab’s estimation that current tariffs will cost the average U.S. household about $1,800 per year is a testament to the tangible repercussions of Trump’s economic adventurism. It’s a hefty price tag for a policy that was supposed to make America affordable.

Marble Over Toilet Paper: A Misplaced Priority

The closing note of the article, highlighting Trump’s renovation of a White House bathroom in marble, is perhaps the most poignant symbol of misplaced priorities. In a world where the cost of toilet paper sees a significant hike, the focus on opulence over affordability is stark. It’s not just about the marble or the toilet paper; it’s about understanding the real issues that face everyday Americans.

Conclusion: The Reality of Trump’s America

Trump’s presidency, at least in the economic sense, has been a journey of misguided policies and broken promises. The administration’s approach to tackling economic challenges has not only fallen short but has actively made life more expensive for the average American. The truth, as the New York Times editorial board rightly points out, is “obvious to people who pay their own bills or shop for their own groceries.” In the end, Americans need a president who prioritizes their affordability over aesthetic renovations. It’s time to focus on the cost of living, not the cost of luxury.
