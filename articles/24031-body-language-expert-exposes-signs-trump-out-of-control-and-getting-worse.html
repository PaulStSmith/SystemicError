Body language expert exposes signs Trump 'out of control' and getting worse

A Spasm Is Not a Diagnosis—and Speculation Is Not Accountability

The Claim

Reporting on Donald Trump’s address to the 81st session of the United Nations centered on an interpretation by clinical and forensic psychologist John Paul Garrison. After reviewing video, Garrison described two movements as possible “seizure activity,” argued that Trump appeared to be undergoing neurological decline, and suggested a prior stroke as one possible explanation. The reporting provides no medical records, direct examination, diagnosis from Trump’s physicians, or independent corroboration.

Expertise Cannot Replace Evidence

Garrison may possess relevant clinical expertise, but analyzing edited or replayed public footage is not the same as examining a patient. His description of the movements is observable interpretation; his claims about focal motor seizures, a possible stroke, and worsening judgment are medical inferences.

The article blurs that distinction. Words such as “significant,” “alarming,” and “decline” create certainty that the evidence presented does not sustain. Even Garrison’s strongest conclusions remain his assessment, not an established diagnosis.

The Spectacle Displaces the Substance

The article briefly notes that Trump’s speech contained rhetoric others considered alarming, then abandons that politically assessable material for slow-motion scrutiny of his body.

That is a profound act of misdirection. A president’s statements can be quoted, policies examined, orders traced, and consequences measured. A twitch can be replayed indefinitely while proving very little. Turning political coverage into amateur neurology replaces accountable conduct with an irresistible but weakly grounded spectacle.

Power Is Exercised Through Decisions

Trump’s institutional power does not depend on what an outside psychologist believes a movement might signify. It rests in the presidency and is expressed through decisions, directives, appointments, and public commitments. Those are the proper objects of scrutiny.

If credible evidence ever establishes that a president cannot perform the office, that would carry obvious institutional consequences. This article does not establish that premise. It offers one commentator’s interpretation of video and treats the severity of his language as a substitute for verification.

The Accountability Gap

The story’s central failure is not that it raises a health question. Presidential capacity is politically relevant. The failure is that it supplies neither authoritative disclosure nor disciplined uncertainty.

This is how weak political journalism launders conjecture: an expert speculates, a headline hardens the speculation, and readers are invited to mistake repetition for confirmation. Meanwhile, the harder work—examining the president’s actual rhetoric and the government actions within his control—recedes from view.

A System Addicted to Diagnostic Theater

The larger pattern is a political media culture that prefers decoding bodies and personalities to mapping power. It turns visible gestures into purported revelations because spectacle is easier to package than institutional analysis.

That habit does not expose authority. It obscures it. Political accountability begins with what officials demonstrably say, decide, enable, and impose—not with an unsupported diagnosis extracted from a few seconds of video.
