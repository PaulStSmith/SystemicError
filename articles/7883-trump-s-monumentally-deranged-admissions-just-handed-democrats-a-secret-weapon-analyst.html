President Donald Trump made a stunning admission about his pardon of Honduran former President Juan Orlando Hernandez that undercuts his rationale for bombing alleged drug smuggling boats.

The president told Politico’s Dasha Burns in a new interview that he knew “very little about him” before issuing a pardon for his conviction in a U.S. court for drug trafficking, which Trump claimed was “an Obama-Biden type setup,” and The New Republic’s Greg Sargent said the admission “handed Democrats a new weapon to hound Republicans mercilessly if they fail to exercise oversight over these ongoing crimes.”

“This is monumentally deranged stuff,” Sargent wrote. “Note that Trump admits he knows next to nothing about Hernandez’s case, even though prosecutors won a conviction after alleging that he helped bring more than 500 tons of cocaine into the United States. Trump pardoned him with zero serious reflection on the impact he had on drug use in our country. This is by Trump’s own admission.”

Burns asked whether the Hernandez pardon sent “the wrong message to drug dealers,” and Trump claimed the former Honduran president had been the victim of “weaponized” government, as he claims to be himself, and Sargent said that was also telling.

“Combine this with Trump’s confession that he granted this pardon because people ‘asked me to do it,’ and the takeaway is inescapable,” Sargent wrote. “He did it as a favor, probably corruptly, or out of his narcissistic association of Hernandez with himself, or a combination of both: His narcissism subjects him to easy manipulation by corrupt actors.”

U.S. troops have killed more than 80 people in the Caribbean Sea on Trump’s orders, but the administration has not presented serious evidence that any of them were smuggling drugs into the United States, and Trump told Bash he’s considering an invasion of Venezuela and a dramatic escalation of his bombing campaign by targeting Mexico and Colombia.

“Trump’s disastrous admissions in this interview reveal again that he is profoundly, catastrophically unfit to be making any of these decisions,” Sargent wrote. “By even the most minimal standard of public service, this should induce more Republicans to get to the bottom of all of it. Democrats should use these new revelations to hound them relentlessly if they refuse, as they surely will.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Pardon of Hernández: A Cocktail of Ignorance and Narcissism

In a recent revelation that could only be described as both shocking and yet, somehow, entirely predictable, President Donald Trump admitted to Politico’s Dasha Burns that his decision to pardon former Honduran President Juan Orlando Hernández was made with a stunning lack of information. Hernández, convicted in a U.S. court for drug trafficking, apparently received the golden ticket of a pardon from Trump, who confessed he knew “very little about him” before making this decision. This admission is not just a blip in Trump’s presidency; it’s a glaring showcase of his decision-making process, which seems to oscillate between uninformed whims and outright dangerous impulses.

The Dangerous Impulse: Undermining His Own Rationale

Trump’s rationale for military action against alleged drug smuggling operations is now laughably undercut by his own admission. The modus operandi of bombing alleged drug smuggling boats, which has led to the death of more than 80 people in the Caribbean Sea, is questionable at best. Trump’s lack of serious evidence linking these individuals directly to drug smuggling into the United States only adds to the absurdity. Yet, he contemplates not just continuing this folly but escalating it to include potential invasions of Venezuela and bombings in Mexico and Colombia. This is not just reckless; it’s a terrifying glimpse into the thought process of a man once at the helm of the most powerful country in the world.

A Narcissistic Favor?

Greg Sargent of The New Republic points out the disturbing implications of Trump’s actions and justifications. The pardon, granted because people “asked me to do it,” alongside Trump’s delusional comparison of Hernández’s situation to his own perceived victimhood, paints a picture of a President easily swayed by flattery or shared delusions of persecution. This is not leadership; it’s a dangerous blend of narcissism and gullibility that has tangible, harmful impacts on real lives and international relations.

A Call to Action for Both Parties

This entire debacle should serve as a wake-up call, not just to Democrats but to Republicans as well. The admission that Trump pardoned a convicted drug trafficker with little to no understanding of the case is alarming. It’s not just a political blunder; it’s a glaring red flag about the decision-making process at the highest levels of government. Democrats are rightly urged to use these revelations to demand accountability, but this should transcend party lines. The issue at hand is not just political; it’s about the safety, integrity, and reputation of the United States on the global stage.

Conclusion: The Unfit Leader

Trump’s admissions in this interview should not just concern us; they should alarm anyone who cares about the integrity of the United States and the safety of its people. The pardon of Juan Orlando Hernández is a case study in how not to lead a nation, showcasing a blend of ignorance, narcissism, and recklessness that is unacceptable in any public servant, let alone a President. As we move forward, this incident should not be forgotten or brushed aside. It must serve as a reminder of the dangers of placing individuals who lack the requisite knowledge, morality, and reflection in positions of power. The cost is simply too high.
