Election chaos is already costing states millions —and it's only May

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 15, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A Calculated Chaos: How Political Power Plays Disrupt U.S. Elections

A Calculated Chaos: How Political Power Plays Disrupt U.S. Elections

Late-Stage Redistricting: A Strategic Power Grab

In a blatant maneuver of power, state governors and lawmakers are redrawing electoral maps mid-election cycle, throwing the primary elections into turmoil. Take Louisiana, where Governor Jeff Landry, wielding his executive authority, suspended a congressional primary already in progress, effectively disenfranchising the 42,000 voters who had already cast absentee ballots. This action, while framed as a response to a Supreme Court decision, reeks of strategic electoral manipulation. The decision to redraw districts at such a critical juncture serves primarily to confuse voters and maintain political control, costing the state millions in unplanned expenses.

The Cost of Confusion

The financial repercussions of these redistricting efforts are staggering. Louisiana’s decision alone has set the stage for an additional, costly primary, with estimates around $8 million. In Florida, updating voters on their new districts is expected to drain hundreds of thousands from state coffers. Alabama’s plea to revert to an older congressional map hints at a chaotic, costly electoral landscape, with a projected expense of $4.5 million. This pattern of fiscal irresponsibility underlines a broader strategy: spend freely to secure power, with taxpayers footing the bill.

Voter Disenfranchisement by Design

The redistricting chaos serves as a smokescreen for a more insidious agenda: voter disenfranchisement. In Tennessee, for example, a new law eliminates the requirement for counties to notify voters by mail of changes in their polling places or precincts. This decision, ostensibly a cost-saving measure, shifts the burden of staying informed onto voters themselves, potentially reducing voter turnout due to confusion or lack of information. Virginia’s recent overturning of a voter-approved congressional map further illustrates a disturbing trend of disregarding voter intent in favor of political expediency.

Misdirection and the Trump Mail Voting Order

Amidst the redistricting chaos, another disruption looms: President Donald Trump’s executive order granting the U.S. Postal Service unprecedented control over mail ballots. This move, currently mired in legal challenges and governmental confusion, adds another layer of uncertainty to the electoral process. The Department of Justice’s stance that the lawsuits against this order are “premature” because the federal agencies haven’t started implementing the order yet is a clear deflection from addressing the potential for significant electoral impact.

The Broader Pattern: Manipulation and Control

These developments are not isolated incidents but part of a larger pattern of electoral manipulation by those in power. The strategic timing of redistricting, the unnecessary complexities introduced into the voting process, and the imposition of potentially disruptive executive orders are all tactics designed to maintain control by sowing confusion and suppressing voter turnout. What’s particularly alarming is the brazen nature of these tactics, deployed even as primaries are underway and despite clear adverse impacts on voter participation and state finances.

Toward a More Transparent Election System

The ongoing manipulation of electoral rules highlights an urgent need for transparency and accountability in election administration. Voters deserve a system that respects their choices and provides stability and clarity, not one manipulated by those in power to serve partisan goals. As we move closer to the 2026 midterms, it is critical for media, civic groups, and voters themselves to remain vigilant and push back against these disruptive tactics. The integrity of U.S. elections depends on it.
