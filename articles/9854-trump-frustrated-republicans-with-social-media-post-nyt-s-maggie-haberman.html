Trump 'frustrated' Republicans with social media post: NYT's Maggie Haberman

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Underlying Frustration: Unraveling Trump’s Latest Social Media Fiasco

CNN screenshot

Some Republicans are quietly expressing their “frustration” with President Donald Trump over a social media post, according to the New York Times’ Maggie Haberman.

Early Thursday morning, Trump posted on Truth Social that he met with Russ Vought, leader of the Office of Management and Budget, who he described as being “of Project 2025 fame.” Trump has tried to distance himself from the plan during the campaign trail, despite appointing multiple people who authored chapters to his administration.

Haberman discussed the impact of Trump’s social media post on Republicans during an interview on CNN’s “The Source” with Kaitlan Collins.

“I will say that even some republicans were quietly frustrated by President Trump’s social media post highlighting Russ Vought’s connection to Project 2025, even though the notion that the president had no idea what Project 2025 was during the campaign was absurd, or that he didn’t know the people who were involved with it was absurd,” Haberman said.

“It was not actually his campaign’s platform, but there was overlap between his campaign’s advisers or some of their advisers and the people who worked on Project 2025,” she continued. “It was also an unpopular agenda item in a lot of polling, just objectively unpopular. And that was part of the reason why you saw the trump campaign trying to distance him from it.”

“Now he’s just openly talking about it, and it is just providing fresh fodder for democrats who the white house had been trying to blame for the fact that the shutdown exists in the first place,” she continued.

Our Analysis:

The Underlying Frustration: Unraveling Trump’s Latest Social Media Fiasco

In the ever-turbulent sea of American politics, former President Donald Trump has once again stirred the waters with a social media post that has left some Republicans quietly seething. According to Maggie Haberman of the New York Times, Trump’s early morning musings on Truth Social about meeting with Russ Vought, the figurehead behind the contentious Project 2025, have sparked not just frustration but a deeper examination of the ties that bind Trump to the very policies he attempted to disavow on the campaign trail.

The Absurdity of Denial

Let’s cut through the political doublespeak: the notion that Trump was blissfully unaware of Project 2025’s existence or its architects during his campaign stretches the bounds of credulity to its breaking point. Trump’s administration was a revolving door for authors of Project 2025, making his attempts at distancing laughable at best. Haberman hits the nail on the head when she labels such denials as “absurd.” The cognitive dissonance required to believe Trump’s feigned ignorance would be comical if the stakes weren’t so high.

The Unpopular Truth

Project 2025 wasn’t just another policy proposal; it was, by all accounts, an “objectively unpopular” agenda item among the American populace. Its unpopularity was so pronounced that even Trump’s campaign strategists, those navigators of political winds, attempted to steer the SS Trump away from these choppy waters. Yet, here we are, with Trump not only acknowledging but highlighting his connection to Project 2025 and, by extension, its unpopular policies. One must wonder, does Trump believe his base has such a short memory, or is he banking on their unwavering loyalty to shield him from the fallout?

A Gift to Democrats

In an almost Shakespearean twist, Trump’s inability to refrain from self-sabotage has handed his political adversaries fresh ammunition. The Democrats, previously blamed by the White House for a governmental shutdown, can now point to Trump’s own words as evidence of his complicity in unpopular agendas. Haberman succinctly points out that Trump’s openness about Project 2025 has not only reignited scrutiny but has also provided Democrats with “fresh fodder.” In an ironic turn, Trump, who prides himself on being a master strategist, has become a strategist’s nightmare.

Conclusion: The Sound of Quiet Frustration

The frustration among Republicans, though quiet, speaks volumes about the internal conflicts plaguing the party. It’s a reflection of a deeper unease, a concern that Trump’s penchant for controversy and his inability to let sleeping dogs lie may ultimately harm the party’s prospects. This latest episode is not just about a social media post; it’s a microcosm of the broader challenges facing a party tethered to a leader whose actions are as unpredictable as they are divisive.

In the grand chessboard of American politics, Trump’s move might just be a blunder that his opponents were waiting for. While some Republicans may be quietly frustrated, the Democrats are likely not so quiet in their jubilation. As for the American public, one can only hope they’re paying close attention to the man behind the curtain and the machinations that continue to define the tumultuous landscape of U.S. politics.
