The Manufactured Fog Around Antisemitism

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 19, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Platforming the UnthinkableHollowing Out the Meaning of the WordThe Weaponization Against Jews ThemselvesThe Real Normalizers

When reactionaries cry that antisemitism is “being normalized,” they’re often pointing in the wrong direction — and usually on purpose. The latest eruption of this bad‑faith narrative came wrapped in social‑media outrage over the election of Zohran Mamdani, as if a Muslim lawmaker merely existing in office is somehow a threat to Jewish safety. It’s a neat sleight of hand: accuse your political opponents of the very harm your own movement is actively fostering.

Labelle’s cartoon cuts directly through the noise. It names what the political class refuses to confront: antisemitism gains traction not because Muslims get elected or because someone critiques Israel, but because far‑right figures with actual neo‑Nazi sympathies are repeatedly granted mainstream legitimacy.

Platforming the Unthinkable

Nick Fuentes should have been exiled from public life the moment he opened his mouth. Instead, he became a recurring guest in the conservative media circus, boosted by influencers and pundits who pretend shock while cashing in on the outrage economy. When institutions like the Heritage Foundation flirt with or downplay this filth, antisemitism isn’t merely normalized — it’s endorsed through the back door.

The pattern is unmistakable: elevate extremists, then blame the left or marginalized communities for the resulting climate of hatred. It’s cowardice mixed with opportunism.

Hollowing Out the Meaning of the Word

Nothing accelerates normalization faster than turning “antisemitism” into a political convenience. The term becomes a blunt weapon when the ADL treats empathy for Palestinians or criticism of a nation‑state as suspicious, while simultaneously downplaying grotesque behavior from the wealthy and powerful. When Elon Musk casually performs a Nazi salute at a presidential inauguration and watchdogs respond with a shrug, the term loses its gravity. Real antisemitism — the kind that destroys lives — gets buried under partisan theatrics.

This deliberate dilution benefits only those who wish to mask the spread of genuine hate behind a cloud of false accusations.

The Weaponization Against Jews Themselves

Perhaps the most cynical twist is the trend of using antisemitism as a cudgel against Jews who refuse to toe a hard‑line political stance. Labelle highlights a truth many prefer buried: dissenting Jewish voices are often smeared, silenced, or recast as traitors for challenging nationalist or authoritarian narratives.

When the accusation of antisemitism is turned against Jews who support Palestinian rights, challenge state abuses, or critique far‑right alliances, the term loses legitimacy while bigots gain cover.

This isn’t protection. It’s exploitation.

The Real Normalizers

Antisemitism flourishes when leaders sanitize the unacceptable. It grows when pundits wink at fascist rhetoric, when watchdogs abandon consistency for political convenience, and when communities are pitted against each other for ideological gain.

Labelle’s comic doesn’t just illustrate the issue — it exposes the machinery behind it. The normalization of antisemitism doesn’t come from Muslims winning elections or from activists who demand human rights. It comes from power structures that reward hate, excuse it, or repurpose it to silence critics.

The cartoon is a reminder that the fight against antisemitism requires clarity, not manufactured panic. And clarity begins by naming the real culprits instead of the convenient targets.
