Trump complains about 'losing' India and Russia to our 'deepest, darkest' adversary

Donald Trump looks on in the Oval Office at the White House. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump lamented what he characterized as the "loss" of India and Russia to China Friday morning following the Chinese summit this week, and in a social media post, sarcastically gave the two countries his blessing and well wishes for their “long and prosperous future together.”

“Looks like we’ve lost India and Russia to deepest, darkest, China,” Trump wrote in an online post on his social media platform Truth Social. “May they have a long and prosperous future together!”

Trump shared an image of Chinese President Xi Jinping walking alongside Russia’s Vladimir Putin and India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi, the three of them having grown closer amid Trump’s antagonistic trade policy with India, and failed peace talks with Russia.

The leaders met this past week in eastern China for an annual summit, where Xi took several jabs at the United States, and called for world leaders attending the event to reject a “Cold War mentality,” a not-so-subtle jab at the United States’ hostile posture toward China.

While Russia has historically never been among the United States’ closest allies, India has, historically, been a key trading partner. That relationship has soured during Trump’s second term, however, with the president slapping the nation with a 50% tariff rate in retaliation for its continued purchasing of Russian oil, which India, in turn, responded to by pausing purchases of American-made weapons.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Losing Allies: Trump's Diplomatic Folly

In a move that can only be described as diplomatically disastrous, President Donald Trump took to his social media platform, Truth Social, to lament the supposed "loss" of India and Russia to China. This statement, dripping with sarcasm and a lack of self-awareness, highlights the depth of Trump's failure in international relations during his tenure.

Trump's post, where he sarcastically wishes India and Russia a "long and prosperous future together" under the shadow of China, is an admission of defeat. It's an acknowledgment that under his watch, the geopolitical landscape has shifted away from the United States' favor, particularly with nations of significant strategic importance. This is not just a diplomatic misstep; it's a full-blown stumble into the abyss of international isolation.

The Summit of Irony

The irony of Trump's lamentation cannot be overstated. He points to a photograph of Chinese President Xi Jinping walking with Russia’s Vladimir Putin and India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi as evidence of this "loss." Yet, what Trump fails to recognize—or perhaps, conveniently ignores—is that his own policies have been instrumental in driving these countries closer together.

During Trump's presidency, his antagonistic trade policy with India and the failed peace talks with Russia did not showcase a strategic mastermind at work but rather a bull in the china shop of delicate international relations. By slapping India with a 50% tariff rate in retaliation for its continued purchasing of Russian oil, Trump did not strengthen the U.S.'s position but weakened it, pushing India to reconsider its reliance on American-made weapons.

Rejecting the Cold War Mentality... Or Embracing It?

At the annual summit in eastern China, where these leaders grew closer, President Xi took the opportunity to jab at the United States, urging world leaders to reject a "Cold War mentality." This statement, though directed at the international community, was a clear critique of the United States' increasingly hostile posture towards China under Trump's guidance.

What's perhaps most baffling is Trump's apparent surprise at these developments. His foreign policy, characterized by hostility, tariffs, and a penchant for economic sanctions, has naturally led to a realignment of global alliances. In his quest to "make America great again," Trump has inadvertently made America more isolated.

India and Russia: From Allies to Frenemies?

While Russia and the United States have had a complicated relationship at best, India's shift is more concerning. Historically a key trading partner, India's relationship with the U.S. has soured significantly during Trump's second term. The response to Trump's tariffs—pausing purchases of American-made weapons—signals not just a temporary disagreement but a potential long-term estrangement.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Lost Opportunities

Trump's post on Truth Social is emblematic of his presidency's approach to foreign policy: reactive, devoid of strategic foresight, and ultimately counterproductive. The "loss" of India and Russia to China is not a testament to China's diplomatic prowess but rather to Trump's failure to nurture and maintain vital international relationships.

As the world becomes increasingly interconnected, the United States cannot afford to alienate its allies through shortsighted policies and erratic diplomacy. Trump's lamentation should serve as a cautionary tale of how quickly international alliances can shift when they're not tended to with care and strategic acumen. In the grand chessboard of global politics, Trump's administration has been playing checkers.
