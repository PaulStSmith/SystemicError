Lawfare’s Roger Parloff on Saturday noted that Blanche made the DOJ’s case vulnerable in the matter involving a mistakenly deported man named Kilmar Abrego Garcia. Abrego Garcia is a migrant who was wrongfully deported to El Salvador due to an “administrative error” but has since been returned to the U.S. and is fighting deportation efforts.

Parloff flagged a legal filing that says, “Most tellingly, Attorney General Bondi’s direct report, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, linked Abrego’s criminal charges to Abrego’s civil lawsuit in Maryland.”

“Strikingly, during a television appearance Deputy Attorney General Blanche revealed that the government started ‘investigating’ Abrego after ‘a judge in Maryland ... questioned’ the government’s decision, found that it ‘had no right to deport him,’ and ‘accused the government of doing something wrong,’” according to the court document.

Parloff highlighted those lines and wrote, “Blanche‘s explicit linkage of the vindictive start of govt’s Abrego criminal probe to govt’s loss of Abrego’s civil case may be the most foolish thing he’s done since his cross of Michael Cohen.”

Parloff then quoted Blanche’s now-infamous line to Trump’s ex-lawyer and fixer Cohen in a courtroom: “[Y]ou went on TikTok and called me a ‘crying little s---,’ didn’t you?”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Missteps in the DOJ’s Handling of Kilmar Abrego Garcia’s Case

In a glaring display of legal mismanagement, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche has been spotlighted for a critical mistake that not only undermines the integrity of the Department of Justice (DOJ) but also exposes a dangerous precedent in mixing criminal and civil matters within the United States legal system. This incident, involving the wrongful deportation of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a migrant mistakenly sent back to El Salvador, serves as a textbook example of how not to handle a sensitive legal matter.

The Heart of the Matter

At the core of this debacle is an “administrative error” that led to Garcia’s wrongful deportation. This error, while grave, was compounded by the DOJ’s handling of the aftermath. According to legal journalist Roger Parloff of Lawfare, the DOJ, under Blanche’s oversight, made the situation exponentially worse by intertwining Garcia’s criminal charges with his civil lawsuit in Maryland, a move that can only be described as both reckless and strategically unsound.

Blanche’s Blunder

Blanche’s decision to link Garcia’s criminal investigation with the outcome of a civil case not only raises eyebrows but also serious questions about the motivations behind the government’s actions. When a television appearance revealed that the government began scrutinizing Garcia following a Maryland judge’s criticism of the deportation, it painted a picture of a vindictive and reactionary DOJ. This revelation is particularly damning, as it suggests that the initiation of criminal proceedings may have been spurred not by the merits of the case but as a retaliatory measure against a perceived legal slight.

The Consequences of Mixing Civil and Criminal Matters

The explicit linkage, as pointed out by Parloff, between the start of the government’s criminal probe and the loss in Garcia’s civil case is not just “foolish” but potentially undermines the very foundation of the American legal system. The separation of civil and criminal matters is a cornerstone principle meant to ensure fairness and impartiality in legal proceedings. Blanche’s actions, as highlighted in this incident, blur these lines in a manner that could have far-reaching implications for legal ethics and the impartial administration of justice.

A Misguided Strategy

Parloff’s comparison of Blanche’s mishandling of the Garcia case to his infamous courtroom exchange with Michael Cohen is telling. It suggests a pattern of behavior unbecoming of a Deputy Attorney General, marked by personal vendettas and a disregard for legal norms. The reference to Blanche’s “crying little s---” comment to Cohen not only underscores a lack of professionalism but also highlights a penchant for allowing personal feelings to dictate legal strategy.

In Conclusion

The unfortunate mishandling of Kilmar Abrego Garcia’s case by Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche serves as a cautionary tale. It reveals the dangers of conflating civil and criminal matters, the potential for governmental abuse of power, and the importance of maintaining professional decorum in legal proceedings. This incident should prompt a thorough review of DOJ policies and procedures to ensure that such errors are not repeated in the future. The integrity of the American legal system depends on it.
