Trump's elite legal team collapses ahead of criminal appeal

Trump’s Vanishing Legal Team Is Not a Staffing Story

The Real Power Is Still Trump’s

Politico’s account is narrow: Sullivan & Cromwell’s Trump defense bench is thinning, and Robert Giuffra Jr. may be left carrying three high-stakes appeals almost alone. That is the surface fact. The political fact is simpler: Trump still has enough institutional power to pull elite lawyers into his orbit, then drain them back out into government and the judiciary.

This is not a story about an overworked client or an unlucky firm. It is a story about access, reward, and capture. The same political operation that produced the legal exposure is now reshaping the institutions around it.

The Firm Is the Symptom, Not the Subject

Sullivan & Cromwell is being treated like the main stage because elite law firms make convenient characters. But the firm is only the delivery system. The decision-making power sits with Trump, who can elevate lawyers into administration or judicial roles, and with the institutions willing to treat that as normal.

That is the actual bargain: professional prestige in exchange for service to power. The revolving door is not accidental. It is the structure.

The Story’s Framing Softens the Damage

The reporting emphasizes that the team is “unraveling,” as if this were a management problem. It is more revealing than that. The relevant question is not whether Giuffra can handle three appeals at once. It is why a former president can still command elite legal labor while undercutting the institutions meant to constrain him.

Even the language of “elite legal team” carries its own distortion. Prestige does not equal legitimacy. A bench full of Supreme Court clerks and former government lawyers does not make the underlying project less authoritarian or less corrosive. It just makes it better represented.

What the Appeals Really Show

The pending briefs on the New York criminal conviction, the civil fraud case brought by Letitia James, and the $83.3 million E. Jean Carroll verdict all point to the same pattern: Trump converts legal defeat into another arena for elite compliance. He does not simply defend himself. He forces the system to keep negotiating with him.

That is the consequence worth naming. The legal system can issue judgments, but enforcement and finality still depend on institutions that are willing to absorb delay, prestige pressure, and political intimidation. Trump’s legal operation works because too many powerful actors still behave as though access to him is a prize.

The Larger Pattern Is Institutional Cowardice

This is what democratic decay looks like in plain clothes. Not only coups and barricades, but firms, courts, and offices that keep laundering Trump’s power into procedure. Lawyers leave for better offers. Lawyers get rewarded with posts. A firm pretends the work is ordinary. And the public is asked to treat the churn as a routine personnel update.

It is not routine. It is the administrative face of impunity.

Democracy Cannot Survive This Kind of Normal

The deeper story is not that Trump may have to file briefs with fewer partners. It is that American institutions still bend themselves around him, then call it professionalism. That is how authoritarian politics survives in a system that refuses to admit what it is dealing with: not a difficult client, but a political force that turns every institution into a tool.
