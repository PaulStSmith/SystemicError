'Totally false': MSNBC expert undercuts Trump before crime press conference even begins

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Before Donald Trump even hit the stage in the White House briefing room to detail his plans to reportedly militarize the streets of Washington, D.C. due to a crime wave, MSNBC's Ken. Dilanian undercut the president.

With Trump late as usual, host Anna Cabrera asked Dilanian, "Talk about D.C.'s crime being down when you compare year to year? President Trump has made these outlandish claims, saying D.C. is one of the most dangerous cities in the world, not just in the US, but the world. That's clearly not accurate. Beyond D.C. comparing numbers to itself, can you set the record straight for us?"

"Yeah, that is totally false on it," he shot back. "You can Google this. Actually, there are lots of different ways to measure the most dangerous cities in the United States, but if you just look at per capita violent crime, the amounts of violence, the numbers of violent crime based on a percentage of the population, D.C. is not even in the top 25 __ it's not even close."

"And a lot of it's generally smaller and medium-sized cities that are the most dangerous," he added. "Now, look, some neighborhoods in D.C. If you stack them up as a city next to Memphis or some of these other very dangerous cities, the crime rates there are higher than in other parts. But generally, D.C. and other major American cities are not the most dangerous cities."

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump's Misinformation and the Reality of D.C.'s Crime Rate

In an era where truth often falls victim to political rhetoric, the recent claims by Donald Trump regarding Washington, D.C.'s crime rates not only stretch the bounds of credibility but leapfrog into the realm of the absurd. Before Trump could even grace the stage with his presence to propose a militarized response to what he paints as a dystopian hellscape of crime in D.C., Ken Dilanian of MSNBC was quick to dismantle the fortress of falsehoods.

Trump's claims that D.C. ranks as one of the most dangerous cities globally is not just an oversimplification; it's a gross misrepresentation that flies in the face of actual data. Dilanian, in his response to Anna Cabrera's prompt, didn't mince words: “Yeah, that is totally false on it,” highlighting the ease with which one can debunk such claims with a simple Google search.

Fact vs. Fiction: The Real Crime Rates

When we dive into the numbers, the narrative Trump attempts to spin unravels quicker than a poorly-made sweater. According to Dilanian, when considering per capita violent crime rates, Washington, D.C. doesn't even come close to making the top 25 list of America's most dangerous cities. This isn't just an oversight; it's a testament to the willingness to bend reality to fit a political agenda.

The assertion that “a lot of it's generally smaller and medium-sized cities that are the most dangerous,” not only contradicts Trump's claim but shifts the focus to a more nuanced understanding of crime in America. It's not the sprawling metropolises that are the hotbeds of violence but rather, in many cases, smaller cities with their own unique challenges.

The Disingenuous Comparison

Trump's comparison of D.C. to the world's most dangerous cities is not just misleading, it's irresponsible. It fans the flames of fear for political gain, ignoring the complexities of crime and its root causes. Dilanian's counterpoints remind us that even within D.C., crime rates vary significantly, with certain neighborhoods experiencing higher rates of violence. However, these nuances are lost in Trump's broad-stroked portrayal of chaos.

The strategy of proposing militarization as a solution to D.C.'s so-called crime wave is not just an overreaction; it's a dangerous proposition that could exacerbate tensions and undermine community trust in law enforcement. It’s a classic case of using a sledgehammer to crack a nut, and the implications could be far-reaching.

Conclusion: The Importance of Fact-Checking in Political Rhetoric

This scenario underscores the critical importance of fact-checking in today's political discourse. Misinformation, especially when it comes from figures of authority, can have real-world consequences. As Dilanian aptly demonstrates, a little bit of research can go a long way in debunking falsehoods and presenting a more accurate picture of reality.

In a time where the truth is often a casualty of political warfare, it’s more important than ever to stay informed, question narratives, and demand accountability. Trump's claims about D.C.'s crime rates don't just fall short; they're a glaring example of the disconnect between political narratives and the on-the-ground realities Americans live with every day.
