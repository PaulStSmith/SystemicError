'Useless': Attempt to ingratiate himself with Trump blows up in MAGA Republican's face

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Deep Dive into Cornyn's QuagmireThe Failed Trump AppealThe BacklashThe Bigger PictureConclusion

U.S. President Donald Trump wears a ‘Make America Great Again’ (MAGA) hat as he attends the commencement ceremony at West Point Military Academy in West Point, New York, U.S., May 24, 2025. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

Embattled Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) attempted to make himself look good to President Donald Trump on Monday amid polls showing mortal danger for him in the upcoming primary — but it didn't work out well for him.

Cornyn is facing a well-funded and high-profile challenge by state Attorney General Ken Paxton, and the two have been at each other's throats for months, with Cornyn knocking Paxton's history of legal issues and accusing him of being absent from the legislative standoff as Democrats left the state to paralyze passage of a new gerrymandered congressional map.

For all that, though, he hasn't managed to attract Trump's endorsement, the one thing his team believes could turn things around. And on Monday, he posted a spirited attack on Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) following Trump's own racially-charged Truth Social rant calling her "low IQ."

"I hope she is the Democratic nominee for the Texas Senate race next year," wrote Cornyn. "A Momdani Democrat! Should be fun!" (Presumably being a misspelled reference to progressive New York City mayoral nominee Zohran Mamdani.)

However, this tweet prompted instant blowback — and not just from Democrats and supporters of Crockett. Some MAGA commenters who saw it as cheap pandering.

"And she endorsed you last week! How fun!" wrote right-wing pastor Daniel Hayworth.

"Well, I guess we know who you will be supporting against @KenPaxtonTX, who will be the Republican nominee for that seat," wrote Bo French, the chairman of the Tarrant County Republican Party.

"Why is your low level staffer continuously editing this post to delete my comment from it?" wrote Breanna Morello, a podcaster who claims to have left Fox over COVID-19 mandates, and who Cornyn initially reposted after she shared Trump's Truth Social rant on X.

"Again: Sir, please don’t quote tweet my posts. I don’t want people thinking I align with Deep State hacks that lie about FISA to the American people. You’ve been useless for Texans. We fully support Ken Paxton as your replacement."

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into Cornyn's Quagmire

Embattled Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) finds himself in the political equivalent of quicksand. His latest maneuver, an attempt to curry favor with President Donald Trump, has not only failed spectacularly but has also managed to alienate him further from both sides of the political spectrum. Let's dissect this latest episode in Cornyn's saga of self-sabotage.

The Failed Trump Appeal

Cornyn, facing mortal danger in the upcoming primary due to a challenge from state Attorney General Ken Paxton, seems desperate to secure Trump's golden endorsement. Yet, his method of doing so—mimicking Trump's own divisive rhetoric—has backfired. In a spirited attack on Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX), Cornyn parroted Trump's racially-charged language, calling Crockett "low IQ" and making a cryptic reference to a "Momdani Democrat," a likely misspelled jab at progressive Zohran Mamdani.

The Backlash

Cornyn's tweet was met with immediate backlash, illustrating a clear misstep in his strategy. Not only did Democrats and Crockett's supporters voice their disdain, but so did some within the MAGA camp, viewing Cornyn's attempt as cheap pandering. Right-wing pastor Daniel Hayworth's sarcastic commendation, alongside the pointed comments from Bo French, chairman of the Tarrant County Republican Party, and Breanna Morello, a podcaster, underscore the widespread disapproval of Cornyn's tactics.

It's particularly telling when Morello, a self-proclaimed ex-Fox podcaster, criticizes Cornyn for aligning with "Deep State hacks" and openly declares support for Paxton. This sentiment reveals a deep rift within the Republican base, one that Cornyn seems to have miscalculated in his bid for Trump's endorsement.

The Bigger Picture

This incident is emblematic of a larger issue within the Republican Party: the struggle to balance traditional conservative values with the populist, often divisive rhetoric championed by Trump. Cornyn's failed attempt to bridge this gap not only highlights his own political vulnerability but also reflects the ongoing identity crisis facing the party.

Moreover, Cornyn's attack on Crockett, particularly through the use of racially-charged language, raises serious questions about the ethical boundaries of political discourse. In his effort to appear tough and aligned with Trump's base, Cornyn has instead exemplified the lowest common denominator of political engagement.

Conclusion

John Cornyn's predicament is a stark reminder of the dangers of political pandering and the importance of maintaining a principled stance, even in the face of electoral threats. His failure to secure Trump's endorsement, coupled with the backlash from his own attempted attack, places him in an increasingly precarious position. As the primary looms, Cornyn finds himself caught in a crossfire of his own making, alienated from potential allies on both sides of the aisle.

In the grand chessboard of politics, Cornyn's move was nothing short of a blunder. And in the high stakes game for Texas' Senate seat, there's little room for error. Cornyn's saga serves as a cautionary tale for any politician attempting to navigate the treacherous waters of partisan politics in the Trump era. It's not enough to mimic the rhetoric of the day; one must also understand the currents that drive it.
