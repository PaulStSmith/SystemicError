Two authors are warning that the right has a secret “master plan” to help billionaires buy elections.

In a Rolling Stone commentary piece published Monday and adapted by David Sirota and Jared Jacang Maher’s upcoming book “Master Plan: The Hidden Plot to Legalize Corruption in America,” the authors describe how two lawsuits heading to the Supreme Court could eliminate “the last restrictions on campaign donations and obstructing law enforcement’s efforts to halt bribery.”

One of those lawsuits is led by Vice President JD Vance, “aiming to reverse a 2001 Supreme Court ruling and eliminate restrictions on political parties’ coordinated spending with candidates.”

The high court’s Citizens United decision set the “precedent that empowers oligarchs to buy elections,” which conservative groups hope to expand, even though many Americans would argue that money can corrupt the political process, the authors argue.

“If those rules are killed off, party committees could become pass-through conduits for big donors to circumvent donation limits and deliver much larger payments in support of lawmakers who can reward them with government favors,” the authors write.

“As we recount in our new book ‘Master Plan,’ the Citizens United case was the culmination of conservatives’ 50-year master plan to deregulate the campaign finance system and legalize corruption. What started as an incendiary memo from soon-to-be Supreme Court Justice Lewis Powell became one ruling equating money with constitutionally protected speech and another extending personhood rights to corporations,” the authors write.

This “master plan,” according to the authors, would further make it “increasingly impossible to prosecute public corruption cases.”

“In essence, they’re asking justices to believe a bank CEO’s donation to a political party coordinating with a lawmaker’s campaign can in no way influence how that lawmaker drafts banking legislation,” according to the authors.

The authors also argue there are still a few ways for “this nightmare to be stopped,” and that’s by making anti-corruption a focus in politics.

“Yes, in buying elections, packing the courts, and eliciting precedents legalizing corruption, the master planners have created the democracy crisis, pushing America one step closer to the kleptocratic death spiral of history’s collapsing empires,” the authors write.

Our Analysis:

The Not-So-Secret Blueprint: Unmasking the Right’s Ambition to Legalize Corruption

In an era where the lines between political influence and outright corruption blur with alarming regularity, David Sirota and Jared Jacang Maher’s revelations in their Rolling Stone commentary piece, adapted from their upcoming book “Master Plan: The Hidden Plot to Legalize Corruption in America,” serve as a chilling forecast of a democracy in peril. The duo uncovers what appears to be a meticulously crafted strategy by conservative forces to dismantle the remaining barriers against unfettered campaign donations and institutionalize bribery under the guise of legal proceedings.

The Supreme Court as an Instrument of Oligarchy

Central to this strategy are two lawsuits inching their way towards the Supreme Court, with one spearheaded by none other than Vice President JD Vance. The goal? To obliterate the 2001 Supreme Court ruling that places restrictions on political parties’ coordinated spending with candidates. This maneuver isn’t happening in isolation. It builds on the notorious Citizens United decision, a ruling that has already set a dangerous precedent by essentially decreeing that the wealthiest among us can wield their fortunes to sway elections—turning democracy into a playground for oligarchs.

The potential eradication of these restrictions threatens to transform party committees into “pass-through conduits” for massive donations. These are not mere contributions but investments in securing government favors—a clear path to institutionalized corruption.

The 50-Year Master Plan to Dismantle Democracy

Sirota and Maher trace this nefarious plot back to a memo by the soon-to-be Supreme Court Justice Lewis Powell, laying the groundwork for a campaign that equated monetary contributions with constitutionally protected speech and extended personhood rights to corporations. This isn’t just a strategy; it’s a 50-year crusade to erode the foundations of a fair electoral system, setting a precedent that makes it increasingly challenging to prosecute public corruption.

The audacity of the plan is breathtaking. It relies on convincing the justices that substantial donations from influential figures like bank CEOs to political parties, in coordination with lawmaker campaigns, won’t affect legislative outcomes. This premise is not just flawed; it’s insultingly naive, suggesting a willful blindness to the mechanics of power and influence.

The Path Forward: An Anti-Corruption Crusade

Despite the dire portrait painted by Sirota and Maher, all is not lost. The authors argue that there remains a sliver of hope if anti-corruption becomes a central political focus. This isn’t merely about stopping a few lawsuits but about halting the descent into a kleptocracy—a government ruled by those who seek status and personal gain at the expense of the governed.

The revelation of this “master plan” by Sirota and Maher is a call to arms. It’s a stark reminder that the battle for the soul of American democracy is ongoing. The oligarchs have made their move, cloaked in legal battles and strategic lawsuits, pushing the nation towards what the authors describe as the “kleptocratic death spiral of history’s collapsing empires.”

In the face of such a calculated assault on democratic principles, complacency equals complicity. It’s incumbent upon those who value democracy to stand up, be counted, and fight back against the normalization of corruption. The survival of a government “of the people, by the people, for the people” depends on it.
