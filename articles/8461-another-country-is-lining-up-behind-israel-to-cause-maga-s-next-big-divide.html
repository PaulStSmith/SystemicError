Israel may not be President Donald Trump’s only international policy threatening MAGA unity, said Ransom Miller, reporting for the Hill.

“More Trump voters disapprove of the president’s handling of China than any other foreign policy issue,” said Ransom, according to a public opinion poll by his team at the Institute for Global Affairs. “Even as Trump makes overtures to Beijing, most of his voters continue to view China as a moderate or severe threat (83 percent), and almost a third agree that it intends to destroy the U.S. or replace the world order.

Trump is facing open revolt from his base regarding Chinese student visa after he reversed course from his earlier crackdown. The president announced the U.S. would grant 600,000 H-1B visas to Chinese students in the next two years, citing international students’ high fees and the value they add to the U.S. economy. He then doubled down on Wednesday, claiming that welcoming H-1B visa workers is “MAGA.”

But Miller said his message isn’t resonating with the MAGA base, with surveys revealing that more than half of Trump’s constituency feels that Chinese students shouldn’t be allowed to study in the U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, far-right activist Laura Loomer, Fox News host Laura Ingraham and longtime Trump advisor Steve Bannon also disagree, according to Miller.

Likewise, Trump’s approach to Chinese export controls are also causing argument with his high-tariff strategy damaging American growth, while Trump signals openness to exemptions on selling advanced U.S. technology to China, including semiconductors.

“For some, this is the worst of both worlds, as Americans are forced to pay higher costs for goods and Chinese military manufacturers gain greater access to American technology,” said Miller. “Trump’s former Deputy National Security Advisor Matt Pottinger predicts that allowing semiconductor sales to China would be ‘unilaterally deindustrializing America.’”

Miller added that Trump’s former National Security Council China director described the policy as “playing 2-D chess while Beijing is playing 4-D chess.”

But while Trump clings to China many of his MAGA voters want a clean break from the Chinese economy.

“White House Senior Counsel Peter Navarro continues to argue that U.S. industrial weakness has invited foreign aggression and that the U.S. must reshore manufacturing through high tariffs,” said Miller.

From their perspective, complete withdrawal remains the end goal. Steve Bannon went so far as to advise: “If you don’t do this, you’re always going to have the threat of the gun to your head.”

“The president may be able to tamp down on a potential insurgency as he dictates what is and isn’t America First. However, if the conversation among MAGA today is any indication, the worst for Trump be yet to come,” said Miller.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s China Conundrum: A MAGA Rift?

The political landscape is rarely simple, but President Donald Trump’s current tightrope walk over U.S.-China policy is a high-wire act of political acrobatics that could very well leave him without a safety net. Ransom Miller’s report for the Hill shines a harsh spotlight on this precarious balance, revealing a MAGA base at odds with Trump’s diplomatic maneuvers.

The MAGA Base vs. China: A Deep Divide

First, let’s dissect the astonishing revelation that more Trump voters disapprove of his handling of China than any other foreign policy issue. This is no small feat considering the myriad of international challenges facing the Trump administration. The fact that 83% of his base views China as a moderate or severe threat, with almost a third believing China aims to destroy the U.S. or replace the world order, speaks volumes. It paints a picture of a base in dire need of reassurance—a reassurance they’re not getting.

H-1B Visas: Trump’s Achilles’ Heel?

Trump’s decision to grant 600,000 H-1B visas to Chinese students over the next two years is a staggering reversal from his previous stance. Trump’s proclamation that welcoming H-1B visa workers is “MAGA” is a bizarre attempt to square a circle that his base clearly wants no part of. With over half of his constituency against Chinese students studying in the U.S., Trump’s policy is not just a misstep; it’s a full-on stumble that could cost him dearly. The opposition from key figures like Marjorie Taylor Greene and Steve Bannon only adds fuel to the fire.

The Semiconductor Saga: A Double-Edged Sword

Trump’s approach to Chinese export controls and his ambiguous stance on semiconductor sales to China is nothing short of a policy quagmire. On one hand, his high-tariff strategy is purportedly bleeding American growth. On the other, his openness to exemptions for advanced U.S. technology sales to China is a breathtaking gamble, risking American industrial might for uncertain gains. Matt Pottinger’s prediction that this could lead to America’s unilateral deindustrialization is a dire warning that cannot be ignored.

A 4-D Chess Game Trump Can’t Afford to Lose

The analogy of playing 2-D chess while Beijing plays 4-D chess perfectly encapsulates the strategic mismatch Trump is facing. The call from within his own ranks for a complete withdrawal from the Chinese economy underscores a fundamental rift in MAGA’s vision for America’s future. With figures like Peter Navarro and Steve Bannon advocating for a hardline stance, Trump’s current trajectory seems increasingly out of step with his base’s desires.

The Crux of the Matter

In essence, Trump’s China policy appears to be a tightrope act over a chasm of political and economic repercussions. The MAGA base’s demand for a “clean break” from the Chinese economy is a stark contrast to Trump’s diplomatic overtures. This discord within his own camp poses a significant challenge to his presidency’s stability and his definition of “America First.”

As Miller aptly concludes, the potential for a MAGA insurgency looms large. Trump’s political acumen is being tested as never before. The question now is not just about policy, but about political survival. Can Trump realign with his base, or is the rift too deep to mend? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the stakes could not be higher.
