Another member of Trump's circle caught flying Epstein airlines: report

Another high-profile name and someone within President Donald Trump‘s inner circle was linked to late financier and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, CNN reported Friday.

Current Navy Secretary John Phelan was listed on a flight manifest in 2006 from London to New York. The flight was before he was named Navy secretary.

Phelan was on board with about 13 people; six of those names were redacted.

A close friend, whose name was not released, told CNN that it was the only time he and Epstein interacted, and he was invited by Bear Stearns CEO Jimmy Cayne. Phelan reportedly did not realize it was Epstein’s plane until they arrived on it, and there was no contact after that.

There are no allegations of wrongdoing against Phelan, who declined to comment to CNN through a spokesperson.

No other indications showed that Epstein and Phelan had interacted beyond the flight.

Epstein and Jean-Luc Brunel were reportedly on the flight. Brunel, who was charged with sexual assault, including a minor, was a French model scout and owner of a modeling agency with significant financial backing from Epstein. Brunel died by apparent suicide in 2022 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, much like Epstein allegedly had in 2019.

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look: The Uncomfortable Proximity of Power and Scandal

Yet again, the tendrils of Jeffrey Epstein’s infamous legacy have ensnared another high-profile figure, this time reaching into the heart of the U.S. Navy. John Phelan, the current Navy Secretary, has been linked to Epstein via a flight manifest from 2006, as reported by CNN. This disclosure isn’t just a mere footnote in the ongoing saga of Epstein’s dark and convoluted story; it’s a glaring spotlight on the uncomfortable intersections of power, privilege, and scandal that seem to permeate the upper echelons of society and governance.

The Fact of the Matter

Phelan found himself aboard Epstein’s plane, a detail that would have probably remained buried beneath the annals of time had it not been for the meticulous investigations following Epstein’s downfall. The flight, which transported Phelan from London to New York, included around 13 individuals, with six names redacted for reasons untold. This occurrence predates Phelan’s tenure as Navy Secretary, a detail that offers little in the way of comfort or exoneration.

A “close friend” of Phelan’s, conveniently unnamed, offered a narrative of this being a singular interaction between Phelan and Epstein, facilitated by none other than Bear Stearns CEO Jimmy Cayne. According to this account, Phelan was ostensibly unaware that the aircraft belonged to Epstein until after his arrival on it. Moreover, this was purportedly their first and last contact.

The Alibis and the Silence

Phelan’s decision to decline comment through a spokesperson does little to quench the thirst for transparency and truth. It’s a maneuver as old as time, one that leaves more questions than answers and does nothing to untangle the web of associations that Epstein wove throughout his life.

The insistence that Phelan and Epstein’s paths never crossed again after this flight is difficult to swallow without a grain of salt. The presence of Jean-Luc Brunel on the same flight — a figure deeply entangled in Epstein’s web of sexual assault and trafficking allegations — only adds a layer of grim context to the narrative.

The Larger Implication

What we are witnessing is not just a story about a flight taken years ago; it’s a testament to the murky waters of elite networking and the potential for complicity in silence. The fact that there are no allegations of wrongdoing against Phelan does not absolve the chilling reality that Epstein’s network extended far and wide, ensnaring individuals in positions of considerable power and influence.

This revelation should not fade into the background as just another entry in the Epstein saga. It should serve as a catalyst for deeper inquiry and reflection on the dynamics of power, the responsibilities of public officials, and the pervasive influence of individuals like Epstein. The interconnections between high-profile individuals and convicted sex offenders should not be treated as mere coincidences or unfortunate accidents but as opportunities to question and scrutinize the structures that allow such associations to form and, seemingly, flourish.

In the end, the link between Phelan and Epstein, however brief or seemingly inconsequential, is a stark reminder of the proximity between reputable public service and the sordid underbelly of elite criminality. It’s a reminder that the flight logs of Jeffrey Epstein’s planes are not just lists of names but maps of a dark network that has yet to be fully unraveled and understood.
