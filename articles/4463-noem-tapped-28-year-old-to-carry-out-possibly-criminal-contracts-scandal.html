Noem tapped 28-year-old to carry out 'possibly criminal' contracts scandal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 9, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem at One World Trade Center in New York City, U.S., January 8, 2026. REUTERS/David ‘Dee’ Delgado

Outgoing Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem is embroiled in a scandal involving public contracts for the millions she spent on ads for recruitment videos for Immigration and Customs Enforcement. It has now been revealed that the staffer whom Noem tapped to her former political director, Madison Sheahan, to handle funding.

NBC News reported on Thursday that the sum she managed was $100 million, but Washington Examiner reporter Anna Giaritelli noted on Friday that they “did some digging” and found that Sheahan oversaw the entire budget of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

“The random helper was Madison Sheahan, a 2019 Ohio State University graduate who worked as Noem’s political director when she was South Dakota governor. Sheahan was roughly 23-25 years old at the time. Last year, at the approximate age of 28 (give or take a year) and with no immigration law experience, Sheahan was tapped by Kristi Noem to oversee ICE’s $9 billion budget,” wrote Giaritelli.

She was responding to a comment from the Daily Wire’s Luke Rosiak, who discovered that Sheahan didn’t handle just one $100 million contract. She ordered that a second contract be given to the same firms.

“The contracts scandal at DHS is extremely bad, possibly criminal,” wrote Rosiak on X.

“Noem claimed that ‘career officials’ somehow picked three firms connected to her politically as the only ones who should be able to bid on the $200M ads, just like ‘career officials’ kept [Corey] Lewandowski’s time to make sure he didn’t work more than 130 days per year, which would violate [Special Government Employees] SGE rules,” he added.

Sheahan is now running for Congress in Ohio.

There have been huge conflicts at the Department of Homeland Security’s Office of the Inspector General. The IG, appointed by President Donald Trump, wrote a letter saying that leaders under Noem “systematically obstructed” his inquiries, including a “specific pending criminal investigation.”

“Does anybody have any idea how bad it has to be for the OIG in this agency to come out and do this publicly?” asked Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) during a hearing before the Senate Judiciary Committee on Tuesday. “That is stonewalling, that’s a failure of leadership, and that is why I’ve called for your resignation.”

With Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-Okla.) taking over the department, the IG will have a new opportunity to investigate any allegedly questionable behavior under Noem’s leadership.

Our Analysis:

The DHS Debacle: A Scandal Ripe for Dissection

In an era where political scandals are as common as colds, the unfolding drama involving outgoing Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem, Madison Sheahan, and a staggering misuse of public funds for ICE recruitment ads, demands a special spotlight for its audaciousness and the glaring questions it raises about accountability and nepotism in high government offices.

Kristi Noem’s Dubious Decisions

First, let’s address the elephant in the room: Kristi Noem, in her capacity as Secretary of Homeland Security, apparently saw fit to entrust the ICE’s behemoth $9 billion budget to Madison Sheahan, a former political director from her gubernatorial days in South Dakota, with no notable experience in immigration law or managing budgets of this magnitude. At a tender age where most are navigating the early stages of their careers, Sheahan was handed the reins to a budget that impacts national security and immigration policy. The mind boggles at the thought process behind such a decision, assuming there was one beyond political payback or cronyism.

The Scandal That Keeps on Giving

NBC News initially pointed the finger at a $100 million ad recruitment campaign, but it was the diligent digging by the Washington Examiner’s Anna Giaritelli that revealed the full extent of Sheahan’s involvement, overseeing the entire ICE budget. Further revelations by Luke Rosiak of the Daily Wire have shown not just one, but multiple contracts worth $200 million were funneled to firms connected to Noem politically, under the guise that “career officials” had made these decisions. This is not just a red flag; it’s a blazing inferno signaling systemic abuse of power for political gain.

Conflict of Interest: A Family Affair?

The audacity doesn’t stop with budget mismanagement. Sheahan, now eyeing a seat in Congress, seems to have gracefully glided from one significant governmental responsibility to another, leaving a trail of questionable decisions and potential conflicts of interest. The implications of such a move, from overseeing a $9 billion budget to potentially legislating on Capitol Hill, are profound and deeply concerning.

The Inspector General’s Alarm Bells

The Department of Homeland Security’s Office of the Inspector General (OIG) has been vocal about the “systematic obstruction” faced during investigations into Noem’s leadership, an accusation so severe that it prompted Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) to call for Noem’s resignation. When an OIG, particularly one appointed by President Donald Trump, raises alarms about obstruction and potential criminal investigation, it’s not just a bad look; it’s a five-alarm fire signaling deep-rooted corruption.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

As Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-Okla.) steps in to potentially clean up the DHS’s act, one can only hope that this scandal serves as a wake-up call for stringent oversight and accountability in government spending and appointments. The DHS scandal is not just “extremely bad” or “possibly criminal” as noted by Rosiak; it’s a glaring indictment of a system that allows individuals to play fast and loose with taxpayer dollars and the nation’s security for political gain.

In a just world, this scandal would lead to significant reforms and repercussions for those involved. Yet, in the political climate of today, one must wonder if it will be swept under the rug, only to resurface when the next scandal erupts. It’s high time for a systemic overhaul to restore integrity and trust in government operations.
