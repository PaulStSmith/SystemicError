'Better shoot straight!' House Dem grilled after 'yelling' at troops in LA

CNN's Brianna Keilar challenged Rep. Maxine Waters (D-CA) on Wednesday about her actions toward the National Guard outside the Los Angeles Detention Center this week.

Waters was denied entry into the federal building by the National Guard when she tried to check on David Huerta, president of the Service Employees International Union, who was arrested by ICE during the anti-deportation protests.

Afterwards, Waters posted to social media, “Today, I came to the Metropolitan Detention Center to exercise my constitutional rights as a Member of Congress to check on the safety and conditions of SEIU California President David Huerta, who was arrested by ICE. I pled with the National Guard, which was heavily armed, not to use their weapons against peaceful demonstrators who were simply exercising their rights to freedom of speech and protest."

Keilar played a video of Waters shouting at the troops, "What are you gonna do? You gonna shoot some kid who's afraid of you and runs? You gonna shoot an elected official? If you shoot me, you better shoot straight."

Keilar asked if "yelling at them was the right way to show" concern over what could happen with armed service members in the streets.

"What I was really trying to do was to get the attention to the seriousness of what was going on," Waters answered. "And, so I thought, as an elected official, if I talk about, so that it would be in earshot of all the media and everybody else that was there and people were listening, that we were raising concerns about armed guards being in Los Angeles and what they are ordered to do."

Keilar then asked, "I think many people share your concerns that this is a dangerous situation in Los Angeles. Would you want your constituents and other Angelenos to be yelling at guardsmen like that?"

Waters took issue with Keilar's assertion that she was "yelling" at the guardsmen.

"It is not a matter of yelling; it's projecting so that you will be heard," Waters answered.

Our Analysis:

Maxine Waters vs. The National Guard: A Clash of Constitutional Rights and Media Narratives

In an era where political theater often overshadows substantive policy debate, the recent confrontation between Rep. Maxine Waters (D-CA) and the National Guard outside the Los Angeles Detention Center serves as a quintessential example of how media narratives can distort and oversimplify complex issues. CNN's Brianna Keilar's interview with Waters post-incident is a textbook case of this phenomenon, where the lines between advocacy, journalism, and sensationalism blur uncomfortably.

The Incident: Rep. Waters, in an act of oversight and advocacy, attempted to enter a federal building to check on the condition of David Huerta, an arrested anti-deportation protestor and union president. Denied entry by the National Guard, Waters took to social media to express her concerns, framing her actions as an exercise of her constitutional rights.

The Confrontation: Keilar's focus on Waters' vocal confrontation with the National Guard, including a clip of Waters challenging the troops, encapsulates the media's penchant for amplifying the most dramatic elements of political discourse. Waters' rhetorical question, "What are you gonna do? You gonna shoot some kid who's afraid of you and runs? You gonna shoot an elected official?" is presented not as a part of her broader critique of militarized responses to civil protest but as an isolated moment of conflict.

The Analysis: Keilar's questioning of Waters' methodology — specifically, whether "yelling at them was the right way to show concern" — misses the forest for the trees. It's a moment that demands deeper analysis. Waters' response underscores a critical point: her actions were not about the act of yelling but about the necessity of being heard in a chaotic and potentially dangerous environment.

Media Narratives and Political Reality

This incident, as reported, is emblematic of how media narratives can sometimes skew more towards sensationalism than substance. Keilar's framing of the event around the confrontation, rather than the reasons behind Waters' presence or the broader issues at play (such as the militarization of the response to civil protests and the rights of elected officials to oversee government operations), reflects a systemic issue within political coverage.

The Real Questions Unasked: - Why is the National Guard being used to block elected officials from accessing federal buildings? - What does this say about the state of democracy and civil liberties in the United States? - How are peaceful protesters and their concerns being addressed by the government?

Conclusion: The Need for Nuanced Reporting

In the end, this incident is not merely about whether Waters was "yelling" or "projecting." It's about the fundamental rights of assembly, speech, and oversight in a democracy — and the role of the media in covering those issues with the depth and nuance they deserve. It's crucial for the media to not only capture the drama of the moment but to also provide context and analysis that helps the public understand the complex dynamics at play.

The interaction between Rep. Maxine Waters and the National Guard, and its subsequent coverage, serves as a reminder of the challenges facing both our political and media landscapes. In a time of heightened tensions and polarized discourse, the need for clarity, depth, and understanding in reporting has never been more critical.
