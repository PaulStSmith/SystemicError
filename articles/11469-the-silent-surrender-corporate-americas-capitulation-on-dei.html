The Silent Surrender: Corporate America’s Capitulation on DEI

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Fraud of Conditional CommitmentFascism Works in SilenceThe “Meritocracy” LieThe Politics of CowardiceHanging Together—or Hanging AloneThe Road AheadConclusion

The Fortune piece on the decline of DEI language in corporate filings is more than just another datapoint in the culture wars—it is a flashing red warning light about the speed and depth of corporate America’s capitulation to Trumpism and the GOP’s authoritarian project. This isn’t a cosmetic shift in vocabulary. It’s an act of submission, a bow to the pressure campaign that seeks not only to kill diversity initiatives but to erase even the mention of them from public discourse. If “equity” has become a dirty word in the boardrooms of the most powerful companies in the world, then the fascist forces already hold the upper hand.

Let’s start with the facts. According to The Conference Board’s report, the use of “DEI” declined 68% from 2024 to 2025 in major corporate filings. More than a third of S&P 100 companies have simply abandoned the word “equity” altogether. Disclosures about workforce demographics—such as the number of women in management or on boards—have fallen sharply. And all of this coincides with Trump’s new executive orders and the GOP’s growing legal crusade against diversity programs. The timing isn’t a coincidence. It’s the cause.

The executive class is reading the political room. They see a federal government openly hostile to the very concept of diversity, a judiciary stacked with right-wing ideologues itching to strike down anything branded as “woke,” and a Republican Party eager to drag CEOs into court if their companies dare acknowledge systemic inequities. Faced with this environment, corporate America is retreating—not fighting, not resisting, not even equivocating. They’re retreating, erasing their own words from their own reports to stay on the good side of an ascendant authoritarian regime.

The Fraud of Conditional Commitment

The most galling aspect of this retreat is that it lays bare just how shallow and transactional the corporate embrace of DEI always was. In 2020, when George Floyd’s murder sparked worldwide protests and companies were desperate to avoid being branded as complicit, they flooded the airwaves with pledges, commitments, and lofty promises. They hired Chief Diversity Officers, they promised transparency, they committed to equity benchmarks. It looked, at least on the surface, like a turning point.

But 2025 reveals the truth: their commitment was never rooted in conviction. It was rooted in PR calculus. As soon as the winds shifted, as soon as Trump returned to power and began wielding the hammer of executive authority against diversity programs, the pledges crumbled. DEI wasn’t a value, it was a brand exercise—and like any brand strategy, it was abandoned once it became a liability.

This is not the first time corporate America has followed this script. Think of climate change pledges that evaporated once oil prices surged, or LGBTQ-friendly campaigns that disappear the moment red-state boycotts threaten sales. The pattern is familiar: progressive language when it’s profitable, silence when it’s costly. The question is whether we, as a society, will finally stop mistaking corporate opportunism for moral leadership.

Fascism Works in Silence

Authoritarianism doesn’t always march in boots. Sometimes it advances in whispers and omissions. The silencing of DEI language in corporate filings may seem minor, but it is a crucial battlefield. Because when corporations stop even saying the word “equity,” they help normalize the idea that equity is dangerous, subversive, or un-American. They reinforce the narrative that diversity itself is legally risky. And they send a chilling message down the organizational ladder: stay quiet, or you might be next.

This is precisely how fascism operates. Not always by outright banning ideas, but by making them radioactive—too dangerous to mention in public, too costly to defend. Once the chilling effect sets in, the silence becomes self-reinforcing. CEOs don’t need to be told to censor themselves; they do it voluntarily, to protect the bottom line. And when the richest and most powerful corporations in America silence themselves, the authoritarian project is already halfway complete.

The “Meritocracy” Lie

The quotes in Fortune’s piece are telling. Ani Huang of the HR Policy Association insists that companies are still “focused on meritocracy” and are merely choosing to pursue diversity “quietly” for legal reasons. This is a perfect encapsulation of the corporate delusion. Meritocracy has always been the convenient myth used to paper over structural inequities. To claim that DEI can be quietly advanced without public accountability is to admit that it will be quietly abandoned when convenient.

DEI without disclosure is performative at best, fraudulent at worst. The entire purpose of disclosure was to hold companies accountable—to measure whether their boardrooms, their management ranks, their pay structures were actually changing. To retreat from disclosure while insisting the commitment remains is the corporate equivalent of saying, “Trust us.” But if 2025 has proven anything, it’s that corporations cannot be trusted when it comes to equity. Without transparency, there is no accountability. Without accountability, there is no progress.

The Politics of Cowardice

Make no mistake: this retreat is political. Companies aren’t hiding diversity language because it’s irrelevant to their operations. They’re hiding it because they fear the wrath of Trump and the GOP. They fear lawsuits, investigations, boycotts. They fear being targeted as examples in the next culture-war skirmish. And so they choose silence over solidarity.

This cowardice isn’t just shameful—it’s dangerous. By abandoning even the vocabulary of equity, corporations are signaling that they will not defend their own employees if doing so risks political consequences. They are throwing women, people of color, and marginalized groups under the bus to preserve short-term shareholder value. They are proving that in the contest between human dignity and political expediency, expediency wins every time.

Hanging Together—or Hanging Alone

There’s a lesson here that echoes across history: authoritarian movements thrive when their opponents refuse to stand together. “We must all hang together, or assuredly we shall all hang separately,” Benjamin Franklin said during another era of existential struggle. The same principle applies now. When companies peel off one by one, choosing silence and secrecy, they isolate themselves and weaken the broader resistance. If every company “does it quietly,” then equity dies quietly—and Trumpism wins loudly.

This is the deeper tragedy of the DEI rollback. It isn’t just about corporate filings or HR policies. It’s about the symbolic surrender of some of the most powerful institutions in America. If they cannot stand up to Trump’s bullying, who can? If they cannot defend the principle of equity, who will? Their silence does not buy safety; it buys complicity.

The Road Ahead

What happens next depends on whether workers, shareholders, and the public demand more than quiet compliance. It depends on whether we see through the “meritocracy” smokescreen and insist on real transparency, real disclosure, real accountability. It depends on whether we recognize that fascism isn’t defeated by whispers, but by collective, public defiance.

The Fortune report makes it plain: the corporate class is not going to save us. They never were. Equity will survive only if people outside the boardroom force it to. Through unions, through organizing, through public shaming, through shareholder revolts. By making silence costlier than speech. By refusing to let the most powerful companies in the world pretend that they are “quietly committed” while actively erasing equity from the record.

Conclusion

The decline of DEI language in corporate America is not a coincidence. It is a symptom of a broader authoritarian project that seeks to erase diversity, equity, and inclusion from the American lexicon. It shows us that corporate America’s commitment was always conditional, always fragile, always expendable. And it reminds us that unless we stand together, proudly and publicly, against the forces of fascism, we will each be picked off in silence.

The choice is stark. Hang together, or hang alone. Equity can survive only in the light, never in the shadows.
