'Obvious risks': 8 state treasurers confront Tesla chair over Elon Musk’s ability to lead

Tesla/SpaceX/X.com leader Elon Musk is, like his close ally, President Donald Trump, a highly polarizing figure in U.S. politics. And Musk's political activities, including his work with his Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) in the United States and his support of Europe's far-right Alternative For Germany party, aren't helping Tesla from a business standpoint: the company is being targeted for boycotts and protests in the United States and other countries.

According to the European Automobile Manufacturers Association, sales of Tesla's electric vehicles fell by 49 percent during the first two months of 2025.

Now, according to Bloomberg News reporter Dana Hull, the treasurers of eight U.S. states — including California's Malia Cohen and Illinois' Michael Frerichs — are voicing their concerns to Tesla chair Robyn Denholm in an open letter.

Hull, in an article published on Tuesday, April 22, reports, "The overseers of funds and investments for California, Illinois and half a dozen other states cite Tesla's sagging stock, disappointing first-quarter deliveries and surge of trade-ins by vehicle owners as being among their causes for concern…. The letter — pulled together in coordination with the advocacy group Americans for Responsible Growth — heaps more pressure on Tesla and Musk ahead of the company releasing first-quarter earnings results after the close Tuesday."

In their letter, the eight state treasurers told Denholm, "CEO Elon Musk continues to divide his attention across multiple companies and a high-profile advisory role within the federal government. These external commitments raise serious questions about whether Tesla's leadership is fully engaged in addressing the company's core challenges."

Cohen, Frerichs and the six others urged Tesla's board of directors to take action.

"The board's role is especially critical now — to provide strong oversight, uphold fiduciary standards and ensure that the company's leadership is aligned with the long-term best interests of the company," the state treasurers write. "Public officials like us do not take the step of raising these concerns lightly except when the obvious risks demand it."

Our Analysis:

The Musk Conundrum: Political Polarization Meets Corporate Accountability

Elon Musk, a figure synonymous with both visionary tech advancements and contentious political affiliations, is once again at the heart of controversy. This time, the backlash isn't just echoing through the halls of social media or speculative opinion pieces—it's palpable in Tesla's plummeting sales figures and the collective outcry of state treasurers across the United States.

The Fallout of Political Allegiances

Musk's dalliance with divisive political activities, notably his association with President Donald Trump and support for Europe's far-right Alternative For Germany party, has transcended the realm of personal ideology to materially impact Tesla's business operations. The consequence? A staggering 49 percent drop in Tesla's electric vehicle sales in the first two months of 2025, as per the European Automobile Manufacturers Association.

This stark downturn is not merely a reflection of consumer disenchantment but a resounding rebuke of Musk's political entanglements. The efforts to intertwine Silicon Valley innovation with populist and far-right politics have not only alienated a significant portion of Tesla's consumer base but have also sparked calls for boycotts and protests on a global scale.

A Plea for Corporate Governance

In an unprecedented move, treasurers from eight U.S. states, led by figures such as California's Malia Cohen and Illinois' Michael Frerichs, have taken their grievances directly to Tesla's doorstep—or, more accurately, to Tesla chair Robyn Denholm. Their open letter, a meticulously coordinated effort with Americans for Responsible Growth, lays bare the anxieties surrounding Musk's divided attentions and the perceived negligence in confronting Tesla's spiraling challenges.

The state treasurers' letter is not merely an administrative formality; it's a clarion call for robust corporate governance. It underscores a pressing need for Tesla's board of directors to exert stronger oversight, uphold fiduciary duties, and realign the company's leadership with its long-term interests. This intervention is portrayed not as an overreach but as a necessary stance against the "obvious risks" posed by Musk's external commitments, including his high-profile advisory role within the federal government.

The Broader Implications

This episode transcends the immediate business implications for Tesla and taps into a larger discourse on the responsibilities of corporate leaders in politically charged times. Musk, by virtue of his outsized influence and the platforms at his disposal, wields considerable power to shape public opinion and political landscapes. However, with great power comes great responsibility—a maxim seemingly lost in Musk's pursuit of political engagement.

The treasurers' letter and the ensuing public discourse may serve as a watershed moment, prompting a reevaluation of the extent to which corporate figures can and should engage in political activities. Moreover, it highlights the increasingly blurred lines between business interests and political advocacy, urging a recalibration of corporate governance norms to safeguard against the risks of such entanglements.

In Conclusion

The saga of Elon Musk and Tesla's political and business entanglements is a stark reminder of the delicate balance between corporate leadership and political activism. As Tesla grapples with the fallout of Musk's political affiliations, the broader implications for corporate governance and political neutrality in business practices loom large. The message from the state treasurers, though directed at Tesla, resonates far beyond, serving as a cautionary tale for corporate leaders navigating the treacherous waters of political involvement.
