'Huge failure': Ex-Trump aide warns Trump's staff isn't telling him the truth

"The View's" co-host Alyssa Farah Griffin believes that President Donald Trump's staff either isn't sharing the reality with him or isn't telling him the truth.

Trump spoke to ABC News in an interview for the first 100 days, and made statements that concerned his former White House communications director.

Among the things Trump argued about was that a Maryland man, whom the government acknowledged in court was wrongfully deported, was an MS-13 gang member because he had the letters written on his hand. In fact, Kilmar Ábrego García had tattoos on his hand that included images like a cannabis leaf, a smiley face, a cross and a skull. Someone digitally put the letters above the symbols, claiming they represented MS-13.

Speaking to ABC News, however, it became clear Trump was confused, and thought those typed letters and numbers were part of the tattoo. Family photos confirm that the MS-13 had been digitally written.

"What stood out was two things, this MS-13 bit — it seemed very clear to me, knowing Donald Trump, he was not briefed that that is not a real image and it was photoshopped," said Griffin. "That is a huge failure of his staff if they are not saying before these interviews, sir, you should not reference that."

Co-host Sunny Hostin said that she thinks Trump's staff is "scared of him."

"And it speaks to a sort of timidity," Griffin continued. "Donald Trump won on immigration and he's underwater. Only 45% on it. And it's because he's focusing on deporting people without due process under law. What makes America the greatest nation on Earth is that you get that due process. Donald Trump of all people should be glad that you're innocent until proven guilty."

The crowd laughed and applauded at the comment.

It isn't the only issue. In an interview with TIME magazine, Trump was asked whether he was defying the U.S. Supreme Court, which demanded the administration "facilitate" the release of Ábrego García from the prison in El Salvador.

“That’s not what my people told me — they didn’t say it was, they said it was — the nine to nothing was entirely different," Trump said.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Disconnect: Trump and the Truth

In a recent episode of "The View," co-host Alyssa Farah Griffin, a former White House communications director under President Donald Trump, shed light on an alarming issue: the potential disconnect between Trump and reality, as facilitated by his own staff. This commentary slices through the veneer of political maneuvering to expose a deeper systemic failure within Trump's administration.

The MS-13 Misinformation

At the heart of Griffin's concern is Trump's misrepresentation of a Maryland man, Kilmar Ábrego García, as an MS-13 gang member in an ABC News interview. This claim was based on digitally altered images that falsely added the letters "MS-13" above genuine tattoos on García's hand. Griffin's assertion that Trump was likely uninformed about the image being a fabrication implies a dangerous gap in the flow of accurate information to the President. It's a stark example of how misinformation can be weaponized at the highest levels of government, impacting individuals' lives and broader immigration policies.

The Staff's Silence: Fear or Complicity?

Sunny Hostin's observation that Trump's staff might be "scared of him" and Griffin's subsequent remarks on their "timidity" spotlight a toxic culture of fear and silence. This environment, where correcting or challenging the President becomes taboo, not only undermines the integrity of the office but also jeopardizes the democratic principles of transparency and accountability.

Trump's Immigration Stance: A Dangerous Oversimplification

Griffin criticizes Trump's focus on deporting individuals without due process, highlighting a fundamental misunderstanding (or deliberate misrepresentation) of America's legal values: the right to due process and the presumption of innocence. Trump's stance on immigration, as framed by Griffin, reflects a broader systemic error—a willingness to sacrifice these principles for political gain.

Defying the Supreme Court?

The article also touches on an interaction between Trump and TIME magazine regarding the U.S. Supreme Court's order to facilitate Ábrego García's release from an El Salvadorian prison. Trump's confused response, suggesting misinformation from his advisers, further underscores the disconnection between the President and the judicial realities of his policies. This not only raises concerns about the competence of Trump's staff but also about his willingness to adhere to the rule of law.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error of Monumental Proportions

The insights from Griffin and Hostin on "The View" reveal more than just anecdotal evidence of Trump's misinformation; they highlight a systemic flaw within his administration. The failure to accurately inform the President, whether due to fear or incompetence, and the consequent spread of misinformation, poses a direct threat to democratic governance and justice. Furthermore, Trump's apparent disregard for judicial mandates and due process encapsulates a broader disregard for the pillars of American democracy.

In essence, the issue is not just about a single digitally altered image or a misinformed statement; it's about a systemic error where misinformation, fear, and disrespect for legal principles are allowed to flourish. This scenario is a cautionary tale of how leadership detached from truth and accountability can veer dangerously off course, with real consequences for individuals and the nation as a whole.
