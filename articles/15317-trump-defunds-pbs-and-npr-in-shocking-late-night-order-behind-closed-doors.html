Trump defunds PBS and NPR in shocking late-night order behind closed doors

U.S. President Donald Trump on Thursday signed an executive order calling for an end to taxpayer funding for NPR and PBS, an escalation of his assault on the media that could shutter hundreds of local stations across the country.

The president's order, which he signed behind closed doors, echoes a section of Project 2025, a far-right agenda that called for stripping public funding from NPR, PBS, and other broadcasters on the grounds that they "do not even bother to run programming that would attract conservatives."

Trump's order instructs the Corporation for Public Broadcasting (CPB)—a private nonprofit corporation created and funded by Congress—to "cease direct funding to NPR and PBS, consistent with my administration's policy to ensure that federal funding does not support biased and partisan news coverage."

The executive order, which is expected to face legal challenges, also directs all federal agencies to "identify and terminate, to the maximum extent consistent with applicable law, any direct or indirect funding of NPR and PBS."

Trump's move was expected, and it came in the wake of reports that the administration intends to ask Congress to rescind previously approved funding for CPB, which is already engaged in a court fight with the president over his attempt to fire several of the organization's board members. The Associated Press reported Thursday that the rescission request "has not yet been sent to Capitol Hill."

According to the organizations' estimates, federal funding accounts for roughly 1% of NPR's annual budget and 15% of PBS's yearly revenue.

In a letter to congressional leaders earlier this week, a coalition of civil society groups led by Reporters Without Borders (RSF) warned that, if enacted, Trump's proposed funding cuts for public broadcasting "will result in the shutdown of dozens, if not hundreds, of local, independent radio and television stations serving Americans in every corner of the country."

"As it stands, public media journalists are often the only reporters attending a school board meeting, or a local zoning hearing, or at the scene of a crime," the groups wrote. "They are the journalists most likely to hold local public officials accountable and expose
corruption. Faraway digital media outlets will not replicate this coverage, and the American public will lose out."

Trump's attack on public broadcasters is part of his administration's broader effort to undermine journalism in the United States and around the world.

RSF said in a report published Friday that Trump's "early moves in his second mandate to politicize the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), ban The Associated Press from the White House, or dismantle the U.S. Agency for Global Media, for example, have jeopardized the country's news outlets and indicate that he intends to follow through on his threats, setting up a potential crisis for American journalism."

"After a century of gradual expansion of press rights in the United States," the group said, "the country is experiencing its first significant and prolonged decline in press freedom in modern history, and Donald Trump's return to the presidency is greatly exacerbating the situation."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Crusade Against Public Broadcasting: A Dangerous Gamble with American Democracy

In a move that starkly illustrates the Trump administration's ongoing campaign against the free press, the President has signed an executive order aimed at stripping public funding from NPR and PBS. This action not only escalates his war on the media but also poses a direct threat to the survival of hundreds of local stations across the nation. The decision, made behind the veil of secrecy, draws its rationale from the far-right Project 2025 agenda, which accuses these broadcasters of failing to cater to conservative audiences.

The Pretext of Bias: A Cloak for Censorship

Trump's justification for this draconian measure rests on the allegation of biased and partisan news coverage. By instructing the Corporation for Public Broadcasting (CPB) to end funding for NPR and PBS, the President claims to be safeguarding federal funding from supporting what he deems "biased" media. This action is not only a gross misrepresentation of public broadcasting's mission and record but also a clear attempt to punish media outlets that he perceives as enemies.

It's critical to note that federal funding constitutes a minor part of NPR's budget (roughly 1%) and a more significant but still manageable portion of PBS's revenue (15%). Therefore, the argument that taxpayer dollars are being squandered on partisan programming falls flat. Instead, what this move signifies is a blatant attempt to silence critical journalism that doesn't align with the administration's narrative.

The Real Cost: A Blow to Local Journalism

The ramifications of Trump's executive order extend far beyond the ideological battlegrounds of Washington politics. By targeting public broadcasting's financial lifeline, the administration endangers the very fabric of local journalism. Public broadcasters have long been the backbone of local news ecosystems, often providing the only coverage of critical community issues such as school board meetings, local governance, and public accountability.

A coalition of civil society groups, including Reporters Without Borders (RSF), has rightly warned that these funding cuts could lead to the shutdown of countless local, independent radio and television stations. This would not only be a loss for journalism but a direct hit to the public's right to know, particularly in underserved and rural areas where local media is already in retreat.

Undermining Democracy

The broader context of Trump's attack on NPR and PBS is his administration's systematic effort to undermine journalism both in the United States and globally. Early actions to politicize the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), restrict press access, and dismantle international broadcasting entities signal a deep-rooted antipathy towards press freedom. This trajectory represents a significant regression in the country's commitment to press rights, marking a "prolonged decline in press freedom" in modern American history.

Conclusion

Trump's executive order is not merely a policy disagreement or an exercise in budget trimming; it is a dangerous gamble with the principles of democracy and free speech. Public broadcasting has served as a critical medium for unbiased, locally-focused journalism that holds power to account and informs the American populace. This attempt to dismantle a cornerstone of American journalism reveals a disturbing willingness to sacrifice democratic ideals for political gain.

As these developments unfold, it's imperative that citizens, lawmakers, and the courts recognize the stakes involved and act to protect one of the nation's most valuable assets: a free, independent, and vibrant press. The future of American democracy may very well depend on it.
