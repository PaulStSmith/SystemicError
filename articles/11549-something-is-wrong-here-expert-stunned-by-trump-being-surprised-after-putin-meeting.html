'Something is wrong here': Expert stunned by Trump being 'surprised' after Putin meeting

By Paulo Santos

In a post-mortem on Donald Trump’s meeting with Vladimir Putin in Alaska on Friday, the former head of the Council on Foreign Relations expressed alarm that the U.S. president appeared to be “surprised” at the turn the talks took on ending the Ukraine war.

Appearing on MSNBC’s “The Weekend,” diplomat Richard Hass claimed negotiations have a long way to go before reaching the “peace” Trump is now claiming is the next step.He then went on to note what he felt was alarming at how the U.S. entered into the diplomatic efforts.

“To me, one of the most surprising things of Alaska was how unconditional the entire thing was,” he told the hosts. ‘You're right, Putin not just was, but is a pariah; the attacks continue, the war crimes continue.”

“While they were meeting, it appears,” co-host Eugene Daniels interjected.

“Absolutely,” Haas agreed. “So to give him an unconditional reception seems to me wrong.”

He then continued, “This all should have been wired, which is my larger point: the idea that the American officials seem surprised and disappointed.”

“Look,” he elaborated. “I've worked for several presidents. I worked for four years at the White House for George Bush, the father. The idea that a president would go to a summit only to be surprised? These ought to be the most wired interactions. There was almost nothing left for uncertainty.”

“No conversations that happened beforehand, the president just putting a bow on it,” Daniels prompted his guest.

"Exactly, 99 percent of it ought to be wired,” Haas replied. “Not a guarantee, but wired. This seems to me to have been just the opposite. And I don't know if Putin misled the United States, if the envoy, Steve Witkoff, simply got played. I don't know if Donald Trump simply was so cocky that he thought the power of his personality could bring Putin around, but the idea that you would have had Putin come literally and figuratively –– the red carpet –– and have virtually nothing result from it? And then the president leaves saying it's so now it's up to Zelenskyy? It just seems to me something is wrong here with how this was handled diplomatically, how this was handled. The foreign policy craft, if you will, of this seems sorely lacking.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Putin Summit: A Study in Diplomatic Folly

The recent post-mortem analysis of Donald Trump's meeting with Vladimir Putin, as provided by former Council on Foreign Relations head Richard Haas on MSNBC, offers a startling glimpse into what can only be described as a diplomatic debacle of significant proportions. The details of this meeting, rather than suggesting a step towards peace in Ukraine as Trump claimed, reveal a staggering level of unpreparedness and naivety that is alarming at best and dangerously incompetent at worst.

The Summit of Surprises

First and foremost, the very notion that a U.S. president could be "surprised" by the direction of talks with a figure as predictably unpredictable as Putin is in itself a colossal failure. This isn't just a minor oversight; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of how diplomacy operates at the highest levels. As Haas pointed out, these summits are supposed to be "wired" to the nth degree, with virtually nothing left to chance. The fact that Trump and his team were apparently caught off-guard speaks volumes about their approach to foreign policy - an approach that seems to rely more on bravado and wishful thinking than on strategic planning and expert insight.

The Unconditional Reception

Haas's criticism of the meeting's unconditional nature is particularly damning. By rolling out the metaphorical red carpet for Putin - a leader continuously engaged in acts that starkly violate international norms and human rights - the U.S. not only undermines its own position but also tacitly legitimizes Putin's actions on the world stage. This is not just a diplomatic misstep; it's a moral failure. The war crimes and attacks that continued even as the talks were happening should have been a red line, not a backdrop for negotiations.

The Diplomatic Misfire

The analysis further suggests a disturbing lack of coordination and preparation on the part of the American officials involved. Whether it was a case of being misled by Putin, outplayed by envoy Steve Witkoff, or Trump's misplaced confidence in his personal charm, the outcome is the same: a squandered opportunity to assert U.S. interests and values. The assertion that the summit's results (or lack thereof) now somehow leave the ball in Ukraine's court is not just absurd but irresponsible. Diplomacy, especially in matters of war and peace, is not a game to be passed off when convenient.

Conclusion: A Lesson in What Not to Do

This post-mortem dissection of the Trump-Putin summit by Richard Haas is not merely an academic exercise; it's a cautionary tale about the perils of hubris and the critical importance of expert-led diplomacy. The world is far too complex, and the stakes are far too high, for international diplomacy to be treated as an afterthought or a stage for personal grandstanding.

Moving forward, one can only hope that this episode serves as a stark reminder of the need for preparedness, strategic thinking, and a deep understanding of international relations in the crafting of foreign policy. Anything less is not just inadequate; it's dangerously negligent.
