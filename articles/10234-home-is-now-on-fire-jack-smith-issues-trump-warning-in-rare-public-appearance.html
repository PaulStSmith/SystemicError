'Home is now on fire': Jack Smith issues Trump warning in 'rare public appearance'

Former Special Counsel Jack Smith issued a stark warning about Donald Trump in a "rare public appearance," according to a legal expert.

Legal analyst Allison Gill, better known as Mueller, She Wrote, noted on Saturday that Smith "delivered remarks at George Mason University this week, warning of the weaponization of the Justice Department under the Trump administration."

After reminding readers that Smith "was appointed by Merrick Garland in November of 2022 to continue the ongoing investigations into the January 6th attack on the Capitol and the unlawful retention of national defense information and subsequent obstruction of justice by Donald Trump," Gill pointed out how she got the details about the lowkey meeting.

"Former Deputy Director of the FBI Andrew McCabe, was in the audience and described to me what he heard," she wrote. "Smith spoke about the rule of law being under attack in a way that he hasn’t seen in his lifetime, and that he’s saddened by the firing of selfless public servants, the government using its vast powers to target citizens for exercising their constitutional rights, and the loss of credibility with the courts and the people."

She also added, "If you listen to the UnJustified podcast, you’ll recall that Andy and I have been talking about the destruction of the presumption of regularity and the loss of the court’s faith in the Justice Department at great length. Jack Smith shared those sentiments in his speech."

“The DOJ was my home for many, many years. That home is now on fire,” Smith said, according to Gill.

The report also states, "He spoke at length about what a society absent the rule of law looks like. It’s a society where critics and perceived enemies are targeted in an effort to silence them. One where prosecutors are left to figure out the basis for a case after the fact. And one where friends and allies of the administration face no legal consequences."

“In a society where the rule of law is eroding, you can have a situation where if you’re a Latino citizen… you can be stopped, detained, your liberty taken because of the color of your skin. Or maybe because you were speaking Spanish… whereas we all know that if you were white, that would never happen," Smith reportedly said.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Warning from Jack Smith: A Critical Analysis

In a move that breaks away from the shadows of discretion typically enveloping the corridors of justice, Former Special Counsel Jack Smith has projected a chilling narrative concerning the Trump administration's alleged manipulation of the Department of Justice (DOJ). This revelation, courtesy of legal analyst Allison Gill, better known as Mueller, She Wrote, sheds light on an alarming discourse delivered at George Mason University. Here, we dissect the implications of Smith's warnings, not just as political rhetoric, but as a foreboding of a constitutional crisis at our doorstep.

The Weaponization of Justice

Smith's tenure, initiated by Merrick Garland in November 2022, has been anything but a tranquil pursuit. Tasked with unraveling the convoluted threads of the January 6th Capitol attack alongside allegations of unlawful retention of national defense information by Donald Trump, Smith's role is pivotal in safeguarding the sanctity of democracy and the rule of law. His observations, therefore, carry the weight of firsthand engagement with the intricacies of these investigations.

"The rule of law being under attack in a way that he hasn’t seen in his lifetime" is not merely an opinion; it's a stark indictment of the current state of affairs. The dismissal of "selfless public servants," the targeting of citizens for exercising constitutional rights, and the diminishing credibility of the DOJ in the eyes of the courts and the populace, as Smith outlines, are symptoms of a democracy in distress.

The Loss of Presumption of Regularity

The concept of the presumption of regularity — the assumption that public officials perform their duties correctly and legally — is fundamental to the trust between the government and governed. Smith's lamentations, echoed by Gill and former Deputy Director of the FBI Andrew McCabe, underscore a profound erosion of this trust. The assertion that the DOJ, once Smith's "home," is now "on fire" is a metaphor too powerful to ignore. It speaks volumes about the internal decay facilitated by partisan manipulations and vendettas.

A Society Absent the Rule of Law

Smith's portrayal of a society floundering without the rule of law is dystopian. Critics and perceived enemies of the administration targeted to silence dissent, prosecutors fishing for justifications post-hoc, and a selective application of the law that shields friends and allies of the administration — these are the hallmarks of authoritarian regimes, not a republic founded on democratic principles and the rule of law.

The mention of racial profiling — where "a Latino citizen… can be stopped, detained, your liberty taken because of the color of your skin" — is a powerful reminder of the intersectionality of justice, race, and the erosion of civil liberties. It's a scenario where the rule of law is not just eroding but is selectively applied based on race and ethnicity, a direct contravention of the equal protection under the law guaranteed by the Constitution.

In Conclusion

Jack Smith's rare public appearance and the subsequent revelations should not be dismissed as mere political commentary. They represent a clarion call to recognize and rectify the creeping politicization and weaponization of the Department of Justice. The implications of Smith's warnings extend beyond the tenure of any single administration; they touch upon the very foundations of American democracy and its enduring commitment to the rule of law.

The question now is not just about the legal battles that lie ahead for Donald Trump, but about whether the systemic issues highlighted by Smith can be addressed — to extinguish the fires within the DOJ and restore the presumption of regularity, without which the fabric of justice in America risks unraveling.
