US ally puts Iran deal in jeopardy as top official urges defiance of Trump

Ben-Gvir’s Table-Pounding Is Not Diplomacy. It Is a Veto by Proxy.

The Real Center of Power

The source story is not really about a peace process. It is about who can interrupt one. President Donald Trump is trying to assemble a deal with Iran, but the Israeli security minister Itamar Ben-Gvir is openly signaling that Israel will try to block it by pressuring Netanyahu to intervene. That is the power map: Washington negotiates, Jerusalem sabotages, and the public is asked to mistake the sabotage for hard-nosed urgency.

Who Made the Choice

The consequences in Lebanon are not accidental drift. Israel has kept bombing southern Lebanon despite Trump’s own statement on April 17 that Israel was “prohibited” from bombing Lebanon any longer. Israel answered the next day with more strikes, including the killing of four paramedics, then expanded its ground invasion beyond its own demarcation line. Those are decisions, not misunderstandings. The chain of responsibility runs through the political leadership that authorizes escalation and the officials who cheer it on when restraint becomes inconvenient.

The Blame Game

Ben-Gvir’s demand that Netanyahu “pound the table” is a familiar maneuver: recast aggression as firmness, and recast any constraint as weakness. That framing is useful because it shifts attention away from the actual victims in Lebanon, where the Health Ministry says more than 3,100 people have been killed and nearly 10,000 injured since early April. The rhetoric turns deliberate punishment into an argument about tone. It invites the press to cover political theater instead of the machinery of violence.

Deliberate Cruelty Disguised as Security

Ben-Gvir’s call to cut electricity to southern Lebanon is not a policy argument in any ordinary sense. It is a proposal to deepen a humanitarian crisis by targeting infrastructure that civilians need to survive. The logic is collective punishment with a ministerial accent. This is how authoritarian habits travel inside democratic institutions: not through formal declarations of barbarism, but through officials speaking as though deprivation were prudence and punishment were deterrence.

Trump’s Negotiation, Israel’s Spoiler Role

The source makes clear that any emerging U.S.-Iran deal likely hinges on Israel halting its bombing campaign in Lebanon. That is the point Ben-Gvir cannot tolerate. His complaint is not about ambiguity or misunderstanding. It is about losing freedom to escalate. The Israeli far right does not want a diplomatic settlement that constrains military action, because restraint would expose what the bombing already is: a policy choice with a civilian body count attached.

The Pattern

This story shows how war sustains itself through layered responsibility. One leader seeks a deal, another demands a veto, and the civilians below are left to absorb the consequences. The public narrative will likely treat this as a tangle of competing pressures. It is simpler than that. There is the power to stop bombing, the choice not to use it, and the loud insistence that continued destruction is somehow the only serious option.
