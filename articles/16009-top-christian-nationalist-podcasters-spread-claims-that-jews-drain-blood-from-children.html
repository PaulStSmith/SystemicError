Top Christian nationalist podcasters spread claims that Jews 'drain blood' from children

A pair of right-wing podcasters trying to establish a Christian nationalist community in Tennessee have been promoting virulent anti-Semitism.

Pastor Andrew Isker and his sidekick C.Jay Engel, the hosts of what they describe as "the No. 1 Christian nationalist podcast in the world," have spread anti-Semitic claims and hosted Holocaust deniers and Nazi sympathizers on their Contra Mundum podcast, reported WTVF-TV.

“Jews in the biblical sense, right, they do not exist today anymore, right?" Isker said in a recent interview with far-right pastor Joel Webbon. "It's only Christians and non-Christians."

The pair have invited technology entrepreneur Ron Unz, who runs a website that links to white supremacist and anti-Semitic content, to discuss Nazi sympathizer Charles Lindberg, who Engel declared as "one of the great American heroes of the 20th Century."

"Ron Unz is an idea man and, unfortunately, the vast majority of his ideas are anti-Semitic and the vast majority of the people who he platforms share many of those anti-Semitic ideas," said Aryeh Tuchman, director of the Anti-Defamation League’s Center on Extremism.

Isker – who holds unconventional views about airport scanners – promoted essays written by Unz in which he claims that "per capita Jews were the greatest mass murderers of the 20th Century” and “religious Jews apparently pray to Satan almost as readily as they pray to God," and he endorses the vile blood libel myth alleging that Jews once "kidnapped small Christian children in order to drain their blood for use in various magic rituals."

"Essentially, they are promoting [Unz] as a source of truth, as a trusted source of opinion about historical issues — and this is the person who has denied the Holocaust and has cast the history of the world as one in which Jewish people are the villains," Tuchman said.

Isker has said that he believes Jews should be treated as second-class citizens because, in his view, “this country belongs to Jesus,' but the pair issued a statement denying that were anti-Semitic.

“We deny accusations of anti-Semitism and see them as evidence that there is no actual story here, but merely the obsession of an activist journalist in the midst of Legacy Media’s decline,” Isker and Engel told the TV station in a written statement.

"From time to time we have guests on our podcast that we think will be interesting to our audience," they added. "We don't share all their views on every topic."

Tuchman isn't buying their explanation, however.

"I believe it says quite a bit about the willingness of these people to essentially raise up an anti-Semite and essentially create an on-ramp for their viewers to engage further with Mr. Unz and his ideas," Tuchman said.

Isker and Engel argued that Unz cannot be anti-Semitic because he claims to be Jewish himself, and Tuchman said that explanation is absurd.

“It's a pathetic and laughable defense that anti-Semites use when they are called out," Tuchman said.

Isker and Engel moved to Tennessee with aims of creating a community so-called "Heritage Americans," who are depicted in their social media posts with images of Norman Rockwell's idyllic depictions of midcentury life, and they have explicitly stated their longing for the American way of life before women joined the workforce and civil rights "ruined everything."

The real estate component of their Jackson County project is led by two related companies, New Founding and RidgeRunner, which started with an initial project in nearby Burkesville, Kentucky, before launching the Tennessee phase last year.

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Shadows of 'Christian Nationalism'

In a recent exposé, WTVF-TV has shed light on the disturbing activities of Pastor Andrew Isker and C.Jay Engel, who have taken it upon themselves to champion what they dub "the No. 1 Christian nationalist podcast in the world," Contra Mundum. What unfolds is not just a story about two right-wing podcasters but a chilling glimpse into the heart of a movement that seeks to cloak its vile ideologies in the guise of faith and patriotism.

Promotion of Anti-Semitism: A Dangerous Game

At the core of Isker and Engel's rhetoric is a virulent strain of anti-Semitism. Their claims—ranging from denying the Jewish identity in the modern world to promoting Holocaust deniers and Nazi sympathizers—are not only abhorrent but dangerously revisionist. By stating that "Jews in the biblical sense...do not exist today anymore," Isker not only erases the identity and history of an entire group of people but also sets a dangerous precedent for the erasure of their rights and existence in society.

The duo's promotion of Ron Unz, a figure notorious for his anti-Semitic claims, further underscores their dangerous agenda. Unz's assertions, including the insidious claim that "per capita Jews were the greatest mass murderers of the 20th Century,” are not just repulsive; they are a deliberate attempt to rewrite history and fuel hatred.

A Flawed Defense

In response to accusations of anti-Semitism, Isker and Engel have offered a defense that is as weak as it is telling. Their claim that such accusations stem from "the obsession of an activist journalist" is a classic diversion tactic, aiming to discredit any criticism by attacking the credibility of the critic rather than addressing the content of the criticism.

Moreover, their assertion that hosting guests with controversial views does not equate to endorsing those views is a thinly veiled attempt to absolve themselves of responsibility. By providing a platform for these ideas, Isker and Engel are, in effect, endorsing and spreading them. Their attempt to shield themselves by highlighting Unz's Jewish heritage—as if it were a get-out-of-jail-free card for promoting anti-Semitism—is both laughable and deeply insulting.

The 'Heritage Americans' Project: A Step Backwards

Isker and Engel's endeavor to establish a Christian nationalist community in Tennessee, aimed at returning to a pre-civil rights and pre-feminist era, is a stark reminder of their regressive vision for America. Their nostalgic longing for an "idyllic" past, represented by Norman Rockwell paintings, ignores the systemic inequalities and injustices that plagued that era. It's a dangerous idealization that seeks to roll back the clock on decades of progress in civil rights and gender equality.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The activities of Isker and Engel, and their attempts to establish a so-called "Heritage Americans" community, represent a broader threat that extends beyond their podcast. It's a glimpse into a movement that seeks to redefine American identity in its own intolerant and exclusionary image. Their brand of Christian nationalism, marked by its anti-Semitism, revisionism, and nostalgia for a bygone era of inequality, must be met with vigilant opposition.

As they attempt to cloak their bigotry in the guise of faith and patriotism, it's crucial that we remain alert to the dangers they pose—not just to the Jewish community, but to the very fabric of American society. The fight against such ideologies is not just about countering false narratives but about protecting the values of diversity, inclusion, and equality that define the best of what America stands for.
