'We're not unified': Rebel GOP lawmaker has unique shield as he lobs attacks at Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 8, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Lone Wolf: Rep. Don Bacon's Stand Against GOP's Tax Legislation

FILE PHOTO: U.S. Representative Don Bacon of (R-NE) faces reporters as he arrives for a House Republican conference meeting to choose a nominee in the race for House Speaker at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, U.S., October 24, 2023. REUTERS/Leah Millis/File Photo

Nebraska centrist Rep. Don Bacon has been one of the rare House Republicans to publicly criticize president Donald Trump, and his vote will be critical to the GOP's tax legislation.

The 61-year-old congressman favors works requirements and more frequent eligibility checks for Medicaid, but he won't budge on his objection to cuts exceeding $500 billion to the health-insurance program that serves low-income and disabled people, despite efforts by GOP leadership to back down, reported the Wall Street Journal.

“Here’s the tactic they’ve been using: ‘Don’t worry about the Senate, they’ll fix it,' and now we’re getting ready to take our third vote on this,” Bacon said, noting that Republican leaders say the Senate will never pass the $900 billion cuts sought in the House. “We feel like we’re being pushed up to the edge of the cliff here.”

Bacon has spoken out against the Trump administration on tariffs, its hostility to allies and defense secretary Pete Hegseth using the non-secure Signal app for military planning, and he also plans to vote against a GOP bill to rename Gulf of Mexico as the Gulf of America, as the president wants to do, and he said fellow Republicans aren't too happy with him.

"[Party leadership] has asked me to try to tone it down a little bit, and I want to be respectful,” he said. “Well, I say I could do a lot more. I’ve been looking for opportunities not to have the opportunity.”

Bacon said he takes only two TV appearances a week out of about 20 requests, while fielding 30 to 40 texts a day from reporters, and he's been taking back stairways at the Capitol to avoid the media.

“I don’t want to be too predictable," he said, "so you got to mix up which doors you’re going out of."

However, Trump has held off on criticizing Bacon during the budget negotiations because Republicans feel they need him to keep a seat that would otherwise go to a Democrat in his highly competitive district, which backed Kamala Harris by nearly 5 points and re-elected Bacon by just 2 points – even if Republicans express frustration with his stands.

“I think that his position is ridiculous,” said Rep. Eric Burlison (R-MO). “He’s got a fixed number, but what if you found out that there’s $500 billion in savings … and he says, ‘Oh, no, I’m committed only to cutting $300 [billion].’ That is an illogical decision.”

Bacon hasn't said he'll seek a sixth term representing Omaha, even while GOP leaders are urging him to run, the congressman said his family has a say in the matter, quoting his 6-year-old grandson.

“Grandpa, when are you going to retire? We want to see you more,” Bacon said, quoting the boy, but said he believes one of his grown children put him up to it.

“It’s easier when you can defend your team,” Bacon added. “I feel like we’re not unified, unfortunately.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Lone Wolf: Rep. Don Bacon's Stand Against GOP's Tax Legislation

In a political landscape that often appears monolithic in its partisanship, Nebraska centrist Rep. Don Bacon emerges as a rare specimen of dissent within the Republican ranks. His public criticism of former President Donald Trump and his crucial stance on GOP's tax legislation spotlight a fissure in the party's facade of unity. Let's dissect this anomaly and its implications with the rigor it demands.

Bacon's Line in the Sand: Medicaid Cuts

At the heart of Bacon's rebellion is his objection to the proposed cuts to Medicaid, a program vital to low-income and disabled Americans. While the GOP leadership, in a maneuver as predictable as it is disheartening, attempts to slash the health-insurance program by over $500 billion, Bacon stands his ground. His refusal to endorse cuts exceeding this amount, despite the party's pressure, is a rare show of spine in a Congress often criticized for its spinelessness. It's a crucial reminder that, within the halls of power, there are still those who prioritize the welfare of the vulnerable over party lines.

Tactics of Diversion and Pressure

The strategy employed by GOP leadership, as Bacon describes, is one of evasion and false assurances - "Don’t worry about the Senate, they’ll fix it." This tactic, aimed at pushing controversial legislation through the House, exemplifies the cynical machinations that erode public trust in the legislative process. Bacon's critique of being pushed "up to the edge of the cliff" is a vivid portrayal of a party willing to gamble with the lives of millions for tax cuts.

A Voice of Dissent Amidst Party Pressure

Bacon's dissent extends beyond Medicaid cuts. His opposition to the renaming of the Gulf of Mexico and his criticism of Trump's tariffs and foreign policy missteps paint the picture of a congressman unwilling to toe the party line blindly. Yet, this independence comes at a cost. The call from party leadership to "tone it down" and Bacon's strategic avoidance of the media spotlight underline the isolating nature of dissent within party ranks. It's a stark reminder of the pressure to conform that pervades our political institutions, often stifling meaningful debate.

The Conundrum of Loyalty and Logic

Rep. Eric Burlison's critique of Bacon's stance as "ridiculous" and "illogical" is emblematic of the ideological rifts that hamper collaborative governance. The insistence on unfettered cuts, without consideration for the real-world impact on citizens, is a troubling testament to the prioritization of fiscal austerity over human welfare. Bacon's commitment to a fixed limit on cuts, far from being illogical, represents a line of defense against unchecked reductions in vital services.

The Personal Toll of Public Service

Finally, Bacon's candid reflection on the toll his career takes on family time adds a humanizing layer to the narrative. The innocuous query from his grandson about retirement is a poignant reminder of the personal sacrifices demanded by public service. It also hints at the internal conflicts faced by those in power, torn between duty to the public and to their families.

Conclusion: The Bacon Paradox

Rep. Don Bacon's stance against excessive Medicaid cuts and his broader critique of his party's direction underscore a critical paradox. In an era where partisan loyalty often trumps principled governance, Bacon's actions offer a glimmer of hope. Yet, they also highlight the systemic flaws within our political institutions, where such dissent is seen not as healthy democratic debate but as betrayal. As Bacon navigates the treacherous waters of political independence, one must wonder: Can the voice of one, however principled, shift the course of the GOP, or will it be drowned out by the cacophony of conformity? Only time will tell, but for now, Bacon's defiance serves as a critical case study in the dynamics of power, principle, and partisanship.
