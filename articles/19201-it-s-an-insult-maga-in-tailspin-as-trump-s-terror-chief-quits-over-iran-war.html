'It's an insult': MAGA in tailspin as Trump's terror chief quits over Iran war

By Paulo Santos

MAGA followers were enraged Tuesday after Joe Kent, head of the National Counterterrorism Center, resigned in protest over the U.S. war in Iran.

Kent, a longtime MAGA ally of President Donald Trump who was appointed by him, shared his public statement and rejection of the conflict in the Middle East. This was the first time an administration official had rebuked the military action and spoken out against it.

MAGA loyalists had seething responses to Kent’s announcement:

“Gold Star wife here. My husband didn’t die for Israel. He died because this war has been targeting Americans for decades. Long before most people ever started paying attention. We didn’t suddenly stumble into this because of Israel. Our service members have been in the crosshairs of Iranian-backed terror networks for years. That’s the reality families like mine have lived with. So spare me the talking point that this is ‘Israel’s war.’ Some of us have already paid for it in American blood,” Sharrell Anne, who self-describes as “America First” wrote in a post on X.

“Very disappointing from you. It’s an insult to Trump to imply he has no agency and no ability to resist pressure from anyone, including Israel,” Jon Murray, who shares pro-MAGA content and commentary, wrote on X.

“’ Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation’ ok good you left because seems like you’re an ignorant ...,” Samuel Cardillo, who frequently shares MAGA talking points, wrote on X.

“’Without getting us drawn into never-ending wars.’ It’s been 3 weeks lol,” conservative commentator and attorney Matt Bilinsky wrote on X.

“Joe Kent is a crazed egomaniac who was often at the center of national security leaks, while rarely (never?) producing any actual work. He spent all of his time working to subvert the chain of command and undermine the President of the United States. This isn’t some principled resignation—he just wanted to make a splash before getting canned. What a loser,” Taylor Budowich, former deputy White House chief of staff and cabinet secretary to Trump, wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Joe Kent’s Resignation

Joe Kent’s resignation from the National Counterterrorism Center over the U.S. war in Iran has triggered a storm of reactions, particularly among MAGA followers. Parsing through the layers of outrage and support for Kent’s decision reveals not just a division within a political faction but also highlights the complex interplay of power, responsibility, and the narratives that shape our understanding of military engagement.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Institutional Power: The decision to engage militarily in Iran is fundamentally a decision made at the highest levels of U.S. government, primarily by the President, with advice and consent from key figures in the administration, military, and, to some extent, Congress. Joe Kent, as head of the National Counterterrorism Center, while influential, was not in a position to dictate foreign policy or military actions.

Decision-Makers: The article directly implicates President Donald Trump as the person who appointed Kent, a MAGA ally, and by extension, suggests Trump’s administration is behind the war in Iran. The criticism Kent levies against the war, therefore, is a criticism of the decision-makers who have the power to initiate and continue such conflicts.

Misdirection of Responsibility

The framing of the article and the reactions from MAGA followers point to a significant misdirection of responsibility. There’s an implied notion that Kent, by resigning, is somehow betraying a cause or misrepresenting the situation. However, this narrative skews the reality that Kent, in his position, had limited control over the broader strategic decisions taken by the U.S. regarding Iran.

Critics of Kent, including Taylor Budowich, former deputy White House chief of staff, attempt to dismiss the resignation as a personal failing or a bid for attention, rather than addressing the substantive issues Kent raises about the war. This misdirection serves to obscure the accountability of those in power who made the decisions Kent is protesting.

Analysis of Criticism

Sharrell Anne and others who align with the “America First” perspective express a viewpoint that the U.S. has been engaged in a long-standing conflict with Iranian-backed forces, suggesting the war is a continuation of previous policy rather than a new direction under Trump. This argument, while grounding its legitimacy in personal sacrifice, does not address the decision-making process that led to the current situation.

Jon Murray and Samuel Cardillo‘s criticisms imply that Kent’s resignation unfairly targets President Trump, suggesting Trump has the agency to resist external pressures, including from allies like Israel. This perspective fails to critically engage with the question of why, if possessing such agency, the decision was made to escalate military involvement.

Matt Bilinsky and Taylor Budowich‘s comments attempt to minimize Kent’s actions as either naive or self-serving. However, these attacks on Kent’s character and motives sidestep the core issue of whether the war in Iran is justified or in the best interests of the United States.

Conclusion

The resignation of Joe Kent and the fallout among MAGA followers reveal a deeper struggle over the narrative and justification of the U.S. war in Iran. By focusing on Kent’s personal motivations or framing the conflict in terms of loyalty and betrayal, critics are diverting attention from the substantive question of who made the decision to go to war and why.

It is essential to cut through these narratives and examine the underlying power dynamics and decision-making processes. Criticism should be aimed not at those who question or dissent from policy decisions but at those who have the institutional power to make and implement those decisions. In this case, the administration, led by President Trump, is the proper focus for any discussion about the war in Iran, not a single official who resigns in protest.
