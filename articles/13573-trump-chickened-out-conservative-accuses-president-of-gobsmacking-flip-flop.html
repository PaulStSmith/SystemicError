'Trump chickened out': Conservative accuses president of 'gobsmacking flip-flop'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of the Flip-Flop: Trump's Immigration Pivot

U.S. President Donald Trump disembarks Air Force One as he arrives in Daytona Beach to attend the Daytona 500 race, in Daytona Beach, Florida, U.S. February 16, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

Donald Trump just engaged in a massive flip-flop, and the "story hasn't received the attention it really deservers," according to conservative commentator Charlie Sykes.

Sykes released his Substack newsletter on Sunday, covering the most recent news stories. One of them is labeled, "The Mother of All TACOS," referring to the financial trading term Trump Always Chickens Out.

"Given the frenetic pace of the news cycle, this story hasn’t received the attention it really deserves: After making mass deportation and ICE raids on workplaces a centerpiece of his presidency, Donald J. Trump chickened out. Once again," Sykes said. "It was a flip-flip so gobsmacking that it left some of his closest aides stunned."

Sykes quoted Trump as saying, "Our farmers are being hurt badly. They have very good workers [that] have worked for them for 20 years. They’re not citizens, but they’ve turned out to be great. And we’re going to have to do something about that. We can’t take all their people and send them back ... And leisure too, and hotels."

Sykes reported:

"Later in the day, ICE ordered agents to 'hold on all work site enforcement investigations/operations on agriculture (including aquaculture and meat packing plants), restaurants and operating hotels.' The NYT reports that Trump’s reversal came after lobbying from the industries most affected by the raids."

He added, "But… dammmmmm. His serial blinks on tariffs pale compared to this volte-face."

He then quoted the NYT:

"For months, Mr. Trump and his aides have said they would target all immigrants without legal status in the United States to make good on his campaign promise for mass deportations. While the administration came into office saying it would initially target undocumented immigrants with criminal records, it has in recent weeks expanded to raiding work sites and sweeping up other undocumented immigrants broadly. On Thursday, Mr. Trump acknowledged that the crackdown might be alienating industries he wanted to keep on his side."

Sykes added, "WTAF?" which likely refers to, "What the actual f---."

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Flip-Flop: Trump's Immigration Pivot

In a political landscape where consistency is often sacrificed at the altar of convenience, Donald Trump's recent about-face on immigration enforcement is a spectacle worthy of the grandest stages. According to conservative commentator Charlie Sykes, in what he's dubbed "The Mother of All TACOS" (Trump Always Chickens Out), this seismic shift in policy has not garnered the uproar it rightly deserves. Let's dissect this, shall we?

The Flip-Flop Heard Around the Workplaces

After a presidency marked by hardline immigration policies and the specter of mass deportations and ICE raids, Trump has executed a pivot so abrupt it's induced whiplash in even his staunchest allies. Quoting Trump, Sykes highlights the former president's newfound empathy for the agricultural sector, a cornerstone of the American economy, which apparently relies heavily on undocumented workers. "Our farmers are being hurt badly," Trump laments, acknowledging the value of these non-citizen workers and signaling a halt to operations targeting them.

Why This Matters

This isn't merely a change of heart; it's a stark revelation of the chasm between political rhetoric and economic reality. Trump's acknowledgment that industries such as farming, leisure, and hotels are dependent on undocumented workers is a glaring admission that his administration's policies were at odds with the needs of significant sectors of the U.S. economy.

The Impact of Lobbying

The power of lobbying shines through in this narrative, with the NYT reporting that Trump's reversal came after significant pressure from the industries most affected by the raids. This underscores a fundamental truth about American politics: when economic interests speak loudly enough, even the most hardline policies are subject to reevaluation. It's a classic case of economic pragmatism trumping (pun intended) ideological purity.

But… Dammnnn

Sykes's reaction, "But… dammmmmm," encapsulates the shock and disbelief that this about-face has provoked. It's a testament to the unpredictable nature of Trump's decision-making process and, more broadly, the often capricious nature of political leadership.

The Serial Blinks on Tariffs

Trump's history of "serial blinks" on tariffs, as Sykes puts it, pales in comparison to this latest policy volte-face. It's indicative of a broader pattern of behavior where tough talk on the campaign trail gives way to more measured actions when faced with the complexities of governing and the realities of economic interdependence.

WTAF?

Indeed, "What the actual f---?" This sentiment, cleverly abbreviated by Sykes, captures the collective bewilderment at this sudden shift. It's a stark reminder of the gulf between campaign promises and the practicalities of policy implementation.

In Conclusion

The tale of Trump's immigration pivot is a sobering lesson in the realities of political power, the influence of economic interests, and the often wide gap between rhetoric and action. It's a reminder that in politics, as in life, actions speak louder than words. And in this case, the actions are speaking volumes about the complexities and contradictions at the heart of Trump's America.

This episode, while perhaps underreported, is a critical piece of the puzzle in understanding the Trump administration's legacy. It's a narrative that deserves not just attention but deep reflection on the nature of leadership and the intricate dance between policy, economy, and the human lives caught in the balance.
