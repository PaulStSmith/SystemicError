'Secret Trump payoff': Dems suspect right-wing 'side deal' sweetened CBS merger

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump attends the Congressional Picnic at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

A trio of Democratic U.S. senators on Monday launched a probe into reports that President Donald Trump's legal team struck a secretive side deal with Skydance—the prospective owner of CBS—that includes millions of dollars worth of "broadcast transmissions" supporting right-wing causes.

In a letter to Skydance CEO David Ellison, Sens. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.), Ron Wyden (D-Ore.), and Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) wrote that Trump himself appeared to confirm the existence of the side deal in comments to reporters about his $16 million settlement with Paramount, the current owner of CBS that is seeking federal approval for its pending merger with Skydance.

Trump suggested the settlement was worth twice as much as the publicly reported figure, saying, "We did a deal for about $16 million plus $16 million—or maybe more than that in advertising... So it's like $32 to maybe $35 million. I think that's what they did."

The senators described the alleged side deal as a "potential secret Trump payoff."

"This admission appears to corroborate reporting that claims you reached a 'side deal' with the president, the terms of
which involve CBS airing public service announcements 'and other broadcast transmissions' worth between $15 million and $20 million that 'support conservative causes supported by President Trump.' The nature and existence of this arrangement are uncertain, with at least one anonymous source calling the reports 'false.'"

The president's comments aligned with a New York Post story published earlier this month alleging that Ellison vowed to "run between $15 million and $20 million of public service ads to promote causes supported by the president" once the Skydance CEO takes over CBS.

Paramount denied having any knowledge of a side deal.

The Democratic senators wrote in their letter to Ellison that the president's comments and related reporting "raise fresh questions about corruption in the Trump administration and President Trump's willingness to accept payments from entities with significant policy interests before agencies he controls."

The senators demanded that Ellison provide answers to seven questions, including whether there is "currently any arrangement under which you or Skydance will provide compensation, advertising, or promotional activities that in any way assist President Trump, his family, his presidential library, or other administration officials" and whether Ellison has "personally discussed with President Trump, any of his family members, any Trump administration officials, or presidential library fund personnel any matters related to the Paramount-Skydance transaction."

The letter was sent days after Ellison met with Federal Communications Commission Chair Brendan Carr—a Trump loyalist—to discuss the pending merger with Paramount.

The meeting, according to Skydance's counsel, included a discussion of "Skydance's commitment to unbiased journalism and its
embrace of diverse viewpoints, principles that will ensure CBS' editorial decision-making reflects the varied ideological perspectives of American viewers."

As Variety noted, "That's significant because Carr, appointed by Trump, had reopened an agency probe into a 'news distortion' complaint against CBS over the allegedly deceptive editing of a '60 Minutes' interview with Kamala Harris segment—the same interview that Trump sued Paramount and CBS over."

"The application of the FCC's 'news distortion' policy in this way was highly unusual, according to former agency officials," Variety reported.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Alleged Side Deal: Trump, Skydance, and the Shadows of Corruption

In an astonishing revelation that could only find its roots in the tumultuous soil of Trump-era politics, a trio of Democratic U.S. senators has launched a probe into what appears to be an unprecedented blend of media manipulation, political payoffs, and the blurring lines between corporate interests and government authority. The subjects of this probe, President Donald Trump, and Skydance Media, under the prospective leadership of CEO David Ellison, are accused of concocting a secretive side deal involving millions in "broadcast transmissions" purportedly aimed at bolstering right-wing causes. This maneuver, if proven true, not only reeks of corruption but also sets a dangerous precedent for the integrity of media and democracy.

The Allegations: A Side Deal Exposed

Senators Elizabeth Warren, Ron Wyden, and Bernie Sanders are spearheading this investigation, fueled by Trump's own comments that allude to a $32 to $35 million settlement with Paramount—figures that starkly contrast the public narrative. Trump's candid acknowledgment of the deal, which seemingly includes a hefty sum for advertising that supports conservative agendas, has ripped open the facade of a standard legal settlement to reveal potential underpinnings of political machinations.

What stands out is not just the alleged deal's existence but the brazenness with which it was discussed and disclosed. The implications are vast, suggesting a scenario where political figures can leverage media assets for personal or ideological gain—a scenario that should alarm any observer regardless of political affiliation.

The Response: Paramount and Skydance in the Hot Seat

Paramount's denial of knowledge regarding such a deal only thickens the plot. David Ellison's silence—or potential future explanations—will be critical in untangling the web of claims and counterclaims. The senators' letter to Ellison, demanding answers to seven pointed questions, is not just a quest for clarity but a public call to accountability.

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) meeting, involving Ellison and Trump loyalist Brendan Carr, further complicates the narrative. Discussions on "unbiased journalism" and diverse viewpoints, as reported, strike a dissonant chord against the backdrop of the alleged side deal. It's a juxtaposition that screams hypocrisy, especially considering the FCC's unusual application of its 'news distortion' policy in reopening a complaint against CBS over a '60 Minutes' interview.

The Implications: Beyond the Deal Itself

This saga transcends the specifics of the deal, touching on broader, more systemic issues within American politics and media. The potential corruption aside, the situation underscores the vulnerabilities in the American media landscape, where political power and media moguls can collude, potentially swaying public opinion and policy under the guise of "public service announcements."

Moreover, this situation shines a spotlight on the Trump administration's willingness to engage with entities that have significant policy interests, raising questions about the integrity of governance and the influence of personal and political interests over public duty.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The alleged side deal between Trump's legal team and Skydance Media, if true, represents more than a simple corruption scandal. It signifies a dangerous confluence of media power and political maneuvering, threatening the very pillars of democracy and media integrity. As this investigation unfolds, one must wonder: how deep do these ties go, and what can be done to sever them?

In a political era marred by allegations of corruption and unethical conduct, this probe may only be the tip of the iceberg, revealing the murky waters where politics and media mix a little too comfortably. The outcome of this investigation could potentially echo far beyond the tenure of any single administration, challenging us to reconsider the safeguards necessary to protect the sanctity of media and democratic principles.
