Trump exposed for eye-watering taxpayer cost of DC takeover

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Turned D.C. Into a Stage Prop and Taxpayers Paid for It

The Power Was Always His

This was not a neutral public-safety intervention. Donald Trump used an executive order to seize control of Washington’s police apparatus, then layered in National Guard forces and federal law enforcement to perform authority. The power here sat at the top: the president chose the target, chose the posture, and chose the expense.

The watchdog report from CREW shows what that decision actually cost. It is not a vague administrative overreach. It is a deliberate use of federal muscle, money, and staffing to dramatize a crisis that the reporting says did not match the underlying crime trend.

The Decision Was the Point

The stated rationale was “crime,” but the source says major crime had been declining for years in Washington, as in many big cities. That matters because it strips away the usual excuse. If the problem was not an objective emergency, then the operation was not a remedy. It was messaging backed by uniforms.

The report also points to the likely trigger: Trump’s response to the assault on Edward Coristine, a former DOGE employee. That is not a public-policy rationale; it is grievance politics with federal authority attached. One incident, amplified upward, became justification for a takeover that should never have been normalized.

The Real Bill

CREW says the Marshals Service alone ran up more than $18 million in taxpayer costs from August 2025 to March 2026. That is on top of $332 million tied to the National Guard deployment as of February. The scale is the message: this was not a cheap flourish. It was a costly conversion of federal institutions into campaign-grade theater.

The damage was not only financial. The report says resources were pulled from district courts nationwide, including Guam and the Virgin Islands, and that the Marshals’ tactical operations unit, investigative operations division, and D.C. Superior Court all lost staffing to the effort. So while Trump was playing strongman in Washington, the federal system was stripping capacity from places that actually needed it.

Blame Was Misdirected

The usual trick is to present the spectacle as a response to chaos, then blame the chaos on everyone else. Here, the weaker actors are the city, the courts, the marshals, and the public. They absorb the cost while the political actor who issued the order gets to pose as the fix.

That is the core misdirection: deliberate administrative disruption is dressed up as concern. The reporting shows abrupt deployment, staffing shocks, and resource diversion. That is not confusion. It is a decision. When the state creates its own strain and then markets itself as the only cure, the fraud is structural.

What This Pattern Reveals

This story is about more than Washington. It shows how federal power can be used to manufacture an emergency, redirect institutions toward symbolic control, and bill the public for the privilege. The target does not need to be the country’s most dangerous place. It only needs to be useful for the performance.

That is the pattern: manufactured urgency, centralized command, hidden institutional costs, and a political benefit that flows upward while the damage spreads downward. The cruelty is not incidental. It is embedded in the design.
