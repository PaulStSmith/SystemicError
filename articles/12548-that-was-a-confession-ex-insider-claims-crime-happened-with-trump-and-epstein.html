'That was a confession': Ex-insider claims 'crime happened' with Trump and Epstein

Donald Trump's former "trusted operative" on Sunday made the case that the president is indeed hiding something potentially criminal with his connection to Jeffrey Epstein.

Trump has categorically denied all allegations of inappropriate behavior as it relates to underage women, but that isn't stopping Lev Parnas, a former associate of Trump who worked with the White House team on behind-the-scenes matters, and has recently been speaking out about Trump's actions on Substack.

Recently, Parnas accused Trump of suffering mental decline.

Now, Parnas is going a step further in his allegations.

"As I sit here this morning writing this letter, I feel something I haven’t felt in a long time. Not rage. Not even shock. Just a deep, nauseating disgust," Parnas wrote. "Disgust—not only at what Donald Trump has done, but at the people now lining up to excuse it, minimize it, or pretend it never happened."

Noting the refusal of the media to make a determination that Trump himself committed a crime, Parnas wrote, "Over the past week, I’ve watched pundits, influencers, and media figures trip over themselves to 'fact check' the Wall Street Journal bombshell that Donald Trump personally sent Jeffrey Epstein a birthday letter—handwritten, no less—while trying to distance him from one of the most notorious pedophiles in modern history."

"They say: 'Well, we have no direct evidence Trump did anything wrong with Epstein'," he added. "Let me say this as clearly as I can—as a father of seven, including three daughters: You don’t need to watch someone commit the act to know the crime happened."

Parnas goes on to cite the E. Jean Carroll case, saying, "If the sky is rumbling with thunder, and you hear the rain pounding—and you walk outside to find everything soaking wet—you don’t need to see the storm to know it rained."

"You need common sense. And common sense, combined with mountains of circumstantial, legal, and spoken-word evidence, tells us what we’ve known all along: Donald J. Trump is a sexual predator," Parnas wrote. "This is not opinion. This is not theory. It’s fact. In 2023, Trump was found liable for sexual abuse by a New York jury in the case brought by E. Jean Carroll. He appealed. He lost."

Parnas also reminded readers about Trump's infamous "Access Hollywood" comments, which saw Trump talking about "grabbing" women without their consent. Of those comments, Parnas wrote, "That wasn’t locker room talk. That was a confession."

Parnas went on to say, "Let’s talk about Katie Johnson, a girl who alleged that Trump and Epstein sexually assaulted her when she was 13 years old."

"She filed a lawsuit. She was threatened," the ex-insider wrote. "And then her case quietly disappeared. But ask yourself—Trump sues everyone. He sued the Wall Street Journal for $10 billion. But he’s never sued Katie Johnson. Because the one thing Trump can’t afford is discovery."

Parnas lays out the evidence as he sees it, adding, "Let’s not forget: Jeffrey Epstein died under Donald Trump’s DOJ."

"Under Attorney General Bill Barr. And I say this as someone who’s been prosecuted by Bill Barr’s DOJ: That department wasn’t interested in uncovering the truth. It was interested in burying it," he wrote. "What if Epstein’s arrest was never meant to bring him to justice—but to silence him permanently? And who else, besides Donald Trump, had something to lose? Is this why we’re seeing people like Pam Bondi and Kash Patel work overtime to shut down any real investigation into the Trump–Epstein nexus?"

He then asked, "What else are they protecting?"

While the White House hasn't responded to Parnas' claims, Trump and his administration have flatly rejected all criminal allegations against the Republican president and his advisers.

Our Analysis:

The Allegations Unveiled: Trump's Ties to Epstein

In a recent exposé that reads more like a thriller than a political revelation, Lev Parnas, a former "trusted operative" of Donald Trump, pierces through the veil of denial and obfuscation surrounding Trump's connections to Jeffrey Epstein. Trump, whose presidency and post-presidency periods have been marred by controversy, finds himself in the spotlight once again, not for his policies, but for alleged associations with one of modern history's most infamous figures.

Parnas, shedding his previous allegiance, adopts the role of whistleblower, bringing forth allegations that, if true, add a sinister layer to Trump's already controversial persona. His words, "deep, nauseating disgust," encapsulate the sentiment of many who have followed Trump's trajectory closely, yet they signify more than just a personal revulsion. They hint at a deep-seated corruption within circles of power, where morality is often sacrificed at the altar of convenience and loyalty.

The Media's Delicate Dance

Parnas criticizes the media's reluctance to outright declare Trump's guilt. This cautious approach by the press, while legally prudent, often acts as a smokescreen, obscuring the gravity of the allegations. The media's hesitancy to connect the dots, preferring instead to 'fact check' around the periphery of the issue, does a disservice to the public discourse. It's a delicate dance of semantics over substance, where the fear of legal retribution paralyzes journalistic integrity.

The Weight of Circumstantial Evidence

Parnas's argument hinges on the accumulation of circumstantial evidence, legal precedents, and Trump's own incriminating words. The reference to the E. Jean Carroll case, where Trump was found liable for sexual abuse, is a stark reminder of Trump's pattern of behavior. This isn't mere conjecture; it's a documented legal outcome. Yet, the Carroll case, significant as it is, becomes just one thread in a complex tapestry of alleged misconduct.

A Confession in Disguise?

Trump's "Access Hollywood" comments, where he boasted about grabbing women without consent, are highlighted by Parnas not as idle talk, but as a confession. This interpretation strips the incident of any benign interpretation, framing it as a window into Trump's psyche and modus operandi. It's a bold stance, one that challenges the listener to reconsider the narrative of locker room banter as a cover for deeper, more disturbing tendencies.

The Silence Around Katie Johnson

The case of Katie Johnson, who accused Trump and Epstein of assault, only to see her lawsuit disappear, is particularly telling. Trump's litigious nature is well-documented, yet his silence here is deafening. The absence of a lawsuit against Johnson, by Parnas's logic, is not an act of restraint but an act of self-preservation. The specter of discovery, with all its potential for revelation, hangs heavily over this episode.

Epstein's Death and Barr's DOJ

Parnas then veers into the territory of speculation, albeit grounded in a cynical understanding of power dynamics. He suggests that Epstein's arrest and subsequent death under the Trump administration's Department of Justice were not aimed at justice but at silencing a potential threat. This assertion, while speculative, raises uncomfortable questions about the lengths to which power will go to protect itself.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

As Parnas lays out his allegations, the picture that emerges is one of a deeply flawed system, where truth and accountability are often casualties of power plays. His call to action is not just about Trump or Epstein; it's a demand for a reevaluation of the systems that allow such figures to flourish unchecked.

While Parnas's own credibility and motivations may be questioned, the gravity and specificity of his allegations demand attention. The truth, however murky and contested, deserves to be pursued with vigor and without fear. For in the end, it's not just about the guilt or innocence of individuals; it's about the integrity of the institutions that govern our society.
