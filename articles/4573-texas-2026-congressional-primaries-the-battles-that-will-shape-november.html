Texas 2026 Congressional Primaries: The Battles That Will Shape November

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 4, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The 2026 Texas primaries delivered a complicated political landscape across South Texas and the San Antonio region. Several races advanced predictable candidates into the general election, while others exposed fractures inside the Republican coalition and revealed opportunities Democrats may exploit in November.

Despite aggressive gerrymandering by the Texas legislature in 2025 designed to manufacture additional Republican seats, the broader national environment is shifting. Off‑year elections, special elections, and local contests throughout 2025 and early 2026 have shown a consistent pattern: Democratic turnout remains unusually resilient, while Republican turnout is increasingly dependent on highly motivated MAGA voters rather than broad coalition support.

The result is a paradox. On paper, many Texas districts look safely Republican. In reality, several have become more competitive because demographic changes and suburban realignment continue to erode Republican margins.

Below is a district‑by‑district breakdown of the key races emerging from the primaries and the strategic outlook for November.

Congressional District 15

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: Bobby Pulido

  • Republican: Rep. Monica De La Cruz

The Democratic primary in District 15 produced one of the most unusual nominees of the cycle. Tejano music star Bobby Pulido defeated emergency room physician Ada Cuellar in a bruising primary that drew national attention.

Pulido has deliberately branded himself as a “conservative Democrat,” emphasizing support for the Second Amendment while soft‑pedaling some progressive positions on immigration and gender policy. His campaign strategy leaned heavily on cultural connection rather than ideological confrontation, organizing “ranch hall” events across rural counties featuring music, free food, and community gatherings.

Cuellar represented the more traditional progressive lane, campaigning openly on reproductive rights and appearing alongside nationally prominent Democrats such as Rep. Jasmine Crockett.

The primary result underscores an important strategic shift among South Texas Democrats. Rather than attempting to replicate urban progressive coalitions, candidates in the Rio Grande Valley are increasingly leaning into culturally conservative but economically populist messaging.

Republican incumbent Monica De La Cruz enters the race with structural advantages. The district was redrawn to favor Republicans and Donald Trump carried it comfortably in 2024.

However, Democrats see a potential opening. Latino voter behavior in the region remains fluid rather than locked into a permanent partisan shift. Pulido’s celebrity status and cultural familiarity may allow him to reconnect with working‑class voters who drifted toward Republicans in recent cycles.

Democratic Outlook: Competitive but difficult.

Who Democrats Back: Bobby Pulido

Congressional District 21

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: Kristin Hook

  • Republican: Mark “Tex” Teixeira

District 21 delivered a clear primary outcome for both parties.

Republicans nominated former Major League Baseball player Mark “Tex” Teixeira, who poured $2.5 million of his own money into the campaign and secured Donald Trump’s endorsement. Teixeira dominated the field and avoided a runoff.

Democrats nominated reproductive biologist Kristin Hook, a former policy staffer for Senator Elizabeth Warren with experience at the National Institutes of Health and the Government Accountability Office.

The contrast between the candidates could not be sharper.

Hook represents a technocratic, policy‑focused approach centered on healthcare, reproductive rights, and scientific governance. Teixeira represents the celebrity‑politician model that has become common in modern Republican politics: name recognition, self‑funding, and ideological alignment with Trump.

Unfortunately for Democrats, the structural math of the district remains brutal. The Hill Country seat favored Trump by more than twenty points in 2024.

While Democratic enthusiasm nationally may narrow that margin slightly, flipping this district would require a political earthquake.

Democratic Outlook: Long shot.

Who Democrats Back: Kristin Hook

Congressional District 23

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: TBD

  • Republican: Tony Gonzales or Brandon Herrera

District 23 remains one of the most politically volatile congressional districts in Texas.

Republican incumbent Tony Gonzales failed to secure enough votes in the primary and now faces a runoff against gun influencer Brandon Herrera.

Herrera’s campaign illustrates the ongoing radicalization of the Republican base. Gonzales, already a conservative lawmaker, became a target simply for supporting modest gun safety legislation. Herrera weaponized that vote into a purity test and built a campaign around absolutist Second Amendment rhetoric.

The race has also been overshadowed by personal scandal after reports surfaced that Gonzales engaged in an affair with a staffer.

This internal Republican fight may ultimately create an opportunity for Democrats. The district historically swings between parties depending on turnout and candidate quality.

If Republicans nominate Herrera, the district becomes significantly more competitive. His online persona appeals strongly to the MAGA base but may alienate suburban voters and moderates.

Democratic Outlook: Potential pickup opportunity depending on GOP nominee.

Who Democrats Back: Democratic nominee (pending primary completion)

Congressional District 28

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: Rep. Henry Cuellar

  • Republican: Tano Tijerina

District 28 offers one of the strangest stories of the cycle.

Incumbent Democrat Henry Cuellar won his primary despite the shadow of corruption charges filed in 2024. The case was effectively neutralized after Donald Trump issued a pardon last year.

The pardon itself was politically explosive. It handed Republicans a ready‑made campaign narrative while simultaneously raising questions about why a Republican president would intervene to protect a Democratic congressman.

The district was redrawn during the 2025 redistricting cycle, making it roughly three points more Republican.

Even so, Cuellar has proven extraordinarily resilient. His long tenure and moderate political positioning have helped him maintain support among both Latino Democrats and some conservative voters.

Republican challenger Tano Tijerina will likely focus heavily on ethics issues during the campaign.

Yet Cuellar’s incumbency advantage remains formidable.

Democratic Outlook: Lean Democratic but competitive.

Who Democrats Back: Henry Cuellar

Congressional District 34

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: Rep. Vicente Gonzalez

  • Republican: Eric Flores

District 34 sits along the Gulf Coast and Rio Grande Valley and has become one of the most contested border districts in the state.

Republicans nominated former federal prosecutor Eric Flores after he defeated former congresswoman Mayra Flores in the primary. Donald Trump endorsed Flores, signaling that Republican leadership had largely moved on from Mayra Flores following multiple electoral defeats.

Democratic incumbent Vicente Gonzalez has developed a unique political strategy in the district. Rather than framing himself as a national partisan figure, he frequently emphasizes local issues, border security pragmatism, and economic development.

That approach has allowed him to win crossover support from voters who otherwise lean Republican.

Republicans believe the district’s shifting demographics favor them, particularly after recent redistricting changes.

But Gonzalez has repeatedly demonstrated that candidate quality matters more than partisan labels in this region.

Democratic Outlook: Toss‑up but winnable.

Who Democrats Back: Vicente Gonzalez

Congressional District 35

General Election Matchup:

  • Democrat: TBD (Garcia or Galindo likely)

  • Republican: TBD (Lujan or De La Cruz)

District 35 is a newly engineered battleground created by the Texas legislature’s aggressive redistricting strategy.

Republicans attempted to reshape the district by carving out heavily Democratic Austin neighborhoods while consolidating Republican‑leaning areas around San Antonio.

The Republican primary is headed to a runoff between state representative John Lujan and Trump‑endorsed candidate Carlos De La Cruz.

The Democratic race is similarly unsettled, with sheriff’s deputy Johnny Garcia and mental health consultant Maureen Galindo leading the field.

Garcia represents the “Blue Dog” Democratic tradition — culturally moderate, economically populist, and focused on public safety.

Galindo brings a policy‑focused campaign emphasizing healthcare and mental health services.

The district favored Trump by roughly ten points in 2024, but Democrats are preparing to invest heavily here.

If the national environment continues trending against Republicans, District 35 could become one of the cycle’s surprise battlegrounds.

Democratic Outlook: Difficult but not impossible.

Who Democrats Back: Democratic nominee

Strategic Outlook

Texas remains structurally challenging terrain for Democrats. Gerrymandering, demographic distribution, and entrenched Republican networks continue to tilt many districts toward the GOP.

Yet the broader national pattern since 2022 tells a different story: Democrats have consistently over‑performed in special elections, off‑year races, and suburban districts.

The key dynamic shaping 2026 is turnout asymmetry. MAGA politics energizes a core Republican base but increasingly alienates moderate voters, especially suburban women and younger voters.

If that trend continues into November, districts that appear safely Republican on paper could tighten rapidly.

Texas may not flip wholesale in 2026. But several districts across South Texas and the San Antonio corridor could become warning lights for a Republican Party that continues to shrink its coalition while demanding ideological purity.

And when a party begins purging its own incumbents for insufficient loyalty, it rarely ends well.
