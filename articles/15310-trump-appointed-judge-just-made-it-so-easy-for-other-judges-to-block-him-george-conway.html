Trump-appointed judge just 'made it so easy' for other judges to block him: George Conway

A federal judge appointed to his current position by Donald Trump during his first term has handed a roadmap to his colleagues on the bench to block the president from invoking the Alien Enemies Act (AEA) to do whatever he wants.

That is according to conservative attorney George Conway who appeared on MSNBC's revamped "The Weekend" on Saturday.

Pointing to U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr's ruling, where he wrote Trump's use of the act "exceeds the scope of the statute and is contrary to the plain, ordinary meaning of the statute's terms," Conway suggested Rodriguez did the heavy-lifting other judges can invoke themselves.

Speaking with host Eugene Daniels, the anti-Trump Conway pointed out, "The bottom line is it's a no-brainer."

"I mean, what the judge pointed out was the Alien Enemies Act –– the president has broad powers under the Alien Enemies Act –– but only if certain prerequisites are met. First is there a declared war? No declared war. Second is there a military invasion or incursion? You know, do we have troops or guerrillas going over the border taking property and taking land or blowing things up? That's not happening."

"These are people going here and there in different parts of the United states and allegedly engaging in criminal conspiracy –– that doesn't even come close," he exclaimed. 'The judge made clear to what is. what was supposed to be covered by this law and so it's so easy. The court, the judge made it so easy."

"It's going to be other judges in other districts are going to rule exactly the same way and everybody's going to," he predicted.

Our Analysis:

Judicial Roadmap Against Trump's Use of the Alien Enemies Act: A Closer Look

In a recent development that has sent ripples across the legal and political landscapes, a federal judge, appointed by Donald Trump himself, has essentially laid down the gauntlet against the former president's use of the Alien Enemies Act (AEA). This move, dissected with keen insight by conservative attorney George Conway on MSNBC's "The Weekend," underscores a pivotal moment in the ongoing struggle between authoritarian impulses and judicial restraint.

The Heart of the Matter

At the core of this saga is U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr.'s ruling, a document that doesn't just challenge Trump's actions but also serves as a beacon for future judicial rebukes. Rodriguez's ruling starkly states that Trump's invocation of the AEA "exceeds the scope of the statute and is contrary to the plain, ordinary meaning of the statute's terms." This isn't just a slap on the wrist; it's a full-fledged knockout punch, highlighting a critical overstep by the Trump administration.

George Conway's Sharp Analysis

George Conway, a conservative attorney who has emerged as a vocal critic of Trump, brings to light the essence of Rodriguez's ruling with precision and clarity. Conway's commentary cuts through the legal jargon, boiling down the issue to its simplest form: Trump's utilization of the AEA was not only unfounded but blatantly misaligned with the act's intended scope and prerequisites.

What the AEA Actually Says

The Alien Enemies Act, as pointed out by Conway, grants the president broad powers, but these come with significant caveats. Two key conditions must be met: a declared war and a military invasion or incursion. Trump's application of this statute, as per Conway's and Rodriguez's analyses, fails to meet these conditions spectacularly. The notion that sporadic, criminal activities could be conflated with a military invasion is, to borrow Conway's words, a "no-brainer" in its absurdity.

The Domino Effect Predicted

Conway's prediction that other judges in diverse districts will follow Rodriguez's lead is not just wishful thinking; it's a logical outcome of this clear judicial reasoning. Rodriguez has not merely ruled on a case; he has crafted a template for future legal challenges against similar abuses of power. This sets a precedent that could significantly restrain the potential for presidential overreach under the guise of the AEA or similar statutes.

Conclusion: A Victory for Judicial Oversight

This episode is a stark reminder of the importance of judicial oversight in the face of executive overreach. Judge Rodriguez, by the book yet bold in his judgment, along with George Conway's articulate analysis, have highlighted a critical boundary between lawful authority and authoritarian impulse. It's a victory not just for the law but for the foundational principles of American governance. As this judicial roadmap unfurls, it reinforces the bulwarks of democracy against the whims of tyranny, no matter how high the office from which they emanate.
