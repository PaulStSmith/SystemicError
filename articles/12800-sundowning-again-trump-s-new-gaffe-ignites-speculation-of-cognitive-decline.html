'Sundowning again': Trump's new gaffe ignites speculation of 'cognitive decline'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 13, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump gushed over his Pentagon chief's Friday morning appearance on Fox News — but sharp-eyed observers questioned the state of his cognitive health, noting there was no such appearance.

Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform, "Secretary of War Pete Hegseth was great on FoxNews this morning. Talking about modern weapons and warfare. Thank you also to Brett Velicovich, who really knows his 'stuff.' We are really on our way. MAGA!"

But as The Daily Beast noted, Hegseth, whose title remains secretary of Defense, was traveling Friday and never appeared on the conservative network. Instead, the president appeared to be fooled as the network showed a clip Hegesth shared on his X account. That video was posted on Thursday, taken down, and reposted on Friday.

In it, Hegseth dons a suit outside his Pentagon as drones zip around him, proudly declaring, "You know what? We know why we were put here. We were brought here to rebuild the military."

He said he expedited the production and deployment of drones, and signed a memo acknowledging as such that was handed to him by one of the machines.

"This is the future. We're in the fight," he adds in the clip.

Fox News repeatedly shared the clip, according to The Beast, but the network clearly noted it was from Thursday.

Trump critics let the president have it.

Independent journalist Aaron Rupar wrote on X, "Pete Hegseth was not on Fox News this morning. They did play a clip of a video Hegseth posted on social media. Perhaps a confused Trump thought it was live."

The Republicans against Trump account replied to Rupar on X, "Trump’s cognitive decline is getting hard to ignore."

Software engineer Alex Jewell chided, "I’m not sure Trump knows the difference between live and pre-recorded, if we’re being honest."

Former MSNBC host and Zeteo founder Mehdi Hasan lost it.

"Imagine. If. It. Was. Biden," he railed.

Political writer Mona Burns chimed in, "Coupla things:1. Hegseth wasn't on Fox this morning 2. What's his name clearly did not write this post[.] Even his social media team can't get it right."

Self-described news junkie Lauren Ashley Davis wrote on X, "Trump is sundowning again."

Harry Jarin, candidate for Maryland Congress, added, "Pete Hegseth was not on Fox News today. The position of Secretary of War was eliminated in 1947. Our political establishment is run by feeble, elderly, and increasingly senile politicians from both sides of the aisle."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Fox News Fiasco: A Harbinger of Cognitive Collapse or a Simple Slip?

In a recent spectacle that reads like a satirical skit straight out of a late-night comedy show, President Donald Trump managed to baffle and amuse the political sphere in equal measure. His lavish praise for an appearance by his "Secretary of War" Pete Hegseth on Fox News—a segment that never actually happened—has thrown a glaring spotlight not just on Trump’s media consumption habits, but on the broader, more concerning issue of his cognitive health.

The Imaginary Appearance

Trump’s enthusiastic shout-out to Hegseth for his insights on "modern weapons and warfare" on Fox News was, to put it mildly, a concoction of confusion. Hegseth was nowhere near Fox News studios; he was, in fact, on travel. The President appears to have been duped by a clip from Hegseth's social media, showcasing the latter in a dramatic drone-surrounded monologue about military rebuilding. This clip, despite its Thursday origins and a reposting saga, somehow led Trump to believe it was a live Friday morning Fox News feature.

Cognitive Health in the Crosshairs

This incident has naturally fueled the fires of speculation about Trump’s cognitive state. Critics and commentators wasted no time drawing parallels and sounding alarms. From accusations of Trump’s inability to distinguish between live and pre-recorded content to outright claims of his "cognitive decline," the discourse swiftly moved beyond the realms of a mere media mix-up to the much darker territories of mental fitness for office.

However, it’s essential to slice through the hyperbole and focus on the facts. Yes, the blunder was embarrassing, especially for a sitting President. Yes, it raises questions about Trump’s media literacy or attention to detail. But to leap from a single, albeit public, mistake to diagnosing cognitive decline is a stretch that lacks both sensitivity and scientific grounding.

The Real Punchline: An Obsolete Title

Adding a layer of absurdity to the whole episode is Trump’s reference to Hegseth as the "Secretary of War" — a position that hasn’t existed since 1947. This anachronism could be chalked up to a simple lapse, or perhaps a nostalgic nod to a bygone era. But in the context of the overall fiasco, it serves as a perfect emblem of the disconnection and disarray at play.

Political Grandstanding

Critics, particularly those with platforms and political agendas, seized the moment to amplify their narratives. From the melodramatic despair of former hosts to the pointed barbs of political writers, the response spectrum was as predictable as it was opportunistic. The rush to frame this as a sign of "sundowning" or evidence of senility was not just premature but also indicative of our times’ polarized and sensationalist political discourse.

A Missed Opportunity for a Real Discussion

Lost in the cacophony of mockery and concern-trolling is a genuine opportunity to discuss the demanding nature of political leadership and the transparency required around the health of those in high office. This incident could have sparked a reasoned debate on these issues. Instead, it became yet another flashpoint in the ongoing spectacle of American politics.

Conclusion

Trump’s faux pas, while fodder for a news cycle’s worth of jokes and jibes, is hardly the definitive proof of cognitive failure some claim it to be. It is, however, a stark reminder of the ease with which political discourse can veer into the realm of personal health attacks, often with scant regard for accuracy or empathy. As observers and participants in this political era, our focus should perhaps be less on gleefully highlighting every misstep and more on fostering a climate where genuine concerns about leadership can be discussed with the seriousness and sensitivity they deserve.
