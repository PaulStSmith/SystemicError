Melania walked straight into a trap to uncover Epstein details: Trump biographer

Trump biographer Michael Wolff provided an update on his ongoing lawsuit with Melania Trump in a Daily Beast article and on his Substack late Thursday, confirming details that had only been speculated about.

First, Wolff reviewed his findings from earlier this week: the first lady doesn’t live anywhere near the White House.

“Their relationship is remote at best,” Wolff told Daily Beast executive editor Hugh Dougherty.

Wolff has been involved in an ongoing federal lawsuit with Mrs. Trump after she sued him for defamation, seeking $1 billion. The defamation in question concerns Wolff’s allegations about a connection to trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, once a friend of her husband’s. The Trumps deny Wolff’s claims.

The Trumps want to move the suit from the New York court where Wolff lives because they allegedly live in Florida. Wolff, however, disputes this, saying that Mrs. Trump has been living in New York, not Florida. When their son went to New York University, numerous reports said Mrs. Trump moved back to Trump Tower.

Regardless of whether Mrs. Trump lives in New York or Florida, Wolff said it’s clear the Trumps don’t live anywhere near each other, which isn’t normal for first ladies of the United States.

“Basically, she has never left New York. She is trying to live the life of a superstar in New York,” Wolff alleged.

Wolff said that Mrs. Trump is at work “starting a new business life in New York City and it is a way to exploit her relationship with the president of the United States, while actually distancing herself from the president of the United States. So, building a kind of Melania brand company, I suppose that’s what it is—a way for her to gain financial independence and also geographic independence from the president of the United States.”

Wolff made it clear that even during the four years between administrations, she was largely in New York, and he spent his time in Florida.

One reason it’s important, Wolff said, is that it “is a direct challenge to a White House which has carelessly and unilaterally used its power to go after anybody whose voice and views it disagrees with.”

“You think you have free speech? Well, you don’t if we say you don’t,” he added.

He also noted that in New York and many other states, there are anti-SLAPP laws. SLAPP stands for Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation. These are suits aimed at silencing people. If the Trumps sue Wolff in New York and the judge agrees that this is a SLAPP case, Wolff can launch his own lawsuit and the Trumps must pay him for their legal costs.

Wolff also said, “This lawsuit provides a pushback model” for others fighting the Trumps. “It’s illegal to use the legal process only for the purposes of intimidation and, in this case, intimidation about free speech.”

What the Trumps have done with their lawsuit, however, is open the door for Wolff to ask in court, “What did you know? When did you know it? What did you do?” And they can do it all under oath.

So, not only could Wolff get to the bottom of the Trumps’ relationship with Epstein, he could gather a lot of additional information about their life as well.

The Spectacle of the Trump-Wolff Legal Battle: A Scathing Analysis

In the latest episode of the legal circus that seems perpetually tethered to the Trump name, Michael Wolff, a biographer turned bête noire of the Trump family, has thrown fresh fuel on his fiery legal dispute with Melania Trump. The details, as outlined in a Daily Beast article and Wolff’s own Substack, uncover layers of intrigue, alleged defamation, and the peculiar living arrangements of the former First Lady. Let’s dissect the saga with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned juror.

Remote Relationships and Residences

First and foremost, Wolff’s claim that Melania Trump “doesn’t live anywhere near the White House” and that their relationship is “remote at best” slices through the public facade like a hot knife through butter. This assertion isn’t just tabloid fodder; it’s emblematic of Wolff’s broader critique of the Trumps’ departure from conventional presidential and first lady norms. The alleged physical and metaphorical distance between Melania and Donald Trump isn’t just gossip; it’s a glaring testament to the fragmented personal lives often obscured by their public personas.

The $1 Billion Defamation Lawsuit

The heart of this spectacle is Melania Trump’s $1 billion defamation lawsuit against Wolff, rooted in allegations about her connection to Jeffrey Epstein. Here, the absurdity climbs to dizzying heights. The Trumps’ attempt to shift the lawsuit to Florida, ostensibly because they claim residency there, directly contradicts numerous reports of Melania’s return to New York. Wolff’s counterclaim, asserting that Melania is actually living in New York, not only challenges the Trumps’ narrative but also underscores a bizarre attempt to manipulate legal jurisdictions.

Melania’s Alleged New York Life

Wolff doesn’t stop at simply outlining the Trumps’ geographical inconsistencies. He paints Melania as attempting to carve out a “new business life in New York City,” leveraging her relationship with the presidency while simultaneously distancing herself from it. This portrayal, if accurate, reflects a shrewd, if not entirely unprecedented, move to capitalize on political fame. The Melania brand, as Wolff suggests, is being meticulously crafted to ensure financial and geographic independence from Donald Trump, a maneuver that is as fascinating as it is revealing.

The SLAPP Back

Central to Wolff’s defense is the invocation of anti-SLAPP laws, designed to prevent legal bullying through frivolous lawsuits. Wolff’s argument that the Trumps are using the lawsuit to silence him is a critical one, highlighting a perilous slope where power is wielded to stifle dissent and suppress free speech. This isn’t merely a legal defense; it’s a clarion call for the protection of democratic principles against the encroachments of the powerful.

What Lies Beneath

Perhaps most intriguing is Wolff’s assertion that this lawsuit could force the Trumps to answer questions under oath about their relationship with Epstein and more. This isn’t just about clearing Wolff’s name or settling a personal vendetta; it’s about peeling back the layers of secrecy that have long shrouded the Trumps’ private and public dealings.

Conclusion

The ongoing legal battle between Melania Trump and Michael Wolff is more than a mere defamation lawsuit; it’s a mirror reflecting the tumultuous, often opaque world of political power, personal branding, and the fight for free speech. As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the revelations and ramifications will reverberate far beyond the courtroom. In the end, it may not just be Wolff or the Trumps who are on trial, but the very nature of truth, power, and accountability in our time.
