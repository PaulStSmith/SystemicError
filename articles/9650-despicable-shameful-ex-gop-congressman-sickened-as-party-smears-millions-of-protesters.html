'Despicable! Shameful!' Ex-GOP congressman sickened as party smears millions of protesters

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Fearmongering: A Case Study on the “No Kings” Rallies

Los Angeles, California, USA, July 14, 2025, Protesters holding signs with slogans during the No Kings protest against government policies and supporting democracy and immigrant rights.

Multiple Republican lawmakers on Friday lobbed smears against the upcoming “No Kings” rallies scheduled to take place on October 18 across the US.

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) described the “No Kings” events as “a hate-America rally,” which he said would include “the Antifa crowd, the pro-Hamas crowd, and the Marxists.”

“They’re all going to gather on the [Washington] Mall,” Johnson continued. “We’ve got some House Democrats selling t-shirts for this event. It is an outrageous gathering for outrageous purposes.”

Rep. Tom Emmer (R-MN) echoed Johnson and linked the “No Kings” marches to the continued federal government shutdown, claiming Democrats were refusing to vote to fund the government “to score political points with the terrorist wing of their party, which is set to hold... a hate-America rally in DC next week.”

Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS), during an interview on Newsmax, described the October 18 protests as “a Soros paid-for protest” filled with “professional protesters” and “agitators,” and threatened those who attend with a deployment of armed forces like the ones President Donald Trump has imposed on cities including Chicago and Memphis.

“We’ll have to get the National Guard out,” he said. “Hopefully it will be peaceful. I doubt it.”

Joe Walsh, a former Republican congressman from Illinois who broke with the party over its refusal to hold Trump accountable, expressed disgust with linking the “No Kings” marches to terrorists.

“Looks like everyone in my former political party has signed on to the Donald Trump/Stephen Miller game plan, which means calling Democrats ‘terrorists,’” he wrote in a post on X. “Terrorists? Despicable! Shameful!”

The October 18 “No Kings” rallies are a sequel to nationwide demonstrations that took place this past June and drew an estimated 5 million people across over 2,100 cities and towns.

Contrary to the Republican lawmakers’ claims, these protests were overwhelmingly peaceful demonstrations against what organizers described as “the gross abuse of power that we’ve seen consistently from the Trump administration.”

Ezra Levin, cofounder of progressive organization Indivisible, one of the groups behind the “No Kings” marches, vowed on Thursday that the upcoming events will be “the largest peaceful protest in modern American history.”

Levin also remained defiant in the face of baseless claims that his organization funds violent rioting.

“Trump and Miller can lie, smear, and threaten all they want,” he said. “They will lose.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Fearmongering: A Case Study on the “No Kings” Rallies

In a spectacle of disinformation and fearmongering that would make even George Orwell pause, multiple Republican lawmakers have taken to the airwaves to demonize the upcoming “No Kings” rallies scheduled for October 18 across the United States. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) led the charge with a description of the event that reads more like a fever dream than a factual account, labeling it a “hate-America rally” and lumping together a hodgepodge of groups including “the Antifa crowd, the pro-Hamas crowd, and the Marxists.”

The rhetorical strategy here is as transparent as it is tired: conflate legitimate protest with extremism to delegitimize dissent. Johnson’s claim finds company in Rep. Tom Emmer (R-MN) who ties the protests to the federal government shutdown, suggesting Democrats are in cahoots with what he terms the “terrorist wing of their party.” This leap of logic not only stretches credulity but outright snaps it, painting a picture of a grand conspiracy with no basis in reality.

Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS) doubles down on this fantastical narrative during a Newsmax interview, invoking the boogeyman of George Soros to claim the protests are nothing but a gathering of “professional protesters” and “agitators.” Marshall’s threat to deploy the National Guard echoes dark periods of American history where the state used force to suppress civil rights and anti-war protests. The implication that peaceful assembly warrants a militarized response is both dangerous and antithetical to democratic values.

In stark contrast to these claims, Joe Walsh, a former Republican congressman, offers a rare voice of reason, expressing disgust with the tactic of labeling political opponents as terrorists. Walsh’s critique shines a light on the deep irony of a party that claims to champion free speech and liberty engaging in such undemocratic rhetoric.

The “No Kings” rallies, which follow in the footsteps of previous demonstrations that drew millions in peaceful protest, stand as a testament to the resilience of American democracy. Ezra Levin, co-founder of Indivisible, one of the organizing groups, promises that the event will be “the largest peaceful protest in modern American history.” Levin’s defiance in the face of baseless accusations underscores a commitment to peaceful assembly and democratic principles that the aforementioned lawmakers seem keen to forget.

In their rush to paint the “No Kings” rallies as a hotbed of anti-American sentiment, these Republican figures not only misrepresent the nature of the protests but also betray a fundamental misunderstanding (or perhaps willful ignorance) of what democracy entails. The right to protest, to assemble, to voice dissent, is as American as apple pie. Attempting to smear these demonstrations as anything but an exercise of these rights is not only disingenuous but a dangerous game that seeks to undermine the very fabric of democratic engagement.

In conclusion, the attempt by certain Republican lawmakers to demonize the “No Kings” rallies is a clear exercise in fearmongering aimed at delegitimizing lawful protest. Their statements, steeped in misinformation and hyperbole, serve only to fan the flames of division and distract from the genuine grievances that drive millions to march. As these rallies unfold, it is imperative that we remember the importance of peaceful protest in a healthy democracy and the danger of those who seek to vilify it.
