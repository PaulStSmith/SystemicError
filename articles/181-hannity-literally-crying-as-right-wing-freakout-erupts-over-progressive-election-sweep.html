Hannity 'literally crying' as right-wing 'freakout' erupts over progressive election sweep

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 25, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

When the Party’s Enforcers Lose, They Call It Panic

The Actual Loss

Three progressive Democratic congressional candidates won their New York primary races on Tuesday. That is the basic fact. The rest is the noise that followed: Fox figures, centrist alarmists, and Democratic incumbents acting as if a primary defeat were a civilizational event instead of an electorate rejecting the people already in power.

What mattered politically was not the chatter about “freakout.” It was that Mamdani-backed candidates, including Claire Valdez, Brad Lander, and Darializa Avila Chevalier, won their races, and two of them defeated incumbents whom House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries had campaigned hard to protect.

Who Actually Holds Power

The people with actual institutional power in this story were not the commentators pretending to be shocked. They were the incumbents, the party leadership, and the media ecosystem that normalizes their preferences.

Jeffries was not a spectator. He actively backed the incumbents and still lost. That matters because it exposes how little control party leadership has when its voters stop accepting the script. The losing side still gets treated as the center of gravity, which is how establishment power survives even after defeat: by narrating itself as the default authority.

The Convenient Panic

Sean Hannity’s performance was not analysis. It was a reflex. He cast the election as a “scary transformation” in the Democratic Party, as if a primary result in New York were evidence of collective breakdown rather than a rebuke to stale leadership and pro-Israel orthodoxy.

That framing is useful because it shifts the focus away from decisions and onto anxiety. It invites viewers to react to the emotional temperature of the losers instead of the political meaning of the vote. The same move shows up in Michael A. Cohen’s comment about a “scary night for New York City Jews,” which tries to convert a progressive electoral win into a communal alarm signal. But the source itself notes that New York’s 10th Congressional District is 24.4% Jewish, the second-largest Jewish population of any congressional district in the country. The attempt to blur criticism of Israel into panic about Jews is not subtle. It is political misdirection.

Blame the Electorate, Spare the Leaders

The cleaner story here is that voters in these districts chose candidates aligned with a sharper critique of Israel and rejected the incumbents backed by party leadership. The dishonest story is that this outcome proves extremism, danger, or ethnic hostility.

That dishonest story matters because it protects the people who made the losing bet. It lets Democratic insiders and their media allies avoid saying the obvious: their candidates lost because they were not persuasive enough, and because a slice of the party’s base no longer accepts being disciplined into silence on Israel or told that internal dissent is a moral defect.

The election did not produce confusion. It produced a result. The confusion is in the commentary, where defeat by the establishment is immediately recast as a threat from below.

The Pattern Underneath

This is the recurring structure: when organized voters challenge entrenched power, the response is not to listen but to pathologize. If the challenge comes from the left, it becomes radicalism. If the issue touches Israel, it becomes fear-mongering about Jewish safety. If incumbents lose, the problem is described as a mood swing, a shock, a “freakout,” anything except a loss of legitimacy.

That is the real political pattern here. Institutional actors keep their hands on the levers, then act offended when people notice what those levers are doing.
