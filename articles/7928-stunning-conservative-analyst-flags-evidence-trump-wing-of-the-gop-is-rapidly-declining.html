'Stunning': Conservative analyst flags evidence Trump wing of the GOP is rapidly declining

Ordinarily, an election in an area as conservative as Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District isn’t a major story. That district, which Donald Trump carried by 22 percent in 2024, typically goes hard-right — and a GOP victory comes as no surprise.

But on Tuesday, December 2 in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District, political strategists paid very close attention to a special election that found Democrat Aftyn Behn competing with Republican Matt Van Epps — who won, but by much smaller margins than Trump. The election became largely a referendum on Trump’s second presidency, and according to conservative New York Times columnist David French, the election results are a very bad sign for Republicans ahead of the 2026 midterms.

“The Republican candidate, Matt Van Epps, defeated his Democratic opponent, Aftyn Behn, by just under nine points,” French explains. “In some places, a nine-point Republican margin is considered a resounding victory. But not in Tennessee 7. In 2024, the district voted for Donald Trump by a 22-point margin. At the same time, Mark Green, the Republican Van Epps succeeded last week, won reelection by 21 points. This is not a swing district or one that Democrats expect to win this side of the apocalypse.”

The Never Trump conservative, a frequent guest on MS NOW, emphasizes that Behn didn’t run as a centrist but rather, as a staunch progressive — and has been compared to Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-New York). Yet she only lost by single digits in a deep-red district.

“That it was close at all was stunning, not least because Behn is hardly an ideological match for one of the most conservative districts in Tennessee,” French argues. “She’s been labeled — and not as a gesture of love and respect — the ‘AOC of Tennessee.’ She once posted, then deleted, during the George Floyd protests in 2020, ‘Good morning, especially to the 54 percent of Americans that believe burning down a police station is justified.’ Since the 7th District includes parts of Nashville, it was also unhelpful that Behn said on a podcast, also in 2020: ‘I hate the city…. I hate country music.’”

The “MAGA faction,” French laments, does the GOP a major disservice when it bashes “traditional conservatives.”

“If you’ve been doing nothing but shedding support since Trump was sworn in,” French argues, “and if the Democrats work to win over decent Republicans who are repulsed by what their party has become, then the gerrymandering party may be reminded of one of Solomon’s most memorable proverbs: ‘Pride goeth before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall.’”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Tennessee’s 7th: A Harbinger of GOP Troubles?

In the aftermath of the special election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District, where Republican Matt Van Epps scraped a victory with margins significantly slimmer than those enjoyed by Donald Trump or his predecessor, Mark Green, the political landscape seems ripe for a seismic shift. This isn’t merely about a single election in a conservative stronghold; it’s a symptom of a broader malaise within the Republican Party, one that could forecast a tumultuous 2026 midterm if not addressed with the seriousness it demands.

The Shrinking Margin of Victory: A Red Flag?

Let’s dissect the numbers: Trump carried this district by a whopping 22 percent in 2024, and Green won his reelection by a similar margin. Enter Matt Van Epps, who managed to secure the seat but with a victory margin that’s less than half of those figures. This isn’t just a win; it’s a warning. The GOP’s stronghold in what should have been a safe seat has been visibly weakened, and the implications of this cannot be overstated.

Aftyn Behn: The Progressive Challenger

The fact that Aftyn Behn, a candidate with progressive credentials strong enough to earn her the moniker ‘AOC of Tennessee,’ managed to close the gap to a single-digit deficit is nothing short of astonishing. Her platform, far from being a centrist appeal aimed at swaying undecided voters, was unabashedly progressive. Her performance in such a conservative district suggests a growing discontent with the status quo, even among voters who traditionally lean Republican. This is a significant development, one that signals a potential shift in voter sentiment that the GOP would be foolish to ignore.

The MAGA Dilemma

David French’s analysis touches upon a critical issue plaguing the Republican Party: the internal conflict between traditional conservatives and the MAGA wing. This division is not just ideological; it’s strategic suicide in districts where the party can ill afford to alienate moderate supporters. French’s warning, invoking the proverb about pride going before a fall, should be a wake-up call to the GOP. The party’s unwavering commitment to Trumpism, even in the face of electoral setbacks, may well be its Achilles’ heel.

The Bigger Picture

What this special election underscores is a vulnerability within the Republican ranks that, if left unaddressed, could spell disaster in the 2026 midterms. The party is at a crossroads, facing a choice between continuing down the path set by Trump, with all its divisive rhetoric and polarizing policies, or taking a step back to reassess and realign with the broader electorate.

The GOP’s dilemma is not just about recalibrating strategies or messaging; it’s about recognizing the changing dynamics of American politics. Voters are increasingly showing a willingness to cross party lines, driven by issues rather than blind loyalty. The results from Tennessee’s 7th are a canary in the coal mine, signaling that business as usual may no longer be a viable option for the Republican Party.

In essence, the election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District serves as a poignant reminder that the landscape of American politics is ever-evolving. The GOP’s failure to adapt, to recognize the warning signs, and to bridge the growing divide within its ranks could well pave the way for its downfall. As the party looks ahead to 2026, the lessons from this election will be critical. Will they heed the warning, or will pride indeed go before the fall? Only time will tell, but as of now, the Republican Party stands at a precarious juncture, with its future hanging in the balance.
