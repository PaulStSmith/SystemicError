Following his appearance at the funeral of Pope Francis on Saturday, where he was called out in a homily, Donald Trump jumped onto Truth Social to lash out at the New York Times and, in particular, reporter Peter Baker.

In his long tirade, the president took extreme exception to a Saturday report by Baker about negotiations involving Ukraine where the status of Crimea has become a sticking point.

In his rant, the president accused Baker and the New York Times of having it all wrong and accused them of giving former President Barack Obama a pass on the annexation of Crimea by Russia.

As he wrote, "No matter what deal I make with respect to Russia/Ukraine, no matter how good it is, even if it’s the greatest deal ever made, The Failing New York Times will speak BADLY of it. Liddle’ Peter Baker, the very biased and untalented writer for The Times, followed his Editor’s demands and wrote that Ukraine should get back territory, including, I suppose, Crimea, and other ridiculous requests, in order to stop the killing that is worse than anything since World War II."

He then added, "Why doesn’t this lightweight reporter say that it was Obama who made it possible for Russia to steal Crimea from Ukraine without even a shot being fired. It was also Liddle’ Peter who wrote an absolutely fawning, yet terribly written Biography, on Obama. It was a JOKE! Did Baker ever criticize the Obama Crimea Giveaway? NO, not once, only TRUMP, and I’ve had nothing to do with this stupid war, other than early on, when I gave Ukraine Javelins, and Obama gave them sheets."

"This is Sleepy Joe Biden’s War, not mine," he accused. "It was a loser from day one, and should have never happened, and wouldn’t have happened if I were President at the time. I’m just trying to clean up the mess that was left to me by Obama and Biden, and what a mess it is. With all of that being said, there was no reason for Putin to be shooting missiles into civilian areas, cities and towns, over the last few days. It makes me think that maybe he doesn’t want to stop the war, he’s just tapping me along, and has to be dealt with differently, through 'Banking' or 'Secondary Sanctions?' Too many people are dying!!!"

Our Analysis:

The Unfolding Drama: Trump's Tirade on Truth Social

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the era of social media politics, Donald Trump took to Truth Social to unleash a torrent of criticism against The New York Times and reporter Peter Baker. This latest episode showcases Trump's unyielding contempt for mainstream media and his unique blend of personal vendettas with international diplomacy issues. Let’s dissect the layers of misinformation and irony peppered throughout Trump’s tirade.

The Crimea Conundrum

Trump's rant was ostensibly triggered by a report on the ongoing negotiations involving Ukraine, with the status of Crimea emerging as a major sticking point. Trump accuses Peter Baker and The New York Times of misrepresenting his efforts, claiming that no matter how beneficial any deal he makes might be, it would still be disparaged by them. This claim conveniently ignores the complex geopolitical nuances of the Russia-Ukraine conflict and oversimplifies the situation to a mere matter of personal achievement or failure.

The Obama Obsession

Trump's diatribe swiftly transitions into a critique of the Obama administration's handling of the Crimea annexation by Russia. He alleges that the media, epitomized by Baker and The New York Times, gave Obama a "pass" while unfairly targeting him. This fixation on contrasting his presidency with Obama's is a recurring theme in Trump's rhetoric, often diverting attention from his own administration's actions—or lack thereof.

Misrepresenting Military Aid

Trump's assertion that he "gave Ukraine Javelins, and Obama gave them sheets" is a gross oversimplification and misrepresentation of the aid provided by both administrations to Ukraine. It attempts to paint a picture of Trump as the more supportive president in the context of Ukraine's military needs, despite the complex and multifaceted nature of U.S. aid to Ukraine over the years.

Blaming Biden

In a predictable pivot, Trump lays the responsibility of the ongoing conflict at the feet of "Sleepy Joe Biden," claiming that the war is Biden's, not his. This statement not only absolves himself of any accountability but also ignores the timeline and development of the conflict that predates Biden's presidency. It's a classic Trump move: deflect, deny, and distort.

The Solution According to Trump

Trump hints at a solution through "Banking" or "Secondary Sanctions," suggesting a different approach to dealing with Putin. While the suggestion of exploring alternative strategies in international diplomacy is valid, it is buried under layers of personal grievances and misrepresentations, making it difficult to take seriously.

The Bottom Line

Trump's post on Truth Social is a microcosm of his broader political strategy: distort the facts, deflect blame, and divide opinion. His rant against Peter Baker and The New York Times is less about the intricacies of foreign policy and more about settling personal scores and perpetuating a narrative of victimhood at the hands of the mainstream media.

In the end, Trump's tirade offers little in terms of constructive criticism or viable solutions to the ongoing conflict in Ukraine. Instead, it serves as a reminder of the tumultuous relationship between Trump and the media, and the dangerous precedent it sets for public discourse and international diplomacy.
