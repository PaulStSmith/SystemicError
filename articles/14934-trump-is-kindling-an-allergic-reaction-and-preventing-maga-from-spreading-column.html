Trump is 'kindling an allergic reaction' — and preventing MAGA from spreading: column

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Myopic Vision of a "Global MAGA Embrace"

U.S. President Donald Trump holds a hat after signing an executive order in the Oval Office, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S. February 25, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

The world appears poised to embrace MAGA ideals — but President Donald Trump is kneecapping that global embrace, a new column opined.

“Today, the global economy is arguably fertile ground for a MAGA. Yet, something is not translating,” according to Eduardo Porter.

The Washington Post columnist and editorial board member believes this lack of translation could be a good thing for those on the left. “There is evidence that Trump is kindling an allergic reaction in other pastures.”

Porter pointed to the recent elections in Canada and Australia, “where Trump’s ascension encouraged voters to drop their support for right-wing Trumpy bedfellows and embrace liberal candidates.”

It is also happening in Denmark, where Social Democrats received a “polling boost after Trump started venting his lust for Greenland.”

Democrats in Norway hope their voters will hold a similar sentiment for their elections in September.

According to Porter, Trump’s presence is “eroding support for the right-wing Progress Party in favor of the incumbent Labor government.”

He does note, “Opposition to Trump is not automatically translating to support for liberalism.” Like in China, where they are seeing an upsurge in nationalism.

Likewise, in Germany, the extreme right “ethno-nationalist Alternative for Germany (AfD)” is gaining popularity. However, voters elevated Friedrich Merz, who is predicted to be a center-right chancellor.

Brittain, too, is seeing a surge on the right. The columnist notes, the “right-wing Reform UK party has also risen in the polls, making strides in local elections.”

Regardless, Porter believes something is “standing in the way of a global Trumpian wave.” He later surmised, “What stands against a worldwide Trumpian pseudo-utopia is Donald Trump: Hostility toward the man is clouding views about his message.”

“This offers a sliver of hope to those of us who still cling to the promise that liberal democracy offers around the world,” he said.

“Trump’s America is unlikely to make many friends because of the man’s penchant for bullying and extorting everyone around him.” Porter believes this attitude, “could hold at bay the emergence of an illiberal, Trump-inflected world order.”

Our Analysis:

The Myopic Vision of a "Global MAGA Embrace"

In a recent column that skims the surface of the complex geopolitics of our time, Eduardo Porter posits a rather simplistic view: the world is fertile ground for MAGA (Make America Great Again) ideals, but President Donald Trump himself is the scythe mowing down any chance of these ideals taking root globally. This analysis, however sincere, misses the forest for the trees in several critical ways.

Firstly, Porter's assertion that the global economy is ripe for MAGA is a sweeping generalization that ignores the nuanced and multifaceted reasons behind economic disenchantment worldwide. It's not just about economic nationalism or protectionism; it's about systemic failures, inequality, and the search for sustainable solutions that don't harken back to some mythologized past.

The Misinterpretation of Global Political Shifts

Porter's evidence of Trump's supposed global kneecapping rests on recent elections in Canada, Australia, and a speculative boost for Social Democrats in Denmark due to Trump's bizarre interest in Greenland. This analysis is superficial at best. To suggest that these shifts are merely reactions to Trump's persona, rather than complex decisions made by sovereign electorates with their own diverse concerns, is both reductive and patronizing.

The Oversimplification of Opposition

The column rightly notes that opposition to Trump does not equate to support for liberalism, citing rising nationalism in China and the growth of the ethno-nationalist Alternative for Germany (AfD). Yet, it fails to delve into the deeper reasons behind these phenomena. Instead, it sticks to a surface-level assumption that Trump's personality and actions alone are what's preventing a global swing to the right. This oversimplification ignores broader trends of dissatisfaction with the status quo, economic anxiety, and the failure of traditional political structures to address contemporary challenges.

The Misguided Hope in Trump's Unpopularity

Porter's conclusion that Trump's unpopularity could prevent a global shift towards illiberalism and nationalism is wishful thinking. It's not just about one man's character flaws or political strategies. The issues are systemic, deep-rooted, and require far more than just a rejection of a single figure to address. The real work lies in tackling the underlying causes of disillusionment with liberal democracy, not just celebrating its resilience in the face of an unpopular figure.

The Real Stand Against a "Trumpian Pseudo-Utopia"

To believe that hostility towards Trump is what's standing in the way of a global embrace of MAGA ideals is to fundamentally misunderstand the global political landscape. It's not just about Trump; it's about what he represents: a backlash against globalization, elitism, and the status quo. This is a wave that won't simply dissipate with Trump's departure from the political scene.

In Conclusion

Porter's column, while an interesting read, ultimately provides a shallow analysis of a deeply complex situation. The challenge of preserving and promoting liberal democracy in a changing world order is not as simple as overcoming the unpopularity of Donald Trump. It requires a rigorous examination of our current systems, a willingness to address their failings, and the imagination to envision a future that rectifies these errors. To reduce this global challenge to the personality and politics of one man is not just simplistic; it's dangerously myopic.
