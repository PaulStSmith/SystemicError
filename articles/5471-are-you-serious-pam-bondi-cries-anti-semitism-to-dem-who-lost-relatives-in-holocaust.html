'Are you serious?' Pam Bondi cries 'anti-Semitism' to Dem who lost relatives in Holocaust

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a House Judiciary Committee hearing on oversight of the Justice Department, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 11, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Rep. Becca Balint (D-VT) clashed with Attorney General Pam Bondi after the nation’s top law enforcement official refused to say whether she had investigated Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s ties to sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

“The Epstein files contain evidence of a multi-decade international criminal conspiracy involving some of the wealthiest and the most powerful people in the world,” Balint told Bondi during a House Judiciary Committee hearing on Wednesday. “And obviously, as you know, President Trump’s name is all over them, but so are the names of other senior Trump officials. Howard Lutnick, Secretary of Commerce, John Phelan, the Secretary of the Navy, and Stephen Feinberg, the Deputy Secretary of Defense.”

“Attorney General Bondi, yes or no, has the Justice Department asked Secretary Lutnick about his ties to Epstein?” the lawmaker wondered.

Bondi deflected by noting that Lutnick had addressed the claims during a congressional hearing.

“Has Deputy Secretary Feinberg talked to Department of Justice about his ties to Jeffrey Epstein?” Balint pressed. “Yes or no?”

Bondi again deflected by attacking Balint.

“Oh my gosh!” the Democrat exclaimed. “Across the world, across the world, prominent men who’ve been exposed in the files have resigned their jobs in disgrace. And that’s a good first start.”

“Please stop talking,” she said as Bondi interrupted.

“Shame on you,” Bondi quipped.

“Oh, for goodness sakes,” Balint said. “This is pathetic, Mr. Chair. I am not asking trick questions here. The American people have a right to know the answers to this. These are senior officials and the Trump administration. This is not a game, Secretary.”

“I’m Attorney General,” Bondi stated.

“My apologies, I couldn’t tell,” Balint shot back.

After Balint’s time was up, a Republican gave Bondi a chance to respond.

“I was curious if you, Congresswoman, asked Bill Clinton that?” the attorney general said. “I didn’t see one tweet when Joe Biden was in office about Bill Clinton.”

“Weak sauce,” Balint shrugged.

“And also, I want the record to reflect that, you know, with this anti-Semitic culture,” Bondi said, referring to one of Balint’s votes.

“Oh, do you want to go there, Attorney General?” the lawmaker shouted back. “Do you want to go there? Are you serious? Talking about anti-Semitism to a woman who lost her grandfather in the Holocaust? Really?”

Our Analysis:

The Evasion Dance: A Political Ballet

In a display as predictable as it is disheartening, Rep. Becca Balint’s confrontation with Attorney General Pam Bondi during a House Judiciary Committee hearing quickly devolved into a masterclass in evasion and political deflection. At the heart of the matter? The deeply troubling ties between high-ranking Trump officials and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

Balint, with commendable tenacity, pressed Bondi for a simple “yes or no” on whether the Justice Department had investigated Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s connection to Epstein. The response? A deflection so transparent it would make a pane of glass jealous. Bondi’s choice to sidestep such a critical inquiry by merely noting Lutnick’s previous congressional testimony is not just inadequate; it’s an insult to the notion of accountability.

The dance continued with Balint’s inquiry about Deputy Secretary Stephen Feinberg. Once again, Bondi chose to attack the messenger rather than address the message, a tactic as old as it is ineffective. The exchange descended further into the absurd with Bondi’s rebuttal, invoking Bill Clinton and Joe Biden in what can only be described as a desperate attempt to redirect the conversation. It’s a classic case of whataboutism that serves no purpose other than to muddy the waters.

Balint’s response, “Weak sauce,” hits the nail on the head. Bondi’s attempt to shift focus is not just weak; it’s pathetic. It underscores a fundamental problem in our political discourse: the inability, or unwillingness, of our leaders to confront uncomfortable truths head-on.

Even more disturbing was Bondi’s insinuation of anti-Semitism, a baseless and dangerous accusation made all the more egregious considering Balint’s personal history. To weaponize such a serious allegation in a feeble attempt to discredit or silence a political opponent is not just shameful; it’s morally bankrupt.

The Bottom Line

This exchange is a microcosm of a larger issue plaguing our political system: the erosion of truth and accountability. When elected officials and their appointees can so easily deflect, deny, and distract from legitimate inquiries, the very foundation of our democracy is at risk.

The American public deserves better. They deserve answers, not evasion. They demand accountability, not attacks. And above all, they require leaders who are willing to confront uncomfortable truths, not those who hide behind the veil of political convenience.

In the end, Balint’s effort to shine a light on the dark connections between Epstein and senior Trump officials, despite being met with resistance, is a stark reminder of the courage needed to challenge the status quo. It’s high time for those in power to start providing real answers. The American people are watching, and they are tired of the evasion dance.
