'Every day it's something new': Insiders claim Trump obsessed with remaking Washington

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

President Donald Trump’s laser focus on remodeling the White House and greater Washington, D.C. has reached unprecedented levels when compared with past administrations, according to several White House insiders and officials that spoke with Politico.

“There have always been updates and changes to the White House; this is probably the first time when there are so many at once,” said Stewart McLaurin, president of the White House Historical Association, speaking with Politico for a story published on Monday.

Only seven months into his second term, Trump has made a number of remodeling proposals to both the White House and the D.C., including a new $200 million White House ballroom, a revamped Rose Garden, new flag poles on the White House lawns, a $2 billion street repavement project, and dozens additions to the Oval Office, most involving gold.

And according to a White House insider, the obsession with remodeling comes up daily.

“He seems to wake up and say, what else needs to be improved?” said a White House insider to Politico, described by the outlet as someone “familiar with the president’s plan,” speaking on the condition of anonymity. “Every day, it’s something new.”

While Trump’s focus in recent weeks has largely been on his federal takeover and crime crackdown on the nation’s capital, he’s also regularly talked about making D.C. “beautified,” pledging to do so within a 12-month period.

According to two White House insiders who spoke with Politico, a large part of the fixation on having D.C. “beautified” is Trump’s desire to have said projects completed in time for the United States’ 250th anniversary, which will occur next July 4.

Daniel Abramson, a Boston University professor of European and American architecture, told Politico that he believed Trump’s obsession with remodeling came from the president’s desire for “attention and power.”

“The gilding of the Oval seems to be about creating an imperial or royal setting for the performing of the duties of the presidency and a powerful executive branch,” Abramson said. “This is clearly a president who wants the attention and power concentrated on him.”

Our Analysis:

The Gilded Facade of Governance: Trump's Remodeling Frenzy

In an era where the line between political leadership and royal entitlement blurs, President Donald Trump's latest escapades in remodeling the White House and the broader Washington, D.C., area have catapulted this phenomenon to new heights of absurdity. According to a recent Politico piece, Trump's second term has been characterized by an unprecedented level of focus on aesthetic overhauls, with proposals including a $200 million White House ballroom and a $2 billion street repavement project. This spree of extravagance raises critical questions about priorities, governance, and the symbolic implications of such "improvements."

The Emperor's New Decor

At the heart of Trump's remodeling mania is a conspicuous obsession with gold—dozens of additions to the Oval Office alone—and other grandiose modifications. This fixation isn't merely a matter of taste but a clear signal of a deeper desire for imperial grandeur, as noted by Daniel Abramson, a professor of European and American architecture. The act of gilding the Oval Office and extravagantly revamping public spaces in D.C. speaks volumes about Trump's yearning for an “imperial or royal setting” for the presidency. It's a stark reminder of his penchant for concentrating attention and power onto himself, a trait starkly at odds with the democratic ethos and the principle of public service that should define his office.

Misplaced Priorities in a Time of Crisis

While aesthetic enhancements to public spaces can contribute to national pride and heritage, the sheer scale and cost of Trump's proposals—amidst myriad pressing national issues—highlight a profound misalignment of priorities. The Politico article points out that this remodeling frenzy coincides with Trump's controversial "federal takeover and crime crackdown" in the nation’s capital, suggesting a troubling diversion of focus from governance to glorification.

Moreover, the intention to complete these projects in time for the United States’ 250th anniversary ostensibly frames these endeavors as a patriotic effort. However, one must question the patriotism of allocating billions of taxpayer dollars to superficial projects while countless Americans grapple with healthcare, education, and economic disparities. The contrast between the proposed $200 million ballroom and the underfunded public sector is not just jarring—it's a gross misappropriation of national resources.

The Aesthetics of Power

Trump's daily preoccupation with remodeling, as reported by a White House insider, is not just a quirk but a manifestation of his approach to the presidency—one that prioritizes appearance over substance, and spectacle over service. This approach, while not entirely unprecedented in historical contexts of leadership, is particularly glaring in the context of a democratic leader of the modern era. It reflects a misunderstanding, or perhaps a disregard, for the symbolic role of the presidency as a position of service rather than monarchic rule.

Conclusion: A Legacy Cast in Gold but Lacking in Substance

As Trump's second term progresses, the legacy he appears intent on leaving is one characterized by surface-level enhancements and a reimagining of the presidency in his own opulent image. This Politico report sheds light on a presidency caught up in the aesthetics of power, seemingly indifferent to the real needs and challenges facing the country. The remodeling spree, while perhaps leaving a lasting visual impression, underscores a leadership style more concerned with the trappings of power than with its responsibilities—a gilded facade masking a systemic error in prioritizing the form over the function of governance.
