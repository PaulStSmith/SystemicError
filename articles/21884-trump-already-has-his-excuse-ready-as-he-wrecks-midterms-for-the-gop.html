Trump already has his excuse ready as he wrecks midterms for the GOP

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Revenge Is Not a Governing Program

Power, Not Personality

Donald Trump is not drifting through politics like a random impulse engine. He is the president, and the article’s core fact is simple: he is using the full authority of that office to indulge grievance, spectacle, and retaliation while the country’s actual problems get shoved aside. The reporting notes downward revisions in monthly job figures, a stalled promised boost from World Cup hospitality and leisure spending, and an Iran war that has already dragged past five months. That is the governing backdrop. Trump’s answer is not management. It is revenge.

The Useful Lie

The most revealing part of the story is not Trump’s erratic behavior. It is his preloaded excuse. He is already setting up a defense that says Republicans may lose the midterms, but somehow he will not own the damage. That is classic authoritarian accounting: absorb credit, outsource blame. If the party eats the losses, he will claim emotional distance from the wreckage he created. Nothing in the article suggests he is remotely serious about governing in a way that would spare Republicans from consequences.

Republicans Chose This

This is not just Trump freelancing while Republicans stand helpless on the sidelines. The article shows the party adopting his fixations as its own. The Senate Homeland Committee voting to hold Dr. Anthony Fauci in contempt is not governance; it is ritual vengeance dressed up as oversight. Fauci said the move was driven by Republican obsession with revenge over COVID-19 restrictions, and the piece calls it a ridiculous waste of time. That is accurate. These elected Republicans are not victims of Trump’s moods. They are active participants in converting public office into a grievance theater.

The Misdirection

The framing in the source is useful only up to a point. It captures Trump’s pettiness, but the deeper problem is not that he is distracted from “kitchen table concerns.” It is that he is deliberately replacing those concerns with a politics of punishment and self-worship. The poll cited in the piece, with 68 percent saying Trump is not focused enough on their problems, is not a signal of confusion. It is a measure of abandonment. Voters are seeing a president who treats their lives as background noise.

What He Understands

Trump’s line to Punchbowl News is the giveaway: Republicans may be angry at him, but not at him personally. That is the whole operation. He understands that modern right-wing politics has been trained to separate the leader from the consequences. The party can suffer, the institution can decay, and he can still present himself as the only authentic source of justice. His campaign vow, “I am your justice… I am your retribution,” was never a stray slogan. It was the governing philosophy.

The Pattern

This story is not about one man’s temper. It is about a political movement that has fused state power with personal vengeance and then expects everyone else to pay the bill. Trump sets the tone, Republicans carry it out, and the public is left with the wreckage. That is the larger pattern: when institutions surrender to grievance politics, they stop serving the country and start protecting the ego of the most dangerous man in the room.
