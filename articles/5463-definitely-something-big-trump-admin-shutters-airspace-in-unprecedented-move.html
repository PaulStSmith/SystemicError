'Definitely something big': Trump admin shutters airspace in 'unprecedented' move

The Trump administration’s Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issued an “unprecedented” order Wednesday morning shuttering all airspace around El Paso, Texas for ten days, an order that left one aviation expert stunned.

“I think it’s safe to say it’s something very big, either from a national security standpoint or perhaps testing something; equipment, or something going into the air,” said Kyle Bailey, a former FAA Safety Team member, aviation expert and pilot, speaking with Fox News Wednesday.

Early Wednesday morning, the FAA issued what’s known as a Temporary Flight Restriction (TFR) around El Paso spanning a 10-mile radius and up to 18,000 feet. The FAA cited “special security reasons” as the reason for the TFR, which will remain in effect for 10 days, through Feb. 21.

“Normally temporary flight restrictions could be a day, be two days if it’s for VIPs, but it’s unprecedented for a 10-day temporary flight restriction,” Bailey continued.

“From the surface up to 18,000 feet and a ten-mile radius... if it was like a space ship launch or something like that, that would be unlimited, but 18,000 feet itself is very, very high for a temporary flight restriction. So it’s definitely something big, like a national security event [or] a very high-level VIP.”

The Trump administration has yet to comment on the reason for the TFR, and Fox News’ Ainsley Earhardt noted the unprecedented nature of the order.

“Overnight, the FAA [shut down] all airspace over El Paso, Texas for the next ten days for unspecified ‘security’ reasons, warning any planes violating [it] can be shot down,” Earhardt said. “Closing airspace over a major U.S. city for this reason is very rare and has not happened since after 9/11.”

Earhardt asked Bailey if the TFR could have been issued due to staffing shortages of air traffic controllers. He immediately ruled that theory out given the relatively low air traffic seen around El Paso. Instead, he again suspected the order could have been issued due to the U.S. military – which has two bases in El Paso, Biggs Army Airfield and Fort Bliss – “testing something that’s going into the air.

Our Analysis:

The Unprecedented Mystery of El Paso’s Airspace Closure

The Trump administration’s recent actions have once again catapulted into the limelight, this time for an “unprecedented” move by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to shutter all airspace around El Paso, Texas. This decision, effective for ten days, has left experts and the public alike scratching their heads, pondering the gravity and implications of such a measure.

The Facts: A Closer Look

The FAA’s issuance of a Temporary Flight Restriction (TFR) around El Paso is not just noteworthy for its duration but also for its stated reasons: “special security reasons.” Spanning a 10-mile radius and reaching up to 18,000 feet, the restriction is a clear deviation from the norm, particularly in its duration and altitude. Aviation expert Kyle Bailey’s remarks to Fox News underscore the unusual nature of this TFR, suggesting it could relate to either national security concerns or the testing of new equipment.

However, the silence from the Trump administration on the reason behind the TFR only fuels speculation and concern. The mention by Fox News’ Ainsley Earhardt of airspace over a major U.S. city being closed “for unspecified ‘security’ reasons” and the possibility of planes violating the restriction being shot down is particularly alarming. It is a reminder of the post-9/11 atmosphere of heightened security and the rare instances when such drastic measures are deemed necessary.

Analysis: Between the Lines

What’s immediately striking is the lack of transparency. The administration’s silence on the specifics of the “security reasons” behind the TFR leaves too much to the imagination. In an era where public trust in government is wavering, clear and open communication is paramount, especially when undertaking actions as significant as closing airspace.

The speculation by Bailey that the closure could be due to military testing, given El Paso’s proximity to two major U.S. military bases, is plausible. Yet, this hypothesis raises further questions about the nature of the tests and their potential impacts on the local population and environment. The military’s history with secretive tests and the sometimes subsequent revelation of their hazardous effects warrant public scrutiny and apprehension.

Moreover, Earhardt’s dismissal of the possibility that the TFR was issued due to staffing shortages among air traffic controllers, given El Paso’s relatively low air traffic, points to a more significant underlying reason. This acknowledgement, albeit indirect, hints at the gravity of the situation and the potential risks that warranted such an extensive no-fly zone.

Conclusion: The Need for Transparency and Accountability

In sum, the FAA’s decision, under the Trump administration’s directive, to implement a ten-day airspace closure around El Paso for “special security reasons” is a dramatic move that raises more questions than it answers. The lack of transparency regarding the decision’s rationale is troubling and not conducive to public trust. Whether for national security purposes, military testing, or another undisclosed reason, the American public deserves clarity and honesty from its government.

As we continue to navigate these uncertain times, let us demand more from our leaders. Transparency is not just a principle but a practice that ensures accountability and fosters trust. The situation in El Paso is a stark reminder of the need for both.
