Rep. Jim Jordan (R-OH) was confronted on CNN by new reporting that shows the Department of Justice removed records from the Jeffrey Epstein files related to a woman who accused President Donald Trump of sexually abusing her when she was a minor.

CNN’s Manu Raju hosted Jordan, the chairman of the House Judiciary Committee, on Tuesday to discuss the president’s upcoming State of the Union address, but he also confronted the Ohio Republican whether Trump should accept a request by some Epstein survivors to meet.

“Everyone knows that the president didn’t do anything wrong,” Jordan said, “because if he had, you don’t think the [Merrick] Garland Justice Department and the Biden administration would have brought something up? I mean, everyone knows he didn’t do anything wrong here. Our Justice Department is complying with the legislation we passed and with the courts getting that information out there as the law requires and as the courts have required, making sure you protect people who should be protected but getting the information to Congress, as we, as it said in the law we passed.”

Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, confirmed that a survivor had made serious allegations against the president and had been interviewed by the FBI, but NPR followed up on reporting by independent journalist Roger Sollenberger that found files related to her claims had been removed from the public database.

“Congressman Robert Garcia said today that the DOJ appears to have withheld FBI interviews with a survivor who accused President Trump of serious crimes,” Raju said. “Are you aware of DOJ withholding? Are you going to ask about it or do you want to do you want to see this information?”

Jordan insisted that he had not been aware of the DOJ removing those files from the database.

“I am not,” Jordan said. “I’m not, but I’m confident that Attorney General [Pam] Bondi, Deputy Attorney General [Todd] Blanche, the Justice Department is complying with the law, complying with the courts and getting that information to Congress as as the statute requires.”

Raju then asked about the arrests of Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and Peter Mandelson in the U.K. and whether U.S. officials should prosecute others who appear in the Epstein files.

“If people have done things wrong, then they will be prosecuted,” Jordan said. “In my understanding, in the U.K., it’s driven by, it looks like they’re they’re talking about people who gave illegal information or treasonous kind of actions. It looks like that’s the concern there, so the U.K. can prosecute people for that. They’re doing that, and if there are individuals who need to be held to account, prosecuted, I’m confident this Justice Department will do it.”

Some lawmakers have reviewed the unredacted Epstein files held by the DOJ, but Jordan said he was not among them.

“I have not,” he said. “Because the week that was made available was the week we had Attorney General Bondi. I was getting ready for that hearing,” referring to Bondi giving testimony to Congress.

“Normally we will, I will have someone probably do that, look at that,” Jordan added. “The staff, that’s normally how we do it on the Judiciary Committee. That’s in the Oversight Committee, but Judiciary Committee, we will have we have people who will look at that information and then get the information to us. “

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Deflection and Dismissal in Rep. Jim Jordan’s Comments on the Epstein-Trump Allegations

In a recent CNN interview, Rep. Jim Jordan (R-OH) was confronted with uncomfortable truths regarding the Department of Justice’s handling of records from the Jeffrey Epstein files, particularly those related to allegations against former President Donald Trump. Jordan’s responses, riddled with deflection and assurance without evidence, offer a glaring example of political maneuvering in the face of serious allegations.

The Allegations and DOJ’s Questionable Actions

CNN’s Manu Raju brought to light a disturbing revelation: the Department of Justice removed records from the Jeffrey Epstein files concerning a woman who accused Donald Trump of sexually abusing her when she was a minor. Despite the gravity of such allegations, Jordan was quick to dismiss them. He stated, “Everyone knows that the president didn’t do anything wrong,” a baseless claim that ignores the severity and specificity of the accusations at hand.

Jordan’s Unwavering Confidence in the DOJ

Jordan expressed unwavering confidence in the Justice Department, led by Attorney General Pam Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, to comply with the law. However, this confidence seems misplaced or, at best, naively optimistic given the DOJ’s apparent action to withhold information from the public database. This act of omission raises significant concerns about transparency and accountability within the department, concerns that Jordan glosses over with his assurances.

The Dismissal of Serious Allegations

Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) confirmed that a survivor had made serious allegations against Trump and had been interviewed by the FBI. Yet, Jordan, possibly in an attempt to protect his political ally, disregarded these allegations with a broad stroke, suggesting that if there was any truth to them, the Biden administration would have acted. This line of reasoning is flawed at best and dangerously dismissive at worst, as it ignores the complexities and potential political maneuvering within federal investigations.

Jordan’s Lack of Awareness and Responsibility

When confronted with the specific question of whether he was aware of the DOJ withholding information, Jordan claimed ignorance. His response, “I am not,” followed by a quick pivot back to expressing confidence in the DOJ, highlights a concerning lack of curiosity or responsibility regarding matters of grave importance. It’s worth questioning whether Jordan’s position as chairman of the House Judiciary Committee should come with a greater commitment to seeking the truth, rather than deferring to his staff or the assurances of department heads.

The International Context and Jordan’s Selective Scrutiny

Jordan’s comments on the arrests of Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor and Peter Mandelson in the U.K. were telling. He noted that if individuals had done wrong, they would be prosecuted, a statement that starkly contrasts with his dismissive stance on allegations against Trump. This selective scrutiny raises questions about Jordan’s consistency and the potential use of double standards when it comes to holding individuals accountable for their actions.

Conclusion

Rep. Jim Jordan’s responses in the CNN interview are emblematic of a broader issue within political discourse: the tendency to dismiss, deflect, and deny in the face of uncomfortable allegations. Jordan’s unwavering confidence in the DOJ, despite evidence of questionable actions, and his dismissal of serious allegations against Trump without thorough investigation, underscore a disturbing willingness to prioritize political allegiance over the pursuit of truth and justice. This approach not only undermines the integrity of political offices but also does a disservice to the survivors of Epstein’s network and the public at large.
