GOP dealt a loss in bid to get Texas election-rigging scheme back in place

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Texas Republicans’ Gerrymandering Gambit Hits Judicial Roadblock

Texas flag. (Photo credit: Svet foto / Shutterstock)

Texas Republicans’ first legal salvo to try to get their congressional gerrymander reinstated for the 2026 midterm election has been rejected in court — an expected move that sets them up for an appeal to the Supreme Court.

“The 3-judge panel in Texas has denied the state’s request to stay the ruling striking down the congressional map,” Brennan Center voting rights counsel Michael Li reported on Friday. “Judge Smith would have granted the stay. Under procedural rules, Texas is now clear to seek a stay at SCOTUS.”

“At SCOTUS, once a stay request is filed, the normal process is that the justice with responsibility for the circuit that a state is in (in this case, Justice Alito) will ask for a response from the other side & then refer the motion to the entire court for decision,” Li noted.

Texas’ new congressional map, which at President Donald Trump’s request carved up five Democratic congressional districts to try to make them more Republican-favoring, was unexpectedly struck down this week. A split 2-1 panel of federal judges including two district judges and one appellate judge found that state lawmakers violated the 14th and 15th Amendments by predominantly using illegal racial criteria to draw the new seats, obliterating Black districts while mostly leaving alone white districts.

“The public perception of this case is that it’s about politics,” stated the majority opinion written by U.S. Judge Jeff Brown, a conservative Federalist Society stalwart and Trump appointee who previously served on the Texas Supreme Court. “To be sure, politics played a role in drawing the 2025 Map. But it was much more than just politics. Substantial evidence shows that Texas racially gerrymandered the 2025 Map.”

The dissenting judge, a Reagan appointee named Jerry Smith, spent large parts of his opinion personally attacking Brown, accusing him of judicial misconduct and lamenting, “The main winners from Judge Brown’s opinion are George Soros and Gavin Newsom.”

Our Analysis:

Texas Republicans’ Gerrymandering Gambit Hits Judicial Roadblock

In a striking blow to the Texas GOP’s efforts to reconfigure the political landscape to their advantage, a federal court has thrown a wrench into their plans to gerrymander congressional districts. This decision, aimed at reinstating a controversial map for the 2026 midterm elections, has been met with a resounding judicial “no,” setting the stage for an appeal to the Supreme Court.

The Court’s Decision: A Step Towards Justice or a Stumbling Block?

The 3-judge panel’s refusal to stay the ruling that struck down the contentious congressional map is a critical moment in the ongoing battle for fair representation. The panel’s split decision underscores the deep divisions not only within our political landscape but also within our judiciary. It’s notable that Judge Smith, a Reagan appointee, stood alone in his willingness to grant the stay, a decision that seems less about jurisprudence and more about partisanship.

Supreme Court: The Next Battlefield

As Texas gears up to appeal to the Supreme Court, the spotlight falls on Justice Alito, who oversees the circuit Texas falls within. The procedural steps ahead will not only test the mettle of Texas Republicans’ legal arguments but also the Supreme Court’s stance on gerrymandering, a practice increasingly scrutinized for undermining democratic principles.

The Underlying Issue: Racial Gerrymandering

At the heart of this legal skirmish is a damning accusation: that Texas lawmakers, at the behest of then-President Donald Trump, engaged in racial gerrymandering. By allegedly manipulating district boundaries to dilute the voting power of predominantly Black districts while sparing white ones, they stand accused of violating both the 14th and 15th Amendments. This isn’t just a question of political gamesmanship; it’s a profound issue of racial injustice and inequality.

The Majority Opinion: A Condemnation of Racial Manipulation

U.S. Judge Jeff Brown, despite his conservative credentials and Trump appointment, delivered a scathing majority opinion. His acknowledgment that the map-drawing was influenced by more than mere politics, but by a deliberate attempt to racially gerrymander, is a remarkable admission of the lengths to which some will go to maintain power. It speaks volumes that such an acknowledgment comes from a judge with Brown’s background, suggesting the evidence against the Texas GOP’s actions was too compelling to ignore.

The Dissent: A Diversionary Tactic?

Judge Smith’s dissent, filled with personal attacks against Judge Brown and evocations of George Soros and Gavin Newsom, reads less like a legal argument and more like a political polemic. His focus on vilifying perceived political adversaries rather than addressing the core issues at hand—racial injustice and the undermining of democracy—reflects poorly on his judicial impartiality.

In Conclusion: The Battle for Democracy Continues

This ruling is but a skirmish in the larger war over the soul of American democracy. Gerrymandering, especially of the racial variety, is an affront to the democratic principles of equality and representation. As this issue ascends to the Supreme Court, the nation watches and waits. Will the court uphold the ideals of justice and equality, or will it side with those who seek to manipulate the system for their own gain? The answer to this question will resonate far beyond the borders of Texas, setting a precedent for the integrity of our democratic institutions.
