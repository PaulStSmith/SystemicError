'It infuriates us': Veterans rage at 'abusive' Trump over reports of him stiffing soldiers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Exploiting Loopholes: Trump's Contempt for National Guard TroopsThe Loophole LowdownVeteran OutrageThe Underlying Message

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump salutes troops aboard the USS Wasp (LHD 1) in Yokosuka, south of Tokyo, Japan May 28, 2019. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst/File Photo To match Special Report JAPAN-SOUTHKOREA/USA-NUCLEAR

Donald Trump has reportedly gone out of his way to avoid paying national guard troops sent in to Washington, D.C., resulting in outrage from veterans.

The reports started earlier in the summer that Trump was utilizing a loophole when deploying the national guard, and avoiding paying those individuals certain additional benefits. In June, Vote In Or Out reported that "Trump deployed National Guard troops on multiple 29-day orders—specifically choosing durations under 30 days to avoid paying full Basic Allowance for Housing Type 1 (BAH‑1)."

"Under Title 32 regulations, if orders run fewer than 30 days, members receive only the reduced 'BAH‑Type 2,' not full BAH‑1; full benefits begin only on day 31 and only apply from that point forward—not retroactively," the group wrote. "By repeatedly cycling short orders—ending them on day 29 and restarting on day 31—the administration saved roughly $2,500 per service member per month, based on differences between BAH‑Type 2 and BAH‑1."

Now, there are rumors circulating from veterans on Reddit and other social media that it's happening again.

Early Sunday morning, military analyst WarMonitor reported, "White House put DC National Guard on 29-day orders... 1 day short of 30 needed for full housing and health benefits."

Ex-GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger, himself a veteran, also raised the issue on Sunday morning, writing, "So Trump put the national guard on 29 day orders?!"

"Why does this matter: They don’t pay full housing allowance and no health insurance until 30 days," Kinzinger added. "What an abusive commander in chief. So Trump."

A self-identified veteran replied to Kinzinger, writing, "I’m an ex-Military guy too, and we all know this is the kind of BS thing that infuriates us."

"We swear an oath to the Constitution, not a man - and when a mere man abuses his power this way, we have every right to refuse an order we think is illegal," wrote the user, @EpicJourneyMan1. They added, "Before he pisses us off!"

Another self-identified veteran, known as @the15six, wrote Sunday, "29 day orders. What a giant piece of s--- for a CiC. It's always 'f--- the troops' with Trump."

Our Analysis:

Exploiting Loopholes: Trump's Contempt for National Guard Troops

In a flagrant display of disdain for the welfare of the United States National Guard, Donald Trump has reportedly manipulated deployment durations to skimp on compensating the very troops called upon to ensure the nation's safety. This calculated maneuver to deny National Guard members their due benefits is not just a fiscal sleight of hand; it's a slap in the face to every individual who has sworn to protect this country.

The Loophole Lowdown

According to Vote In Or Out, Trump’s administration has been deploying National Guard troops on 29-day orders—conspicuously one day shy of the 30 needed to qualify for full Basic Allowance for Housing Type 1 (BAH‑1) benefits. This isn’t mere penny-pinching; it's a systematic effort to undercut those who serve. Under Title 32 regulations, a deployment of fewer than 30 days relegates these service members to a reduced BAH‑Type 2, denying them substantial financial benefits. By cycling these short orders, the administration reportedly saved around $2,500 per service member per month. This isn’t fiscal responsibility; it's financial exploitation of servicemen and women.

Veteran Outrage

The backlash from the veteran community has been swift and scathing. Military analyst WarMonitor and former GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger, himself a veteran, have taken to social media to highlight this issue. Kinzinger’s characterization of Trump as an “abusive commander in chief” isn’t hyperbole—it’s a chilling diagnosis of a leader who sees troops not as human beings deserving of respect and adequate compensation but as disposable chess pieces.

Veterans voicing their frustration online underscore a universal truth that loyalty to the country should not be penalized by bureaucratic manipulation. The sentiment echoed by @EpicJourneyMan1, emphasizing allegiance to the Constitution over a “mere man,” and @the15six’s blunt denunciation of Trump’s actions, reflects a broader disillusionment with a commander in chief who seemingly prioritizes cost-saving over the well-being of his troops.

The Underlying Message

Trump's actions send a clear message: financial considerations trump troop welfare. It’s a disturbing precedent that not only undermines the morale of the National Guard but also erodes trust in the institution of the presidency itself. When a leader shows such blatant disregard for the individuals charged with protecting the nation, it begs the question: What are the true priorities of the Trump administration?

This maneuver is more than just a bureaucratic oversight; it's a reflection of a broader disdain for the sacrifices made by military personnel. It’s a tactic that doesn’t just save dollars—it costs respect, trust, and morale among the ranks of those who serve.

In conclusion, the exploitation of deployment duration loopholes to deny National Guard members full benefits is a stark illustration of Trump’s approach to leadership: one characterized by exploitation, disrespect, and a disturbing lack of empathy. The veterans' outrage is not only justified but necessary, calling attention to practices that should have no place in the administration of the armed forces.
