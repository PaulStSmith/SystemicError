'And everybody cried': Trump hijacks Oval Office meeting to claim 'highest mark' on cognitive test

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump on Monday repeated his claim that he took a cognitive exam as part of his physical exam, saying he received "the highest mark."

"And the doctors said, 'Sir, I've never seen anybody get that kind of — that was the highest mark,'" the president told reporters during an Oval Offic press conference with El Salvadorian President Nayib Bukele.

On Sunday, Trump spoke about his physical during a conversation with reporters on Air Force One.

"I think most of you have seen they released my physical... and I understand it was a very good report," the president said.

"The numbers were perfect, and I took a cognitive exam — and I would challenge anybody here to beat those marks because I have a perfect mark," he added.

But Trump seemed unable to mention specific details about the test, saying, “It’s a pretty well known test. Whatever it is. I got every one right.”

He added that he took the test because he “wanted to be a little different than [President Joe] Biden," who faced criticism during his presidency over his advanced age and alleged cognitive decline.

Trump said, “I felt I was in very good shape. Good heart. A good soul. Very good soul.”

Reacting to his remarks, social media users, including political commentators, said the president was "bragging" about taking a simple test.

Juliaa Davis, a columnist for The Daily Beast, wrote on the social platform X, "One of the doctors said, 'Sir, you’re a genius!' And everybody cried."

Author John Pavlovitz said, "Part of Trump's cognitive test should have included explaining what a tariff is."

The X account "Republicans against Trump," wrote, "A normal person doesn’t brag about passing a basic cognitive test where you have to identify an elephant and remember what day it is."

Last week, Trump took his first annual physical of his second presidential term at a hospital in Washington, D.C.

"President Trump remains in excellent health, exhibiting robust cardiac, pulmonary, neurological and general physical function," his doctor, Captain Sean Barbabella, said in a memo following the exam.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Cognitive Claims: A Masterclass in Misdirection

In a recent theatrical display of self-congratulation, President Donald Trump has once again stirred the pot of public discourse, this time by boasting about his results on a cognitive exam. During an Oval Office press conference with El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele, Trump claimed to have received "the highest mark" on the test, a statement that, while on the surface appears to be a testament to his mental acuity, crumbles under the slightest scrutiny.

The Art of Vagueness

Trump's claim, "And the doctors said, 'Sir, I've never seen anybody get that kind of — that was the highest mark,'" immediately raises eyebrows, not for its content, but for its characteristic vagueness. It's a pattern we've seen before: Trump makes an unsubstantiated claim, peppers it with anecdotal evidence, and wraps it in the impenetrable veneer of his personal charisma. This time, however, the claim is so audacious that it borders on the absurd. The president bragging about his cognitive abilities is akin to an adult boasting about passing a kindergarten literacy test. It's expected, not commendable.

A Challenge to Logic

Trump's challenge to "anybody here to beat those marks" because he got a "perfect mark" is not only a bizarre flex but also highlights a misunderstanding (or perhaps a misrepresentation) of what cognitive tests are designed to measure. Cognitive assessments are not contests with winners and losers; they are tools used by medical professionals to detect impairments. That Trump views his results as a high score to be beaten is both a fundamental misunderstanding of the test's purpose and an insight into his competitive view of, well, everything.

Comparison with Biden: A Diversion

Trump's assertion that he took the test to be "a little different than [President Joe] Biden" is a transparent attempt to divert attention from his own controversies and to capitalize on the age-old tactic of comparison. By framing his cognitive health in contrast to Biden's, Trump isn't just deflecting; he's attempting to create a narrative that places him on a higher pedestal. It's a classic Trump move: when in doubt, compare and conquer.

Social Media's Verdict

The reaction from social media users and political commentators was swift and scathing. Juliaa Davis's sarcastic remark about a doctor calling Trump a "genius" and John Pavlovitz's pointed comment on Trump's understanding of tariffs are not just quips; they are symptomatic of a broader disbelief and exhaustion with Trump's antics. The X account "Republicans against Trump" hits the nail on the head by pointing out the absurdity of bragging about passing a basic cognitive test. Their comments reflect a growing consensus that Trump's boasts are not indicative of exceptional cognitive abilities but rather an inability to engage with the seriousness and dignity expected of a president.

The Doctor's Note

Lastly, the memo from Trump's doctor, Captain Sean Barbabella, declaring Trump in "excellent health" with "robust cardiac, pulmonary, neurological and general physical function," reads less like a medical assessment and more like a press release. It's eerily reminiscent of past declarations about Trump's health, which have been criticized for their lack of detail and hyperbolic language.

Conclusion

In essence, Trump's latest boast about his cognitive exam scores is a masterclass in misdirection and self-aggrandizement. It serves as a reminder of his penchant for turning even the most routine aspects of presidential duties into a spectacle. As always, the devil is in the details—or in Trump's case, the lack thereof. While he may claim to have received "the highest mark," the reality is that cognitive tests are not competitions. They are, however, a measure of a person's ability to understand and interact with the world around them. On that score, one might argue that Trump's results are still pending.
