FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino may be looking for a job in the very near future because Donald Trump has grown weary of his act.

That is the opinion of Republican strategist Susan Del Percio on MSNBC on Sunday, where she claimed the president may rid himself of the former conservative podcaster who has created waves at the Department of Justice over differences of opinion on what is exactly in the Jeffrey Epstein files.

According to reports on Friday, Bongino never showed up for work on Friday after storming out on Attorney General Pam Bondi and reportedly giving Trump an ultimatum that either Bondi goes or he will quit.

Speaking with MSNBC's Alex Witt, Del Percio claimed Bondi will end up on top in the squabble that is roiling the DOJ.

Calling Trump's inner circle, "The gang that still can't shoot straight," she elaborated, "There may always be a group that are going to challenge 'what happened with Jeffrey Epstein? Is there a client list? Let me see the video.' But the administration itself gets its signals all crossed."

"That being said, don't put it past Donald Trump to fire Dan Bongino immediately because he has had enough," she predicted. "He does not like what he's seeing in Dan Bongino. But the bigger question is, and I think this is what everyone's kind of keeping an eye out is, how will Donald Trump control the narrative, and will that narrative appease the MAGA base? Does he still have control? Because if he doesn't, that will have him all outraged and doing who knows what."

Our Analysis:

The Fictional Fallout: Trump, Bongino, and the Epstein Controversy

In a stunning example of the chaos that often seems to swirl around the Trump administration, we're treated to a tale of intrigue, conflict, and potential job losses at the highest levels of the FBI. Or at least, that's what one would think if they stumbled upon the narrative involving FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino, a figure who, in reality, does not and never has held this position. The confusion doesn't stop there; the article weaves a complex story of internal conflict, ultimatums, and the shadow of Jeffrey Epstein looming large over the Department of Justice.

The Heart of the Matter

At the center of this whirlwind is the claim that Donald Trump is growing tired of Bongino's "act," suggesting a rift that could lead to Bongino's ousting. Republican strategist Susan Del Percio is quoted, spinning a tale of intrigue and internal squabble that reads more like a political thriller than a piece of factual reporting. The narrative suggests a power struggle between Bongino and Attorney General Pam Bondi, another figure whose role in this saga is as fictional as Bongino's position.

The Epstein Files: A Convenient Distraction

The article fixates on the Jeffrey Epstein files, a topic ripe with conspiracy theories and genuine concerns alike. It's suggested that Bongino's clash with Bondi and Trump stems from differing opinions on these files, adding a layer of mystery and scandal to the story. Yet, the focus on this aspect feels like a deliberate attempt to divert attention from the administration's real issues, using the allure of unanswered questions about Epstein to engage and enrage the reader.

Trump's Alleged Dilemma

Susan Del Percio's commentary, as quoted, paints a picture of an administration at war with itself, unable to "shoot straight." The depiction of Trump as someone ready to fire Bongino "immediately" for disliking his work ethic or for challenging Bondi's stance adds to the chaotic image of the Trump administration. Yet, this portrayal, while entertaining, lacks the grounding in reality necessary to be taken seriously. It's a speculative commentary that fuels the fires of division and speculation without offering substantive evidence or insight.

The Real Issue: Fact vs. Fiction

The crux of the problem with this article lies in its blending of reality and fiction. Dan Bongino, known more for his conservative commentary and podcasting, is not part of the FBI hierarchy. This glaring error undermines any factual basis the story might have had, casting doubt on the entire narrative. It's a stark reminder of the importance of verifying facts before diving into the sensationalist speculation that often dominates political discourse.

Conclusion: A Web of Untruths

In essence, this article serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of misinformation and the ease with which fiction can be intertwined with fact to serve a particular narrative. The real story isn't about Trump, Bongino, or Bondi; it's about the responsibility of those who report and comment on the news to ensure their contributions to the public discourse are grounded in truth. As readers, we're left to sift through the debris of this fictional fallout, reminded once again of the systemic errors that plague our information ecosystem.
