Behold, a Trumper so vile the only surprise is she didn't shoot this dog herself

Chop was a Rottweiler. He lived with his family in a quiet neighborhood in El Paso, Texas.

On September 9, Border Patrol agents showed up at their home to see if migrants were there. When the family’s son answered the door, he permitted the agents to search his home, saying he had nothing to hide.

But he asked if they could wait first while he put the family dog, Chop, a Rottweiler, away in the bathroom before they walked in, as the dog could be aggressive. He did so. But when he went out to his pickup to retrieve the ID agents had requested, the same agents opened the bathroom door and shot Chop.

What’s more, none of the Border Patrol agents helped the family, who desperately tried to render aid to Chop as he bled to death on the kitchen floor. And never mind the detail that, it turns out, Border Patrol terrorized legal citizens and murdered a family member while following a false lead.

Border patrol issued the following statement:

“On Sept. 9 at 7:15 a.m., a U.S. Border Patrol agent was involved in a use of force incident in El Paso, Texas during an investigation into alien smuggling at a residence. The incident involved a canine. The use of force is currently under review by CBP’s Office of Professional Responsibility in accordance with CBP policies. CBP takes such incidents seriously.”

Well, of course, they take it seriously. When you work for a soulless dog murderer like Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, killing a canine is a badge of honor — and likely grounds for a juicy promotion.

It is a sign of our times that this particular murder didn’t become widely known until this week, when it went viral thanks to the account “We Rate Dogs” — and then others — posting it on Instagram. The initial news story reported by KFOX14 in El Paso had gone unnoticed in the media until then.

I learned about this at the Drudge Report under the blaring headline “ICE SLAUGHTERS FAMILY DOG.” Technically, that’s not precise — ICE and the Border Patrol are separate agencies working under Homeland Security for the same purpose under Donald Trump, which is to terrorize Brown people for sport and political gain.

So I offer no apology for using the headline shorthand of “ICE” — they’re all the same to me. If America can survive the Trump presidency, ICE in its current form should be dismantled and its legitimate functions restructured. After some of its perpetrators face justice.

We are living, in real time, through one of the darkest periods in the nation’s history. Look at what has happened this week alone:

  • In Everett, Massachusetts, ICE took a 13-year-old from police custody after a school arrest, moved him to Virginia, and never told his waiting mother. A “disappearance” — proudly modeled in the image of President Vladimir Putin.

  • In Washington, D.C., where the National Guard already patrols under Trump’s “crime emergency” declaration, ICE sweeps have forced businesses to close. City officials say they got no warning.

  • In Chicago, a community-run Facebook page used to track ICE activity was taken down by Meta at the request of the Justice Department. Nothing says North Korea better than a little state censorship of people trying to avoid being swept up by government forces.

  • In Los Angeles, ICE raids became so chaotic that the county declared an emergency. Shelters were overrun. Families vanished. And no one in the federal government gave a damn.

ICE and Border Patrol are no longer legitimate law enforcement agencies. They represent a paramilitary force with zero transparency and all the swagger of a dictatorship’s interior ministry.

And the moral fiber of Kristi Noem.

As for Chop? He wasn’t even an undocumented dog.

Our Analysis:

The Tragedy of Chop: A Microcosm of a Larger Issue

In a heart-wrenching incident that encapsulates the brutality and recklessness of the current state of border enforcement in the United States, a Rottweiler named Chop became the latest victim. The episode not only exposes the cruel indifference of the Border Patrol agents involved but also serves as a damning indictment of the broader systemic issues plaguing agencies under the Department of Homeland Security (DHS). Let’s dissect the layers of this tragedy, the response it elicited, and what it signifies about the era we’re navigating.

The Incident: A Stark Display of Unnecessary Force

On a seemingly ordinary day in El Paso, Texas, the facade of normalcy was shattered when Border Patrol agents, operating under the guise of investigating migrant smuggling, invaded the privacy and sanctity of a family’s home. Despite the compliance and precautionary measures taken by the family, their beloved Chop was senselessly killed. The agents’ decision to open the bathroom door, despite being informed of the potential aggression of the dog, speaks volumes about their disregard for the well-being of those they interact with - animal or human.

The Aftermath: A Cold Response

The cold, bureaucratic response from Border Patrol, promising an investigation that remains shrouded in the opaque processes of the CBP’s Office of Professional Responsibility, does little to assuage concerns. It’s a familiar script, one that too often ends with minimal accountability or meaningful change. The statement from Border Patrol reeks of detachment and fails to acknowledge the severity of the agents’ actions, reducing a family’s trauma to a mere procedural footnote.

The Bigger Picture: Emblematic of Systemic Brutality

This incident, while tragic, is not an anomaly but a symptom of a much larger, more insidious problem. The agencies under DHS, particularly ICE and the Border Patrol, have morphed into entities that operate with a disturbing level of autonomy, impunity, and, as illustrated by this incident, cruelty. The author’s linkage of these events to the broader political context, specifically under the Trump administration’s policies, underscores the systemic nature of this brutality. Comparing the secretive and aggressive tactics of ICE to those of authoritarian regimes isn’t just hyperbolic; it’s an alarmingly apt comparison.

The Role of Media and Public Awareness

It’s telling that the mainstream media initially overlooked the killing of Chop, only for the story to gain traction through social media and alternative news outlets. This delay in coverage reflects a troubling desensitization to the ongoing abuses perpetrated by border enforcement agencies. The role of platforms like “We Rate Dogs” in amplifying this story underscores the power and necessity of alternative media in challenging and exposing systemic injustices.

Concluding Thoughts: A Call for Accountability and Reform

The outrage over Chop’s death, while warranted, should also serve as a catalyst for deeper introspection and action. We must demand accountability not just for the individual agents involved but for the leaders and policymakers who enable such a culture of impunity. The call to dismantle ICE and restructure its legitimate functions isn’t just about one agency or one administration; it’s about fundamentally rethinking how we approach border security, immigration, and law enforcement in a manner that upholds dignity, humanity, and justice.

In the end, Chop’s story is a tragic reminder of the costs of a militarized and dehumanized approach to border control. It’s a call to action for all of us to advocate for a system that protects rather than destroys, unites rather than divides. For Chop, and for the countless others who have suffered under the weight of systemic error, the time for change is now.
