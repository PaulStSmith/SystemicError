'What the hell is he talking about?' Charlie Kirk buried on CNN over 'silly' manhood rant

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 3, 20255 min read

Charlie Kirk, Anny Phillip (CNN screenshot)

A rant by Turning Point USA head Charlie Kirk on who is manly and who is not led to an explosion of laughter on CNN's "Table for Five " on Saturday morning.

As host Abby Philip pointed out before sharing the clip, "According to one MAGA star, it's a man's world and anyone who's not MAGA is just living in it."

In the clip, the frequently derided Donald Trump influencer exclaimed, "I do think that there is a direct correlation between someone's testosterone and their politics. I would love to have a study done on this on young men –– the lower their testosterone, the more likely they are to be democratic."

"Democrats are not going to be able to win over high testosterone men because they're too self-directed so differently," he suggested. "The lower your testosterone is, of course, the more likely you are going to be subservient and compliant, okay? Our republic needs alpha men."

In the middle of all the panel laughter, CNN's Harry Enten blurted "What the hell is he talking about? Oh my god! Does anyone here know?"

RELATED: Watch: Charlie Kirk's wife grimaces as he lectures women on 'finding a husband'

A laughing Phillip then said she would defer to the men at the table because she didn't want to touch it before joking, "Are you guys taking testosterone tests and then, like, registering to vote?"

Podcaster Touré offered, "I'm sure that he would love to see a correlation between testosterone and masculinity. There actually is social science on this, right? There are people who study this stuff; empathy is the core thing that divides the left from the right many times, so many of the right's policies are about this is good for me and so much of what the left is about is this is good for everybody in our view."

"So that really the point, not like 'I have more testosterone,' that's silly," he scoffed.

Former Donald Trump White House communications director Alyssa Farah Griffin smiled and observed, "I've also just never heard a man I consider masculine spend time thinking about another man's testosterone levels. I think that's where you lost me."

Another former Trump official, attorney Jim Schultz, got off the unkindest cut by telling the panel, "I'm not so sure that Charlie Kirk hits the weights all that much either," to renewed laughter.

Our Analysis:

The Masculinity Mirage: A Critical Analysis of Charlie Kirk's Testosterone Tirade

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as equal parts absurd and troubling, Charlie Kirk, the head of Turning Point USA, took to the airwaves with a theory that reeks of desperation and pseudo-science. His claim? That there's a direct correlation between a man's testosterone levels and his political affiliations, specifically suggesting that lower testosterone levels are indicative of Democratic leanings, while presumably, higher levels are a badge of honor among Republicans.

The Misguided Metrics of Manhood

Kirk's comments, aired on CNN's "Table for Five," could easily be dismissed as the rantings of a man painfully out of touch with both science and the complexities of human behavior. Yet, they offer a troubling glimpse into a mindset that equates political ideology with physical and, by extension, moral superiority. "Our republic needs alpha men," Kirk declares, invoking the kind of language that wouldn't be out of place in a bad 1980s action movie or a misguided attempt at reviving ancient Sparta.

The panel's reaction—ranging from disbelief to mockery—highlights the absurdity of Kirk's stance. CNN's Harry Enten's incredulous "What the hell is he talking about?" echoes the sentiment of any rational viewer confronted with such a bizarre claim. And yet, the laughter masks a darker implication of Kirk's rant: the weaponization of masculinity as a political tool.

The Fallacy of Biopolitics

Kirk's assertion falls squarely into the realm of biopolitics, where biological characteristics become grounds for political and social discrimination. The idea that testosterone levels could—or should—dictate one's political beliefs is not only scientifically unfounded but dangerously close to the kind of eugenic thinking that has been used to justify some of history's greatest atrocities.

Moreover, Kirk's focus on testosterone as the arbiter of true masculinity is laughably reductive. Real masculinity, if one must define it, is about character, integrity, and how one interacts with the world and the people in it—not about hormone levels or physical prowess. Former Trump White House communications director Alyssa Farah Griffin hits the nail on the head, noting, "I've also just never heard a man I consider masculine spend time thinking about another man's testosterone levels."

The Real Divide: Empathy, Not Testosterone

Podcaster Touré provides a much-needed dose of reality, pointing out that the true difference between the political left and right often boils down to empathy. The left's policies, he argues, are generally aimed at what's good for the collective, while the right's focus more on individual gain. This observation cuts to the heart of the matter, revealing Kirk's testosterone theory for what it truly is: a smokescreen designed to distract from the substantive issues that divide us.

Conclusion: Beyond the Binary

Charlie Kirk's attempt to boil down political affiliation to a matter of testosterone levels is not just scientifically baseless; it's a dangerous game of identity politics that seeks to divide us further. It's a distraction from the real issues—climate change, inequality, justice—that should be commanding our attention.

The mockery on CNN's panel might be humorous, but the underlying issue is not. We must resist the temptation to categorize and demonize based on arbitrary and flawed metrics like Kirk proposes. Instead, let's focus on what truly makes a society great: empathy, understanding, and a collective effort to improve the lives of all its members, regardless of how "manly" a political pundit thinks they are.
