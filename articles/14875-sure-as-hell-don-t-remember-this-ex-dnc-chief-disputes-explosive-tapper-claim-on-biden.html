'Sure as hell don't remember this': Ex-DNC chief disputes explosive Tapper claim on Biden

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 16, 20255 min read

CHICAGO, Ill. – August 19, 2024: Democratic National Committee Chair Jaime Harrison and Democratic National Convention Committee Chair Minyon Moore address the 2024 Democratic National Convention. (Photo credit: Ben Von Klemperer / Shutterstock)

The former chair of the Democratic National Committee is disputing a claim laid out in a new book on former President Joe Biden's cognitive decline and cover-up by CNN's Jake Tapper and Axios' Alex Thompson.

Much of Biden's re-election campaign was dominated by speculation about his fitness to run again, culminating in a disastrous presidential debate in which he struggled to speak clearly. He ultimately exited the race in favor of his running mate, then-Vice President Kamala Harris.

The book, "Original Sin: President Biden’s Decline, Its Cover-up, and His Disastrous Choice to Run Again," includes several claims that the former president's struggles were more severe behind the scene than the public knew, including that he repeatedly failed to recognize George Clooney at a key fundraiser, that his aides wondered whether they'd have to put him in a wheelchair if he won a second term, and that even many of his Cabinet secretaries were excluded from access to the president as his condition worsened.

But Harrison took issue with another claim from the book, which, according to the New York Post, stated that “Democratic National Committee chairman Jaime Harrison later that fall met Biden at a Congressional Black Caucus event and the president kept shaking his hand without appearing to recognize him.”

"Better check my cognitive abilities as well because I sure as hell don’t remember this," wrote Harrison on X.

Tapper and Thompson's book has come under fire from both sides of the aisle. On one hand, more liberal-leaning pundits and analysts trashed the book as overwrought and exaggerated, particularly in light of the visible mental deficiencies of sitting President Donald Trump. On the other hand, Trump supporters have asserted with little evidence that Tapper and Thompson were sitting on this information during the campaign itself to shield Biden from scrutiny.

As the two reporters feel the political pressure over their book, they have reportedly hired a crisis PR expert to protect their reputations.

Our Analysis:

The Biden Controversy: A Closer Look at the Allegations and Defense

The political sphere is ablaze with controversy following the release of Original Sin: President Biden’s Decline, Its Cover-up, and His Disastrous Choice to Run Again, penned by CNN's Jake Tapper and Axios' Alex Thompson. This book thrusts former President Joe Biden's cognitive capabilities—or the alleged lack thereof—into the harsh spotlight, raising eyebrows and tempers on both sides of the ideological divide. But let's slice through the sensationalism and examine the meat of these claims, the responses they've elicited, and the implications they carry.

The Claims: More Than Just Gossip?

The authors present a narrative that goes beyond mere speculation about Biden's fitness for office, depicting a scenario where his cognitive struggles were a significant concern internally, far more severe than what was portrayed to the public. The anecdote involving George Clooney and the grim musings about a potential second-term wheelchair are particularly evocative, painting a picture of a campaign and presidency in crisis mode behind the scenes. However, one must question: Are these accounts a faithful representation of reality, or are they dramatized for effect?

The Defense: A Question of Memory or Misrepresentation?

Enter Jaime Harrison, the former chair of the Democratic National Committee, who challenges a specific anecdote from the book. Harrison's cheeky retort on X (formerly Twitter) to not remembering the event as described raises an important point about the reliability of the narratives being peddled. Is Harrison's memory the one at fault, or could it be that the book leans more towards sensationalism than factual accuracy? This instance underscores the necessity of scrutinizing the sources and motivations behind such explosive claims.

The Political Fallout: A Double-Edged Sword

The reactions to Tapper and Thompson's work are as polarized as one might expect in today's political climate. Critics from the liberal camp decry the book as exaggerated, pointing to what they see as a double standard, especially in light of the "visible mental deficiencies" of then-President Donald Trump, which they argue received comparatively less attention. Meanwhile, Trump supporters seize the narrative as evidence of a cover-up, accusing the authors of withholding crucial information until after the electoral fray. This dichotomy of reception highlights a broader issue: the weaponization of mental health and fitness for political ends, a dangerous game that risks undermining public trust in political reporting and discourse.

The Crisis PR: A Telling Move

Perhaps most telling in this saga is the authors' decision to hire a crisis PR expert. This move, while practical from a damage control perspective, inadvertently lends credence to the criticisms levied against them. It suggests a level of concern about the book's reception and its impact on their reputations that might not exist were they confident in the unassailable truth of their reporting. This development is a critical piece of the puzzle, suggesting that the fallout from the book's allegations is more complex and far-reaching than mere partisan bickering.

Conclusion: The Search for Truth in a Sea of Sensationalism

In dissecting the uproar surrounding Original Sin, it becomes evident that the truth is not only elusive but also multifaceted. The allegations against Biden, and the subsequent defenses, reflect more than just concerns over one man's cognitive capacity; they reveal a deeper malaise within American political culture—a propensity for sensationalism, a polarization that skews perception, and a troubling trend of politicizing personal health issues. As observers and participants in this ongoing drama, it is imperative to approach such claims with a critical eye, recognizing the thin line between legitimate scrutiny and sensational exploitation. The quest for truth must not be overshadowed by the spectacle of controversy.
