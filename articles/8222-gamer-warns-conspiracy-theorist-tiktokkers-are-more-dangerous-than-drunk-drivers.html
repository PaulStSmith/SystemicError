Gamer warns conspiracy-theorist TikTokkers are more dangerous than 'drunk drivers'

Slate reports videogames stuff a lot of entertainment into one sandbox, and some of them augment their storylines with every kind of conspiracy imaginable.

The two-decade-old Assassin’s Creed games are peppered with “almost every kind of conspiracy theory you can think of,” reports Joshua Rivera. “In the fiction of Assassin’s Creed, humanity is descended from ancient aliens, and this knowledge is suppressed throughout history; the tide of world events is influenced by a shadow war between two secret societies; the media exists to manipulate the public. This makes for an exciting series of video games with a near-limitless scope. It also echoes uncomfortable real-world conspiracy theories that have proven consequential in our lifetime.”

But how seriously do players take conspiracies that try to rope aliens in with an over-controlling order of the Knights Templar and a “bloodthirsty” Pope Alexander VI — a pope more known for nepotism, unexciting papal decrees and for his reputation as a patron of the arts? Media critic and scholar Cameron Kunzelman tells Slate no more than any other media source.

“Conspiracy exists as a crime that you can be charged with, because conspiracies do exist!” said Kunzelman. “There are groups of people who make decisions together that can impact other people, and they can do that secretly. Seemingly lots of things that are involved in what’s being released right now in the Epstein files are what we would call dyed-in-the-wool, true conspiracy. From human trafficking to much more banal, but just as bad, practices of money moving around and political meetings.”

But Kunzelman said today a wide variety of conspiracies are getting “mashed together” into big ugly “metaconspiracies.”

“Where conspiratorial movements at one time were seen as discrete from one another, now they kind of attach to each other and they get folded into QAnon,” said Kunzelman, whose new book “Everything is Permitted” claims that this massive folding of conspiracies mirrors how entertainment franchises are now built.

But, like zombies and other things that should not exist, they are perpetual motion machines that sometimes keep churning away long after their creators are dead and gone, using the engine of capital “to keep themselves revolving and moving,” said Kunzelman.

But Kunzelman’s book points out that algorithms on things like YouTube and other sites shunt viewers into “harder stuff,” that even our basic entertainment patterns make us minor conspiracy theorists.

“Every part of our lives where we engage with the internet is about putting us in a ditch that leads to another ditch that leads to another ditch,” Kunselman told Slate. “And unfortunately, the scale of that, the allure of that, often leads into things that will harm us in some way. It’ll remove us from our actual communities. It will put us into kind of epistemic places that are only engaged with their own ideas. … whatever happens to you is whatever happens to you.”

Oddly, Kunzelman is one video game enthusiast who thinks platforms that shunt audiences down a rabbit hole need more policing, arguing that we don’t even build open roads without some guardrails.

“I think being a very influential conspiracy-theorist TikTokker is probably on the whole more dangerous than being a drunk driver for an afternoon,” said Kunzelman. “I think it’s harming more people in serious and real ways. But we don’t take it seriously at all. It’s a bipartisan belief that these industries should not be constrained by the law, and by any concern for other human beings. I think that’s bad.”

Our Analysis:

The Blurred Lines Between Fiction and Reality in Video Game Narratives

The recent Slate report delves into the intricate web of conspiracy theories interwoven into the fabric of popular video games, particularly spotlighting the Assassin’s Creed series. This analysis serves not just as a critique of the gaming industry but as a stark reflection of our society’s fascination with and susceptibility to conspiratorial thinking. Let’s dissect the key arguments presented and the broader implications they hold.

The Alluring Fiction of Conspiracies in Assassin’s Creed

Assassin’s Creed is highlighted for its rich tapestry of conspiracies, ranging from ancient alien ancestry to shadow wars controlling global events. These elements, while making for an enthralling gaming experience, echo real-world conspiracy theories that have had tangible, often harmful, impacts. This parallel is not to be taken lightly. The game’s narrative, while fictional, plays into a larger cultural and psychological predilection towards believing in vast, unseen forces shaping our world. The question of how seriously players take these narratives is moot; the underlying issue is the ease with which these games blur the lines between entertainment and endorsement of conspiratorial thinking.

The Real-World Consequences of Fictional Conspiracies

Cameron Kunzelman’s insights provide a sobering perspective on the matter. Conspiracies, as he rightly points out, are not mere figments of the imagination but have roots in reality, affecting geopolitical and social landscapes. However, the amalgamation of various conspiracy theories into overarching “metaconspiracies” reflects a dangerous trend of disinformation, where disparate, unfounded beliefs are interconnected, amplifying their reach and impact. Kunzelman’s comparison of entertainment franchises to perpetual motion machines, endlessly propagating these narratives, underscores the self-sustaining nature of such disinformation.

The Role of Algorithms in Amplifying Conspiracies

The discussion on algorithms further illuminates the issue. Platforms like YouTube not only facilitate but actively encourage the descent into increasingly radical ideas. This “ditch leading to another ditch” phenomenon is particularly insidious because it exploits human psychology and the fundamental way we interact with the internet, nudging us towards extremism under the guise of entertainment or self-education.

The Need for Responsible Content Curation

Kunzelman’s call for more policing on platforms is an urgent one. The analogy between influential conspiracy-theorist influencers and drunk drivers is stark but apt. The harm perpetrated by spreading unfounded conspiracies can be widespread and severe, impacting individuals’ mental health, societal trust, and even public safety. The laissez-faire attitude of both the industry and policymakers towards this issue reflects a dangerous negligence.

In Conclusion: The Dangerous Game of Conspiracies

The Slate article, through its focus on Assassin’s Creed and the commentary provided by Kunzelman, offers a critical lens on the interplay between entertainment, technology, and societal health. It is a call to action for creators, platforms, and regulators alike to recognize the power of narratives and the responsibility that comes with it. While video games and other forms of media undoubtedly have the right to explore fantastical themes and speculative fiction, there is a fine line between entertainment and the propagation of harmful ideologies. As consumers, creators, and citizens, it is imperative to navigate this landscape with caution, critical thinking, and a commitment to truth.
