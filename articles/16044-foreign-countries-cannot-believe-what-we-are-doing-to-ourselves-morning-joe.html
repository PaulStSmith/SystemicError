Foreign countries 'cannot believe what we are doing to ourselves': Morning Joe

By Paulo Santos

MSNBC

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough blasted president Donald Trump for eroding American advantages as he pushes through unpopular and legally questionable policies.

China has been working to build new trade relationships with nations that Trump has slapped with massive tariffs, as foreign universities are recruiting U.S. researchers who have lost jobs thanks to Elon Musk's funding cuts, and the "Morning Joe" host weighed in on the issue from a trip to the United Kingdom.

"If I could if I could sum up everything I learned yesterday, three months after I was here, and I've talked about it on the show a good bit," Scarborough said. "But three months, after all I heard in Britain and Europe was about America's supremacy, economic supremacy, and how they wanted to be more like us, and that was three months ago, four months ago in December. Things have changed so dramatically, and just as a journalist talking about China's new diplomatic and economic opportunities, what I'm hearing here is new economic opportunities for Britain and the rest of Europe, economic, diplomatic opportunities."

The Trump administration has tried to take control of Harvard, ordering the university to eliminate its DEI programs and screen international students for ideological concerns, but the president ordered $2.3 billion in federal funding to the school after it rejected those demands as other cuts threaten research elsewhere.

"Institutions here, whether you're talking about Cambridge, Oxford, all across Europe, they can't believe their luck," Scarborough said. "They can't believe that America is dumb enough to cut funding to [the National Institutes of Health], America is dumb enough to cut funding [research and development] funding. They're dumb enough to chase off the best and the brightest students, and they're getting they're getting those students coming here and, actually, so Britain and a Europe that three or four months ago said we're never going to catch up with America, just said, my God, they're actually slowing down and going to let us catch up."

"That Harvard story, they think they're owning Harvard, they think they're owning Columbia, they think they're owning these schools," Scarborough added, "and I mean, they've said, all right, we're going to destroy, we want to destroy one of these big schools. All they're doing by cutting those grants is they're helping China, they're helping the EU, they're helping Britain. They're helping economic competitors and allies by basically saying, yeah, you know what? We're not interested in this anymore, we're we're going after an ideological agenda, and you can have the best and brightest minds in tech, you can have the best and brightest minds in AI, you can have the best and brightest minds and bio research. It's really, again, they cannot believe what we are doing to ourselves."

Sparky's Analysis:

Trump's Policy Fallout: A Dissection

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough, in a recent tirade from the United Kingdom, has laid bare the consequences of President Donald Trump's policies with a focus so sharp, it could cut glass. Scarborough's critique, grounded in observations from his travels and discussions in Britain and Europe, paints a picture of a United States rapidly hemorrhaging its global supremacy in economic and academic spheres, thanks to a concoction of unpopular, legally dubious policies, and outright funding cuts. Let's dissect this debacle with the precision it demands.

Eroding American Advantages

Scarborough's narrative begins with a stark contrast: merely months ago, Europe looked up to America's economic supremacy as a model worth emulating. Fast forward to the present, and the scenario has flipped on its head. Trump's massive tariffs have not only alienated the United States on the trade front but have also paved the way for China to swoop in and build new trade relationships with those the U.S. has spurned. The irony? It's as rich as the tariffs are hefty.

The Academic Exodus

Enter Elon Musk and his funding cuts, a subplot in this drama that sees U.S. researchers losing their jobs and foreign universities, particularly those in Britain and Europe, reaping the benefits. Scarborough's observations here are particularly damning. The U.S., once the beacon of intellectual pursuit and innovation, is now perceived as "dumb enough" to cut funding to pivotal institutions like the National Institutes of Health, and research and development sectors. The result? A brain drain that sees the brightest minds in tech, AI, and bio research being welcomed with open arms by countries that were once considered economic competitors.

Harvard and the Ideological Agenda

The Trump administration's attempts to control Harvard University, demanding the elimination of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) programs and the screening of international students for "ideological concerns," is a subplot that reads like a page out of a dystopian novel. The administration's subsequent order to channel $2.3 billion in federal funding to Harvard after it rejected these demands is a twist that might have been comical if it weren't so tragically counterproductive. According to Scarborough, this move is akin to the U.S. shooting itself in the foot, aiding not only China but also the EU and Britain in the global economic race.

Conclusion: Ideology Over Intellect

Scarborough's analysis, scathing as it is, sheds light on a troubling trend under the Trump administration: the prioritization of an ideological agenda over intellectual and economic advancement. The consequences of these policies are not just internal but have put the U.S. on a backfoot globally. As foreign nations capitalize on the self-inflicted wounds of the United States, one cannot help but wonder about the long-term repercussions of these decisions.

In essence, Scarborough's message from across the pond is a wake-up call. It's a critique that doesn't just skim the surface but dives deep into the heart of the matter, exposing the systemic errors in a policy approach that seems hell-bent on eroding the very foundations of America's global standing. As we watch this unfold, one thing becomes clear: the path to reclaiming America's lost prestige is not through isolation and ideological rigidity, but through openness, innovation, and a steadfast commitment to intellectual advancement.
