'I am the pick of the president!' Alina Habba fumes after her 'disturbing' dismissal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Legal Labyrinth: A Commentary on Habba's Judicial MisadventuresJudicial Juggling and Legal LoopholesA Deluge of DefianceIncompetence on DisplayConclusion: A Systemic Failure

In an article labeled “Trump’s dumbest lawyer throws a fit after judge gives her the boot,” New Republic writer Edith Olmsted says New Jersey AG appointee Alina Habba is not taking her second dismissal well.

Olmsted reports that New Jersey federal judges ousted Habba earlier this year, refusing to vote to extend her 120-day appointment as U.S. attorney for New Jersey. But the Trump administration found a loophole to bypass the Senate and judges by ordering U.S. Attorney Pam Bondi to fire the first assistant U.S. attorney approved to replace Habba. Trump then appointed Habba to the first assistant position.

Earlier this week, however, another federal judge ruled that Habba had been illegally serving as U.S. attorney for New Jersey since the beginning of July 1, and he blocked her office from prosecuting two criminal cases in which defendants challenged her appointment.

Habba, who was Trump’s personal attorney in a defamation case involving writer E. Jean Carroll — which he lost — claimed the judges opposing her are set against her because they are “Obama and Biden appointed.”

“I am the pick of the president. I am the pick of Pam Bondi, our attorney general, and I will serve this country like I have for the last several years in any capacity,” Habba said. “… It’s disturbing what we’re seeing. It’s not surprising, but it’s disturbing. They think they have a voice for five minutes, they try to be activists. And Pam Bondi called it like it is: The attorney general said it today: We will not fall to rogue judges.”

“We will not fall to people trying to be political when they should just be doing their job: respecting the president,” Habba added.

Olmsted pointed out, however, that “a judge’s job is to uphold the law, not bend to the president’s every whim. “

E. Jean Carroll claims she watched Habba and Trump interact in court during her defamation case and said Habba “didn’t know the first thing about law.”

“Not the first thing. Couldn’t do it,” Carroll told columnist Jen Rubin, “but she did what Donald Trump told her to do. I could hear almost everything he was saying to her. He belittled her. He grumbled. He actually hissed, spit, moaned and groaned to her. ‘Stand up, stand up, stand up!’ he kept telling her. She didn’t know why she was standing up, but she would stand up and defend the man as brilliantly as she could.”

Trump's Legal Labyrinth: A Commentary on Habba's Judicial Misadventures

In an article that reads like a script from a political satire, the New Republic's Edith Olmsted delivers a scathing critique of Alina Habba's tumultuous tenure as U.S. Attorney for New Jersey under the Trump administration. Habba, a figure who has been characterized as "Trump’s dumbest lawyer," finds herself at the center of a legal and political debacle that underscores the chaotic nature of Trump's modus operandi and his administration's flagrant disregard for legal norms and procedures.

Judicial Juggling and Legal Loopholes

Olmsted's account of Habba's judicial journey is nothing short of a rollercoaster. Initially ousted by New Jersey federal judges, Habba's reappointment through a loophole exploited by the Trump administration is a glaring testament to the lengths to which Trump would go to sidestep established legal and political processes. The maneuver to bypass the Senate and judges by ordering U.S. Attorney Pam Bondi to terminate Habba's successor only to reinstate her is a Machiavellian play that would make even the most seasoned political operatives blush.

However, this legal charade came to a screeching halt when a federal judge ruled that Habba had been illegally serving as U.S. Attorney since July 1, effectively barring her office from prosecuting two criminal cases. This decision not only highlights the precariousness of Habba's position but also serves as a stark reminder of the importance of adhering to legal protocols and the rule of law.

A Deluge of Defiance

Habba's response to her dismissal is as defiant as it is delusional. Her claim that opposition from judges, allegedly appointed by Obama and Biden, is politically motivated is a tired trope that has been repeatedly used by Trump and his allies to discredit and undermine the judiciary. Her assertion that she is "the pick of the president" and will serve the country "in any capacity" rings hollow in the face of the legal scrutiny and judicial decisions that have found her appointment and continued service to be illegitimate.

Moreover, Habba's attack on "rogue judges" and her assertion that they are being "political" instead of doing their job is both ironic and hypocritical. As Olmsted rightly points out, "a judge’s job is to uphold the law, not bend to the president’s every whim." This fundamental principle seems to be lost on Habba and, by extension, the Trump administration, which has shown time and again a profound disrespect for the judiciary and its role in maintaining the checks and balances that are the cornerstone of American democracy.

Incompetence on Display

The commentary from E. Jean Carroll regarding Habba's performance in court is a damning indictment of her legal acumen. According to Carroll, Habba's lack of understanding of basic legal principles and her subservience to Trump's directives—regardless of their legal merit or lack thereof—paint a picture of an attorney out of her depth and beholden to the whims of a man whose respect for the law is, at best, questionable.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

Olmsted's article is not just an exposé of Alina Habba's failed tenure as U.S. Attorney for New Jersey; it is a reflection of the systemic failures and ethical lapses that plagued the Trump administration. The saga of Habba's appointment, dismissal, and subsequent legal battles is emblematic of an administration that operated on the fringes of legality, often crossing into territories that were at once unethical and unconstitutional.

The real tragedy here is not just the personal and professional fallout for Habba, but the erosion of public trust in the institutions that are supposed to serve as the pillars of American democracy. When legal appointments are treated as political pawns and the judiciary is attacked for performing its duty, the very fabric of the republic is put at risk. This is the legacy of Trump's tenure—a legacy that will take years, if not decades, to repair.
