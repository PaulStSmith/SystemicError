Ranchers issue blistering reply to president: 'Cattlemen can't stand behind Trump'

National Cattlemen’s Beef Association has issued a blistering response to President Donald Trump’s comments about American cattle ranchers.

Trump on Wednesday said, “The Cattle Ranchers, who I love, don’t understand that the only reason they are doing so well, for the first time in decades, is because I put Tariffs on cattle coming into the United States, including a 50% Tariff on Brazil.”

“If it weren’t for me, they would be doing just as they’ve done for the past 20 years — Terrible!” he claimed. “It would be nice if they would understand that, but they also have to get their prices down, because the consumer is a very big factor in my thinking, also!”

The association reposted Trump’s comments on Facebook, along with a reply.

“The reality is that ranchers’ success is driven by their own hard work,” it stated. “America’s cattlemen and women operate in one of the most competitive marketplaces in the world. U.S. cattle producers are proud to provide the safest, highest-quality beef on earth. We simply ask that the government not undercut them by importing more Argentinian beef in order to manipulate prices.”

The group continued, adding, “Cattlemen and women cannot stand behind President Trump while he undercuts the future of family farmers and ranchers by importing Argentinian beef.”

“It is imperative that President Trump and Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins let cattle markets work without interference,” the organization then concluded. “If the administration is truly an ally of America’s cattle producers, we call on him to abandon this effort to manipulate markets and focus instead on completing the promised New World Screwworm facility in Texas; make additional investments that protect the domestic cattle herd from foreign animal diseases such as FMD; and address regulatory burdens, such as delisting of the gray wolf and addressing the scourge of black vultures.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Cattle Conundrum: A Missteak or Policy Beef?

In a recent outburst that has left both hooves and eyebrows raised, President Donald Trump took to the political stage with claims that would make even the most seasoned rancher spit out their coffee. Trump, in his infinite wisdom, credits himself for the prosperity American cattle ranchers are experiencing, attributing it to his implementation of tariffs, including a hefty 50% tariff on Brazilian cattle. According to Trump, without his intervention, the cattle ranchers would be wallowing in two decades of despair. His remarks, however, were met with a blistering response from the National Cattlemen’s Beef Association (NCBA), a group not known for mincing words or beef, for that matter.

The Beef with Tariffs

Trump’s claim is a classic example of his penchant for oversimplification and self-aggrandizement. To hear him tell it, the sole reason for any positive trend in the American cattle industry is his tariff policy. This is, to put it mildly, a reductionist view that ignores the complex interplay of market forces, environmental factors, and the sheer hard work of America’s cattlemen and women. The NCBA’s response was swift and scathing, pointing out that ranchers’ success is hardly a gift from Washington but the result of their own sweat and toil.

The Real Issues at Steak

The NCBA didn’t just stop at defending the honor of cattle ranchers; they took the opportunity to highlight the real issues facing the industry. Their response underscores a critical point: the health of America’s cattle industry relies on a balance of competitive market forces and sensible regulation, not market manipulation through tariffs or the importation of foreign beef intended to adjust prices. They argue that such strategies do not support the long-term sustainability of domestic cattle farming but rather, undercut the future of family farmers and ranchers.

What the Ranchers Really Want

In their call to action, the NCBA lays out a clear vision for what government support should look like, focusing on infrastructure, disease prevention, and regulatory reform. They specifically mention the completion of the New World Screwworm facility in Texas, investments to protect against foreign animal diseases like Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD), and addressing regulatory burdens such as the delisting of the gray wolf and the problem of black vultures. These are the unglamorous but critical issues that truly impact the viability of cattle ranching in America.

Conclusion: A Call for Real Support, Not Lip Service

Trump’s comments reveal a fundamental misunderstanding of the cattle industry and what it takes to ensure its prosperity. The NCBA’s response is a powerful reminder that ranchers need more than just tariffs; they need policies that address the real challenges they face every day. As the debate rages on, it becomes increasingly clear that if the administration wants to be a true ally of America’s cattle producers, it needs to listen more closely to their needs and less to the sound of its own voice. In the end, this isn’t just about tariffs or trade—it’s about the future of American agriculture and the rural communities that depend on it.

The spat between Trump and the NCBA encapsulates a broader issue: the need for policy that is informed by the realities on the ground, not just political rhetoric. As we chew on the implications of this confrontation, let’s hope it leads to a more nuanced approach to agricultural policy, one that truly supports the hardworking men and women who feed America and the world.
