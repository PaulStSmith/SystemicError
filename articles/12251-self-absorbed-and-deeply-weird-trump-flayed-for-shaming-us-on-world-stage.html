'Self-absorbed and deeply weird': Trump flayed for shaming US on world stage

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump walks with British Prime Minister Keir Starmer at Trump International Golf Links, in Aberdeen, Scotland, Britain, July 28, 2025. Jane Barlow/Pool via REUTERS

President Trump embarrassed the U.S. on the world stage with a “self-obsessed and deeply weird” performance during his trip to Scotland, a columnist opined.

And, wrote USA Today’s Rex Huppke, it showed international leaders that America was being led by a man “in obvious mental decline.”

“Over the span of a weekend, the U.S. president’s addled brain raced about like a dull-witted Labrador attempting to outsmart squirrels,” wrote Huppke.

“He went on lengthy diatribes about windmills. He ranted about the ungratefulness of starving children. He forayed into nonsensical conspiracy theories regarding the Jeffrey Epstein scandal consuming his administration, while laughably saying upon arriving in Scotland on July 25: 'I’m not focused on conspiracy theories.'”

The trip, which involved meetings with leaders including the president of the European Commission and the prime minister of the United Kingdom, was widely seen as tax-payer funded promotion of Trump’s golf courses.

“News coverage tends to trim Trump’s voluminous prattling into digestible sound bites that sound vaguely sane,” Huppke wrote.

“But if we care about a president’s lack of mental acuity – and I’ve been told by many that we do – it’s worth sticking your head in the high-pressure stream of nonsense that shoots out every time Trump opens his face.”

Huppke listed several examples, describing Trump raging against immigration into the UK before segueing into an attack on windmills.

He veered into starvation in Gaza, and complained that nobody was grateful for aid the U.S. had sent. “It would be nice to have at least a thank you.,” he said.

And then he went into the Jeffrey Epstein saga.

“Trump’s mental dullness led him to keep talking about the Epstein scandal he wants everyone to stop talking about, effectively telling the world the Epstein stuff is “not a big thing,” but probably involves former Democratic President Bill Clinton and a former president of Harvard University and “hedge fund guys” and was probably made up by Democrats who probably put fake stuff in the Epstein files but then, I guess, didn’t use any damning information against Trump before the election," Huppke wrote.

“He also gave a third explanation for why he stopped being friends with Epstein and said of the convicted sex offender’s notorious island: “I never had the privilege of going to his island.”

“The privilege? Spoken like a man whose mind has turned to mush.

“So nice of Trump to share his cognitive decline with the world.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump's Scottish Fiasco

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a poorly written satirical play, President Trump's recent escapade in Scotland has once again thrust the United States into the international spotlight for all the wrong reasons. Described by USA Today's Rex Huppke as a "self-obsessed and deeply weird" performance, Trump's antics not only embarrassed the nation but alarmingly showcased a leader in the throes of what appears to be an "obvious mental decline."

The Spectacle of Incoherence

Huppke's portrayal of Trump's behavior as akin to a "dull-witted Labrador attempting to outsmart squirrels" is not only a masterclass in savage metaphor but a distressing observation of reality. The President's rambling diatribes, ranging from bizarre vendettas against windmills to insensitive remarks about starving children, paint a picture of a man not only disconnected from the gravity of his office but from reality itself.

The Windmill Diatribes: Trump's fixation on windmills is not only perplexing but emblematic of his broader disdain for renewable energy. His baseless rants reflect a dangerous ignorance at a time when climate change demands serious and informed leadership.

The Starvation Insensitivity: To lament the lack of gratitude from starving children, as if they owe a debt of thanks to their benefactor, is not only morally repugnant but reveals a startling lack of empathy and understanding of global humanitarian crises.

The Epstein Conspiracy Carousel: Trump's inability to steer clear of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, even going so far as to diminish its significance while implicating Bill Clinton and others without evidence, is deeply troubling. His comments reflect a penchant for conspiracy theories and a disturbing disregard for the gravity of Epstein's crimes.

A Taxpayer-Funded Golf Promotion?

The fact that this entire debacle was, in essence, a taxpayer-funded promotion of Trump's golf courses adds insult to injury. The misuse of public funds for personal gain is a glaring example of the Trump administration's habitual blurring of the lines between public service and personal enrichment.

The Dangers of Normalizing Nonsense

Huppke's critique of the media's role in trimming Trump's "voluminous prattling into digestible sound bites that sound vaguely sane" is a poignant reminder of the dangers of normalizing such behavior. As the leader of the free world spouts nonsense with alarming regularity, there is a grave risk that the public becomes desensitized to the absurdity and danger of his utterances.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection

Trump's performance in Scotland was not just an embarrassment on the world stage; it was a stark illustration of a presidency plagued by cognitive decline, self-obsession, and a deep disconnect from the realities of governance and global leadership. As Huppke eloquently puts it, it's time for the nation to take a long, hard look at the "high-pressure stream of nonsense" emanating from Trump's presidency and reflect on the direction in which it is being led. The consequences of failing to do so could be dire, not just for the United States but for the global community at large.
