Trump's Iran war boosters in panic as he gets boxed in: 'The terms have changed'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 18, 20265 min read

Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting at the White House. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Donald Trump’s most loyal Iran war hawks are experiencing buyer’s remorse as what they envisioned as a quick military victory threatens to metastasize into a prolonged, unpopular quagmire requiring American boots on the ground, according to a report.

More than two weeks into the campaign, insiders close to the White House are sounding the alarm — the president has lost control of the conflict’s trajectory, Politico reported. Iran now holds the upper hand, they warn, and Trump may have boxed himself into a corner where escalation — potentially including a full ground invasion — becomes the only face-saving option.

“They decide how long we’re involved — and they decide if we put boots on the ground. And it doesn’t seem to me that there’s a way around that, if we want to save face,” one White House insider told Politico.

The core problem: securing oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz against Iranian attacks almost certainly requires seizing Iranian territory — a step that would inevitably mean deploying American troops on Iranian soil.

“The terms have changed,” said a second person familiar with U.S. operations in Iran. “The off-ramps don’t work anymore because Iran is driving the asymmetric action.”

The deteriorating situation is rattling Trump’s “America First” coalition, which fears the president is stumbling into exactly the kind of open-ended Middle East morass he spent a decade condemning. With Iran weaponizing oil market disruption to drive up gas prices, Republicans worry the conflict has become a political time bomb — especially heading into midterms when voters are already furious about inflation and affordability.

“For the White House, now the only easy day was yesterday,” the insider added bluntly. “They need to worry about an unraveling.”

Some Trump allies are still urging restraint, arguing the administration retains non-military tools to pressure Tehran. But they acknowledge each U.S. escalation narrows the exit ramps further.

The severity of the initial strikes compounds the problem. By killing Ayatollah Ali Khamenei along with dozens of senior commanders and family members, Trump may have made Iranian capitulation impossible. The new supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei — the late leader’s son — has far less incentive to back down than his predecessor.

“You’ve killed one guy, the next guy up is even more radical. You killed his dad and his wife,” one White House source said. “Do you think he’s gonna be more — or less — reasonable?”

Our Analysis:

The Quagmire of War: Unpacking the Responsibility

The recent Politico report paints a grim picture of the unfolding military engagement between the United States and Iran, highlighting a scenario where initial hawkish enthusiasm has given way to strategic anxiety and potential regret. This situation, as described, offers a complex interplay of decision-making power, strategic miscalculations, and the realpolitik of war’s unpredictable nature. Let’s dissect the layers of responsibility and narrative framing to understand where accountability truly lies.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

At the heart of this scenario is President Donald Trump, who, as the Commander-in-Chief of the United States Armed Forces, holds the ultimate institutional power to direct military actions and strategies. The decision to initiate military strikes, especially those that resulted in the death of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and several high-ranking Iranian figures, rests with him and his administration. This is a critical point of accountability that cannot be understated; the strategic direction and initial decisions that have led to the current quagmire are fundamentally those of President Trump and his closest advisors.

The Framing of Responsibility

The article suggests that the situation has spiraled beyond the control of the White House, with Iran now dictating the pace and scale of conflict escalation. This framing raises questions about the extent to which responsibility is being misdirected toward external actors (in this case, Iran), potentially obscuring the agency and decision-making power of the U.S. administration. While Iran’s responses and strategies significantly influence the conflict’s trajectory, the initial decision to escalate, and the strategic choices made by the U.S., are pivotal. The narrative of losing control should not absolve the administration of its responsibility for those decisions or their outcomes.

Misdirection and Limited Control

The mention of Trump’s “America First” coalition and Republican concerns about the political fallout from escalating conflict suggests an attempt to broaden the scope of responsibility beyond the administration to its political base or broader geopolitical dynamics. While these concerns are valid and influence the context within which decisions are made, they do not equate to decision-making power. The administration’s strategic choices cannot be solely attributed to external pressures or the unforeseen advantages Iran might leverage.

Critique of the Article’s Analysis

The Politico report, while insightful, leans into a narrative that could underplay the administration’s autonomy and responsibility in leading the U.S. into this precarious situation. By focusing on insider perspectives that emphasize lost control and Iranian ascendancy, there’s a risk of diverting attention from critical questions about the initial rationale for escalation, the decision-making process, and the strategic misjudgments that have characterized the U.S.’s approach.

In conclusion, while Iran’s actions and the broader geopolitical context shape the conflict’s dynamics, the root of the current quagmire lies in the decisions made by President Trump and his administration. The framing of the situation should reflect the complexity of these decisions and the central role of U.S. leadership in navigating the unfolding crisis. Misdirected responsibility or narratives that obscure the locus of decision-making power do a disservice to a nuanced understanding of the situation and the accountability required in its analysis.
