The Dow plunges 1,000 points and the Nasdaq reels as Trump attacks Powell again

U.S. stocks dropped sharply on Monday, continuing a volatile stretch for Wall Street as geopolitical tensions, earnings anxiety, and Fed uncertainty collide.

The tech-heavy Nasdaq led the losses, down 3.1%, with the S&P 500 off 2.8% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling 1,060 points, or 2.7%, as of 12:10 p.m. Eastern. The VIX jumped more than 12% as gold surged to a record $3,433 per ounce.

Meanwhile, the U.S. dollar fell to a three-year low, and stocks fell further after President Donald Trump again fiercely criticized Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, escalating his pressure campaign on the central bank to cut interest rates.

“‘Preemptive Cuts’ in Interest Rates are being called for by many,” Trump said on his social media site Truth Social. “With Energy Costs way down, food prices (including Biden’s egg disaster!) substantially lower, and most other ‘things’ trending down, there is virtually No Inflation. With these costs trending so nicely downward, just what I predicted they would do, there can almost be no inflation, but there can be a SLOWING of the economy unless Mr. Too Late, a major loser, lowers interest rates, NOW.”

His latest salvo against Powell came days after he said the central bank chief’s “termination cannot come fast enough.” The attacks havedeepened investor fears over the Fed’s independence and added to the broader sense of policy unpredictability driving market volatility. It’s no mystery: Long-term investors and operators alike know that trust and stability are core to functioning markets. When leadership injects chaos or undermines institutional credibility, the costs show up everywhere — from higher borrowing rates to delayed investment decisions.

Nvidia stock dropped more than 5% after new White House export restrictions on its AI chips appeared to hand a competitive edge to Chinese rivals such as Huawei, which reportedly plans to ship its own advanced chip to local customers as soon as next month. Tesla (TSLA) stock fell almost 7% ahead of Tuesday’s earnings, as investors brace for lackluster results and look for any sign of stabilization in demand or margins.

With more than 100 companies reporting earnings this week — including Tesla, Google parent Alphabet (GOOGL), IBM (IBM), Boeing (BA), and Procter & Gamble (PG) — traders are looking for any signs of resilience, or at least clarity, as a chaotic second quarter kicks into gear.

Nvidia leads tech giants down

Nvidia (NVDA) stock was down after reports that Huawei will begin mass shipments of a new AI chip to Chinese customers as soon as next month. The move highlights Beijing’s push to reduce reliance on U.S. technology — and deepens investor anxiety around Nvidia’s access to the massive China market after the White House imposed new rules on the company’s chip sales.

It’s been a whiplash month for equities, with intraday swings as large as 5% and every major index well off its pre-“Liberation Day” levels. The coming week may determine whether that trend deepens, or finally breaks.

Earnings from tech giants including Tesla and Google will be scrutinized not just for growth but for signs of stability in a market that’s grown more erratic by the day. With more than 100 companies reporting this week, the next few days could set the tone for the rest of the second quarter.

‘Deal progress’ comments don’t count

“The Street does not care anymore about words and ‘deal progress’ comments,” Wedbush analysts said in a Sunday note. “The economic damage done from this Trump back and forth tariff plan has likely pushed the economy towards a recession path already as cap ex [has been] halted across the board, hiring plans paused, price increase worries, and supply chain shock/chaos has sparked a level of uncertainty in the US not seen since Covid days.”

The difference, Wedbush noted, is that this time it’s “self-inflicted,” concluding bluntly: “We are heading into a tech earnings season that we expect minimal/no guidance as C-level executives are playing darts blindfolded with gauging the growth and spending plans of their customers.”

One bright spot? Netflix (NFLX). In the first quarter of 2025, the company posted an almost-$3-billion profit on $10.54 billion in revenue — with an eye-popping 31.7% operating margin. Wall Street seems especially approving of Netflix’s ad-supported $7.99 monthly membership, which appears less vulnerable to recession-driven household savings measures.

Netflix stock rose 2% on Monday.

Our Analysis:

Chaos Dividend: Wall Street’s Nervous Breakdown Was a Long Time Coming

Another Monday, another meltdown. Wall Street just did what Wall Street does best when reality refuses to behave — it panicked. The Nasdaq plunged 3.1%, the S&P 500 followed close behind with a 2.8% fall, and the Dow dropped over 1,000 points like a sack of cement in a wind tunnel. The VIX, the market’s fear thermometer, spiked over 12%. Gold hit a record $3,433. And the U.S. dollar? It’s down to a three-year low — which is what happens when confidence in American leadership takes a nosedive.

Why? Because the usual suspects — geopolitical anxiety, Fed hand-wringing, and tech fragility — collided headfirst with a fresh injection of political poison.

Trump’s New Target: Monetary Policy

Donald Trump is back doing what he does best: picking fights with institutions that still pretend to be independent. His latest social media rant wasn’t just bizarre — it was economically dangerous. After calling for preemptive rate cuts due to his personal assertion that “there can almost be no inflation,” he took a flamethrower to Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell:

“Mr. Too Late, a major loser.”

Subtlety died screaming.

Markets don’t respond well to erratic power plays, especially when they come wrapped in all-caps populism. The Fed’s independence — already bruised — took another hit. And every trader, investor, and CFO in the country knows what happens when faith in the central bank starts cracking: borrowing becomes riskier, investment slows, and long-term planning goes out the window.

This isn’t strategy. This is a tantrum disguised as policy.

Tech Takes a Beating, Led by Nvidia

Nvidia — the golden child of U.S. AI — lost over 5% after two things happened simultaneously:

  1. The White House tightened chip export rules again.

  2. Huawei announced it’s shipping its own AI chips to domestic buyers next month.

The message is clear: China’s done playing catch-up. They’re building their own playground now, and the Biden administration just gifted Huawei a spotlight. With Nvidia effectively muzzled on one of its largest markets, investors are starting to wonder: is peak AI euphoria already behind us?

If this keeps up, we may be watching the de-Americanization of high-end semiconductors in real time.

Tesla and Tech Earnings: Smoke, Mirrors, and Panic

Tesla dropped nearly 7% ahead of its earnings report, with analysts expecting a limp quarter and even flimsier guidance. Google, IBM, Boeing, Procter & Gamble — all on deck this week. But don’t expect clarity.

As Wedbush bluntly put it:

“C-level executives are playing darts blindfolded.”

Translation? Nobody has a clue what the next quarter holds. Between tariffs, trade head-fakes, and leadership instability, companies aren’t forecasting — they’re fortune-telling.

And when guidance dies, volatility is king.

Netflix: The Lone Island of Sanity?

Netflix, against all odds, made actual money. Nearly $3 billion in profit on $10.5 billion revenue — and a stunning 31.7% operating margin. Why? Their $7.99 ad-supported plan is recession-proof gold.

In a time when people are canceling gym memberships and skipping the second egg at breakfast, Netflix feels like a luxury most are still willing to afford. Investors noticed. The stock ticked up 2%, the only green in a sea of red.

Markets Crave Stability — and Got a Circus Instead

Markets don’t need perfection. But they do need predictability. What they got instead was a three-ring circus:

  • A former president publicly threatening the Fed chair

  • A geopolitical chokehold on the world’s most valuable tech company

  • A Fed paralyzed by political crossfire

  • Executives too spooked to issue guidance

Welcome to the self-inflicted recessionary spiral — no natural disaster, no global plague required. Just a cocktail of bad leadership, short-term thinking, and political theater too absurd to parody.

Final Word

This isn’t just a market correction. This is the bill coming due for years of eroding trust in institutions — and the interest is compounding.

Leadership matters. Stability matters. And if your economic strategy involves threatening central bankers and antagonizing trade partners while hoping the stock market keeps smiling?

You’re not running a country. You’re spinning a roulette wheel.

TL;DR:

Wall Street plunged as Trump reignited his war on the Fed, Nvidia took a hit from new export rules and rising Chinese competition, and tech giants braced for earnings without a roadmap. Markets crave stability — and got chaos instead. This isn’t volatility. It’s a credibility crisis.
