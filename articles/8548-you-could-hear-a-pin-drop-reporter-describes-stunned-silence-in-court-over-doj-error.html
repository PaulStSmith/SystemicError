'You could hear a pin drop': Reporter describes 'stunned silence' in court over DOJ error

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Fallon Gallagher/MS NOW

President Donald Trump’s hand-picked federal prosecutor admitted to a major mistake in her case against James Comey, and a reporter described the stunned reaction to her testimony inside the courtroom.

U.S. District Judge Michael Nachmanoff grilled interim U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan on the grand jury indictment charging Comey with lying to and obstructing Congress, and the federal prosecutor admitted under oath that only two grand jurors were present when she presented the full indictment – which MS NOW reporter Fallon Gallagher set in motion a chain reaction.

“We could hear about what happens with Lindsay Halligan’s fate and then subsequently what will happen with the investigation,” Gallagher said. “But that’s all to say that Judge Nachmanoff inside this hearing today could rule on these grand jury proceedings soon. He didn’t set a specific timeline on when he wants that briefing on that 1969 case that I referenced, but he seemed extremely stunned.”

“I would say the room was so silent that you could hear a pin drop when we learned about what actually happened in that grand jury room” she added. “I would expect this to continue to move forward. This is a case that has historically, this is a court that has historically moved very quickly, but we still have another set of motions, hearings on motions to dismiss set for, I believe, Dec. 9. So these will all chug along. I don’t want to predict exactly when we’ll know, but I would imagine in the next few weeks.”

Comey’s attorneys had asked the court to dismiss the case as “selective and vindictive,” but Halligan’s admission could give them even stronger evidence to justify getting the case dismissed with prejudice.

“That’s a key distinction that they’re making in all of their arguments,” Gallagher said. “If you dismiss a case with prejudice, you can’t bring it back. In this case, the statute of limitations has already expired now. So if they were to dismiss the case with prejudice, there’s no loophole that the government could use to try to bring these charges against James Comey again.”

Our Analysis:

The Grand Jury Fiasco: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In an episode straight out of a legal drama, President Donald Trump’s appointed federal prosecutor, Lindsey Halligan, has admitted to a critical and frankly astonishing error in the case against James Comey. This admission, revealed during a courtroom interrogation by U.S. District Judge Michael Nachmanoff, exposes not just personal failure but systemic dysfunction at the heart of high-stakes legal proceedings. The fact that only two grand jurors were present when the full indictment against Comey was presented is not just a “major mistake”; it’s a glaring indictment of the prosecutorial process under this administration.

A Courtroom in Disbelief

The scene described by MS NOW reporter Fallon Gallagher is one of collective shock. The courtroom’s stunned silence in the wake of Halligan’s admission speaks volumes about the gravity of the error. Such a fundamental flaw in the grand jury process is more than a procedural hiccup; it’s a catastrophic oversight that undermines the integrity of the judicial system. The requirement for a quorum in a grand jury proceeding is not a mere formality; it’s a critical safeguard of the legal process, ensuring that charges are thoroughly vetted by a representative segment of the community before a case proceeds to trial.

Implications for Comey’s Case

James Comey’s attorneys have labeled the case as “selective and vindictive,” a characterization that seems increasingly apt in light of recent developments. Halligan’s blunder provides Comey’s defense team with potent ammunition to argue for the case’s dismissal with prejudice, a move that would permanently close the door on these charges due to the expiration of the statute of limitations. This isn’t just a potential escape hatch for Comey; it’s a damning reflection on the prosecutorial zeal and, perhaps, the political motivations underlying this case.

The Larger Picture

This debacle transcends the immediate legal battle and touches on the broader issues of prosecutorial conduct and accountability. The fact that such a critical error could occur, under oath, in a case of this profile, suggests a troubling lack of oversight and a cavalier attitude towards the mechanisms of justice. If the prosecution can mismanage a case at this level, what does it say about the countless other, less scrutinized cases moving through the federal legal system?

Furthermore, the role of political influence cannot be ignored. The appointment of federal prosecutors by political figures inherently carries the risk of politicizing the justice system. When those appointees falter, it raises questions about the criteria for their selection and the pressures they may face to pursue cases with political undercurrents.

Conclusion

In the end, this incident is a stark reminder of the fragility of the legal process and the importance of vigilance, competence, and impartiality in upholding the principles of justice. It’s a call to action for greater accountability and reform within the federal prosecutorial system, which should be immune to such glaring errors, irrespective of the political affiliations of those involved. The Comey case, regardless of its outcome, should be a wakeup call for an evaluation of prosecutorial practices and a reaffirmation of the commitment to fair and unbiased justice.
