'Go big and go fast': JD Vance reportedly talked Trump into decapitating Iran's leadership

Vice President JD Vance overcame his longstanding objections to U.S. military interventions in the Middle East and persuaded President Donald Trump to expand his offensive against Iran, according to news reports.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had been working for months to steer the 79-year-old president onto the path of war against what he argued was a weakened regime, and the foreign leader worked Feb. 11 during an Oval Office meeting to make sure diplomatic talks with Iran did not undermine the secret military plans the U.S. and Israel had been developing for weeks, reported the New York Times.

“In public, Mr. Trump appeared to take a circuitous path to military action, alternating between saying that he wanted to strike a deal with Iran’s government and that he wanted to topple it,” the Times reported. “He made little effort to try to convince the American public that a war was necessary now. And the limited case he and his aides made included false claims about the imminence of the threat that Iran posed to the United States.”

Trump warmed up to the idea of attacking Iran following the successful U.S. operation that removed Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro in January, and few members of his inner circle spoke up against a military offensive – although there was some disagreement over the best plan of action.

“On Feb. 18, on an unseasonably warm day in Washington, Mr. Vance; Mr. Rubio; John Ratcliffe, the C.I.A. director; and Susie Wiles, the White House chief of staff, gathered with Mr. Trump in the Situation Room to discuss military planning,” the Times reported.

“During the meeting, [Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman Gen. Dan] Caine discussed an array of options, among them that U.S. forces could carry out a limited strike as a way to push Iran in the negotiations, or a larger campaign with the goal of toppling the government,” the report added. “The latter option in particular, he said, carried high risk of American casualties, could destabilize the region and significantly deplete stocks of American munitions.”

Caine stressed that all of those operations would be far more successful than the capture of Maduro, but Vance argued against a narrow operation despite his apparent skepticism of the entire matter.

“For his part, Mr. Vance, who appeared to personally lean against military attacks, argued that a limited strike was a mistake,” the Times reported. “If the United States was going to hit Iran, he told the group, it should ‘go big and go fast.’”

After the meeting, Trump posted on Truth Social that Caine had told him military action against Iran would be “something easily won,” and other administration officials made similarly misleading claims during a Feb. 24 private session with the so-called Gang of Eight leaders of the House and Senate and heads of intelligence committees.

“Secretary of State Marco Rubio made no mention that the Trump administration was considering regime change, according to people familiar with his comments,” the Times reported.

Trump gave the order to attack three days later while flying on Air Force One to an event in Corpus Christi, Texas, with actor Dennis Quade.

“Operation Epic Fury is approved,” Mr. Trump said, according to the Times. “No aborts. Good luck.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Diplomacy: Trump’s Path to War with Iran

The latest revelations surrounding President Donald Trump’s decision to expand military operations against Iran, as detailed in a recent New York Times article, expose a troubling confluence of political maneuvering, misinformation, and a blatant disregard for the consequences of war. The narrative that unfolds is one where Vice President JD Vance, overcoming his own objections, and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, play pivotal roles in steering the U.S. toward a conflict with Iran—a move fraught with risks and based on dubious justifications.

The Netanyahu Influence

The influence of foreign leaders on U.S. foreign policy under the Trump administration has reached a new height, with Netanyahu’s months-long campaign to push the U.S. into a war with Iran. The report that Netanyahu worked to derail diplomatic talks in favor of secret military plans with the U.S. underlines a dangerous bypassing of diplomatic channels and the potential for foreign interests to dictate American military actions.

The Misleading Path to War

Trump’s public indecision and contradictory statements regarding Iran’s government—fluctuating between seeking a deal and seeking to topple the regime—mask a more sinister reality. The administration made “limited case” and “false claims” about the threat Iran posed, deceiving the American public about the necessity and imminence of war. This strategy, or lack thereof, highlights a troubling lack of transparency and accountability in the decision-making process leading to military action.

Disagreement and Decision

The internal discussions within Trump’s circle, as described, showcase a disturbing lack of consensus and a preference for extreme measures. The meeting in the Situation Room, with key figures like Vance and Rubio, revealed a divide in strategies, yet the gravitation towards a more aggressive approach—”go big and go fast,” as Vance put it—prevailed. This preference for escalation over caution underscores a reckless approach to international conflict, with scant regard for the potential loss of American lives and the broader implications for regional stability.

The Deception Continues

The narrative further details how Trump and his administration misled both the public and congressional leaders about the nature and scope of the planned military operations. Claims made by Trump on Truth Social and by administration officials in private sessions with congressional leaders were at best misleading, painting a picture of an easily won conflict that starkly contrasted with the realities outlined by military experts.

Operation Epic Fury: A Foregone Conclusion

Trump’s casual order to commence “Operation Epic Fury” while aboard Air Force One, with no room for second thoughts or aborts, epitomizes the cavalier attitude with which this administration has approached matters of war and peace. It’s a chilling reminder of the power vested in the presidency to initiate conflict, seemingly on a whim, without due consideration for the aftereffects.

Conclusion: A Reckless Gamble

The sequence of events leading up to the military offensive against Iran is a damning indictment of a foreign policy dictated by personal whims, foreign influence, and a disturbing disinterest in diplomatic solutions. The Trump administration’s actions, as reported, represent a reckless gamble with global security and the lives of countless individuals, driven by motivations that seem detached from any rational assessment of national interest or the pursuit of peace.

In sum, the path to war with Iran, as laid bare by these revelations, is a stark testament to the failures of the current administration to uphold its responsibility to its citizens and the international community. The decision to engage in military conflict should never be taken lightly, nor should it be based on misleading justifications or the influence of foreign leaders. The consequences of such actions are far-reaching, and the recklessness displayed in this instance is nothing short of alarming.
