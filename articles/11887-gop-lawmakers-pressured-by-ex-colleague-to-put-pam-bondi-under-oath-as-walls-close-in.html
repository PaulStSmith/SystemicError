GOP lawmakers pressured by ex-colleague to put 'Pam Bondi under oath' as walls close in

Republican lawmakers may have no choice but to call in Attorney General Pam Bondi and put her on the spot, under penalty of perjury, to get answers on what the Department of Justice has on Jeffrey Epstein, according to a former Republican lawmaker.

During an appearance on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," to talk about the war over redistricting that is spreading across the country, ex-Rep. Carlos Curbelo (R-FL) moved on to the Epstein crisis that has the White House on its heels and GOP lawmakers scrambling to distance themselves as evidenced by House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) sending his caucus home early.

As he told the MSNBC hosts, they may only have one way to escape before they get caught up in the wrath of the MAGA base that is clamoring for scalps.

After pointing out that GOP lawmakers are already worried the gerrymandering battles "may come back to bite them," he moved on to their fears that the Epstein firestorm may do the same.

"In this case, it's the hardcore conservatives, the MAGA base, the president's strongest supporters in the House who are demanding this information that the president has asked them to stop doing," Curbelo pointed out. "So it's a very complex situation."

"It's not going to go away until Republicans address it in a meaningful way, unless they get Pam Bondi out there under oath, answering questions, unless they're able to release some information," he warned. "This drumbeat is going to continue and if they don't, then this really does threaten their chances in the 2026 election. President Trump's a guy who has bet everything on his base. If his base isn't enthusiastic, if they're not motivated, that spells big trouble for House Republicans in particular."

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Quagmire: A GOP Conundrum

In a political climate where the truth often seems more malleable than Play-Doh, the recent comments by former Rep. Carlos Curbelo (R-FL) on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" slice through the noise with the precision of Occam's razor. Curbelo, addressing the twin tempests of redistricting wars and the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, has laid bare a stark reality for the Republican Party: they're caught between a rock and a hard place, or more aptly, between gerrymandering gripes and the Epstein fallout.

The Gerrymandering Gambit

Let's begin with the less sensational but equally sordid matter of gerrymandering. Curbelo's assertion that GOP lawmakers are fretting over potential blowback from their redistricting efforts is a delicious irony. Gerrymandering—essentially, the dark art of drawing electoral district lines to favor one party over another—is a tool as old as politics itself. But to hear that the architects might be fearing their own creation? That's poetic justice. Yet, it's the Epstein debacle that's truly putting GOP lawmakers in a sweat.

Epstein: The Elephant in the Room

Jeffrey Epstein, whose name is synonymous with a vile nexus of wealth, power, and sexual predation, casts a long shadow. According to Curbelo, the GOP is in a state of internal panic. The House Speaker, Mike Johnson (R-LA), in a move that reeks of desperation, sent his caucus home early, a clear attempt to dodge the issue. But as Curbelo insightfully points out, this is a storm that can't be outrun.

The call for Attorney General Pam Bondi to testify under oath is a clarion call for transparency and accountability, yet it's also a testament to the GOP's dire situation. The fact that it's the MAGA base—the president's most ardent supporters—baying for blood, makes this a particularly thorny issue. Trump's gamble on his base's loyalty is facing a severe test.

The 2026 Election: A Looming Shadow

Curbelo's warning about the potential repercussions for the 2026 election should not be taken lightly. In politics, momentum is everything, and a demotivated base can spell disaster. The MAGA crowd's demands for information and accountability on Epstein are not just a passing storm but indicative of deeper currents of dissatisfaction.

Conclusion: Between a Rock and a Hard Place

The Republican Party finds itself in an unenviable position, trying to navigate the Scylla and Charybdis of modern American politics. On one hand, there's the gerrymandering controversy, a self-inflicted wound that may yet fester. On the other, the Epstein scandal, a morass of moral and legal peril.

Curbelo's analysis is a stark reminder of the GOP's precarious balancing act. The call for Pam Bondi to step into the fray is more than a demand for answers; it's a symptom of a party at a crossroads. The GOP's future, it seems, hinges not just on its political maneuvers, but on its moral compass—or lack thereof.

As the drumbeat for accountability continues, one thing is clear: evasion is not a strategy, and silence is not an option. The GOP's handling of the Epstein crisis could well be a defining moment, for better or worse.
