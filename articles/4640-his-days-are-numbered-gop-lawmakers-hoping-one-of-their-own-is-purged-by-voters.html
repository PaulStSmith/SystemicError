'His days are numbered': GOP lawmakers hoping one of their own is purged by voters

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 3, 20264 min read

U.S. Rep. Tony Gonzales, R-San Antonio, offers remarks on the introduction of the HIRE Act outside of the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., Tuesday, July 18, 2023. Rod Lamkey via REUTERS

House Republican leadership is deliberately refraining from forcing out scandal-plagued Rep. Tony Gonzalez (R-TX), hoping instead that primary voters in his district will do their dirty work for them with a primary loss, sparing them losing his seat in the general election.

Gonzalez has resisted calls to resign despite facing accusations that his sexual harassment of staffer Regina Santos-Aviles drove her to suicide. Republicans have largely remained silent, primarily because their razor-thin House majority cannot afford to lose a single member.

Instead of pushing him out, party leaders are betting that Gonzalez will lose his primary race, allowing them to retain his vote until a Republican successor can presumably hold the seat in November.

According to Politico, neither President Donald Trump nor Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) have withdrawn their endorsements of Gonzalez, which were issued months earlier. However, a growing number of House Republicans are privately pinning their hopes that he loses the primary, according to interviews with more than a dozen lawmakers and aides granted anonymity to discuss the sensitive matter.

Rep. Mike Haridopolos (R-FL) openly expressed the sentiment, stating, “Should he lose the primary, which a lot of us expect him to do, he’ll probably just serve out his term. People still deserve to have a congressman doing the constituent work, et cetera.”

Haridopolos, like other House Republicans, characterized the allegations against Gonzalez as “very serious, to say the least,” adding: “I think the voters in Texas are going to speak pretty loudly. And I would guess that his days are numbered in Congress.”

The situation has created enormous complications for Speaker Johnson, who is already managing a precarious majority. Assembling sufficient Republican votes to advance legislation requires near-perfect party discipline. The GOP can afford no more than one defection on party-line votes when all members are present, making controversial legislation a constant political tightrope.

Our Analysis:

The Calculated Cowardice of Political Expediency

In a display that could only be described as a masterclass in moral abdication, the House Republican leadership is making a calculated bet that is as cynical as it is cowardly. The core of this bet? That the voters of Texas’ district will relieve them of the messy business of holding Rep. Tony Gonzalez (R-TX) accountable for allegations of sexual harassment so severe they’re linked to a staffer’s suicide. This isn’t just politics as usual; it’s a chilling demonstration of power preservation over principle.

The Unspoken Truth: A Majority Over Morality

At the heart of this decision is the unyielding grip on a razor-thin House majority. The Republican leadership’s silence and inaction scream louder than any words could. Their message? A single seat matters more than the well-being of their staffers or the integrity of their office. By refusing to act against Gonzalez, they signal that their majority is precariously perched on a foundation so fragile, they’re willing to overlook allegations of the most serious nature to maintain it.

The Disturbing Bet: Primary Voters as Unwitting Executioners

Leadership’s strategy to rely on primary voters to oust Gonzalez is not just cowardly; it’s inherently flawed and deeply disturbing. It outsources moral responsibility to the electorate, absolving themselves of immediate action and conveniently forgetting that their endorsements carry weight. The continued support from prominent figures like President Donald Trump and Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) not only undermines any pretense of ethical leadership but actively contributes to the problem by bolstering Gonzalez’s standing.

The Cost of Silence: A Precedent of Complicity

What’s perhaps most alarming is the precedent this sets. By prioritizing a majority over accountability, the GOP leadership is signaling to its members and the public that ethical lines can be crossed without immediate consequence, provided one’s political utility outweighs their misconduct. This isn’t just a failure of leadership; it’s a roadmap for future scandals, where the severity of allegations plays second fiddle to political calculus.

The Illusion of Representation: Constituent Work as a Fig Leaf

Rep. Mike Haridopolos (R-FL) attempts to justify this inaction by emphasizing the importance of Gonzalez continuing “constituent work.” This argument is a fig leaf at best, a thinly veiled attempt to mask the realpolitik at play with a veneer of concern for Gonzalez’s constituents. It’s an insult to those constituents, suggesting their representation in Congress is merely a function of party politics rather than a matter of ethical governance.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The handling of the situation surrounding Rep. Tony Gonzalez by the House Republican leadership is not just a failure of courage; it’s a stark revelation of priorities that places political power above all else, including basic decency and accountability. It’s a clear message to the public and to their own ranks that ethical considerations are secondary to the maintenance of power.

This isn’t just a systemic error; it’s a systemic failure—one that demands not just introspection but immediate rectification. The voters may indeed have the final say, but leadership’s role isn’t to passively await that judgment. It’s to lead, to uphold standards, and to act decisively when those standards are violated. Anything less is a dereliction of duty and a betrayal of the public trust.
