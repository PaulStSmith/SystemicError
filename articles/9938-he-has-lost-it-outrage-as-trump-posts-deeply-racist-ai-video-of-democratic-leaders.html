'He has lost it': Outrage as Trump posts 'deeply racist' AI video of Democratic leaders

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Deepfake Debacle: A New Low in Political Discourse

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures, as he arrives at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland on Sept. 26, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

President Donald Trump took to social media Monday night to share a deepfake video depicting House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) with a handlebar moustache and a sombrero, and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) calling himself and Democrats “woke pieces of s---” — a post that drew immediate outrage from critics and lawmakers alike.

Set to the well-known “Mexican hat dance” song, the video sees Schumer – whose voice and likeness are convincingly recreated likely using generative artificial intelligence – say that Democrats want to allow unrestricted immigration because Americans don’t like Democrats.

“Look guys, there’s no way to sugar-coat it: nobody likes Democrats anymore. We have no voters left because of all of our woke trans bull----,” the deepfake version of Schumer is heard saying.

“Not even Black people want to vote for us anymore, even Latinos hate us. So we need new voters, and if we give all these illegal aliens free health care, we might be able to get them on our side so they can vote for us. They can’t even speak English, so they won’t realize we’re just a bunch of woke pieces of s---, at least for a while until they learn English and they realize they hate us too.”

The post was immediately condemned by several lawmakers, including by Jeffries himself, who Monday night called it a “disgusting video,” and clapped back with his own social media post that included a photo of Trump and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, captioning the post with “this is real.”

Rep. Yassamin Ansari (D-AZ) condemned Trump’s post as well, and argued that its timing – being shared just hours before a likely government shutdown – was indicative of Trump’s misplaced priorities.

“A deeply racist AI video posted by the president of the United States – on the brink of a government shutdown and a health care crisis he created,” Ansari said in a social media post on X Monday night. “This is peak Donald Trump and it’s a goddamn tragedy for our country.”

Others took to calling for immediate action, such as X user “Dave,” who frequently posts material critical of Trump and has amassed nearly 3,000 followers.

“The President of the United States posted this racist, altered video on his Truth Social account tonight,” they wrote in a social media post on X Monday night.

“This is obviously unacceptable. He has lost it. He needs to go; 25th amendment now. But I doubt JD Vance or Trump’s Cabinet Secretaries have the courage to get rid of him.”

Our Analysis:

The Deepfake Debacle: A New Low in Political Discourse

In an era where the line between fact and fiction blurs with alarming frequency, the latest stunt by President Donald Trump plunges political discourse into previously uncharted waters of irresponsibility and racism. Trump’s decision to share a deepfake video targeting House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer is not just a juvenile prank but a dangerous manipulation of media that could have far-reaching consequences.

The Video: A Masterpiece of Misinformation

The video, as described, is a classic example of how deepfake technology can be weaponized to undermine political figures, fabricating scenarios that never happened. By depicting Jeffries in a stereotypical and racially insensitive manner and putting words in Schumer’s mouth that he never said, Trump isn’t just crossing the line of political decency; he’s obliterating it.

Racism and Misogyny: The Trump Brand

The content of the deepfake—racist, misogynistic, and fear-mongering—aligns perfectly with Trump’s well-documented playbook. The portrayal of Democrats as desperate for votes from immigrants, coupled with a gross misrepresentation of minority communities’ sentiments, is not only false but deeply offensive. It perpetuates harmful stereotypes and stokes the flames of division and xenophobia.

Misplaced Priorities Amidst National Crisis

Rep. Yassamin Ansari’s criticism hits the nail on the head: Trump’s timing, amidst a looming government shutdown and a healthcare crisis, speaks volumes about his priorities—or the lack thereof. It’s a stark reminder of Trump’s governance style, one that favors spectacle over substance, and divisive tactics over unity or problem-solving.

The Dangerous Power of Deepfakes

This incident underscores the potent dangers posed by generative artificial intelligence and deepfake technology. When used irresponsibly, as Trump has shown, it can create convincing lies that fuel misinformation, erode trust in public figures, and deepen societal divisions. The fact that a sitting president chose to engage in such tactics is not only unprecedented but deeply concerning.

A Call to Action

The outcry from lawmakers and the public is a testament to the widespread recognition of the video’s harm. Yet, condemnation alone is insufficient. This incident should serve as a wake-up call for the urgent need to regulate deepfake technology and hold those who misuse it accountable. Furthermore, it highlights the necessity for political leaders to maintain a standard of integrity and respect in their discourse.

The Real Tragedy

Ansari’s characterization of the incident as a “goddamn tragedy for our country” couldn’t be more accurate. At a time when unity and leadership are desperately needed, Trump’s actions serve only to further divide and distract. The real tragedy is the erosion of democratic norms and the descent of presidential conduct into the realm of the absurd and the malicious.

Conclusion

Trump’s deepfake debacle is a symptom of a larger problem—a political landscape where lies can be dressed up as truth, and where leaders can stoke division without consequence. It’s a dangerous precedent, and one that demands immediate action, not just in the form of words but in substantial changes to how we regulate and respond to the misuse of powerful technologies. The integrity of our political system and the fabric of our society depend on it.
