'Such a coward': Ted Cruz's 'courage disappears' defending Trump's 9/11 claim: critics

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20263 min read

Ted Cruz’s Job Was to Turn a Lie Into a Feeling

The Claim

Donald Trump used a ceremony honoring emergency responders to insert himself into the history of September 11. He claimed firefighters lifted him away from danger near Ground Zero, but the reporting cited no evidence placing him there during the episode. The earliest identified image of Trump near the site dates to September 13, 2001.

That is enough context. The political story is what happened when CNN asked Senator Ted Cruz to confront the unsupported claim.

Power Protecting Power

Cruz was not an uninformed spectator struggling to evaluate an obscure anecdote. He was a United States senator responding to a claim made by the president at an official White House event.

He had several honest options. He could have said the account required evidence. He could have rejected exaggeration about a mass-casualty attack. He could even have declined to judge the facts. Instead, Cruz vouched for Trump’s sincerity because Trump is “a New Yorker” who “loves New York.”

That answer was not evidence. It was political submission disguised as empathy.

Turning Facts Into Feelings

When Kaitlan Collins pressed him, Cruz recategorized Trump’s account as “feelings” and “sentiments.” The maneuver matters. Trump had described a concrete event involving identifiable people, a specific place and physical danger. Cruz converted that testable claim into an interior emotion that no fact-check could reach.

This is how propaganda receives institutional protection. The powerful make claims about reality; their allies insist those claims should be understood symbolically. Facts remain mandatory for everyone else.

The article’s language of “ridicule” risks shrinking the episode into another humiliating television appearance. Cruz’s cowardice is real, but embarrassment is not the consequence that matters. A senator used his office and public credibility to shield the president from factual accountability.

Borrowed Heroism

Trump’s story does more than inflate his biography. It borrows moral authority from firefighters who entered an actual disaster zone and from the dead whose memory gives the event its political weight.

According to the source, no cited account places Trump at the scene when the nearby building was feared to be collapsing. A former FDNY battalion chief said he knew of no witness who saw him there. The unsupported story nevertheless casts Trump as both endangered participant and intimate companion of rescuers.

That is appropriation, not commemoration: presidential power using public sacrifice as material for personal mythology.

The Loyalty Test

Cruz once competed against Trump for the presidency. Now, confronted with a straightforward question about whether anyone should exaggerate a role in September 11, he retreated into autobiography and sentiment.

The larger pattern is familiar: loyalty to the leader requires allies to abandon ordinary standards of proof. They need not repeat every claim exactly. Their function is subtler—to blur the distinction between an event and an impression, between evidence and affection, until challenging the story seems needlessly literal.

Cruz enabled that blurring deliberately, whatever his private beliefs may be. His institutional role gave the evasion more weight than the online insults it attracted.

The System Rewards the Alibi

The central failure here is not that one politician told an unsupported story or that another looked ridiculous defending it. It is that power supplies its own escape routes.

A president turns national trauma into personal legend. A senator translates the legend into emotion. Coverage then gravitates toward mockery, reaction posts and individual cowardice. Each step moves attention away from the governing structure that makes the performance useful.

Authoritarian politics does not run on the leader’s fabrications alone. It depends on officials willing to make factual scrutiny sound petty, to treat self-glorification as sentiment and to offer power an alibi whenever reality becomes inconvenient.
