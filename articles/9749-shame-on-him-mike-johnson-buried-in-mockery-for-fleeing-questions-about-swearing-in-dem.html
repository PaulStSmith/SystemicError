Reactions flooded in on Tuesday after House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) fled when asked about when he would swear in a newly elected Arizona Democrat, in what one politician described as “pathetic.”

Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ) was slated to be sworn in, but as the government shutdown continues, this move has been delayed. She has said Johnson may be blocking her to prevent the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

Grijalva, the daughter of the late longtime Arizona Rep. Raúl Grijalva (D), who died after battling cancer, won the race last month to succeed him. Her win further narrows the gap for the Republican majority in the House.

Democrats have speculated that Johnson is trying to prevent her from signing on to a discharge petition circulated by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) to force a vote on releasing evidence from Epstein’s sex trafficking case, and Grijalva agreed that’s possible.

People chimed in after Johnson’s reaction Tuesday, sharing their responses on social media:

“Pathetic,” Gov. Gavin Newsom (D-CA) wrote on X.

“Your daily reminder that Republicans are doing everything in their power to cover up the Epstein files,” Rep. Jason Crow (D-CO) wrote on X.

“Rep-elect Grijalva should contemplate filing a lawsuit. Or one of her constituents should, considering they’re being denied representation when they’ve elected someone,” Nick Frisone, 46th District Democratic Central Committee Member, wrote on X.

“ Mike Johnson is straight-up denying the voters of #AZ07 the representation they deserve. Why? To keep the Epstein files hidden and shield creepy elites from accountability. Shame on him,” Tiffany Muller, president of End Citizens United, wrote on X.

“He can’t continue to do this if the public does not let up on the demand to release of the Epstein files. KEEP GOING,” Amee Vanderpool wrote on X.

“.@HouseGOP is keeping the government shut down to protect pedophiles,” Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Fleeing Speaker: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

The recent spectacle of House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) fleeing from questions about the swearing-in of Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ) is not just a fleeting moment of political cowardice; it’s a glaring illustration of the systemic rot that plagues our political institutions. In a move described by one politician as “pathetic,” Johnson’s evasion speaks volumes about the lengths to which some will go to maintain power and obscure truth.

The Delayed Swearing-In: A Strategic Maneuver?

Adelita Grijalva, set to succeed her late father, Raúl Grijalva, in representing Arizona, finds herself barred from taking her rightful place in the House. This delay, occurring amidst a government shutdown, conveniently benefits a Republican majority clinging to power by a thread. The reason? Fear of what Grijalva might do with her access to the contentious Jeffrey Epstein files.

Democrats, including Grijalva herself, speculate that Johnson’s obstruction is a desperate attempt to halt a discharge petition aimed at unveiling evidence from Epstein’s sex trafficking case. This maneuver reeks of political manipulation, prioritizing party interests over democratic process and justice.

Social Media Erupts: The Court of Public Opinion

The public’s response to Johnson’s actions has been swift and scathing. From Governor Gavin Newsom’s succinct dismissal of Johnson’s behavior as “pathetic” to Rep. Jason Crow’s reminder of the Republican efforts to cover up the Epstein files, the outcry spans a broad spectrum of political figures and activists. Each voice shares a common thread: a demand for accountability, representation, and transparency.

Nick Frisone’s suggestion of legal action, and Tiffany Muller’s condemnation of Johnson’s denial of representation to Arizona’s 7th District, underscore the tangible implications of such political stonewalling. Meanwhile, Amee Vanderpool and Sen. Ruben Gallego highlight a collective resolve to not relent in demanding the release of the Epstein files, framing the issue as a broader fight against protecting the powerful at the expense of the vulnerable.

The Bigger Picture: Shielding the Creepy Elites

At the heart of this debacle lies a sinister intent: to shield “creepy elites” from accountability. This sentiment, articulated by Muller and echoed by others, paints a damning picture of a political hierarchy willing to compromise democratic principles to protect its own. Johnson’s actions—or rather, his inactions—are emblematic of a system that too often shields the misdeeds of the influential, leaving victims and the public in search of justice.

Conclusion: The Demand for Change

Mike Johnson’s evasion is more than a moment of political cowardice; it’s a clarion call for systemic reform. The public’s outcry, amplified by social media, serves as a potent reminder of the power of collective voice in demanding accountability. As this saga unfolds, it remains to be seen whether Johnson will heed these calls or continue to prioritize obfuscation over transparency.

In the end, the saga of Johnson’s flight is a microcosm of a larger struggle against a political system marred by manipulation, obstruction, and the protection of the powerful at the expense of truth and justice. The demand for Grijalva’s swearing-in, and the release of the Epstein files, thus transcends partisan politics; it is a demand for a return to the core principles of democracy itself.
