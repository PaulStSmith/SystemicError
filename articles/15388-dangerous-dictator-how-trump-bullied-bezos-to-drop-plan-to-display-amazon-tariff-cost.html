Reports indicated on Wednesday that Trump also contacted Amazon founder Jeff Bezos to voice his discontent. An adviser informed CNN that “he was pissed” because of Amazon’s decision.

But Amazon was quick to abandon the plan. Trump openly celebrated Bezos submitting to pressure, signaling a concerning trend of increasing autocratic intimidation.

The New Republic reported Wednesday that a new PRRI poll reveals public opinion about Trump’s authoritarianism, and a majority now sees him as a “dangerous dictator.”

"As Trump bullies private companies to stop them from telling the truth about his tariffs, a public opinion researcher shares new data illustrating the depths of public revulsion over his dictatorial reign," the New Republic report said.

Earlier on Tuesday, the White House press secretary reacted to a report that Amazon has decided to place a “tariff price” next to every item they sell, indicating how much of the price of that item results from Trump’s tariffs. Leavitt described it as a "hostile and political act."

“This is a hostile and political act by Amazon. Why did Amazon do this… It’s not a surprise because Amazon has partnered with a Chinese propaganda arm," she claimed during a press briefing Tuesday.

Following Leavitt's remarks, Amazon issued a statement saying it “never” had any plans to show tariff costs for products on their main website.

"The team that runs our ultra low cost Amazon Haul store has considered listing import charges on certain products. This was never a consideration for the main Amazon site and nothing has been implemented on any Amazon properties," the statement said.

Our Analysis:

The Autocrat's Pressure and the Corporate Fold: A Tale of Trump, Amazon, and Tariffs

In what reads like a script torn from the annals of authoritarian regimes, we once again find President Donald Trump at the center of a disturbing narrative. This time, his target was none other than the e-commerce giant, Amazon, in a saga that lays bare the dangerous intersection of political power, corporate compliance, and the erosion of democratic norms.

Trump's Autocratic Intimidation

The unfolding drama began with Amazon's announcement that it might disclose the impact of Trump's tariffs on its products—a move that would have illuminated the real cost of these policies to the American consumer. However, Trump, in a move that reeks of autocratic intimidation, reportedly directed Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt to launch a public offensive against Amazon. The message was clear: the administration would not tolerate any narrative that challenges or undermines its policy decisions, regardless of their transparency or truth.

Trump's personal engagement in this campaign, including a direct communication with Amazon founder Jeff Bezos, underscores the extent to which he is willing to leverage his position to suppress dissenting voices. An adviser's comment to CNN that Trump "was pissed" reveals not just the president's anger but the personal vendetta style of governance that has become all too familiar.

Amazon's Swift Capitulation

Amazon's rapid retreat from its initial plan is nothing short of a capitulation to political pressure. Despite the potential public benefit of understanding the real-world impacts of tariffs, Amazon chose to abandon transparency at the altar of political expediency. This decision not only undermines the company's credibility but also highlights a worrying trend of corporate giants bending to the will of political power—especially when wielded in a manner that threatens reprisal.

A Dangerous Celebration

Trump's public celebration of Bezos' submission is emblematic of a deeper, more sinister issue: the glorification of autocratic behavior in the highest echelons of power. By openly reveling in the successful intimidation of Amazon, Trump signals to other corporations and individuals alike that dissent will not be tolerated. This stance, dangerous on its own, is amplified by the broader context of Trump's authoritarian tendencies, as highlighted by a new PRRI poll indicating a majority of the public views him as a "dangerous dictator."

The Alarming Rhetoric of "Hostile and Political Acts"

Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt's characterization of Amazon's potential action as a "hostile and political act" is a gross misrepresentation that further distorts the line between legitimate business decisions and political maneuvering. By labeling transparency and consumer information as hostile, the Trump administration demonstrates its commitment to controlling the narrative at all costs—even at the expense of public understanding and awareness.

Amazon's Response: A Missed Opportunity for Courage

Amazon's response, stating it "never" had plans to show tariff costs on their main website, represents a missed opportunity to stand up for transparency and consumer rights. This backtracking, whether a result of internal miscommunication or a strategic retreat, highlights the chilling effect of Trump's pressure campaign. It also leaves us wondering what could have been, had Amazon chosen to illuminate the true costs of tariffs to its vast audience.

Conclusion: The Stakes Could Not Be Higher

The saga of Trump's pressure on Amazon over tariffs is a stark reminder of the fragile state of democracy and corporate integrity in today's political climate. It's a scenario where power not only corrupts but also coerces, leaving in its wake a trail of missed opportunities for truth, transparency, and accountability. As we navigate these troubled waters, the importance of vigilance, courage, and resistance—by individuals, corporations, and institutions alike—has never been more critical.
