New docs blow hole in Trump admin’s mortgage fraud allegations against Fed official

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 14, 20254 min read

New documents obtained by Reuters contradict allegations made by a Trump administration bureaucrat that Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook committed mortgage fraud.

Federal Housing Finance Agency director Bill Pulte had previously claimed to have unearthed documents indicating Cook, who had been unwilling to follow President Donald Trump's demands to lower interest rates, had declared two separate residences as her primary home in mortgage statements, which, depending on the jurisdiction, can be against the law when it causes banks to lend under more favorable terms.

But according to Reuters, "A loan estimate for an Atlanta home purchased by Lisa Cook ... shows that Cook had declared the property as a 'vacation home.'"

"The document, dated May 28, 2021, was issued to Cook by her credit union in the weeks before she completed the purchase and shows that she had told the lender that the Atlanta property wouldn’t be her primary residence," said the report. "The document appears to counter other documentation that Cook’s critics have cited in support of their claims that she committed mortgage fraud by reporting two different homes as her primary residence, two independent real-estate experts said."

The revelation could be an obstacle to Trump's efforts to fire Cook from the Federal Reserve, which he justified by pointing to Pulte's unproven allegations. Cook has filed a lawsuit challenging her dismissal as unlawful.

Pulte, whose agency oversees the government-sponsored housing finance entities Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, has issued similar mortgage fraud claims against other Trump critics, including Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and New York Attorney General Letitia James, both of whom deny any wrongdoing.

A recent report found that Pulte's own father and stepmother may have been pulling that exact scheme on properties in Michigan and Florida. Upon Reuters' investigation into the matter, officials in Bloomfield Township, Michigan, revoked the couple's homestead exemption.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Allegations and Their Unraveling

In what reads like a prime-time political drama, allegations of mortgage fraud have been lobbed against Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook by Trump administration bureaucrat Bill Pulte. But, as the latest findings by Reuters reveal, these accusations appear to be crumbling under the weight of their own inconsistency.

The Allegations: Pulte accused Cook of declaring two separate residences as her primary home in mortgage statements, ostensibly to secure loans under more favorable terms. This move was painted as retaliation for Cook's refusal to bend to President Donald Trump's demands to lower interest rates. Such allegations, if true, could indeed paint a damning picture of financial impropriety.

The Reality Check: The recently obtained documents by Reuters tell a different story. A loan estimate for an Atlanta home purchased by Lisa Cook clearly marked the property as a "vacation home," directly contradicting the allegations of mortgage fraud. This document, dated May 28, 2021, proves that Cook had informed her lender the Atlanta property would not serve as her primary residence. This revelation not only dismantles Pulte's claims but also highlights a concerning trend of using unsubstantiated allegations as weapons in political warfare.

The Political Undercurrents: The motivation behind Pulte's allegations seems to be more than just a concern for legal propriety. Attempting to fire Cook from the Federal Reserve based on these unfounded claims, as Trump sought to, points to a broader strategy of political retribution and manipulation. Cook's subsequent lawsuit challenging her dismissal underscores the legal and ethical quagmire that such political maneuvers create.

The Irony of Accusation: Adding a layer of irony to the situation, it has come to light that Pulte's own family members may have engaged in the very scheme he accuses Cook of. This revelation, following an investigation into the matter that led to the revocation of a homestead exemption for Pulte's father and stepmother, serves as a stark reminder of the adage, "Those in glass houses should not throw stones."

Broader Implications: Beyond the immediate legal and personal ramifications for those involved, this episode raises significant concerns about the integrity of political discourse and the mechanisms of accountability. When allegations of serious misconduct are wielded so cavalierly, the potential for damage extends far beyond the individuals targeted; it erodes public trust in the institutions meant to serve as the bedrock of governance and justice.

In Conclusion: The unraveling of allegations against Lisa Cook not only clears her name but also exposes a dangerous propensity within certain political circles to leverage unfounded accusations as tools of coercion and vendetta. This incident should serve as a cautionary tale about the consequences of prioritizing political gain over truth and integrity. As the dust settles, one must wonder how many more such baseless claims are wielded in the shadows of power, away from the scrutinizing eye of investigative journalism.
