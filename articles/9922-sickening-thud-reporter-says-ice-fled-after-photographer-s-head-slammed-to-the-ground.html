'Sickening thud': Reporter says ICE fled after photographer's head slammed to the ground

On Tuesday, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents grabbed or shoved several media members at a New York courthouse, and it was all captured on video by Getty photographer Stephanie Keith.

The Department of Homeland Security spokesperson, Tricia McLaughlin, stated in a news release that “officers were swarmed by agitators and members of the press, which obstructed operations ... Officers repeatedly told the crowd of agitators and journalists to get back, move, and get out of the elevator.”

However, amNewYork photojournalist Dean Moses called that outright false while speaking to MSNBC on Wednesday.

“So, just to be clear, the most important aspect I want people to know is that it was not clear that ICE was detaining anyone at that point,” said Moses. “Usually, what happens is we, as journalists, wait outside the courtrooms where ICE are masked. ICE agents also wait. So, we were waiting in the hallway. And usually what happens is when someone leaves the courtroom, they usually accost them.”

The agents then look through their paperwork and ID and demand information. They take someone into the stairwell and can be rough with them or not. In this case, the woman left the courtroom, walked to the elevator, pressed the button, and walked inside when she was met with physical force. Meanwhile, photographers and reporters followed her, trying to get an interview about her story.

“So, I stepped into the elevator and then all of a sudden they became enraged, pushing us, screaming obscenities to get out the elevator. And that’s when the chaos ensued,” said Moses.

An ICE agent can be heard telling Moses, “Get out of the elevator. Get out of the f---ing elevator!”

He noted that there was no announcement, the ICE agents never said a word, much less asked them to move so they could detain someone.

The claim that they were swarmed was also untrue, he continued.

“I have to say, in this instance, it’s an absolute falsehood. None of that is accurate,” Moses continued. “I’d be lying if I said this hasn’t happened in the past. There have been protesters. There’s been activists who have attended court hearings who have, you know, I would say fairly impeded detentions. But in this case, there were no activists, there were no agitators. It was just members of the press.”

The only person in the elevator was the woman who had walked out of the courtroom.

“Then directly after I went [into the] elevator, when I was grabbed and shoved, another ICE agent shoved my colleagues, which was the gentleman who was pushed to the ground,” Moses recalled. “So, there was nobody other than the press involved.”

Photojournalist Olga Fedorova was also shoved to the ground and appeared to land partially on Elibol, but continued to take photos and was able to stand up. Elibol wasn’t as lucky.

“So, actually, you know, you could hear the slam,” he said of his colleague’s head hitting the floor. “It was a really sickening thud of when his head and his back hit the back of the very hard floors in the hallway there. And the ICE agents just rushed away. Thankfully, my other colleagues and some of the actual security guards who work there independent of ICE did step in and tried to call for medical attention. He was left there for some time, I would say 30 to 40 minutes, really not moving at all until EMS was able to come and take him away.”

Elibol was put in a neck brace and taken away on a stretcher by paramedics in the New York Fire Department.

Last week, one ICE agent was relieved of his duties after a video showing him harassing a woman and ultimately shoving her to the ground.

Our Analysis:

The Brutality of ICE Agents Unveiled: A Scathing Analysis

In a recent, disturbing incident at a New York courthouse, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents not only crossed the line of decency but seemingly obliterated it, engaging in what can only be described as an unprovoked assault on members of the press. The events, captured by Getty photographer Stephanie Keith, starkly contradict the narrative peddled by the Department of Homeland Security. Here, we dissect the incident and the glaring discrepancies in the official account.

Contradictions and Falsehoods

Tricia McLaughlin, a spokesperson for the Department of Homeland Security, issued a statement claiming that ICE officers were “swarmed by agitators and members of the press,” leading to an obstruction of operations. This account was swiftly debunked by amNewYork photojournalist Dean Moses, who provided a firsthand account of the events to MSNBC. According to Moses, the assertion that the press and supposed ‘agitators’ swarmed the officers, impeding their operations, is an outright falsehood.

Moses’s detailed recounting paints a vastly different picture; one where journalists, performing their civic duty, followed a woman exiting a courtroom in hopes of capturing her story. Their pursuit led them to an elevator encounter with ICE agents, who, without warning, resorted to physical aggression and verbal abuse. The claim of a swarm of agitators is not only false but laughably so, given that the only individuals present, aside from the woman, were members of the press.

The Assault on Journalism

The aggression did not stop at verbal commands. L. Vural Elibol, a colleague of Moses, was slammed to the ground by these agents, sustaining injuries severe enough to warrant emergency medical attention. The violence did not discriminate, as Olga Fedorova, another photojournalist, was also shoved to the ground in the melee. This incident isn’t isolated but part of a troubling pattern of behavior by ICE agents, one of whom was relieved of his duties last week after a similar display of unnecessary force.

A Pattern of Violence and Misinformation

This event underscores not only the physical dangers journalists face while reporting on ICE operations but also the broader issue of misinformation disseminated by governmental agencies. The Department of Homeland Security’s attempt to frame the incident as a result of journalists and non-existent agitators impeding ICE operations is not just incorrect; it’s a deliberate obfuscation of the truth. It’s an alarming attempt to vilify the press and shift blame away from the excessive force employed by its agents.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The incident at the New York courthouse is a stark reminder of the lengths to which certain government entities will go to suppress the free press and avoid accountability. The physical assault on journalists by ICE agents is reprehensible and should be condemned in the strongest terms. It’s imperative that there is a transparent investigation into these actions and that those responsible are held accountable.

In an era where the truth is often manipulated and obscured, the role of the press as the Fourth Estate is more crucial than ever. We must not allow the intimidation and violence against journalists to become normalized. It’s not just an attack on individual reporters; it’s an assault on the very principles of democracy and transparency.

The Department of Homeland Security’s attempt to paint this incident as anything other than an unjustifiable attack on the press is not only misleading; it’s a dangerous precedent. We stand in solidarity with those journalists who faced unwarranted violence while performing their duties and call for immediate action to ensure such incidents are not repeated. The truth must not be a casualty in the enforcement of immigration laws.
