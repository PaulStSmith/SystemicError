'Vote no next time': MAGA senator shamed by House Republican after 'spying' complaint

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Representative Thomas Massie (R-KY) arrives prior to a closed briefing on Iran for members of the House of Representatives on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 27, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) drew a sharp rebuke from Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) Thursday after she demanded a “reckoning” on the surveillance of her Senate GOP colleagues, something, Massie suggested, she herself helped facilitate with her past votes.

“We need to know why AT&T and Verizon did not challenge the subpoena for the phone records of 8 United States Senators when the Biden FBI spied on us during an anti-Trump probe,” Blackburn wrote Wednesday in a social media post on X. “There needs to be a reckoning for this.”

The following day, Massie responded with a sarcastic rebuke, reminding her of her own voting record.

“It’s called the Patriot Act, [the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act], and [the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency],” Massie wrote on social media, responding to Blackburn’s comments. “Please vote no next time.”

As a House member, Blackburn voted several times for extending provisions of the Patriot Act, which was first signed into law following the 9/11 attacks to enhance national security by drastically expanding the federal government’s authority to conduct surveillance. She’s also voted to increase the ability of CISA to share “cyber threat indicators” with private companies.

Massie was not the only person to jump on Blackburn’s comments, with X user “simulxxx,” who operates the artificial and technology consulting company Q32 and has amassed more than 5,500 followers, calling out what they characterized as the senator’s “gaslighting.”

“Because it’s the law that your party made happen after 9/11 and still won’t back down on supporting,” they wrote in a post on X. “Stop gaslighting. There is ONE political party: propping up fed power. And TWO ‘flavors of lies.’”

Even pro-Trump self-described conservatives were quick to call out Blackburn on her supposed outrage over the alleged monitoring of her GOP Senate colleagues, including X user “MissBeck12,” who frequently posts pro-Trump content and has amassed nearly 15,000 followers.

“Patriot Act, FISA, and CISA. Perhaps Republicans should vote NO the next time these come up for renewal?” they wrote in a post on X.

“All members of Congress know how unconstitutional and intrusive these are, but you all believed you’re immune to the consequences of your votes. Until the laws you pass jump up and bite you in the a$.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Hypocrisy: Senator Blackburn’s Surveillance Outrage

In an episode that can only be described as political theater at its most absurd, Senator Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) has sparked a firestorm of criticism, not least from her own party ranks, for her sudden and convenient outrage over surveillance practices—a monster of her own making. Her demand for a “reckoning” over the surveillance of Senate GOP colleagues, as a result of an anti-Trump probe, has been met with a swift and scathing reminder of her own complicity in creating the very surveillance state she now decries.

The Irony of Blackburn’s Outrage

“We need to know why AT&T and Verizon did not challenge the subpoena for the phone records of 8 United States Senators when the Biden FBI spied on us,” Blackburn proclaimed, igniting a wave of incredulity among those who have watched her, without a hint of irony, support every expansion of the surveillance state she could vote on. Her call for a “reckoning” is so rich in irony that it would be hilarious if it weren’t so deeply troubling.

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), in a display of brutal honesty and common sense seldom seen in the halls of Congress these days, delivered a sarcastic yet pointed rebuke: “It’s called the Patriot Act, [the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act], and [the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency], Please vote no next time.” Massie’s response is a stark reminder of Blackburn’s voting history, which starkly contradicts her recent expressions of outrage. As a House member, Blackburn not only voted for the Patriot Act but also supported legislation to beef up the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency’s (CISA) ability to share “cyber threat indicators” with private companies.

The Reality of the Surveillance State

The Patriot Act, a piece of legislation that has been criticized for its overreach and infringement on civil liberties since its inception following the 9/11 attacks, has been a tool in the steady erosion of privacy rights in the name of national security. Blackburn’s support for this and other similar measures has directly contributed to the expansive surveillance capabilities of the federal government—a fact she now finds inconvenient.

Moreover, the critique from X user “simulxxx” cuts to the heart of the matter, highlighting the senator’s gaslighting. Their comment underscores the bipartisan nature of the surveillance state’s expansion: “Because it’s the law that your party made happen after 9/11 and still won’t back down on supporting,” calling out the illusion of difference between the parties when it comes to propping up federal power.

A Call for Accountability

Even among pro-Trump conservatives, there’s a palpable frustration with Blackburn’s perceived hypocrisy. X user “MissBeck12” encapsulates this sentiment perfectly, pointing out the blatant oversight and arrogance of members of Congress who believe themselves above the consequences of their own actions. “All members of Congress know how unconstitutional and intrusive these are, but you all believed you’re immune to the consequences of your votes. Until the laws you pass jump up and bite you.”

Conclusion: A Lesson in Responsibility

Senator Blackburn’s sudden indignation over surveillance practices that she herself has consistently endorsed is more than just political posturing; it’s a glaring example of the lack of accountability and foresight that plagues our political system. It’s a reminder that the laws and policies politicians enact in the name of security can, and often do, come back to haunt them—sometimes, quite literally spying on them.

The backlash from both sides of the aisle should serve as a wake-up call not just for Blackburn but for all members of Congress. The surveillance state they’ve built does not discriminate based on party lines. As they continue to erode the privacy rights of American citizens, they too become ensnared in the web of surveillance they’ve woven. Perhaps next time, they’ll think twice before voting yes on measures that infringe upon the very freedoms they purport to protect.
