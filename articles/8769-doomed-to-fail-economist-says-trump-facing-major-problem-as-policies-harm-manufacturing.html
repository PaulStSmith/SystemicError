'Doomed to fail': Economist says Trump facing major problem as policies harm manufacturing

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 13, 20254 min read

An economist has warned Donald Trump’s “dismantling” of the economy will make a major problem for the Republican Party.

Mariana Mazzucato has said recent policies are a “hodgepodge” of ideas from the GOP that are going to cause trouble in the near future. Speaking to Politico, the top economist urged the president and his administration to rethink their overall policy. Mazzucato likened the current plan to “throwing money around and imposing tariffs”. This, along with a proposed 50-year mortgage plan, doesn’t appear to be working and will cause trouble in the future for Trump’s proposed economic plan, she said.

The Institute for Innovation and Public Purpose at University College London head has warned that the current trajectory of the economy under Trump would see the “competitiveness” of the US wither away. The problem, Mazzucato says, is with the “dismantling” of that competition.

Mazzucato said, “U.S. competitiveness will wither away because he’s dismantling the backbone of U.S. competitiveness which has been, in the past, smart, capable, strategic, outcome-oriented, mission-oriented state agencies.”

The economist explained that manufacturing in the US will struggle under Trump, though it may look as though it is working at first.

Mazzucato said, “The problem is, if you reshore manufacturing, is manufacturing in the U.S. going to continue to be on the technological frontier? Or will it simply be in the U.S.? So, for a little bit of time, it will be employing U.S. workers because it will be in the U.S.”

“But they are just doomed to ultimately fail because they are not going to be growing, dynamic industries that are benefiting from what I have always argued is required to allow industry to benefit, which is an active, smart industrial strategy.”

The proposed reforms to manufacturing were not a “long-run growth strategy,” according to Mazzucato, who says the focus should be on “dynamic relationships with small companies.”

She added, “I don’t think it’s a long-run growth strategy to simply talk about reshoring. What you want is those companies to arrive and to have dynamic supply chains and to have dynamic relationships with small companies, which also through government, demand-side investments can grow. It is basically what the U.S. has always done through the military, which, over time, is starting to also happen in health and energy.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Missteps: A Recipe for Disaster

In a recent article, Mariana Mazzucato, a leading economist, has issued a stark warning that should send shivers down the spine of anyone with even a passing interest in the future of the American economy. Mazzucato’s critique of Donald Trump’s economic policies lays bare a troubling reality: the Republican Party is steering the United States towards an iceberg, with policies that range from misguided to downright hazardous.

The Hodgepodge of GOP Policies

Mazzucato’s assessment of Trump’s economic strategies as a “hodgepodge” of GOP ideas is a diplomatic way of saying it’s a chaotic mess. The article highlights a concerning lack of coherence in Trump’s approach, likening it to “throwing money around and imposing tariffs.” This scattergun approach to economic policy is not just ineffective; it’s actively damaging. The proposed 50-year mortgage plan is a band-aid solution that fails to address the root causes of economic instability and housing unaffordability.

The Dismantling of U.S. Competitiveness

One of the most alarming points Mazzucato makes is about the deterioration of U.S. competitiveness on the global stage. Trump’s policies aren’t just failing to bolster the economy; they’re actively undermining it. Mazzucato pinpoints the dismantling of “smart, capable, strategic, outcome-oriented, mission-oriented state agencies” as a critical error. These institutions have historically been the backbone of American economic competitiveness, fostering innovation and strategic growth. Under Trump’s watch, this backbone is being systematically eroded.

The Illusion of Manufacturing Prosperity

Trump’s stance on reshoring manufacturing jobs to the U.S. is a perfect example of a policy that sounds good on paper but falls apart under scrutiny. Mazzucato argues that simply bringing manufacturing jobs back to American soil without a comprehensive industrial strategy is a short-sighted move. It may create a temporary illusion of prosperity, but without investment in technology and a dynamic industrial strategy, these jobs are “doomed to ultimately fail.”

The Need for a Long-Run Growth Strategy

Mazzucato’s call for a focus on “dynamic relationships with small companies” and demand-side investments is a breath of fresh air. It highlights a fundamental truth: real economic growth comes from innovation, strategic investment, and fostering a dynamic, interconnected business ecosystem. Trump’s policy of reshoring for reshoring’s sake misses this point entirely. It’s a policy that looks backward, not forward.

Conclusion: A Misguided Economic Vision

Mazzucato’s analysis paints a grim picture of the future of the American economy under Trump’s guidance. The Republican Party’s current economic policies are not just inadequate; they are a recipe for long-term disaster. This is a wake-up call for the GOP and the Trump administration. The path to prosperity is not paved with tariffs, short-sighted manufacturing policies, and dismantling strategic state agencies. It requires a nuanced, forward-thinking approach that fosters innovation, competitiveness, and dynamic economic relationships.

In short, Trump’s economic plan isn’t just failing; it’s actively harming the very fabric of the American economy. It’s time for a radical reevaluation of priorities before it’s too late.
