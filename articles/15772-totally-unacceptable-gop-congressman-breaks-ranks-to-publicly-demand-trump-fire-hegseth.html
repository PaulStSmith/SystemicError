'Totally unacceptable': GOP congressman breaks ranks to publicly demand Trump fire Hegseth

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth gestures as he meets with El Salvador Defense Minister Rene Merino Monroy at the Pentagon in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 16, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

In a rare moment of disunity among House Republicans, Politico reports GOP Rep. Bacon (R-Neb.) advised President Donald Trump to fire U.S. Sec. of Defense Pete Hegseth, saying the resulting chain of chaos is why Republicans were leery of approving him in the first place.

“If it’s true that he had another [Signal] chat with his family, about the missions against the Houthis, it’s totally unacceptable,” said Bacon, a lead Republican on the House Armed Services Committee. “I’m not in the White House, and I’m not going to tell the White House how to manage this … but I find it unacceptable, and I wouldn’t tolerate it if I was in charge.”

Bacon is referring a New York Times report that Hegseth shared sensitive information about military air strikes in Yemen in a commercially-available Signal app that also included his wife, his brother and a personal lawyer. The New York Times report is the second report of Trump officials using an unclassified messaging platform to share sensitive information.

“I had concerns from the get-go because Pete Hegseth didn’t have a lot of experience,” added Bacon, who also chairs the subcommittee on cyber issues. “I like him on Fox. But does he have the experience to lead one of the largest organizations in the world? That’s a concern.”

Critics say Hegseth “lacks the credibility and experience required” to lead a massive entity as the Department of Defense,” and that his confirmation as secretary of defense “is a pick for loyalty over competence.”

Hegseth served as an Army National Guard officer from 2002 to 2021, with combat deployments in Iraq and Afghanistan, but he has no record of managing a body even a fraction of the DOD’s scale. His only leadership roles involve heading small nonprofit organizations Vets for Freedom and Concerned Veterans for America between 2008 to 2016. Audits revealed both organizations spent more money than they raised under his leadership. And his former Fox News colleagues said that as a part-time weekend host, he frequently showed on set hung over and smelling of alcohol.

Hegseth is also criticized for believing the U.S. need not respect the rules of the Geneva Conventions. He also supported illegal torture techniques and lobbied for impunity for service members accused of war crimes.

Bacon is more nervous about what he perceives as Hegseth’s lack of reliability in preserving military secrets.

“It looks like there’s a meltdown going on,” Bacon said. “There’s a lot — a lot — of smoke coming out of the Pentagon, and I got to believe there’s some fire there somewhere.”

Our Analysis:

Hegseth's Hullabaloo: A Symptom of Systemic Dysfunction

In a spectacle that would be richly entertaining if it weren't so deeply troubling, we've been treated to the latest episode of dysfunction within the House Republicans, as reported by Politico. GOP Rep. Don Bacon's public rebuke of U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth encapsulates a broader narrative of incompetence, misplaced loyalty, and the perilous undermining of national security protocols that seems to have become the hallmark of this administration.

The Allegations: At the heart of Bacon's discontent is a damning accusation. Hegseth, apparently, shared sensitive information about military air strikes in Yemen through a Signal chat that included his wife, brother, and a personal lawyer. This isn't just a faux pas; it's a flagrant breach of security protocols, one that could potentially jeopardize operations and lives. It's the kind of mistake that freshman cadets are taught to avoid, let alone the Secretary of Defense.

Loyalty Over Competence: Critics have long voiced concerns over Hegseth's suitability for the role of Secretary of Defense. His resume, while decorated with military service, lacks any semblance of the experience required to manage the behemoth that is the Department of Defense. Hegseth's primary qualification seems to be his unwavering loyalty to Trump, a criterion that is alarmingly insufficient for the staggering responsibilities the position demands. His tenure at Fox News and as a leader of small nonprofit organizations hardly equips him for the complexities of national defense.

A Dangerous Precedent: The Politico report sheds light on a disturbing trend within the Trump administration: the use of unclassified messaging platforms to share sensitive information. This is not the first time Trump officials have been caught in such acts of negligence. It sets a dangerous precedent and sends a message of impunity that could have far-reaching consequences for national security.

The Broader Implications: Bacon's criticisms, while pointed, touch on a more systemic issue. The appointment of Hegseth, a man criticized for his lack of experience, credibility, and a troubling disdain for international law and norms, speaks volumes about the priorities of the administration. It underscores a worrying willingness to sacrifice expertise and ethics at the altar of loyalty and ideological conformity.

The Smoke and Fire: Bacon ominously notes the "meltdown" within the Pentagon, suggesting that the visible chaos may only be the tip of the iceberg. When a leading Republican on the House Armed Services Committee expresses such grave concerns, it's indicative of deep-rooted problems that could have serious implications for national security.

In conclusion, the situation with Hegseth is not just a case of individual failure but symptomatic of a larger, systemic error within the administration. It's a stark reminder of the consequences of prioritizing loyalty over competence, of eschewing experience for allegiance. As the saga unfolds, one can only hope that it serves as a wake-up call for a return to sanity, professionalism, and respect for the protocols that safeguard the nation. But if history is any guide, this hope may be as misplaced as the trust in Hegseth's capabilities.
