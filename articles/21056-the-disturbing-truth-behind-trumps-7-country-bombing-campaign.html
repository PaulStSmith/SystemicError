The disturbing truth behind Trump’s 7-country bombing campaign

Based on an article by Nur Makdisi

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The War Machine Is the Point

War as a Revenue Model

The article describes a presidency that does not treat war as an exception to governance but as its organizing business model. Trump campaigned on “ending wars” and then, according to the source, escalated them: bombing seven countries in the first 11 months of his second term, helping drive a bombing campaign in Iran that killed more than 3,000 people, and threatening Greenland for missile bases and minerals. That is not strategic confusion. It is a governing method built around force, scarcity, and profit.

Who Holds Power

The actual power in this story sits with the White House, the Pentagon, military contractors, mining firms, and the financial networks that profit when conflict creates shortages. Trump is not merely “surrounded by” this system. He is presented as the political broker who connects it, expands it, and cashes in on it. The proposed $1.5 trillion defense budget, the $100 billion in the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” for critical minerals and related projects, and the rush to rebuild weapons stockpiles all point to the same fact: the state is being used to feed the state’s own war economy.

AI as a Battlefield Product

The article also shows how military power is being modernized into something colder and less accountable. The Pentagon’s billions in contracts with Google, Oracle, Microsoft, Amazon, Palantir, and Anduril are not side notes. They are the infrastructure of a war machine that wants faster targeting, more automation, and less human restraint. Maven Smart System is the clearest example: a tool that helps sift data and select targets at speed. When the source says the Pentagon has not explained whether AI played a role in the bombing of Shajareh Tayyebeh Girls School, that is not a technical gap. It is an accountability vacuum inside a machine built to kill first and explain later.

Corruption in Plain Sight

The article is strongest when it names the patronage structure directly. Donald Trump Jr. helping select Pentagon personnel while firms linked to his investment network win contracts is not “influence” in some vague Washington sense. It is family-adjacent state capture. The same goes for the shell corporation tied to Trump’s sons investing in a mining group involved in a $1.6 billion tungsten project in Kazakhstan, approved under the Trump administration. Elizabeth Warren’s phrase, “corruption in plain sight,” fits because the system no longer bothers with plausible deniability. It just needs enough bureaucracy to launder theft into policy.

Misdirection and Scapegoats

What this story exposes is how often the language of “national security” is used to hide plain predation. The source correctly identifies the military-industrial complex, but the deeper misdirection is the idea that this is a technocratic scramble for resilience or supply-chain security. It is not. It is extraction under armed protection. The administration’s reported willingness to withhold HIV assistance from Zambia to pressure access to minerals makes the coercion explicit. When the U.S. destabilizes regions, threatens aid, and expands mining and weapons contracts at home, the cruelty is not collateral. It is operational.

Extraction Is the Doctrine

The larger pattern is simple: militarization, extraction, and privatized political power are merging into one regime of rule. Critical minerals are not just inputs for industry in this story; they are the material basis for endless war, AI-enabled targeting, and oligarchic enrichment. The article is right to connect environmental destruction, Indigenous dispossession, and geopolitical conflict, because that is the real architecture here. Trump is not an aberration from the system. He is what the system looks like when it stops pretending.
