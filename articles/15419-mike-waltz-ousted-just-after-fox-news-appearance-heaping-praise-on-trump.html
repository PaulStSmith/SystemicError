Trump administration National Security Advisor Mike Waltz and other staffers are out at the National Security Council, according to multiple reports.

The move came after he appeared on Fox and Friends Thursday morning. He and anchor Brian Kilmead spoke on the peace deal reached with Ukraine and Russia.

He also spoke on the “first of its kind” mineral deal which was reached with Ukraine, which he claimed is “good for American taxpayers” and allowed the United Stated “recoup the investment the U.S. made in the country.

Waltz previously served as a Florida congressman and is a decorated combat Green Beret.

According to Fox News Waltz and his deputy Alex Wong, were ousted Thursday.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Dismissal of Mike Waltz from the National Security Council

The recent ousting of Mike Waltz from the National Security Council (NSC) underscores a tumultuous era in U.S. politics, where appearances on media outlets can precipitate significant career shifts. This event is not just a simple staffing change; it's emblematic of the deeper power dynamics and the fragility of tenure within the current administration.

The Catalyst: A Fox News Appearance

Waltz's appearance on Fox and Friends, a show known for its cozy relationship with certain political figures, was ostensibly the last straw. During the interview, Waltz discussed a peace deal between Ukraine and Russia, as well as a mineral deal with Ukraine, claiming it was "good for American taxpayers" and would allow the United States to "recoup the investment the U.S. made in the country."

The timing of his dismissal, immediately following this appearance, raises questions about the tolerance for public discourse within the administration. The fact that this discussion—ostensibly positive in its portrayal of U.S. foreign policy achievements—could lead to such a drastic outcome suggests a brittle environment where policy discussions are concerned.

The "First of its Kind" Mineral Deal

Waltz's mention of a "first of its kind" mineral deal with Ukraine, purportedly beneficial for American taxpayers, is a significant claim, begging further scrutiny. The assertion that this deal allows the U.S. to "recoup" its investments suggests a transactional view of foreign aid and diplomacy, which could have broad implications for how the U.S. engages with allies and strategic partners. Without more details, it's hard to evaluate the true value of this deal, but the claim alone is indicative of the administration's approach to international relations—a mix of pragmatism and opportunism.

The Implications of Waltz's Ouster

The removal of Waltz and his deputy, Alex Wong, signals a purge within the NSC, potentially motivated by internal politics rather than national security interests. Waltz, a decorated Green Beret and former Florida congressman, undoubtedly brought a unique perspective to the NSC. His military background and legislative experience could have been assets in advising on national security issues, yet these were seemingly overshadowed by the politics of his media appearance.

This event highlights the precarious nature of serving in a highly politicized administration where aligning with the prevailing winds is paramount. It also raises concerns about the freedom of public officials to engage with the media, especially when such engagements can lead to immediate dismissal.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Administration Dynamics

The ousting of Mike Waltz from the NSC is a microcosm of the larger political dynamics at play within the administration. It serves as a stark reminder of the volatility that characterizes this era of American politics, where media appearances can determine the fate of national security advisors. Furthermore, it underscores the administration's approach to foreign policy—a blend of transactional diplomacy and strategic opportunism, wrapped in a veil of media sensitivity.

As observers, we must ask: what does this mean for the stability and direction of U.S. national security policy? And more importantly, how does it affect America's standing on the global stage? These are questions that demand answers as we navigate these turbulent political waters.
