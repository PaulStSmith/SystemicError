Now 79, President Donald Trump continues to feel highly competitive with a wide range of people, from former President Barack Obama to Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-Kentucky) to fellow real estate developers. Trump will insist that he has a higher IQ than past Republican presidents, and he repeatedly brags about how well he thinks he plays golf.

But according to sports journalist/author Rick Reilly (of Sports Illustrated and ESPN fame), Trump’s claims about his golfing record are wildly exaggerated.

Trump once again bragged about his golfing record during a livestreamed conversation with troops on Thanksgiving 2025.

The president claimed, “I know a lot about golf. I’ve won 38 club championships, and I don’t get to practice much. I won one last year. I won a club championship at a big club, beating a 27-year-old kid. I said, ‘You know, I’m decades older than you.’ But I said, ‘The fairway doesn’t know how old you are as you walk up the middle and he’s in the rough.’ And, uh, I’ve been a good golfer over the years.”

Reilly, however, is questioning Trump’s “38 club championships claim.”

On X, formerly Twitter, Reilly posted, “Ha! Trump adds about 10 every time he brings this up.”

Daily Beast reporter Janna Brancolini notes, “The president has been dogged for years by accusations that he cheats at his favorite pastime by kicking balls from the rough into the fairway, instructing his caddies to throw opponents’ balls into bunkers, and awarding himself numerous ‘gimme putts,’ according to The Times of London. He also regularly claims to ‘win’ tournaments at his own clubs, including competitions where he has skipped rounds or where nobody has seen him play, The Palm Beach Post reported.”

Brancolini adds, “During his Thanksgiving call, Trump insisted that all of his tournament wins had been ‘legitimate’ and then launched into a rant about (former President Joe) Biden.”

Trump insisted that he is a much better golf player than Biden, telling troops, “A lot of people talk, but they can’t play — like Biden. Biden can’t hit a ball 30 yards. I’m telling you, I looked at his swing. He cannot hit a ball 30 yards. He said he was a 6-handicapped. He said — that was the only thing that made me angry during the debate with him — he said he was a 6. I said, ‘You’re not a 6.’ And he said, ‘Well, I’m an 8.’ I said, ‘That was quick. I picked up 2.’ But he’s not 100.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Exaggeration: Trump’s Golfing Claims Unraveled

In an era where the line between fact and fiction increasingly blurs in political discourse, former President Donald Trump’s assertions about his golfing prowess stand as a towering monument to the art of exaggeration. At 79, his competitive spirit remains undimmed, not just in the political arena but also on the golf course—a domain where he claims unparalleled supremacy. Yet, as we peel back the layers of Trump’s self-aggrandizing narrative, the foundation appears as shaky as a sand trap after a heavy rain.

Trump’s Tall Tales on the Tee

Trump’s recent boast during a livestreamed Thanksgiving 2025 conversation with troops—that he has clinched “38 club championships”—is a claim that stretches credulity to its breaking point. Sports journalist Rick Reilly, armed with nothing but a keen eye for the truth and a well-honed BS detector, quickly took to X (the platform formerly known as Twitter) to call out Trump’s inflation of his golfing record. Reilly’s skepticism is well-founded, given Trump’s historical penchant for, let’s say, gilding the lily with regard to his golfing achievements.

A Game of Shadows

The accusations that have dogged Trump’s footsteps on the fairway are numerous and damning. From the alleged tactical kicks of balls from the rough to the fairway, instructing caddies to sabotage opponents’ plays, to claiming victories in tournaments where his participation is, at best, a matter of heated debate—the list reads like a playbook for how not to play golf. Daily Beast reporter Janna Brancolini highlights these controversies, underlining the chasm between Trump’s claims of golfing glory and the murky reality of how those “victories” were achieved.

Golfing Glory or Gimmickry?

Trump’s insistence that all of his tournament wins were “legitimate” does little to dispel the clouds of doubt. His rant against former President Joe Biden’s golfing capabilities during the same call veers into the realm of the absurd, focusing on trivialities rather than substantial matters of state. This not only underscores Trump’s obsession with besting his perceived rivals but also highlights a disturbing penchant for valuing personal vendettas over pressing national concerns.

The Fairway of Fiction

What emerges from Trump’s fantastical narratives is a portrait of a man deeply invested in the construction of his own mythos—a narrative where every swing is a victory, and every critique is dismissed as the bitter grapes of envy. Yet, in the grand scheme of things, Trump’s golfing tales are emblematic of a broader issue: the erosion of truth in public discourse. When the line between fact and fiction is not just blurred but obliterated, the very foundations of accountability and credibility are at risk.

In the final analysis, Trump’s golfing claims are more than just a curious footnote in his post-presidential life; they are a microcosm of the challenges facing a society increasingly divided by differing realities. As we navigate this fractured landscape, it’s crucial to remember that the truth—like a well-played golf shot—should aim straight and true, unencumbered by the rough of falsehoods or the bunkers of exaggeration.
