'Devastating': Internet erupts over conservative outlet's 'damning' report on Trump allies

The conservative Wall Street Journal is causing an internet uproar with its new report on Donald Trump allies who are cashing in on ending the Russia-Ukraine war.

The Journal’s piece ahead of the weekend, Make Money Not War: Trump’s Real Plan for Peace in Ukraine, claims that, “The Kremlin pitched the White House on peace through business. To Europe’s dismay, the president and his envoy are on board.”

“For the Kremlin, the Miami talks were the culmination of a strategy, hatched before Trump’s inauguration, to bypass the traditional U.S. national security apparatus and convince the administration to view Russia not as a military threat but as a land of bountiful opportunity, according to Western security officials,” the Wall Street Journal reported. “By dangling multibillion-dollar rare-earth and energy deals, Moscow could reshape the economic map of Europe—while driving a wedge between America and its traditional allies.”

The report made waves immediately.

Former speechwriter to President George W. Bush, David Frum, called it a “devastating report on the real Trump-Russia deal: betray Ukraine in exchange for privileged business benefits for Trump insiders.”

Activist Garry Kasparov said, “As I said in my Halifax speech a few days before this damning WSJ report, this has always been personal business for Trump, not national interest.”

“It’s how Putin turned Russia into a mafia state and it’s been Trump’s goal from day one of his new unleashed admin,” he added Saturday.

Going further, he said, “And also as happened with Putin, politicians and pundits spend too much time looking for complicated motivations from ideology or psychology or blackmail. It’s money. It’s always money. They’re crooks. With immense power, but still crooks. Don’t overcomplicate things.”

Bloomberg Opinion columnist Ronald Brownstein chimed in, “If the betting markets did Pulitzer odds, this remarkably reported [WSJ] piece would be a comet.”

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Alliance: Trump, Putin, and the Pursuit of Power and Profit

The recent exposé by the Wall Street Journal, titled “Make Money Not War: Trump’s Real Plan for Peace in Ukraine,” has ignited a firestorm across the digital realm, revealing a disturbingly cozy relationship between the Trump administration and the Kremlin. This piece, by laying bare the intricate web of financial enticements and geopolitical maneuverings, underscores a glaring systemic failure in American politics: the prioritization of personal gain over national security and democratic values.

The Kremlin’s Long Game

First, let’s dissect the Kremlin’s strategy, as outlined by the Journal. The notion that Russia viewed its relationship with the U.S. not as a geopolitical chess game but as a commercial transaction is as audacious as it is alarming. By proposing “peace through business,” Russia sought to realign the economic and political landscape of Europe, effectively undermining the continent’s security architecture. The Journal report highlights a profound systemic error: the susceptibility of the U.S. political system to manipulation by foreign powers, facilitated by the promise of lucrative deals.

Trump’s Betrayal

The allegations against Trump and his allies, as detailed by the Wall Street Journal, are nothing short of a betrayal of the Ukrainian cause in exchange for “privileged business benefits.” This is not just a matter of foreign policy misjudgment; it is a dereliction of duty, a selling out of democratic principles and allies for personal enrichment. David Frum’s characterization of the report as a “devastating report on the real Trump-Russia deal” is apt but perhaps underplays the severity of the situation. It’s a scandal of monumental proportions, revealing a presidency potentially compromised by financial incentives.

The Motivating Factor: Greed

Garry Kasparov’s commentary cuts to the heart of the matter: “It’s money. It’s always money.” This simple yet profound observation lays bare the fundamental flaw in the political ethos of certain leaders. Trump’s administration, according to Kasparov, mirrored Putin’s kleptocratic regime, where the conflation of state and personal business blurs the lines of legality and morality. This pursuit of wealth, at the expense of national interest and international stability, is a systemic error that undermines the very foundations of democracy.

The Verdict

Ronald Brownstein’s suggestion that the Journal’s investigation is worthy of a Pulitzer is not an overstatement. This “remarkably reported piece” does more than just shed light on a series of questionable transactions and alignments; it reveals a systemic vulnerability to corruption and manipulation through the allure of financial gain.

In conclusion, the Wall Street Journal’s report is a damning indictment of a political system that allows, if not encourages, the pursuit of personal profit over public good. It serves as a stark reminder of the need for robust safeguards against such vulnerabilities, for the sake of preserving the integrity of democratic institutions and international alliances. The revelations call for a critical reassessment of the mechanisms through which foreign entities can exert influence over American politics and highlight the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and, above all, a recommitment to the principles of democracy and national security.
