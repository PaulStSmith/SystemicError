'He just gets angry': Trump insiders spill that president is keeping Epstein story alive

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 14, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Eternal Tangle of Trump and Epstein: A Closer Look

Donald Trump delivers remarks in the Oval Office. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump can’t move past his longtime association with Jeffrey Epstein, and his allies suggested that he’s botching his response.

A huge batch of emails written by the late sex offender got dumped into public view Wednesday by the House Oversight Committee, shedding new light on the president’s relationship with Epstein, and members of Trump’s inner circle told Axios why they believe the scandal lingers on.

“The minute he thinks it’s unfair to him and it’s a personal attack, he just goes into fight mode,” said one confidant. “No one wants to ask him what’s going on here [with Epstein] because he just gets angry.”

Trump famously has been able to skate past scandals – Epstein mentions that penchant in one email, marveling that his “strength is remarkable” – but some controversies have clung tighter to him due to his attempts to shut them down.

“Whether it’s a question about the current affordability crisis, COVID in 2020 or the Russia probe in 2017, he has a penchant for pushing back against attacks by calling them Democratic hoaxes or con jobs,” Axios reported. “He then tries to kill the controversy with such a heavy hand that it helps keep the story alive.”

The Epstein files exploded into controversy because Trump and his top officials – including Vice President JD Vance, FBI director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi – promised to release them during the 2024 campaign, but the president inflamed his MAGA base by locking them down after returning to the White House.

“He told Pam not to release the files,” said a Trump insider. “We don’t exactly know why.”

Trump was first elected to office by hyping the WikiLeaks release of emails that impugned Hillary Clinton in the weeks before the 2016 vote, and Axios noted the similarities between that scandal and the Epstein files.

“As in this case, the emails showed no clear sign of wrongdoing by the intended target,” Axios reported. “But the fact that there was such a big volume of behind-the-scenes details ensured the story dominated media coverage and social media, creating the appearance of a scandal. And appearance can be reality in politics.”

The Eternal Tangle of Trump and Epstein: A Closer Look

In an era where political scandals transmute into media frenzies with the speed of a tweet, the latest revelations surrounding President Donald Trump and his disturbingly cozy relationship with the late Jeffrey Epstein have once again thrust the former president into the eye of the storm. According to a recent Axios report, despite Trump’s notorious Teflon persona, this scandal, with its deep and dark implications, seems to cling to him with a peculiar tenacity. Let’s dissect the layers of this ongoing saga with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

Firstly, the leak of a “huge batch” of emails by the House Oversight Committee is not just a drip in the ocean of Trump-related controversies; it’s a veritable flood. These correspondences paint a vivid picture of Trump’s association with Epstein, a convicted sex offender, and highlight a troubling aspect of Trump’s modus operandi: his infamous fight mode. The report quotes a Trump confidant saying, “The minute he thinks it’s unfair to him and it’s a personal attack, he just goes into fight mode.” This immediate defensiveness, coupled with a refusal to address the core issue, is a recurring theme in Trump’s playbook. It’s as if the man is more interested in battling perceived slights than engaging with substantive critiques of his actions and associations.

Moreover, Trump’s history of skating past scandals is legendary, yet this particular association seems to be an albatross around his neck. Epstein himself is quoted marveling at Trump’s resilience in the face of controversy, but one must ponder: at what point does resilience become sheer denial? The report insightfully draws parallels between Trump’s handling of past controversies — be it the “affordability crisis”, the COVID pandemic, or the Russia probe — and his current predicament. Trump’s strategy, it seems, is to decry everything as a “Democratic hoax” or a “con job”, attempting to squash the controversy with such force that it paradoxically breathes more life into it.

The decision to withhold the Epstein files, promised during the 2024 campaign, is particularly telling. It’s a stark reminder of Trump’s 2016 campaign strategy, where he weaponized WikiLeaks emails against Hillary Clinton. The irony here is palpable — Trump, once the beneficiary of leaked correspondences, now finds himself haunted by the specter of his own associations, revealed through similar means. The Axios report aptly notes, “But the fact that there was such a big volume of behind-the-scenes details ensured the story dominated media coverage and social media, creating the appearance of a scandal. And appearance can be reality in politics.”

This raises a fundamental question about the nature of political scandals in the digital age: Is the semblance of impropriety as damaging as impropriety itself? In Trump’s case, the persistent association with a figure as nefarious as Epstein, coupled with his aggressive attempts to suppress the narrative, does nothing to dispel the shadows of doubt. Instead, it only further entrenches the narrative of a man willing to go to great lengths to protect his image, perhaps at the expense of transparency and truth.

In conclusion, while Trump’s association with Epstein is but one thread in the complex tapestry of his political career, it is a thread that refuses to be ignored. As the former president navigates the treacherous waters of public opinion and legal scrutiny, one wonders if this time, the Teflon might finally show signs of wear. The Epstein saga is not just a story of two men; it’s a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked power, the sanctity of truth, and the ever-blurring line between appearance and reality in the political arena.
