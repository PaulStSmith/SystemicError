'More like an audition': MSNBC legal analyst raises alarm over judge's pro-Trump dissent

Reacting to one of Donald Trump’s latest legal setbacks, MSNBC legal analyst Lisa Rubin explained on Wednesday morning that the sole dissent in an immigration case supporting Donald Trump is “just as important” as the ruling itself because of what it portends for the future.

Late Tuesday the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit ruled that the Alien Enemies Act (AEA) could not be applied in the case of the Venezuelan immigrants the president wants to deport because it found no evidence of an “invasion or predatory incursion” by a foreign power.

At the center of the case was Trump’s use of the AEA as a work-around to support the deportation plans, which two judges balked at.

Speaking with “Way Too Early” host Ali Vitali, Rubin instead focused on the dissent by Judge Andrew Oldham, who was placed on the bench by Trump in 2018, and singled out his explanation for supporting the president as alarming.

In his dissent, Oldham lectured his colleagues over second-guessing Trump and wrote, “The majority’s approach to this case is not only unprecedented—it is contrary to more than 200 years of precedent.”

That led Rubin to urge viewers to remember Oldham’s name.

"The dissent here is as important as that two-judge majority opinion,“ she told the host. “Judge Oldham is expected to be among the front-runners for any Supreme Court seat that Trump has to fill. And his dissent, which he says represents the first time in the 227-year history of the nation that a court has blocked a president from using the AEA and very strongly reads to me more like an audition than a legal opinion.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Dissent: A Prelude to Future Legal Battles

In the shadow of a recent legal defeat for Donald Trump regarding the deportation of Venezuelan immigrants, a dissenting opinion has garnered just as much, if not more, attention than the ruling itself. This spotlight isn't due to the dissent's legal acumen or its persuasive power but because of the ominous precedent it potentially sets for the future. MSNBC legal analyst Lisa Rubin's analysis of Judge Andrew Oldham's dissent in the immigration case reveals a concerning trajectory for the judiciary, particularly with an eye towards the highest court in the land.

The Legal Context and the Oldham Dissent

The crux of the case lies in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit's rejection of using the Alien Enemies Act (AEA) as a justification for the deportation of Venezuelan immigrants. The court found the application of the AEA inappropriate, as it requires evidence of an "invasion or predatory incursion" by a foreign power, which was evidently lacking. However, Judge Andrew Oldham, a Trump appointee, saw things differently.

Oldham's dissent is not just a divergence in legal opinion; it's a stark reminder of the politicization of the judiciary. His lecture to his colleagues for overstepping their bounds in second-guessing Trump is less a legal argument and more a political endorsement of executive power overreach. Rubin rightly points out that Oldham's dissent seems more like an audition for a higher role, presumably on the Supreme Court, than a grounded legal opinion. This scenario raises alarms about the future direction of the judiciary, especially if it leans towards validating presidential overreach without substantive legal grounding.

The Implications of Oldham's Position

Rubin's call to remember Oldham's name is not just a cautionary note; it's a siren warning. If Oldham is indeed among the front-runners for a Supreme Court seat, as Rubin suggests, his dissent signifies a concerning future where legal precedents and the Constitution could bend to the will of political agendas. The assertion that this dissent is as pivotal as the majority opinion underscores the critical nature of dissenting opinions in shaping future legal landscapes. They are often indicative of a jurist's legal philosophy and potential trajectory on the bench.

A Political Audition Disguised as a Legal Opinion

What stands out in Rubin's analysis is the characterization of Oldham's dissent as an audition rather than a rigorous legal opinion. This distinction is crucial. It implies that the dissent is designed not to convince his current colleagues but to signal his loyalty and legal philosophy to potential future patrons, namely Donald Trump and his allies. This approach undermines the judiciary's supposed impartiality and independence, suggesting that some judges may prioritize political alignment over legal integrity.

The Bigger Picture

The broader implication of Rubin's analysis and the case at hand is the erosion of trust in an impartial judiciary. When judges are perceived as political actors auditioning for higher roles rather than impartial arbiters of the law, it diminishes the judiciary's credibility and the public's trust in its decisions. The concern is not just about one dissenting opinion but about what it represents: a judicial philosophy that is willing to prioritize political loyalty over legal precedent and constitutional fidelity.

Conclusion

Lisa Rubin's focus on Judge Oldham's dissent is a clarion call to those concerned with the integrity of the judiciary and the balance of powers within the U.S. government. It's a stark reminder that the judiciary, ideally the most apolitical branch of government, is not immune to political influences and ambitions. As such, the legal community and the public must remain vigilant, scrutinizing not just the decisions that shape our legal landscape but the dissents that signal future battles.
