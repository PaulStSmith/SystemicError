'Too stunned to respond': What Trump said to Biden behind closed doors

Based on an article by Frank Yemi

Paulo SantosNovember 10, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

Donald Trump told Joe Biden behind closed doors that they might have been friends “if it weren’t for politics,” suggested they would golf together, praised Biden’s job creation as “extraordinary,” and said he was relieved not to have run against him. Biden writes in an excerpt published by The Atlantic and covered by AlterNet that the flattery left him “too stunned” to answer.

Biden says the Oval Office meeting, attended by chiefs of staff Jeff Zients and Susie Wiles, stretched from a scheduled 30 minutes to nearly two hours. Biden tried to brief Trump on Ukraine, NATO, Russia, and the Middle East, while Trump reportedly concentrated on world leaders’ personalities and gossip.

The encounter appears in Biden’s forthcoming book, Promise Me, America. Biden also condemns Trump’s subsequent reversal of his policies, clemency for more than 1,500 January 6 defendants, and renewed false claim that he defeated Biden in the 2020 election.

The flattery was not the power

The private charm is interesting only because of what followed it. Trump could compliment Biden’s record in the Oval Office and then use presidential authority to dismantle parts of that record upon returning to power. The consequential act was not the buttering up. It was the exercise of executive power.

That distinction exposes the emptiness of treating political conflict as a personality problem. Trump’s friendliness did not constrain his decisions, preserve Biden’s policies, or moderate his public lies. Personal warmth and institutional destruction can coexist perfectly well when the warmth carries no obligation.

Biden mistook contradiction for mystery

Biden says he did not understand why Trump was flattering him. But the reported conduct does not require a hidden theory. Trump could enjoy Biden’s company, seek an agreeable transition meeting, or indulge in praise while remaining committed to reversing Biden’s work. None of those possibilities alters who had agency once power changed hands.

Biden’s bafflement reflects a familiar establishment habit: expecting private manners to reveal public commitments. That habit turns deliberate political choices into an interpersonal puzzle. Trump’s decisions were expressed through orders, pardons, and public claims—not through the tone he adopted beside a fire.

Gossip displaced governing substance

Biden reportedly raised wars, alliances, and geopolitical risks. Trump focused instead on the personalities of foreign leaders. That is not merely a contrast in conversational style. A presidency organized around personal loyalty, grievance, and leader-to-leader chemistry places enormous public consequences inside one officeholder’s impressions.

The power imbalance is stark. Foreign leaders, civil servants, allies, and affected populations must navigate decisions made by the president. They do not gain equal agency because coverage presents his attention to personalities as colorful gossip.

Clemency made the hierarchy explicit

Trump’s sweeping clemency for more than 1,500 January 6 defendants, including people convicted of assaulting police officers, was an exercise of presidential power with concrete beneficiaries. Whatever rhetoric Trump and his supporters use about law enforcement, the reported act placed executive protection behind people punished in connection with an attack on the transfer of power.

Biden’s denunciation is forceful, but retrospective condemnation is not institutional restraint. The central political fact is that the presidency supplied Trump with the authority to grant that clemency and reverse prior policy. Moral clarity after leaving office does not substitute for durable limits while governing.

The system keeps searching for a personality key

The teaser invites readers to decode what Trump “really meant” behind closed doors. The larger pattern is less mysterious: elite political coverage repeatedly privileges access, demeanor, and private contradiction over the machinery that produces consequences.

Trump’s compliments did not create jobs, preserve alliances, pardon defendants, or reverse policy. Presidential authority did. When political reporting centers the astonishing conversation rather than the unequal distribution of power, it turns governance into psychodrama and leaves the system itself conveniently offstage.
