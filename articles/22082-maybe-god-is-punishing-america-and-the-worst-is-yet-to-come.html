Maybe God is punishing America — and the worst is yet to come

Based on an article by John Stoehr

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Wokeness as Cover for Plunder

The Convenient Story

The article is really about a trick of power: when elites want to defend a rotten order, they invent a moral panic that blames the wrong people. Its target here is the lazy, self-protective obsession with “wokeness,” which gets treated by commentators like David Brooks as if campus grievance were the origin of American breakdown. That framing is useful because it points away from the people actually making decisions and toward the people easiest to caricature.

Who Actually Holds Power

The answer is not obscure. The people with real institutional power are the billionaire class, the political grifters they bankroll, and the media figures who launder their excuses into respectable language. The source names the scale plainly: sixteen people in America are worth nearly $4 trillion; they own media, tech platforms, and parts of both parties; they are trying to own money itself. That is not commentary drifting into abstraction. That is the power map.

The Blame Shift

Jamelle Bouie’s point, as quoted in the source, lands because it exposes the fraud at the center of the “woke” panic: the made-up claim that the left purged conservatives, imposed a caste system, and stamped out dissent. Brooks says these things happened when they did not. That matters. False stories do political work. They give elite resentment a respectable costume and turn reactionary politics into a supposed response to some exaggerated offense by the weak.

Impunity Is the Engine

The deeper pattern is not just decadence. It is impunity. The source links the decline in white-collar prosecution after the 2007-2008 financial panic to the rise of men like Elon Musk from rich to obscenely rich. That is the structural point: when elites learn that large-scale harm will not be punished, they scale up the harm. The corruption becomes routine, then normalized, then politically defended.

Crypto, Corruption, and the Small-Scale Theft

Crypto is not presented here as a neutral technology with mixed uses. It is described as a machine for tax evasion, money laundering, and crime, and the article ties that directly to Trump’s family cashing in on meme coins while Trump shrugs that “everybody’s profiting.” The numbers the source gives are telling enough: Trump’s coin down 98 percent, his wife’s down 99 percent, while the family allegedly pockets $1.4 billion. That is not innovation. It is extraction with a digital veneer.

The Democratic Collapse

The article’s strongest claim is that the country did not stumble into decay. It was built there by people who wanted no consequences. Musk talks like immigrants are the problem; the source correctly identifies the real threat as men like him, men who want to remake society so they never answer for what they have done. The same goes for pundits who prefer fables about wokeness to an honest account of oligarchy, corruption, and state failure. When those stories dominate, democracy does not merely weaken. It is hollowed out by people who still insist they are the adults in the room.

The System They Chose

This is the pattern: elite panic manufactures a decoy, elite impunity protects the wreckers, and elite commentary performs innocence while the republic is stripped for parts. The country is not being undone by too much equality or too much dignity. It is being damaged by concentrated wealth, criminal indifference, and the institutions willing to translate both into normal politics. That is the real scandal. The rest is propaganda with better manners.
