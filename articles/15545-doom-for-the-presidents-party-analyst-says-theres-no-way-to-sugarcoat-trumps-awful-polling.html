'Doom for the president’s party': Analyst says there’s 'no way to sugarcoat' Trump’s 'awful' polling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S President Donald Trump speaks to members of press onboard Air Force One on a flight to Fiumicino Airport near Rome to attend the funeral of Pope Francis, April 25, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

Americans are “turning on” President Donald Trump “after propelling him back into office just six months ago,” CNN’s Jessica Dean reports.

In a segment on CNN’s Inside Politics Sunday, Dean invited data analyst Harry Enten on to discuss Trump’s 41 percent approval rating — which she described as “the lowest for any president at this point in his term in the history of modern polling.”

“These numbers are just horrible — there’s no way to sugarcoat it,” Enten told Dean. “And the first way we'll sort of point that out is we'll look at where Donald Trump is now versus where he was 100 days into his first presidency. And you see it here, you don't have to be a mathematical genius. 41 percent approve of him now. It was 44 percent back in 2017 around the 100 day mark.”

“The American people do not like what they are seeing at this point from Donald John Trump,” Enten added.

“The bottom line here is just very simply the numbers for Donald Trump are awful, awful, awful in this poll,” Enten said.

The CNN data analyst noted “54 percent of Americans have no confidence that Trump will use the power of the presidency responsibly.”

"And so, this just tells you, on every single different data point you could possibly look at, whether it be Trump's overall approval, whether it be the issues for Donald Trump or how he conducts himself in office, all of these numbers are pretty much terrible for Donald Trump at this point,” the analyst said.

Wall Street Journal political correspondent Molly Ball said the fact Trump has “lost his advantage on the economy is a really big blinking red light that a lot of Republicans are concerned about, because for so long that was his his signature advantage.”

“The fact that that has turned upside down and his ratings on the economy are now worse than his overall ratings is a really big red flag, I think, for him and for the entire Republican party," Ball said.

Enten later argued "there is pretty much zero chance — if Donald Trump's approval rating is 41 percent in November of 2026 — the Republicans have any shot of holding the House of Representatives" in the midterm elections in 2026.

"There is no -- I repeat, there is no -- precedent for a president's approval rating being as low as Donald Trump's is right now and then his party holding on to the House in the midterm elections,” Enten said. “… When you look at approval ratings this low, this early, you look at where the generic ballot is this low this early, that usually spells doom for the president’s party when it comes to the midterm elections.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Discontent: The Plummet of Trump's Approval Ratings

In a recent segment on CNN's Inside Politics, a stark picture was painted of President Donald Trump's current standing with the American public. With a reported approval rating of a mere 41%, Trump's favorability seems to have nosedived, marking him as the president with the "lowest" approval at this stage in his term "in the history of modern polling." This figure is not just a number; it is a glaring red flag signaling a profound disenchantment among the American populace.

The Numbers Game: A Failing Report Card

According to data analyst Harry Enten, comparing Trump's current approval rating with that of his early presidency highlights a clear decline. From 44% around the 100-day mark in 2017 to now 41%, the drop, while seemingly small, is significantly telling in the realm of political capital. But the issue runs deeper than mere percentages. A staggering "54 percent of Americans have no confidence that Trump will use the power of the presidency responsibly." This statistic is not merely a setback; it is a scathing indictment of Trump's tenure and his perceived incapacity to wield presidential power with judiciousness and integrity.

Economic Advantage: Lost in Translation

Molly Ball from the Wall Street Journal brings to the forefront another critical aspect of Trump's dwindling support base — his waning "signature advantage" on the economy. Once a bulwark of his presidency, the economy's downturn under his watch has become a "blinking red light" for Republicans. This shift is not just a blow to Trump's image as a savvy businessman-turned-politician; it represents a seismic shift in public trust and confidence in his leadership abilities.

The Midterm Election Forewarning

Enten's analysis offers a grim prognosis for the Republican Party's chances in the 2026 midterm elections if Trump's approval rating remains static. The assertion that there is "pretty much zero chance" for the Republicans to hold onto the House should serve as a clarion call. Historical precedence supports this claim, underscoring the uphill battle the GOP faces if current trends persist. The low approval ratings coupled with unfavorable generic ballots spell potential doom for the party's aspirations to maintain control.

The Verdict: A Presidency in Peril

The narrative that unfolds from the CNN segment is not just a story of declining numbers. It is a testament to a presidency grappling with its own contradictions and controversies. Trump's leadership, once heralded for its unconventional approach, now appears to be hemorrhaging support, even among those who once propelled him to the highest office in the land.

The article alludes to broader implications, hinting at a disillusioned electorate that is growing weary of Trump's antics. From threatening the toy industry ahead of the holiday shopping season to legal doctrines that could potentially topple his presidency, the litany of issues seems endless. Trump's presidency, described by some as "drowning in hubris," is at a critical juncture.

As the 2026 midterms approach, the Republican Party must reckon with the reality of Trump's sagging approval ratings and their implications. The party's future, much like Trump's presidency, hangs in the balance, teetering on the precipice of public opinion and political viability. The message is clear: adapt and address the underlying causes of discontent, or face the electoral consequences. In the end, the numbers do not lie, and for Trump and the GOP, the numbers are indeed "horrible, awful, awful."
