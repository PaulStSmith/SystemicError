'Grotesque': South African-born cleric unloads 'holy anger' at Trump

The Reverend Nontombi Naomi Tutu torched the Trump administration in a blistering op-ed Friday that denounced a decision to fast-track refugee status for white South Africans – which she blasted as a “grotesque falsification” of what it means to seek refuge.

Tutu, a South African-born Episcopal priest in Atlanta, praised the church’s decision to cut ties with a federal refugee resettlement program rather than “elevated a lie that will affect refugee resettlement for years to come.”

“The Episcopal Church has taken a moral stand,” she wrote Friday in an MSNBC op-ed. “The Boers who arrived on U.S. soil this week are not refugees. They are white people using their privilege to leap over legitimate refugees who have been waiting to escape political repression and life-threatening situations.”

Tutu, a dual citizen of the U.S. and South African, then scorched the MAGA administration.

“In welcoming them and expediting access to the U.S., the Trump administration has proved its racist bona fides,” Tutu wrote. “It has stopped the asylum of Afghans and Iraqis who fought alongside American troops only to resettle a group that views the loss of absolute domination of South Africa’s Black majority as oppression.”

She went on to express her deep anger over the issue, while acknowledging she was “glaringly aware of how my anger will be perceived.”

“I considered tempering it. But this is a holy anger,” Tutu wrote. “And if I silence it, I harm not only myself but all those who are harmed by this grotesque falsification of what it means to be a refugee.”

Tutu concluded her piece Friday with a call to action.

“Far too often, we as the human family look back at injustices and evil and say, ‘We did not know,’” she told readers. “Future generations will not allow us to make that claim about our inaction in 2025.”

Our Analysis:

The Reverend Nontombi Naomi Tutu's Fiery Rebuttal to Refugee Policy

The Reverend Nontombi Naomi Tutu does not mince words in her scathing op-ed published on MSNBC, where she vehemently condemns the Trump administration's decision to fast-track refugee status for white South Africans. This decision, according to Tutu, is not just a policy misstep; it is a "grotesque falsification" of what it means to seek refuge, wrapped neatly in the cloak of white privilege and racism.

A Moral Stand Against a Racist Policy

Tutu praises the Episcopal Church's decision to sever ties with a federal refugee resettlement program, a program that has now been tainted by the administration's decision to prioritize white South Africans over other refugees in dire situations. This action by the church is hailed as a moral victory, a beacon of hope in a sea of morally bankrupt policies that elevate a dangerous lie.

The core of Tutu's argument lies in the undeniable fact that the individuals arriving from South Africa this week are not refugees in the traditional sense. These are individuals who, as Tutu puts it, are "using their privilege to leap over legitimate refugees" waiting to escape genuine political repression and life-threatening situations. This is not just an abuse of the system; it is a mockery of the very essence of what it means to be a refugee.

The Trump Administration's Racist "Bona Fides"

Tutu does not shy away from calling out the Trump administration for what she views as its clearly racist agenda. By rolling out the red carpet for white South Africans while simultaneously halting the asylum process for Afghans and Iraqis who have risked their lives alongside American troops, the administration reveals its priorities loud and clear. This, according to Tutu, is nothing short of proving its "racist bona fides".

What is particularly striking in Tutu's op-ed is her articulation of what she refers to as a "holy anger". This is not mere frustration or political disagreement; this is a profound moral outrage at the injustice being perpetrated under the guise of refugee resettlement. Tutu refuses to temper her anger, recognizing that to do so would be to silence a necessary and powerful voice against oppression.

A Call to Action

Tutu concludes her piece with a poignant call to action, urging readers not to allow future generations to look back at this moment with the excuse of ignorance. The injustices and evils of today's policies should not be something we passively acknowledge in hindsight. The urgency in her message is clear: the time to act is now, lest we find ourselves complicit through inaction.

In Summary

The Reverend Nontombi Naomi Tutu's op-ed is a fierce critique of a deeply flawed policy that favors white privilege over genuine need, wrapped in a broader condemnation of the Trump administration's approach to refugee resettlement. Her message is a rallying cry for those who believe in justice, equality, and the true meaning of refuge. In a world where policies are too often dictated by racism and privilege, Tutu's voice is a clarion call for moral clarity and action.
