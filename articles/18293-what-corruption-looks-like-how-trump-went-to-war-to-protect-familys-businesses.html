'What corruption looks like': How Trump 'went to war' to protect family’s businesses

Unveiling the Veil: Trump’s Administration and the Betting Market Manipulation

A Clear Conflict of Interest

In the latest expose from the Popular Information substack, it’s clear that President Donald Trump’s administration is not just watching the betting market but is actively shaping it to benefit personal and familial business interests. The report sheds light on how Trump, his son Donald Trump Jr., and their allies in the industry are immersed in the affairs of prediction market platforms like Kalshi, Polymarket, and Crypto.com. This isn’t a subplot in the grand political narrative; it’s a glaring example of how power is wielded to protect and proliferate personal wealth under the guise of regulatory oversight.

The Power Play Behind Legal Shields

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), under Trump’s directive, is counter-suing states that attempt to regulate these betting platforms. This strategic move to file counter lawsuits against Arizona, Connecticut, and Illinois, who have accused these platforms of operating illegal gambling sites, reveals a disturbing misuse of federal power to buoy private ventures. By framing these services as “distinct from traditional gambling,” both the platforms and the Trump-led CFTC are parroting a crafted narrative aimed at sidestepping state laws designed to protect the public from unchecked gambling operations.

Network of Influence and Investment

The network of financial and advisory connections between the Trump family and these betting platforms is extensively mapped out. With Trump Jr. as a paid advisor and major investor, and significant business flowing between Trump Media and Crypto.com, the lines between personal profit and public duty are not just blurred—they’re obliterated. Additionally, influential figures like Peter Thiel and firms like Andreessen Horowitz, who have openly supported Trump’s political ambitions, are deeply invested in these markets, further entangling financial gains with political favors.

Misdirection and the Real Stakeholders

The narrative that these prediction markets are fundamentally different from gambling is a calculated misdirection. It shifts focus from the core issue: a powerful family and its allies using federal power to sculpt a market in which they have deep financial stakes. This isn’t just about market regulation; it’s about who controls the market and who benefits from its profits. The real stakeholders, the public, whose interests these state laws aim to protect, are sidelined in the power play.

Systemic Insight: Power and Profit Over Policy

This scenario is a textbook case of how political power is used to shape policies and regulations not for the public good but for personal gain. The Trump administration’s actions reflect a broader, troubling pattern where government authority is leveraged to serve the financial interests of those in power, at the expense of lawful state-level governance and public welfare. This intertwining of business with government underlines the urgent need for robust mechanisms to ensure that those in power cannot easily manipulate the system for personal gain.

In sum, the betting market saga is not just a story about gambling; it’s about how democratic institutions are being manipulated to serve the oligarchs. The real bet being made is by the Trump administration on the public’s passivity in the face of such overt corruption.
