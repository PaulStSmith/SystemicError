Trump and Netanyahu don't want you to see the true reasons for their attack on Iran

Donald Trump and Benjamin Netanyahu’s attack on Iran is premised on a gossamer web of assumptions and inferences.

Trump says Iran has enough nuclear material to build a bomb within days, will soon have long-range missiles capable of hitting the United States, and plans an attack. But he has offered no evidence. Most experts say he’s wrong.

Here’s the real reason for this war. Trump wants it to divert Americans’ attention from everything that’s gone to s--- on his watch: the economy, ICE’s cruel raids and murders, the crisis in public health as exemplified by the measles epidemic, our loss of friends and allies around the world, his boundless corruption, and his increasing unpopularity as shown in plummeting polls.

Oh, and there are the Epstein files, rapidly closing in on the man whose history of sexual assaults and braggadocio make his complicity highly likely.

Netanyahu is also using this war as a giant diversion. He doesn’t want the world to dwell on the genocide in Gaza and the West Bank.

As former Israeli prime minister Ehud Olmert wrote recently, “A violent and criminal effort is under way to ethnically cleanse territories in the West Bank. Gangs of armed settlers persecute, harm, wound and even kill Palestinians living there.”

Like Trump, Netanyahu has been trampling constitutional rights — seeking a judicial coup to eliminate the separation of powers, purging Israel’s independent attorney general of his powers, trying to dismiss his own corruption trial, and politicizing appointments to what had been a neutral civil service.

Trump and Netanyahu are using the same authoritarian playbook.

A big part of that playbook is war. War takes over the news. War blots out criticism. War divides a nation’s people, subjecting those against it to being called unpatriotic. War grants leaders all sorts of emergency powers. War consumes everything else.

We mustn’t let this war do so.

I finally watched a tape of Trump’s State of the Union address (I couldn’t bring myself to watch it at the time). It was even more horrendous than I’d imagined.

What stood out for me was all the important problems Trump didn’t mention, as if they didn’t exist. Climate change. Widening inequality. Monopolies driving up prices. Declining real incomes. The growing scourges of poverty — homelessness, hunger, disease, and violence — in America and around the world. Unregulated AI.

If and when he ever mentions them, he calls them “hoaxes.”

Instead, he’s worsened all of them — helping fossil fuels while killing off wind and solar, eviscerating antitrust enforcement and letting monopolies consume entire industries, giving the rich more tax cuts while cutting back Medicaid and food stamps, destroying USAID and discouraging lifesaving vaccines while letting measles run rampant.

And he’s trying to divert attention to fake problems: non-Americans voting in elections (they don’t), Greenland and Venezuela (they pose no threat), “disloyal” Americans who criticize him or judges who try to hold him accountable (thank goodness they’re still trying).

And now, the biggest diversion of all: full-scale war in the Middle East.

Hopefully, the casualties will be limited. Hopefully, Americans will see through this. Hopefully, this will strengthen the resistance to Trump. Hopefully, it will lead to an even greater landslide victory for Democrats and independents in the midterm elections — if Trump allows midterm elections.

Please remain hopeful. Don’t give in to war fever. Stay strong. Be safe. Hug your loved ones.

Our Analysis:

Trump and Netanyahu’s War: A Masterclass in Diversion

The recent article shedding light on Donald Trump and Benjamin Netanyahu’s rationale for attacking Iran exposes not just a precarious leap of logic but a terrifying glimpse into how modern-day leaders manipulate global politics and public perception to mask their failures. The claims made by Trump about Iran’s nuclear capabilities and intentions are not just unsupported by evidence; they’re categorically disputed by experts. Yet, the narrative pushes forward, unencumbered by truth or reality.

The Distraction Doctrine

At the heart of Trump’s strategy lies a cynical, yet time-tested tactic: diversion. With a presidency plagued by economic turmoil, human rights abuses, public health crises, and an erosion of international alliances, Trump has seemingly found his panacea in war. It’s a maneuver as transparent as it is reckless. The suggestion that a conflict with Iran could somehow overshadow the myriad disasters of his administration is not just wishful thinking—it’s a dangerous gamble with human lives.

Netanyahu, for his part, mirrors this strategy. Facing his own domestic turmoil and international condemnation for the treatment of Palestinians, a war serves as the perfect smokescreen. The audacity of both leaders to exploit military conflict as a political tool is not only morally reprehensible but indicative of a broader, more insidious trend in global leadership.

Authoritarian Playbook 101

The parallels between Trump and Netanyahu extend beyond their mutual penchant for diversion. Both are walking a dangerous line towards authoritarianism, with attempts to undermine constitutional rights, manipulate judicial systems, and dismantle checks on their power. It’s a playbook that is as effective as it is frightening, with war serving as the ultimate chapter. In rallying a nation to war, they seek to silence dissent, expand their powers, and distract from their failures.

The irony of Trump’s State of the Union address cannot be overstated. As he stood before the nation, a litany of unaddressed crises lay bare: climate change, income inequality, monopolies, and a growing underclass living in dire conditions. Yet, these issues find no champion in Trump. Instead, they’re dismissed as hoaxes, while he exacerbates them with policies that favor the wealthy and corporations.

The Illusion of Crisis

Trump’s mastery of creating and then combating imaginary threats is nothing short of a political magic trick. The specter of non-Americans voting, the demonization of Venezuela and Greenland, and the vilification of critics and judges are all part of a broader strategy to distract and divide. And now, the pièce de résistance: a full-scale war in the Middle East, presented as a necessary response to manufactured crises.

A Call to Action

Robert Reich’s concluding plea for hope and resistance is a beacon in these troubling times. The hope is that the American public, and indeed the world, will see through this thinly veiled attempt to divert attention from the real issues plaguing society. The upcoming midterm elections present an opportunity for a collective reckoning. The challenge is significant, especially considering the possibility that Trump might seek to undermine the electoral process itself.

In Conclusion

The article serves not just as a critique of Trump and Netanyahu’s war-mongering but as a clarion call to resist the allure of diversionary tactics. It’s a reminder that in the face of manufactured wars and crises, the real battles—for justice, equality, and democracy—must continue. The stakes could not be higher, and the need for vigilance, engagement, and resistance has never been more urgent.
