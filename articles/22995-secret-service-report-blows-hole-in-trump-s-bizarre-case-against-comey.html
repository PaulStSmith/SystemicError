Secret Service report blows hole in Trump's bizarre case against Comey

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosSeptember 3, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The PretextWho Has PowerThe Real RecordMisdirection by DesignThe PatternWhat This Reveals

Prosecuting a Seashell, Protecting a President

The Pretext

Trump says James Comey threatened his life by posting seashells arranged to read “8647.” The new Secret Service material cuts against that story: the case was marked “non-referred,” and participants inside the probe described parts of the response as “overkill” and “legally questionable.” That is the key fact. Not whether the post was stupid. Whether the state was being used to inflate stupidity into danger.

Who Has Power

The power in this story sits with Trump and the machinery around him, especially Attorney General Todd Blanche, who repeated the threat narrative in public and presented it as a serious prosecutorial theory. Comey is the weak actor here: a former official with no institutional leverage, now being recast as a menace because the president needs an enemy that can be criminalized. The post is not the source of the power. The state response is.

The Real Record

The source material also points to why Trump’s obsession with Comey is political, not personal. CBS reports that Trump remains angry because Comey’s actions helped trigger the FBI’s Russia investigation, which followed Trump’s firing of Comey in 2017. That is the deeper grievance: not a seashell post, but a former FBI director whose decisions exposed the Trump presidency to scrutiny. The White House cannot erase that history, so it tries to bury it under a security panic.

Misdirection by Design

This is what propaganda looks like in an advanced form. A flimsy image gets converted into a mortal threat. A controversial investigation gets recast as evidence of persecution. A government report that undercuts the case gets treated as background noise. The point is not to persuade every reader. The point is to flood the zone with enough accusation that accountability starts to look like victimhood. Blanche’s testimony about a “body of evidence” is part of that laundering process.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is familiar: power manufactures danger, then claims the right to punish it. Trump’s people are not simply reacting to a post. They are trying to build a state narrative in which loyalty to Trump is synonymous with national security and prior investigations into Trump become proof of malice. That is the authoritarian move in plain view: not law enforcement, but revenge wearing a badge.

What This Reveals

The story is not about seashells. It is about a presidency that treats criticism as threat, uses institutions to pursue grudges, and demands that prosecutors pretend a weak case is a strong one. The Secret Service report matters because it shows even within the apparatus there was recognition that this was not serious. The danger is not that Comey spelled out the wrong number. The danger is that Trump can still turn the state into a grievance machine and call it justice.
