Don't Pop the Champagne Yet: Amy Coney Barrett's Moment of Sanity Doesn't Erase the Record

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 17, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

This week, a flicker of judicial restraint from the U.S. Supreme Court sent certain corners of the internet into a cautious celebration. During oral arguments in a case with potentially seismic consequences for birthright citizenship, Justice Amy Coney Barrett—yes, that Amy Coney Barrett, handpicked by Donald Trump to help cement a hard-right majority on the Court—raised alarm bells about the implications of her own side's argument. Specifically, she warned that adopting the Trump-aligned stance could lead to millions of individuals being stripped of their constitutional rights, all without a shred of legal recourse.

Cue the liberal wishful thinking. The memes flooded in. The celebratory tweets. The triumphant posts claiming a "twist ending" in the making—Barrett, the unexpected guardian of immigrant rights! But let’s pump the brakes.

That sound you heard? It wasn’t the tectonic plates of jurisprudence shifting. It was the sound of people grasping for hope in a court dominated by ideologues. Let’s not mistake a well-worded question during oral arguments for a genuine pivot in philosophy. One moment of reasoned skepticism doesn’t change Barrett’s deeply reactionary track record.

Let’s be crystal clear: it’s good—refreshing, even—that she raised these concerns. It’s rare to see any pushback on far-right overreach from this Court, and rarer still from one of its most ideologically consistent members. But before you start pouring champagne into your Ruth Bader Ginsburg commemorative flute, remember who Barrett is and what she stands for. This is the same woman who joined the majority to decimate Roe v. Wade, who’s nodded along with decisions that erode voting rights, who’s treated corporate power as sacrosanct, and whose judicial compass consistently points to theocratic originalism.

This isn’t a moment to celebrate. It’s a moment to document—and to stay alert. Because the Supreme Court’s right flank has a long, cynical history of theatrical questioning during oral arguments that has zero bearing on the opinions they later hand down. They lull the public into false hope with sharp critiques and probing hypotheticals, then issue rulings that are devastating in their scope and ideological purity. It’s a political shell game cloaked in the language of jurisprudence.

Conservative justices, especially those groomed by the Federalist Society machine, know how to perform nuance. They know how to sound balanced. But when the decisions land, they rarely break from the agenda that got them appointed in the first place: dismantling hard-won rights, weakening regulatory protections, and shoring up minority rule through judicial fiat.

This court is not moderate. It is not an impartial arbiter floating above the political fray. It is a captured institution, and despite what one viral image or soundbite might suggest, Amy Coney Barrett hasn’t suddenly discovered a burning commitment to civil rights in a forgotten corner of her chambers.

So let’s watch what they do, not what they say. Because when it comes to human rights—especially the rights of immigrants, marginalized communities, women, and workers—the conservative majority has made one thing excruciatingly clear: they are not here to defend those rights. They are here to dismantle them, brick by brick, precedent by precedent.

If Barrett ultimately votes against this naked attempt to shred the Fourteenth Amendment, she will have done the bare minimum. And yes, we will note it. But we won't canonize her for doing what every justice—left, right, or center—should do as a matter of basic constitutional duty.

So, stay vigilant. Stay critical. Pay attention not to the soundbites, but to the decisions. Because when the champagne cork pops prematurely, it’s often followed by a gavel dropping—and with it, another hard blow to justice.

Don’t trust the robed executioners just because one of them momentarily blinked.
