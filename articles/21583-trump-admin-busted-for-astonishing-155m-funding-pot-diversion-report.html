Trump admin busted for astonishing $155M ‘funding pot’ diversion: report

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20263 min read

The Earmark Was Never the Point

A Pretext, Then a Reroute

The reported story is simple: Congress set aside $170 million to monitor alleged illegal Chinese fishing, and the Trump administration spent most of it somewhere else. About $155 million went to counter-drone technology for events like World Cup matches and America250 tributes, with smaller amounts shuffled to Coast Guard communications and a Merchant Marine credentialing system. That is the surface fact. The political fact is uglier: the money moved from one declared threat to a more convenient set of projects after the appropriation was already sold.

Power Sat With the Executive

This was not an accident of bookkeeping. The power to divert the funds sat with the Department of Homeland Security under Kristi Noem. Congress, through Ted Cruz, helped write the check and package the optics. The executive branch then decided what the money would actually do. That is the real chain of responsibility: lawmakers manufactured a security claim, and the administration treated the earmark as flexible political currency.

Cruz’s admission that “the bulk of the funding has not gone where it needed to go” is the kind of complaint that tries to sound shocked while preserving the system that made the theft possible. He secured the money, celebrated the posture, and now acts startled that an administration built on contempt for accountability did what it always does: repurpose public dollars toward whatever serves its own priorities.

Security Theater Wins Again

The original justification was maritime monitoring of Chinese fishing vessels. The destination was counter-drone gear for showcase events and patriotic pageantry. That tells you everything about the hierarchy of needs in this government. Real enforcement against an externalized threat was secondary to visible domestic spectacle. Security became a branding exercise.

This is not just waste. It is misdirection with a budget line attached. The administration kept the language of national defense while spending on infrastructure that protects events and symbols. That is how political actors launder indifference: they leave the rhetoric intact, then redirect the resources toward what is politically useful, not what was promised.

The Same Bill, the Same Class Logic

The funding diversion sits inside the larger structure of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act: cuts to social safety net programs, a permanent extension of 2017 Trump tax cuts, and a fiscal design that the Congressional Budget Office said could increase the national debt by as much as $4.7 trillion through 2035. That is the governing logic in plain view. Public scarcity for the many, durable tax privilege for the rich, and elastic “security” spending that can be reassigned after the fact.

The bill’s beneficiaries are not mysterious. The source notes that the majority of the tax-cut benefits go to the top 1% of earners. So the same political operation that strips support from ordinary people also preserves room for executive improvisation when elite priorities shift. That is not fiscal discipline. It is class discipline.

Accountability, as Designed, Goes Missing

Markwayne Mullin, the current DHS secretary, did not respond. Noem no longer holds the post, but the damage is in the structure, not the individual. The agency can be used as a routing mechanism; Congress can pretend the appropriation was specific; the public is left with an after-the-fact revelation and no meaningful correction. That is how institutional cowardice works when everyone involved benefits from pretending the original story was sincere.

The article’s framing is weak if it treats this as a peculiar diversion instead of a method. The method is familiar: declare a threat, authorize money, redirect the money, then offer a fog of administrative silence when the allocation no longer matches the sales pitch.

What This Reveals

This is a story about how power moves when it is not watched closely enough to be embarrassed. Congress authorizes, the executive repurposes, and the public gets a lesson in what security language is for: not always to solve a problem, but to create a politically defensible path for spending and control.

The broader pattern is not fiscal sloppiness. It is an anti-accountability machine that can promise one thing, deliver another, and hide the switch inside procedural authority. That is how the state becomes less answerable without becoming less active.
