Trump claims US now has 'permanent control' in Greenland and a large force is coming

Based on an article by Daniel Hampton

Sovereignty by Press Release

The Claim Came Before the Agreement

Donald Trump says the United States has secured permanent control over Greenland’s security and “all other needs,” with unlimited authority to decide what happens on the island. Denmark and Greenland have confirmed no such arrangement. No text has been released. The governments supposedly surrendering this power have not said they surrendered it.

That is the essential fact. The president announced dominion first and left everyone else to argue over whether it exists.

Washington Holds the Leverage

This is not a dispute among equal parties. The United States commands the military power, economic weight, and alliance machinery behind the pressure campaign. Trump has already threatened Denmark and seven other European countries with escalating tariffs unless a deal was reached for Greenland’s purchase. He later withdrew that threat and pledged not to use military force, but coercion does not disappear merely because its author temporarily changes instruments.

Greenlanders have been considerably clearer than the White House. Their prime minister has called sovereignty non-negotiable. Eighty-five percent opposed joining the United States in a January 2025 poll. Roughly 4,000 people—about a quarter of Nuuk—joined the largest protest in Greenland’s history.

Trump’s answer to that expressed will is to announce “complete ability” over their territory.

The Misdirection Is the Deal

Coverage that treats this as diplomatic confusion misses the political method. The conflicting accounts are not incidental noise around an agreement; they are central to how power is being exercised.

After one meeting, Denmark described exploratory talks about American security concerns. The White House described technical discussions about acquiring Greenland. Trump previously announced another framework granting “total access,” only for Denmark and Greenland to reaffirm that sovereignty was not negotiable. The details never appeared.

Now the claim has expanded into permanent control at “NO COST.” That phrase gives away the sales pitch: imperial authority presented as a bargain for Americans, with Greenlanders reduced to an expense column that somehow totals zero.

Consent Has Been Edited Out

Trump’s language recasts a populated, self-governing territory as strategic real estate awaiting competent management. Greenlandic consent becomes an inconvenience. Danish authority becomes an obstacle. American desire becomes the only fact that matters.

That is authoritarian political language: power declares an outcome, treats affected people as subordinate, and burdens them with disproving the declaration. Even the name “Infinite Life” attempts to brand away the central question—who agreed, through what lawful mechanism, and with whose consent?

Security concerns may be real. They do not grant Washington ownership rights. A military interest is not a deed.

Allies Are Responding to Coercion

Denmark has deployed troops to Greenland in an operation involving roughly a dozen European countries. Canada and France have opened consulates in Nuuk. Those moves reflect a wider consequence of Trump’s campaign: allied governments are reinforcing their presence around territory coveted by the president of the United States.

Yet Trump presents the episode as unilateral mastery over foreign adversaries. The more immediate rupture is within the alliance itself. NATO partners are being compelled to defend the sovereignty of an allied territory against the declared ambitions of NATO’s most powerful member.

The Empire of the Unpublished Agreement

The larger pattern is government by assertion. Announce a sweeping victory, withhold the document, inflate exploratory talks into submission, and force weaker parties to respond inside the reality Washington has fabricated.

Whether this particular claim survives contact with Denmark, Greenland, or an actual text remains unconfirmed. Its political purpose is already visible. Trump is normalizing the proposition that American power can override the stated wishes of Greenlanders and that sovereignty becomes negotiable whenever a stronger state calls domination “security.”

The systemic error is not mere diplomatic sloppiness. It is the habit of treating power as proof: if the president can announce control loudly enough, the people whose country is being discussed are expected to become footnotes.
