Trump busted for claiming credit for something he didn't do

The President Who Pretends to Run Walmart

The Stunt

Donald Trump posted that Walmart was lowering prices “at my administration’s request,” then tied the move to his 250th birthday vanity project. Catherine Rampell called out the obvious problem: Walmart runs promotions all the time, and seasonal discounts are not a presidential achievement. That is the whole game here. Trump takes ordinary retail behavior, slaps his name on it, and hopes the performance outruns the facts.

Who Actually Decides

Walmart sets Walmart’s prices. Not the White House. Not a press release. Not a social post written for applause. The company has its own business model, its own promotions, and its own calendar. When a Walmart spokesperson had to correct Trump in January after he falsely claimed the company had closed more than 250 stores in California, that was not a misunderstanding. It was a reminder that the president can broadcast nonsense, but he does not get to rewrite corporate reality.

Credit Without Responsibility

Trump’s habit is not just exaggeration. It is a political method: seize credit for what others were already doing and disown the costs of what he actually controls. Rampell pointed to the Alabama aluminum plant, Corning, J&J, Stargate, SoftBank, and Apple as part of the same pattern. The common thread is simple. If something good happens nearby, Trump claims authorship. If his own policies create damage, he hides behind noise, branding, and denial.

Tariffs Are Not Strategy

The more serious story is not the Walmart stunt. It is the economic uncertainty Trump is actively manufacturing. Walmart’s CEO told him his tariff policy was “not good for anybody.” Former Commerce official Robert Shapiro explained why: businesses make investments based on expected costs and demand, and Trump’s trade chaos undermines both. That is not abstract policy debate. It is a direct attack on planning, investment, and worker stability, dressed up as toughness.

The Misdirection

This is what happens when the White House is run like a content farm. Trump is not governing; he is narrating himself as the cause of everything that flatters him. Rampell’s “influencer-in-chief” line is accurate, but the deeper issue is power without accountability. He can pressure institutions rhetorically, claim victories he did not earn, and use the presidential seal as a prop for personal branding.

The Pattern

The pattern is authoritarian in miniature: concentrate attention on the leader, erase the real decision-makers, and confuse spectacle for power. Trump’s media trick works because it treats the public like an audience and the economy like a backdrop. But the facts keep leaking through. Retail promotions are not presidential feats. Tariffs do create uncertainty. And when a president lies this casually about something as basic as a store discount, he is not merely mistaken. He is training the country to accept fraud as governance.
