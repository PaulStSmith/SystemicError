Washing Dishes Under the Gun: The Militarization of Labor and the War on the Working Class

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Weapon of ChoiceThe Spectacle of ForceWho’s the Real Threat?From Enforcement to OccupationThe Price of SilenceConclusion: This Is a Warning

“Why is this type of weapon necessary to detain individuals washing dishes?”

That question, posed by James Tate, does more than point out the absurdity of militarized policing. It slices through the polished veneer of law and order and exposes the rot beneath. Because make no mistake—“washing dishes” isn’t just an innocent description of someone scrubbing plates at a diner. It’s a razor-sharp euphemism. It means immigrants. It means Latinx laborers. It means the underpaid, undocumented backbone of the American service industry.

It means the people who clean hotel rooms you trashed, prep your meals, pick your vegetables, and yes—wash the dishes—so the rest of the country can pretend it’s civilized.

So when a heavily armed Homeland Security officer rolls into a residential neighborhood carrying what appears to be a six-shot 40mm launcher—designed for war zones, not kitchens—and aims it at a population whose most dangerous act is overstay a visa or cross a border in search of work, you’re not witnessing law enforcement. You’re witnessing a class execution in slow motion.

This isn’t about crime. It’s not even about immigration. It’s about optics. It’s about power. It’s about reminding the people who do the dirty work that they’re never truly safe—because in America, the person who scrubs your floors can be treated like a terrorist if they don’t have the right paperwork.

The state doesn’t just deport immigrants anymore. It hunts them. With tactical teams. With armored vehicles. With flashbangs and assault rifles. It rips them from homes, schools, workplaces—and yes, kitchens—under the same operational protocols it would use against cartel leaders. The line between immigration enforcement and counterinsurgency has all but evaporated.

And what makes it even more perverse is that these people are not outsiders. They’re already part of the American economy—just not the part that gets bailouts, bonuses, or benefits. They’re the exploited underclass, the labor without rights. And when the state rolls out grenade launchers and militarized raids against dishwashers, janitors, and farmhands, it’s making something brutally clear: servitude is acceptable—but visibility is not.

This isn’t law and order. This is modern feudalism with body armor and DHS patches.

The Weapon of Choice

Let’s talk about the gear. The officer in the image is gripping a 40mm multi-shot grenade launcher—likely an M32 or one of its variants. This is not some specialized breaching tool. It’s a weapon made for the battlefield, capable of launching explosive ordnance or so-called “less-lethal” rounds like tear gas, smoke, or rubber bullets. These munitions are intended for crowd control, not neighborhood policing.

Except that in the U.S., neighborhoods are treated like hostile territory—especially if they’re filled with Black and Brown people.

Police departments acquired billions of dollars in military surplus through programs like 1033, turning beat cops into spec ops cosplayers. The result? A generation of officers trained to view communities as warzones and civilians as enemy combatants.

And when you arm a police force like an occupying army, don’t be surprised when they behave like one.

The Spectacle of Force

We must ask: why would a Department of Homeland Security officer walk into a neighborhood carrying a literal grenade launcher to apprehend a dishwasher?

Because it sends a message. Not just to the target, but to every neighbor, coworker, and child watching through a cracked window:

We can come for you. At any time. With any weapon. And there’s nothing you can do.

This is psychological warfare. It turns arrests into acts of terror. It deters solidarity, erodes trust, and paralyzes entire communities with fear.

The goal isn’t just compliance. It’s submission.

Who’s the Real Threat?

Let’s put this in perspective. The United States has no shortage of actual threats—violent extremists, armed militias, white supremacists stockpiling weapons, domestic terrorists with manifestos.

But none of those get a six-barrel grenade launcher at their doorstep.

Those people get negotiations. Those people get months of surveillance. Those people get leniency.

But a dishwasher? A maid? A landscaper?

They get a raid at dawn.

Why? Because they’re low-hanging fruit. Because they’re vulnerable. Because they can’t lawyer up. Because their visibility is enough to trigger panic in the xenophobic underbelly of American politics.

They’re not threatening anyone. They’re threatening a narrative.

And narratives are what empires kill to protect.

From Enforcement to Occupation

There’s a reason this scene looks more like a military patrol in Kabul than a routine arrest in Southern California. It’s because immigration enforcement has ceased to be about policy and has become performance.

Every raid, every convoy, every televised arrest is staged to demonstrate power. Not federal competence—but raw, unrestrained dominance.

ICE isn’t functioning like a law enforcement agency. It’s behaving like an internal security force in a collapsing state—the kind of force that doesn’t just apply the law, but invents it on the fly, and backs it up with militarized violence.

This is the territory of fascism.

The Price of Silence

Perhaps most damning is the silence that follows. No breaking news alerts. No bipartisan outrage. No prime-time condemnation.

Just another tweet. Just another image. Just another dishwasher in the crosshairs of a launcher.

Why? Because the victims aren’t photogenic. Because they don’t have lobbyists. Because they don’t make campaign donations.

Because this is who America is willing to sacrifice to preserve its fantasy of control.

And when we allow weapons of war to be used against the hands that feed us, clean for us, build for us—we reveal what our democracy actually values: power over people.

Conclusion: This Is a Warning

The next time someone says “we don’t need immigration reform,” show them this picture.

The next time someone insists “police don’t use excessive force,” show them the launcher.

And the next time someone claims “this is the land of the free,” ask them why freedom needs to be enforced at grenade-point.

Because the truth is this: when the state brings the machinery of war to a kitchen job, it is not enforcing the law.

It is practicing authoritarianism.

And if we don’t stop it, it won’t end with immigrants.

It never does.
