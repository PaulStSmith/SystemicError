Over the weekend, Trump posted a video of him flying a fighter jet named “King Trump” over what appears to be the “No Kings” protests that more than 7 million Americans participated in. Throughout the video, Trump dumps excrement on protesters, including Sisson.

Sisson, 23, discussed the video in a new episode of “The Daily Beast Podcast” on Monday.

“I don’t have a particular answer as to why I’m getting under their skin, I think that they are really threatened by what they saw on ‘No King’s Day,’” Sisson said. “And I think they’re really threatened by the wins the Democratic Party has had, not only in media recently, but also in courts and things like that.”

“They’re running scared,” he continued. “Trump has a habit of when he’s panicked and really nervous of just lashing out.”

Trump and Republicans like Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) called the “No Kings” protests a “Hate America Rally” ahead of the protests.

Experts have suggested the protest, which was one of the largest in American history, exposed the Trump administration’s vulnerability to public pushback.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s AI-Generated Antics: A Desperate Flail or Calculated Intimidation?

In a political landscape that often feels like a circus, the latest act by President Donald Trump takes the absurdity to new heights. Or, should we say, new lows? The spectacle in question is an AI-generated video posted by Trump himself, featuring the President as the pilot of a fighter jet—dubbed “King Trump”—engaging in the juvenile act of dumping excrement on protesters. Among the targets was Democratic influencer Harry Sisson, who recently shared his thoughts on this bizarre and frankly, disturbing display on “The Daily Beast Podcast.”

The Disturbing Imagery and Its Implications

Let’s dissect the grotesque imagery here. Trump, a figure who should embody the values of dignity and leadership, instead opts to literally defile his opponents in a digital fantasy. This isn’t just a poor taste joke; it’s a vivid illustration of how Trump views dissent: something to be attacked, ridiculed, and demeaned. It’s a clear indicator of his disdain for the democratic process and those who dare to challenge him.

The “No Kings” Protests: A Thorn in Trump’s Side

The context of this video cannot be ignored. It was a response to the “No Kings” protests, a massive movement drawing over 7 million Americans to the streets. The name itself—a direct rebuke to the autocratic tendencies displayed by Trump and his administration—suggests a populace fed up with a government that increasingly seeks to consolidate power and stifle opposition.

Sisson’s analysis hits the nail on the head. Trump’s actions reek of desperation. The “No Kings” protests, along with recent Democratic victories in media and court battles, have clearly struck a nerve. Trump and his ilk, including Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) who disparagingly called the protests a “Hate America Rally,” are showing their fear. Not fear of losing power per se, but fear of a populace that is increasingly aware, mobilized, and resistant to their authoritarian impulses.

The Bigger Picture: Attack on Democratic Norms

This is far more than a petty squabble or a meme-worthy moment of political theater. It’s emblematic of a broader assault on democratic norms and values. Trump’s willingness to engage in and promote such a vile depiction of his opponents is not just an attack on Sisson or the protesters—it’s an attack on the very notion of peaceful protest and free speech. By creating an us-versus-them mentality, where opponents are not just wrong but are literally dehumanized and attacked, Trump is further polarizing an already divided nation.

Moreover, the use of AI to generate this video adds another layer of concern. The technology, which has the potential for great good, is here employed to spread hatred and division. It’s a stark reminder of the dual-edged sword that is digital innovation—a tool that can either uplift humanity or degrade it, depending on the hands that wield it.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

What we’re witnessing is not normal, and it should not be dismissed as just another Trump antic. It’s a clear signal that the fight for the soul of America is far from over. The “No Kings” protests and the victories in media and courts are significant, but they are merely battles in a larger war against a tide of authoritarianism and disrespect for democratic principles.

The response to Trump’s video should not be outrage alone but a renewed commitment to defending democracy. Every meme, every tweet, every AI-generated fantasy that seeks to undermine the principles of respect, dignity, and free expression must be met with unwavering resistance. The stakes are too high, and the battle too important, to do any less.
