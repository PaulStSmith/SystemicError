White House calls ex-CNN reporter 'scumbag' for noting Trump insults female journalists

The White House lashed out at former CNN correspondent Kate Bennett, calling her a “scumbag,” after she pointed to President Donald Trump’s habit of insulting female reporters.

“You know, everyone keeps writing these stories about how President Trump is only insulting female reporters by calling them piggy, ugly, stupid etc,” Bennett noted in a Friday post on X. “But, it’s actually because the women are the ones asking all the tough questions. Give that a thought.”

Several hours later, the official White House Rapid Response account responded to the former CNN reporter.

“Give this a thought: how big of a scumbag you must be to have been fired from CNN of all places,” the White House team wrote.

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt recently defended the president after he referred to a female reporter as “piggy.”

“Look, the president is very frank & honest with everyone in this room,” she insisted. “You’ve all seen it yourselves. You’ve all experienced it yourselves. And I think it’s one of the many reasons the American people reelected this president, because of his frankness.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A White House Masterclass

In a recent spectacle that could only ensue in the hallowed halls of the current administration, the White House has escalated its war against the press to new, and frankly, astonishing lows. At the heart of this latest debacle is former CNN correspondent Kate Bennett, who dared to highlight President Donald Trump’s particularly vicious streak towards female reporters. Bennett’s crime? She suggested, quite perceptively, that Trump’s barrage of insults towards women—ranging from “piggy” to “ugly” and “stupid”—might not be random acts of misogyny but rather a targeted attack on those who challenge him most effectively.

The White House’s Counterpunch: A Lesson in Unprofessionalism

In what can only be described as a knee-jerk reaction, the White House, through its Rapid Response account, stooped to calling Bennett a “scumbag”. This response, aside from being astonishingly unprofessional, serves as a stark reminder of how low our political discourse has sunk. The administration’s choice of words—especially the term “scumbag”—is not just a personal attack on Bennett but a signal flare for how this administration views and engages with its critics, especially women.

The Defense: A Thin Veil Over Misogyny

Karoline Leavitt, the White House Press Secretary, offered a defense so flimsy it would make Saran Wrap jealous. By praising the president’s “frankness”, Leavitt attempts to dress up what is clearly a pattern of misogyny as a virtue. The implication here is that disrespect and personal attacks are not only acceptable but commendable if cloaked under the guise of honesty. This defense, if one can call it that, is not just laughable—it’s an insult to the intelligence of the American public.

The Heart of the Matter: Dodging Accountability by Attacking the Messenger

Let’s be clear: the issue here is not about Trump’s “frankness”. It’s about a systematic attempt to undermine and discredit the press, particularly women in the press who dare to ask the hard questions. By focusing their counterattack on Bennett’s character and her professional history, the White House is engaging in a classic case of deflection. Instead of addressing the substance of Bennett’s argument, they’ve opted to shoot the messenger, hoping the message gets lost in the noise.

Conclusion: The Cost of Silence

In an era where political leaders are expected to maintain a certain level of decorum and respect, the current administration’s approach is not just disappointing; it’s dangerous. It sends a message that those in power can evade accountability through personal attacks and intimidation. What’s even more concerning is the silence from the president’s own party. Their unwillingness to condemn these actions suggests a complicity that transcends political allegiance—it’s about the basic principles of respect and decency.

In the end, this isn’t just about one reporter or one administration. It’s about the kind of society we want to be. Do we want leaders who lead with integrity and respect, or do we want bullies who can’t withstand scrutiny? The answer, for anyone who values democracy and decency, should be clear.
