The Realpolitik of Opportunism: Unpacking Trump’s Shift from Putin to Xi

Opportunism Over Ideology

Donald Trump’s apparent cooling on Vladimir Putin, as reported by Sarah Baxter in The i Paper, isn’t about ideological shifts or strategic realignments; it’s about opportunism. Trump, whose admiration for autocrats has been consistently clear, seems to recognize the diminishing returns of his association with Putin. The Russian leader’s faltering war effort in Ukraine and his reduced influence on the global stage has made him a less valuable ally. This isn’t about Trump suddenly embracing democratic ideals; it’s about abandoning a sinking ship for more buoyant vessels.

Power Play in the Global Arena

Trump’s readiness to engage with Chinese President Xi Jinping, juxtaposed with his distancing from Putin, illustrates a simple maxim: power attracts. As Putin’s “axis of influence” shrinks, Trump is pragmatically pivoting to China, where he seeks to secure a “clean win” ahead of the Beijing summit. This maneuvering reveals a fundamental truth about Trump’s foreign policy approach: it’s less a chess game and more a playground scramble for the upper hand, where loyalty extends only as far as the next opportunity.

Misdirection and Scapegoating

The narrative that Trump is disenchanted with Putin because of the latter’s “loser” status in Ukraine simplifies and misdirects. It frames Trump’s motivations as aligned with performance rather than power. By focusing on Putin’s failures rather than Trump’s opportunistic pattern, Baxter’s analysis risks misreading the broader implications of these geopolitical shifts. Furthermore, the portrayal of Trump as reactive to Putin’s decline rather than proactive in seeking stronger alliances underplays his agency and muddles the understanding of his strategic recalibrations.

The Consequences of Fair-Weather Friendships

Trump’s shifting allegiances underscore a volatile and unpredictable element in international relations. This behavior not only affects bilateral U.S. relations with Russia and China but also sends a broader message about the reliability and consistency of U.S. foreign policy under leaders like Trump. Such volatility can destabilize international norms and influence other nations’ policies and alignments, potentially leading to a more fragmented and tense global order.

Broader Insights: The Cost of Opportunistic Politics

Trump’s behavior is a textbook example of opportunistic politics, where alliances are temporary and loyalty is contingent upon perceived benefits. This approach, while not unique to Trump, highlights a dangerous precedent for international relations: the reduction of diplomatic engagements to transactional exchanges that can be easily upended. It reflects a broader pattern in some political circles where power politics overshadows sustainable diplomatic strategies, risking long-term global stability for short-term gains.

In conclusion, Trump’s pivot away from Putin towards Xi isn’t a strategic masterstroke or a shift towards a more coherent policy but a continuation of his opportunistic style. This style, while immediately beneficial for individual political figures, is detrimental to broader international relations and stability, showcasing a systemic error in how foreign policy is conceptualized and executed in certain government quarters.
