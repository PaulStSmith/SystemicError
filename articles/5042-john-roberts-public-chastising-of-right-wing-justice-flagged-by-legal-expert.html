A legal scholar on Friday zeroed in on Justice Brett Kavanaugh’s dissent in the Supreme Court’s sweeping tariff ruling, saying it stood out not just for its shaky legal footing but for how closely it mirrored the government’s arguments.

In an interview on CNN, NYU law professor Ryan Goodman said he was struck by Kavanaugh’s role in the three-justice dissent – and by how the majority responded to it.

“It’s a bit of a surprise to see him in this three-member dissent because they’re basically wrong on the law and the supermajority of federal judges have said so,” Goodman said. He pointed to what he called an “unusual” addition to the majority opinion authored by Chief Justice John Roberts, where Roberts openly criticized the dissent.

“Chief Justice Roberts does something quite unusual,” Goodman said. “He actually chastises Kavanaugh a bit by saying that the government’s argument was echoed point by point by the principal dissent.”

When Roberts made that observation, Goodman explained, “he’s basically saying Chief Justice Roberts is saying that Kavanaugh was parroting the government’s position.”

But Goodman suggested that Kavanaugh siding with the MAGA administration’s position in the tariff ruling may be a way of setting himself up to strike down future challenges to the Supreme Court.

“The fact that he parrots it, the fact that he’s in the dissent, I think, gives him more room to maneuver back into the majority when other cases are coming up before the court, in which I do think he might very well invalidate some of the actions,” Goodman said about the Trump-appointed justice.

“I think he’s bought himself and the court more legitimacy and more room to do so,” he added.

Our Analysis:

Kavanaugh’s Controversial Dissent: A Strategic Maneuver or Legal Misstep?

In the recent Supreme Court ruling on tariffs, Justice Brett Kavanaugh’s dissent has not only raised eyebrows but also sparked a fierce critique for its apparent alignment with the MAGA administration’s arguments. NYU law professor Ryan Goodman’s observations on CNN underscore a significant and troubling phenomenon: the blurring lines between judicial independence and governmental influence.

Point-by-Point Parroting: A Legal Scholar’s Critique

Goodman’s analysis brings to light a disturbing trend in Kavanaugh’s dissent. It’s not merely that he sided with the minority; it’s the manner in which his dissent seems to have been crafted. According to Goodman, Kavanaugh’s arguments were not just aligned with the MAGA administration’s position—they mirrored them “point by point.” This raises a fundamental question about the integrity of judicial reasoning. When a Supreme Court Justice’s dissent is indistinguishable from a government’s argument, it’s not just a legal misstep; it’s a leap into dangerous territory where the autonomy of the judiciary is at stake.

Chief Justice John Roberts’s critique further illuminates this issue. Roberts’s decision to openly chastise Kavanaugh’s dissent is nothing short of remarkable. It’s a rare glimpse into the internal dynamics of the Court, revealing a level of disagreement that goes beyond legal interpretation. Roberts’s critique suggests that Kavanaugh’s alignment with the government’s stance was so apparent that it warranted an unusual rebuke. This isn’t just a matter of legal disagreement; it’s a matter of judicial principle.

The Long Game: Legitimacy and Strategic Positioning

Goodman posits an intriguing theory: Kavanaugh’s dissent may be a strategic move designed to bolster both his and the Court’s legitimacy in the face of future cases. By siding with the MAGA administration on this issue, Kavanaugh might be attempting to position himself as a more moderate figure in future rulings. This strategy, if true, is a calculated gamble. It suggests a level of political maneuvering that undermines the ideal of justices as impartial arbiters of the law.

The notion that a Supreme Court Justice would use a dissenting opinion as a strategic tool to gain “more room to maneuver” in future cases is deeply troubling. It implies a level of foresight and planning that should have no place in judicial decision-making. The role of a Supreme Court Justice is to interpret the law as it stands, not to play a long game where decisions are made with an eye towards future legal battles.

A Reflection on Judicial Independence

This episode serves as a stark reminder of the challenges facing judicial independence. When justices’ opinions echo political arguments so closely, it erodes public trust in the judiciary. The Supreme Court’s power and legitimacy are derived from the perception that it stands above the political fray, making decisions based solely on legal merit. Any deviation from this principle not only undermines the Court’s authority but also diminishes the foundational idea of an impartial and independent judiciary.

In conclusion, Kavanaugh’s dissent in the Supreme Court’s tariff ruling is more than a legal argument; it’s a symptom of a larger issue concerning the integrity of the judicial system. The criticisms leveled by Goodman and the unusual rebuke from Chief Justice Roberts highlight the need for a rigorous examination of how justices’ opinions are formed and the influences that shape them. As this case demonstrates, the line between legal reasoning and political strategy is one that must be carefully guarded to maintain the judiciary’s independence and the public’s trust.
