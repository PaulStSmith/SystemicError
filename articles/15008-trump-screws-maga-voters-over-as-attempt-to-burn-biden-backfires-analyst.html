Greg Sargent, staff writer at the New Republic, wrote over the weekend that the president "grew angry over a Biden-era program with the word 'equity' in its name, so he ended it."

"But a key part of the program was sending money to red states to expand internet access in rural/MAGA country. Now they might not get it," Sargent wrote on social media.

Sargent is referring to the Digital Equity Act, which "caught the White House’s attention" because of its use of "equity."

"So now Trump is moving to nix funding—but it could cut off millions destined for red America," he wrote Saturday in an article entitled "Angry Trump Kills 'Woke' Program—and Accidentally Screws MAGA Voters."

According to Sargent, Trump noticed the "equity" program and concluded that "this could only mean the initiative was designed to help undeserving minorities."

"Which presented Trump with a ripe opportunity to demagogue about supposed reverse racism against white people," he wrote. "Except there’s a problem here. The program in question, the Digital Equity Act, contained huge amounts of money for governments in GOP-run states, as well as Democratic ones, to expand high-speed internet access in underserved communities—very much including red states’ rural areas."

Going even further, Sargent says, "What’s more, every red state government had submitted proposals designed to garner their states large amounts of this funding."

"These proposals were explicitly designed by these GOP governments partly to serve their states’ rural areas," he added.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Irony: Trump's Misguided Crusade Against 'Equity'

In a recent spectacle of political self-sabotage, Donald Trump has evidently decided to wage war against a Joe Biden-era program, purely due to its association with the term 'equity'. According to Greg Sargent of the New Republic, this move not only showcases Trump's knee-jerk reaction to progressive terminology but also, and quite hilariously, undermines his own voter base. Let's unpack the layers of absurdity and myopia in this political blunder.

The Root of the Issue: A Misguided Hatred for 'Equity'

Trump's disdain for the Digital Equity Act, as reported, seems to stem from nothing more than its nomenclature. The term 'equity', often associated with efforts to level the playing field for marginalized communities, triggered Trump to such an extent that he decided to axe the program without delving deeper into its benefits or the constituents it serves. This move reeks of a superficial and reactionary approach to governance, where decisions are made based on buzzwords rather than substantive policy analysis.

The Unintended Victims: Trump's Own Supporters

The irony thickens as we delve into who gets hurt by Trump's decision: the rural/MAGA country, a significant portion of his voter base. The Digital Equity Act, despite its 'woke' title in Trump's eyes, was designed to funnel funds into expanding internet access in underserved communities, including those in red states. Sargent points out that every red state government had even submitted proposals to benefit from this funding, highlighting the broad, bipartisan need for such an initiative.

A Case of Political Myopia

Trump's move to nix the funding out of a misplaced vendetta against 'equity' showcases a profound lack of foresight. It's a stark reminder of the dangers inherent in allowing ideological biases to cloud judgment on policy matters. This isn't just about a misstep; it's a glaring exhibition of how disconnected Trump's reactionary politics are from the actual needs of his constituents.

The Bigger Picture: What This Means for 'Red America'

The fallout from this decision could be significant for red states. By cutting off funds meant to improve internet access, Trump is inadvertently stifolding economic opportunities and educational resources for the very communities that supported him. In the digital age, internet access is not a luxury; it's a necessity for education, business, healthcare, and more. Trump's vendetta against a program's name thus risks exacerbating the digital divide in his own stronghold areas.

Conclusion: A Self-Inflicted Wound

In a twist laden with irony, Trump's attempt to take a stand against what he perceives as 'woke' culture has backfired spectacularly, potentially disenfranchising his own supporters in the process. This incident serves as a cautionary tale about the pitfalls of politicizing basic needs and services. One can only hope that such self-defeating strategies are recognized for what they are: not just political errors, but systemic failures to address the real issues facing Americans today.

In the end, the Digital Equity Act debacle is more than just a political faux pas; it's a stark illustration of the chasm between ideologically driven decisions and the practical needs of the American populace. It's time for political leaders to rise above petty squabbles over terminology and focus on the substantive impact of policy. For the sake of 'Red America' and beyond, one hopes lessons are learned, albeit not at the expense of the very constituents these leaders claim to represent.
