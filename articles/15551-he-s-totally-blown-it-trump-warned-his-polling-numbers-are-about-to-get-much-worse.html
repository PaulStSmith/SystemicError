'He's totally blown it': Trump warned his polling numbers are about to get much worse

Reacting to new polling that shows Donald Trump's approval ratings are cratering, a longtime GOP insider claimed the embattled president's slide with voters will continue unless he makes major changes.

Appearing on MSNBC's "The Weekend," GOP strategist Brendan Buck took issue with co-host Michael Steele who told the panel to slow down their enthusiasm over the president's growing unpopularity.

"Obviously it's just a snapshot, but I certainly don't think it's going to get better for him anytime soon," Buck countered.

"I mean, this is a president who absolutely believes in what he's doing and they throw around this word mandate a lot; they think they had a mandate to come and I'm, no actually –– I don't know that they actually did have a mandate to do that and every president says that they have a mandate," he added with a grin. "If he had a mandate to do anything, it was to address the cost of living and he's certainly not doing that."

"I think what's amazing in all of this polling is how much he has undermined the advantages he had going in," he elaborated. "He had a real advantage on the issue of immigration–– now he's underwater on immigration. That's remarkable and, of course, on the economy, this economy was ready to get going, it was going to be roaring if he had just gotten out of the way a little bit."

"Just keep things humming, especially in contrast to what we had been through under Joe Biden," he added. "Just stay out of the way, you would have been fine, and he's totally blown it."

'To your point, tariffs are just getting started. this is only going to get worse," he predicted. "You know, if anything is going to sort of check him, the guardrails are public opinion. So maybe he'll see some of this and realize that it shouldn't, he shouldn't go forward. But he's not shown himself to be somebody who, you know, recalibrates."

Our Analysis:

Parsing the Political Spin: A Closer Look at GOP Strategist Brendan Buck's Commentary

In a recent appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend," GOP strategist Brendan Buck offered his insights into President Donald Trump's plummeting approval ratings. Buck's analysis, while seemingly straightforward, deserves a deeper dive to unpack the layers of political maneuvering and implications behind his statements. Let's dissect this with the precision of a scalpel, cutting through the facade to reveal the underlying truths and omissions.

The Illusion of a Mandate

Buck's commentary pivots around a central, yet highly disputable claim that President Trump believes he had a "mandate" upon entering office. This word, mandate, is tossed around with the casualness of a coin flip, yet its implications are monumental. Buck slyly discredits this perceived mandate, stating, "I don't know that they actually did have a mandate to do that." Here, he dances on the line between acknowledging Trump's aggressive policy agenda and questioning its legitimacy. The subtext? A subtle nod to those questioning the very foundation of Trump's presidency without alienating the hardline base.

The Economic Mirage

Buck's analysis then veers into the economic realm, where he credits Trump with an initial advantage on issues like immigration and the economy, only to argue that Trump has squandered these supposed benefits. "He had a real advantage on the issue of immigration — now he's underwater on immigration," Buck laments, painting a picture of a president who has not only failed to capitalize on his positions but has actively undermined them. This narrative cleverly shifts blame onto Trump's shoulders, absolving the broader GOP of responsibility for widespread policy failures, particularly in relation to the economy and immigration.

The Tariff Tangle

Perhaps the most pointed critique comes with Buck's mention of tariffs, predicting worsening conditions due to Trump's trade policies. "Tariffs are just getting started. This is only going to get worse," he warns. Here, Buck touches on a raw nerve within the GOP, many of whom have traditionally championed free trade. By isolating tariffs as a singular issue rather than a symptom of a larger ideological shift within the party, Buck attempts to quarantine the damage to Trump's legacy, rather than acknowledging a seismic shift within the GOP towards protectionism.

Public Opinion as the Ultimate Arbiter

Buck concludes his analysis by suggesting that public opinion will serve as the "guardrails" for Trump's presidency. This assertion is both revealing and misleading. On one hand, it acknowledges the power of voter sentiment to check presidential power. On the other, it sidesteps the responsibility of elected officials and party leaders to act as a counterbalance to executive overreach. By placing the onus on public opinion, Buck absolves the GOP of its complicity in Trump's controversial policies and actions.

Conclusion

Brendan Buck's commentary, while coated in a veneer of critique, is a masterclass in political deflection. By focusing on Trump's personal failings and policy missteps, Buck attempts to distance the broader GOP from the fallout, preserving the party's viability in the eyes of disenchanted voters. However, this tactic only serves to highlight the party's internal conflicts and its struggles to define itself in the post-Trump era.

In essence, Buck's analysis is not just a critique of Trump; it's a desperate attempt to salvage the GOP's tarnished image. But in doing so, he inadvertently exposes the party's lack of direction and moral clarity. As we peel back the layers of Buck's statements, what emerges is a portrait of a party at war with itself, unsure of its future and haunted by the specter of its recent past.
