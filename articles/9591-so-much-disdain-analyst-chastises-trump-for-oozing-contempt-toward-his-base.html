'So much disdain': Analyst chastises Trump for 'oozing contempt' toward his base

Late Saturday night, Trump posted on Truth Social that former President Joe Biden placed “274 FBI agents” in the crowd during the January 6 insurrection.

“ If this is so, which it is, a lot of very good people will be owed big apologies. What a SCAM - DO SOMETHING!!!” Trump’s post reads.

Cohen said posts like the one Trump authored on Saturday have become a norm for the “lawless president.”

“Donald Trump has so much oozing contempt for the people who trust him, who listen to him, that he is literally sitting here trying to convince them that Joe Biden is responsible for an insurrection that he incited when he was president. As if that information isn’t readily accessible.

“There is no difference here than Donald Trump trying to convince his supporters that two plus two is five,” Cohen said. “The truth is right there in front of everybody’s faces. And yet he has so much disdain for the people who trust him that he will tell them a bald-faced lie and expect them to just lap it up.”

“Trump may claim to hate the left, but there is truly no one he has more contempt for than his own voters,” he added.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Contempt for His Own Voters: A Deep Dive

In a recent tirade on Truth Social, Donald Trump has once again showcased his unparalleled ability to weave narratives out of thin air, this time implicating Joe Biden in the January 6 insurrection by claiming he planted “274 FBI agents” in the crowd. This assertion, devoid of evidence and logic, was rightly criticized by Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen. Cohen’s analysis sheds a bright, unforgiving light on Trump’s modus operandi, revealing a contempt not just for the truth, but for his own supporters.

The Art of Deception

Trump’s post, as Cohen points out, is a masterstroke in the art of deception. By blaming Biden for the insurrection, Trump not only absolves himself of any responsibility but also attempts to rewrite the narrative of that day, despite overwhelming evidence to the contrary. This isn’t just misinformation; it’s a calculated attempt to manipulate his base. Cohen’s comparison of Trump’s tactics to convincing someone that “two plus two is five” is spot on. It’s not just a lie; it’s an insult to the intelligence of his followers.

Contempt for His Base

Cohen’s assertion that Trump harbors more contempt for his own voters than he does for the left is a damning indictment of Trump’s view of his base. Trump’s actions suggest that he sees his supporters not as constituents to be respected, but as pawns to be maneuvered. His willingness to propagate such a blatant falsehood indicates that he believes his followers will accept anything he says, no matter how ludicrous. This isn’t just disdainful; it’s exploitative.

The Role of Social Media

The platform for this latest falsehood, Truth Social, is Trump’s own creation, designed as a haven from the scrutiny of mainstream social media. However, this incident highlights how such platforms can become echo chambers for unverified claims, allowing figures like Trump to disseminate falsehoods unchallenged. It’s a stark reminder of the role social media plays in shaping political narratives, often at the expense of truth.

Conclusion

Brian Tyler Cohen’s critique of Donald Trump’s late-night ramblings on Truth Social is more than warranted; it’s necessary. In an age where misinformation can spread like wildfire, calling out falsehoods, especially those propagated by individuals in positions of power, is crucial. Trump’s post is not just a lie; it’s a reflection of his modus operandi and his view of his own supporters. Cohen’s analysis exposes the manipulation and contempt at the heart of Trump’s message, serving as a reminder of the importance of vigilance in the face of deliberate misinformation. The contempt Trump shows for his base is alarming, but the readiness with which some accept his narratives is equally concerning. It’s a systemic error that needs addressing, starting with holding those in power accountable for their words and actions.
