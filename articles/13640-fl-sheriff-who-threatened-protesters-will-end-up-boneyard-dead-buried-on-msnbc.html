FL sheriff who threatened protesters will end up 'boneyard dead' buried on MSNBC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Anatomy of Authoritarian Rhetoric: The Case of a Florida Sheriff

Brevard County Sheriff Wayne Ivey told reporters he would kill protesters if they threatened the lives of one of his deputies. (Brevard County Sheriff’s office)

Calling a Florida sheriff's threats against "No Kings" rally participants "beyond chilling," MSNBC host Jonathan Capehart and authoritarianism expert Ruth Ben-Ghiat hammered the lawman on Saturday morning.

On Thursday, Brevard County Sheriff Wayne Ivey held a press conference and addressed the Saturday nationwide protests, "If you throw a brick, a firebomb, or point a gun at one of our deputies, we will be notifying your family where to collect your remains because we will kill you, graveyard dead. We’re not going to play."

He later added, "If you try to mob rule a car in Brevard County, gathering around it, refusing to let the driver leave in our county, you’re most likely going to get run over and dragged across the street. If you spit on us, you’re going to the hospital, then jail. If you hit one of us, you’re going to the hospital, jail, and most likely get bitten by one of our big, beautiful dogs we have here."

After sharing the clip, Ben-Ghiat shook her head in disbelief as Capehart asked, "Talk about how talk like that from an official like that feeds into your work looking at strongmen."

"Unfortunately, what happens is when you have a culture of kind of institutionalized lawlessness and where our the head of our government tried to overthrow the government and is calling protesters insurrectionists, but had incited a violent insurrection of his own, so when someone like that is in power, there's a kind of encouragement for all kinds of what they call a broad state security forces, including law enforcement and state militias and the military to feel empowered to be lawless themselves and speak like that," she explained.

"And so this kind of talk that any protests, and when protesters are criminalized and called insurrectionists, then all protesters are suspect. and you can speak the way that this individual was speaking,' she continued, "saying that we're going to shoot you graveyard dead, which is, again, a kind of trickling down of this idea that the strongman does not tolerate any force and, and sees any opposition as a personal affront that has to be met with maximum violence."

Our Analysis:

Anatomy of Authoritarian Rhetoric: The Case of a Florida Sheriff

In a chilling display of authoritarian bravado, Brevard County Sheriff Wayne Ivey's recent statements serve as a stark reminder of the encroaching shadows of despotism on American democracy. MSNBC host Jonathan Capehart and authoritarianism expert Ruth Ben-Ghiat took to the airwaves to dissect this alarming development, casting a glaring spotlight on Ivey's threats against participants of the "No Kings" rally. Their analysis, while distressing, is crucial for understanding the broader implications of such rhetoric in today's political climate.

Sheriff Ivey's Disturbing Proclamation

Sheriff Ivey's message was unambiguous and terrifying: "If you throw a brick, a firebomb, or point a gun at one of our deputies, we will be notifying your family where to collect your remains because we will kill you, graveyard dead." This statement, devoid of any pretense of due process or restraint, crosses a line from law enforcement into a realm of violent retribution that has no place in a civil society. It's a leap into the dark ages of justice, where the rule of law is supplanted by the rule of force. Moreover, Ivey's additional threats — suggesting that protesters could be run over, hospitalized, or attacked by dogs — only compound the horror, painting a picture of a police force more interested in punishment than protection.

The Echoes of Authoritarianism

Ben-Ghiat's analysis ties Ivey's rhetoric to a broader pattern of authoritarianism, where such violent posturing is not just accepted but encouraged. It reflects a disturbing trend where the lines between protesting and insurrection are deliberately blurred, criminalizing dissent and legitimizing excessive force. This narrative, as Ben-Ghiat points out, feeds into a "culture of institutionalized lawlessness," where leaders see opposition as a personal affront that must be met with "maximum violence." This is the language of tyrants, not public servants, and it signals a dangerous erosion of democratic norms and values.

The Implications of Ivey's Threats

What is perhaps most alarming about Ivey's comments is the implicit sanctioning of extrajudicial violence against citizens. By framing protesters — indiscriminately and without evidence — as violent insurgents, Ivey not only dehumanizes them but also sets the stage for potential human rights abuses under the guise of maintaining order. This is a tactic straight out of the authoritarian playbook, where fear is wielded as both weapon and shield to suppress opposition and consolidate power.

A Call to Vigilance

The response from Capehart and Ben-Ghiat is not just a critique but a clarion call for vigilance. In the face of such overt threats to democratic principles, silence or indifference is tantamount to complicity. It is imperative that the media, public officials, and citizens alike stand firm against the normalization of authoritarian rhetoric and actions. We must remember that the strength of a democracy lies not in its ability to exert force, but in its commitment to justice, accountability, and the protection of civil liberties for all.

In the end, Sheriff Ivey's statements are a grim reminder of the fragility of democracy and the constant vigilance required to protect it from those who would seek to undermine it from within. Let us not be desensitized to the dangers of authoritarianism, no matter how subtly it seeks to creep into our public discourse and institutions.
