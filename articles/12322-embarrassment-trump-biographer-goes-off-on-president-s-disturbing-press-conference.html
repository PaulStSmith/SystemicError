'Embarrassment': Trump biographer goes off on president's 'disturbing' press conference

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20254 min read

Donald Trump in Scotland (MSNBC screenshot)

Sitting in with host Ali Velshi on Sunday after Donald Trump completed a press conference in Scotland with Ursula von der Leyen, president of the European Commission, one of the president's biographers called the Trump's answers, "profound ignorance" and demeanor "disturbing on a number of levels."

After host Velshi started the segment by blurting, "Wow. That was batcoop crazy," biographer and Bloomberg Opinion Editor in Chief Tim L. O'Brien looked equally distressed.

"Tim, this was so full of nonsense. I truly am a bit of a loss for words, so please help," Velshi prompted his guest.

"Well, so as you as you've noted, Ali, this this has happened before, this is classic Trump," O'Brien began. "But this was a press conference that was unusually off the rails and it's really disturbing on a number of levels."

"First and foremost, it's just an embarrassment to the United States to have the president on a global stage and not be able to string together coherent thoughts on a series of fundamental issues that are important to the world," he observed. "They're important to the United States, and they're important to citizens everywhere."

"First and foremost for me was how this press conference began which was questions about Gaza," he continued. "There is a humanitarian crisis in Gaza, there is starvation in Gaza. He was asked about Israel's disposition toward all of that and his answer was we don't get thanked enough for the humanitarian aid we provide Gaza –– he completely dodged the question."

"Now, people who attribute strategy to Trump will say, well, that was good for him, he avoided getting into the gnarly and troublesome details of the Gaza war," he elaborated. "But then he continued on and on, and he proceeded to say at one point that Hamas was stealing humanitarian supplies. Trump's own government, I think just 2 or 3 days ago, after a study, said there was no evidence that Hamas was stealing supplies."

Noting that the president seemed unable to recall the name of Israel's Ariel Sharon, he called it a "another feature of these press conferences."

"It is not strategic rambling, it is profound ignorance on issues that are essential and important that he is ill-informed of, and that he doesn't care to study up on," he accused. "And it matters, it matters in how he presents himself."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Press Conference: A Display of Ignorance or a Calculated Dodge?

In a recent spectacle that unfolded during a press conference in Scotland alongside Ursula von der Leyen, president of the European Commission, Donald Trump's performance was nothing short of a theatrical display of ignorance. Described by his own biographer, Tim L. O'Brien, as "disturbing on a number of levels," the former president managed to underscore his tenure's hallmark: a profound lack of coherence and understanding on critical global issues.

"Batcoop Crazy" or Par for the Course?

Ali Velshi's reaction, "Wow. That was batcoop crazy," might seem hyperbolic if it weren't so painfully accurate. The press conference, as recounted by O'Brien, wasn't just a series of off-the-cuff remarks but a glaring exhibition of Trump's habitual disregard for facts and nuanced understanding. From the crisis in Gaza to the misidentification of key political figures, Trump's responses were a mix of evasion, misinformation, and, frankly, disrespect.

The Gaza Dodge: Ignorance or Strategy?

O'Brien's analysis of Trump's response to the Gaza situation is particularly telling. Trump's claim that the US doesn't get "thanked enough" for its humanitarian aid, alongside the unfounded accusation that Hamas was stealing supplies, is not just a dodge—it's a dangerous distortion of reality. The assertion flies in the face of his own government's findings, revealing a disconcerting gap between Trump's rhetoric and verifiable facts.

Forgetting Ariel Sharon: A Slip or a Symptom?

The inability to recall Ariel Sharon's name might seem minor in isolation, but it's emblematic of a broader issue: a lack of preparation and respect for the intricacies of international relations. Trump's disregard for the essential details, whether strategic or due to sheer ignorance, undermines the gravity and complexity of global diplomacy.

Conclusion: The Disturbing Implications

What's most alarming about this press conference is not just the surface-level gaffes but what they signify about Trump's approach to leadership and governance. O'Brien's observations point to a disturbing trend: a leader who not only fails to grasp critical issues but also doesn't seem interested in learning.

This is not just an "embarrassment to the United States" on the global stage, as O'Brien asserts, but a stark reminder of the dangers posed by leaders who prioritize rhetoric over reality, evasion over engagement. Trump's performance is a case study in the consequences of electing officials who lack the curiosity, humility, and respect necessary to navigate the complex web of global politics. In this light, the press conference was not just "batcoop crazy"—it was a warning.
