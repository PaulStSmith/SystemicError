The Great American Delusion: Why the Average White Man Won't Pick Strawberries

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Every election cycle, a chorus of conservative voices erupts with the same tired refrain: “They're stealing our jobs!” The “they” here, of course, refers to immigrants—particularly undocumented ones—who are demonized as the reason for wage suppression, job loss, and the decline of the so-called “American Dream.” But peel back the layers of this narrative, and what you find isn’t theft, but willful abdication. The uncomfortable truth? The average white American male isn’t being forced out of the fields—he’s running from them.

The Myth of the American Laborer

There’s a pervasive myth in right-wing rhetoric that, if not for immigrant labor, Americans would roll up their sleeves and take back their rightful place in the workforce. It’s a fantasy rooted in nostalgia and arrogance, not reality. According to U.S. Census data, only about 36% of adults over 25 hold a bachelor’s degree or higher. The rest have a high school diploma, a GED, or less. That in itself is not a condemnation—there is honor in all forms of education. But it exposes a contradiction: if so many Americans are supposedly prepared to work, why won’t they?

Because they can’t. Or more accurately: they won’t. Not under the current conditions.

Agricultural Work is Brutal

Field labor is not a weekend at the “pick-your-own” orchard. It's not a wholesome, Instagrammable family outing with a sunhat and a wicker basket. Real agricultural work means waking up before dawn, working through blistering heat or freezing rain, often without shade, rest, or reliable access to water. It means repetitive motion injuries, chemical exposure, and backbreaking labor that starts at sunrise and ends when your hands are raw.

It is also wildly underpaid.

Even when offered $25/hour, as one California farmer recently reported, white laborers did not show up—and those who did quit within a week. Why? Because they weren’t cut out for it. Because no amount of patriotic bluster prepares you for 14-hour days bent over lettuce rows in 100-degree heat.

“They're Taking Our Jobs” is a Lie

This talking point is nothing more than a scapegoat for a deeper economic truth: employers don’t want to pay living wages. Immigrants aren’t stealing jobs; employers are offering them—eagerly—to people they can exploit. And when the alternative is an entitled citizen demanding $100K, health benefits, and a 401k to pick tomatoes, the decision is obvious.

What Republicans call “job theft” is actually capitalism doing exactly what it's designed to do: maximize profit by minimizing labor cost. It’s not theft. It’s a market choice. And the average American worker has priced himself out of the market, not because he’s unqualified, but because he’s unwilling to tolerate exploitation.

A Culture of Decline and Denial

This unwillingness isn’t new. It’s generational. White, rural men—the supposed backbone of America—have traded in physical labor for cultural resentment. Many live in counties flooded with opioid addiction, funded by disability checks, or propped up by farm subsidies they pretend to hate. They worship Trump, a man who wouldn’t last four hours on a dairy farm and whose policies actively hurt their economic interests, because he validates their identity, not their labor.

Meanwhile, immigrant workers do the labor. Quietly. Invisibly. They pick your food, clean your hotels, build your homes, and slaughter your meat—all while being paid less, protected less, and respected not at all.

Educational Realities and Economic Fantasy

Let’s not pretend there’s some huge untapped reserve of patriotic field hands waiting to be activated. The average white American male today isn’t trained, conditioned, or psychologically prepared for agricultural labor. A generation raised on the myth of the “self-made man” has zero understanding of the physical toll it takes to keep a country fed. They have YouTube channels, gym memberships, and truck nuts—not calloused hands and aching knees.

The right loves to mock the “liberal elite” for their soy lattes and gender studies degrees, but here’s the truth: the only thing more fragile than a college-educated coastal progressive is a red-blooded American man asked to dig a ditch for less than $30/hour.

Who Does the Work?

The answer is simple: immigrants. Legal or not, they are the beating heart of America’s essential labor force. Not because they are stealing anything, but because they are willing to endure what others will not. Because survival, for them, isn’t theoretical. It’s immediate.

Meanwhile, the GOP sells fantasies to a base that would rather blame brown people than admit they’re out of touch with the realities of hard work. They talk about jobs being stolen, when the truth is they left them sitting in the field, rotting.

Conclusion: Stop the Lies, Face the Truth

America’s economy doesn’t run on bootstrap myths. It runs on exploited labor. And if you took away immigrant workers tomorrow, the fields would go fallow, the produce would rot, and the very people shouting “Build the wall!” would starve or go bankrupt paying $9 for an onion.

It is long past time to stop pretending that the average white American male is the embodiment of hard work. He’s not out there milking cows or picking lettuce. He’s on Facebook, sharing memes about immigrants ruining the country—while eating fruit picked by the very people he despises.

The cult of victimhood is strong. But the truth is stronger. And the truth is this:

Immigrants didn’t take your job. You gave it up, because you didn’t want it.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
