'Blatant lies': Mike Johnson skewered over stunning Medicaid claim

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) was reamed on social media by Rep. Becca Balint (D-VT) for his claim that he actually strengthened Medicaid by making deep cuts to it.

Balint was reacting to an interview Johnson sat for on "Meet The Press" Sunday, during which he was asked by Kristen Welker about a bill by Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO) to repeal some of the Medicaid cuts in Trump's tax cut megabill. "Why would Josh Hawley introduce a bill to roll back cuts in Medicaid if there are not cuts in Medicaid? He says the people in his state are going to suffer."

"I will tell you that the 'One Big Beautiful Bill' safeguards the program," replied Johnson.

Balint laid into the speaker on X over these comments.

"The Speaker is spewing blatant lies about his spending bill," wrote Balint. "He is lying to the American people, I suspect he is lying to his caucus, I guess he's even lying to himself. YES, the 'One Big Beautiful Bill' cuts Medicaid significantly. Read it for yourself."

The bill, which was signed into law by President Donald Trump at the beginning of the month following months of turmoil, controversy, and infighting, partially pays for massive new tax cuts for the wealthy by cutting around $1 trillion from Medicaid. The bill introduces draconian new work requirements that could throw millions of people off their insurance even if they have jobs, and restricts how much states can collect from the federal government as part of provider tax funds-matching, a move that hospital executives warn could devastate rural providers in particular.

GOP lawmakers tried to lessen the blow of that second provision by adding a $50 billion bailout fund for rural hospitals, but as the conservative Washington Examiner noted, this bailout doesn't actually require the funding to be spent on rural hospitals.

Our Analysis:

The Grand Deception: Cutting Medicaid to Feed the Wealthy

In a display of political theater that would make Orwell blush, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has taken to the airwaves, armed with a narrative so brazenly detached from reality it demands scrutiny. During a recent appearance on "Meet The Press," Johnson boldly claimed that the "One Big Beautiful Bill," a legislative monstrosity signed into law by President Donald Trump, somehow strengthens Medicaid. This assertion was made despite the bill's glaring gutting of the program to the tune of approximately $1 trillion.

The Art of the Lie

Johnson's assertion is not just misleading; it's a masterclass in deception. The bill, celebrated for its massive tax cuts for the wealthy, achieves these fiscal gymnastics on the backs of the most vulnerable Americans by slashing Medicaid funding. It introduces draconian new work requirements that threaten to strip millions of their insurance, even those gainfully employed. Furthermore, it restricts the amount states can collect from the federal government in provider tax funds-matching—a move so catastrophic that hospital executives have sounded alarms about the potential decimation of rural healthcare providers.

A Token Gesture to Rural Hospitals

In a feeble attempt to mitigate the backlash, GOP lawmakers tacked on a supposed $50 billion bailout fund for rural hospitals. However, this gesture is as empty as it is insulting. The conservative Washington Examiner itself pointed out that the provision does not mandate the allocation of these funds to rural hospitals. This token gesture is akin to offering a Band-Aid for a severed limb, highlighting the disingenuousness of the bill's proponents.

The Fallout of Political Gamesmanship

Rep. Becca Balint (D-VT) took to social media to call out Speaker Johnson's audacious claims, labeling them as blatant lies. Balint's critique cuts to the heart of the matter, exposing the chasm between the rhetoric used by Johnson and the harsh realities embedded within the "One Big Beautiful Bill." The Speaker's narrative is not just a lie; it's a multifaceted deception that seeks to obfuscate the truth about who truly benefits from the bill—the wealthy—and who pays the price—the poor and the sick.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The dialogue surrounding the "One Big Beautiful Bill" and its impact on Medicaid is emblematic of a broader trend in American politics: the prioritization of wealth accumulation for the few over the health and well-being of the many. Johnson's attempts to spin the Medicaid cuts as a form of strengthening the program is not just a falsehood; it's an affront to the intelligence of the American public and a callous disregard for the lives that will be irrevocably impacted by these policy decisions.

As this debacle unfolds, one must question the moral compass of those who champion such policies. The need for accountability has never been more stark, nor the divide between political rhetoric and reality more apparent. The true measure of a society is found in how it treats its most vulnerable members. By this measure, the architects of the "One Big Beautiful Bill" have been found woefully lacking.
