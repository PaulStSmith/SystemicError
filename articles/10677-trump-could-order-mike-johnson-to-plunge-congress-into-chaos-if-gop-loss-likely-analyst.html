Trump could order Mike Johnson to plunge Congress 'into chaos' if GOP loss likely: analyst

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hands with U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 22, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimur

President Donald Trump is reportedly petrified of losing his Republican majority in the House next year, which could set the stage for Democrats to once again investigate his scandals and possibly line up another impeachment — but there might be one particular, last-ditch tool he could use to try to upend any loss, warned Ari Berman for Mother Jones.

For the most part, Trump has already tipped his hand about much of his strategy to try to head off GOP losses, including rigging congressional districts in Republican-controlled states and attempting to prohibit mail-in voting.

But there's another strategy he could use if it looks like he could actually lose the election, Berman wrote — the same gambit to block election certification he tried when he lost in 2020. Only there's a simpler legal path for him to do so.

"Imagine this scenario: control of the House isn’t called on election night 2026," wrote Berman. "The balance of power depends on one or more close races in California, which can take weeks to count votes. Trump alleges widespread fraud, making spurious claims about post-election 'vote dumps' and noncitizens voting. The president pressures Speaker of the House Mike Johnson — who voted against certifying the electoral votes from Arizona and Pennsylvania in 2020 and supported a lawsuit to overturn that presidential election in four battleground states — not to seat any Democrats in disputed elections."

In this case, he continued, Johnson, unlike former Vice President Mike Pence who didn't go along with the scheme, "persuades the House, while it is still under GOP control before the next speaker is chosen, to block members of California’s delegation, plunging the body into chaos."

This isn't too far-fetched, he said, because the Federal Contested Elections Act of 1969 allows congressional candidates to make challenges, and a number of congressional races have been contested throughout history — often for malicious reasons: "In the volatile period after the Civil War, when Reconstruction was violently overthrown and Jim Crow established in the South, white members of Congress repeatedly overturned the elections of Black members."

In other words, the analysis concluded, "Johnson and his allies could attempt to prevent or override state-level certification of a member and declare their own winner." But he won't be doing so unchallenged: "there is also a broad coalition of lawyers, pro-democracy groups, election officials, and state leaders preparing to fight back, whether in the courtroom, at the ballot box, or on the streets."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Desperation: A Dangerous Playbook Unveiled

In an intriguingly ominous reflection on the potential future of American democracy, Ari Berman for Mother Jones sheds light on President Donald Trump's apparent dread over losing the Republican majority in the House. This isn’t just about party dominance; it’s a saga of survival, where Trump fears the resurgence of investigations into his scandals, and possibly, facing another impeachment. But it's the depths to which he might sink to avert this perceived disaster that warrants a scathing examination.

The Old Tricks: Gerrymandering and Voter Suppression

Trump's strategy, as outlined by Berman, isn't particularly novel. It reeks of the desperation and manipulation we've grown uncomfortably accustomed to. Rigging congressional districts in Republican-controlled states and attempting to prohibit mail-in voting are moves pulled straight from the authoritarian playbook. Yet, they're merely the appetizers in Trump's feast of subversion.

A More Sinister Strategy: Blocking Election Certification

The crux of Berman's warning revolves around a potentially catastrophic scenario for the 2026 elections. Trump, envisaging a loss, might lean on the Federal Contested Elections Act of 1969 to block election certification. This isn’t mere speculation; it's a well-grounded fear based on Trump's past actions and his apparent willingness to trample democratic norms to cling to power.

Imagine the scene: the control of the House hangs in balance, votes are slowly counted, and Trump, in a replay of his 2020 performance, cries fraud without a shred of evidence. He pressures the Speaker of the House — a loyalist who has previously sided with Trump's attempts to overturn election results — to not seat Democrats from contested elections. This isn't just an attack on the Democrats; it's a full-blown assault on the electoral process and the democratic principles that underpin American governance.

A Historical Echo: The Shadow of Reconstruction

Berman astutely draws parallels to the post-Civil War era, a time when the elections of Black members of Congress were overturned by white members in the South. This historical context isn’t just a reminder of America's dark past; it's a warning. It illustrates how the manipulation of electoral outcomes has been used as a tool to suppress representation and maintain power. The scenario Berman describes isn’t just a potential future; it's a reflection on the cyclical nature of democratic backsliding.

The Counterforce: A Coalition of Democracy Defenders

However, all is not lost. Berman mentions a "broad coalition of lawyers, pro-democracy groups, election officials, and state leaders" gearing up to defend the democratic process. This isn't just a fight to be fought in the courtrooms or at the ballot box; it's a struggle for the soul of American democracy, one that might spill onto the streets in a display of public will against autocratic overreach.

Conclusion: The Fight Ahead

Trump's strategy, as dissected by Berman, isn’t just a testament to his fear of losing power; it's a glaring red flag for American democracy. It underscores the lengths to which he may go to subvert the electoral process, and the critical importance of vigilance, resistance, and collective action in safeguarding democratic institutions.

As we move forward, the question isn't just about whether Trump will attempt to execute this desperate strategy; it's about the resilience of American democracy and the collective will of its people to stand against such blatant attempts at subversion. The battle lines are drawn not just in legislative halls and courtrooms, but in the very fabric of the democratic society. It's a battle we cannot afford to lose.
