JD Vance’s Instability Is the Job

The Real Power Center

George Will’s column is about JD Vance’s whiplash shifts: from Trump critic to Trump loyalist, from one religious posture to another, from performative decency to vulgarity and back again. But the deeper story is not Vance’s personality. It is the political machine that keeps rewarding him for obeying the man at the center of it. In this arrangement, Trump holds the power, and Vance’s value is not principle but usefulness.

That is the first fact worth naming plainly: the vice president is not being judged by consistency, honesty, or public service. He is being measured by how effectively he can become whatever the moment requires for MAGA’s survival.

Conversion as Career Method

Will mocks Vance as a serial convert, and the ridicule lands because the evidence is right there in the source material. Politics, economics, history, religion: everything is movable if the move advances the climb. That is not a glitch in Vance’s biography. It is the skill set his lane in the Republican hierarchy demands.

What looks like instability is actually opportunism with better branding. The movement does not punish contradiction when contradiction is the point. It rewards the politician who can betray yesterday’s position without embarrassment and still sell himself as a man of conviction. That is how authoritarian politics metabolizes sincerity: it strips it for parts and keeps the loyalty.

Religion as Border Policy

Vance’s religious talk is not presented in the source as theology. It is political vocabulary with a cross on it. “Christian statesman,” “social cohesion,” “America’s creed” are not neutral phrases. In the column’s telling, they are code for immigration restriction, racial grievance, and cultural gatekeeping.

That is the misdirection built into the performance. Religion is being used as a permission structure for exclusion. Vance’s invocation of Christianity does not widen moral concern; it narrows membership. His claim that Americans no longer have to apologize for being white, and his appeal to Civil War ancestry as a higher claim on the nation, are not stray remarks. They are the politics of inherited entitlement dressed up as spiritual seriousness.

The Election Lie Was the Test

The most revealing detail in the source is not Vance’s later decorum, but his declaration that the 2020 election was “rigged” and that he would not have certified it. That is not a side issue. It is a qualification. In MAGA politics, willingness to break with constitutional procedure when the leader demands it is not disqualifying. It is career-enhancing.

This is where the column’s attack on Vance’s inconsistency points to a larger truth it does not fully name: the movement is not destabilized by lying. It is stabilized by it. A politician who can float whatever claim the moment requires is exactly the kind of operator an anti-democratic project needs.

Will Sees the Hypocrisy, Not the System

Will is right to ridicule the hypocrisy. Vance is obviously opportunistic, and the source gives enough material to show it. But stopping at personal fraud misses the structure that makes personal fraud useful. The scandal is not that one ambitious man contradicts himself. The scandal is that contradiction is rewarded because it can be turned into obedience.

That is why this story matters beyond one vice president. It shows a Republican ecosystem that no longer filters for conviction or competence, only for pliability. Once that standard takes hold, the loudest fraud becomes the safest bet, and the most unstable politician is the one most likely to be promoted.

What This Really Reveals

The point is not that Vance lacks principles. It is that his principles are irrelevant to the power he serves. MAGA does not need a coherent philosophy. It needs enforcers who can translate resentment, religious language, racial hierarchy, and election denial into administrative power.

That is the larger pattern here: modern authoritarian politics does not merely tolerate opportunists. It selects them. It elevates the person who can keep shifting shape while making the project of exclusion, domination, and rule-breaking look like moral seriousness.
