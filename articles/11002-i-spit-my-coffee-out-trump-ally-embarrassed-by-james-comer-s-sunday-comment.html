'I spit my coffee out': Trump ally 'embarrassed' by James Comer's Sunday comment

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20253 min read

U.S. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) attends a dinner with Republican members of congress hosted by U.S. President Donald Trump, in the East Room of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 22, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

U.S. Rep. James Comer (R-KY) on Sunday made a comment that had a fellow MAGA ally spitting out her coffee.

Comer over the weekend appeared on Fox News, where he said he is noticing that Trump has been "more active" on Truth Social, where the president is "encouraging his own Justice Department" to hold people accountable for crimes.

This didn't sit well with Laura Loomer, who has Trump's ear and has even been known to decode his social media posts. She weighed in on Sunday.

"Just now on Fox News, [Comer] said he’s glad President Trump is active on social media encouraging the DOJ to hold people accountable," Loomer wrote, adding a clown emoji. "Uh, why does Trump have to tell his own AG to hold people accountable?"

Loomer continued, saying, "If our AG wasn’t Blondi, maybe people actually would be held accountable."

"Just another indictment of her total incompetence," she then added. "How embarrassing. I spit my coffee out."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Call for Justice or Just More Social Media Antics?

In a recent spectacle that underscores the theatrical absurdity of modern American politics, U.S. Representative James Comer (R-KY) expressed his delight on Fox News over former President Trump's "active" engagement on Truth Social. Trump, according to Comer, has been vigorously "encouraging his own Justice Department" to hold people accountable for crimes. This statement, as one might expect in the convoluted web of MAGA politics, did not land softly across the board. Laura Loomer, a figure with close ties to Trump and a penchant for "decoding" his social media escapades, found Comer's remarks laughably outrageous.

The Irony of Accountability

Loomer's reaction is a fascinating spectacle of irony. Her disbelief at the notion that Trump must personally prod his Attorney General into action is telling. "Uh, why does Trump have to tell his own AG to hold people accountable?" she asks, highlighting a bizarre expectation of autonomy in an administration marked by loyalty over legality. Loomer's critique of the Attorney General, whom she derisively nicknames "Blondi," points to a deeper malaise within the Trump camp—a dissatisfaction with the performance of their own appointed officials.

MAGA Infighting: A Symptom of a Larger Malady

What stands out here is not just the infighting within the MAGA camp—though Loomer's public spat with Comer is emblematic of the movement's fractious nature. More significantly, it's the blurring lines between governance and spectacle. Trump's use of social media to ostensibly direct the Justice Department underscores a profound departure from traditional mechanisms of power. It raises the question: Are we witnessing the reduction of presidential influence to mere social media posturing?

The Clown Emoji: A Fitting Symbol

Loomer's use of the clown emoji in her critique of Comer's comments is perhaps the most fitting symbol of the entire debacle. It reflects the circus-like atmosphere that has enveloped American politics, where serious discussions about justice and accountability are reduced to social media banter and emoji-laden tirades.

Conclusion: The Theatre of the Absurd

In sum, James Comer's remarks and Laura Loomer's reaction are but acts in the ongoing theatre of the absurd that is contemporary American politics. The expectation that a former president should use social media to direct the Justice Department's actions is a damning indictment of the state of the U.S. political system. It's a system where the lines between serious governance and social media spectacle have not just blurred—they've been utterly obliterated.
