Watch: Texas Dem locked in House abruptly ends bathroom call after 'felony' threat

Democratic Texas state Rep. Nicole Collier, who was locked in the House chamber after refusing a police escort, faced a felony threat for taking a call about redistricting in the facility's bathroom.

The incident occurred on Wednesday during a Democratic National Committee (DNC) call with Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) and California Gov. Gavin Newsom (D).

"These maps will prevent Black and brown individuals from selecting the candidate of their choice because they're cracking and packing these districts," Collier explained before the call ended. "We're going to fight as much as we can, whether it's on the rules, or it's even on the policy, we will continue to push back on the Republicans' quest to dilute our voice."

Moments later, Collier announced that she had been threatened with a felony.

"Sorry, I have to leave. They said it's a felony for me to do this. Apparently, I can't be on the floor or in the bathroom," she said.

"Well, you told me I was only allowed to be here in the bathroom," the lawmaker complained to someone off camera before adding, "No, hang on. Bye, everybody. I've got to go."

Booker responded with outrage.

"Representative Collier in the bathroom has more dignity than Donald Trump in the Oval Office," he insisted. "What they're trying to do right there is silence an American leader, silence a Black woman. And that is outrageous. And I hope everybody took note of that. The fact that she can't even let her voice be heard is freaking outrageous."

"What we just witnessed them trying to shut her down and say it's illegal for her to be in the bathroom, and on this call, this is the lengths that they're going to in Texas to try to bring about a system so unjust," he added. "I hope that, I hope people felt what just happened like I did, because I've watched for too long in this country as women like her, as they come after people like her to try to silence her, to try to silence them. But no more. This is not going to happen."

Our Analysis:

The Absurd Theatre of Texas Politics: A Scathing Review

The recent debacle involving Democratic Texas State Rep. Nicole Collier is a stark reminder of the lengths to which political entities will go to maintain power, often at the expense of democracy and basic human dignity. Collier's ordeal—a felony threat for simply participating in a Democratic National Committee (DNC) call about redistricting in a bathroom—encapsulates the farcical yet deeply troubling tactics employed in the political arena.

A Felony for a Phone Call?

Let's dissect the absurdity of the situation. Collier was threatened with a felony for taking a call in the only place she was allegedly allowed to be at the time: a bathroom. This isn't just a minor overreach; it's a grotesque misuse of power aimed at silencing opposition. The call in question wasn't a clandestine operation or a breach of national security. It was a discussion on redistricting—a process that should be transparent and fair, not shrouded in secrecy and manipulation.

Redistricting or Rigging?

The heart of the matter lies in Collier's poignant critique before the call was abruptly ended. She exposed the stark reality of the situation: "These maps will prevent Black and brown individuals from selecting the candidate of their choice because they're cracking and packing these districts." This isn't just a technical issue. It's a fundamental assault on the democratic principle of fair representation.

The act of "cracking and packing" districts to dilute the voice of minority communities is a shameful strategy that undermines the very fabric of democracy. It's a clear indication that the political system is malfunctioning, prioritizing power over principle.

A Struggle for Dignity

Senator Cory Booker's response to the incident sheds light on the broader implications of silencing voices like Collier's. His outrage is palpable and justified. "Representative Collier in the bathroom has more dignity than Donald Trump in the Oval Office," he remarked, highlighting the irony and injustice of the situation. The attempt to silence Collier is not just an attack on her but an attack on the principles she stands for: fairness, representation, and the fight against systemic racism.

Booker's statement, "What they're trying to do right there is silence an American leader, silence a Black woman," underscores a disturbing trend in American politics: the systematic suppression of minority voices. This is not just a Texas problem; it's an American problem. It reflects a broken system that resorts to legal threats and intimidation to maintain the status quo.

The Verdict

The incident involving Rep. Nicole Collier is a microcosm of the systemic issues plaguing American politics. It's a vivid illustration of how power dynamics are manipulated to silence opposition and maintain control. The use of a felony threat to prevent a lawmaker from participating in a call about redistricting in a bathroom is not just absurd; it's an affront to democracy.

This episode should serve as a wake-up call. The fight for fair representation and against the dilution of minority voices is not just Collier's fight; it's a fight for the soul of American democracy. The egregious tactics witnessed in Texas are a symptom of a deeper malaise—a systemic error that needs urgent correction.
