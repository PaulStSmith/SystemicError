The Real Story Behind Stephen Miller’s Attack on the 14th Amendment

Stephen Miller’s latest maneuver to influence the Texas state legislature to deny public education to undocumented immigrant children is not just a policy proposal—it’s a direct assault on the 14th Amendment and an echo of a darker past. This strategy, as dissected by New York Times columnist Jamelle Bouie, reveals a deeper, troubling aim: to reshape American society by revisiting and potentially overturning established constitutional protections.

A Strategic Play Using Institutional Power

Miller, a key architect behind President Donald Trump’s immigration policies, is leveraging the Republican majority in Texas as a tactical play against the Supreme Court’s 1982 ruling in Plyler v. Doe. This landmark decision mandates states to provide free public education to undocumented children just as they do for children of legal residents. By pushing this agenda, Miller is not merely testing the waters; he’s probing the current Supreme Court’s stance on a long-standing precedent.

The Historical and Political Context

The implications of Miller’s push extend beyond immediate legal battles—it touches on the very essence of the 14th Amendment. Originating from the ashes of the Civil War, this amendment was a cornerstone in redefining citizenship and rights in America, aiming to dismantle the racial hierarchies that slavery had entrenched. Bouie’s exploration of the amendment’s genesis underscores its role in establishing birthright citizenship and equal protection under the law, notions that Miller’s proposal seeks to undermine.

The Misdirection and Historical Revisionism

It’s crucial to note here that while Miller and his supporters might frame this move as a response to illegal immigration, the underlying motivations, as Bouie suggests, seem to reflect an enduring desire to reinstate a societal hierarchy based on race and nationality. This is not just a policy debate but a historical struggle against the very principles that define a free and equal society.

Justice Brennan’s words in the Plyler v. Doe decision remind us that denying education to undocumented children prevents them from integrating and contributing to the nation’s progress. Miller’s proposal, therefore, does not just threaten these children, but also the future societal fabric of the United States.

Conclusion: Guarding Against Historical Erosion

Stephen Miller’s actions, supported by the MAGA movement, represent a clear intent to revisit and possibly revise the fundamental aspects of American constitutional rights. This is more than a legal challenge; it’s a political strategy aimed at reshaping societal norms and values. It’s imperative to recognize the broader implications of such maneuvers and the historical contexts in which they are rooted.

Bouie’s call to counter these efforts with a “positive political vision” is not just a defensive stance but a necessary strategy to affirm the principles of equality and justice that the 14th Amendment enshrines. As observers and participants in this ongoing narrative, it is essential to look beyond the legalistic interpretations and understand the broader political battles at play. The fight against such regressive measures is not only about protecting the rights of a few but about defending the foundational values that hold the promise of America for all its people.
