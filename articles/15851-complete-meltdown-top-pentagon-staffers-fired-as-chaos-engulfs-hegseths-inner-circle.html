'Complete meltdown': Top Pentagon staffers fired as 'chaos' engulfs Hegseth’s inner circle

An exodus of top staffers reporting directly to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is apparently roiling the Pentagon as part of a growing investigation into potential leaks.

Politico reported Friday that three senior Pentagon officials have now been fired as part of the leak investigation: Hegseth senior advisor Dan Caldwell, deputy chief of staff Darin Selnick and Colin Carroll — who was Deputy Defense Secretary Stephen Feinberg's chief of staff — have all been officially terminated. And Joe Kasper, who was Hegseth's own chief of staff, is now leaving the Pentagon for a separate job in the Pentagon.

With these staffing changes, the top United States military official is now without a chief of staff, a deputy chief of staff and a senior advisor, less than 100 days into his tenure. An unnamed "senior defense official" suggested to Politico that Hegseth made poor choices for who to trust in his inner circle, with another source saying there would likely be more "chaos" to follow in the coming days.

“There is a complete meltdown in the building, and this is really reflecting on the secretary’s leadership,” the source said. “Pete Hegseth has surrounded himself with some people who don’t have his interests at heart.”

Additional firings may be announced soon. According to CBS senior White House reporter Jennifer Jacobs, "at least one uniformed Pentagon official" was also fired in addition to Hegseth's top aides. Chris Meagher, who was assistant Defense secretary for public affairs during former President Joe Biden's administration, opined that the exodus of top staff at the Pentagon was proof that the former part-time weekend Fox News host may not be capable of heading the Department of Defense.

"Everyone knew that Pete Hegseth did not possess the leadership qualities, background, or experience to be Secretary of Defense,” Meagher told Politico. “Everything we’ve seen since then — the firing of several American heroes because of perceived lack of loyalty, the sloppiness of Signalgate, the complete lack of transparency, and now several political staff being shown the door — has only confirmed he doesn’t have what it takes to lead."

As Meagher mentioned, the current leak investigation isn't Hegseth's first scandal as secretary of Defense. In March, Hegseth shared highly sensitive attack plans for airstrikes on Houthi rebels in Yemen on a Signal group text thread with other top administration officials. The details of that chat were made public by Atlantic magazine editor-in-chief Jeffrey Goldberg, who was inadvertently added to the chat by National Security Advisor Mike Waltz.

Our Analysis:

Pentagon in Peril: Hegseth's Hapless Leadership

In what reads like a script torn straight from a political thriller, the Pentagon finds itself embroiled in a saga of leaks, firings, and alleged incompetence at the highest levels of defense leadership. The central figure in this drama, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, appears to be navigating a ship that's taking on water from all sides. But is this merely a case of a few bad apples, or does it point to a more systemic rot under Hegseth's command?

The Exodus Explained

Politico's report outlines a distressing scene at the Pentagon: a significant exodus of top staffers, including Hegseth’s senior advisor Dan Caldwell, deputy chief of staff Darin Selnick, and Colin Carroll, alongside the departure of Joe Kasper, Hegseth’s chief of staff. This leaves the top U.S. military official startlingly isolated less than 100 days into his tenure. The cause? A growing investigation into potential leaks that has already claimed several high-profile casualties.

Leadership or Lack Thereof?

A "senior defense official" quoted by Politico hints at a troubling narrative: Hegseth's inability to pick a loyal and effective inner circle. This critique isn't just about personal failings; it's a damning commentary on his capacity to lead one of the most crucial departments of the U.S. government. When the stability of the Pentagon is compromised, it's not just an administrative issue—it's a national security concern.

The Specter of Incompetence

Chris Meagher, a voice from the past administration, doesn't mince words when he describes Hegseth's tenure as proof of his unsuitability for the role. The incidents cited—the firings over perceived disloyalty, the mishandling of classified information (dubbed "Signalgate"), and a general opacity in operations—are symptoms of a leadership style that seems both reckless and unprepared.

The Signalgate Scandal

Perhaps the most glaring error on Hegseth's record is the Signal incident, where highly sensitive military plans were shared in a group text, only to be leaked. This isn't just a faux pas; it's a glaring breach of operational security. When secrets of this magnitude slip through the cracks, it not only undermines the Pentagon's integrity but also puts lives and strategic interests at risk.

Conclusions?

The Politico article paints a picture of a Defense Department in disarray under Hegseth's watch. While it's tempting to lay the blame solely at the feet of disloyal staffers or operational mishaps, the recurring theme of these issues suggests a leadership vacuum at the very top. Hegseth's tenure, marred by scandal and instability, raises critical questions about his fitness for the role and the judgment of those who placed him there.

In the grand scheme of U.S. defense policy and national security, these aren't just administrative hiccups; they're harbingers of potential crisis. As the situation unfolds, one can only hope that the necessary steps are taken to restore confidence and competence at the Pentagon. However, based on the current trajectory, optimism seems more like wishful thinking than a realistic outlook.
