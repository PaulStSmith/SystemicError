CNN torn apart for claiming outrage over Trump’s attack on its reporter

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

CNN Helped Build the Stage Trump Used

Not a Neutral Audience

CNN is trying to act shocked that Donald Trump used the White House Correspondents’ Dinner to humiliate one of its own reporters. That shock is theater. The network chose to show up, chose to help legitimize the event, and chose to treat attendance as apolitical while sitting inside a ritual that turns political power into media pageantry. Afterward, Semafor reported that CNN staffers were furious and some leaders were talking about scaling back next year’s dinner presence.

The Power Was Never in the Room Full of Journalists

The people with actual power in this story were not the outraged commentators on X and not the journalists performing their wounded dignity after the fact. The power sat with Trump, who used the platform to attack Kaitlan Collins with misogynistic and transphobic contempt, and with the media institutions that handed him the microphone and the audience. CNN was not merely targeted. It was part of the machinery that made the attack legible, viral, and profitable.

Outrage After Participation Is Cheap

CNN’s leadership reportedly wanted to attend to celebrate its reporters and avoid “making a political statement” by skipping the event. That is the exact sort of self-protective nonsense that keeps elite journalism trapped inside its own rituals. Refusing to name a political choice does not make it neutral; it makes it cowardly. If the organization believed the dinner mattered as a civic institution, it also had a duty to understand that Trump would use it as a stage for cruelty. That is not a surprise. It is the function.

Trump Did Not Derail the Event. He Revealed It.

The dinner was already delayed because of an attempted shooting at the earlier scheduled event. That alone should have stripped away the fantasy that this is some harmless industry banquet. Instead, CNN still showed up, still elevated the room, and still let the spectacle roll on. When Trump attacked Collins and CNN, he did not break the format. He exploited it exactly as designed: provoke, dominate attention, force the press into a posture of offended helplessness, then let the whole ecosystem circulate the clip.

The Real Failure Is Institutional Cowardice

The sharpest criticism here is not that CNN attended one dinner. It is that major media outlets keep confusing proximity to power with accountability for power. They want to be seen as guardians of democracy while behaving like guests at a palace event. That is how authoritarian politics gets normalized: not only through overt propaganda, but through institutions that keep mistaking access, ceremony, and self-congratulation for resistance.

What This Story Actually Shows

This is not a lesson about one rude president and one embarrassed network. It is a story about how elite media helps reproduce the political culture that humiliates it. Trump attacks, the press reacts, the outrage becomes content, and the institution that helped stage the whole thing calls itself victimized. If journalism wants to matter, it has to stop laundering power through social rituals that reward the very people degrading democratic life.
