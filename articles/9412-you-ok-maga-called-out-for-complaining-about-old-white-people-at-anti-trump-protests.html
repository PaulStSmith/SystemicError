'You ok?': MAGA called out for complaining about 'old white people' at anti-Trump protests

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Supporters wearing ‘MAGA’ caps gather ahead of a campaign rally featuring Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump in Grand Rapids, Michigan, U.S., November 4, 2024. REUTERS/Emily Elconin

Multiple MAGA commentators noted an abundance of older people protesting Donald Trump at “No Kings” protests across the country, leading one ex-MSNBC host to call them out.

Tony Ortiz (Current Revolt) posted a video on X on Saturday, writing, “Currently at the No Kings protest in Carrollton, TX and it’s almost exclusively old white people.”

That led to Sen. Ted Cruz sharing the video along with laughing emojis and the words, “Boomers gotta boom….”

Democratic activist Michelle Kinney replied to Cruz, writing, “You ok pumpkin?

MAGA commentator Benny Johnson separately posted video of the protests, writing, “I’ve seen dozens of videos and I’m convinced there’s not a single person under 50 at these ‘No Kings’ protests.”

Brian Mistrot, a “politically homeless conservative,” replied to Johnson, writing, “At least 80% here are under 50. But keep telling yourself that.”

Ex-MSNBC voice Mehdi Hasan noticed the pattern, writing on X, “Republicans are now complaining about old white people.”

“Now I’ve seen everything lol,” he added on Saturday.

Our Analysis:

The Ageist Mockery of MAGA Commentators: A Closer Look

In a recent wave of protests titled “No Kings,” aimed presumably at Donald Trump’s authoritarian tendencies, an interesting narrative has emerged courtesy of MAGA commentators and their allies. They’ve taken to social media to belittle the protests, focusing not on the content of the protesters’ grievances but rather on the age and racial demographics of those in attendance. This strategy, while perhaps entertaining to their base, underscores a deeper issue within the political discourse propagated by these commentators.

Tony Ortiz of Current Revolt and Benny Johnson are among those who have highlighted the purported predominance of “old white people” at these protests. Their comments are not just observations; they are dismissive jabs aimed at undermining the legitimacy of the protests based on the age of participants. Ortiz’s video, shared by Sen. Ted Cruz with a quip about “Boomers,” and Johnson’s assertion of a lack of youth presence paint a picture of a movement they deem irrelevant merely because of the age of its supporters.

However, their narrative is not only flawed but also misleading. Brian Mistrot, self-described as a “politically homeless conservative,” counters Johnson’s claim, stating that “at least 80% here are under 50.” This discrepancy in observation hints at a selective portrayal by MAGA commentators, designed to belittle and dismiss rather than engage with the substance of the protests.

The reaction from Mehdi Hasan, an ex-MSNBC host, captures the irony of the situation perfectly. Republicans, traditionally seen as the party of older, white demographics, are now ridiculing a movement for supposedly embodying these very characteristics. Hasan’s bemusement is shared by many who see this as a hypocritical and cynical maneuver.

What This Says About the Current State of Political Discourse

This episode reveals much about the state of political commentary, especially from MAGA-aligned figures. It’s a demonstration of how ageism and a dismissive attitude towards legitimate political expression are utilized to sidestep substantive debate. Instead of addressing the reasons behind the “No Kings” protests, these commentators have chosen to attack the protesters themselves in the most superficial manner possible.

The Use of Ageism and Diversion: By focusing on the age of the protesters, MAGA commentators are engaging in a classic diversion tactic. It’s easier to mock the messenger than to address the message, especially when the message is a critique of authoritarian tendencies within their ranks. This approach is not only intellectually lazy but also deeply disrespectful to the democratic process.

The Irony of the Critique: The irony of MAGA commentators mocking a movement for being composed of “old white people” cannot be overstated. It reflects a lack of self-awareness or, perhaps, a cynical attempt to weaponize demographics against their opponents, ignoring the diversity of thought and background within their own support base.

Conclusion

The mockery of the “No Kings” protests by certain MAGA commentators is a telling episode. It reveals a preference for ad hominem attacks over substantive debate, a troubling trend in political discourse. Ageism and racial demographics should not be the focus of political commentary; the focus should be on the issues at hand.

As the political landscape continues to evolve, one hopes for a shift towards more meaningful engagement with the issues. The citizens protesting under the “No Kings” banner, regardless of their age, are participating in the democratic process—a process that deserves respect, not ridicule.
