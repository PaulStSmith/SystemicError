'Gaslighting the people': MAGA lawmaker breaks with Trump on key election issue

President Donald Trump’s insistence that affordability isn’t an issue amid rising prices is leading one MAGA lawmaker to break with the president and accuse him of “gaslighting voters.”

“Gaslighting the people and trying to tell them that prices have come down is not helping,” said Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) while appearing on former White House Press Secretary Sean Spicer’s show, NPR reported Friday.

“It’s actually infuriating people because people know what they are paying at the grocery store, they know what they’re paying for their kid’s clothes and school supplies. They know what they’re paying for their electricity bills.”

Trump has repeatedly claimed that the economy is improving under his second administration; “costs are way down,” Trump said Wednesday in the Oval Office; “the affordability is much better with the Republicans,” he said last week, telling reporters he didn’t “want to hear about affordability.”

However, voters aren’t buying Trump’s assessment of the economy, according to the data. A recent poll from YouGov showed that the number of Americans who say the economy is growing worse skyrocketed to 54% as of Nov. 1, an increase of 18 percentage points from when Trump took office in January. That same poll showed that 39% described the economy as poor, an increase of 8 percentage points since Trump took office.

Even Trump’s former economic advisor is sounding the alarm that the president is underwater with voters when it comes to affordability, telling NPR Friday that voters aren’t buying his pitch anymore that costs are trending down.

“It is true factually that the average family has more purchasing power today than they did when Biden left office,” said Stephen Moore, a conservative economist who advised Trump during his first administration, speaking with NPR.

“And yet people don’t feel it. You know, they’re not feeling the love. And I can’t explain why that is except that people tend to focus on things where their prices are rising. People are kind of in a crabby mood right now when it comes to the economy.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Delusions: A Reality Check

In a stunning display of disconnection from the everyday realities of American citizens, President Donald Trump continues to tout an economic fairy tale, one where “costs are way down” and “affordability is much better with the Republicans.” This narrative, as bold as it is blatantly false, has finally pushed one MAGA lawmaker, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), to call out the president for what she describes as “gaslighting voters.”

The Affordability Mirage

While Trump parades around in his own economic wonderland, the citizens he vows to serve are left to grapple with a starkly different reality. According to a recent YouGov poll, a whopping 54% of Americans believe the economy is worsening—an 18 percentage point increase since Trump’s inauguration. Furthermore, 39% of Americans describe the economy as poor, marking an 8 percentage point increase under Trump’s watch. These numbers are not just statistics; they are a resounding rejection of Trump’s economic fantasies.

A MAGA Defection?

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s public rebuke of Trump on Sean Spicer’s show is not just noteworthy because it signals a crack in the MAGA facade; it’s a rare moment of truth-telling in a party often seen marching in lockstep with Trump’s every whim and fantasy. Greene’s acknowledgment of the palpable frustration among voters over rising costs—be it groceries, children’s clothing, or electricity bills—underscores a fundamental truth: people are not blind to the realities of their financial struggles, no matter how much Trump attempts to convince them otherwise.

The Dissonance of Trump’s Economic Advisors

Even Stephen Moore, a conservative economist and a former advisor to Trump, admits to NPR that despite claims of increased purchasing power since Biden’s departure, the American people “don’t feel it.” Moore’s bewildered admission that people are focusing on the areas where prices are rising rather than feeling grateful for supposed overall improvements is telling. It highlights a fundamental misreading or perhaps a willful ignorance of the public’s daily experiences and concerns.

The Bottom Line

Trump’s insistence on a thriving economy under his leadership flies in the face of reality for most Americans. This dissonance is not just a matter of differing perspectives; it’s a dangerous game of denial that threatens to further alienate voters who are already struggling to make ends meet. When even members of his own party and former advisors struggle to square Trump’s economic narratives with the lived experiences of the American people, it’s clear that the issue isn’t just about affordability—it’s about credibility.

In the end, Trump’s economic gaslighting may prove to be a costly miscalculation, as voters, weary of being told their hardships are figments of their imagination, seek leaders who not only recognize their struggles but are also willing to address them head-on. The question remains: will Trump’s economic fantasies catch up with him, or will voters continue to see through the smoke and mirrors? Only time will tell, but one thing is for certain—the gap between Trump’s economic narrative and the American reality is growing ever wider, and no amount of gaslighting can bridge that divide.
