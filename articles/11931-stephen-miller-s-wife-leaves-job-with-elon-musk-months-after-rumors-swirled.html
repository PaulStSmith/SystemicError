Stephen Miller's wife leaves job with Elon Musk months after rumors swirled

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 8, 20254 min read

White House deputy chief of staff for policy Stephen Miller attends the annual White House Easter Egg Roll with his family, including his wife, Katie Miller, left, on the South Lawn of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 21, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

Katie Miller, a former White House aide and wife of deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller, is ending her work for Elon Musk and starting up a self-titled podcast.

The 33-year-old Miller had been a liaison between the White House and Musk when he oversaw the Department of Government Efficiency, but she followed him back to the private sector when he left the government at the end of May, leading to the swirling of rumors, and now she's leaving her post supporting the tech mogul's ventures and taking on investors to launch her online talk show, reported Axios.

"For MAGA and President Trump's legacy to grow long-term, we must talk to conservative women," Miller said.

Miller envisions her podcast as "a place for conservative women to gather online," and while she doesn't intend to focus on politics, she also pointed out there's "no conservative answer to 'Call Her Daddy,'" the hugely popular, pop culture-focused podcast hosted by Alex Cooper.

"There isn't a place for a mom like me to get lifestyle information, news, laugh with our friends, gossip about what's going on in the world from our perspective," Miller said. The podcast will be "about women, for women — with men, too, talking about what matters to women."

Miller interviewed Vice President JD Vance on Wednesday for her introductory podcast, and she has also talked with boxing legend Mike Tyson and former ESPN broadcaster Sage Steele, and she said her husband has been "incredibly supportive" of her new venture and has even helped her land guests.

"I'm incredibly grateful to President Trump and Elon for the ability to enact lasting change," Miller said. "We got a lot done. ... We reshaped how the federal bureaucracy views government spending. I hope Elon is a listener to the podcast and I hope to have him as a guest one day."

Our Analysis:

The Miller Podcast: A Strategic Move Cloaked in Casual Attire

In an era where the lines between politics, entertainment, and media are increasingly blurred, the launch of Katie Miller's self-titled podcast is not just another drop in the ocean of digital talk shows. It's a calculated move, emblematic of the broader strategies employed by political figures and their affiliates to maintain relevance and influence. Let's dissect the layers of this seemingly innocuous announcement and unearth the strategic implications beneath.

Katie Miller's Transition: From Politics to Podcasting

First, let's address the elephant in the room: Katie Miller, former White House aide and spouse to Stephen Miller, a figure notorious for his hardline immigration policies under the Trump administration, is making a pivot. This pivot from a behind-the-scenes political operative to a public-facing media personality is not merely a career change; it's a strategic positioning. The choice to launch a podcast “for conservative women” and, by extension, “with men, too”, signals a clear intent to cultivate a specific audience demographic, one that feels underserved or misrepresented by mainstream media outlets.

The MAGA Movement's Media Strategy

Miller's assertion that “for MAGA and President Trump's legacy to grow long-term, we must talk to conservative women” is a telling admission of the MAGA movement's strategy. It's an acknowledgment of the critical role media and messaging play in sustaining political movements. By framing her podcast as a space “about women, for women” yet simultaneously ensuring it's not pigeonholed as exclusively political, Miller is attempting to broaden the appeal of conservative ideologies under the guise of lifestyle content. This approach is not novel but remains effective, as seen in the success of platforms like Fox News in blending political commentary with entertainment.

The Unaddressed Contradiction

Amid the strategic underpinnings, there's an inherent contradiction in Miller's endeavor. The claim that there's “no conservative answer to 'Call Her Daddy,'” a podcast known for its liberal stance on sexual liberation and feminism, juxtaposed with the intent to create a space for conservative women, raises questions about the content's nature and appeal. Can a platform rooted in conservative values replicate the success of its liberal counterparts without engaging in the politics it seeks to influence? This remains to be seen.

The Influence Game

The mention of high-profile guests like JD Vance, Mike Tyson, and the potential of featuring Elon Musk, underscores the podcast's ambition not just as a forum for discussion, but as a tool for networking and influence. By leveraging her connections within the political and business realms, Miller is ensuring her podcast becomes a nexus for conservative thought leaders, thereby amplifying the reach and impact of the ideas discussed therein.

Conclusion: A Calculated Move with Broader Implications

In essence, Katie Miller's foray into podcasting is more than a personal or professional pivot; it's a microcosm of the broader strategies employed by political movements to infiltrate and influence cultural and media landscapes. Whether this venture will succeed in its mission to become a rallying point for conservative women—or if it will merely echo in the chambers of its existing base—remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the battle for influence has expanded well beyond the traditional arenas of politics, and the lines will continue to blur as figures like Miller navigate these intersecting worlds.
