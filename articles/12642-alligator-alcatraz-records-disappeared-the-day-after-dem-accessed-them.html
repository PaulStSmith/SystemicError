'Alligator Alcatraz' records disappeared the day after Dem accessed them

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security posted this illustration on its X page, with the caption, “Coming soon!.” (Via @DHS.gov)A prominent Democratic state representative in Florida is alleging that private contracts to build and maintain the controversial immigrant detention facility in the Everglades known as "Alligator Alcatraz" vanished from the online Florida Accountability Contract Tracking System just a day after she accessed them, reported the Miami New Times.

"It seems that the DeSantis administration just pulled more than a dozen contracts tied to the Everglades Immigration Detention facility from a public database of state contracts," wrote Anna Eskamani, now running for mayor of Orlando, in a post on X. "These contracts total more than $200 million in taxpayer spending, and they are public records."

"According to Eskamani, other contractors whose contract documents are no longer in the database include Gothams LLC, Garner Environmental Services, SLSCO LTD, Lemoine CDR Logistics LLC, CDR Health Care Inc., and Meridian Rapid Defense Group," noted the report. The Florida government" paid Miami-based CDR Health, an affiliate company of FIU Board of Trustees Chair Carlos Duart's CDR Companies, $17.5 million. His wife, Tina Vidal-Duart, is the CEO of a healthcare consulting firm."

Alligator Alcatraz was billed by Florida state officials as a facility for the "worst of the worst," but recent reporting indicates that it has been filled largely by highway patrol pulling people over for minor issues and uncovering their immigration status.

Already, horror stories are emerging of the conditions within the camp, including wild temperature swings, flooding, and infestations of giant insects.

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis hailed the construction of this facility and indicated his desire to build more of them, but this week appeared to backtrack on that idea.

Our Analysis:

The Alligator Alcatraz Controversy: A Closer Look

In what reads like a script from a dystopian novel, the state of Florida finds itself embroiled in a scandal surrounding the "Alligator Alcatraz"—a detention facility for immigrants tucked away in the Everglades. The Miami New Times recently shed light on allegations by a Democratic state representative that contracts related to the construction and maintenance of this facility mysteriously disappeared from a public database. This isn't just a case of bureaucratic oversight; it's a glaring example of the cloak-and-dagger tactics that seem to be at play under Governor Ron DeSantis's administration.

Vanishing Acts and Taxpayer Dollars

Representative Anna Eskamani, now eyeing the mayor's office in Orlando, pointed out the vanishing act of over a dozen contracts from the Florida Accountability Contract Tracking System. These were not inconsequential deals; they were contracts amounting to more than $200 million of taxpayer money. The contracts in question were linked to companies like Gothams LLC, Garner Environmental Services, and notably, CDR Health—a company affiliated with Carlos Duart, Chair of the FIU Board of Trustees, and helmed by his wife. The removal of these contracts from public view raises not just eyebrows but serious questions about transparency and accountability in the DeSantis administration.

"Worst of the Worst" or Just Caught Driving?

Alligator Alcatraz was pitched as a necessary evil, a place to house the "worst of the worst." Yet, reality paints a starkly different picture. It seems the facility's inhabitants are largely those unlucky enough to be pulled over for minor traffic infractions, only to have their immigration status brought to light. The conditions within the facility are nothing short of horrific, with reports of extreme temperature fluctuations, flooding, and insect infestations.

Governor DeSantis, who once championed the construction of this facility and entertained the idea of more like it, has seemingly backtracked. Could it be the public outcry or the light shined on this dark corner of his administration's policies that's causing this change of heart?

Analysis

The story of Alligator Alcatraz is a microcosm of a larger issue at play within the DeSantis administration and, broadly, within the current state of political accountability. The disappearance of contracts linked to a controversial facility raises significant concerns about the administration's commitment to transparency. It's not just about the hiding of documents; it's about what those documents represent—a hefty sum of taxpayer money funneled into projects that appear to contravene basic human rights and decency.

Moreover, the involvement of companies closely tied to political figures in the DeSantis orbit suggests a troubling confluence of interests that merits further investigation. The case of CDR Health receiving $17.5 million is particularly egregious, hinting at a possible quid pro quo that should concern every Floridian.

Conclusion

"Alligator Alcatraz" is more than a detention center; it's a symbol of the systemic issues plaguing the current administration's approach to immigration, transparency, and the use of public funds. As Floridians, and indeed as Americans, we must demand better. We deserve a government that operates in the light, not in the shadows of the Everglades. Governor DeSantis's administration owes the public answers and, more importantly, a commitment to rectify these egregious lapses in judgment and governance.
