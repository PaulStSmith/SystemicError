The mask slips as Susan Collins desperately panics

Based on an article by Michael Signorile, The Signorile Report

Susan Collins and the Fraud of the Moderate Mask

The Record Is the Clue

Susan Collins is not being misunderstood. She is being re-read. The source material points to the basic fact that matters: she has spent years presenting herself as a centrist conscience while repeatedly delivering power to Senate Republicans and Donald Trump when it counted. The Kavanaugh vote, and her refusal to say she regrets it, did more than alienate voters. It exposed the operating system.

Power Before Principle

Collins’s recent interviews read like a politician who knows her brand is decaying and is willing to damage anyone nearby to keep the seat. Her jab about Troy Jackson’s family life was not “candor.” It was desperation with a smile. So was her dismissal of Liz Cheney as having “no impact,” as if principle matters only when it pays immediate electoral dividends. That is not moderation. It is careerist contempt for anyone who treats politics as more than a vote-counting exercise.

The Mask Only Works One Way

The article correctly shows how Collins’s supposed moderation has always been selective. She could posture as an ally to LGBTQ voters when that image had value. Now, with the same cold opportunism that defines so much of Republican politics, she is signing anti-trans petitions and benefiting from ads attacking Jackson on transgender issues. The pattern is not confusion. It is adaptation to the party’s base instincts while preserving a polished outer shell for Maine voters who were supposed to believe she was above all that.

Money, Networks, and Useful Blindness

Her willingness to shrug at Elon Musk’s support says more than any stump speech. Musk’s PAC is spending money to help elect her, while his platform has become a sewer for extremist content and sexualized Grok controversies. Collins cannot plausibly sell herself as a guardian of decency while accepting help from that machinery and pretending not to know what it is. The reporter could have pressed harder, but the larger truth is already clear: elite Republican survival now runs through plutocratic patronage, culture-war laundering, and strategic amnesia.

The Real Story Is the System

This is not simply about one senator’s hypocrisy. It is about how a supposedly “moderate” Republican can keep receiving institutional rewards while helping normalize authoritarian politics, anti-LGBTQ escalation, and cynical personal attacks. Collins’s problem is not that the mask slipped once. It is that the mask has been doing most of the work for years. The broader lesson is blunt: democratic politics cannot keep mistaking polished branding for democratic behavior, especially when the actual votes, alliances, and beneficiaries keep pointing the other way.
