'This is America!' Sickened Morning Joe shames Stephen Miller with heartbreaking photo

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Mika Brzezinski, Joe Scarborough (MS NOW screenshot)

Donald Trump advisor Stephen Miller was raked over the coals on MS NOW on Monday morning after “Morning Joe” co-host Joe Scarborough shared a New York Times report about small children being left behind as their parents are abducted and deported by ICE.

To make his point, he held up the paper which showed a photo of a two-year-old child named Jorge, whose mother was grabbed and shipped out of the country in error.

Discussing Donald Trump’s cratering popularity, Scarborough suggested Miller is contributing to the president’s collapse, even among Republicans, over Miller’s heartless round-up of immigrants which has infuriated communities.

“What is serious, though, is this New York Times, you actually have children, children being left alone without their parents,” he explained as he held up a physical copy of the Times.

“Because you have the administration so obsessed on reaching their numbers,” he added before accusing, “And Stephen Miller thinks this is a good idea. This is Stephen Miller, thinks this is a good idea and Donald Trump lets it happen. Can we zoom in on this picture, please? Donald Trump lets this happen. Where children, here’s a two-year-old who are (sic) left without their parents because they’re trying to meet quotas that we said from the beginning, and we’ll say it again, quotas that will never, ever be met.”

Tossing aside the paper, he added, “This is America in 2025.”

“It’s that image and that headline why Donald Trump’s numbers on immigration are so underwater,” a disgusted Jonathan Lemire contributed. “This was supposed to be a signature issue. Americans are saying that’s too much. Outside, maybe that thrills the hardest core faction of his base, but we know that the numbers they set out, the millions they want deported are not going to happen. And that though Americans, you know, in 2024 showed this, they want the southern border to be managed better.”

Our Analysis:

The Harsh Reality Behind Stephen Miller’s Immigration Policies

In a segment that cuts deep into the heart of America’s immigration debate, MS NOW co-host Joe Scarborough sheds light on a harrowing story reported by The New York Times: small children, including a two-year-old named Jorge, abandoned as their parents are forcibly taken and deported by ICE. This isn’t just a tragic anecdote; it’s a damning illustration of the Trump administration’s ruthless immigration policies, with Stephen Miller, a senior advisor to Donald Trump, at the helm.

The Unforgiving Numbers Game

Scarborough’s critique zeroes in on the obsession of the Trump administration with “reaching their numbers,” a pursuit so fervent that it seemingly blinds them to the human cost. The image of young Jorge, left parentless, serves as a potent symbol of this policy’s cruelty. The administration’s focus on quotas—described by Scarborough with palpable disdain—reveals a bureaucratic heartlessness that underpins their approach. It’s a stark reminder that behind the sterile numbers are real human lives shattered.

Stephen Miller’s Controversial Role

Stephen Miller’s influence on Trump’s immigration stance can’t be overstated. Known for his hardline views, Miller’s policies have not only drawn criticism for their severity but have also been implicated in the broader decline of Trump’s popularity, even among Republicans. Scarborough’s assertion that Miller’s “heartless round-up of immigrants” has “infuriated communities” is a testament to the widespread backlash against these policies. It’s clear that Miller, and by extension Trump who allows these policies to be enacted, prioritizes aggressive immigration quotas over the well-being of children and families.

A Signature Issue Gone Awry

Jonathan Lemire’s input further emphasizes the point: immigration was supposed to be a cornerstone of Trump’s appeal, yet it has become a source of national dismay. The “signature issue” has backfired spectacularly, with Americans rejecting the administration’s heavy-handed approach. The hardline stance, designed to appease the “hardest core faction” of Trump’s base, has alienated the broader electorate. The failed promise of mass deportations, coupled with a mismanaged southern border, underscores a fundamental misreading of the American public’s appetite for draconian immigration policies.

2025: A Grim Projection

Scarborough’s closing remark, “This is America in 2025,” is a chilling forecast of what could become a norm if such policies continue unchecked. It’s a warning—a call to action for all who are alarmed by the current trajectory of U.S. immigration policy.

In conclusion, the segment on MS NOW isn’t just a critique; it’s a clarion call for compassion, rationality, and a reevaluation of what America stands for. The story of young Jorge and the countless others like him should serve as a sobering reminder of the real costs of policies crafted in the pursuit of questionable quotas. It’s a stark indictment of the Trump administration’s immigration policies, with Stephen Miller’s influence casting a long, dark shadow over the nation’s conscience.
