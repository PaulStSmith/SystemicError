The New York Times on Sunday published an article called, "Can AriZona’s 99-Cent Iced Tea Survive Trump’s Tariffs?" in which the outlet notes that "the price has been AriZona’s calling card for nearly three decades," yet Trump's touted "50 percent tariff on imported aluminum may change that."

The report notes that company co-founder Don Vultaggio may not have an option in increasing prices.

"The Trump administration’s 50 percent tariff on aluminum imports may leave him no choice," according to the report. "If the price of a tallboy of AriZona Iced Tea had kept pace with inflation, the company would today be selling it for $1.99. Instead, the 99-cent price remains so central to the company’s identity that the numbers are displayed on the can boldly and prominently."

According to the report, "AriZona uses more than 100 million pounds of aluminum a year for its cans, and about 20 percent of that comes from Canada."

"Mr. Vultaggio is hopeful that the tariff dispute will be resolved, but if it is not, he said, 'at some point the consumer is going to have to pay the price,'" it states.

The report continues:

"'I hate even the thought of it,' Mr. Vultaggio, 73, said, adding, 'It would be a hell of a shame after 30-plus years.'"

Scott Lincicome, the director of general economics and Cato's Herbert A. Stiefel Center for Trade Policy Studies, responded to the admission from the company co-founder by saying, "Trump's aluminum tariffs may force AriZona’s famous 99-Cent Iced Tea to increase in price for the first time in 3 decades."

"If they come for the Costco hotdog meal, we riot," he added.

Our Analysis:

AriZona's Iconic 99-Cent Iced Tea: A Victim of Tariff Policies?

In an era where the price of living skyrockets, yet wages stagnate, AriZona has stood as a beacon of affordability with its unwavering 99-cent price tag for nearly three decades. Enter the tumultuous trade policies under the Trump administration, and this beacon may soon flicker out. The New York Times' recent article, "Can AriZona’s 99-Cent Iced Tea Survive Trump’s Tariffs?" dives into the heart of this issue, highlighting a stark reality: the Trump administration's 50 percent tariff on imported aluminum threatens to disrupt a pricing tradition that has become a part of AriZona’s identity.

The Price of Tariffs: A Bitter Brew

The article outlines the core issue at hand: the Trump administration's aluminum tariff is not just a statistic; it's a policy decision with tangible effects on American businesses and consumers. AriZona, a company that uses over 100 million pounds of aluminum annually, with about 20 percent sourced from Canada, now faces a dilemma. The tariffs may force a price increase, a move that company co-founder Don Vultaggio finds distasteful and contrary to the brand’s identity.

Inflation vs. Tradition

The narrative woven by The Times is not merely about aluminum; it's a commentary on the broader economic forces at play. If AriZona’s prices had kept pace with inflation, a can of iced tea would be selling for $1.99 today. Yet, the company has fiercely held onto its 99-cent tag, a decision that underscores its commitment to affordability. This stubborn defiance against inflation is emblematic of a deeper ethos – one that may soon be upended by the heavy hand of tariff policies.

A Symptom of a Larger Malaise

What is striking in the report is the palpable resignation from Vultaggio, a sentiment that mirrors the broader despair felt by many American business owners caught in the crossfire of trade wars. The Trump administration's tariffs, presented under the guise of protecting American jobs and industries, here manifest as potential antagonists to consumer affordability and business sustainability.

The End of an Era?

The article ends on a note of uncertainty, with Vultaggio hopeful yet realistic about the future. The potential price increase of AriZona’s iced tea is emblematic of a larger issue: the unintended consequences of trade policies that ripple through the economy, affecting everything from a can of iced tea to the broader consumer price index.

Conclusion: Beyond the Can

The New York Times article is more than a story about a beverage; it's a lens through which we view the complexities of economic policies, their far-reaching impacts, and the real cost of tariffs on the American dream of affordability and entrepreneurship. As Scott Lincicome from Cato's Herbert A. Stiefel Center for Trade Policy Studies quipped, touching on the broader significance of such price hikes, "If they come for the Costco hotdog meal, we riot." This jest underscores a grave reality: when policies touch the pockets of the average American, the backlash could be significant.

In conclusion, the AriZona iced tea saga is a stark reminder of the delicate balance between protecting industries and preserving consumer affordability. It highlights the need for thoughtful policy-making that considers the full spectrum of consequences, including those borne by businesses and consumers who have long relied on the stability of such affordable icons. The potential demise of AriZona's 99-cent iced tea is not just about the end of an era; it's a wake-up call to the real-world impacts of tariff policies.
