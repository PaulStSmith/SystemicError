Controversial Trump official tied to president's racist Obama video as outrage mounts

After a member of Pastors for Trump said he personally demanded that President Donald Trump fire a staffer who the White House claimed was responsible for posting a racist video of Barack and Michelle Obama, an investigative reporter flagged a potential culprit.

Bipartisan fury burned ahead of the weekend after Trump published a video on his Truth Social account in which the last few seconds contained the offensive Obama images. Pastor Mark Burns made comments about a “staffer” that stood out.

“The President made it clear to me that this post was made by a staffer and not by him. My recommendation to the President was direct and firm. That staffer should be fired immediately, and the President should publicly condemn this action,” Burns wrote. “This kind of insensitive and racist communication does not reflect the heart, values, or leadership of the President of the United States, nor does it represent the America we are striving to build.”

According to an investigative reporter with experience in the area, it’s entirely possible that the staffer is the controversial White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller.

Seizing on Burns’ comments, reporter Jason Leopold noted, “YEARS ago, I obtained documents via #FOIA from the Mueller investigation that revealed Stephen Miller sometimes wrote Trump’s tweets.”

The investigative reporter also included a screenshot from FOIA records that stated, “Miller would draft a variety of twitter messages for Trump, Trump would put a check by the ones he wanted, and [Dan] Scavino would send out the tweets.”

Our Analysis:

The Blame Game in the Theatre of the Absurd

In a recent spectacle that underscores the ongoing farce within American politics, a member of Pastors for Trump, Pastor Mark Burns, has thrust himself into the limelight with claims that could easily fuel a season’s worth of political drama. After President Donald Trump shared a video tainted with racist imagery of Barack and Michelle Obama, Burns stepped forward, not with a torch of enlightenment, but with a bucket of gasoline, ready to ignite further controversy.

Burns’ assertion that he personally advised Trump to fire the responsible staffer is a masterclass in political theater. This claim, wrapped in the guise of moral superiority, does little but add another layer of obfuscation to an already murky situation. Pastor Burns, in his grandiose statement, declared, “This kind of insensitive and racist communication does not reflect the heart, values, or leadership of the President of the United States, nor does it represent the America we are striving to build.” Yet, this rhetoric feels hollow in the context of the Trump administration’s history and the divisive nature it has often embraced.

Enter investigative reporter Jason Leopold, who, with the patience of a seasoned angler, casts his line into the murky waters of this controversy, hooking a potential lead on the identity of the so-called staffer. The name Stephen Miller surfaces—a figure synonymous with controversy and the architect behind some of the Trump administration’s most contentious policies. Leopold references documents obtained through the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) from the Mueller investigation, revealing that Miller had a hand in crafting Trump’s tweets. This isn’t just a smoking gun; it’s a fireworks display in the realm of plausible deniability.

The notion that Stephen Miller, Trump’s Deputy Chief of Staff, could be the culprit isn’t far-fetched. It aligns with the pattern of behavior and policy-making observed during Trump’s tenure, where inflammatory and divisive content was not an anomaly but a strategy. Miller drafting tweets for Trump, with the latter approving them with a simple checkmark, paints a picture of a presidency managed not just by the man at the helm but by those he empowers to steer the ship.

The saga of the racist video—and the ensuing blame game—underscores a fundamental issue within the Trump administration and, by extension, American politics: the evasion of accountability. By attributing the post to a nameless staffer, the administration attempts to distance Trump from the fallout, a tactic as transparent as it is tired. Pastor Burns’ role as the intermediary, rather than a beacon of moral clarity, serves to muddy the waters further, allowing Trump to avoid direct responsibility while ostensibly taking action.

In this episode of political theatre, the actors are many, but the script remains the same. The use of scapegoats, the shirking of accountability, and the reliance on controversial figures like Miller to craft the president’s message are not just plot points but indicative of a deeper systemic rot. This isn’t about a single tweet or the individual who drafted it; it’s about a culture of governance that prioritizes provocation over unity, division over discourse.

As this story unfolds, one can’t help but view Pastor Burns’ admonition and the speculation surrounding Stephen Miller with a critical eye. The real tragedy isn’t the act itself but the environment that allows such acts to flourish. In the end, the question isn’t just about who posted the video, but about the leadership and values—or lack thereof—that made such a post conceivable in the first place.
