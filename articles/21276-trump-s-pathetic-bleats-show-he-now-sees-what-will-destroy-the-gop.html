Trump's pathetic bleats show he now sees what will destroy the GOP

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

El-Sayed Isn’t the Story. Oligarchy Is.

A Vote They Can’t Process

The source text begins with Abdul El-Sayed’s victory in Michigan, but the victory is not the point. The point is the panic it triggers in the people who benefit from a political order built to insulate wealth from democracy. The red-scare reflex, the sneering at “democratic socialists,” and the lazy “communism” accusation are not analysis. They are the language of a system trying to defend itself.

What voters are rejecting, in the source’s own terms, is not some abstract ideology. They are rejecting a country where medical debt destroys families, schools are starved, tuition is a racket, voting is deliberately made harder, climate damage is normalized, and the rich buy the rules.

Who Actually Holds Power

The article is right to identify the real power center: billionaires, CEOs, major donors, and the legal-political machine they have built around themselves. The names matter. Lewis Powell’s corporate blueprint. Nixon’s appointment of Powell to the Supreme Court. Bellotti. Citizens United. Reagan’s rise on oil money. The think tanks, media operations, and donor networks that turned public life into a managed asset class.

That is not a misunderstanding of democracy. It is the replacement of democracy with purchased access. When a tiny fraction of families can flood elections with money and outside groups can dominate campaign spending, the outcome is not “influence.” It is capture.

The Misdirection Industry

Republicans want the story to be about socialism because they need a cartoon enemy. Corporate Democrats often help by softening the conflict into civility, procedure, and vague calls for “bipartisanship.” Both serve the same function: they move attention away from ownership of the system and toward theatrical arguments about tone.

That is the real fraud in American political coverage. The public is told to worry about labels while the machinery of rule stays intact. The source names the basic structure plainly: foreign money, corporate power, billionaire money, voter suppression, bought judges, and a political class trained to treat all of it as normal. That is not noise around the system. That is the system.

Reagan Was Not an Era. He Was a Transfer

The article ties the present to the Reagan revolution, and that link is essential. Reaganism did not just cut taxes and break unions. It reoriented government toward the demands of capital and taught the political class to serve wealth as if that were common sense.

That transfer still governs the country. Wages lag. Debt rises. Public goods decay. The rich accumulate more while ordinary people absorb the costs. The source’s examples, from health care to housing to student debt, all point to the same underlying arrangement: the state is used to protect wealth, not to distribute security.

The Democratic Side Has Work To Do

If there is a weakness in the source, it is that it lumps “corporate Democrats” into the same sentence as Republicans and pundits, which is emotionally satisfying but politically incomplete. The more useful critique is sharper: Democrats are the necessary institutional vehicle against authoritarian politics, but too many of them still behave like custodians of managed decline instead of builders of democratic power.

The left does not need flatterers. It needs Democrats who will fight donor capture, stop laundering elite preferences as pragmatism, and build material power for people who actually live under the consequences of policy. El-Sayed’s rise matters because it shows where the energy is: not in donor class reassurance, but in people who are done asking permission from the institutions that failed them.

The Pattern Beneath the Noise

This story is not about one candidate or one election. It is about a country where wealth learned to legislate, litigate, and propagandize itself into permanence. The backlash to progressive politics is so loud because it threatens the one thing oligarchy cannot tolerate: democratic majorities deciding that the economy should work for everyone.

That is the pattern. Voters are not drifting toward chaos. They are pushing back against a rigged order that has spent decades calling theft “freedom” and calling resistance “extremism.”
