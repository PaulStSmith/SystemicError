Trump's biographer says this is the real reason ABC caved to president on Kimmel

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Capitulation of ABC: A Harsh Lesson in Media Cowardice

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media before departing for a state visit to Britain, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 16, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

One of President Donald Trump's biographers revealed on Thursday why ABC capitulated to the Trump administration's seeming threats to remove late-night television host Jimmy Kimmel's show from the air.

Michael Wolff, who has written four books on Trump, discussed ABC's decision to indefinitely suspend "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" on Wednesday on a new episode of the "Inside Trump's Head" podcast that he co-hosts with The Daily Beast's Chief Content Officer Joanna Coles.

Wolff noted that Disney's CEO, Bob Iger, seems like the kind of person who could withstand a barrage from Trump because he is a billionaire in his own right. He framed Iger's response as a pragmatic business decision.

"He realizes that the ire of Donald Trump will be bad for business, and, therefore, we submit to that," Wolff said. "There are many other stakeholders in a media company, and they have to maintain credibility. If you lose that, then you lose the store, would have been the old rationale on this."

"In this instance now, if you lose Donald Trump, you lose the store," Wolff added.

Wolff's comments come at a time when ABC's decision to suspend Kimmel's show is under intense scrutiny. Some media experts have called out the barely veiled threat FCC chair Brendan Carr levied on a MAGA influencer's podcast. Some conservatives have defended the move as within Disney's rights as a private company.

Our Analysis:

The Capitulation of ABC: A Harsh Lesson in Media Cowardice

In a recent revelation that should surprise precisely no one yet somehow still manages to drop jaws, we've been handed yet another glaring example of the pervasive, unyielding influence wielded by Donald Trump, even outside the Oval Office. Michael Wolff, a biographer with a penchant for burrowing into the Trump psyche, has illuminated the murky circumstances surrounding ABC's indefensible decision to indefinitely suspend "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" This act of corporate kowtowing was dissected on the "Inside Trump's Head" podcast, revealing not just a singular instance of media capitulation but a broader, more disturbing trend of fear-driven decision-making at the highest levels of corporate America.

Bob Iger's Pragmatic Cowardice

Bob Iger, Disney's CEO, is no stranger to the high-stakes poker game of corporate America. Yet, according to Wolff, Iger folded like a cheap suit in the face of Trump's bluster, choosing the path of least resistance in what he perceived to be a tactical retreat for the greater good of the company. Wolff characterizes this move as a pragmatic business decision, designed to sidestep the "ire of Donald Trump" which, in Iger's calculation, would be "bad for business."

Let's be clear: Iger's response is not pragmatism; it's pure, unadulterated cowardice. It's a testament to the chilling effect Trump continues to have on free speech and the press, even as a private citizen. The notion, as Wolff suggests, that losing Trump's favor could equate to "losing the store" is a stark indication of how the former president's approval has become a perverse form of currency in the media world—a currency that executives like Iger are all too willing to trade in, at the expense of journalistic integrity and backbone.

The FCC's Veiled Threats and the Illusion of Free Speech

The suspension of Kimmel's show, while alarming, is merely a symptom of a much larger disease. The barely veiled threats issued by FCC chair Brendan Carr on a MAGA influencer's podcast underscore the lengths to which Trump's allies will go to exert control over the media narrative. It's a brazen display of power that lays bare the illusion of free speech in today's America, where regulatory bodies can be weaponized against those who dare to criticize the MAGA movement.

The Right's Hypocrisy

Amidst this debacle, some conservatives have leaped to defend Disney's actions as within the rights of a private company. This is a laughable defense, dripping with hypocrisy. The same voices that cry foul over perceived censorship and the silencing of conservative voices are now all too eager to champion corporate autonomy—so long as it serves their interests. It's a convenient about-face that exposes the right's selective outrage and its conditional support for free speech.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The suspension of "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" is not just about one television show or one comedian's right to lampoon the powerful. It's a glaring red flag, signaling the erosion of our collective ability to speak truth to power without fear of retribution. When media giants like ABC capitulate to threats, veiled or otherwise, they set a dangerous precedent that emboldens would-be tyrants and erodes the very foundation of our democracy.

In the end, the lesson here is clear: the corporate spinelessness exemplified by ABC's decision does far more than silence a single voice—it sends a message that in the face of power, submission is preferable to defiance. And that is a message we can neither afford nor accept.
