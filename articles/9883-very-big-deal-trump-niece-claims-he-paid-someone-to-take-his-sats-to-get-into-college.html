'Very big deal': Trump niece claims he 'paid someone to take his SATs' to get into college

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump walks at the White House. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump only got into college because “his older sister, did all his homework for him,” according to the president’s niece.

Mary Trump, a psychologist and the niece of the president, said in a recent video that her father was passed over by the family patriarch in favor of Donald Trump.

“In the simplest terms: my father was the oldest son, my grandfather’s namesake. But beyond that, he was also smart, funny, accomplished, kind, and generous. These are qualities that most of us value,” she wrote. “When he was 25, he became a pilot for TWA at the dawn of the jet age which at the time was a very big deal. Donald, on the other hand, was given everything despite having done nothing.”

She went on to say that her uncle only got into college through nefarious means.

“The only reason his grades were good enough to apply to the University of Pennsylvania was because my Aunt Maryanne, his older sister, did all his homework for him,” she claimed. “The only reason he got in was because he paid someone to take his SATs for him and my dad’s fraternity brother worked in Admissions. The only reason Donald was able to make it into Manhattan real estate was because my grandfather was worth hundreds of millions of dollars and he had very deep connections to New York real estate and New York politics.”

She then added, “Every step along the way, Donald’s rise has been enabled by somebody else with more power and their own malign motives: from my grandfather to the New York media, from the banks to Mark Burnett at NBC; from the Republican Party to the f------ Supreme Court. And yet, Donald continues to be petty. For him, having everything isn’t enough unless you’re humiliating the person you stole everything from.”

Our Analysis:

The Unearned Ascendancy of Donald Trump: A Critical Analysis

In a recent revelation that’s as shocking as it is unsurprising, Mary Trump, a trained psychologist and the niece of Donald Trump, has laid bare the nefarious machinations that propelled the former president into positions of power and prestige. According to her, Trump’s academic and professional successes were not the fruits of his own labor but rather the result of manipulations, connections, and outright deceit. In this analysis, we’ll dissect the implications of these claims and the broader commentary it provides on privilege, nepotism, and the corrosion of meritocracy in America.

The Alleged Academic Fraud

Mary Trump’s assertion that her uncle, Donald Trump, only managed to secure a place at the University of Pennsylvania because his sister did his homework and he paid someone to take his SATs, if true, is a damning indictment of his character. But more than that, it’s a glaring spotlight on the systemic failures that allow for such deceit to pave the way to prestigious institutions. It’s not just about one individual’s lack of integrity; it’s about a system that enables and often rewards such behavior.

Nepotism Over Merit

The narrative that Donald Trump was favored by their grandfather over Mary Trump’s father, despite the latter’s qualifications and character, is a classic tale of nepotism trumping merit. This theme recurs throughout Trump’s ascent, with Mary attributing his success to the “deep connections” and wealth of his father, Fred Trump. When family connections and wealth overshadow talent and hard work, it’s a clear sign that the system is broken.

Enablers at Every Step

Perhaps the most scathing indictment in Mary Trump’s account is the role of enablers in Donald Trump’s rise. From family members to the New York media, from banks to NBC’s Mark Burnett, and from the Republican Party to the Supreme Court, Trump’s journey to the top seems to have been facilitated by a cadre of power brokers with their own agendas. This network of enablers didn’t just fail to hold him accountable; they actively contributed to his unearned ascent.

The Cycle of Privilege and Power

What Mary Trump’s revelations lay bare is not just the story of one man’s unscrupulous climb to power but a broader societal issue. When individuals can bypass the rules and climb the ladder through nepotism, fraud, and connections, it erodes public trust in institutions. It sends a message that the system is rigged in favor of those with money and influence, demoralizing those who play by the rules.

Conclusion: A Call for Systemic Change

The tale of Donald Trump’s rise, as detailed by his niece, is a clarion call for systemic change. It underscores the need for institutions—be they educational, financial, or political—to implement safeguards that ensure integrity and meritocracy. Without such reforms, the cycle of privilege and power will continue unabated, undermining the foundational principles of fairness and democracy.

In the end, Mary Trump’s allegations are a stark reminder that for some, “having everything isn’t enough unless you’re humiliating the person you stole everything from.” This mindset, devoid of empathy and ethical considerations, poses a significant threat not just to the individuals in its path but to the very fabric of society. It’s high time to reflect on these systemic errors and work diligently to rectify them.
