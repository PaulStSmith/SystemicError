'Soulless': Ex-GOP speechwriter bashes Trump's late-night 'hate tweeting' session

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 26, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Late-Night Tyrades of a President: A Sharp Critique

Donald Trump in the White House in Washington, D.C. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

A former GOP speechwriter bashed President Donald Trump on Wednesday after the president went on a late-night “hate tweeting” session.

Late Tuesday night, Trump fired off a series of posts on Truth Social about late-night television hosts who have criticized him. For instance, he asked his more than 11.2 million followers which of the large networks has the worst late-night show, and claimed the hosts had “no talent.” He also appeared to threaten to revoke the broadcast licenses of networks that are “100% negative” against him.

“If Network NEWSCASTS, and their Late Night Shows, are almost 100% Negative to President Donald J. Trump, MAGA, and the Republican Party, shouldn’t their very valuable Broadcast Licenses be terminated? I say, YES!” Trump wrote.

Tim Miller, host of “The Bulwark Podcast,” reacted to Trump’s late-night tweet storm in a new “Bulwark Takes” video on Wednesday.

“I hope you don’t have anyone in your life behaving like the President of these United States on this holy holiday,” Miller said. “The president was up late last night, around midnight hate-tweeting the television.”

“You’d think that this was drinking, or drunk, posting. He doesn’t even drink,” Miller said. “He just is so barren and soulless that he cannot find community or validation from any other way besides bleating about how much he hates the stories on the TV.”

Our Analysis:

The Late-Night Tyrades of a President: A Sharp Critique

In an era where the absurd often blurs with the alarming, the recent actions of President Donald Trump have once again illuminated the perilous intersection of personal vendetta and presidential power. The incident in question—a late-night barrage of posts on Truth Social targeting late-night television hosts—serves as a stark reminder of Trump’s penchant for pettiness and his dangerous misunderstanding of the fundamental principles of free speech and press freedom.

Trump’s Attack on Late-Night Shows: A Misguided Crusade

Trump’s inquiry to his 11.2 million followers about which network has the worst late-night show is not just a trivial pursuit of personal vendettas; it is a calculated attempt to undermine and delegitimize any form of criticism. By labeling these hosts as “no talent” and suggesting a revocation of broadcast licenses for networks critical of him, Trump not only showcases a disturbing disdain for dissent but also a blatant disregard for the First Amendment. His suggestion that networks be punished for being “100% negative” towards him, MAGA, and the Republican Party is not just an idle threat—it’s an authoritarian impulse laid bare.

Tim Miller’s Scathing Rebuke: A Voice of Reason

Tim Miller, in his response to Trump’s late-night tirade, encapsulates the bewilderment and concern that many feel. His hope that no one else has to endure such behavior, especially from the President of the United States, on a holiday no less, is a sentiment that resonates with the broader public’s exhaustion with Trump’s antics. Miller’s characterization of Trump as “barren and soulless,” seeking validation only through online vitriol, is a cutting diagnosis of a president who often seems more concerned with settling scores than governing.

A Dangerous Precedent for Press Freedom

What stands out most alarmingly in this episode is Trump’s casual suggestion that broadcast licenses should be terminated for networks critical of his administration. This is not just an attack on the late-night hosts he despises but an existential threat to the very concept of a free press. The idea that a sitting president would even entertain, let alone publicize, such a notion is antithetical to the democratic values that underpin American society. It is a reminder that Trump’s threats to the fundamental institutions of democracy are not idle musings but potential policies of a man who has once held—and seeks to reclaim—the highest office in the land.

Conclusion: The Urgent Need for Vigilance

Trump’s late-night “hate tweeting” session is more than a story about a president lashing out at television hosts; it’s a vivid illustration of his broader assault on the principles of free expression and objective truth. In the face of such attacks, it is imperative for the public, and especially for journalists and commentators, to remain vigilant and unyielding in the defense of these principles. As Tim Miller’s critique demonstrates, it is only through calling out and condemning such behavior in the clearest terms that the foundations of democracy can be protected.

In essence, Trump’s behavior is not merely unbecoming of a president; it is a direct threat to the fabric of American democracy. It is incumbent upon all who value this democracy to recognize and resist such authoritarian impulses with the full force of our collective will.
