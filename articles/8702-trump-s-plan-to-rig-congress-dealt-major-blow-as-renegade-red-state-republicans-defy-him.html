Trump's plan to rig Congress dealt major blow as renegade red state Republicans defy him

President Donald Trump suffered yet more bad news on Friday in his bid to redraw congressional maps in Republican states to give himself extra seats, as Indiana — a state he has spent months trying to pressure into his plan — still appears to be a no-go.

According to Politico’s Adam Wren, Indiana Senate President Pro Tempore Rodric Bray reiterated the same thing he said weeks earlier: that there aren’t enough voters in the state Senate to move forward with the plan.

“Over the last several months, Senate Republicans have given very serious and thoughtful consideration to the concept of redrawing our state’s congressional maps,” Bray said in a statement. “Today, I’m announcing there are not enough votes to move that idea forward, and the Senate will not reconvene in December.”

So far, Trump has succeeded in pressuring Republicans in Texas, Missouri, and North Carolina to enact mid-decade gerrymanders that delete Democratic seats from the map, although all these moves are under litigation, and Missouri activists are collecting signatures for a ballot referendum that would suspend the new map there.

But moving forward has been trickier in Indiana, where Republicans could potentially carve up Democratic seats in Indianapolis and Gary to give all nine of the state’s districts to themselves. While Trump and Vice President JD Vance have made multiple trips to Indiana to sell redistricting, and while the entire GOP congressional delegation and Gov. Mike Braun are behind it, there are just enough GOP state senators against the plan to deny it the votes to proceed.

This comes as a number of other problems have cropped up for Republicans in a congressional redistricting war they were initially confident they could win.

This month, California voters passed Proposition 50, which temporarily suspends that state’s redistricting commission and passes a map that seeks to draw out five GOP seats to offset the new Texas map. In Ohio, where state law required a mid-decade redistricting because its initial map didn’t pass on a bipartisan basis, Democrats cut a deal with the GOP that preserves most of the current Democratic seats. Kansas and New Hampshire Republicans backed off their plans to redistrict, at least for the time being. And a state judge in Utah adopted a map that is expected to create a new Democratic district in Salt Lake City.

Our Analysis:

The Gerrymandering Gambit: Trump’s Failing Crusade

The latest episode in the ongoing saga of American political theater reveals President Donald Trump‘s audacious yet faltering attempts to manipulate congressional maps across Republican states—a strategy aimed ostensibly at cementing GOP dominance but mired in controversy and resistance. The recent development from Indiana serves as a stark reminder of the complexities inherent in redrawing electoral boundaries for partisan gain, with the state Senate’s refusal to bend to Trump’s will signaling not just a setback for the former president but a notable fracture within the Republican Party itself.

Indiana: A Line in the Sand

Despite Trump’s relentless pressure campaign, Indiana has emerged as an unexpected bulwark against his redistricting ambitions. Senate President Pro Tempore Rodric Bray‘s declaration—that there simply isn’t enough support in the state Senate to advance the redistricting plan—underscores a broader tension within the GOP. Bray’s statement, “Over the last several months, Senate Republicans have given very serious and thoughtful consideration to the concept of redrawing our state’s congressional maps,” hints at a deeper, more principled stand against the manipulative tactics associated with gerrymandering. This resistance within a typically red state illuminates the growing unease among some Republicans regarding the ethical implications and potential backlash of overt electoral manipulation.

A Nationwide Battleground

Trump’s redistricting efforts have seen mixed results across the country, with Texas, Missouri, and North Carolina capitulating to his demands through mid-decade gerrymanders aimed at eroding Democratic representation. However, these victories are pyrrhic at best, marred by legal challenges and public outcry that highlight the precarious nature of undermining democratic norms for short-term political gains.

Contrastingly, the narrative in Indiana aligns with a series of setbacks for Republicans in their redistricting endeavors elsewhere. From California‘s Proposition 50, which aims to counteract GOP gerrymandering efforts, to Ohio, Kansas, and New Hampshire seeing either pushback or reconsideration of redistricting plans, there’s a discernible shift in the redistricting war landscape. Furthermore, a Utah state judge’s decision to adopt a map expected to create a new Democratic district in Salt Lake City signifies judicial pushback against partisan redistricting efforts.

The Ethical Quagmire of Gerrymandering

The strategic manipulation of electoral boundaries, known colloquially as gerrymandering, has long been a contentious issue within American politics. Both major parties have engaged in this practice when opportune, yet Trump’s recent crusade to reshape the political map in favor of the GOP has spotlighted the inherent dangers of such manipulation. Gerrymandering not only undermines the principle of fair representation but also erodes public trust in the electoral process—a foundational pillar of democratic governance.

Conclusion: A Fractured Strategy

Trump’s redistricting efforts, while ambitious, reveal a fractured strategy that is increasingly meeting resistance not just from Democrats or the judiciary, but from within his own party. The case of Indiana, where even a unified front of GOP officials and concerted efforts by the former president and Vice President JD Vance failed to secure the necessary votes, exemplifies the limits of partisan pressure in the face of ethical considerations and public scrutiny.

As the battle over congressional redistricting continues to unfold, it serves as a litmus test for the resilience of American democracy against attempts to skew its foundational mechanisms for partisan advantage. The unfolding drama not only highlights the contentious nature of redistricting but also signals a potential turning point in how political power is wielded and contested in the United States.
