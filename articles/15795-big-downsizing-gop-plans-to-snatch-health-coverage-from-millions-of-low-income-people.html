'Big downsizing': GOP plans to snatch health coverage from 'millions of low-income' people

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Perennial GOP War on Obamacare: A Critical Analysis

Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (Reuters)

When Democrats recaptured the U.S. House of Representatives in the 2018 midterms and enjoyed a net gain of 41 seats, President Donald Trump's unpopular efforts to overturn the Affordable Care Act of 2010, a.k.a. Obamacare, were cited as a major factor. Obamacare, many Democratic strategists argued, had become a toxic issue for Republicans.

But during his 2024 campaign, Trump once again called for the ACA to be repealed.

In an article published by the conservative website The Bulwark on April 19, journalist Jonathan Cohn warns that millions of Americans could lose their health insurance if Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-Louisiana) succeed in undermining Obamacare and Medicaid.

READ MORE: 'Punish them again': Dems gear up to hammer GOP on key government program

"The likelihood of Donald Trump and his allies in Congress taking Medicaid away from millions of low-income Americans — and, in the process, rolling back a huge piece of the Affordable Care Act — has increased significantly in the last two weeks," Cohn explains. "The change has been easy to miss, because so many other stories are dominating the news — and because the main evidence is a subtle shift in Republican rhetoric. But that shift has been crystal clear if you follow the ins and outs of health care policy — and if you were listening closely to House Speaker Mike Johnson a week ago, when he appeared on Fox News."

On Fox News, Johnson said, "We have to root out fraud, waste, and abuse. We have to eliminate people on, for example, on Medicaid who are not actually eligible to be there — able-bodied workers, for example, young men who are — who should never be on the program at all."

Johnson's remarks, Cohn notes, "may sound like a defense of Medicaid" but included "the language Medicaid critics have been using to describe a big, controversial downsizing of the program."

"Here, it helps to remember what the Affordable Care Act sought to accomplish, and the key role Medicaid played in that," Cohn writes. "The law's main goal was to make decent health insurance available to all Americans, as part of a decades-long, still unfinished campaign to make health care a basic right, as it is in every other economically advanced nation. That meant getting coverage to the uninsured, including low-income Americans who didn't have a way to get insurance on their own because their jobs didn't offer coverage or made coverage available at premiums they couldn't afford, and because individual policies — the kind you buy on your own, not through a job — were either too expensive or unavailable to them because of pre-existing conditions."

Our Analysis:

The Perennial GOP War on Obamacare: A Critical Analysis

In the swirling vortex of political maneuvering and rhetoric, the Republican party's long-standing crusade against the Affordable Care Act (ACA), colloquially known as Obamacare, remains as relentless and misguided as ever. The recent article from The Bulwark, penned by journalist Jonathan Cohn, thrusts into the spotlight the potential catastrophic consequences of the GOP's unwavering mission to dismantle a key pillar of American healthcare. With surgical precision, Cohn dissects the undercurrents of GOP strategy, revealing a chilling prospect: millions of Americans stand on the precipice of losing their health insurance.

The Ghost of Healthcare Past

Rewind to the 2018 midterm elections: Democrats seized control of the U.S. House of Representatives, riding a towering wave of public sentiment against the GOP's attempts to obliterate Obamacare. This outcome served as a resolute rebuke of President Donald Trump's and his allies' efforts, illustrating the ACA's transformation from a political liability into a bastion of healthcare security for countless Americans. Fast forward to the present, and Trump, in an astonishing display of political amnesia, has resurrected his call to repeal the ACA during his 2024 campaign trail. This move is not just a throwback to a failed strategy; it's a glaring testament to the GOP's obstinate refusal to acknowledge the critical lifeline the ACA and Medicaid provide to millions.

A Subtle Yet Sinister Shift

Cohn astutely highlights a "subtle shift" in Republican rhetoric, a harbinger of the party's renewed assault on Medicaid and, by extension, the ACA. House Speaker Mike Johnson's (R-Louisiana) appearance on Fox News, where he invoked the need to "root out fraud, waste, and abuse" in Medicaid, is emblematic of this shift. While these words may masquerade as a defense of the program's integrity, they echo the age-old narrative peddled by Medicaid critics aimed at slashing the program under the guise of reform. This tactic is not only deceptive but dangerous, signaling a potential rollback of critical health coverage for low-income Americans.

The ACA's Unfinished Symphony

At its heart, the ACA represents a monumental step towards the ideal of universal healthcare, an aspiration that places the United States on par with other economically advanced nations. Cohn reminds us of the ACA's noble ambition: to extend healthcare coverage to those left in the cold by their employers or priced out of the market due to pre-existing conditions. This mission, while far from complete, has significantly bridged the chasm of healthcare inequality in America.

The Bulwark's Call to Arms

Jonathan Cohn's analysis is not just a critique; it's a clarion call for vigilance and action. The GOP's strategy, masked in the language of fiscal responsibility and program integrity, threatens to unravel the safety net that millions rely on for their health and well-being. As Democrats gear up to challenge the GOP's narrative, it's imperative that the public and policymakers alike recognize the stakes at play.

In essence, the Republican party's dogged pursuit to dismantle Obamacare, despite its proven benefits and the clear preference of the electorate, is a stark reminder of the ideological chasm that divides American politics. The ACA, with all its imperfections, stands as a testament to the progress made towards healthcare equity in the U.S. Any efforts to undermine it not only disregard the will of the people but also jeopardize the health and security of millions of Americans. In this critical juncture, one can only hope that reason, compassion, and a commitment to the public good will prevail over the machinations of political expediency and ideological obstinacy.
