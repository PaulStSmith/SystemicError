Fox News is struggling to cope with Trump’s 'deflated' electorate’

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosJune 26, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Weakness, Fox’s Panic, and the Conservative Misinformation Machine Running Out of Road

Who Actually Holds Power

Donald Trump still sits at the center of the Republican Party’s machinery, which means the damage is not accidental and it is not diffuse. The source describes a party he has “sandblasted” into collapse, with Fox News and other loyalists now struggling to keep the story afloat. That is the power structure: one man sets the terms, and an ecosystem of obedient media tries to launder the consequences.

What the Source Really Shows

The reporting point here is simple: Trump’s popularity is at a career low, Republican enthusiasm is weak, and Democrats are outperforming expectations in recent elections. John Heilemann points to actual votes, not punditry. Nicole Wallace says the atmosphere on Fox has changed. Angelo Carusone says even Fox’s strongest allies can no longer fully carry Trump’s narrative.

That is not a drama about “optics.” It is evidence of political exhaustion.

The Enablers

If the outcome is a deflated Republican electorate, the decision-makers are not mysterious. Trump made the party into an extension of his personal brand. Fox News then spent years acting as a repair crew for the wreckage, treating accountability as a technical inconvenience. The source even captures the telling detail that a Fox panel’s old confidence has given way to silence when challenged on air. That is not independent journalism failing. That is a propaganda system losing control of the room.

The Misdirection

The article’s weakest frame is the familiar one: Trump is “dragging” the party down, as if the party were an unwilling passenger. It was not. Republican leadership, Republican media, and Republican voters all built the conditions that made this possible. The damage was enabled by actors with money, airtime, institutional leverage, and partisan discipline.

The bigger dodge is treating Trump’s collapse as a mood swing. The source makes clear it is showing up in elections. When enthusiasm drops and special-election performance improves for Democrats, that is not vibes. That is political reality breaking through the fog.

Fox Can’t Fix It Anymore

Fox News matters here because it is not merely coverage. It is infrastructure. It has been the strongest support structure for Trump’s account of himself and his political project. The problem now is that even that structure is strained. Once the network’s loyal performers can no longer maintain the same choreography, the collapse becomes visible to its own audience.

That visibility is dangerous for them because the conservative media model depends on managed perception. It can survive scandal, contradiction, even obvious bad governance. It struggles when the audience starts to notice the gap between the performance and the result.

The Larger Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like at the level of media maintenance: one leader generates failure, loyal institutions absorb the blast, and partisan outlets try to reframe defeat as noise. But there is a limit to how much institutional lying can substitute for public confidence. When the coalition stops believing its own script, the machine does not become honest. It becomes brittle.

The deeper story is not that Trump is “having a bad news cycle.” It is that a political order built on grievance, intimidation, and captive media is discovering that repetition is not the same thing as consent.
