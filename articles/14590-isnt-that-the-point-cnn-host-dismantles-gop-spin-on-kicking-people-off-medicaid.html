'Isn’t that the point?' CNN host dismantles GOP spin on kicking people off Medicaid

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 22, 20256 min read

(Screengrab via CNN)

Two conservatives found themselves under fire from CNN anchor Abby Phillip, who pressed them on the GOP's plans for Medicaid, as President Donald Trump threatens members of his own party who threaten to blow up his "One, Big, Beautiful Bill."

Phillip opened her show late Tuesday, recounting Trump's threat to Republican hardliners: "Get in line, or else," she paraphrased, noting Trump's agenda is "on the brink tonight."

Trump’s budget seeks to make his 2017 tax cuts permanent, but critics say they're a thinly disguised attempt to benefit the wealthy.

"The plan puts more money into the border and the Pentagon, and less money into programs like food stamps," Phillip noted. "And even though Trump told Republicans on the Hill today, 'Don’t F around with Medicaid,' his plan will do just that by enforcing work requirements that would result in millions losing coverage."

Steve Moore, former Trump economic adviser, told Phillip that if lawmakers fail to pass Trump's budget, the average American will see a $3,000 tax increase next year.

"I don’t think anybody wants that," he warned, calling it the "biggest tax increase in history."

Moore also took issue with criticism of the GOP's plans for Medicaid.

"Look, I object a little bit to this language, for example, that work requirements are going to throw all these people off unemployment and Medicaid," he began.

But Phillip refused to concede that point.

"But isn’t that the point? In order to save money?" she interjected.

"We did this, Abby. Abby, we did this under Bill Clinton. He was the last, and it was extremely successful. 75% of the people got work, and they climbed the economic ladder," he said, steamrolling over Phillip, who continued to insist the point of the change is to get the GOP to "a certain number of savings."

"And in order to do that, they need fewer people to get benefits. Is that not the point?" she pressed.

Before Moore could answer, fellow conservative and Trump ally Scott Jennings jumped in to defend the party's agenda.

"Yes. But isn’t it the point that you want people to go to work? Isn't that the point?" he shot back.

Phillip turned to her colleague and continued pressing..

"Okay — no, no, no. Scott, hold on. I’ll ask you the exact same question I asked Steven. Isn’t the point that they want to save money?" Phillip asked. "So in order to save money, you’ve got to get people off the program. How is that not taking people off the program?"

Jennings agreed the move is designed to save money, but pushed back that the goal of Medicaid is "not to trap people."

"It’s not supposed to be a lifestyle. It’s supposed to be a temporary deal. So yes, we don’t want to trap people on Medicaid. We want to get them to go to the workplace. And, I mean, there are able-bodied people all over this country who do nothing," Jennings insisted.

Phillip refused to let the conservatives off the hook, demanding to know if Trump was merely fishing for a headline by saying, "Don't F with Medicaid."

"It’s going to mess with Medicaid for sure," she noted.

"Well, it is — but it messes with it in a good way because it forces people to go to work," Jennings replied, visibly frustrated. "Do you want a country where nobody works? Or do you want a country where people go to work?"

"So, yes?" Phillip pressed. "So he is trying to get a headline but not be honest about why."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Evasion: A Deep Dive into GOP Medicaid Rhetoric

In what can only be described as a masterclass in political evasion and rhetorical gymnastics, CNN anchor Abby Phillip recently found herself in a verbal tug-of-war with two conservative figures, Steve Moore and Scott Jennings. The topic? The GOP's controversial plans for Medicaid under President Donald Trump's budget proposal. Trump's message to the Republican hardliners, paraphrased by Phillip as "Get in line, or else," sets the stage for a showdown that's less about policy and more about political posturing.

Trump's Budget: A Cloak for Inequality

The heart of the matter lies in Trump's budget proposal, which aims to make his 2017 tax cuts permanent. Critics, including this publication, have lambasted these cuts as a thinly veiled scheme to fatten the wallets of the wealthy. Phillip points out that the plan also diverts more funds to the border and the Pentagon while slashing programs vital to the less fortunate, such as food stamps. Trump's hollow call to Republicans not to "F around with Medicaid" is immediately contradicted by his plan to enforce work requirements, a move that could see millions lose their coverage.

The Great Tax Increase Scare

Steve Moore, a former Trump economic adviser, raises the specter of a $3,000 tax increase for the average American should Trump's budget not pass. This claim, designed to instill fear, is a classic tactic to rally support for policies that are, at their core, detrimental to the majority. Moore's objection to criticism of the GOP's Medicaid plans, particularly the work requirements, is a thinly veiled attempt to sanitize a policy that essentially penalizes the poor for being poor.

The Work Requirement Facade

Both Moore and Jennings attempt to defend the indefensible, arguing that work requirements for Medicaid are intended to lift people out of poverty. This narrative is deeply flawed and ignores the complex reality many Americans face. By asserting that Bill Clinton's implementation of similar requirements was a success, Moore conveniently overlooks the long-term consequences and the vulnerable populations that were adversely affected.

Jennings' argument that Medicaid is not meant to be a "lifestyle" is a disturbing simplification of the issue. It perpetuates the myth of the "lazy poor" and ignores the structural barriers that prevent many from accessing stable employment. The claim that these requirements will "force people to go to work" disregards the realities of the job market, underemployment, and the challenges facing those with disabilities or caregiving responsibilities.

Phillip's Persistence: A Beacon of Journalism

Abby Phillip's refusal to let Moore and Jennings off the hook is commendable. By persistently questioning their justification of the GOP's plan, she highlights the disconnect between political rhetoric and the lived experiences of millions of Americans. Phillip's interrogation reveals the underlying motive of the Medicaid changes: to cut costs at the expense of the most vulnerable.

Conclusion: The True Cost of "Savings"

The conversation between Phillip, Moore, and Jennings is a microcosm of the broader debate surrounding welfare and work requirements. It exposes the GOP's plan not as a well-intentioned effort to encourage employment, but as a cynical ploy to reduce spending by stripping away the safety net from those who need it most. The insistence on framing Medicaid work requirements as a push towards self-sufficiency masks the harsh reality of these policies: increased poverty, worsened health outcomes, and a society more divided than ever.

In the end, the question we must ask ourselves is: What kind of country do we want to be? One that measures success by the wealth of a few or by the well-being of the many? As the GOP continues to push for policies that undermine the latter, it is clear that the battle for the soul of America is far from over.
