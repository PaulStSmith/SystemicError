Trump blows up Cabinet member’s phone at 1 am to gossip about TV

By Paulo Santos

Almost every night, President Donald Trump apparently calls top Cabinet officials for late-night conversations. One of those officials recently opened up about what he and Trump discuss.

Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick recently did an extensive interview with the New Yorker's Antonia Hitchens (daughter of the late writer Christopher Hitchens), in which he discussed a variety of topics surrounding the president's second term. He told the outlet that every night before he goes to bed — usually around 1 AM — Trump will call him to talk. He noted that while some of these conversations are about policy, Trump also sometimes just wants to gossip.

Lutnick said that aside from "real stuff" like international trade, Trump also likes talking about "sporting events, people, who’d you have dinner with, what was this guy like, can you believe what this guy did, what’s the TV like, I saw this on TV, what’d you think of what this guy said on TV, what did you think about my press conference, how about this Truth" and other topics.

"Trump has other people he calls late at night," Lutnick continued, though he didn't mention specific names.

In one revealing section, Hitchens reported that Trump was furious with Lutnick after rolling out his "Liberation Day" tariffs in April, in which he slapped broad import taxes on virtually every U.S. trading partner, causing significant shock in financial markets. The formula for the tariffs had been found to have been calculated by finding a country with which the U.S. had a trade deficit , then taking the amount of goods imported from that country and dividing it by two.

"Trump called Lutnick in a rage, wanting to know how the tariff amounts had been determined. Lutnick himself wasn’t certain. Trump told Lutnick to go on TV and defend them anyway. Lutnick would take the fall, even if he didn’t really know what had happened," Hitchens wrote.

Hitchens quoted several unnamed sources who were less than complimentary of the commerce secretary. One administration source said that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was seen as the "smart" economic voice in Trump's Cabinet, with another source dismissing Lutnick as "not a real actor" but an "errand boy" for the president.

Our Analysis:

Late-Night Whispers and Economic Blunders: Unpacking the Trump-Lutnick Dialogues

In what appears to be a blend of casual banter and high-stakes policy discussion, President Donald Trump’s nightly phone calls to Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick have shed light on the inner workings—or rather, the disarray—of the current administration. The recent revelations from Lutnick's extensive interview with the New Yorker's Antonia Hitchens not only underscore the President's unorthodox approach to governance but also reveal a startling lack of coherence in the administration's economic policies.

Gossip Over Governance

The notion that the President engages in nightly conversations with top Cabinet officials about topics as trivial as “sporting events, people, who’d you have dinner with”, and “what this guy did” is, frankly, ludicrous. It’s a stark reminder of the administration's penchant for valuing personal loyalty and idle chatter over expertise and professionalism. The casual nature of these discussions, as recounted by Lutnick, is emblematic of a broader issue: a leadership style more concerned with social banter than the sober realities confronting the nation.

Economic Policies: A Game of Guesswork?

The revelation about Trump's “Liberation Day” tariffs is particularly alarming. The approach to determining tariff amounts—dividing the value of goods imported from countries with which the U.S. has a trade deficit by two—is not just oversimplified; it's economically reckless. This method, devoid of any nuanced analysis or strategic planning, led to significant shockwaves in financial markets, underscoring the administration's cavalier attitude towards global economics.

Trump’s subsequent fury and his directive for Lutnick to defend these tariffs on national television, even without a clear understanding of their basis, is a glaring example of the administration's irresponsibility and lack of accountability. It's a disturbing scenario where the commerce secretary, rather than being a key architect of economic policy, is reduced to an "errand boy" tasked with cleaning up the messes wrought by hasty and ill-conceived decisions.

A Cabinet of Echoes, Not Voices

The commentary from unnamed sources within the administration paints a bleak picture of the dynamics within Trump's Cabinet. The distinction between Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent as the “smart” economic voice and Lutnick as a mere “errand boy” is telling. It suggests a hierarchy of influence based not on competence or expertise, but on one's ability to echo the President's whims. This environment, where serious economic policies are sidelined in favor of personal loyalty and ad-hoc decision-making, is deeply troubling.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The insights from Lutnick’s interview are more than just a peek behind the curtain; they are a clarion call for a reevaluation of leadership within the highest echelons of American governance. The mix of trivial gossip and impulsive policy-making is not just unbecoming; it’s dangerous. It reflects a systemic error in the administration's approach to governance—one that prioritizes personal whims over informed decision-making and accountability.

As the nation grapples with the repercussions of these revelations, the need for a leadership that is both competent and transparent has never been clearer. The administration must move beyond late-night conversations and errand runs, adopting a more reasoned, responsible, and inclusive approach to policymaking. Anything less is a disservice to the American people and the global community at large.
