During a Sunday interview, Fox News host Kevin Corke asked Cooke to respond to White House aide Stephen Miller's threat to suspend the right to challenge detentions in court, which is known as habeas corpus.

"Look, Kevin, I think the first and the last line of defense today are our courts," Cooke explained. "And when you have judicial activism where courts aren't following the rule of law, then President Trump has to look at every single constitutional option, and it has to be on the table, such as suspending habeas corpus."

"President Trump has a constitutional duty to protect the American people under Article 2," she continued. "The Supreme Court's decision previously has been misguided. The Supreme Court has told President Trump he needs to pause and allow for these trials to occur for every single illegal alien. But at the end of the day, it's public safety and national security that's going to trump habeas corpus."

Ultimately, Cooke concluded that Miller was correct with his assessment of suspending habeas corpus rights.

"President Trump should be looking at suspending habeas corpus if the Supreme Court cannot understand that the rule of law is with him and not these judicial activist judges," she said.

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Rhetoric of Suspending Habeas Corpus

In a recent Fox News interview, right-wing attorney Mehek Cooke took a stance that blasts through the bedrock of American constitutional rights with the finesse of a sledgehammer. Cooke's assertion that President Donald Trump should suspend habeas corpus rights if the U.S. Supreme Court fails to align with his views on deporting migrants without due process is not just a radical departure from democratic principles; it is a direct assault on the very foundations of the rule of law.

The Misguided Appeal to Article 2

Cooke bases her argument on a wildly expansive interpretation of Article 2 of the Constitution, suggesting that the president has a "constitutional duty to protect the American people" that somehow trumps the fundamental right to challenge detentions in court. This interpretation is not only a gross misreading of the Constitution but a dangerous precedent that would place unchecked power in the hands of the president.

The notion that "public safety and national security" should override habeas corpus is a slippery slope to authoritarianism. Cooke's argument conveniently ignores the fact that habeas corpus is itself a critical component of national security, ensuring that government power is not exercised arbitrarily and that individuals have a recourse against unlawful detention.

The Role of the Supreme Court and Judicial Activism

Cooke's disdain for the Supreme Court's decisions and her quickness to label them as "misguided" and the result of "judicial activism" reveal a fundamental misunderstanding of the judiciary's role. The Supreme Court is precisely the institution designed to interpret the Constitution and safeguard individual rights against overreach by the executive or legislative branches.

Labeling decisions that protect individual rights as "judicial activism" is a tired tactic used to undermine the legitimacy of the judiciary and pave the way for executive overreach. It is a dangerous game that threatens the separation of powers that is central to the American constitutional system.

The Real Threat to Public Safety and National Security

The real threat to public safety and national security is not the Supreme Court's defense of habeas corpus; it is the rhetoric that argues for its suspension. History is littered with examples of leaders who have suspended civil liberties in the name of national security, almost invariably leading to abuses of power and the erosion of democratic governance.

Cooke's conclusion that President Trump "should be looking at suspending habeas corpus" if he disagrees with the Supreme Court is a chilling endorsement of autocracy. It suggests that the whims of the executive should have the authority to override the checks and balances that are the hallmark of a democratic society.

Conclusion

The argument presented by Mehek Cooke on Fox News is not just a legal misinterpretation; it is a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism. The suggestion that habeas corpus—a fundamental right that is the cornerstone of American freedom—could be suspended at the president's discretion is antithetical to the principles of democracy and the rule of law.

It is essential that we recognize these arguments for what they are: an assault on the very foundations of our constitutional democracy. We must remain vigilant against such rhetoric and defend the importance of habeas corpus and the judiciary's role in protecting individual rights against government overreach.
