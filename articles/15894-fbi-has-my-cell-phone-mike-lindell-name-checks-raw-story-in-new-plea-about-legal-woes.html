“‘Mike Lindell Vindicated’ is finally showing up in headlines — even from outlets that once smeared me,” he wrote. “But you and I cannot get complacent. You and I are the reason we are winning. The Uni-Party and the Deep State are afraid of We The People.”

“Even Raw Story ran a piece that wasn’t a hit job. For the first time in nearly five years, searching my name doesn’t look like a smear campaign,” Lindell wrote.

He is quoting a Raw Story report published last week about Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene's (R-GA) claims that Lindell was "vindicated" after Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard said that voting machines were "vulnerable" to hackers.

But he says his tribulations are not all over.

“The FBI still has my cell phone. I am still in court. I am still under attack. The goal has always been the same: silence me and stop the movement to get rid of the machines,” he opined.

The owner of ‘My Pillow’ continued his plea for election integrity, penning, “I am not backing down — and neither should you. Not today, not tomorrow, not ever. This is the golden age of election integrity: yet we must continue to press forward.”

Lindell is being investigated for identity theft and conspiring to damage a computer connected to a suspected voting equipment security breach. The 63-year-old is also facing an audit from the IRS.

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Mike Lindell's "Vindication" Saga

Mike Lindell, the infamous owner of 'My Pillow' and a prominent figure in the propagation of election fraud conspiracy theories, has recently taken to the media to declare himself "vindicated." This claim is as astonishing as it is misleading, rooted more in the realms of fantasy than in the cold, hard reality of factual evidence.

The Illusion of Vindication

Lindell's so-called vindication appears to be a house of cards built on a singular statement by Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene and a report by Raw Story regarding vulnerabilities in voting machines. This is a classic example of conflating unrelated issues to sew a narrative convenient for oneself. Greene's endorsement and a report on machine vulnerabilities do not equate to a vindication of Lindell's broader claims about election fraud. It's a logical leap that would make Evel Knievel proud.

The Reality of Legal Woes

Despite his triumphant tone, Lindell's legal challenges are far from over. The FBI's possession of his cell phone and ongoing court battles are testament to the seriousness of the allegations against him, including identity theft and conspiring to damage a computer connected to a suspected voting equipment security breach. These are not minor accusations that can be brushed aside with a wave of the "vindication" wand. They are serious legal challenges with potential for significant consequences.

The Misguided Crusade for "Election Integrity"

Lindell's plea for election integrity and his call to get rid of voting machines is a dangerous distraction from the real issues facing America's electoral system. Election integrity is crucial, but Lindell's approach is not about protecting the sanctity of the vote; it's about undermining faith in the democratic process under the guise of fixing it. His rhetoric is not just misguided; it's harmful to the very foundation of democracy.

The Bottom Line

Mike Lindell's narrative of vindication is a masterclass in manipulation, attempting to transform his precarious legal and public relations situation into one of triumph. However, the reality remains starkly different. The legal system does not operate on the currency of public opinion or media reports, but on evidence and facts.

Lindell's ongoing legal battles and the serious nature of the accusations against him should not be obscured by his bombastic claims of being vindicated. As these proceedings unfold, it will be the courts, not the court of public opinion, that determine his fate.

In the meantime, it's essential to remain vigilant and critical of attempts to distort the narrative and mislead the public. The integrity of the electoral process and the health of our democracy depend on it.
