Judges overseeing Trump lawsuits raise red flags over new 'unsettling' intimidation tactic

Judges who are being tasked with ruling on the flood of lawsuits filed against Donald Trump's administration are finding themselves on the receiving end of a new intimidation tactic designed to remind them that critics know where they and members of their family live.

According to a report from the Washington Post, judges have reached out to the Department of Justice and the U.S. Marshals Service after receiving unsolicited pizzas delivered at their places of residence which began in February and have continued to increase since then.

As the Post's Derek Hawkins wrote, "The U.S. Marshals Service has been tracking the deliveries, and judges have been sharing details about their experiences in hopes of finding out more about what they call an ongoing attempt at intimidating the judiciary."

Noting that the deliveries have also gone to the judges' family members too, the report adds, "The Marshals Service declined to discuss the deliveries in detail, including the number of deliveries that judges have reported or how many judges have been targeted."

According to D.C. U.S. Circuit Judge J. Michelle Childs, she has been the recipient of seven pizzas since she ruled against the Trump administration over the firing of a government watchdog.

As she told the Post, “It’s unsettling because I’d like to go to work every day, even with the hardest case, just feeling like there’s no sense of intimidation,” adding, “It’s really an unnecessary and an unfortunate threat to our security when we’re trying to be judicial officers in a very neutral position with respect to our cases. You need a strong judiciary for the system to work. This is infringing on democracy generally.”

According to Childs, she and her husband have been forced to take extra precautions when answering the door, telling the Post, "We’ve been asking questions, not opening the door, just looking through the Ring [camera]. Each time, we learn a little bit more.”

The Post's Hawkins noted, "The deliveries to Childs and others share some characteristics with swatting, the popular and dangerous form of harassment in which law enforcement officers are called to respond to a fake crime at a person’s homeas a hoax.Judges, administration officials and other public figures have been targeted with swatting in recent years. Authorities in many swatting cases have struggled to track down the callers, who use various technologies to hide their identities."

Our Analysis:

A Chilling Tactic: The Weaponization of Pizza Deliveries Against the Judiciary

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a dystopian novel, judges ruling against the Trump administration are encountering a bizarre yet unsettling form of harassment: unsolicited pizza deliveries to their personal residences. This tactic, as reported by the Washington Post, serves as a stark reminder of the lengths to which critics will go to intimidate and unsettle those tasked with upholding the law.

The Sinister Side of a Simple Pizza Delivery

The notion that something as innocuous as a pizza delivery could be weaponized is ludicrous on the surface, yet deeply troubling upon closer examination. It is a stark illustration of how traditional forms of harassment have evolved in our digital age. The judges, including D.C. U.S. Circuit Judge J. Michelle Childs, find themselves in a predicament that is as ridiculous as it is frightening. Childs, who reported receiving seven pizzas following her ruling against the Trump administration, highlighted the impact of such harassment on the judiciary's ability to operate without fear or favor.

The Intimidation Factor: It's essential to recognize the underlying motive of these deliveries as an attempt to intimidate. It's an insidious message that says, "We know where you live." This is not just about the inconvenience or the cost associated with these unsolicited deliveries; it's about creating a climate of fear and uncertainty amongst those who serve in our judiciary.

The Response: A Need for More Than Just Awareness

The U.S. Marshals Service's tracking of these deliveries and the judges' efforts to share their experiences are steps in the right direction but fall short of addressing the root of the problem. The judiciary finds itself in a catch-22 situation, striving to remain neutral and impartial in the face of blatant attempts to undermine its authority and independence.

A Threat to Democracy: Judge Childs' remarks underscore a broader concern: such harassment is not just an attack on individual judges but an affront to the democratic principles that underpin our society. It is an attempt to erode trust in the judiciary, a cornerstone of democracy, by fostering a sense of vulnerability among its members.

The Bigger Picture: Harassment in the Digital Age

This incident is emblematic of a larger trend of harassment against public figures, reminiscent of swatting but with its own unique twist. Authorities' struggle to track down the perpetrators, who hide behind the anonymity that technology affords, highlights the challenges of combating such harassment.

Conclusion: The Need for a Robust Response

What's unfolding is not just an innovative form of harassment but a direct challenge to the judiciary's role in our democracy. It requires a response that goes beyond mere awareness or tracking. There's a pressing need for legislation that addresses this new form of harassment, protecting not just the judiciary but all individuals from tactics designed to intimidate and silence.

In the face of such tactics, it is imperative that we stand firm in our support for the judiciary and for the principles of democracy that it upholds. Let it be clear: the weaponization of pizza deliveries, or any form of harassment against the judiciary, is an attack on the very foundations of our democratic system. It should be met with the full force of the law and a resolute commitment to preserving the integrity and independence of our judicial system.
