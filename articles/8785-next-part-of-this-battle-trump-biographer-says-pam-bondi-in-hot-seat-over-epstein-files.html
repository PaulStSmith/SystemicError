'Next part of this battle': Trump biographer says Pam Bondi in hot seat over Epstein files

Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) is about to move a monstrous problem President Donald Trump is facing from the back burner to the “front burner,” according to two analysts, and Pam Bondi may be in the hot seat next.

Lawmakers on the Hill are debating a legislative package that will reopen the government before the Thanksgiving holiday. In exchange for their votes, eight Democrats agreed to pass the package for a vote on the expiring Affordable Care Act subsidies in December.

Joana Coles, chief content officer of The Daily Beast, and controversial journalist Michael Wolff, who has written four books about Trump, discussed the government reopening and what it means for the president during a new episode of “Inside Trump’s Head,” a podcast the two analysts co-host.

“ Mike Johnson is now reopening the house, which means guess what’s back on the docket? The Epstein files,“ Coles said.

“It was simmering on the back burner, and now it’s been moved to the front burner,” she added.

Wolff added that it is likely the House will vote to subpoena the Epstein files once the government reopens. Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva is expected to be sworn in soon, and she has said she will vote to release the files once she is seated.

He also predicted that part of the next fight will focus on Attorney General Pam Bondi’s statement that she had the files on her desk, ready to be released.

“I assume what she had on her desk was nothing. Blank paper, pieces of paper,” Wolff said. “Which is part of the problem. And that is now going to become the next part of this battle.”

However, Wolff added that Democrats may be at a disadvantage when it comes to subpoenaing the files.

“They are going to subpoena something, but they don’t know what they are subpoenaing,” Wolff said. “Then, they’ll have to figure out exactly what that is, and the executive branch, which will have to deliver this material, will define what that is.”

“I suspect confusion about what it is and where it is, and then what they redact and don’t redact,” he continued. “So the executive branch, even with a vote in Congress, is still basically in charge of the Epstein files.”

Our Analysis:

The Political Chessboard Heats Up

In a recent turn of events that could only be described as a political whirlwind, Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) seems poised to shift the tectonic plates of the current political landscape, putting President Donald Trump and potentially Pam Bondi in rather precarious positions. This move, as analyzed by Joana Coles and Michael Wolff on their podcast “Inside Trump’s Head”, could effectively bring the simmering issue of the Epstein files from the shadows to the glaring spotlight of public and legal scrutiny.

The Deal and The Docket

At the heart of this development is a legislative package aimed at reopening the government before Thanksgiving, a move that smacks of both necessity and strategic bargaining. The package, which managed to win over eight Democrats, comes with a side order—a vote on the expiring Affordable Care Act subsidies in December. This legislative maneuvering is not just about keeping the government lights on; it’s a chess move in a much larger game that involves high stakes and even higher tensions.

The Epstein Files: A Political Hot Potato

The Epstein files, long simmering on the back burner of political priorities, are about to get a front-row seat in the political arena, according to Coles. This isn’t just about reopening old wounds; it’s about exposing potentially new and damning evidence that could have far-reaching implications. Wolff’s prediction that the House will vote to subpoena the files underscores a looming battle that could ensnare figures like Attorney General Pam Bondi, who has already made waves with her statements about having the files “ready to be released.”

The Specter of Empty Promises

Wolff’s skepticism about the contents of what Bondi claimed to have on her desk is both biting and warranted. His assertion that she might have had nothing but “blank paper, pieces of paper” is not just a scathing indictment of Bondi’s credibility; it’s an emblematic critique of the opacity and obfuscation that often characterizes political machinations around sensitive issues like the Epstein saga.

Subpoena Power vs. Executive Branch Ambiguity

Wolff’s analysis also touches on a critical aspect of the subpoena process: the inherent disadvantage that Democrats might face. The executive branch, tasked with delivering the material, essentially holds the reins on what gets disclosed and what remains hidden. This potential for “confusion about what it is and where it is” could stymie efforts to bring clarity and accountability, leaving the executive branch with the upper hand in defining the narrative around the Epstein files.

Conclusion: The Battle Ahead

As the government gears up to reopen and the Epstein files inch closer to the limelight, the stage is set for a showdown that could redefine political alliances and the balance of power in Washington. The implications of this saga extend beyond the fate of Trump and Bondi; they touch on the very integrity of the political process and the accountability of those in the highest echelons of power. The coming weeks will not just be about legislative victories; they will be a litmus test for the democratic values and principles that underpin the American political system. In this high-stakes game, the only certainty is that the battle lines are drawn, and the political chessboard is more volatile than ever.
