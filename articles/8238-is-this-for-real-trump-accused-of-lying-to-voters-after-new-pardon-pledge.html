'Is this for real?' Trump accused of 'lying' to voters after new pardon pledge

President Donald Trump announced he has pardoned Juan Orlando Hernández, the former president of Honduras, who is serving a 45-year sentence in a U.S. prison for drug trafficking.

Trump wrote in a Friday post to his Truth Social platform that he was endorsing Nasry “Tito” Asfura in Sunday’s presidential election in Honduras, and that his administration would be “very supportive” of Asfura’s government if he won. He then pivoted to his decision that he would be “granting a Full and Complete Pardon” to Hernández, saying he had been “treated very harshly and unfairly.”

“VOTE FOR TITO ASFURA FOR PRESIDENT, AND CONGRATULATIONS TO JUAN ORLANDO HERNANDEZ ON YOUR UPCOMING PARDON,” Trump wrote in his signature all-caps style.

The DOJ stated last year that it was sentencing Hernández to 45 years in prison for conspiring “to facilitate the importation of an almost unfathomable 400 tons of cocaine to this country.” The former Honduran president committed his crime in concert with his brother, Tony Hernández, who is currently serving a life sentence for drug trafficking and firearms crimes. New York Magazine contributor Yashar Ali pointed out that prosecutors found that Hernández accepted millions of dollars in bribes from drug traffickers connected to the notorious Sinaloa Cartel. Freelance journalist Thomas van Linge said Trump’s pardon was an “absolutely unacceptable decision as this comes on the eve of crucial elections in Honduras.”

“As President of Honduras, Juan Orlando Hernández abused his power to support one of the largest and most violent drug trafficking conspiracies in the world, and the people of Honduras and the United States bore the consequences,” then-Attorney General Merrick Garland stated at the time. “... The Justice Department will hold accountable all those who engage in violent drug trafficking, regardless of how powerful they are or what position they hold.”

Trump’s announcement caught many off-guard, with various journalists, commentators and experts denouncing the president for freeing a convicted drug trafficker – especially as the Trump administration continues to carry out fatal and potentially illegal strikes on boats it alleges are trafficking drugs to the United States. Western Washington University senior instructor Richard B. Simon likened the pardon to Trump “using the U.S. military to assassinate rival crime lords and using pardon power to reward and elevate crime lord allies.”

“Is this for real? We are killing people we just suspect of drug trafficking and pardoning convicted drug kingpins?!” Meteorologist Alan Gerard wrote on Bluesky.

“Trump: I will kill the drug cartels. Also Trump: Pardoning a powerful drug trafficker,” attorney Bradley P. Moss quipped.

“I was told by Republicans that they want to punish drug traffickers, not pardon them,” tweeted Jeopardy! champion Hermant Mehta. “They’re lying to you. And a bunch of voters fall for it every time.”

Trump did not immediately state his reason for pardoning Hernández. However, the former Honduran leader is connected to multiple wealthy Trump supporters, including Silicon Valley billionaire Peter Thiel. The New York Times reported in 2024 that Thiel (who bankrolled Vice President JD Vance’s 2022 U.S. Senate campaign), along with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and venture capitalist Marc Andreesen, worked with Hernández when he was president of Honduras’ congress to launch a “start-up city” called Próspera.

“Próspera is a private, for-profit city, with its own government that courts foreign investors through low taxes and light regulation,” the Times‘ Rachel Corbett wrote. “Businesses can choose a regulatory framework from a menu of 36 countries or customize their own.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Puzzling Pardon: A Deep Dive into the Hernández Decision

In an astonishing move that has left many scratching their heads in disbelief, former President Donald Trump announced his decision to pardon Juan Orlando Hernández, the ex-president of Honduras, who was serving a 45-year sentence in the U.S. for his role in facilitating a staggering 400 tons of cocaine into the country. This decision, announced via Trump’s Truth Social platform, is not only controversial but also deeply troubling for a myriad of reasons.

The Irony of Trump’s War on Drugs

Trump’s tenure was marked by a vociferous war on drugs, often characterized by aggressive rhetoric and actions, including fatal strikes on suspected drug traffickers. The pardon of Hernández, therefore, reeks of hypocrisy. Richard B. Simon’s comparison of Trump’s military actions against alleged drug lords to pardoning an actual convicted drug kingpin illustrates a bewildering contradiction in Trump’s policies. Is this for real? Yes, Alan Gerard, it appears we are indeed living in an era where Trump’s actions defy the very principles he purports to stand for.

The Political Chess Game

Trump’s endorsement of Nasry “Tito” Asfura for the upcoming presidential election in Honduras, coupled with the pardon of Hernández, appears to be a calculated political maneuver. It’s as if Trump is using the pardon as a chess piece in a larger game of international politics, where the endgame is to install a government in Honduras that aligns with his interests. However, it’s a dangerous game that undermines the rule of law and the fight against global drug trafficking networks.

The Shadows of Wealth and Influence

The revelation that Hernández is connected to multiple wealthy Trump supporters, including Peter Thiel, shines a light on the murky intersection of wealth, politics, and justice. The involvement of Silicon Valley giants in the Próspera project raises questions about the role of economic interests in political decisions. It’s deeply unsettling to consider that the scales of justice could be tipped by the hands of the wealthy and powerful, turning the pardon into a tool for political and financial gain rather than a measure of mercy.

The Impact on U.S.-Honduras Relations

The pardon and Trump’s overt political endorsements have the potential to strain U.S.-Honduras relations. By meddling in Honduran politics and pardoning a figure who has caused significant harm to both Hondurans and Americans, Trump risks alienating a key partner in Central America. The long-term consequences of this decision on diplomatic ties and cooperation in combating drug trafficking are yet to be seen but could be far-reaching.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

Trump’s pardon of Juan Orlando Hernández is a systemic error that highlights a dangerous precedent where political expediency and personal interests can trump justice and accountability. It’s a stark reminder of the flaws in our political and justice systems, where power and money can outweigh the scales of justice, leaving the principles of fairness and rule of law as mere casualties in the game of politics.

The decision to pardon Hernández is not only a misstep but a clear indicator of the systemic errors that plague our political landscape. It raises critical questions about the integrity of our leaders and the influence of money and power in shaping policy and justice. As we move forward, it is imperative that we scrutinize these decisions, hold our leaders accountable, and demand a system that values justice over political gain.
