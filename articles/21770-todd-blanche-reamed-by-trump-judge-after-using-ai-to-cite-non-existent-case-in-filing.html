Todd Blanche reamed by Trump judge after using AI to cite 'non-existent case' in filing

The Government Faked the Paper, Not the Power

Power Has a Name

Todd Blanche is not some stray bureaucrat who got lost in a filing system. He is Donald Trump’s former personal criminal defense attorney, now the acting attorney general, sitting atop the federal law enforcement apparatus while awaiting Senate confirmation. When a government brief goes out under his name, the responsibility is not abstract. It belongs to the office, the chain of command, and the political operation that put him there.

The Citation Was the Symptom

A federal judge appointed by Trump said Blanche’s team filed a brief that appeared to rely on a nonexistent case, one the court could not find anywhere. The judge said it was likely generated by artificial intelligence and left open the possibility of sanctions later. That is the surface-level scandal: a top federal legal filing citing fiction. But the bigger story is what the filing was trying to protect.

Detention by Delay

The underlying dispute was not complicated. An Immigration and Customs Enforcement detainee, Izzeddin Daghra, had been granted a $35,000 bond by an immigration judge. The government appealed and used that appeal to keep him locked up in the meantime. That is the real mechanism at work: not law as neutral process, but law as a stall tactic to extend confinement.

The Point Wasn’t Confusion

The judge’s rebuke is useful, but it can also obscure the structure underneath. A fake citation is embarrassing. Holding a person in custody while the government drags out review is the policy. Whether the bad brief came from laziness, incompetence, or machine-assisted slop, the outcome served the same purpose: keep the detainee detained until the clock ran out. The 90-day automatic hold expired, and only then was Daghra free to post bond and go home.

ICE Is the Crime Scene

This is why pretending ICE can be fixed with better training or tighter oversight is a dodge. ICE is not a neutral agency that occasionally overreaches. It is a detention regime built to separate people from liberty first and ask questions later. The article’s real evidence is not just that Blanche’s office submitted garbage lawyering. It is that the federal government treats confinement as a default administrative tool, then wraps it in procedural fog to make the violence look ordinary.

The Pattern Is Institutional Rot

The judge exposed a false citation, but the larger pathology is older than AI and broader than one acting attorney general. Trump’s legal orbit keeps putting loyalists, defenders, and enforcers into offices that are supposed to restrain state power, then uses that power to delay, detain, and distort. The paperwork may be fraudulent. The machinery is not.
