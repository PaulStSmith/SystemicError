Wall Street Journal writer Josh Dawsey says the White House is growing increasingly frustrated with the antics of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, though he noted President Donald Trump is loathe to fire anyone in his second administration out of bitterness to his enemies.

“The president has told senior officials in the administration he does not want to fire Cabinet secretaries at this time. And he wished that he wouldn’t have had to fire so many people in the first time. And it led to leaks and drama and he doesn’t want to give scalps to his critics or the media,” Dawsey told MS NOW anchor Katy Tur. “So, I think that’s one reason you see some survivals.”

Noem currently faces bipartisan frustration in Congress after she falsely claimed Minnesota resident Alex Pretti was “a domestic terrorist” who was “brandishing” a gun and was intent on “killing law enforcement”—a lie for which she has yet to apologize.

More recently, the Wall Street Journal exposed Noem’s unpredictable behavior, revealing she had fired a federal employee for leaving her blanket on a plane.

“Within DHS, Noem and Lewandowski frequently berate senior level staff, give polygraph tests to employees they don’t trust and have fired employees—in one incident, Lewandowski fired a U.S. Coast Guard pilot after Noem’s blanket was left behind on a plane, according to people familiar with the incident,” Dawsey reported in WSJ.

Then, the simmering criticism boiled over within the last few months with the killings of Minneapolis, Minnesota residents by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents. Noem’s policies and publicity moves backfired in Minneapolis, prompting some in Trump’s inner circle to try to persuade him to fire Noem and her top adviser, Corey Lewandowski.

Dawsey confirmed “there’s widespread frustration” with Noem inside the White House, but Trump refuses to fire her, at least for now.

“By all intents and purposes, he does still support her, even though many of his aides and advisers have been frustrated. But, you know, you’ve watched the president for a long time, Katy. He supports someone until suddenly he doesn’t. You know, sometimes so and so has his support, has his support, has his support — and then they’re out.”

Analysis of Wall Street Journal’s Coverage on Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem

The Wall Street Journal’s recent dive into the turbulent waters surrounding Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, as penned by Josh Dawsey, is a tale of political intrigue, stubborn loyalty, and questionable leadership that reads more like a screenplay for a political thriller than a chapter out of the conventional political playbook. Dawsey’s reporting sheds light on the underlying tensions brewing within the Trump administration, a scenario where loyalty appears to triumph over competency and ethical governance. Let’s dissect this piece, shall we?

The Reluctance to Fire

Firstly, Dawsey informs us that President Trump, in his second term, is loathe to fire anyone, primarily due to his perceived repercussions from his first term in office. This stance, as described, is less about maintaining a stable administration and more about avoiding “leaks and drama.” It’s a fascinating, albeit concerning, insight into the decision-making processes at the highest level of government. The refusal to dismiss cabinet members, despite bipartisan frustration and controversy, is indicative of a leadership style that prioritizes personal grievances over public accountability.

Kristi Noem’s Controversies

Noem’s tenure, as highlighted by Dawsey, is rife with controversy and seemingly erratic behavior. The false accusation against Minnesota resident Alex Pretti as a “domestic terrorist” is not just a grave error; it’s an unfathomable breach of trust and duty. Yet, the lack of apology or correction from Noem speaks volumes of her disregard for accountability. Moreover, the firing of a federal employee over something as trivial as a forgotten blanket on a plane borders on the absurd. These incidents, as reported, are not merely “antics” but serious lapses in judgment and leadership.

Internal White House Frustration

Dawsey’s reporting on the “widespread frustration” within the Trump administration regarding Noem’s actions and the president’s steadfast support for her, despite this, is particularly telling. It underscores a significant disconnect between the president’s loyalty to his appointees and the expectations of his administrative team. The narrative that Trump “supports someone until suddenly he doesn’t” hints at an unpredictable and volatile leadership style that can have far-reaching implications for governance and policy implementation.

Conclusion: A Systemic Flaw?

The details unearthed by Dawsey in the Wall Street Journal are not just a reflection of Noem’s conduct but are indicative of a systemic flaw within the current administration. When loyalty and personal vendettas outweigh competence and ethical considerations, the machinery of government is compromised. The administration’s reluctance to address Noem’s controversial actions and behavior, as described by Dawsey, is a glaring testament to this flaw.

In essence, the article paints a picture of an administration caught in a web of loyalty, controversy, and political calculation. The enduring support for Noem, despite the bipartisan frustration and her questionable judgment, is a stark reminder of the complex dynamics that govern political appointments and their implications for public trust and governance. As observers and constituents, we must remain vigilant and critical of the leaders entrusted with our safety, security, and well-being.
