'Defeating that king': Ex-Tea Party congressman warns of Trump spies at weekend protests

Donald Trump and Republicans have a specific agenda for how they want the upcoming “No Kings” protests to play out, according to a former Tea Party lawmaker who is now a Dem.

Joe Walsh issued an alert on his Substack for those attending the upcoming protests, telling them that the GOP will have scouts at “every” single rally. According to Walsh, it’s important for participants not to bring Palestinian flags, or anything else that could be perceived as not just anti-American, but non-American.

“I don’t mean to offend anybody with what I’m about to write. But it needs to be said, because there’s too much on the line. This Saturday—No King’s Day—is too darn important for niceties,” he wrote. “On Saturday, please keep your Palestinian flags at home. I don’t want to see a single Palestinian flag at the No King’s Day protests. This isn’t about Gaza. So leave your Israeli flags home as well. And your Ukrainian flags too, for that matter.”

For Walsh, it’s not just about the flags.

“Saturday is No Kings Day. It’s not about any policy. I guess what I’m trying to say is, please don’t bring your own personal passions, beefs, and biases beyond this one: we are united to defeat fascism, defend democracy, and restore the rule of law. That’s it. That’s enough,” he wrote. “We have a fascist in the White House. The very thing our founding fathers feared is sitting the White House right now, and he’s got millions of enablers and an entire political party utterly in thrall to him. No Kings Day is about defeating that king.”

The focus is especially important, Walsh warns, because of spies.

“Be smart. Republicans want you to f--- up. They want you to get violent, because they want the videos. They’re going have people out at every rally. And they want videos of people waving the Palestinian flag too. Don’t do it,” he said. “Express your love for America on Saturday. That’s what it’s all about, by the way. It’s all about love for this country. Do not go out Saturday and hate on America. That’s what Trump wants. That’s what JD Vance wants. That’s what Republicans want.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the “No Kings” Protest Strategy and the GOP’s Anticipated Response

In an intriguing turn of events, Joe Walsh, a former Tea Party lawmaker turned Democrat, has issued a stark warning to participants of the upcoming “No Kings” protests. His advice, while seemingly straightforward, unpacks a complex web of political strategy, underlying biases, and the perpetual battle for the narrative superiority in American politics.

The Call for a Unified Front

Walsh’s plea for protesters to leave their Palestinian, Israeli, and Ukrainian flags at home is more than a mere suggestion for thematic consistency. It’s a strategic move to prevent the dilution of the protest’s message. Walsh emphasizes unity against fascism, the defense of democracy, and the restoration of the rule of law as the sole focus. This is a crucial point, especially in an era where protests can quickly become muddled with a myriad of intersecting causes. However, the instruction to suppress these symbols of international solidarity or dissent reveals a deeper tension within American political movements about prioritizing domestic over global injustices.

The GOP’s Anticipated Infiltration

Walsh’s warning about Republican scouts at these rallies is not only a testament to the high stakes of the political battlefield but also an acknowledgment of the sophisticated tactics employed by political parties to undermine each other. The claim that the GOP desires footage of violence or controversial flags to discredit the protest is a classic example of how modern protests are fought as much in the media and online as they are on the streets. Walsh’s assertion that “Republicans want you to f--- up” is a blunt reminder of the manipulative games at play.

Questioning the Simplification of Patriotic Expression

Walsh’s call to “express your love for America” and avoid “hating on America” is a contentious point. It simplifies the rich tapestry of what it means to be patriotic. Can one not criticize their country’s policies or actions and still love it? This dichotomy between love and criticism seems to be a reductive approach to patriotism, one that does not account for the nuanced views many Americans hold about their country’s role in the world and its internal issues.

The Invocation of Fascism

Walsh’s reference to a fascist in the White House is a powerful and polarizing statement. It aligns with his broader call to action against what he perceives as a threat to democracy. However, the use of “fascism” is a loaded term that requires careful consideration. While it is clear that Walsh is sounding the alarm on authoritarian tendencies, the term itself can sometimes serve more to inflame than to inform, potentially alienating those who might be persuaded by a more nuanced critique.

The Unaddressed Irony

There is an irony in Walsh’s transformation from a Tea Party conservative to a Democrat warning against the Republican tactics at protests. This evolution is a fascinating subplot to the broader narrative, highlighting the fluid nature of American political identities and the complex web of allegiances and beliefs that define the country’s political landscape.

In Conclusion

Walsh’s warnings and recommendations for the “No Kings” protests are a microcosm of the larger battles being waged in the American political arena. The strategies, both overt and covert, reflect a deep understanding of the power of narrative and the importance of controlling the optics of public dissent. However, they also raise questions about the sacrifices made in the name of unity and the complexities of expressing patriotism in a divided nation. As always, the devil is in the details, and the unfolding of the “No Kings” protests will likely offer valuable lessons for the future of political activism in America.
