'Shameful': Analysts slam Trump's warning for D.C. homeless to 'move out immediately'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Latest Fiasco: A Masterclass in Misdirection and Cruelty

Donald Trump gestures to reporters from the roof of the White House. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Analysts and advocates for people who are homeless were outraged on Sunday after President Donald Trump issued a warning to the unhoused in Washington, D.C.

Trump posted on Truth Social that people experiencing unsheltered homelessness must "move out immediately." It was issued at a time when federal data shows homelessness increased by 18% between 2023 and 2024, with more than 771,000 people who are homeless counted during the last survey.

"We will give you places to stay, but FAR from the Capital (sic). The Criminals, you don’t have to move out. We’re going to put you in jail where you belong," Trump's post reads in part.

Analysts and advocates for people who are homeless responded on social media.

"The billionaires at the Cicero Institute have their hands all over these shameful steps," Jesse Rabinowitz, an activist and spokesperson for the National Homelessness Law Center, wrote on Bluesky.

"Again, Republicans are using DC as a sandbox for their failed, racist, and backwards policies. Pay attention to what happens here, because it will soon happen everywhere," Rabinowitz added.

"I hardly see anybody on the streets in DC these days. If there are encampments, they’re hiding," Steve Berg, chief policy officer at the National Alliance to End Homelessness, wrote on LinkedIn. "Could it be somebody thinks he can prove how tough he is by threatening … people who are homeless??"

"Trump is going to traffic homeless people," Melanie D'Arrigo, executive director for the Campaign for New York Health, wrote on X. "Many won’t have ID, and will likely end up incarcerated with immigrants who are being arrested, trafficked, and detained, without due process."

"People are not criminals or dangerous, by virtue of their unhoused status," the Washington Legal Clinic for the Homeless wrote on X. People are struggling to afford rent and food in an expensive city. We should not have homelessness in our nation’s capital. But the path to ending homelessness is housing, not displacement.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Latest Fiasco: A Masterclass in Misdirection and Cruelty

In a move that can only be described as both breathtakingly callous and painfully predictable, President Donald Trump's latest decree has sparked outrage among advocates for the homeless and analysts alike. His directive, issued via Truth Social, to the unhoused population of Washington, D.C., to "move out immediately" is not just a failure in policy—it's a disgrace to the principles of compassion and decency.

The Numbers Speak: A Crisis Ignored

Let's start with the cold, hard facts that Trump conveniently ignores. Federal data indicates a staggering 18% increase in homelessness between 2023 and 2024, bringing the count to over 771,000. These numbers aren't just statistics; they represent real people facing unimaginable hardship. And yet, the response from the Trump administration is not to address the root causes or to provide support but to issue an eviction notice.

Displacement Over Dignity

Trump's message is as clear as it is horrifying: "We will give you places to stay, but FAR from the Capital. The Criminals, you don’t have to move out. We’re going to put you in jail where you belong." This is nothing short of social and spatial cleansing under the guise of urban beautification. It's a tactic as old as time, leveraging fear and disdain for the impoverished to rally support from his base, while effectively doing nothing to solve the underlying issue of homelessness.

The Sandbox of Failed Policies

Jesse Rabinowitz hits the nail on the head, pointing out the Cicero Institute's involvement and lambasting the Republicans for using D.C. as a "sandbox for their failed, racist, and backwards policies." It's a pattern we've seen time and again: rather than addressing systemic issues with compassion and comprehensive policy, the go-to move is displacement and criminalization.

Steve Berg's observation underscores a chilling reality: the disappearing homeless population isn't a sign of success in combating homelessness; it's evidence of a strategy to hide the problem rather than solve it. Trump's move to "traffic homeless people," as Melanie D'Arrigo aptly puts it, is a gross violation of human rights, treating the most vulnerable members of society as chess pieces in a game of political posturing.

Housing, Not Displacement

The Washington Legal Clinic for the Homeless brings us back to the undeniable truth: "People are not criminals or dangerous, by virtue of their unhoused status." The crisis of homelessness in a city as affluent as Washington, D.C., is a glaring indictment of our collective failure to ensure the basic rights of housing and dignity for all.

The path to ending homelessness, as the Legal Clinic points out, is through housing, not displacement. It's a simple concept that seems to elude the Trump administration: you can't just wish away the homeless crisis by pushing it out of sight. It requires actual work, resources, and, most importantly, a fundamental shift in how we view and treat the most vulnerable among us.

In Conclusion

Trump's latest dictate is a stark reminder of the systemic cruelty at the heart of his administration's approach to governance. It's not just about the inhumanity of telling people to "move out immediately"; it's about the utter failure to engage with the crisis of homelessness with the seriousness and compassion it demands.

In a just society, the measure of our success is not in how well we beautify our streets by removing those who have fallen through its cracks but in how we uplift and support every member of our community. Trump's failure to grasp this is not just a failure of policy—it's a failure of humanity.
