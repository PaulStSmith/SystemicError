MAGA Epstein fury shows even Trumpers have a line they will not cross

As Republicans continue their journey backwards in time to a dark place before science, vaccines, critical thinking, diplomacy, and decency, it is important the rest of us understand that it is what is still ahead that really matters — hard as that can be right now.

While I was crashing through my newspapers on Sunday morning, reading about all the Republican blood and gore flowing down off Capitol Hill, I decided to settle my stomach, and try something else.

So I poured another cup of coffee, rubbed my tired eyes, and googled: “ACTUARIAL LIFE TABLE OBESE 79-YEAR-OLD WHITE MAN.”

The answer is roughly 86, maybe even less because all these tables I looked at wouldn't factor in the “obese” part of my hasty query.

You already know whose life expectancy I was looking into …

Donald Trump is a very sick old man, and cult leader, who doesn’t have much longer.

This is a real relief to those of us from the New York/New Jersey/Connecticut area who have been sick and tired of this sick and tired guy for going on 50 years. We’ve always known he was a loudmouth, bigot, cheater, and liar, but we didn’t know until 2016 how many of us also thought this loudmouth, bigot, cheater and liar should be president.

That one’s admittedly been a tragic eye-opener for me, and many of my old friends might as well be dead and gone, because I want nothing to do with them.

Other people outside the tri-state area, stumbled onto the guy when he was in debt to seemingly everybody, and was bailed out by landing a starring role as a ruthless businessman on some stupid reality TV show that has contributed to the dumbing-down of America.

By 2015, the mass media decided everybody else needed to be introduced to the career loudmouth, bigot, cheater and liar, and after he descended in the direction of hell on that escalator, they shoved him down our throats in a steady stream.

Thus began the beginning of the end ...

That was 10 years ago now, and easily the longest century in American politics.

He should be in jail for his attack on America, but I’ve written that column before, and would rather focus this one on his end date, which we know for sure is taking up all his time these days.

The ghastly Trump is the most self-involved man on the planet. How else to explain the hours and hours he spends in front of the mirror taping stringy, hyena-colored hair strands to his head and puffing them up just so, while slathering gooey, rust-orange paste to his face.

He looks completely ridiculous, of course, but makes sure to surround himself with a steady collection of ass-kissers, who would never dare tell him the truth.

Billions around the world know, though ...

And it’s when he gets out of his protective, choreographed bubble these days where he really starts running into trouble.

Four weeks ago, I wrote a piece, Stick a Fork Him, because I didn’t (and still don’t) believe that with a chilling 1,430 days left in his grotesque presidency that he has the stamina or the ability to make it to the end.

He is crashing.

I wrote that piece in the wake of his sudden surrender to world leaders at the G7 Summit in Canada. The haggard Trump was out of his tiny element, and in a faraway place.

Mostly he was lost, and outclassed by the younger, smarter leaders who know for sure that time is on their side, and Trump is best when he’s left in his protective, million-gallon Tupperware container.

The diminishing orange man couldn’t even fake a 3rd-grader’s understanding of world affairs, so he did what any outclassed and failing person would do: He just wandered away. He left the meetings early because of “What’s going on in the Middle East” but really because the “dog-ate-my-homework” excuse had finally reached its shelf life.

The world leaders who remained at the summit no doubt let out a deep sigh of relief, and went on with their business professionally and in peace and quiet.

Canada’s savvy new leader, Mark Carney, seated a young, up-and-comer named Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who had energetically traveled 5,000 miles just to be there, in Trump’s empty chair.

I still don’t think that this first-class shade and 1,000-watt signaling got enough attention, so let’s do something about that:

A meeting at the G7 Leaders' Summit in Kananaskis, in Alberta, Canada. REUTERS/Suzanne Plunkett/Pool

That’s the bearded Zelenskyy seated to Carney's left in the chair that had been occupied by the low-energy Trump, who abandoned the meetings to update the color of his face after his long nap home.

Four weeks later, things have only gotten worse for the failing Trump — much worse.

He is literally abandoning the world and isolating himself in his safe, golden cocoon. Each day he is becoming tinier and cheaper as his old attention-grabbing schtick now dribbles off his chapped lips and onto his stained bib.

He is the old man screaming at people he can no longer see to stay off his lawn.

The world doesn’t much care. They are moving on, forming new alliances, because they, too, know how to read an actuarial table.

This weekend the Fox Sewer System released 40-minutes of propaganda from their dear leader as he took a series of scathing questions from his daughter-in-law.

Read that again: HIS DAUGHTER-IN-LAW.

So I’ll bite: What would have been the reaction in the press if Joe Biden had done this?

Even parts of Trump’s odious base are peeling away from him. Not because he is taking their health care away, or because he would rather see them drown than get help from catastrophic flooding that is overrunning so many of their towns, but because pedophilia is a line-crosser even for them.

Rather than releasing the Jeffrey Epstein files as he campaigned on, to expose the “abuse of children by the elites,” Trump is just trying to make the whole thing go away.

Wait’ll these MAGAs find out that Trump referred o Epstein as a “fun and terrific guy who likes beautiful women as much as (he does), and many of them are on the younger side …"

They are promising this isn’t going away, but Trump is …

Regular readers will know I don’t see the end of Trump as the end of all our problems, but I do see it as a helluva hopeful start. He has re-exposed a dark underbelly of America that is only skin-deep. Nobody in my lifetime has been as effective at exploiting race and gender to his political advantage as this sickly old man.

As every hour and day ticks by we are moving closer to his end. That day is coming as sure as I am typing this. Not even a professional, lowlife grifter like Trump can cheat death.

One day, hard as it is to imagine right now, he will be extinct, and it will be our jobs to make sure as much of his gruesome legacy as possible dies with him.

(D. Earl Stephens is the author of “Toxic Tales: A Caustic Collection of Donald J. Trump’s Very Important Letters” and finished up a 30-year career in journalism as the Managing Editor of Stars and Stripes. You can find all his work here.)

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of "The End of an Era: The Fading Trump"

In an era where political discourse frequently teeters on the edge of hyperbole and vitriol, D. Earl Stephens' article plunges headlong into the abyss with an unapologetically scathing critique of Donald Trump and his enduring impact on American politics. However, beneath the surface of its vehement rhetoric lies a complex web of assertions that merit a closer examination. Let's dissect the key elements of Stephens' argument, peeling back the layers of sensationalism to uncover the factual basis and potential biases within.

The Premise: Trump's Declining Influence

Stephens begins with a poetic lamentation of the Republican Party's alleged regression, positioning Trump as the embodiment of this backward slide. The bold assertion that Trump is "a very sick old man, and cult leader" sets the tone for the entire piece, blending personal attacks with a grim prognosis for Trump's longevity. While the use of actuarial tables to speculate on Trump's life expectancy might strike some as morbidly humorous, it arguably crosses a line from political commentary into the realm of personal vendetta.

The Critique: Media and Political Legacy

The article's critique isn't limited to Trump alone; it extends to the mechanisms that propelled him to prominence. Stephens rightly points out the role of mass media in the "dumbing-down of America," highlighting the paradox of how a figure widely criticized in some circles for his character and capabilities was simultaneously elevated to a position of immense power and influence. This observation raises valid questions about the media's role in shaping political landscapes, albeit marred by Stephens' overt disdain.

The Comparison: International Relations and Leadership

Stephens' analysis of Trump's performance at the G7 Summit and the subsequent elevation of Volodymyr Zelenskyy to his "empty chair" is a compelling illustration of Trump's often criticized international diplomacy. The imagery of Trump as "out of his element" among younger, more dynamic leaders encapsulates a broader critique of his foreign policy and leadership style. However, the narrative flirts dangerously with oversimplification, ignoring the complex dynamics of international politics in favor of a stark dichotomy between competence and incompetence.

The Allegations: Controversies and Criticisms

The article doesn't shy away from tackling the thorniest aspects of Trump's presidency, including his controversial statements and policies. Stephens' anger is palpable when discussing the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal and its implications. Yet, the transition from legitimate critique to outright condemnation without a nuanced examination of the facts risks undermining the article's credibility. The leap from criticizing policy to invoking pedophilia as a political weapon exemplifies the dangers of conflating separate issues to vilify an opponent.

The Conclusion: Legacy and Hope

In concluding, Stephens posits Trump's eventual "extinction" as a symbolic purge of his "gruesome legacy." While the desire to move beyond a turbulent political era is understandable, the fixation on Trump's personal demise as a cathartic endpoint for his political influence is a troubling motif. It suggests a view of politics as a zero-sum game of personal victories and defeats, rather than a continuous process of societal evolution and negotiation.

Final Analysis

D. Earl Stephens' article is a fiery denunciation of Donald Trump, marked by personal animus and a deep sense of betrayal by the political system. While it raises legitimate concerns about media influence, leadership, and the direction of American politics, its analysis is often overshadowed by its own bitterness. In the quest to critique Trump, the article occasionally loses sight of the larger picture, sacrificing depth for the sake of diatribe.

As readers, we must navigate these passionate critiques with a critical eye, recognizing the difference between valid criticism and personal invective. The challenge lies not in dismissing the entirety of Stephens' argument, but in extricating the insightful critiques from the mire of resentment. In the end, the strength of our democracy depends not on the vilification of individuals, but on our ability to engage in thoughtful, informed, and nuanced discourse.
