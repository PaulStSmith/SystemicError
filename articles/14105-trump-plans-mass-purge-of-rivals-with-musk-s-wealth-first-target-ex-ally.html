Trump plans mass purge of rivals — with Musk's wealth first target: ex-ally

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unraveling Bromance: Trump vs. Musk

U.S. President-elect Donald Trump greets Tesla CEO and X owner Elon Musk during a rally the day before Trump is scheduled to be inaugurated for a second term, in Washington, U.S., January 19, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Donald Trump's longtime "fixer" predicts things will get ugly between the president and Elon Musk.

Michael Cohen, the president's former personal attorney who has since become an ardent critic, told MSNBC's "The Weekend: Primetime" on Sunday that Trump and his billionaire benefactor's "bromance" would soon come to an end, reported The Daily Beast.

“I’m going to say Trump will ultimately go after Elon’s money next, because it bothers him that he is the richest man in the world,” Cohen said. “Yes, he used him for his money. Now he’s going to go and he’s going to figure out how Elon, with his companies, took advantage of the United States of America.”

Musk formally exited his government role last week, and Cohen speculated that Trump would use the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) that the billionaire led before his departure to target the electric vehicle tax credit and other government subsidies the Tesla CEO relied on to build his massive fortune.

“They’re going to turn around, use DOGE to go, ‘Right, government efficiency, fraud, waste, and abuse, how is it that he got $7,000 as a credit for every Tesla vehicle that was sold?'" Cohen said. "'That’s where his billions were made. Why should the United States pay for electric vehicles? Pay for your own car if that’s what you want.’"

"So they’re going to claw it back,” Cohen added.

Cohen then made a dark prediction about how bad things could unravel in their relationship.

“He’s going to do to Elon Musk, my prediction, what Mohammed bin Salman did to the other members of the royal family,” Cohen said.

After his elevation to crown prince, bin Salman purged the royal family of rivals and threats through a mass arrest campaign in 2017. Cohen speculated that Trump could target his "first buddy" in a similar way.

“Donald doesn’t care about anyone or anything other than himself," Cohen said. "Look at what he did to me, for God’s sakes. Guy who was with him for a decade and a half, who literally would have taken a bullet for him ― but for him being the one pulling the trigger.”

Cohen pleaded guilty in 2018 to campaign finance violations, tax evasion, lying to Congress and other crimes related to his work for Trump during the 2016 campaign, and the former attorney served over a year in prison before being released to home confinement in 2020 due to COVID-19.

The Unraveling Bromance: Trump vs. Musk

In a narrative that reads less like headline news and more like the plot of a political thriller, Michael Cohen, once the loyal "fixer" for Donald Trump, has forecasted a dramatic souring in the relationship between the former president and billionaire Elon Musk. According to The Daily Beast, Cohen shared his ominous predictions on MSNBC's "The Weekend: Primetime," suggesting that the erstwhile "bromance" is on the brink of collapse. Let's dissect the dynamics at play and the implications of Cohen's statements.

First, Cohen's Credentials: Cohen, now a vocal critic of Trump, comes with baggage heavy enough to fill a cargo plane. Having served time for a laundry list of crimes, including campaign finance violations, tax evasion, and lying to Congress, all committed in the service of Donald Trump, his insights carry the weight of personal experience and the sting of betrayal. His transition from Trump's "fixer" to a whistleblower offers a unique lens through which to view the impending conflict.

The Billionaire's Battle: The crux of Cohen's prediction hinges on Trump's envy of Musk's wealth and the latter's recent departure from his government role leading the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE). Cohen speculates that Trump's next move will be to target Musk's finances, particularly focusing on the government subsidies that have been a significant boon to Musk's ventures, like Tesla. The electric vehicle tax credit, which provides a $7,000 credit for every Tesla sold, is pinpointed as a key area of contention.

An Untenable Comparison: Cohen draws a parallel between Trump's anticipated tactics against Musk and Mohammed bin Salman's purge of the Saudi royal family. This comparison, while dramatic, underscores the perceived severity of Trump's potential actions. It serves to highlight Cohen's view of Trump as a figure who prioritizes self-interest above all else, even at the cost of former allies.

A Pattern of Betrayal: Cohen's personal testimony—"Look at what he did to me, for God’s sakes"—underscores a pattern of behavior that many critics of Trump have highlighted over the years: a willingness to discard loyalists the moment they cease to be useful or become a liability. This pattern, if Cohen's predictions hold true, suggests Musk could be the next in a long line of allies turned adversaries in Trump's orbit.

Conclusion: While Cohen's insights are undoubtedly colored by his personal experiences and possible biases, they raise important questions about the dynamics of power, loyalty, and self-interest in the highest echelons of political and financial influence. The potential clash between Trump and Musk, two titans of their respective fields, promises not only a spectacle but a revealing study in the nature of contemporary power struggles. Whether Cohen's predictions will come to fruition remains to be seen, but if history offers any lessons, it's that the fallout could be as significant as it is sensational.
