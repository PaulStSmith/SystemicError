Trump's woke-free Kennedy Center announces season packed with drag shows

Donald Trump’s-revamped Kennedy Center announced its upcoming season Monday — and the stage will be packed with drag queens.

The president overthrew the prestigious performing arts center's board of directors in February, taking over as chairman and vowing to rid it of wokeness — including drag performances.

“Just last year, the Kennedy Center featured Drag Shows specifically targeting our youth — THIS WILL STOP,” Trump wrote on Truth Social as he announced the purge of the D.C. venue.

But the upcoming season, announced at a ceremony the president attended, contains a lot of performers in drag, the Daily Beast reported.

Take Mrs. Doubtfire, for example — a tale of a man who dresses as a nanny in order to see his children.

And then there’s Chicago, in which Mary Sunshine’s wig is pulled off to expose things are ‘not always as they appear.” And Moulin Rouge’s Baby Doll is a drag queen.

The choice of shows the center could pick was more limited than normal as many artists have refused to perform there after Trump’s changes.

The Kennedy Center serves as a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy. As a cultural institution, it hosts over 2,000 performances and events each year, featuring diverse artistic disciplines like music, theater and dance.

On Monday, he bragged that he had eliminated DEI at the center and “brought back family-friendly programming that will attract large audiences once again.”

Our Analysis:

The Ironic Twist of Trump's Kennedy Center

In a move that can only be described as utterly paradoxical, Donald Trump's overhauled Kennedy Center, which he commandeered with the promise to exorcise it of its so-called 'wokeness,' seems to have inadvertently embraced that very element. The announcement of the upcoming season, drenched in drag performances, is not just a slap in the face to Trump's avowed mission but an exquisite display of the inconsistency and superficiality that plagues his cultural crusade.

The Drag Dilemma

Trump's passionate diatribe against drag shows, specifically those targeting youth, as per his Truth Social proclamation, has boomeranged in the most spectacular fashion. The upcoming season boasts Mrs. Doubtfire, Chicago, and Moulin Rouge, each incorporating elements of drag, directly contravening his earlier declarations. This isn't just a minor oversight; it's a glaring spotlight on the hollowness of Trump's cultural war cries.

Mrs. Doubtfire, a heartwarming tale of a father's desperate attempt to be with his children, Chicago, a narrative that peels layers off societal facades, and Moulin Rouge, an ode to love and bohemian ideals, all feature drag in ways that are integral to their storylines. To miss this fact is to demonstrate a profound misunderstanding of both the art form and the narratives themselves.

The Grift That Keeps on Giving

Trump's tenure at the Kennedy Center has not been without its controversies. His 'exclusive grift' that overshadowed even the notorious Qatari plane scandal is a testament to his modus operandi. The paradox of his position—a president who once vowed to purge drag from the Kennedy Center, now presiding over its flourishing—is a narrative twist not even the most imaginative playwright could concoct.

The Boycott Backlash

The selection of shows for the upcoming season was notably constrained, a direct consequence of the boycott by many artists refusing to perform in the wake of Trump's alterations. This boycott not only underscores the resistance within the artistic community but also highlights the cultural cost of politicizing art. The Kennedy Center, a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy, is meant to be a beacon of cultural diversity and artistic freedom, ideals seemingly at odds with the current administration's agenda.

The Illusion of DEI Elimination

Trump's boast of having eradicated Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) from the Kennedy Center in favor of "family-friendly programming" is a hollow victory at best. The inclusion of drag in the upcoming season—whether intentional or an oversight—underscores the inevitability of diverse narratives in any genuine artistic endeavor. Art, by its very nature, resists censorship and thrives on diversity; to claim victory over its essence is to misunderstand its most fundamental principles.

Conclusion

The Kennedy Center's upcoming season, replete with drag performances, is a poetic justice of sorts. It reveals the futility of trying to sanitize culture, to fit it into a narrow, prescribed box. Trump's attempt to rid the center of 'wokeness' has not only failed but backfired, highlighting a crucial misunderstanding of both art and the audience it serves. As the curtains rise on this unexpected act, one can't help but wonder: is the joke, in the end, on Trump himself?
