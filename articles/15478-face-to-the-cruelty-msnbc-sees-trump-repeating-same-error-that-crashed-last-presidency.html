The president is bleeding support for his handling of immigration 100 days into his second term, with 46 percent of adults in one poll saying they approve while 53 percent disapprove, a three-point swing in each direction since last month. Heilemann said that horrific stories about individual families were once again dragging Trump down.

"Immigration is the strongest issue they have and he is, across all of that polling, his numbers on immigration aren't great," Heileman said, "and I think you got to think back to the first term, when immigration was an important reason why he won the first term, often overlooked by people in analyzing the 2016 race, and what happened was he lost the plot with family separation.

"The family separation policy was when people started to put a human face to the cruelty of that policy, and he lost a lot of support on immigration around that issue, and I think it was predictable that if he tried to do an immigration policy that was as draconian as he promised, that the same phenomenon would happen."

Individual cases are breaking through, such as the administration's refusal to return Kilmar Abergo Garcia, a legal U.S. resident who was mistakenly deported to El Salvador, and Heilemann said others are beginning to drag down Trump's support on the issue.

"They are dragging his numbers down on an issue where, in general, he has a lot of support among the American people, and, in general, it's always been one of his strongest issues," Heilemann said. "They are just comprehensively, politically speaking, a huge amount of trouble with the American people right now."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Tides of Trump's Immigration Policy: A Redux of Failures

In a recent commentary, MSNBC's John Heilemann sheds light on a recurring nightmare in President Donald Trump's political saga—the handling of immigration policy. This isn't just a critique; it's a glaring spotlight on a cycle of failure, a political Groundhog Day, where Trump seems to be reliving the same mistakes that marred his first term.

The Polls Speak: Declining Approval

With a mere 46 percent approval rating on his immigration policy 100 days into his second term, Trump is witnessing a significant slip—a three-point dip, to be precise. This isn't just a numeric slide; it's a testament to the president's failure to learn from his past. The numbers, stark and unyielding, reflect a growing discontent among Americans, a significant portion of whom now disapprove of his approach to immigration, a cornerstone of his political platform.

The Heart of the Issue: Draconian Policies

Heilemann points out a critical error in Trump's strategy: the dehumanization embedded in his immigration policies. The narrative of family separation, a dark chapter from Trump's first term, is a prime example. It wasn't just a policy; it was a symbol of systemic cruelty that, once individual stories of suffering began to surface, significantly eroded public support for Trump's immigration stance.

One cannot help but draw parallels between past missteps and the current scenario, where the administration's refusal to correct egregious errors—like the deportation of Kilmar Abergo Garcia, a legal U.S. resident—only adds fuel to the fire of public outrage. These are not isolated incidents but rather illustrative of a broader, more troubling pattern.

The Core Misunderstanding

At the heart of Trump's faltering support on immigration is a fundamental miscalculation: the assumption that draconian policies would bolster his standing. Heilemann astutely observes that while immigration might have been one of Trump's strongest issues, the implementation of his policies, especially those perceived as excessively harsh or unfair, has led to a significant backlash.

The Political Quagmire

What we're witnessing is not merely a dip in poll numbers but a comprehensive political miscalculation. Trump's immigration policies, designed to be the bedrock of his tough-on-immigration stance, are instead becoming his Achilles' heel. Heilemann doesn't mince words; he calls it a "huge amount of trouble with the American people right now," a succinct summary of the quagmire in which Trump finds himself.

Conclusion: A Cycle of Failure

In essence, Trump's handling of immigration is a stark lesson in political hubris. The failure to evolve, to learn from past mistakes, and to understand the human cost of draconian policies has led to a predictable outcome: declining support. As Heilemann's analysis suggests, Trump's second term appears to be a rerun of his first, marked by the same strategic missteps that cost him dearly before. It's not just a failure of policy but a failure to grasp the fundamental values at the heart of the American ethos: compassion, fairness, and respect for individual rights.

In the end, this isn't just about numbers or approval ratings. It's about the very soul of America and what kind of nation it aspires to be. Trump's immigration policy, with its echoes of past mistakes, offers a clear choice between repeating a cycle of failure or learning from it. The answer, however, remains to be seen.
