James Comer 'going to hurt the president' as latest gambit 'backfires': ex-GOP congressman

Bill and Hillary Clinton are set to testify later this month before the House Oversight Committee in its ongoing Jeffrey Epstein probe, a GOP-led effort that “Morning Joe” hosts argued Friday could ultimately backfire “spectacularly” on President Donald Trump and Republicans.

Last month, the House Oversight Committee – led by Rep. James Comer (R-KY) – voted to hold the Clintons in contempt for defying subpoenas to testify on their knowledge of Epstein’s alleged crimes. Both have since agreed to testify before the committee, and are now scheduled to appear on Feb. 26 and 27.

And, while Republicans may have assumed that the Clintons’ testimony might be damaging to Democrats, “Morning Joe” host Willie Geist predicted that it may very well end up hurting Republicans far more.

“I think that’s going to backfire on Comer spectacularly. Why on earth [would] Republicans want to go back and put their hand on that hot stove again and rip off the scab of the Epstein files?” Geist said.

“[Why would Republicans want to] let Hillary [and] Bill Clinton talk about the Epstein files, and let Democrats in the hearing talk about the Epstein files, and how many times the president’s name appears in the Epstein files? It baffles not just us, but I think it sounds like it baffles and frightens President Trump himself.”

Bill Clinton has had a well-documented friendship with Epstein for years, having been visited by Epstein in the White House at least 17 times during his administration. Less of a connection exists between Epstein and Hillary Clinton, though Epstein’s associate Ghislaine Maxwell – who’s serving 20 years for sex trafficking – had attended Chelsea Clinton’s wedding in 2010.

Nevertheless, the Clintons’ testimony has the potential, argued “Morning Joe” host Jonathan Lemire, to deliver a damaging blow to Trump, who called Comer’s targeting of the Clintons a “miscalculation.”

“Epstein conspiracy theories were largely born on the right, sort of in MAGA world, and it was perceived as a Democratic problem. Certainly there are some [Democrats] who were in the files, but what’s been the flaw in that plan all along is the close association that Donald Trump had with Jeffrey Epstein for a long time!” Lemire said.

“Even if there’s nothing criminal there, it is an embarrassing friendship for Trump, which is why he was never at the forefront of the push to release the files. I think you are seeing Trump worried about the door that’s opened here. We’ll see; the Republicans think they’re trying to help, they may be actually hurting him.”

Joe Scarborough came away with a far more certain conclusion: “They are definitely going to hurt the president,” he said.

Our Analysis:

The Clinton-Epstein Testimony: A GOP Strategy Gone Awry?

In a move that seems ripped straight from the script of a political thriller, Bill and Hillary Clinton are slated to testify before the House Oversight Committee concerning their connections with Jeffrey Epstein. This scenario, set against the backdrop of a GOP-led initiative, strikes as a peculiar strategy, one that “Morning Joe” hosts argue could “spectacularly” backfire on President Donald Trump and the Republicans. Let’s dissect this bewildering strategy and its potential repercussions.

GOP’s Misguided Gambit

At the core of this unfolding drama is the House Oversight Committee’s decision, spearheaded by Rep. James Comer (R-KY), to hold the Clintons in contempt for defying subpoenas. This decision to drag the Clintons into the Epstein imbroglio is less a calculated maneuver and more a desperate grasp at political straws. Why indeed would Republicans, already entangled in the complex web of Epstein’s notorious legacy, “put their hand on that hot stove again”? The answer, as Willie Geist rightly questions, is as baffling as it is misguided.

A Potentially Self-Inflicted Wound

The Clintons’ connections to Epstein, particularly Bill Clinton’s documented interactions, are no secret. Yet, the decision to spotlight these connections under the guise of oversight seems to overlook a critical factor: Donald Trump’s own ties to Epstein. Jonathan Lemire’s observation hits the nail on the head—this could very well morph from a calculated attack on the Democrats to a glaring spotlight on Trump’s “embarrassing friendship” with Epstein.

The Irony of MAGA World’s Conspiracy Theories

The irony of this entire situation cannot be overstated. Epstein conspiracy theories, largely birthed and nurtured in the right-wing echo chambers, have boomeranged. Initially framed as a Democratic debacle, these theories now threaten to engulf Trump, highlighting a glaring oversight in the GOP’s strategy. The Republicans, in their zeal to malign the Clintons, may inadvertently underscore Trump’s uncomfortably close association with Epstein.

A Strategic Miscalculation

Trump’s characterization of Comer’s targeting of the Clintons as a “miscalculation” perhaps best encapsulates the essence of this entire episode. It’s a rare instance where Trump’s assessment aligns with a more rational analysis of the political landscape. This testimony, rather than delivering a coup de grâce to the Democrats, might just unravel as a self-inflicted wound for Trump and the Republicans.

Conclusion: A Spectacular Backfire?

As Joe Scarborough concludes, this move will “definitely hurt the president.” The GOP’s attempt to weaponize the Epstein controversy against the Clintons is poised to boomerang with potentially damaging consequences for Trump. In their eagerness to discredit their political adversaries, the Republicans seem to have lost sight of the bigger picture, embarking on a path that could lead to an embarrassing unraveling of their own ranks.

In this spectacle of political maneuvering, one thing is clear: the GOP’s strategy is not just a gamble; it’s a spectacular miscalculation, one that could indeed backfire with profound implications for the party and its most prominent figure, Donald Trump.
