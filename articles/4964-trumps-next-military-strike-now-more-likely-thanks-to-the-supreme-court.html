Trump’s next military strike now more likely — thanks to the Supreme Court

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 24, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump during a White House press briefing in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 20, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

When Donald Trump launched his 2016 presidential campaign, reporters often described his Patrick Buchanan-influenced “America First” views as “isolationist” and “paleoconservative.” And his ideas on foreign policy were a major departure from the hawkish neocons of the George W. Bush Administration.

But Trump, since returning to the White House 13 months ago, has favored a much more interventionist foreign policy — from capturing former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro to calling for Canada to become “the 51st state” (an idea that Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney adamantly opposes) to wanting to buy Greenland and make it a U.S. territory (which a long list of European leaders are dead-set against). Now, according to Politico, Trump’s next target appears to be Iran.

In a podcast and column posted on February 23, Politico reporters Jack Blanchard and Dasha Burns stress that Trump’s apparent focus on Iran comes right after the U.S. Supreme Court, in a 6-3 decision, struck down most of his tariffs as illegal.

“The dust has barely settled on Trump’s seismic defeat at the Supreme Court last Friday — but attention is moving rapidly to the next crisis on the horizon,” Politico reports. “The world is holding its breath as the president mulls air strikes against Iran as soon as this week. The stakes could not be higher, given the history of U.S. entanglements in the Middle East. This is a legacy-defining moment for Trump. And let’s be clear: All the signs point to imminent military action…. Boat-spotters (yes, they exist) photographed the fully laden USS Gerald R. Ford entering the Mediterranean on Friday, meaning it should be within strike range any day.”

Politico continues, “The simple fact is that America has never built up a military force like this and not used it. It’s hard to believe they’ll all be sent home without firing a shot.”

Politico notes that Trump “warned Tehran last week he is ‘going to get a deal one way or another’ and suggested a 10-15-day maximum timeframe.”

“Iran would have to come up with one heck of an offer now for Trump to be able to walk back all his threats and stand down all this military hardware without being accused of another damaging ‘TACO’ moment,” according to Politico. “And the Supreme Court’s decision last Friday to slap down the president’s use of tariffs only makes an attack more likely, in the view of Trump’s former commerce secretary, Wilbur Ross. ‘I don’t think he can take this loss and then be seen as backing down on Iran,’ Ross told the (Wall Street Journal).”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Doctrine: A Masterclass in Unpredictability

As we dissect the latest developments in what can only be described as the Trumpian approach to foreign policy, it’s essential to highlight the sharp pivot from the “America First” rhetoric that defined Donald Trump’s 2016 campaign to his current stance, which can be best described as interventionist on steroids. This transformation underscores not just a departure from his previously claimed “isolationist” and “paleoconservative” ideologies but a complete obliteration of any coherent foreign policy doctrine.

The Volatility of Trump’s Foreign Ambitions

The audacity of Trump’s foreign policy maneuvers, from the fantastical proposition of making Canada the “51st state” to the aggressive posturing towards acquiring Greenland, borders on the surreal. However, none of these capricious endeavors compare to the looming specter of military action against Iran. According to Politico, the trigger-happy inclination towards Iran is not just a mere flexing of military muscle but appears to be a distraction tactic following a significant legal blow with the U.S. Supreme Court striking down most of his tariffs as illegal.

It’s bewildering to witness the rapid shift in focus from a domestic legal defeat to the brinkmanship of international warfare. The Politico report ominously hints at imminent military action against Iran, a decision fraught with consequences and the potential to further destabilize an already volatile region. The deployment of the USS Gerald R. Ford into the Mediterranean is not just a show of force but a glaring indication of Trump’s willingness to leap into conflict.

The Supreme Court Decision: A Catalyst for War?

The connection drawn between the Supreme Court’s decision and the heightened likelihood of an attack on Iran is both alarming and telling. Wilbur Ross’s comments to the Wall Street Journal suggest that Trump views military action not just as a foreign policy tool but as a means to salvage his bruised ego and political standing. The concept of engaging in potentially catastrophic military action to avoid being perceived as weak is a dangerous gamble with global security at stake.

The Illusion of a “TACO” Moment

Politico’s mention of a “TACO” moment — an instance where Trump might have to retract his aggressive posturing due to Iran’s compliance or diplomatic maneuvering — seems increasingly implausible. The setup suggests that Trump has cornered himself into a position where military action appears to be the only exit strategy that wouldn’t tarnish his image as a deal-maker. The irony here is palpable; a president who prides himself on unpredictability and deal-making now finds himself predictably cornered by his own rhetoric and actions.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Recklessness

As the world watches with bated breath, the potential for military action against Iran represents not just a “legacy-defining moment” for Trump, but a critical juncture for global peace and stability. Trump’s erratic approach to foreign policy, marked by a glaring disregard for the ramifications of military intervention, sets a dangerous precedent. The shift from isolationism to interventionism under Trump’s command is not driven by a coherent strategy but by a series of impulsive decisions that risk entangling the United States in conflicts with unpredictable outcomes.

In the end, Trump’s foreign policy legacy may well be defined by a reckless gamble on Iran, a move that could have dire consequences for the Middle East and beyond. The real question remains: Is the world prepared to deal with the aftermath of Trump’s interventionist itch?
