Latest ICE killings expose a horrifying — and unexpected — truth

Based on an article by John Casey

ICE Shot First, Then the Bureaucracy Lied

The Power to Kill

The source centers on Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a 52-year-old construction company owner in Houston who had lived in the United States for 35 years. On July 7, 2026, ICE officers followed him in unmarked vehicles during a traffic stop operation, shot him in the torso, and later claimed he tried to run over an agent. His family says he panicked, believing thieves were after his van and tools. Video released after the shooting reportedly undercuts ICE’s account.

The Story Behind the Story

The important fact is not that ICE says “self-defense.” The important fact is that ICE had the guns, the vehicles, the masks, the narrative machine, and the authority to turn a morning commute into a fatal encounter. Lorenzo was not even the target of the operation. He was a man trying to get to work. The agency chose the conditions, escalated the confrontation, and then reached for the standard federal violence script: blame the dead man before the facts can move.

Misdirection Is the Point

This is how institutional impunity works. The public is asked to focus on whether a driver “weaponized” a vehicle, whether the officer felt threatened, whether the situation was “confusing.” That framing launders a deliberate act of state violence into a tragic misunderstanding. It hides the basic asymmetry: masked federal agents in unmarked vehicles hunted down a worker, then shot him, then immediately told the country why it was fine.

That is not a neutral account. It is a cover story with a badge on it.

Numbness Is Not Innocence

The piece is right to dwell on numbness, because numbness is how repeated cruelty becomes administratively normal. The article notes that this was one of eight deaths in the current wave of ICE enforcement, with another fatal shooting reported in Maine just as the column was being finished. Repetition does not make these killings smaller. It trains the public to absorb them without resistance, which is exactly what violent institutions need.

The danger is not only that ICE kills. It is that the killings are processed as routine updates, then filed away beneath the next outrage.

Abolish ICE

This is not an oversight problem. It is not a training problem. It is not a “few bad actors” problem. ICE is built to wage coercion against migrants and anyone it decides to treat like one, and it keeps proving that its operational logic ends in blood. When an agency can kill a father, a business owner, and a longtime resident, then immediately move to self-exonerate, the institution itself is the scandal.

ICE should be abolished. Not renovated, not “reformed,” not given another round of public relations cleanup. Abolished.

The Larger Pattern

Lorenzo Salgado Araujo’s death is not an isolated tragedy. It is what happens when the state normalizes masked force, rewards false certainty, and treats migrant lives as disposable enough to lie about after the fact. The deeper pattern is familiar: power acts first, justifies later, and counts on public fatigue to do the rest.

That is the system. The violence is not incidental to it. The violence is how it keeps working.
