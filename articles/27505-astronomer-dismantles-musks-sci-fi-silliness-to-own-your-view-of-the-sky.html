Astronomer dismantles Musk’s 'sci-fi silliness' to own your view of the sky

Based on an article by The Conversation

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20261 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The promised dispute is absentA headline cannot substitute for evidence

AlterNet’s headline promises an astronomer’s rebuttal to Elon Musk and an explanation of how someone could “own” the public’s view of the sky. The supplied material establishes none of it. It contains only a string of names and political search terms, with no astronomer, no Musk proposal, no quotation, no event and no reported outcome.

The promised dispute is absent

There is no factual account from which to identify the astronomer, describe Musk’s alleged “sci-fi silliness,” or explain what control over the sky supposedly means. The material does not establish whether the dispute concerns satellites, spaceflight, astronomy, advertising or something else entirely.

Nor does it provide evidence about who possesses institutional authority, what decision was made, or who would bear its consequences. Any attempt to supply those details would be invention, not reporting.

A headline cannot substitute for evidence

The unexplained list—George Conway, Noam Chomsky, Kayleigh McEnany, Melania Trump, Paul Krugman, Lindsey Graham and others—does not substantiate the headline’s claim or form a coherent account of political power. Treating it as one would merely convert search-engine debris into a fabricated story.

The only defensible conclusion is therefore narrow: the provided text does not answer the headline’s question or support a standalone article about Musk, astronomy or control of the night sky.
