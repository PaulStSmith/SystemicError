Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. was called out by the editors of the Wall Street Journal opinion page for declaring war on Tylenol as a putative cause of autism cases, claiming he is doing it in the service of pals who will profit from the accusations.

In an editorial late Tuesday, the editors acknowledged that more research needs to be done into autism, but claimed that Kennedy is taking his department down the wrong path. And they questioned his motives.

According to some analysts, Kennedy, who has no medical training, has severely disrupted his department’s mission by firing experts and replacing them with like-minded cronies. And the Journal is now accusing him of not only sketchy science, but also possible criminality.

The editors asked, “why the sudden alarm, complete with a presidential presser?” to which they added, “The Occam’s razor answer is the influence of RFK Jr., who is carrying water for his friends in the plaintiffs bar. A who’s-who of lawsuit shops are pushing the Tylenol-autism link in federal court. The litigants include the Lanier Law Firm, Beasley Allen, Cooper Law Partners, and Keller Postman. Most have been allies of Mr. Kennedy in other mass torts, such as against the weed-killer Roundup and Merck’s HPV vaccine.”

Having established that link, they wrote, “The lawyers are hoping the government will breathe new life into them—conveniently timed for their appeal at the Second Circuit Court of Appeals this fall. Lead attorney Ashley Keller told Reuters last week that he planned to alert the appellate court ‘if HHS follows through and does the right thing.’”

The Journal then suggested that Donald Trump, who participated in the press conference on Monday, may not realize what Kennedy is trying to pull off.

“Perhaps no one told Mr. Trump about this trial lawyer campaign, but the costs of his intervention aren’t benign. He’s raising public fear about a useful medicine in a way that could harm maternal and fetal health. Whatever happened to do no harm?” they wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Wall Street Journal Takes Aim at Kennedy’s Tylenol-Autism Crusade

The Wall Street Journal editorial board has unleashed a scathing critique of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., accusing him of launching a misguided war against Tylenol and its purported links to autism. This narrative, steeped in controversy and skepticism, raises critical questions about the intersection of science, politics, and vested interests. Let’s dissect the key allegations and the murky waters in which they float.

Kennedy’s Suspect Science and Questionable Allies

First and foremost, the editorial lambasts Kennedy for his alleged anti-science crusade, suggesting that his actions are less about public health and more about serving the interests of his “pals” in the legal industry. Kennedy, lacking medical credentials, is depicted as a figure who’s not only veering his department off course but also engaging in potentially criminal behavior.

The mention of Kennedy’s replacement of experts with cronies paints a damning picture of a department in disarray, prioritizing loyalty over expertise. It’s a classic tale of power misused, a narrative that’s all too familiar in the annals of political history. But here, the implications are dire, touching on the delicate threads of public health and safety.

The Litigation Connection: A Convenient Partnership?

The editorial does not stop at questioning Kennedy’s scientific rationale; it delves into the murky waters of litigation politics, linking Kennedy to a network of law firms eager to exploit the Tylenol-autism narrative. Firms like the Lanier Law Firm and Beasley Allen are spotlighted as not just allies in litigation but as benefactors of Kennedy’s contentious policy maneuvers. This connection, if true, reeks of a conflict of interest, transforming a public health issue into a chess piece in the legal and political power games.

Trump’s Unwitting Role in the Drama

Adding a layer of complexity to this saga is former President Donald Trump, whose involvement in a press conference has been interpreted as either naivety or complicity. The editorial suggests Trump may be unaware of the potential consequences of his support, namely the public fear and potential health risks stemming from a campaign against a widely used medication. This portrayal of Trump as possibly an unwitting pawn in Kennedy’s game adds a layer of political intrigue to the narrative.

The Real Victims: Public Health and Trust

Amid the political maneuvering and legal strategizing, the editorial underscores a critical casualty: public trust and health. By casting doubt on the safety of Tylenol, a staple in medicine cabinets worldwide, Kennedy’s campaign—if as misguided as the Wall Street Journal claims—threatens to undermine both maternal and fetal health and erode public confidence in health authorities.

Conclusion: A Call for Clarity and Integrity

The Wall Street Journal‘s critique of Kennedy serves as a potent reminder of the intricate dance between science, politics, and the law. While the pursuit of truth in public health is noble, the editorial argues that Kennedy’s approach is anything but. It’s a tale of potential corruption, misplaced priorities, and the dangerous politicization of health science.

In the end, the call is for rigorous science, transparent motives, and a return to a health policy grounded in the best interests of the public, not the agendas of political or legal allies. As this saga unfolds, one can only hope that clarity and integrity will prevail over the shadowy influences of power and profit.
