'They always fold': Mike Johnson insider taunts hardliners after Trump 'let them have it'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of the Bully: Trump's Influence in the House

Former President Donald Trump and Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (Image via Mike Johnson/X)

In a post-mortem on how House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) negotiated and cajoled his caucus into passing what Donald Trump has called his "big beautiful bill," details are coming out from Politico on how he used Donald Trump to seal the deal with his special brand of bullying.

According to a report from Politico's Rachel Bade, Johnson spent months hammering out details and left some of the tougher calls until the end, which aggravated some House Republicans, where push came to shove as Trump lowered the boom on the balkers, with one insider stating, "He let them have it.".

According to insiders who witnessed the GOP meeting with Trump, the president took delight in making personal attacks on recalcitrant lawmakers.

Calling them "grandstanders," Trump stunned one "bewildered" House member who admitted, "He insulted several people with a great intensity."

According to Bade, "Trump’s tongue lashings and Johnson’s hardball approach to muscling the bill through this week rattled some even long-time GOP lawmakers. It was clear there was no room for dissent," leading to one Republican lawmaker to claim, "I could never have imagined when I started in politics that we would have this kind of a scenario,” before lamenting, “But who else do we turn to, besides Johnson?”

With everything done and now to be negotiated with Senate Republicans, one Johnson associate praised the bullying tactics and bluntly claimed, "They always fold."

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Bully: Trump's Influence in the House

In a recent exposé courtesy of Politico's Rachel Bade, the inner workings of House Speaker Mike Johnson's (R-LA) strategy to pass what Donald Trump has whimsically dubbed his "big beautiful bill" have been laid bare, revealing a disturbing reliance on intimidation and personal attacks. This narrative isn't just a peek behind the curtain; it's a glaring spotlight on the dysfunction and moral bankruptcy at the heart of certain political machinations.

The Bully Pulpit Reimagined

Trump's role in this political theater wasn't just as a former president with lingering influence; he was the enforcer, the one who "lowered the boom" on those not in line. Calling lawmakers "grandstanders" and launching into intense personal attacks, Trump didn't just cross the line; he obliterated it. This is not the art of negotiation; it's the art of the bully, a strategy that relies on fear, intimidation, and personal degradation to achieve its ends.

The Complicit Silence

What's equally troubling is the reaction—or lack thereof—from those within the GOP. Descriptions of being "bewildered" or claims of never imagining "this kind of scenario" in politics do not absolve these lawmakers of their complicity. They stand as witnesses to the erosion of political discourse and the degradation of their own ranks, yet the silence is deafening. The lament of having no one else to turn to but Johnson, as one Republican lawmaker put it, is less a statement of fact and more an admission of moral defeat.

The Unsurprising Conclusion

And then, there's the chilling endorsement of these tactics by one of Johnson's associates: "They always fold." It's an acknowledgment that the strategy of bullying isn't just a one-off desperation move; it's a calculated, frequently employed tactic. This isn't leadership; it's coercion. The endgame isn't about achieving what's best for the American people; it's about winning at any cost, even if that cost is the integrity of the political process itself.

The Bigger Picture

This Politico report sheds light on a troubling trend within American politics, where the ends justify the means, and where bullying is not only tolerated but celebrated by some. This isn't just about one bill or one political victory. It's a symptom of a deeper systemic problem—a rot within the political culture that threatens the very foundations of democracy. When lawmakers are cowed into submission by threats and personal attacks, the legislative process is compromised, and the will of the people is sidelined.

The Conclusion We Must Draw

As observers, participants, and victims of this political environment, the question becomes: How long will this be tolerated? The normalization of such tactics represents a dangerous slide away from reasoned debate and principled leadership towards an era of political strong-arming and moral bankruptcy. This isn't just about Trump, Johnson, or any single individual; it's about what kind of politics we are willing to accept. If this is the new normal, then democracy itself is under threat.

The revelations from Politico are a call to action: It's time to demand more from our leaders, to reject the politics of bullying and intimidation, and to reclaim the principles of democracy that have been so carelessly trampled upon. The cost of inaction is too great, and the price—our very democracy—is too precious to be lost to the whims of bullies.
