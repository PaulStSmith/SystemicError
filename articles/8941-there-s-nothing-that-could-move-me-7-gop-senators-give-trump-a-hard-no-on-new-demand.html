'There's nothing that could move me': 7 GOP senators give Trump a 'hard no' on new demand

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20254 min read

President Donald Trump’s initial response to Republicans’ lopsided losses in multiple elections on Tuesday night was to call on Republicans to end a longstanding practice in the U.S. Senate in order to ram through his policies with minimal opposition. But multiple senators are already pouring cold water on the proposal.

In a series of Truth Social posts Tuesday night, Trump demanded that Senate Republicans “TERMINATE THE FILIBUSTER“ and make it possible for the GOP to pass restrictions on voting, ban mail-in ballots and impose a national voter ID law, among other policies. As long as the filibuster remains in place, those policies would need 60 votes in the Senate in order to clear the upper chamber of Congress (Republicans currently have just 53 seats).

However, Semafor correspondent Burgess Everett reported Wednesday that scrapping the filibuster isn’t likely anytime soon, according to multiple Republican senators. Sens. Susan Collins (R-Maine), Ted Cruz (R-Texas), John Neely Kennedy (R-La.), Lisa Murkowski (R-Alaska), Rand Paul (R-Ky.) and Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) all communicated to Everett that the idea was a non-starter.

“There’s nothing that could move me,” Tillis said.

Additionally, Bloomberg congressional reporter Steve Dennis tweeted that Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.), who was the top Senate Republican for more than a decade, stopped him in a Capitol hallway and said of abolishing the filibuster: “We’re not going to do that.”

There are likely more than just those seven who are opposed to eliminating the filibuster, according to Sen. Steve Daines (R-Mont.), who told Everett that several more of his colleagues are not fans of the idea.

“There’s 10 to 15, probably, hard no’s in the Senate,” Daines said. Let’s focus on getting the government back up here with 60 votes. I think we’re getting close.”

Sen. Tammy Baldwin (D-Wis.) told Semafor that Tuesday’s elections may prove to be a turning point in negotiations to end the ongoing federal government shutdown, which officially became the longest in U.S. history this week.

“There are some changes in circumstance with regard to the president weighing in, and we’re going to see how those shake out — saying ‘the shutdown needs to end’ … I think it will catch the attention of Republicans,” Baldwin said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Filibuster Folly: A Tale of Political Hubris

In an almost comical display of political desperation, President Donald Trump has called for the nuclear option in the wake of Republicans’ spectacular failure in recent elections: abolish the Senate filibuster. This move, aimed at bulldozing through a slew of controversial policies including restrictive voting laws, showcases not only a profound misunderstanding of democratic processes but also a dangerous penchant for authoritarianism.

The Unraveling of a Desperate Strategy

Trump’s demand, splattered across Truth Social, to “TERMINATE THE FILIBUSTER“ is less a coherent strategy and more a knee-jerk reaction to electoral loss. The irony is palpable - a leader of the free world, unable to secure a simple majority, seeks to dismantle the very checks and balances designed to temper the excesses of majority rule.

The Senate’s Cold Shoulder

The response from the Senate, however, has been a resounding rebuke. Key Republican Senators, including Susan Collins, Ted Cruz, and Lisa Murkowski, have outright rejected Trump’s proposal, rendering it dead on arrival. Even Mitch McConnell, a figure not known for his democratic scruples when it comes to court appointments, has flatly dismissed the idea. This collective cold shoulder not only underscores the proposal’s impracticality but also signals a rare moment of bipartisan agreement on preserving Senate norms.

The Filibuster: A Double-Edged Sword

The filibuster, while often criticized for enabling minority obstructionism, serves as a crucial bulwark against the tyranny of the majority. Trump’s cavalier attitude towards it betrays a fundamental misconception: that governance is about ramming through one’s agenda without compromise or consideration for the opposition. It’s a short-sighted view that ignores the cyclical nature of politics - today’s majority can easily become tomorrow’s minority.

A Diversion from Real Issues

Trump’s call to “end the shutdown” and focus on securing a governing majority of 60 votes speaks volumes about the current state of the Republican Party. It’s a tacit admission of defeat, an acknowledgment that their policies cannot command the broad support necessary to advance through regular order. More insidiously, it distracts from the urgent need to address the ongoing federal government shutdown, now the longest in U.S. history, according to Sen. Tammy Baldwin.

Conclusion: A Misguided Gambit

In the final analysis, Trump’s proposal to abolish the filibuster is nothing more than a misguided gambit, borne out of frustration and political expediency. It lays bare a willingness to sacrifice foundational democratic principles on the altar of short-term gains. Fortunately, the Senate’s response reaffirms a commitment to those very principles, providing a glimmer of hope in an otherwise bleak political landscape.

In these tumultuous times, it’s imperative that we remain vigilant against such assaults on our democratic institutions. The filibuster, for all its flaws, remains a testament to the enduring value of compromise and bipartisanship. Let us not be seduced by the siren song of authoritarianism, for the price of democracy is eternal vigilance.
