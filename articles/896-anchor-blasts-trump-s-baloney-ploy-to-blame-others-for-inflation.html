Anchor blasts Trump's 'baloney' ploy to blame others for inflation

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Inflation as Cover, Corruption as Business

Who Actually Holds Power

The relevant power in this story is not the shopper in Georgia staring at grocery prices. It is the administration that can shape policy, deploy federal agencies, and then instruct its officials to explain away the consequences. According to the reporting, Trump’s team is trying to pin rising prices on Biden even as the political reality is obvious enough that “90 percent” of Americans notice it in gas and food.

That is the basic structure here: those with authority create or inherit the conditions, then use the public microphone to redirect blame downward and backward.

The Blame Transfer Machine

Brooke Rollins’s exchange with Raphael Warnock is the cleanest example of the trick. Warnock asked about food aid for working families facing higher grocery costs. Rollins answered with a sterile talk-tracking detour about averages and the last administration, then landed on the same exhausted line: blame Biden.

That is not confusion. It is discipline. The point is not to explain prices. The point is to delay responsibility until voters are too tired, too busy, or too hungry to keep asking why the answer to a real hardship is always a slogan. When a cabinet official answers a grocery question with partisan residue, she is not informing the public. She is protecting the people who made the public angrier in the first place.

The Language of Non-Responsibility

Nicole Wallace’s critique gets at the core failure: Trump’s people are pretending that inflation is an accident with no owner. It is a familiar political laundering process. First, create distance between decision and consequence. Then speak as if markets, history, and the previous administration all act on their own.

That framing is convenient for power because it erases agency. Prices rise as if by weather. Families suffer as if by fate. Officials become narrators instead of actors. The result is a politics built on evasion, where the most responsible people in the room speak in the least responsible language.

The Real Beneficiaries

The source makes clear that not everyone is losing. Trump and his family, the reporting says, are making money, and his children are allegedly using the federal government as a “honeypot” through defense and resource-related interests. That matters because it destroys the pretense that this is simply bad governance or incompetent messaging.

If the people defending the policy are also profiting from the state, then the lie about Biden is not merely defensive. It is part of a larger extraction system: public pain on one side, private gain on the other. That is why corruption talk lands so hard here. It is not abstract ethics. It is the visible mismatch between who pays and who cashes in.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

This is the older authoritarian habit in modern, polished clothing: deny the harm, blame a weaker predecessor, and keep the grift moving. The officials in this story are not trying to solve the price problem so much as manage the political fallout long enough for the damage to become background noise.

That is the system error. Power causes pain, then sends out spokespeople to narrate the pain as someone else’s fault. The public gets the bill, the officials get the cover, and the people at the top keep treating the state like a revenue stream.
