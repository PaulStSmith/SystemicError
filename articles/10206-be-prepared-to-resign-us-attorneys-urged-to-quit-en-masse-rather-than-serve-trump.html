'Be prepared to resign': US attorneys urged to quit en masse rather than serve Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analysis: Trump's Alarming Call for Prosecutorial Weaponization

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he boards Air Force One to depart from London Stansted Airport, in Stansted near London, Britain September 18, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump revealed his intentions to target three specific political foes with prosecution, whether or not charges are justified, and a former federal law enforcement official called on current officials to resist his corrupt orders.

The president posted a message over the weekend – perhaps unintentionally – to Attorney General Pam Bondi directing her to pursue criminal cases against FBI Director James Comey, Senator Adam Schiff and New York Attorney General Letitia James, calling all three "guilty as hell" for indicting and impeaching him.

Former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade wrote a column for Bloomberg outlining the dangers.

"Ethical prosecutors are fond of saying that they act without fear or favor," McQuade wrote. "President Donald Trump wants them to act with both."

Trump's post also suggested that prosecutors who refuse his orders should be removed, and he attacked Erik Siebert, his own nominee for U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia, for failing to charge James with a crime. He removed Siebert and nominated White House aide Lindsay Halligan, previously one of his own personal lawyers, as a replacement.

"Stacking the ranks of US Attorney's Offices with loyalists is a dangerous development," McQuade wrote. "Since the Watergate era, the DOJ has abided by norms and policies to protect its independence from political influence. The Justice Department’s Principles of Federal Prosecution prohibit prosecutors from considering partisan politics in charging decisions. In addition, communication between the Justice Department and the White House is severely limited to avoid even the appearance of impropriety."

"During the administration of George W. Bush, the removal of nine U.S. Attorneys even led to the 2006 resignation of Attorney General Alberto Gonzales for undermining public confidence in the integrity of the Department of Justice," she added. "Now, Trump is blasting through those norms."

Politically sensitive cases come through every U.S. Attorney's Office on occasion, but McQuade said what Trump is threatening to do violates ethical guidelines and the rule of law, and she said prosecutors must resist his pressure.

"In this new era of pressure from the president, U.S. Attorneys around the country must ask themselves whether they are willing to become just another instrument of political power," McQuade wrote. "The rule of law commands that each person be treated equally in our legal system, regardless of rank, station, or popularity. Prosecutors would upend the rule of law if they were to make charging decisions out of fear of a president’s retaliation or to curry favor to keep their jobs."

"Each must be prepared to resign rather than baselessly punishing the president’s enemies and simultaneously destroying the integrity of our legal system," she added. "Anyone who fails to do so is unworthy of the office they hold. And a president who demands loyalty over integrity is unworthy of our nation’s highest office."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Trump's Alarming Call for Prosecutorial Weaponization

In an audacious display of contempt for the rule of law, President Donald Trump has openly declared his intention to wield the prosecutorial power of the United States against his political adversaries, a move that not only stomps on ethical guidelines but also threatens the very foundation of American democracy. This revelation, as reported, underscores a chilling willingness to subvert justice for personal vendettas, demanding a critical examination.

Trump's Hit List: Comey, Schiff, and James
Trump’s directive to Attorney General Pam Bondi, aiming the prosecutorial gun at FBI Director James Comey, Senator Adam Schiff, and New York Attorney General Letitia James, is not just an abuse of power; it's a gross perversion of it. Labeling these individuals as "guilty as hell" without a shred of evidence presented in a court of law, Trump has made it clear that he sees the Department of Justice as his personal attack dog rather than a pillar of American law and order.

A Call for Resistance
Former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade’s commentary in Bloomberg serves as a clarion call for the legal community and officials within the Department of Justice to stand firm against such corrupt orders. McQuade’s insight into the erosion of prosecutorial independence under Trump’s administration is not just alarming—it's a red flag warning of an authoritarian shift where loyalty trumps integrity. Her comparison to the Watergate era and the subsequent safeguards installed post-Nixon underscores the gravity of Trump’s actions. The historical parallel serves as a stark reminder that the nation has been down a similar road before, with dire consequences for the integrity of the Department of Justice.

The Dangers of Loyalist Stacking
Trump’s replacement of Erik Siebert with Lindsay Halligan, a former personal lawyer of his, epitomizes the cronyism that threatens the impartiality of the U.S. Attorney’s Office. This move is not just about filling positions with loyalists; it's about ensuring that the scales of justice are tipped in favor of the president, regardless of the law. McQuade’s warning about the dangerous precedent this sets cannot be overstated. The Department of Justice's independence is not a mere tradition but a cornerstone of its credibility and authority.

The Ethical Quagmire
What Trump proposes—and expects—of his prosecutors is not just unethical; it's antithetical to the very principles upon which the American legal system is built. The notion that prosecutors should consider political allegiance in their charging decisions is an affront to the rule of law. McQuade’s call for prosecutors to resist such pressures, even at the cost of their positions, is a somber reminder of the moral and legal quagmire Trump’s presidency has engendered. The integrity of the U.S. legal system, and indeed, its very effectiveness, hinges on its independence from political machinations.

Conclusion: An Unworthy Commander-in-Chief
McQuade’s assertion that a president who demands loyalty over integrity is unworthy of office hits the nail on the head. The presidency, a position of immense power and responsibility, should be held to the highest ethical standards. Trump’s blatant disregard for these standards, his willingness to subvert justice for personal gains, and his attack on the independence of the Department of Justice should concern every American, regardless of political affiliation. It is not just the integrity of individual officials at stake here but the integrity of the American justice system itself. Trump’s actions, as outlined, are not merely unbecanoming of a president; they are unequivocally disqualifying.
