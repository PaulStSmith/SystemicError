'Did I hear that right?' CNN host stunned by latest Pete Hegseth revelation

A military memo sat unsigned for weeks early in defense secretary Pete Hegseth's tenure as the White House was unable to reach him or his top staffer, according to CNN's Natasha Bertrand.

The defense secretary has come under heavy criticism for conducting military planning over an unsecure chat app with family members and others present, and the upper echelons of Pentagon leadership has been plagued with infighting, but Bertrand revealed new details about his tumultuous tenure.

"There are a couple of examples of this that sources pointed out to us, really, that the White House has taken notice of," Bertrand told CNN News Central. "In particular, there was one incident early on in his tenure when the White House was scrambling to get him to sign a memo related to the 'Golden Dome' missile defense system that president Trump has has been pushing here at the Pentagon."

"That apparently sat unsigned on Pete Hegseth's desk for about three weeks because the White House simply could not get ahold of him and his chief of staff to push that," Bertrand added. "There was also another instance that really underscores how secretary Hegseth has a very small inner circle, does not trust a lot of people, and is very, very paranoid and concerned about leaks."

The defense secretary has also drawn criticism for inviting his wife Jennifer Hegseth to high-level meetings with foreign counterparts, and Bertrand said he has raised eyebrows with invitations to other individuals whose presence was questioned by Pentagon officials.

"He earlier last month, he actually went directly to the head of the office of special access programs here at the Pentagon," Bertrand said. "Instead of going through a normal policy process that included lawyers, for example, to ask whether Elon Musk could be read in on over two dozen special access programs, which are highly, highly classified programs here at the Pentagon related to China. Ultimately, when those [Department of Defense] lawyers were looped in, they said, look, this is probably not a good idea, but sources told us that maybe that would have actually panned out if secretary Hegseth had included more people and gone through a regular process."

Host Sara Sidner was stunned by the memo revelations and asked Bertrand whether she had heard her report correctly.

"I just want to go back to what you said about Pete Hegseth, not being able to get a hold of him," Sidner said. "The president could not get ahold of Pete Hegseth at the White House, could not get a hold of him to sign documents. Did I hear that right?"

Bertrand assured her that was accurate, saying that Hegseth and his recently reassigned chief of staff Joe Kaspar, now a special government employee handling "special projects," could not be reached to sign the memo.

"They were having trouble getting, they were having trouble getting ahold, in particular, of his chief of staff, his now former chief of staff, we should say, according to multiple sources," Bertrand said, "They were trying to get him to sign this memo, and for some reason, it just sat unsigned on his desk for about three weeks. Ultimately, he did sign it."

Our Analysis:

The Hegseth Debacle: A Tale of Incompetence and Paranoia

In a tale that reads like a script out of a poorly directed political thriller, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s tenure has quickly devolved into a series of missteps and questionable decisions that would be laughable if they weren't so concerning. Natasha Bertrand's reporting for CNN sheds light on a series of events that paint a picture of a Pentagon in disarray under Hegseth’s watch.

Early Signs of Chaos

Right from the get-go, Hegseth's tenure was marred by an inability to perform one of the most basic functions of his office: signing memos. A crucial document related to the 'Golden Dome' missile defense system, a priority for then-President Trump and a significant piece of national security infrastructure, languished on Hegseth's desk for three weeks. The reason? The White House couldn't get a hold of him or his chief of staff. This isn't just an embarrassing clerical error; it's a glaring red flag about the operational inefficiency at the heart of our defense apparatus.

A Circle So Small It's Suffocating

Bertrand's reporting further reveals Hegseth's crippling paranoia and distrust, traits that have evidently led him to construct a minuscule inner circle. This isn't just an administrative issue; it's a national security concern. When decisions about highly classified programs are being made without the input of seasoned lawyers and policy experts, one has to question the decision-making process at the highest levels of our defense infrastructure.

Questionable Guests and Unsecure Chats

But wait, there’s more. Hegseth’s judgment comes under further scrutiny with revelations of conducting military planning over unsecure chat apps, with family members and other unvetted individuals present. This cavalier attitude towards operational security is jaw-dropping. Additionally, the invitation of his wife, Jennifer Hegseth, to high-level meetings with foreign counterparts, along with attempts to loop in Elon Musk into highly classified programs without due diligence, reads like a how-to manual on operational insecurity.

The Bottom Line

What we have here is not just a series of unfortunate events but a systematic failure of leadership at the Pentagon. The implications of such missteps and misjudgments are far-reaching, impacting not just the operational integrity of the Department of Defense but also the global perception of American military leadership.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's tenure, as outlined by Bertrand, is a case study in how not to run a critical arm of the government. It's a story of neglect, paranoia, and a disturbing disregard for the processes that safeguard our nation's security. In any other context, the events described would be unfathomable. Yet, here we are, left to ponder the cost of such incompetence and the price of paranoia at the helm of the Pentagon.

In the end, one must ask: How many more memos will sit unsigned, and how many more questionable decisions will be made before the chaos at the Pentagon is addressed? The security of the nation and the integrity of its defense mechanisms are at stake.
