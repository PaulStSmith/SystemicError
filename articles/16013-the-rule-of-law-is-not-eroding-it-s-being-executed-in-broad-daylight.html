The American legal system was never perfect. But what we’re witnessing now isn’t a slow unraveling. It’s a public execution of the rule of law — carried out not in secret, not in shadows, but on live television with cheering crowds, crooked lawyers, and a leader who thinks “accountability” is a liberal conspiracy.

Lawrence O’Donnell’s breakdown of Trump’s latest authoritarian posturing is more than commentary. It’s a warning shot fired directly at the heart of the republic.

The Deportation of Abrego Garcia — and the Death of Due Process

Imagine being an American citizen — born here, raised here, with every constitutional right afforded by birthright — and being forcibly deported by your own government to a foreign country.

Not because of a crime. Not because of suspicion. But because the government made a mistake — and then insisted it had the right not to correct it.

That’s what happened to Abrego Garcia. Deported. Imprisoned in El Salvador. Forgotten.

And when the courts intervened to say, “This is wrong. He is a citizen. Bring him home,” Trump’s legal team fought it. Their argument? The President can make a mistake — even one that destroys a life — and the courts should stay out of it.

This isn’t policy. It’s dehumanization weaponized by bureaucracy.

And if that sounds like something out of Pinochet’s Chile or a gulag manual from Stalinist Russia, you’re not wrong. It is a direct violation of due process, of habeas corpus, and of any claim America has to moral authority.

The Harvard Power Grab — Authoritarianism in Academic Robes

Trump’s vendetta against Harvard goes beyond political theater. His team literally asked a federal court to grant him authority to control hiring and firing at Harvard. Professors. Staff. Cafeteria workers. Everyone.

Why? Because Harvard criticized him. Because it refused to bow.

This isn’t about policy disagreements or ideological divides. This is raw, fascist impulse — the belief that if an institution resists you, you should have the power to destroy it. To choke off funding. To erase autonomy. To punish.

When he says he wants to “defund” Harvard-affiliated hospitals, what he means is: make the sick and the poor suffer to get back at a university he cannot control.

This is what authoritarianism looks like in its larval form — before the boots hit the pavement and the libraries burn. And if you think it ends with Harvard, you’re missing the point. This is a dry run. Next comes the ACLU. The press. The courts themselves.

This Is Not Just Lawlessness — It’s Constitutional Dementia

Let’s not mince words: this isn’t incompetence. This isn’t “Trump being Trump.” This is a systematic attempt to destroy checks and balances so that the only rule left is his.

His lawyers are now arguing that his actions should not be reviewed, reversed, or restrained — even when they violate the Constitution. That his mistakes must stand. That he alone defines justice.

That is the end of the republic.

We Are All Abrego Garcia. We Are All Harvard.

Because when a government claims the right to be wrong — permanently, fatally, and without recourse — no one is safe.

When the President can deport a citizen and not bring them back.
When the President can demand control over a university because of a bruised ego.
When “mistake” becomes the new word for state-sanctioned abuse.

Then we’re no longer living in a democracy.

We are all Abrego Garcia. We are all Harvard.
Because when the rule of law dies, it does not die alone.

It takes all of us with it.
