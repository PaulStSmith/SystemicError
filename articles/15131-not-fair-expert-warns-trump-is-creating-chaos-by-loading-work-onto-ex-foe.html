However, many experts are questioning the decision to give Rubio, a former Florida Senator, four different roles within the administration. Those four roles are Secretary of State, National Security Advisor, U.S. Archivist, and Head of USAID.

" Marco Rubio is very talented, but no one can do that," Joel Rubin, a former senior State Department official, told the outlet.

Rubin served under both Democratic and Republican administrations. He called the workload "not fair to the individual, not fair to the taxpayer, and not fair to President Trump, who needs strong, independent advice from a national security advisor."

Mark Feierstein, a former senior official at USAID, told Fox, "I think it's not, ‘Can Marco Rubio do four jobs?’ It’s, ‘Is he allowed to do his one job – which is Secretary of State?’"

He added, “Foreign counterparts may see him as pleasant in meetings, but they don't necessarily believe he can deliver."

Feierstein pointed out advisors like Stephen Miller, and special envoy Steve Witkoff, who would typically report to Rubio. Instead, they bypass the Secretary of State entirely and go directly to Trump. "You've got loads of people who report directly to Trump or others. So now it's just chaos," he said.

“The national security advisor is traditionally the president’s closest aide on matters of war, peace and global crisis – physically located near the Oval Office and ready to brief the president at any moment,” Fox News' Morgan Phillips wrote. “That proximity is hard to maintain when the same person is flying overseas for diplomatic missions.”

Henry Kissinger was the last person to serve as both Secretary of State and National Security Advisor from 1975 to 1977.

Our Analysis:

The Absurdity of Marco Rubio's Quadruple Role: A Systemic Failure

In a display of political theater so absurd it borders on farce, Marco Rubio has been appointed to not one, not two, but four high-profile positions within the Trump administration, as reported by Fox News. This decision to place Rubio, a former Florida Senator, as Secretary of State, National Security Advisor, U.S. Archivist, and Head of USAID stretches the boundaries of credulity and practicality so thin, they're practically transparent.

Rubio's Jack-of-All-Trades Appointment: A Recipe for Disaster

Let's dissect the insanity piece by piece, shall we? Joel Rubin, a seasoned veteran of the State Department, succinctly encapsulates the predicament by stating that this arrangement is "not fair to the individual, not fair to the taxpayer, and not fair to President Trump." It's a rare moment of clarity in what can only be described as a muddled mess of administrative judgment. Rubio, talented or not, is being set up for failure. The very notion that a single individual could effectively juggle the intricate and demanding responsibilities of these roles is laughable at best and dangerously naive at worst.

Mark Feierstein, a former USAID senior official, raises a point that cuts to the heart of the matter: Rubio's ability to perform any of his roles effectively is severely compromised. The implication here is clear - Rubio, stretched thin across multiple high-stakes positions, is unlikely to wield the influence or command the respect necessary to execute the duties of Secretary of State with any degree of efficacy. This isn't just about Rubio's capacity; it's a systemic failure that undermines the very foundation of effective governance.

Bypassing Rubio: A Symptom of Chaos

Feierstein's observation that advisors and envoys, who would typically report to the Secretary of State, are now bypassing Rubio to go directly to Trump, is indicative of a deeper malaise. This isn't just a logistical hiccup; it's a symptom of a chaotic, arguably dysfunctional administration where traditional lines of communication and command are not just blurred but obliterated.

Furthermore, the comparison to Henry Kissinger, who simultaneously held the roles of Secretary of State and National Security Advisor in the mid-70s, is a red herring. The political, social, and technological landscape of today is vastly different, and arguably more complex, than that of Kissinger's era. To suggest that Rubio, or indeed anyone, could replicate this dual role in today's world, let alone add two more significant roles to their plate, is not only misguided but recklessly optimistic.

The Bottom Line: A Systemic Error of Judgement

The decision to allocate four critical roles to Marco Rubio is emblematic of a broader systemic error within the Trump administration: a failure to recognize the limitations of individual capacity and a blatant disregard for the nuanced demands of each position. This isn't just about Rubio's qualifications or his ability to multitask; it's about an administration that seems to operate with a cavalier attitude towards governance, national security, and foreign policy.

In the end, this isn't just "not fair" to Rubio or the taxpayer—it's a disservice to the very principles of effective governance and leadership. As the dust settles, one can only hope that this decision is revisited and revised, for the sake of Rubio, the administration, and the country at large. The alternative—persisting with this untenable scenario—is too chaotic to contemplate.
