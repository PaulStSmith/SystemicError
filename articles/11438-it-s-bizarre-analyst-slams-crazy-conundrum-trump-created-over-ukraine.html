'It's bizarre!' Analyst slams 'crazy conundrum' Trump created over Ukraine

By Paulo Santos

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hand with Russian President Vladimir Putin, as they meet to negotiate for an end to the war in Ukraine, at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S., August 15, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

A lot of what President Donald Trump has said and done doesn't add up since he met with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Friday in Alaska, according to two analysts.

Following the summit, the Trump administration announced several provisions for Ukraine as a way to end the war with Russia. Those apparent understandings were quickly undercut by Putin's spokespeople, who denied that any agreement had been reached. Trump also left the meeting saying that an immediate ceasefire wasn't necessary to end the war, despite commenting beforehand that he would demand the fighting end.

Analysts Josh Rogin of The Washington Post and political commentator Bakari Sellers joined CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip" to discuss the summit. Monday.

Rogin pointed out that Trump's flip-flopping since the summit has put a lot of world leaders in an awkward position.

"He thinks Putin really wants peace, and he's wrong," Rogin said. "And he's the president of the United States, and that's a huge problem because all of these European leaders have to pretend he's not wrong when he's wrong. That's the crazy conundrum that we're in. That's how we got into this mess."

"It's bizarre, and really bad," Rogin added.

The analyst also noted that people like Steve Witkoff, who are working on the Ukraine deal, also don't appear to have a grasp of the situation they are in.

"The fact that we have a president who believes Putin's promises...an envoy who doesn't understand that the Russian parliament is a rubber-stamp parliament, that's bizarre!" Rogin noted.

Our Analysis:

Trump-Putin Summit: A Theatre of the Absurd

In what can only be described as a diplomatic fiasco, President Donald Trump's recent rendezvous with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Alaska has unfolded more like a tragicomedy than a chapter in international diplomacy. The aftermath of this meeting has left analysts and observers scratching their heads, as the narrative swiftly shifted from potential progress to palpable perplexity.

The Illusion of Agreement

The Trump administration's announcement of provisions for Ukraine, ostensibly aimed at ending the conflict with Russia, was a momentary flicker of hope that was quickly extinguished by Putin's spokespeople. They denied any agreement had been reached, a classic move in the Kremlin's playbook, yet somehow it still seems to catch Western leaders off-guard. Trump's assertion post-meeting that an immediate ceasefire wasn't necessary starkly contrasted his pre-summit promises of demanding an end to the fighting. This flip-flop is not merely disappointing; it's dangerously naive.

Analysts Weigh In

Josh Rogin of The Washington Post and political commentator Bakari Sellers provided a sobering analysis on CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip". Rogin's observation that Trump's inconsistency has placed world leaders in an awkward position is an understatement. It's one thing to be diplomatically outmaneuvered; it's another to be so publicly and transparently played. The assertion that Putin desires peace, while simultaneously undermining peace efforts, underscores a fundamental misread of the situation or, worse, a willful ignorance.

The Bizarre Cast of Characters

Rogin's critique extends to those working behind the scenes on the Ukraine deal, such as Steve Witkoff, highlighting a disturbing lack of understanding about the nature of Russian politics. Believing in Putin's promises or failing to grasp that the Russian parliament is essentially a rubber-stamp institution is not just bizarre; it's alarmingly incompetent.

Conclusion: A Disheartening Performance

What we're witnessing is not just a series of political missteps; it's a comprehensive failure of diplomatic strategy and understanding. The Trump-Putin summit has laid bare the vulnerabilities and naivete at the heart of current U.S. foreign policy toward Russia. It's a stark reminder that in the high-stakes game of international relations, being ill-prepared and overly credulous can have dire consequences.

The real tragedy here is not just the diplomatic ineptitude but the profound implications for Ukraine and the broader geopolitical landscape. As the dust settles on this latest episode, one can't help but wonder about the long-term costs of such blunders and the dangerous precedent they set. In the end, this isn't just "bizarre and really bad"; it's a systemic failure that demands urgent and thorough reevaluation.
