Trump's DOJ makes 'light on details' move to strip Comey of lawyer: report

In a late Sunday filing, described by Politico as “light on details,” federal prosecutors pursuing former FBI Director James Comey are attempting to boot his lead lawyer off the case.

The two DOJ attorneys, brought into the Eastern District of Virginia U.S. attorney’s office by newly appointed former insurance lawyer Lindsey Halligan to make the case that Comey is guilty of making false statements and obstructing a federal proceeding, want the defendant’s lawyer, Patrick Fitzgerald, to be barred for alleged actions they believe represent a conflict of interest.

The filing makes the claim that Fitzgerald was involved “in disclosures to the media shortly after President Donald Trump fired Comey as FBI director in 2017,” which they believe presents an “insurmountable” issue.

Politico reported that government prosecutors Tyler Lemons and Gabriel Diaz are asking U.S. District Judge Michael Nachmanoff to step in with great “urgency” to “approve a proposal for a ‘filter team’ of lawyers to sift through evidence in Comey’s criminal case that could clarify Fitzgerald’s role in the eight-year-old disclosures — without breaching Comey’s attorney-client privilege.”

The prosecutors wrote, “Based on publicly disclosed information, the defendant used current lead defense counsel to improperly disclose classified information. This fact raises a question of conflict and disqualification for current lead defense counsel.”

While not getting into details, the filing cites “a Justice Department Office of Inspector General report from 2019 that found Fitzgerald acted as a middleman when Comey sought to get information to the media about what he viewed as improper efforts by Trump to get him to pledge loyalty in the days before his firing.”

“The inspector general report found that some of the information Comey shared with his attorneys was classified and faulted him for sharing sensitive investigative information with outsiders and the media,” Politico reported. It “also found no evidence ‘that Comey or his attorneys released any of the classified information contained in any of the memos to members of the media.”

The report goes on to point out that during Trump’s first administration the DOJ “declined to prosecute Comey or anyone else over the handling or disclosure of Comey’s memos about his conversations with Trump.”

Our Analysis:

The Comey Conundrum: A Tangled Tale of Legal Strategy and Political Warfare

In a maneuver that reeks of desperation and legal gymnastics, federal prosecutors are attempting to disqualify James Comey’s lead lawyer, Patrick Fitzgerald, over alleged conflicts of interest. This latest development in the Comey saga, as reported by Politico, is a clear testament to the ongoing political witch hunts that have plagued our legal system since the former FBI Director found himself in the crosshairs of the Trump administration.

The Thinly Veiled Attempt to Dismiss Patrick Fitzgerald

The prosecutors, Tyler Lemons and Gabriel Diaz, both of whom were ushered into the Eastern District of Virginia U.S. attorney’s office by Lindsey Halligan, a former insurance lawyer with presumably as much experience in high-profile federal cases as I have piloting spacecraft, have presented an argument that is as flimsy as it is transparent. They claim that Fitzgerald’s involvement in disclosures to the media following Comey’s firing by President Trump in 2017 creates an “insurmountable” conflict of interest. The catch? The evidence they rely on is as “light on details” as their entire premise.

Let’s dissect the absurdity of their argument. They are asking U.S. District Judge Michael Nachmanoff to approve a ‘filter team’ to sift through evidence without breaching Comey’s attorney-client privilege. In simpler terms, they want to embark on a fishing expedition based on a vague claim of conflict of interest, hoping to find something—anything—that might stick.

The Real Conflict of Interest

The true conflict of interest here is not Fitzgerald’s alleged media disclosures. It’s the blatant attempt by the Department of Justice under Trump’s first administration to target Comey, a move that reeks of political retribution rather than the pursuit of justice. The 2019 Justice Department Office of Inspector General report, which the prosecutors so conveniently cite, found that while Comey did share information with his attorneys, there was no evidence that any classified information was released to the media. Moreover, the DOJ itself declined to prosecute Comey over the handling or disclosure of his memos about his conversations with Trump.

What we’re witnessing is not a genuine concern for the integrity of the legal process. It’s a carefully choreographed performance aimed at discrediting Comey and his legal team, a tactic pulled straight from the playbook of political persecution.

The Bottom Line

This latest episode in the Comey case is a stark reminder of the lengths to which certain factions within the U.S. government will go to settle political scores. The prosecutors’ motion to disqualify Fitzgerald is not grounded in a legitimate conflict of interest but in a transparent attempt to weaken Comey’s defense and, by extension, further a political agenda.

The call for a ‘filter team’ and the urgency stressed by Lemons and Diaz betray a sense of desperation—a Hail Mary pass in a game that has long ceased to be about the truth or justice. It’s about power, revenge, and the dangerous politicization of the U.S. legal system.

In the end, the case against Comey, particularly this latest attempt to oust Fitzgerald, is emblematic of the systemic errors that have come to define our era: an era where political vendettas are pursued with more vigor than the truth, and where legal proceedings are manipulated to serve the interests of those in power, rather than the principles of justice.
