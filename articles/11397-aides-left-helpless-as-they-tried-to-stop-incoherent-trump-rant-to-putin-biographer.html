That’s according to the president’s biographer, who spoke to the Daily Beast on Wednesday.

On the podcast "Inside Trump's Head," Michael Wolff said that Trump's version of Cold War history was so mangled that "it would appear that the U.S. and USSR are on the same side."

Friday’s chaotic meeting in Alaska began with Trump launching into what Wolff described as "a combination of flattery" mixed with "things that he's just pulled out of somewhere...observations, it's both inconsequential and incoherent."

When Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Special Envoy Steve Witkoff attempted to redirect the conversation with an actual agenda, Trump simply "talked over them," leaving the meeting rudderless after 20 minutes with "nothing clear about what anyone is doing there except that Putin is totally impassive."

Putin eventually delivered what Wolff characterized as a "history lesson," explaining "why [Russia] should conquer Ukraine." Not to be outdone, Trump launched into his own historical dissertation on the Cold War that bore little resemblance to reality, Wolff said.

He said that Trump's version of the long-running international crisis with the Soviets "didn't seem much like a war at all."

Trump aides’ attempts to stop him proved futile. "They sit there occasionally trying to interject, but you can't really interject because Trump just talks all the time," Wolff said.

He added that this worked "to Putin's advantage" since Trump showed no interest in crucial details about territory or trade-offs.

White House Communications Director Steven Cheung dismissed Wolff with the comment he usually gives the Beast, calling Wolff "a lying sack of s--t" with "Trump Derangement Syndrome."

Our Analysis:

A Chaotic Summit: Trump's Cold War Confusion

In an era where the line between political satire and reality increasingly blurs, former President Donald Trump's recent summit with Vladimir Putin reads like a script from a farcical play, albeit with dire implications. According to insights from Trump’s biographer, Michael Wolff, shared with the Daily Beast and on the podcast "Inside Trump's Head," the former president's grasp of Cold War history is not just lacking—it's alarmingly distorted.

A Summit Spiraling into Incoherence

The meeting in Alaska, intended for high-stakes diplomatic dialogue, quickly devolved into chaos. Trump's strategy, if one can call it that, combined flattery with a scattergun approach to historical facts. Wolff's portrayal of events paints a picture of a summit where the U.S.'s position was weakened from the onset—not by external adversaries but by its own leader's incomprehensible ramblings.

Trump's Alternative Cold War

Trump's interpretation of Cold War history, as recounted by Wolff, is so egregiously flawed that it suggests the U.S. and USSR were allies rather than adversaries. This isn’t just a minor faux pas; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of one of the 20th century's pivotal geopolitical tensions. When Trump's aides attempted to course-correct, they were steamrolled by his incessant monologuing, leaving the meeting devoid of direction or purpose.

The Aides' Sisyphean Task

The aides’ efforts to interject or steer the conversation were described as nearly impossible tasks. Trump’s dominance in the conversation not only stifled any attempt at productive dialogue but also played into Putin’s hands. The Russian leader, portrayed as "totally impassive," was given a "history lesson" by Trump that one can only imagine was received with internal amusement or disbelief.

Dismissal and Denial

The White House's response to Wolff's account, as articulated by Communications Director Steven Cheung, was predictably dismissive. Labeling Wolff a "lying sack of s--t" suffering from "Trump Derangement Syndrome" serves as a textbook example of the administration's approach to criticism: deflect, deny, and disparage. This response not only fails to address the substance of Wolff's account but also epitomizes the Trump administration's aversion to accountability.

Conclusion: A Summit of Missed Opportunities

The Alaska summit, as described by Wolff, symbolizes a missed opportunity of historic proportions. Instead of leveraging the meeting to advance U.S. interests or address global challenges, Trump's bewildering performance underscored a fundamental lack of preparedness. This episode reinforces the critical importance of understanding history, the nuances of international relations, and the art of diplomacy. For a leader to enter such a high-stakes environment without these tools is not just reckless—it's a systemic error that undermines the very fabric of U.S. foreign policy.

In this light, the summit wasn't just a diplomatic failure; it was a glaring exposé of the vulnerabilities within the highest echelons of American leadership. The repercussions of such missteps are not confined to the political arena but extend to the global stage, where perceptions of U.S. competency and stability are inevitably shaped by the actions and understanding of its leader.
