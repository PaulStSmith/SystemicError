'Rubbed me the wrong way': Dem blasts Hakeem Jeffries over 'Mission Accomplished' moment

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 7, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Alexander Willis, Raw Story / Hakeem Jeffries

The Democrats had a great day in the latest election, but Hakeem Jeffries is being called out for purportedly taking a page out of George W. Bush’s “Mission Accomplished” playbook.

Jeffries was put on the spot by a former lawmaker who is now a registered Democrat. Joe Walsh was once a Tea Party Republican congressman, but he abandoned his party with the coming of Trump, and recently he flagged a “much, much, much, much, much bigger” than Trump.

Now, Walsh is calling out Jeffries with profanity.

“The day after a HUGE win for Democrats all over the country, I’ve got just a quick bone to pick with Hakeem Jeffries,” Walsh wrote. “So late last night, after this romp happened, what did House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries say? The Democratic Party is not dead. Can we cool it with these premature obituaries of the Democratic Party? The Democratic Party is back. And I thought, ‘The Democratic Party is back’? Respectfully, Hakeem, what happened yesterday had nothing to do with the Democratic Party.”

He continued:

“Voters came out yesterday in America and repudiated Donald Trump. They voted for Mikie Sherrill and Abigail Spanberger and Zohran Mamdani. They voted for the Bucks County, Pennsylvania, school board. They voted for Pennsylvania’s three Supreme Court justices. They voted for two statewide candidates down in Georgia. I could go on and on and on... Americans yesterday who voted were so pissed off and so scared to death that they would have stripped butt-a-- naked and crawled across broken glass for miles to vote. That’s how pissed off and afraid we all were.”

He then added, “This was all because of the grassroots voters, the resistance. That’s who carried the day yesterday. It wasn’t because of the Democratic Party, Hakeem Jeffries. It wasn’t because of you, Hakeem Jeffries. It wasn’t because of Chuck Schumer. It wasn’t because of Ken Martin and the DNC. No. That kind of rubbed me the wrong way.”

“Look, I get it. Democrats had a good day. Thank God. But Hakeem Jeffries declaring that the party is back is a bit like George W. Bush and the infamous ‘mission accomplished’ sign,” according to Walsh, who then asked, “Why even say that? You won one Election Day here, Hakeem. Chill, baby. You’ve got a lot more to prove.”

In his conclusion, Walsh wrote, “You wanna show me the Democratic Party is back? Then you need to meet the f------ moment.”

Our Analysis:

A Premature Victory Lap? Analyzing Hakeem Jeffries’ Declaration and Joe Walsh’s Critique

In the aftermath of what many are heralding as a triumphant election day for the Democrats, Hakeem Jeffries, the House Minority Leader, has found himself at the center of controversy. His claim that “The Democratic Party is back” has ignited a firestorm, spearheaded by none other than Joe Walsh, a former Tea Party Republican turned Democrat. Walsh’s scathing critique of Jeffries’ premature victory declaration offers a raw glimpse into the internal and external tensions facing the Democratic Party.

The Misstep of Overconfidence

Jeffries’ statement, coming off the heels of significant electoral wins, is emblematic of a potentially perilous overconfidence. Comparing Jeffries’ remarks to George W. Bush’s infamous “Mission Accomplished” moment is not only apt but serves as a stark warning. The dangers of declaring victory too soon cannot be overstated, especially in the volatile arena of American politics where fortunes can change with the wind.

Joe Walsh’s Piercing Analysis

Walsh, with his unique perspective as a former Republican who defected from the party in the age of Trump, provides a blistering counterpoint to Jeffries. His assertion that the electoral successes were not the doing of the Democratic Party’s machinery but rather the result of a grassroots repudiation of Trump is compelling. By arguing that voters were driven by fear and anger to the extent that they would endure significant hardship to cast their ballots, Walsh underscores a crucial point: the victory was more about Trump than it was about a resurgent Democratic Party.

The Role of Grassroots Movements

One cannot ignore the significance of grassroots movements in the recent electoral outcomes. Walsh’s emphasis on “the resistance” as the true architects of the day’s victories is a reminder that political success often springs from the ground up, not the top down. This serves as a rebuke to Jeffries and, by extension, the Democratic leadership, suggesting a disconnect between the party’s hierarchy and its base.

A Call to Action, Not Celebration

Walsh’s conclusion is not just a critique but a clarion call for the Democratic Party. “Meet the f------ moment,” he implores, signaling that the work is far from over. This is perhaps the most salient point of all. In the face of ongoing challenges, including those within their own ranks, the Democratic Party cannot afford complacency. The road ahead demands action, strategy, and an unwavering commitment to the principles that ostensibly underpin the party.

In Conclusion: A Lesson in Humility and Purpose

Jeffries’ premature declaration of a Democratic resurgence is a misstep, one that illustrates the perils of overconfidence in the unpredictable chessboard of American politics. Joe Walsh’s critique, though harsh, serves as a necessary corrective and a reminder of the true engines of political change: the grassroots voters. As the Democratic Party basks in the glow of recent victories, it must heed Walsh’s advice to truly “meet the moment” with humility, focus, and a renewed dedication to the values that it claims to champion. Only then can it prove that it is truly “back” and capable of leading in these tumultuous times.
