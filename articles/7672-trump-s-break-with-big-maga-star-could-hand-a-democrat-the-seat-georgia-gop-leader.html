Trump's break with big MAGA star could 'hand a Democrat the seat': Georgia GOP leader

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 15, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Supporters of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. president Donald Trump raise MAGA hats, on the day Trump returns for a rally at the site of the July assassination attempt against him, in Butler, Pennsylvania, U.S., October 5, 2024. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

The chair of a major Republican Party committee in Georgia is sounding the alarm that President Donald Trump’s messy break with Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) may end up handing her seat to a Democratic candidate, The Wall Street Journal reported Sunday.

Once one of the president’s most loyal and vocal supporters, Greene has grown increasingly critical of Trump in recent months, having openly criticized his administration’s approach on health care, its reluctance to release files on Jeffrey Epstein, and it’s military and geopolitical support for Israel amid its siege on Gaza, which a number of human rights organizations have labeled a genocide.

Trump has since pulled his endorsement of Greene – vowing to endorse a GOP opponent – and called her a “fake politician” who “betrayed the entire Republican Party.”

This week, the chair of the GOP District Committee in Greene’s district, Jackie Harling, warned fellow Republicans that the spat could end up dealing their party a decisive electoral blow.

“She warned that Greene’s departure has created ‘a dangerous situation,’ with at least a dozen people announcing plans to succeed her in a special election scheduled for March,” writes reporter Cameron McWhirter in the Journal’s report Sunday.

“Too many Republican candidates, she cautioned, could dilute the vote and hand a Democrat the seat. She urged people to pray, and described the coming vote as ‘D-Day.’”

Harling’s comments were made at the Whitfield County Republican Party Christmas dinner last Tuesday, an event typically defined by party unity, but this year, was something of an awkward affair, with McWhirter describing the mood as being “akin to a family gathering after the parents had announced their divorce.”

McWhirter spoke to several Republicans who attended the dinner, many of whom continued to back Greene despite Trump’s relentless attacks on her.

“He’s changed,” said 21-year-old Matt Fowler, speaking to the Journal about Trump. “She’s stayed the same.”

Many at the dinner openly rebuked Trump’s condemnation of Greene, with Harling defending Greene’s reputation.

“Marjorie Taylor Greene represented us accurately,” Harling said, remarks that drew applause from the crowd according to the Journal. “She spoke on our behalf. She was a mama bear that was not perfect. Neither is any of us. But she left big shoes to fill.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Greene Rift: A Prelude to Republican Unraveling?

In what reads like a script from a political soap opera, the rift between President Donald Trump and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) is casting a long, ominous shadow over the Republican stronghold in Georgia. The drama, as detailed in a recent report by The Wall Street Journal, not only threatens the unity of the GOP but also raises the specter of a seat once considered safely Republican flipping to the Democrats. This saga, ripe with betrayal, ideological clashes, and a fear of electoral repercussions, serves as a microcosm of the broader tumult within the Republican Party.

Trump’s Abrupt Withdrawal of Support: A Calculated Betrayal?

Trump’s denunciation of Greene as a “fake politician” who “betrayed the entire Republican Party” marks a dramatic departure from their previous alliance. Greene, once among Trump’s most zealous supporters, has found herself on the receiving end of his ire following her criticisms of his administration’s policies and decisions. This public spat has not only spotlighted the fractious nature of Republican politics but also underscored the perils of dissent within its ranks.

The Potential Electoral Fallout: A Self-Inflicted Wound

The warning from the chair of the GOP District Committee in Greene’s district, Jackie Harling, about the “dangerous situation” created by this rift cannot be overstated. Harling’s analogy of the upcoming vote as ‘D-Day’ encapsulates the existential threat this internal squabble poses to Republican dominance in the district. The fear that a crowded field of Republican hopefuls in the special election could “dilute the vote” and hand victory to a Democrat is not just speculative; it’s a stark reminder of the practical implications of political infighting.

A Divided Base: Unity in Disarray

The scene at the Whitfield County Republican Party Christmas dinner, as described by McWhirter, is emblematic of a party at a crossroads. The division between those who continue to support Greene and those who side with Trump highlights a deeper ideological and strategic rift within the party. The fact that many at the dinner rebuked Trump’s condemnation of Greene, coupled with Harling’s defense of Greene’s representation of their values, suggests a base struggling to reconcile its loyalty to Trump with its local political convictions.

The Larger Implications: A Party in Peril?

This episode is more than just a local political skirmish; it’s a harbinger of the challenges facing the Republican Party as it grapples with its identity in the post-Trump era. The potential for this rift to result in electoral losses is real and reflects a broader trend of internal divisions hampering Republican unity and effectiveness. As the GOP navigates these turbulent waters, the question becomes whether it can overcome these divisions or if it will continue to be plagued by a cycle of self-destructive infighting.

In sum, the Trump-Greene saga is not just a fleeting news story; it’s a critical examination point for the Republican Party’s future. The outcome of this dispute and the special election in March could very well signal the direction the party is heading. Will it be a return to unity and strategic coherence, or will it mark a continued descent into fractiousness and electoral vulnerability? Only time will tell, but the stakes, both for the GOP and for American politics at large, could not be higher.
