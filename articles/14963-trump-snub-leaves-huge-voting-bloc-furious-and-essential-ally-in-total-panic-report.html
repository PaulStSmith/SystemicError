Trump snub leaves huge voting bloc furious — and essential ally in 'total panic': report

President Donald Trump is visiting three Middle Eastern countries this week, but one he’s left out has an extremely powerful voting bloc furious.

Trump and his entourage will make stops in Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates.

Israel is not on the list.

The New York Times reported it was not the first time Trump has been seen as sidelining Israel’s Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The perceived snub followed a truce the president announced with Yemen-based Houthi rebels which did not involve Israel. Just days later, Trump was said to be considering a nuclear deal with Saudi Arabia — again without making preconditions involving that nation’s relationship with Israel, the Times reported.

“Many Israelis are wondering whether Israel is the next U.S. ally to be left behind by a president they considered, just months ago, to be the most pro-Israel in history,” the Times reported.

“It’s total panic,” said ex-Netanyahu aide Shalom Lipner about the reaction to Trump's behavior.

Former Middle East envoy Dennis Ross told the newspaper there were worries that Israel’s concerns, “are not being taken into account, or if they are, they’re dismissed.

“What you’re seeing is that President Trump has an idea of what is in our interest, and that comes first. He defines the nature of our interests abroad not through a geopolitical or security context, but an economic, financial and trade frame. I think President Trump might have the view that ‘We give them $4 billion a year in military assistance. I do plenty to support the Israelis.’”

The shift in mood is dramatic, coming just months after Netanyahu called Trump’s election victory “history’s greatest comeback.”

But, the Times reported, “The mood began to shift even before Trump’s inauguration. Privately, some Netanyahu allies groused about Trump’s special envoy to the Middle East, Steve Witkoff, pressuring Netanyahu into a ceasefire agreement with Hamas and claimed that Witkoff was influenced by personal business ties with Qatar.

“In the Oval Office in April, Trump announced in front of Netanyahu and the assembled press that the U.S. would hold direct talks with Iran over its nuclear program. The Israeli prime minister, who has pushed U.S. leaders for more than a decade to use military force to dismantle Iran’s nuclear facilities, glanced away, appearing visibly surprised.

“One Trump adviser, who described Trump’s treatment of Netanyahu as “one notch above” his fractious White House meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky three months ago.”

Israeli political analyst Amit Segal told the Times, “There is more and more criticism, with people saying: We put all our eggs in one basket and now we are empty-handed.

“What will Israel do now? Call [Rep.] Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez? It’s a problem.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Middle Eastern Diplomatic Shuffle: Israel Left in the Lurch

In a move that can only be described as a geopolitical faux pas of significant magnitude, President Donald Trump's latest Middle Eastern tour itinerary reads like a who's who of regional powerhouses, with one glaring omission: Israel. The New York Times reports a palpable sense of betrayal among Israeli ranks, stemming from Trump's decision to sideline Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and, by extension, the state of Israel in a series of diplomatic ventures. This analysis peels back the layers of this diplomatic snub, revealing the broader implications for U.S.-Israel relations and the Middle East at large.

The Snub Heard Around the World

Trump's itinerary includes stops in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates, but conspicuously leaves out Israel, a move that has not gone unnoticed. This isn’t the first time Trump has been perceived as sidelining Netanyahu, but the timing now seems particularly egregious. It follows a truce with Yemen-based Houthi rebels and considerations for a nuclear deal with Saudi Arabia—both conducted without Israel's involvement or even a nod to its strategic interests in the region.

The Times aptly notes the growing disillusionment within Israel, quoting an ex-Netanyahu aide, Shalom Lipner, who summed up the mood as "total panic." This sentiment is echoed by former Middle East envoy Dennis Ross, who points to a worrying trend of Israel’s concerns being dismissed or outright ignored by the Trump administration.

Economic Interests Over Geopolitical Alliances

Trump's approach to foreign policy, as Ross suggests, prioritizes economic, financial, and trade considerations over traditional geopolitical or security alliances. This reorientation raises questions about the sustainability of U.S.-Israel relations, historically predicated on shared security interests. Trump's apparent belief that financial aid equates to unwavering support is a gross oversimplification of international diplomacy, particularly in a region as volatile as the Middle East.

Netanyahu's Diminishing Returns

The shift in the U.S.-Israel dynamic is stark, coming off the heels of Netanyahu's enthusiastic endorsement of Trump's presidency as "history’s greatest comeback." The deterioration began even before Trump's inauguration, with Netanyahu's allies privately expressing concerns over Trump's Middle East envoy, Steve Witkoff, and his pressure on Israel for a ceasefire agreement with Hamas. Witkoff's alleged personal business ties with Qatar only add a layer of intrigue and potential conflict of interest to the mix.

In a telling Oval Office encounter, Trump blindsided Netanyahu with the announcement of direct talks with Iran over its nuclear program, a move that visibly unsettled the Israeli Prime Minister. This incident, among others, underscores a pattern of disregard for Israel's strategic concerns and Netanyahu's counsel.

A Basket of Broken Eggs

Israeli political analyst Amit Segal’s critique encapsulates the growing sentiment within Israel: a realization that their steadfast support for Trump might have been misplaced. The analogy of putting all their eggs in one basket, only to find it empty-handed, speaks volumes of the current predicament facing Israel.

The underlying issue here is not just about diplomatic etiquette or the lack thereof; it's about the realignment of U.S. foreign policy priorities in the Middle East. Trump's actions signal a shift towards transactional diplomacy, where economic benefits trump (pun intended) longstanding strategic alliances. This approach not only alienates key allies like Israel but also introduces a degree of unpredictability and instability into an already volatile region.

The question that now looms large is: What will Israel do in response to this diplomatic cold shoulder? Turning to figures like Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, as Segal jests, might not be on the agenda, but reassessing its reliance on and approach to its relationship with the U.S. certainly should be.

In conclusion, Trump's Middle Eastern diplomatic maneuverings serve as a cautionary tale about the perils of prioritizing economic interests over strategic alliances. The repercussions of this snub will likely reverberate through U.S.-Israel relations for years to come, underscoring the need for a more balanced and considerate foreign policy approach.
