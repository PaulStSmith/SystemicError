'Keep dancing': Stephen Miller's 'sycophant' wife ridiculed for wild Fauci claims

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

The Cruelty Was Never the Point

A Small Scene With a Clear Hierarchy

The immediate story is simple: Katie Miller, a former Trump administration official and Stephen Miller’s wife, tried to cast Dr. Anthony Fauci as a manipulative enemy after his diaries showed he had been in regular contact with her during her pregnancy and COVID anxiety. She was mocked online for declaring journals a sign of narcissism while also admitting she spoke with her “enemy” nearly every day.

That is the surface. The real story is about power, access, and the habit of laundering dependence through grievance.

Who Actually Held Power

Fauci was not a random bystander. He was a public health official with institutional authority during the pandemic, and the diaries show he was doing what institutions are supposed to do for people under stress: taking calls, checking in, calming fear.

Katie Miller was not powerless either. She was inside the Trump world, married to one of its hardline architects, and still close enough to the center of political power to expect personal attention when it suited her. That is the part her post wants erased. She did not come to Fauci as an ordinary citizen seeking help from a distant bureaucracy. She came from the political class that spent years attacking him, then acted as if private contact had never happened.

The Claim Is the Cover

Her move is familiar: turn a documented relationship into a morality play about betrayal. In her telling, Fauci supposedly “weaponized” her pregnancy and child for his own gain. But the source material shows the opposite shape of events. Fauci wrote that he was calling her frequently, that she was anxious about getting very ill, and that he calmed her during an anxiety reaction.

That is not proof of sainthood, and it is not the point. The point is that Miller’s accusation tries to convert ordinary human care into sinister manipulation once it becomes politically useful. That is how right-wing grievance works: the same institutions and people they rely on in private become villains in public the moment attention needs to be redirected.

The Real Misdirection

The weak frame here is the one that treats the story as an internet dunk contest. The ridicule matters less than the underlying tactic. Miller’s tweet is not a confused outburst. It is a deliberate attempt to rewrite a record that is inconvenient for her side.

She and Stephen Miller have already declared Fauci someone who should be in prison. That is not a disagreement over policy. It is an authoritarian impulse: criminalize the public official, demonize the institution, and then pretend personal exceptions never existed. When their own family needed reassurance, they took it. When the political utility changed, they reached for persecution theater.

Why This Pattern Keeps Reappearing

This is the governing logic of reactionary politics in miniature. Publicly, it attacks expertise, medicine, journalism, and administration as corrupt. Privately, it depends on all of them when the harm gets close to home. The same people who sneer at journals, science, and public service are perfectly happy to use the state, its officials, and its care when they can extract comfort from it.

That is not hypocrisy by accident. It is the operating system.

The Larger Lesson

The political meaning of this story is not that Katie Miller got embarrassed online. It is that a faction built on cruelty cannot survive without constant rewrites of its own dependence. It wants the benefits of institutional power without the accountability, the care without the recognition, and the public record only when it flatters the tribe.

The scandal is not that Fauci wrote down what happened. The scandal is that the people who weaponize resentment are always the first to demand mercy for themselves.
