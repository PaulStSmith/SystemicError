'This is my time': Kristi Noem scolded as she stonewalls on withholding federal grants

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Grasping the Purse Strings: A Constitutional Clash

Lauren Underwood and Kristi Noem (House Appropriations Committee/screen grab)

Rep. Lauren Underwood (D-IL) grilled Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem about her agency's decision to withhold federal grant money without permission from Congress.

"Let's start with Article 1 [of the Constitution], which gives Congress and only Congress the power of the purse," Underwood told Noem at a House Appropriations Committee hearing on Tuesday. "But this administration is freezing, terminating, and even clawing back federal grants and awards that were already signed into law."

"So, Secretary, let me ask you, do you believe the administration has the authority to subvert appropriations law to freeze and terminate congressional funding?" the lawmaker asked.

"The administration and the Department of Homeland Security used our authorities that we have to evaluate every grant that was in front of us and made statutory requirements, but also to make sure that they were being spent appropriately," Noem replied, adding, "So, of course, the president is within his authority."

"Ma'am, this is my time. Thank you so much," Underwood interrupted. "What I'm asking is whether you believe that you have the authority to ignore appropriations law."

"We have evaluated grants to make sure if they're being spent appropriately, and work that is underway and was authorized has continued," Noem insisted.

"I'm talking broadly, ma'am, and Article 2 does not give the executive the authority to withhold funds, period, and multiple courts have weighed in to agree," Underwood said. "Multiple courts have ruled in agreement. Congress controls the power of the purse."

"That is a fact that's not a question," she added. "That is a fact, and so if the president disagrees with certain spending, Congress passed the Impoundment Act to create a legal process to seek rescissions, and we expect him to follow the law."

Our Analysis:

Grasping the Purse Strings: A Constitutional Clash

In a display of political theater that underscores the enduring tug-of-war between the branches of the United States government, Rep. Lauren Underwood (D-IL) took the stage to confront Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem. The battleground was a House Appropriations Committee hearing, and the weapon of choice? The Constitution itself.

The Heart of the Matter: Article 1, Section 9, Clause 7 of the U.S. Constitution, also known as the power of the purse, explicitly grants Congress—and only Congress—the authority to allocate federal funding. Underwood's opening salvo, invoking this clause, was not just a recitation of constitutional law but a pointed accusation against the current administration's handling of federal grants and awards.

Secretary Noem's Defense: A Study in Evasion or Ignorance? Noem's response to Underwood's probing question about whether the administration believes it has the authority to “subvert appropriations law” was telling in its lack of specificity. Instead of directly addressing the legality of the administration’s actions, Noem opted for a broad statement about evaluating grants and ensuring appropriate expenditure. This response, while perhaps intended to reassure, only highlights a troubling ambiguity in the administration's understanding—or respect—of constitutional mandates.

Underwood's Counterattack: Precision and Clarity Underwood's interruption to reclaim her time and her subsequent clarification request cut to the heart of the issue: does the executive branch believe it has the authority to ignore appropriations law? Noem's reiterated stance on grant evaluation did little to answer this question, instead offering a vague assurance that authorized work has continued.

The Constitutional Checkmate Underwood's closing remarks underscored a fundamental constitutional principle: the executive branch does not have the authority to withhold funds without Congressional approval. Citing multiple court rulings that align with this interpretation, Underwood not only fortified her argument with legal precedents but also highlighted the Impoundment Act as the legally sanctioned avenue for the executive to contest Congressional appropriations.

Analysis: A Clear Violation or a Gray Area? The crux of this confrontation lies not just in the specific incident of withheld federal grant money but in the broader implications for executive overreach and the erosion of Congressional authority. Noem's responses, characterized by a notable absence of direct engagement with the legal questions raised by Underwood, reflect a concerning trend of administrative ambiguity or, worse, disregard for constitutional limits.

The Bigger Picture: Constitutional Integrity at Stake This exchange is emblematic of a larger struggle between the legislative and executive branches over control of federal finances. The administration's actions, as described by Underwood, represent a direct challenge to the constitutional framework designed to prevent the concentration of power. The Impoundment Act, referenced by Underwood, was enacted precisely to curtail the executive's ability to undermine Congressional appropriations decisions.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability The dialogue between Rep. Underwood and Secretary Noem is more than a mere political skirmish; it is a critical examination of the boundaries of executive power in the realm of fiscal governance. The administration's alleged actions, as outlined by Underwood, not only raise serious constitutional questions but also demand a reevaluation of the mechanisms in place to ensure that no branch of government can unilaterally override the checks and balances that underpin American democracy.

In the final analysis, this incident serves as a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance, transparency, and accountability in the stewardship of public funds. The integrity of the United States' constitutional system depends on the adherence to its principles by all branches of government, a responsibility that should never be taken lightly.
