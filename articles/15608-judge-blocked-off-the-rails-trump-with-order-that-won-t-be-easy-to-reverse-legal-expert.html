Judge blocked 'off-the-rails' Trump with order that won't be easy to reverse: legal expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A judge threw up a serious hurdle to president Donald Trump's executive order that would substantially roll back voting rights.

U.S. District judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly paused the president's March 25 executive order, titled "Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections," instructing the independent Election Assistance Commission to change the national mail voter registration form to require applicants to prove their U.S. citizenship, and former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance wrote on her Substack page that the ruling would be difficult to overturn.

"District judges frequently write detailed opinions like this one when they want to make sure there is a solid basis for the courts of appeals to affirm their decisions and as little room as possible for them to reverse," Vance wrote. "This is a detailed, well justified explanation that will not be easy for an appellate court to dismiss, and it’s not one-sided; it denies some of the relief the plaintiffs requested on technical legal grounds."

"Judge Kollar-Kotelly starts with the basics, explaining what executive orders can and cannot be used for," Vance added. "A president, she writes, 'cannot make new law or devise new authority for himself — by executive order or otherwise. He may only wield those powers granted to him by Congress or by the Constitution.'"

Trump wanted the EAC to change its forms to require voters to prove their citizenship before registering to vote or updating their registration, but Kollar-Kotelly blocked that provision.

"The reason our elections, which are held in all 50 states and over 3,100 counties, as well as in some territories and for military members and citizens abroad, are not consigned to the control of the president is fairly obvious," Vance wrote. "The decentralization makes it more difficult for a president or his party to manipulate outcomes in national elections. Trump is nothing if not transparent in this regard."

The order would have required voters to present a passport, Real ID, military identification card or other form of government-issued ID proving citizenship, but Vance said millions of Americans don't have those documents readily available.

"So why do it?" Vance wrote. "The only rationale offered, voter fraud, is something that has been proven, over and over, not to be a problem, especially in federal elections. It’s the same tired excuse used to make it more difficult for 'certain' qualified voters to exercise their rights."

The judge pointed out that the Constitution authorizes Congress and the states, and not the president, to regulate federal elections, and Vance said her ruling was a substantial bulwark against Trump's attempts to consolidate power.

"A decision like this should be a foregone conclusion at the end of this case," Vance wrote. "No president should try to usurp the power to control elections, let alone be able to do so. The executive order and the lawsuits challenging it underscore just how off the rails Trump is. Every day, part of the spectacle of Trump is his assumption of the role of an autocrat at the expense of the American presidency. And the risk is that it all becomes somehow normalized."

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of Trump's Executive Order and its Judicial Rebuke

In an era where the sanctity of voting rights hangs by a thread, the recent ruling against President Donald Trump's executive order, which aimed to significantly curtail these rights, stands as a beacon of judicial prudence. U.S. District Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly's decision to halt Trump's March 25 executive order, ostensibly crafted to "Preserve and Protect the Integrity of American Elections," marks a crucial checkpoint against the executive overreach that has characterized his administration. This order, which sought to amend the national mail voter registration form to include proof of U.S. citizenship, has been seen by many as an unabashed attempt to disenfranchise countless American voters under the guise of combating voter fraud—a problem that is largely nonexistent.

The Illusion of Voter Fraud as a Justification

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance aptly highlights the baseless foundation of Trump's order. The narrative of widespread voter fraud, often peddled with zeal by Trump and his acolytes, has been debunked repeatedly by numerous studies and reports. The insistence on using this phantom menace to justify stringent voter ID requirements reveals a thinly-veiled attempt at voter suppression. Vance's analysis underscores a critical point: the executive order was not an earnest attempt to secure elections but a calculated move to restrict voting access to a select demographic.

Judicial Wisdom as a Bulwark Against Autocratic Tendencies

Judge Kollar-Kotelly's ruling is a masterclass in judicial restraint and constitutional fidelity. By emphasizing the limited scope of executive powers—as delineated by both Congress and the Constitution—she not only rebuffed Trump's overreach but also illuminated the inherent checks and balances designed to thwart such autocratic impulses. This decision reaffirms the decentralized nature of U.S. elections, a feature that serves as a safeguard against manipulation by any single entity, including the President.

The Bigger Picture: Normalization of Autocratic Behavior

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of this entire debacle is the creeping normalization of autocratic behavior. Vance's commentary resonates with a sobering reminder: the spectacle of Trump assuming the role of an autocrat threatens to erode the democratic norms we hold dear. This case is not merely about an executive order; it's a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend towards authoritarianism under Trump's presidency. Every attempt to usurp control over the electoral process, every effort to sideline democratic principles, serves to weaken the fabric of American democracy.

Conclusion: A Victory for Democracy, But the War Continues

In sum, the ruling against Trump's executive order represents a significant victory for voting rights and a reaffirmation of democratic governance. However, it would be naive to view this as the end of the struggle. As long as figures like Trump continue to challenge the foundational principles of democracy, the battle to preserve these ideals must persist. Judge Kollar-Kotelly's decision is a critical step in the right direction, but it also serves as a stark reminder of the vigilance required to protect our democracy from those who seek to undermine it for their own gain.
