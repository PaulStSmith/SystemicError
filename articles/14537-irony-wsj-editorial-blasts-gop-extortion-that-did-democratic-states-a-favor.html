'Irony': WSJ editorial blasts GOP 'extortion' that did Democratic states a 'favor'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The conservative Wall Street Journal scorched House Republicans on Wednesday for their ongoing battle over raising the cap on the state and local tax deduction (SALT), which lets mostly wealthier households offset the cost of what they've paid in certain high-tax states.

Broadly, a number of swing-district Republicans in Democratic-controlled states, particularly New York, are demanding the SALT cap be much higher than GOP leadership's initial offer of increasing it from $10,000 to $30,000. The process is starting to frustrate even President Donald Trump, who told one of the most outspoken SALT rebels, Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY), to just "drop it."

But the editorial board, which has already panned the GOP's SALT battle in earlier analyses, has a key objection: it will let Democrats raise state taxes even higher.

"Few voters in other states will benefit because the standard deduction (which the House bill increases to $32,600 for couples) is bigger than their itemized deductions. Texans pay no income tax and on average $3,872 in property tax. Why should they subsidize Democratic spendthrifts in Albany?" wrote the board. "Don’t be surprised if Democrats who run high-tax states take advantage of Republican generosity by raising taxes even more. It will also relieve political pressure on Democrats to reduce taxes to prevent high earners from fleeing. Don’t expect Gov. Kathy Hochul to send her thanks to Messrs. LaLota and Lawler."

What makes the whole situation more ironic, the board noted, is that "Republicans are doing a favor for high-tax states that Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi refused when Democrats controlled Congress in 2021 and 2022. Recall how Democratic Reps. Josh Gottheimer, Tom Suozzi, and Mikie Sherrill insisted they wouldn’t vote for a spending bill without more SALT." Schumer ultimately called their bluff, passed the Inflation Reduction Act without changing SALT, and got their votes anyway.

And to cap it all off, the board noted, Schumer is deliberately provoking the divide now by attacking Republicans over insufficient SALT relief, when he himself did not deliver that as Senate leader.

"This underscores the folly of the Republican SALT caucus. Democrats will denounce them and the tax bill they rode in on in any case," the board concluded. "Senate Republicans can cut this SALT extortion down to size in their bill, and the country should hope they do."

Our Analysis:

The SALT Saga: A Tale of Political Irony and Misguided Generosity

The Wall Street Journal's recent excoriation of House Republicans over the state and local tax deduction (SALT) debacle not only highlights a glaring inconsistency in fiscal policy but also underscores a broader narrative of political missteps and ironies within the GOP. This analysis will dissect the key points of contention and the peculiar position in which the Republicans find themselves, as they paradoxically champion a cause that seems to undermine their own tax ethos and inadvertently bolster Democratic state policies.

The Battle Over SALT: At the heart of the controversy is the GOP's internal conflict regarding the cap on SALT deductions. The push by swing-district Republicans to significantly raise the cap from $10,000 to $30,000 reveals a fracture within the party, driven by the local interests of lawmakers in high-tax, Democratic-controlled states, notably New York. This rift has grown so pronounced that it even elicited a blunt admonition from former President Donald Trump, who advised Rep. Mike Lawler (R-NY) to simply "drop it." Yet, the insistence persists, highlighting a peculiar departure from the party's usual tax-cutting rhetoric.

A Gift to High-Tax States: The Wall Street Journal's editorial board, with its conservative leanings, articulates a fundamental objection to the GOP's stance on SALT — it essentially acts as a boon to Democratic states by allowing them to hike state taxes without repercussion. The argument posits that by increasing the SALT deduction cap, Republicans are inadvertently subsidizing the fiscal irresponsibility of states like New York, thereby relieving the pressure on Democratic leaders to implement tax reforms. This, in essence, would enable a cycle of tax increases and expanded state spending, further burdening taxpayers in states that practice fiscal restraint.

The Irony of Republican Generosity: What adds a layer of irony to this situation is the fact that Republicans are now championing a cause that Democrats, including prominent figures like Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi, had previously set aside. In a move that can only be described as political irony, the GOP is attempting to deliver on a policy that Democrats failed to prioritize when they had the legislative opportunity. This shift not only highlights a strategic miscalculation but also exposes the GOP to criticism and potential backlash from their base, who may view this as a betrayal of conservative tax principles.

Schumer's Provocation and the GOP's Quandary: Another facet of this convoluted situation is Schumer's strategic provocation, attacking Republicans for not providing sufficient SALT relief — a maneuver that reeks of political gamesmanship. This cunning move serves to exacerbate the divide within the GOP, while also deflecting attention from the Democrats' own failure to address SALT when they had the chance. It places Republicans in a precarious position, caught between appeasing their constituents in high-tax states and adhering to their broader tax policy principles.

Conclusion: A Call for Fiscal Consistency: The Wall Street Journal's critique serves as a stark reminder of the need for fiscal consistency and principle-based policymaking. The GOP's current predicament over SALT is a self-inflicted wound, born out of political expediency and a misguided attempt to court favor in swing districts. It's a vivid illustration of the complexities and contradictions that arise when short-term political calculations override long-standing policy convictions.

As the saga unfolds, one thing becomes clear: the Republican SALT caucus is navigating treacherous waters, risking not only the ire of fiscal conservatives but also the coherence of their party's tax policy stance. Whether this will translate into a significant political fallout remains to be seen, but one thing is certain — the country, and indeed the GOP itself, would be better served by a return to fiscal principles that prioritize restraint, consistency, and the long-term economic health of all states, not just the high-tax ones.
