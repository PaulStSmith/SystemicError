‘We will look like schmucks!’ GOP infighting erupts as senator revolts against leadership

U.S. Senator Ron Johnson (R-WI) speaks during a Senate Homeland Security and Government Affairs Committee hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 12, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Last week, Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) described the idea of “nuking the filibuster” – a procedural rule that allows senators to block a vote – “something that doesn’t have a future,” but on Saturday, Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI) revolted against the notion, arguing on behalf of eliminating the rule as a means to forward the Trump administration’s agenda.

“I understand why there’s a reluctance to end the filibuster because, as Republicans and conservatives, we realize that our ability in the minority to stop awful legislation put forward by Democrats and the socialists in their party... we would have been down a far more destructive path a lot earlier were it not for the filibuster,” Johnson said during an appearance Saturday on Fox News.

“The problem though is times have changed. Democrats have already shown their cards, they’ve already purged the two members that prevented Democrats from ending the filibuster when they were last in this position. We know they’ll nuke the filibuster next time they get into power, and, when they do it, they’ll pack the Supreme Court, they’ll turn D.C. and Puerto Rico into states, they’ll turn this nation into a one-party state!”

Trump has frequently urged Senate Republicans to kill the filibuster, warning last year that failing to do so would result in the GOP losing control of Congress in the 2026 midterm elections. The demand, however, has been met with skepticism from a number of Republican lawmakers, most of whom expressed fears that doing so would provide Democratic lawmakers with unchallenged power when, or if, they reclaim congressional majorities.

Johnson does not consider himself among that group, and warned on Saturday that if his GOP colleagues refuse to nuke the filibuster, Republicans would end up looking like “schmucks.”

“We ought to end the filibuster for the benefit of the American people, to secure our elections, to secure our border, to provide the kind of prosperity that President Trump [is] trying to accomplish with [his] economic policies,” Johnson said.

“I wish that we could maintain the filibuster, but that time has passed! Next time Democrats are in power, they’ll nuke it and we will look like schmucks!”

Our Analysis:

The Filibuster Fiasco: A Tale of Hypocrisy and Political Maneuvering

In a recent whirlwind of political rhetoric, Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) labeled the concept of abolishing the filibuster as a dead end, a stance that was promptly challenged by Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI). Johnson, breaking ranks, advocated for the elimination of this procedural rule, a tool historically used by senators to block a vote, as a means to propel the Trump administration’s agenda forward. This move has ignited a fiery debate within the Republican ranks, exposing a schism that could have far-reaching implications for the American legislative process.

The Irony in Johnson’s Justification

Sen. Johnson’s defense of this radical shift leans heavily on a narrative of necessity and survival. He articulates a fear that without such drastic measures, Republicans stand to lose ground to what he describes as “awful legislation” proposed by Democrats and “the socialists in their party.” This statement, dripping with irony, fails to acknowledge the filibuster’s historical role in enabling minority parties to exert significant influence over the legislative process, safeguarding the system from becoming a mere echo chamber of the majority’s whims.

The paradox here is palpable: Johnson’s argument hinges on a doomsday scenario where Democrats, given the chance, would abolish the filibuster to implement a sweeping, transformative agenda, including expanding the Supreme Court and granting statehood to D.C. and Puerto Rico, thus solidifying their grip on power. Yet, it’s precisely this kind of unilateral power play he advocates for when it suits his party’s interests.

The Specter of a “One-Party State”

Johnson’s alarmist rhetoric about the United States morphing into a “one-party state” under Democratic rule if the filibuster were abolished is both misleading and hypocritical. It’s a scare tactic designed to rally support by painting a dystopian picture of the future, one where political diversity and dissent are crushed under the heels of a Democratic majority. This narrative conveniently ignores the fact that the filibuster itself has been weaponized by both parties to stall legislation, often leading to gridlock and dysfunction rather than constructive debate and compromise.

The Real Losers: The American People

Amidst this political posturing and fearmongering, the ultimate casualties are the American people, whom Johnson claims to champion. The filibuster, for all its faults, serves as a critical check on majority rule, encouraging deliberation and bipartisanship. By advocating for its elimination, Johnson is willing to sacrifice this foundational principle of American governance on the altar of short-term political gain, undermining the very fabric of democratic debate and discourse.

Trump’s Influence Looms Large

The shadow of former President Trump looms ominously over this debate. His insistence that Senate Republicans “nuke the filibuster” to avoid losing control of Congress reflects a broader trend within the party: an increasing willingness to eschew democratic norms and principles in favor of raw political power. Johnson’s echoing of Trump’s demands illustrates a disturbing alignment with this authoritarian bent, prioritizing party loyalty and agenda over the health and integrity of the republic.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

Sen. Johnson’s cavalier attitude toward the filibuster and his characterization of Republicans who oppose its abolition as “schmucks” reveal a dangerous game of political brinkmanship. By framing the debate in such stark, apocalyptic terms, Johnson and like-minded Republicans are not only deepening the divisions within their party but also risking the erosion of the democratic processes that underpin the American political system.

In the end, the debate over the filibuster is not just about parliamentary procedure; it’s a reflection of a broader crisis in American politics, where the pursuit of power increasingly trumps the principles of democracy and governance. As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the real “schmucks” are those willing to undermine the nation’s democratic institutions for their own political gain.
