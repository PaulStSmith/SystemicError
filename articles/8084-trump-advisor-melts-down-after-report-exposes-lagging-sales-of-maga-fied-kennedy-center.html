Trump advisor melts down after report exposes lagging sales of MAGA-fied Kennedy Center

Despite traditionally being regarded as one of America’s most storied cultural icons, the Kennedy Center has had significant difficulty filling seats ever since President Donald Trump took over the vaunted institution earlier this year.

The Daily Beast reported Wednesday that Trump advisor Richard Grenell – his first-term acting Director of National Intelligence who is now president and executive director of the Kennedy Center – erupted after a report by the Athletic (which is owned by the New York Times) showed how the facility’s ticket sales have been lagging for months. The report noted that the Kennedy Center allowed FIFA (which organizes the quadrennial World Cup) to rent its concert hall for a $0 fee.

In a lengthy post to his official X account, Grenell tore into both the outlet’s reporters and former Kennedy Center dance director Jane Raleigh, who confided to the outlet that subscription return rates had seen a 50 percent drop. He also refuted the report by saying FIFA was “paying the Kennedy Center more than $7 million to do their world-wide event FIFA draw” through various “sponsorship opportunities” and a $2.4 million donation.

“The bloggers quoted a failed dance programmer who wasn’t able to pay for the ideas she came up with at the KC - and who traveled the world watching other dance programs - expecting her salary and travel expenses to still be paid by someone,” Grenell wrote. “We fired her for being unable to lead with fiscal responsibility.”

“What these far left radical reporters never grasp is how responsible leaders must pay the bills,” he added.

The Athletic report observed that Trump “ousted 19 members of the Center’s previously bipartisan board of trustees and announced he would take over as chairman,” with a goal of “purging the Center of ‘woke’ programming.” Longtime patron Susannah Joyner told the publication that Trump’s imprint on the Kennedy Center has been “tragic,” and that the administration had politicized a “sacred” place.

“It’s a cultural center, not a business center,” Joyner said of the FIFA agreement.

Our Analysis:

The Kennedy Center Under Trump: A Clash of Cultures and Priorities

In a move that can only be described as breathtakingly audacious, the Kennedy Center, an emblem of American cultural prestige, has found itself at the center of a contentious overhaul under the stewardship of President Donald Trump and his appointee, Richard Grenell. The Daily Beast’s recent exposé into the institution’s struggles to attract audiences—and Grenell’s fiery rebuttal—paints a vivid picture of the ideological tug-of-war ripping at the seams of America’s artistic fabric.

The Controversial Takeover

First and foremost, let’s address the elephant in the room: Donald Trump taking over the Kennedy Center. The very notion reads like a plot twist in a dystopian novel, where cultural sanctuaries are commandeered for political posturing. Under his guidance, the Kennedy Center has seen a dramatic shake-up, including the ousting of 19 board members in favor of a more Trump-aligned oversight. This isn’t just a changing of the guard; it’s a full-fledged cultural coup d’état, aiming to purge the institution of what has been pejoratively labeled ‘woke’ programming.

Financial Fiasco or Fiscal Responsibility?

The Athletic’s report sheds light on a seemingly desperate situation: the Kennedy Center’s plummeting ticket sales and the eyebrow-raising decision to let FIFA use its concert hall for free. Grenell’s counter-argument—that FIFA is, in fact, contributing over $7 million through sponsorships and donations—does little to assuage concerns. It feels like a band-aid solution to a bullet wound, especially when considering the broader implications of prioritizing high-dollar deals over the center’s cultural mission.

Grenell’s attack on Jane Raleigh, the former dance director, and his lambasting of the “far left radical reporters,” reeks of deflection. The issue at hand isn’t just about fiscal responsibility; it’s about the ideological reshaping of a cultural institution. Grenell’s dismissal of criticism as mere radicalism is a tired tactic used to silence dissenting voices.

Cultural Center or Business Center?

Susannah Joyner’s lament that the Kennedy Center has been transformed from a “cultural center” into a “business center” strikes at the heart of the controversy. The inclusion of FIFA, regardless of the financial justification, symbolizes a profound shift in the center’s identity and priorities. This isn’t about being anti-business; it’s about preserving spaces that are dedicated to the enrichment of society through art and culture. The Kennedy Center was never meant to be a cash cow or a vehicle for political agendas.

A Tragic Reimagining

Calling Trump’s imprint on the Kennedy Center “tragic” may sound like hyperbole to some, but for many, it’s an apt description. The administration’s efforts to politicize and commercialize a sacred space have not gone unnoticed. The resistance from long-time patrons and the wider public reflects a deep-seated belief in the arts as a universal language that transcends political divides.

Conclusion

The saga of the Kennedy Center under Trump and Grenell is more than a story of falling ticket sales or financial disputes. It’s a cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing cultural institutions. As this drama unfolds, one thing remains clear: the soul of the Kennedy Center, and by extension America’s cultural landscape, hangs in the balance. Will it remain a beacon of artistic excellence and cultural unity, or will it succumb to the whims of political and financial machinations? Only time will tell, but the early signs are disconcerting, to say the least.
