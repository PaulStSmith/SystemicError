Melania Trump allegedly 'quiet quitting' Christmas — and her marriage: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20256 min read

First lady Melania Trump arrives to attend the annual Marine Toys for Tots Drive hosted by the United States Marine Corps Reserve, at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Quantico, Virginia on Dec. 8, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

It’s been no secret that First Lady Melania Trump has been accused of being a grinch, but an analyst Thursday suggested that she could be “quiet quitting” Christmas — and her marriage.

Salon‘s Andi Zeisler pointed out how “first lady’s retreat from the holiday season suggests a deeper kind of disengagement” and that her lackluster book reading to children this holiday season shows she has done “the barest minimum required.”

“And it wasn’t surprising that when it came time for the annual tradition of reading a picture book to children at Washington, D.C.’s Children’s National Hospital, the least-enthusiastic first lady in living memory did not bring her A game,” Zeisler wrote.

Melania has read to children audiences in the past, showing illustrations to kids. But some have argued that “hating Christmas is simply Melania Trump’s thing, much the same as Nancy Reagan’s thing was astrology and popping into 1980s sitcoms to remind kids not to do drugs,” Zeisler explained.

Yet something has seemed off.

“But the vibes were darker this year, quite literally: Melania showed up to the event dressed less for Yuletide than for the funeral of a second-tier Batman villain,” Zeisler wrote. “She settled into a chair in front of an enormous Christmas tree with the book ‘How Does Santa Go Down the Chimney?’ and proceeded to read it slowly and mechanically, stumbling over words and through awkward pauses. She occasionally looked up to smile, but by all other appearances was reading to herself. Most crucially, she did not once turn the book around and take a brief pause so the children could see the pictures on each page she read.”

Last month, the First lady fled the White House Christmas tree reveal event in under three minutes. And during the first Trump administration, leaked CNN audio from secretly recorded conversations revealed how she really felt about the holidays and decorating. “I’m working… my a-- off on the Christmas stuff, that you know, who gives a f--- about the Christmas stuff and decorations?” Melania reportedly said. “But I need to do it, right?”

After last week, it appeared there could be heightened tension between Melania and President Donald Trump.

“Against this backdrop, Melania Trump’s unwillingness to successfully conduct a Christmas storytime comes across like insult to injury — perhaps not quite on the same level as the jacket she famously wore when visiting a children’s detention center at the border, emblazoned with ‘I Really Don’t Care, Do U?’ but at the same intersection of stingy and cruel,” Zeisler wrote. “But is it possible that the first lady’s shambolic read-along was sparked by a more timely anger? As the president oversaw a hasty demolition of the White House’s East Wing — historically the domain of first ladies — his remark that Melania had ‘loved her little office’ jumped out.”

The First lady could simply be unhappy, the writer added, pointing to a recent report from The Cut that revealed more middle aged women have grown dissatisfied with marriage and silently checked out.

“Perhaps Melania is just matching her husband’s destructive pettiness, in which case she’s joined an allegedly growing number of women of means who opt for quiet quitting their unhappy marriages rather than divorcing,” Zeisler wrote. “She’s still the first lady, but maybe she’s decided to put even less effort into the appearances, the overseas trips and smile for the photo ops demanded of her. Having never signed up for this sad charade, malicious compliance is one of the few weapons she has. (What’s Trump going to do about it, bury her on his golf course?)”

Our Analysis:

Melania Trump: The Unenthusiastic First Lady or a Symbol of Silent Protest?

In an age where the line between political theatre and reality TV blurs, Melania Trump’s recent behaviors have sparked a flurry of speculation and analysis. According to a piece by Andi Zeisler in Salon, the First Lady’s apparent lack of enthusiasm, particularly around the festive season, may be more than just holiday blues—it might signal a deeper disengagement, not only from her duties but possibly from her marriage as well.

The Christmas Conundrum

Let’s cut through the Christmas tinsel here. The portrayal of Melania Trump as the Grinch has been a recurring theme, yet Zeisler’s suggestion that she is “quiet quitting” Christmas—and potentially her marriage—is a bold claim that demands scrutiny. The evidence? A lackluster book reading event and a fleeting appearance at the White House Christmas tree reveal. Is this really a sign of rebellion, or simply the actions of someone who never truly embraced the role thrust upon her?

Melania’s alleged indifference to the holiday spirit isn’t new; remember the leaked CNN audio where she expressed her frustration with Christmas decorations? It’s easy to jump from there to broader conclusions about her satisfaction with her public role and private life. However, the leap from distaste for festive obligations to speculation about marital discord is a giant one, and it’s essential to tread carefully between analyzing public behavior and delving into unverified personal speculation.

A Symbol of Silent Protest?

The comparison of Melania’s demeanor during the book reading to her infamous “I Really Don’t Care, Do U?” jacket episode is an interesting one. Both could be interpreted as acts of defiance or, as Zeisler suggests, “malicious compliance.” The notion that Melania Trump is engaging in a form of quiet quitting, not just in her role as First Lady but also in her marriage, is a provocative one. It aligns with the narrative of a person who never fully consented to the political circus, now using subtle acts of resistance as her escape mechanism.

However, are we giving too much credit to what could simply be a case of personal dissatisfaction being aired in a very public way? The idea that Melania is signaling her discontent through minimal effort in her duties is compelling but ventures dangerously close to armchair psychology.

A Reflection of Broader Issues?

Zeisler’s linkage of Melania Trump’s alleged disengagement to a broader trend of middle-aged women quietly exiting unhappy marriages is an interesting societal observation. However, applying this general trend to the specific context of Melania Trump’s situation is a stretch without more concrete evidence. This comparison risks oversimplifying the unique pressures and scrutiny faced by individuals in the public eye, especially in political contexts.

Conclusion: Between Speculation and Reality

In dissecting Melania Trump’s recent actions, it’s crucial to differentiate between valid criticism of her public role’s execution and unfounded speculation about her personal life. While Zeisler’s article raises intriguing points about the First Lady’s perceived disengagement, it sometimes blurs the line between analysis and conjecture.

The assessment of Melania Trump as performing the “barest minimum required” of her could well be a commentary on her discomfort with the performative aspects of her role. Yet, attributing her actions to a broader, silent protest against her marriage and position requires a leap of faith that facts alone cannot support.

In sum, while it’s tempting to read between the lines of Melania Trump’s public appearances (or lack thereof), doing so without solid evidence veers into the realm of speculation. The real story might be less about quiet quitting and more about the complexities of living life in an unforgiving political spotlight.
