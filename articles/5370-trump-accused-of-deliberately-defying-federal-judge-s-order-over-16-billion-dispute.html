Trump accused of 'deliberately' defying federal judge's order over $16 billion dispute

Earlier in February, a federal judge ended President Donald Trump’s attempted extortion of New York and New Jersey residents by demanding the Trump administration “unfreeze funding” for the sprawling $16 billion Gateway project.

The White House had floated unlocking the Gateway money in exchange for Democrats’ agreeing to rename Penn Station and Washington Dulles International Airport after Trump. But U.S. District Judge Jeannette Vargas halted that effort after New York and New Jersey attorneys general filed suit, arguing that the White House’s order to withhold the funds was illegal and caused economic harm.

One week later, however, the AGs are complaining the Trump administration is intentionally dragging its feet.

“It is now well into business hours today, February 13, and there is no sign of a wire transfer of any money, much less the over $200 million the Government asserted was ready to be disbursed at 1 p.m. on Monday in its filings earlier this week. Communications with Defendants’ counsel have not produced any clear assurances of timing,” argued a letter signed by New York State AG Letitia James, who has already racked up a successful civil fraud case against Trump.

James and the New Jersey AG argue that funds should have been disbursed immediately, but instead the Trump administration is claiming that additional processes are needed, and that even after Defendants complete those processes, the Department of Treasury will require further hurdles to process the disbursements.

“Even worse, Defendants appear to have deliberately delayed these processes,” James argued, saying the Trump administration cancelled the general district court’s reimbursement requests without notifying the court. They then further stalled the process by demanding the court initiate new reimbursement requests.

“Plaintiffs understand that disbursements are generally not instantaneous,” said James, but she argued that hurdles must have already been surmounted if the court expected the money to be disbursed without a stay by 1 p.m. on Monday.

NY Daily News court reporter Molly Crane-Newman posted on X that federal lawyers told Vargas, say the the department of transportation has sent payments to the Treasury and that states won’t get paid until Tuesday.

Our Analysis:

A Blatant Power Play: The Gateway Project Saga

In what reads like a plot pulled straight from a political thriller, President Donald Trump’s attempted extortion of New York and New Jersey over the $16 billion Gateway project serves as a stark reminder of the tumultuous relationship between federal power and local needs. The audacity of trading infrastructure funding for the renaming of iconic locations after oneself is a move that would seem too brazen even for fiction. Yet, here we are, dissecting a real-world scenario where this very proposal was floated.

The heart of this saga lies not just in the ludicrous proposition but in the subsequent legal and bureaucratic tangle that unfolded. U.S. District Judge Jeannette Vargas’s intervention, calling to “unfreeze funding,” was a critical moment. It was a judicial check on what was described as an illegal withholding of funds, which, according to New York and New Jersey attorneys general, caused significant economic harm.

However, the story does not end with this judicial order. The Trump administration’s alleged foot-dragging in the aftermath is telling. The complaints from the attorneys general that the administration is intentionally delaying the disbursement of funds are concerning. It paints a picture of a federal government that, even when legally mandated, seems reluctant to act in the best interest of its constituents.

What’s more, the excuse of needing additional processes as a reason for the delay is a classic bureaucratic stonewalling technique. It’s a frustrating reminder of how red tape can be weaponized to slow down or complicate what should be straightforward processes. The claim by New York State AG Letitia James that the Trump administration deliberately delayed these processes by canceling reimbursement requests without notifying the court adds a layer of deceit to the already complicated proceedings.

This entire episode is a microcosm of the larger issues at play within American politics: the struggle between state and federal authority, the weaponization of funding for political gains, and the bureaucratic hurdles that often impede progress. The Gateway project, essential for countless commuters and crucial for the economic vitality of the region, has become a pawn in a greater game of political chess.

The irony of federal lawyers telling Judge Vargas that payments have been sent but states won’t see the money until a later date is a bitter pill to swallow. It underscores a systemic issue where even after legal battles are won, the war of attrition in the form of bureaucratic delays continues.

In conclusion, the Gateway project saga is more than just a dispute over funding; it’s a glaring example of how political machinations and legal wrangling can stymie critical infrastructure projects. It serves as a cautionary tale of the lengths to which political vendettas can be pursued, at the expense of public interest and economic well-being. The fact that this situation could arise from a demand as petty as renaming landmarks after oneself is a testament to the surreal state of current political affairs. The residents of New York and New Jersey, and indeed all Americans, deserve better.
