'Resign today': Calls grow for Mike Huckabee to step down over groveling

Based on an article by Brad Reed, Common Dreams

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Story, In BriefPower Has a NameThe Usual MisdirectionChristians As CoverAccountability, Not ApologyThe Larger Pattern

Mike Huckabee Is Not Failing Quietly. He Is Choosing Sides.

The Story, In Brief

Sen. Chris Van Hollen says Mike Huckabee, the U.S. ambassador to Israel, has repeatedly failed to condemn Israeli mistreatment of American citizens and should resign. Van Hollen pointed to detentions of American citizens Sama Safi and Adam Karakrah, the killings of nine Americans over the last four years, and violent settler attacks on Christian villages in the West Bank carried out with impunity.

Power Has a Name

This is not a story about diplomatic awkwardness. It is about an ambassador with institutional power refusing to use it on behalf of the people he is supposed to represent. Huckabee does not lack access, visibility, or a platform. He holds the office that exists precisely to defend U.S. interests in a hostile or abusive environment. If American citizens are being detained, abused, or killed, and the ambassador’s public posture is silence or evasion, that is not neutrality. It is a decision.

Van Hollen is right to place responsibility where it belongs: on the official who can speak, pressure, condemn, and escalate, but does not.

The Usual Misdirection

The standard dodge in stories like this is to treat the problem as a failure of tone, temperament, or communication. That is nonsense. The source text describes repeated incidents and repeated non-response. That is not confusion. That is pattern.

When American citizens are harmed and the response is to look away, blame lower-level chaos, or hide behind vague claims of concern, the effect is to launder impunity. The stronger party gets to act, the weaker party gets told to wait for process, and the public is invited to mistake deliberate neglect for diplomatic complexity.

Christians As Cover

Van Hollen also exposes the cheap performance at the center of Huckabee’s posture. Huckabee invokes his own Christianity while violent attacks on Palestinian Christian villages continue. That is not protection of Christian communities. It is branding.

There is a difference between Christian visitors on curated tours and Christians who have lived in the Holy Land for generations under threat. Huckabee’s language appears designed to flatter a constituency, not defend a people. It narrows Christianity into a travel identity and uses that reduction to excuse political abandonment.

Accountability, Not Apology

The source notes that more than two dozen senators have urged Huckabee to hold Israel accountable for the killings of nine Americans by settlers or IDF members over four years. That detail matters because it shows this is not a lone protest or an emotional outburst. The evidence has reached the Senate. The deaths have accumulated. The response remains thin.

At that point, continuing in the job is not a sign of steady diplomacy. It is a sign that the ambassador’s real loyalty lies with the most extreme Israeli government he is willing to excuse, not with the Americans he was sent to defend.

The Larger Pattern

This is what state power looks like when it is captured by ideology and cushioned by impunity: American lives become an inconvenience, abuse becomes a talking point, and accountability is treated as impolite. The governing question is not whether officials understand the harm. They do. The question is who they are willing to protect when protection would require conflict with a favored ally and a domestic political faction.

Van Hollen’s demand for resignation is the minimum serious response. The deeper problem is a political culture that keeps rewarding officials who confuse allegiance with servility and diplomacy with silence.
