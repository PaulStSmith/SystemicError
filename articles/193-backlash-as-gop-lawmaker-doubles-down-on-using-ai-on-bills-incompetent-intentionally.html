Backlash as GOP lawmaker doubles down on using AI on bills: 'Incompetent intentionally'

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosJune 25, 20263 min read

When Congress Outsources Judgment to a Chatbot

The Summary

Rep. Anna Paulina Luna’s office submitted a defense bill amendment summary that still contained the chatbot’s own label, “Claude responded:”, along with timestamp residue and filler text. After the error went viral, Luna said staff had used AI “to correct a draft text” and “didn’t edit,” then told them to be more careful.

The Power Was Never the Problem

The important fact is not that a staffer used AI. The important fact is that a sitting member of Congress has institutional power over legislative language, and her office treated that power like a sloppy prompt session. This was not some anonymous clerk improvising in the dark. It was an elected official’s office handling legislative material with the care of a spam filter.

That matters because Congress does not merely “communicate” policy. It writes the terms under which power operates. When that process is delegated to a chatbot and then pushed out unchanged, the office is not saving time. It is demonstrating how casually it regards the work of lawmaking.

Not a Mistake, a Practice

Luna’s first defense was to normalize the problem: “Most staff use it.” That is the revealing line. The argument is not that the office slipped once. It is that this kind of automation is already treated as routine, and the embarrassment comes only when the machine leaves fingerprints.

The real offense is not the existence of AI in the workflow. It is the refusal to verify, edit, or even read what was being submitted. “We used AI” is now the alibi for not doing the job. That is institutional drift dressed up as efficiency.

The Blame Shifts Downward

Notice where the accountability lands: on “staff,” on a generic carelessness, on the idea that somebody forgot to double-check. That framing keeps the decision-maker at a safe distance. The person with authority gets to sound mildly apologetic while the office absorbs the embarrassment.

That is classic political misdirection. Deliberate delegation gets recoded as innocent oversight. A leadership failure becomes a junior-worker mistake. And because the text came from a public-facing office, the damage is not just comedic. It signals that legislative process itself can be handled with minimal human attention as long as the surface looks functional.

The Larger Pattern

The deeper pattern is administrative hollowing. Modern politics increasingly rewards the appearance of throughput over the substance of judgment. If a machine can produce a passable draft, the institution pretends that the draft is the work. If the draft is wrong, the institution blames haste instead of the architecture that made haste a virtue.

That is how serious functions degrade. Not with dramatic collapse, but with a steady acceptance of low-grade negligence as normal operating procedure. The AI label left in the document is not just a gaffe. It is a tiny disclosure of how much of governance now relies on unexamined automation and post hoc excuses.

The Real Lesson

This story is not about one clumsy amendment summary. It is about a political class willing to hand over public work to tools it barely checks, then call the result an accident. That is not competence interrupted by technology. It is incompetence made more efficient.

The scandal is not that a chatbot spoke in Congress. The scandal is that nobody in the room thought they needed to listen before submitting it.
