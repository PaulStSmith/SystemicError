Deputy FBI director under fire after internal emails reveal Epstein redactions

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 2, 20255 min read

Dan Bongino once made a name for himself as a MAGA-aligned commentator and podcaster, one who was outspoken about the need to disclose files related to sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein. Now, as deputy director of the FBI, he is under fire over his knowledge of he massive effort to redact the files, the Daily Beast reported.

Bongino joined the FBI as deputy director under Kash Patel back in May. At the time, he claimed that the Trump administration’s slow-walk of the Epstein files was part of an effort to protect the victims of the deceased financier and underage sex trafficker. Since then, the Daily Beast noted, he had gone silent on the subject.

Over the weekend, Bloomberg published a report detailing the FBI’s massive effort to comb through and redact large parts of the government’s trove of files related to the Department of Justice Epstein investigations. Part of this report included internal FBI emails showing that the Bureau made Bongino aware of the redaction project as early as the day after he became deputy director.

Following a wave of renewed scrutiny against him and his potential hand in the redactions, Bongino took to X to try and downplay the email, claiming that it was actually from just before he started at the FBI.

“Folks, I entered on duty on March 17th. The emails in the chain you see forwarded to me, at my request, were sent before I began in my position,” Bongino wrote. “I wanted to review what have (sic) been done before I entered on duty. It was a priority and, as you can see, they responded immediately. I’m glad that these emails are available for your review.”

Bloomberg’s report found that FBI officials called in over 1,000 agents to help with the redaction efforts, in order to prepare them for potential release to the public. The administration’s resistance to disclosing the files and reports of these redactions added fuel to the longstanding allegations that they could potentially implicate President Donald Trump, once a close friend of Epstein, in his crimes.

Prior reports from CNN claimed that Bongino was “enraged” by the DOJ’s handling of the Epstein files, particularly a statement from July in which Attorney General Pam Bondi insisted that nothing in the files warranted new charges against third-parties and that there was no client list detailing the individuals Epstein trafficked underage girls for. CNN reported that Bongino took to skipping work and threatened to resign in protest of the DOJ’s attempts to downplay the files.

Our Analysis:

The Bongino Conundrum: A Tale of Silence and Redaction

Dan Bongino’s transition from a MAGA-aligned commentator to the deputy director of the FBI is a tale wrapped in irony and controversy. Once an outspoken advocate for the disclosure of Jeffrey Epstein’s files, Bongino now finds himself embroiled in an operation to redact significant portions of these very documents. The Daily Beast’s report sheds light on a story that seems to be more about political convenience than the pursuit of justice.

The Irony of Silence

Bongino’s silence on the Epstein files since joining the FBI stands in stark contrast to his previous vocal demands for transparency. His earlier claims, that the Trump administration’s slow-walking of the Epstein files was to protect the victims, now seem to be a convenient narrative given his current predicament. This shift raises questions about the genuine motivations behind the redactions and whether victim protection is merely a facade for political protectionism.

The Redaction Revelation

The revelation by Bloomberg of a massive effort by the FBI to redact large parts of the Epstein files is alarming. The internal emails indicating Bongino’s early awareness of this project only a day after his appointment add a layer of intrigue to the narrative. His attempt to downplay his knowledge of the redactions on X (formerly Twitter) does little to dispel concerns. Claiming that the emails were from before his tenure and asserting his eagerness to review past actions does not absolve him of responsibility; it rather points to a premeditated effort to shape the narrative.

The Thousand-Agent Task

The mobilization of over 1,000 agents for this redaction effort is staggering and suggests an unprecedented level of concern over the contents of the Epstein files. The administration’s resistance to disclosing these files, as detailed in the Bloomberg report, fuels speculation that they could implicate high-profile individuals, including former President Donald Trump. This massive undertaking begs the question: Who are they trying to protect?

The DOJ’s Dubious Stance

Bongino’s reported fury over the Department of Justice’s handling of the Epstein files adds another layer of complexity to this saga. CNN’s reports of his outrage, threats to resign, and work absences in protest of the DOJ’s attempts to downplay the files starkly contrast his current role in the redaction process. Attorney General Pam Bondi’s insistence that nothing in the files warranted new charges or revealed a client list directly contradicts the need for such an extensive redaction effort, suggesting a possible cover-up.

Conclusion: The Systemic Error

Dan Bongino’s journey from a crusader for transparency to a participant in the potential obfuscation of justice encapsulates a systemic error in our political and legal systems. The efforts to redact the Epstein files not only undermine public trust but also hint at a deeper malaise within our institutions—a malaise where political affiliations and allegiances can dictate the course of justice, often at the expense of truth and accountability.

In this saga, the victims of Epstein’s crimes and the public’s right to know appear to be secondary considerations to political gamesmanship. As the story unfolds, one can only hope for a resurgence of integrity and transparency within our institutions. However, based on this tale of silence and redaction, such hopes may be in vain.
