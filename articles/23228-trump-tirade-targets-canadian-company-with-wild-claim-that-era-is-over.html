Trump tirade targets Canadian company with wild claim: 'That Era is OVER!'

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosSeptember 8, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trade War as Hostage Note

President Donald Trump has once again turned a trade dispute into a public threat, this time targeting Bombardier and claiming the Canadian airplane maker would be barred from selling in the United States. Reuters notes this is a repeat offense: Trump made a similar threat in January, and nothing came of it.

The Power Is the President

The central fact here is not Bombardier’s revenue mix or Canada’s aviation rules. It is that the president of the United States is using the office’s market power as a cudgel. Trump is the actor with real institutional leverage. He can set the terms of access, threaten tariffs, and use the presidency as a stage for economic intimidation.

That matters because the story is not about an injured American market. It is about a president manufacturing a grievance and assigning himself the role of enforcer.

A Grievance Built for Theater

Trump’s language is not policy. It is cartel talk dressed up as nationalism: “buy American,” “fly on American airliners,” “enjoy American liquor and beverages.” The point is not coherence. The point is submission.

He frames Bombardier as though a Canadian manufacturer “lives off” American buyers and airports, while ignoring the obvious reality that U.S. trade power is what makes this threat meaningful in the first place. This is coercion with a flag draped over it.

The Real Decision Was to Escalate

The decision-maker here is Trump, full stop. He chose to launch the attack on Truth Social. He chose to tie Bombardier to Gulfstream, turning a trade complaint into favoritism theater. He chose to reprise a January threat after Reuters says nothing came of the first one.

That last detail is not a side note. Repeated empty threats are not evidence of seriousness; they are evidence of a governing style built on spectacle, noise, and arbitrary pressure. The uncertainty is the mechanism.

Misdirection by Design

This framing pushes blame downward and outward. Bombardier becomes the villain, Canada becomes the obstacle, and Trump casts himself as the victim of unfair treatment. That is backwards. He is the one wielding state power to punish a company because it is useful to his political script.

The usual trade-war language pretends the market is a neutral battlefield. It is not. The United States is the larger power here, and Trump is using that asymmetry to extract obedience while pretending to defend fairness.

What This Reveals

The deeper pattern is not about airplanes. It is about how authoritarian politics treats economic policy as personal leverage. Rules become threats. Trade becomes punishment. Public office becomes a tool for shakedowns.

That is the logic on display here: not governing, but domination dressed as economic patriotism.
