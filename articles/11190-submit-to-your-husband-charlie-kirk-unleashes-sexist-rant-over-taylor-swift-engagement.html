'Submit to your husband': Charlie Kirk unleashes sexist rant over Taylor Swift engagement

Charlie Kirk speaking with attendees at the 2021 Southwest Regional Conference hosted by Turning Point USA at the Arizona Biltmore in Phoenix, Arizona. (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

MAGA influencer Charlie Kirk predicted that Taylor Swift would become a conservative after her engagement to Travis Kelce.

In a sexist rant on Tuesday, Kirk declared that Swift should "submit to your husband" and have "a ton of children."

"I think there's actually something here that we should all celebrate," Kirk said. "How often do we on this program talk about the three M's make you a better citizen and more conservative, which of course is mortgage, marriage, and mating."

"But maybe one of the reasons why Taylor Swift has been so just kind of annoyingly liberal over the last couple of years is that she's not yet married and she doesn't have children," he continued. "Taylor Swift might de-radicalize herself. She might come back down to reality. I want them to have lots of children."

Kirk asserted that Swift "might go from a cat lady to a JD Vance supporter."

"If she ends up having children, she'll stop this kind of liberal endorsing Joe Biden nonsense," he remarked. "Up until this point, that's not a great role model for young women to wait all the way until you're 35 and just put your career first... It's a great chance for Taylor Swift now to get married and have a ton of children."

"All kidding and sarcasm aside, this is something that I hope will make Taylor Swift more conservative," he added. "Reject feminism. Submit to your husband, Taylor. You're not in charge."

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of Charlie Kirk's Sexist Tirade Against Taylor Swift

In a recent outburst that can only be described as a sexist rant, Charlie Kirk, a MAGA influencer, has laid bare the regressive mindset that still plagues sections of our society. Kirk's comments about Taylor Swift, following her engagement to Travis Kelce, are not just a personal attack on Swift but an embodiment of a broader, deeply rooted misogynistic ideology that seeks to dictate and control the roles of women in society. Let's dissect this with the sharpness it demands and the clarity it lacks in Kirk's original pronouncement.

The Three M's: A Simplistic and Regressive Formula

Kirk's celebration of what he calls the "three M's" – mortgage, marriage, and mating – as a pathway to conservatism and better citizenship is not just simplistic; it's dangerously regressive. This notion suggests that personal life choices, such as marriage and procreation, are what validate one's contributions and standing in society. It's a narrow-minded view that seeks to confine individuals, especially women, into predefined roles that have been challenged and redefined over decades of social progress.

The Misogynistic Underpinnings

Kirk's assertion that Swift's potential marriage and future children could lead her to "de-radicalize" and reject her liberal views is steeped in misogyny. The implication that Swift's liberal stance is a result of her being unmarried and childless is not just offensive; it's a patronizing attempt to delegitimize her political and social views. The idea that a woman's political beliefs are somehow tied to her marital status or motherhood is an archaic notion that undermines women's autonomy and intelligence.

The "Cat Lady" to JD Vance Supporter Pipeline

The transition Kirk envisions for Swift, from a "cat lady" to a JD Vance supporter, is riddled with stereotypes and disdain for independent women. The term "cat lady" itself is a derogatory stereotype used to shame single, independent women, suggesting a life of solitude and eccentricity for those who choose not to marry or have children. The contrast with JD Vance, a conservative author and politician, further implies that a woman's political enlightenment or redemption comes only through embracing conservative, patriarchal values.

The Role Model Argument

Kirk's commentary on Swift's role as a model for young women is perhaps the most insidious aspect of his rant. By critiquing Swift's decision to prioritize her career and remain unmarried until her mid-30s, Kirk not only imposes his personal values on her but also suggests that women who follow a similar path are lesser role models. This stance is a blatant dismissal of the achievements of countless women who have contributed significantly to society, both historically and in contemporary times, without adhering to traditional roles of marriage and motherhood.

The Underlying Call to "Reject Feminism"

At the core of Kirk's tirade is a call for Swift to "reject feminism" and "submit to your husband." This is not just a personal attack on Swift but a broader attack on the principles of feminism, which advocate for equality, autonomy, and the right of women to make their own choices free from societal pressures. Kirk's comments reflect a disturbing desire to return to a time when women's roles were strictly limited and defined by men.

Conclusion

Charlie Kirk's comments about Taylor Swift are not just a personal attack; they are a microcosm of the sexism and misogyny that still exists in certain corners of society. They reflect a desire to control and dictate the lives of women, reducing them to their roles as wives and mothers. Such views should not only be challenged but rejected outright for the archaic and oppressive ideologies they represent. In a modern, progressive society, individuals, regardless of gender, should be free to make their own choices about marriage, parenthood, and careers without being subjected to such narrow-minded scrutiny.
