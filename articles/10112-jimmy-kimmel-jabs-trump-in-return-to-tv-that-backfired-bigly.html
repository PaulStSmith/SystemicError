Jimmy Kimmel jabs Trump in return to TV: 'That backfired bigly!'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 25, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Thin-Skinned Presidency: A Look at the Trump Administration’s Attempt to Silence Jimmy KimmelThe Irony of CensorshipThe Specter of AuthoritarianismA Call to Arms

KMGH-ABC screenshot

Late-night comedian Jimmy Kimmel jabbed President Donald Trump’s administration for its pressure campaign to remove his show from TV in a new episode that aired Tuesday night.

“Jimmy Kimmel Live!” was suspended “indefinitely” last week by Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair Broadcast Group after Kimmel made comments about the killer of slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk that the companies thought were insensitive. Nexstar and Sinclair both continued preempting the airing of Kimmel’s show on Tuesday, a move that impacted about 70 local ABC affiliates nationwide.

Kimmel received a standing ovation when he came out on stage and addressed some of the controversy during his opening monologue.

“You almost have to feel sorry for him,” Kimmel said of the president. “He tried his best to cancel me; instead, he forced millions of people to watch the show. That backfired bigly!”

He mentioned that during his brief suspension, a TV host from Germany offered him a job.

“Can you imagine, our country has become so authoritarian that someone from Germany was like, ‘Hey, you want to come over here?’” Kimmel said.

He also called on Americans to speak out against the un-American” actions of the Trump administration.

“What’s important is that we get to live in a country that allows us to have a show like this,” Kimmel said.

Our Analysis:

The Thin-Skinned Presidency: A Look at the Trump Administration’s Attempt to Silence Jimmy Kimmel

In what reads like a dystopian novel set in a world where freedom of speech is but a quaint concept, we find ourselves dissecting the latest episode in the saga of the Trump administration’s attempts to muzzle dissenting voices. This time, their target was none other than late-night comedian Jimmy Kimmel, whose only crime was exercising his First Amendment rights. The incident in question—a stark reminder of the fragility of these rights—saw Kimmel’s show, “Jimmy Kimmel Live!”, suspended “indefinitely” by Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair Broadcast Group. Their reason? Kimmel made comments about the killer of slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk, which the companies deemed insensitive.

The Irony of Censorship

Let’s pause to reflect on the irony here. The Trump administration, often the first to cry foul over perceived attacks on free speech (usually when such speech aligns with its own agenda), has seemingly no qualms about pressuring media companies to pull the plug on a television show because it hurt their feelings. This is not just hypocritical; it’s a dangerous precedent.

“You almost have to feel sorry for him,” Kimmel quipped about Trump during his triumphant return to the airwaves, which was met with a standing ovation. He pointed out the delicious irony of Trump’s attempts to cancel him only to increase his viewership. “That backfired bigly!” Indeed, it did. In their zeal to punish Kimmel, Trump and his cronies inadvertently turned him into a martyr for free speech, highlighting the administration’s authoritarian tendencies for all to see.

The Specter of Authoritarianism

Kimmel’s observation about the offer from a German TV host—“Can you imagine, our country has become so authoritarian that someone from Germany was like, ‘Hey, you want to come over here?’”—is both funny and chilling. It underscores a disturbing reality: When comedians from countries with their own fraught histories with authoritarianism start offering refuge, you know you’ve crossed into uncharted territory.

A Call to Arms

Kimmel’s closing remarks are a sobering reminder of what’s at stake: “What’s important is that we get to live in a country that allows us to have a show like this.” This is the crux of the matter. The ability to critique, to satirize, to question our leaders without fear of retribution is fundamental to a healthy democracy. When the government starts dictating who can say what on television, we’re not just slipping down the slope to authoritarianism—we’re barreling down it at full speed.

In conclusion, the Trump administration’s ham-fisted attempt to silence Jimmy Kimmel is more than just a failed exercise in censorship. It’s a glaring red flag, warning us of the erosion of democratic norms and the freedoms we too often take for granted. It’s a call to action for all Americans to speak out against these un-American actions. If we don’t, we risk losing not just our late-night comedians, but the very essence of our democracy.
