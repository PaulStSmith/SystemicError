Former GOP speechwriter tears into 'rancid' Megyn Kelly's hateful tirade

The Country’s Real Damage Is Not Coming From a Podcast Mic

The Target Is Not the Power

The source story is about Megyn Kelly using her platform to attack Haitian immigrants, while Tim Miller and Jane Coaston respond with disgust and ridicule. That is the surface. The deeper fact is that Kelly’s bile only matters because it rides on a larger political machine already primed to turn immigrants into public enemies.

Kelly is loud. She is not sovereign. The people with actual power are the politicians and institutions that normalize this language, reward it, and convert it into policy pressure. The outrage is directed at the loudest performer in the room while the real authors of the damage sit higher up the chain.

The Performance Is the Product

Coaston is right about one thing: this is a performance for an audience. Kelly is not stumbling into cruelty; she is packaging it. She invokes Haiti, “assimilation,” drunk driving, and Trump’s debunked cats-and-dogs claim as a ready-made script of racial panic. The point is not accuracy. The point is activation.

That is how reactionary media works now. It does not persuade through evidence. It signals belonging through contempt. The audience is not being informed. It is being trained to enjoy dehumanization.

Trump Set the Frame

Kelly did not invent the underlying script. She is recycling it. The source explicitly ties her attack to Trump’s false claim that immigrants were eating cats and dogs. That matters because it shows where the permission structure came from: presidential-level dishonesty, laundered through media personalities who treat lies as branding.

This is not random talk-radio ugliness. It is downstream of a political operation that turns minorities into props in an ongoing panic cycle. Once a president makes open propaganda socially safe, cable hosts and podcast celebrities no longer need to create the frame. They just monetize it.

The Useful Misdirection

Miller’s anger at Kelly is understandable, but it also risks a familiar misdirection. It is easy to denounce the visible mouthpiece and harder to confront the governing order that benefits from her work. A pundit can be vile and still not be the central problem. In this case, the central problem is the state power that makes anti-immigrant hysteria actionable.

The source notes Miller saying immigrants are “being sent back to Haiti for no reason” and “menaced by our government.” That is the actual political story. Not that one media figure is rude. That a government apparatus exists that can translate xenophobic noise into coercion, removal, and fear. Kelly’s role is to soften the ground.

The NBC Detour Does Not Redeem Anyone

The article reaches back to Kelly’s NBC days, when she was apparently playing a softer, more polished character, even hosting trans children on her show. That history is not evidence of growth or betrayal. It is evidence of adaptability.

This is what elite media careers often reward: not principle, but calibration. Kelly could wear a different mask when the incentives changed. When the market for transgressive anti-immigrant hatred became more lucrative, she dropped the morning-show costume and went back to selling resentment. The costume changed. The function did not.

What This Really Shows

The larger pattern is simple: American reactionary politics increasingly uses media figures to make cruelty feel normal, then uses that normalization to excuse institutional violence. First comes the slur. Then comes the policy atmosphere. Then comes the claim that the resulting harm was just confusion, just backlash, just what the audience wanted.

It is not confusion. It is a pipeline. The politicians, platforms, and commentators who feed it are not bystanders. They are the mechanism.
