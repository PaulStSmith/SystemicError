Our Analysis:

Trump’s Unwavering Focus on “Great Genes”

In a move that surprises absolutely no one, President Donald Trump has once again taken to his social media sanctuary, Truth Social, to express his unsolicited opinions on the latest NFL game. His subject of adulation this time? Cleveland Browns quarterback Shedeur Sanders, who apparently has caught the former president’s eye not just for his skill on the field but, peculiarly, for his “great genes.”

“Shedeur Sanders was GREAT,” Trump proclaimed, in a manner that echoes his characteristic blend of self-assurance and obsession with genetic determinism. “Great Genes. I TOLD YOU SO!” he added, as though anyone had asked or as if his endorsement were a talisman for athletic success.

Trump and His Ideological Kickoff

Trump’s commentary on NFL games is not a new phenomenon. He has had a contentious relationship with the league, oscillating between unsolicited advice to outright condemnation. His critiques have ranged from the introduction of a new kickoff rule—which he derisively labeled as “sissy football” created by “radical leftists”—to lambasting the New York Giants for their strategic decisions. This pattern of behavior is not just Trump being Trump; it’s a glimpse into his worldview where sports are not just games, but battlefields for ideological warfare.

The Unsettling Focus on Genetics

Trump’s fixation on “great genes” is not just an odd compliment; it’s a window into a troubling mindset that skews dangerously close to eugenics. By attributing Sanders’ success to his genetics, Trump not only simplifies the athlete’s hard work, discipline, and talent to mere hereditary luck but also perpetuates a narrative where genetics are a marker of value and potential. This is not just scientifically dubious; it’s morally questionable.

Sanders’ Performance: More Than Just Genetics

On the field, Shedeur Sanders’ debut was indeed impressive. Throwing for 209 yards with one touchdown and completing 11 of his 20 passes is no small feat, especially for a rookie. Yet, the celebration of his performance by fans—and even the former president—should focus on his skill, work ethic, and the potential for a bright future in the NFL. Reducing his achievement to “great genes” is not just a disservice to Sanders; it’s a simplistic and reductive view of human potential.

Conclusion: Beyond the Genetic Rhetoric

As Trump continues to blur the lines between political commentary and sports analysis, his focus on genetics as a determinant of success in sports is both troubling and misplaced. Athletes like Shedeur Sanders deserve to be celebrated for their achievements, their dedication to their sport, and their ability to inspire fans and fellow players alike. Let’s focus on the talent, the training, and the teamwork that makes sports truly great, leaving antiquated notions of “great genes” where they belong—in the past.
