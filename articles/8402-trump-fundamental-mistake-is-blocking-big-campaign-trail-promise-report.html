Trump 'fundamental mistake' is blocking big campaign trail promise: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 25, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump speaks during the ASEAN-U.S. Summit in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia on Oct. 26, 2025. Vincent Thian/Pool via REUTERS

President Donald Trump is making a fundamental and easily avoidable mistake that’s holding back the industrial boom he promised on the campaign trail, according to a report.

The delayed release of September’s jobs report showed slightly better than expected job growth, with 119,000 jobs added to the economy, but the data also showed the manufacturing sector lost 6,000 jobs – which means there are now 94,000 fewer people working in American factories than last year ago and 58,000 fewer than when Trump rolled out his tariffs in April, reported Bloomberg.

“It’s certainly fair to ask, why the doomsday scenarios some economists predicted haven’t happened?” wrote correspondent Shawn Donnan. “The main answer being that Trump has rolled back many of his import duties and that other things have been going on such as that AI data center boom.”

“It’s also fair to ask, why things haven’t been as good as Trump promised when he imposed tariffs with the goal of seeding an industrial rebirth?” he added. “The main answer being how he has rolled out his levies and what he has targeted.”

Trump’s steep tariffs on imported steel and aluminum seem to have boosted the primary metals sector, which added 2,300 jobs so far this year, but there’s little evidence of the blue collar revival he has been promising since entering politics a decade ago, and the U.S. is actually losing blue collar jobs for the first time since the Covid-19 pandemic.

“In September there were 59,000 fewer people working in manufacturing, construction, mining, logging, transportation and warehousing than there were in September 2024,” Donnan reported. “The difference in fortunes between service workers and those in goods-producing industries is stark, especially as senior officials in the Trump administration have talked about reorienting the economy away from Wall Street and services to one in which producing things drives growth.”

Goods-producing industries accounted for only 10,000 of the 119,000 jobs added in September, almost entirely in the construction industry that has benefited from the AI data center boom, but his tariffs are actually holding back an industrial boom.

“What he hasn’t excluded from his tariffs is the very machinery and other inputs needed if you want to build a factory,” Donnan reported. “In fact he’s threatened to impose more tariffs on industrial machines and robots. Which means that fitting out a new manufacturing plant in the U.S. is significantly more expensive than it was when Trump took office and may become pricier still.”

“Trump’s targeting of what economists call intermediate goods amounts to a fundamental mistake if you are trying to encourage an industrial boom,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Trump’s Industrial Strategy: A Critical Analysis

In a recent report, an intriguing paradox has come to light, highlighting a fundamental flaw in President Donald Trump’s approach to reviving the American industrial sector. Despite promises of an industrial boom fueled by aggressive tariff policies, the reality proves to be starkly different, and frankly, disappointing. This analysis will dissect the nuances of this situation, emphasizing the missteps and their implications on the blue-collar workforce Trump claims to champion.

The Mirage of Job Growth

At first glance, the addition of 119,000 jobs to the economy might appear as a feather in Trump’s cap. However, a closer examination reveals a disconcerting detail - the manufacturing sector lost 6,000 jobs, contributing to a net decline of 94,000 manufacturing jobs from the previous year and a drop of 58,000 since the initiation of Trump’s tariffs in April. This stark reality stands in sharp contrast to the industrial renaissance Trump has been promising since his foray into politics.

The Tariff Conundrum

Trump’s steep tariffs on imported steel and aluminum were touted as the catalysts for a blue-collar revival. Indeed, the primary metals sector saw a modest job increase of 2,300. Yet, this pales in comparison to the broader picture of job losses across manufacturing, construction, mining, logging, transportation, and warehousing sectors, totaling 59,000 fewer jobs as of September 2024. This discrepancy not only questions the efficacy of Trump’s tariffs but also highlights the broader economic disconnect between service and goods-producing industries.

A Fundamental Oversight

The crux of Trump’s failure lies in his targeting of intermediate goods through tariffs. These goods are essential components needed to build factories - the very heart of industrial activity Trump purportedly seeks to invigorate. By imposing and threatening further tariffs on industrial machines and robots, Trump has inadvertently increased the cost of establishing manufacturing plants in the U.S., contrary to his objectives. This decision is a glaring oversight, showcasing a fundamental misunderstanding of the economic ecosystem required for an industrial boom.

Conclusion: A Misguided Strategy

Trump’s industrial strategy, marred by a misguided tariff policy, has not only failed to deliver the promised revival but has also hindered potential growth in the sector. The loss of manufacturing jobs and the increased costs of industrial expansion starkly illustrate the disconnect between Trump’s promises and the economic realities facing the American workforce. As the administration continues to pivot the economy away from services and towards goods-producing industries, one must question the viability and foresight of such a strategy, given the current outcomes.

In essence, Trump’s approach serves as a cautionary tale of how not to stimulate industrial growth. The consequences of these policies - job losses and increased operational costs - underscore the need for a more nuanced and informed strategy that truly understands the intricacies of global trade and industrial expansion. As it stands, Trump’s industrial policy is not a blueprint for revival but a roadmap to further decline.
