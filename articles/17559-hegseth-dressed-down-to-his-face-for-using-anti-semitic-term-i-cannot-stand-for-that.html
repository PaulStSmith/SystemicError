Dismantling Disguised Anti-Semitism in High Office

Unmasking Hegemony in Language

In a recent congressional hearing, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth displayed a glaring misuse of power through language, one that escapes the bounds of simple faux pas and veers into overt anti-Semitism. When confronted by Senator Jacky Rosen about his repeated use of the term “Pharisees” to criticize opponents — a term historically weaponized against Jewish communities — Hegseth not only failed to retract his statements but defended his choice of words. This wasn’t a slip of the tongue; it was a calculated reinforcement of a prejudiced narrative under the guise of critique.

The Power Dynamics at Play

The confrontation illuminates the dynamics of power and responsibility. Hegseth, as the Secretary of Defense, holds a significant institutional role, shaping not only military policy but also contributing to the national discourse. His decision to employ and stand by a term fraught with anti-Semitic connotations underlines a misuse of this power. Senator Rosen’s challenge was not just about correcting a term but about holding a high-ranking official accountable for language that perpetuates harmful stereotypes.

Misdirection and Institutional Cowardice

Hegseth’s defense of his language — claiming the term “Pharisees” merely criticizes hypocrisy — is a classic example of misdirection. By focusing on the supposed intent behind the word rather than its historical and cultural weight, Hegseth sidesteps the core issue: the inherent anti-Semitism in his rhetoric. This misdirection is not just a personal failure but a reflection of broader institutional cowardice, where leaders choose linguistic battles over substantive ethical governance.

A Pattern of Authoritarian Cruelty

This incident is symptomatic of a larger, more disturbing trend within certain corridors of power — the use of authoritarian language to otherize and demonize. When leaders like Hegseth employ historically charged terms to undermine critics or political opponents, they do more than misuse words; they actively participate in a form of rhetorical cruelty that has real-world consequences for the targeted communities. This strategy is not about clarity or accuracy but about consolidating power by sowing division.

Conclusion: The Systemic Implications

The exchange between Rosen and Hegseth is not just a moment of political theater but a revealing snapshot of how language becomes a tool of power in high offices. It underscores the necessity for constant vigilance and accountability, especially from those in positions of significant influence. As we move forward, it is crucial to challenge not only the overt actions of those in power but also the subtle ways they shape narratives to serve their ends. The fight against anti-Semitism and other forms of bigotry is not only about stopping overt acts of hate but also about confronting these more insidious tactics of normalization in political discourse.
