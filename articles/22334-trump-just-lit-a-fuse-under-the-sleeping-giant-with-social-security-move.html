Trump just lit a fuse under the sleeping giant with Social Security move

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Social Security Is Not Broken. It Is Being Starved on Purpose.

The Target Is the Guarantee

The source story is about Social Security, but the real subject is power. Martin O’Malley’s core claim is plain: Trump, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and their allies are not accidentally mishandling a beloved program. They are weakening it, then using the damage to justify privatization, market exposure, and a transfer of public security into instruments that serve the wealthy.

That matters because Social Security is not charity and not a political perk. It is a guarantee. The entire attack depends on making that guarantee look old, inefficient, and vulnerable, when the actual vulnerability is being manufactured from above.

Who Benefits From the Wreckage

The decision-makers are not the people standing in line at field offices or the workers trying to process claims. They are the officials and ideological operators inside the Trump orbit who are cutting staffing, pushing “Trump accounts,” and talking about privatization in backdoor language. Bessent, according to the interview, has already said the quiet part out loud.

This is not a story about administrative confusion. It is a story about deliberate degradation. Staffing is being reduced, offices are turning into “ghost offices,” and ordinary tasks like changing a direct deposit account or correcting a survivor benefit are becoming harder to complete. That is not neutral inefficiency. It is how a public institution is softened up for theft.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The White House response is a textbook political smokescreen. Liz Huston claimed Trump will “protect and strengthen Social Security” while touting a so-called “No Tax on Social Security” provision that did not eliminate taxes on benefits and was bundled into the broader “Big Beautiful Bill,” a measure centered on tax breaks for the wealthy.

That is the pattern: announce protection, deliver erosion, and hope the public confuses a temporary tax gimmick with structural defense. Meanwhile, the administration keeps talking about waste and fraud without producing any prosecutions for the imagined dead beneficiaries and phantom immigrants it used to justify the campaign. The fraud was the cover story. The real project is to discredit the program so the public tolerates its dismantling.

Staffing Cuts Are Policy

The article makes clear that the damage is not abstract. O’Malley describes the largest loss of staff in the shortest period in the agency’s history, a 63-year staffing low, and a system strained by rising beneficiaries and shrinking personnel. That is not a technical footnote. It is policy by attrition.

When you pay employees to leave, warn them of restructuring, and create a hostile work environment, you are not merely trimming overhead. You are sabotaging capacity. Then, when service collapses, you point to the collapse as evidence that the agency cannot function. That trick is old. Break the thing, then cite the breakage as proof it should be handed over.

The Class Logic Behind the Attack

Social Security’s financing problem, as described in the interview, is not some natural law. It is the result of income inequality and a payroll tax cap that lets the richest Americans pay far less, relative to their wealth, than everyone else. O’Malley’s prescription is simple: lift or scrap the cap and strengthen the program.

That proposal reveals the moral order under attack. The administration and its allies want a system where retirement security is gambled in markets, including cryptocurrency and Trump-branded financial schemes, instead of guaranteed by public obligation. That is not reform. It is the conversion of social citizenship into private risk.

What This Story Really Shows

This is a test case for how authoritarian politics works in practice. First, define public provision as waste. Then starve the agency. Then blame the resulting breakdown on the program itself. Then offer the market as the rescue. The language of efficiency covers an ideology that treats disabled people, older people, and poor people as disposable.

Social Security exists because the country once accepted a basic democratic principle: no one is surplus. The current right-wing project rejects that principle and replaces it with a brutal ledger. If Democrats want to be more than the lesser evil, they should say so clearly and act accordingly: defend the guarantee, raise the cap, and stop treating public social insurance as something to be managed cautiously instead of fought for.
