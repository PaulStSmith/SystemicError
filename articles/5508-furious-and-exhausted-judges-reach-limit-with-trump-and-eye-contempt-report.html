'Furious and exhausted’ judges reach limit with Trump — and eye contempt: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 11, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Administration’s Defiance: A Scathing AnalysisDefiance as PolicyJudicial FrustrationThe Constitutional InjuryConclusion: A Calculated Assault on the Rule of Law

Judges across the country are growing “increasingly furious and exhausted” as the Trump administration continues to openly defy court orders on mass immigration enforcement, prompting courts to proactively threaten to hold Trump administration officials in contempt, Politico reported Tuesday.

“These petitions are filed due to the illegal actions of Immigration and Customs Enforcement,” wrote U.S. District Judge Harvey Bartle III, an appointee of former President George H.W. Bush, in a particularly scathing memorandum this month.

“Despite hundreds of similar rulings in this and other courts resoundingly in favor of the ICE-detainee petitioners, ICE continues to act contrary to law, to spend taxpayer money needlessly, and to waste the scarce resources of the judiciary.”

And, Politico wrote, Trump’s lawyers are pushing the courts into imminent action.

“As a result, judges have issued more detailed and prescriptive orders to head off potential loopholes or hair-splitting results. And when all else fails, they threaten to hold administration officials in contempt,” the outlet wrote.

The Trump administration has been hard at work at its mass deportation efforts, having already arrested more than 328,000 migrants, more than 73% of whom have no criminal history, despite President Donald Trump’s past pledge to only target the “worst of the worst.” To achieve those numbers, the White House has instituted a daily arrest quota of 3,000, and has frequently defied orders by courts in an attempt to meet it.

The Trump administration’s defiance of the courts appears calculated, with detainees often transferred out of state shortly after their arrest, making it difficult for them to acquire legal representation or sue the district where they’re being held. These transfers also frequently take place after a judge has ordered immigration enforcement officials not to move a detainee.

When successfully sued over an immigration-related arrest, the Justice Department often doesn’t respond, missing deadlines and ignoring orders to release detained individuals. And, when a judge does order detained individuals to be released, ICE will sometimes take weeks to comply, an open act of defiance described by one federal judge as a “constitutional injury.”

“Detention without lawful authority is not just a technical defect, it is a constitutional injury that unfairly falls on the heads of those who have done nothing wrong to justify it,” said U.S. District Judge Jerry Blackwell in a hearing on Tuesday, Politico reported.

“The individuals affected are people. The overwhelming majority of the hundreds seen by this Court have been found to be lawfully present as of now in the country. They live in their communities. Some are separated from their families.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration’s Defiance: A Scathing Analysis

In a brazen display of contempt for the judiciary, the Trump administration’s immigration enforcement policies are not just pushing the envelope; they’re incinerating it. Politico’s recent exposé reveals a judiciary “increasingly furious and exhausted” by the administration’s flagrant disregard for legal orders. This isn’t just about policy disagreements or bureaucratic inertia; it’s a calculated assault on the rule of law.

Defiance as Policy

The heart of the matter lies in the administration’s mass deportation efforts, which have resulted in the arrest of over 328,000 migrants. Astonishingly, more than 73% of these individuals have no criminal history, blatantly contradicting President Trump’s pledge to target the “worst of the worst.” It’s as if the administration set up a daily arrest quota of 3,000 as a challenge to see how quickly it could undermine its own promises.

But it’s the method of defiance that truly astounds. Detainees are frequently whisked away out of state, complicating their legal defense and directly contravening court orders designed to keep them accessible. This maneuver isn’t just a logistical headache; it’s a calculated strategy to render the courts impotent.

Judicial Frustration

Judges, including U.S. District Judge Harvey Bartle III, are at their wits’ end. Bartle’s scathing memorandum decries the wasteful spending of taxpayer money and the needless drain on judiciary resources due to ICE’s “illegal actions.” The response from the bench has escalated, with courts now threatening to hold Trump administration officials in contempt. Yet, even with more detailed and prescriptive orders, the administration persists in its evasion.

The Constitutional Injury

Describing the situation as a mere policy misstep would be an understatement. It’s a constitutional crisis. Detention without lawful authority, as U.S. District Judge Jerry Blackwell notes, inflicts a “constitutional injury” on individuals who, in the overwhelming majority of cases, are lawfully present in the country. The administration’s actions represent not just a failure to respect the judiciary but a direct attack on the constitutional rights of individuals.

Conclusion: A Calculated Assault on the Rule of Law

The Politico report lays bare a disturbing reality: the Trump administration’s immigration enforcement strategy is not just about enforcing the law or protecting the country. It’s about testing the limits of executive power and challenging the very foundations of the judicial system. This is not governance; it’s anarchy cloaked in the guise of order.

As the judiciary grows “increasingly furious and exhausted,” one wonders how this battle will end. Will the courts find a way to enforce their will, or will the administration continue its defiance, eroding the rule of law one ignored order at a time? The stakes couldn’t be higher, and the outcome will define the balance of power in the U.S. for years to come.
