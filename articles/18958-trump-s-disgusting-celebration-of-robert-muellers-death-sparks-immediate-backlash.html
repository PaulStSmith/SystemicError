Within minutes of news breaking Saturday of former FBI Director Robert Mueller’s passing, President Donald Trump took to social media to celebrate his death in a shocking social media post, sparking immediate backlash from critics who called the president’s language “disgusting and despicable.”

“Robert Mueller just died. Good, I’m glad he’s dead,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

The remarks were immediately met with scorn from critics of all stripes, including prominent liberal political commentator Ed Krassenstein, who went on to lavish praise on Mueller for his role in leading the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election.

“This is disgusting and despicable,” Krassenstein wrote to their more than 1 million followers on X. “Trump literally just celebrated Robert Mueller dying. Mueller did so much good for America.”

Ken Dilanian, a justice and intelligence correspondent for MS NOW, responded to Trump’s remarks by making an unflattering comparison between the president and Mueller.

“In an era when many young men – including President Trump – were trying to avoid serving in Vietnam, Mueller not only volunteered for the Marines after graduating from Princeton – he spent a year waiting for an injured knee to heal so he could serve,” Dilanian wrote in a social media post on X. “I have always found that [to] be the most compelling fact about him.”

Trump infamously received a deferment from conscription in 1968 during the Vietnam War, a deferment that allowed the then-22-year-old future president to avoid military service. Furthermore, the deferment – a medical exemption citing alleged bone spurs in his heels – was issued to Trump by a podiatrist who rented office space from Trump’s father, a connection that led The New York Times to theorize that the medical diagnosis may have been “granted as a courtesy to the elder Mr. Trump.”

Others, like Fox sports analyst Ryan Satin, pointed to what they characterized as Trump’s double standard, having belittled Mueller’s death openly while his own administration has vowed to pull visas and deport people who joke about the death of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk.

“Remember when they made a database of people who said anything slightly deemed as negative about Charlie Kirk?” Satin wrote in a social media post on X

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth Behind Trump’s Reaction to Mueller’s Death

In the aftermath of former FBI Director Robert Mueller’s passing, a spectacle unfolded that deserves more than a cursory glance. President Donald Trump’s unabashed celebration of Mueller’s death on Truth Social ignited a firestorm of criticism. This reaction from Trump, while shocking to many, is not isolated but a continuation of his long-standing feud with Mueller, stemming from the latter’s investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election. As we dissect the situation, it becomes imperative to separate personal vendettas from institutional power dynamics and to scrutinize the real actors and their influence.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

At the heart of this controversy is Donald Trump, a former president and a current key figure in American politics, wielding considerable power not only through his political influence but also through his command over a significant segment of the population via platforms like Truth Social. However, it’s crucial to remember that his ability to influence policy or legal proceedings is markedly less than when he was in office. Robert Mueller, on the other hand, represented the institutional power of the FBI and, by extension, the justice system during his tenure as the Special Counsel overseeing the Russia investigation. His role was inherently institutional, with Mueller embodying the investigative powers of the federal government.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

The decision in question – Trump’s public statement on Mueller’s death – was made solely by Trump. The outcome, a widespread backlash, spans across political lines, highlighting the broader societal disdain for disrespect towards the deceased, irrespective of political affiliation. Mueller’s role as the leader of the investigation into Russian interference has been polarizing, but the respect for his service and integrity, as noted by commentators and critics, transcends the immediate political squabbles.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The framing of Trump’s remarks by various commentators and analysts, while focusing on his personal disdain for Mueller, potentially misdirects responsibility. It places Trump at the center, overshadowing the broader systemic issues at play – such as the polarization of American politics and the erosion of public discourse. While Trump’s comments are his own, the reaction and the coverage speak volumes about the current state of political discourse in the U.S.

Moreover, the mention of Trump’s deferment from the Vietnam War juxtaposed with Mueller’s service is a poignant reminder of the different paths and choices made by individuals in power. Yet, this comparison, while highlighting contrasting values, risks overshadowing the immediate issue at hand – the appropriateness of celebrating someone’s death.

Conclusion: Beyond Personal Vendettas

Trump’s comments on Mueller’s death, undoubtedly distasteful to many, reflect a personal vendetta rather than an exercise of institutional power. The reaction underscores a broader societal expectation for decorum in public discourse, especially concerning the deceased. While Trump’s influence remains significant, the institutional power dynamics at play are more reflective of his current status as a political figure rather than a direct actor within the government.

In analyzing this spectacle, we must be wary of attributing too much institutional significance to what is essentially a continuation of personal animosity. At the same time, the backlash offers a glimpse into the societal limits of acceptable discourse, even within the highly polarized landscape of American politics. The real story is not just about Trump’s disdain for Mueller but about the ongoing battle for the soul of public conversation in the face of declining political civility.
