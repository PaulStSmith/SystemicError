Ex-staffer raises alarm that 'clown' Laura Loomer is bypassing Trump's chief of staff

During an appearance on MSNBC on Sunday morning, the former deputy press secretary in Donald Trump's first administration expressed alarm that conservative gadfly Laura Loomer has the president's ear on hiring and firings.

Speaking with the hosts of MSNBC's 'The Weekend," Sarah Matthews claimed she was told that there would be a firewall around Trump in his second term to keep him from following his worst instincts because of outside advice.

Asked by co-host Eugene Daniels, "I've heard a little bit of rumblings of some of the staffers around President Trump, who are actually supposed to have power and influence, getting a little frustrated with this happening with the Laura Loomer's of the world with definitely Elon Musk?" Matthews expressed dismay.

"Well, you know, I was told that Susie Wiles was going to make sure that these kinds of clown-like figures weren't coming into the Oval Office or weren't getting on the phone with the president and having that kind of access to power," she replied.

"Obviously, it seems like you can only do so much; I think she has to kind of pick and choose her battles," she conceded. "And so with Laura Loomer, obviously she's still getting through to Trump, still being very influential in these hiring decisions or firing decisions."

"That is very scary to think that a far-right conspiracy theorist has the president's ear," she added. "And, unfortunately, she has a lot of power and some of these bad actors do too."

Our Analysis:

The Alarming Influence of Laura Loomer on Trump's Decision-Making

In a revealing and frankly alarming appearance on MSNBC, Sarah Matthews, the former deputy press secretary under Donald Trump's first term, shed light on the concerning influence figures like Laura Loomer have on the former president. This inside perspective not only highlights the questionable judgment of Trump but also raises significant concerns about the potential impacts on governance should he secure a second term.

The Firewall That Wasn't

Matthews' account debunks the myth of a so-called "firewall" around Trump, intended to shield him from his worst impulses and the advice of controversial figures. The fact that Laura Loomer, a name synonymous with far-right conspiracies, is not just whispering but shouting in the ears of the former president is a testament to the inefficacy of this firewall. The mere idea that individuals like Susie Wiles could somehow filter the influence of "clown-like figures" on Trump has proven to be wishful thinking at best.

The Power Behind the Throne?

What's particularly disturbing is the indication that Loomer, a well-documented far-right conspiracy theorist, is influencing hiring and firing decisions. This isn't merely about having the president's ear; it's about having his trust and wielding an undue amount of power from the shadows. The implications for policy-making and governance under such influence are dire, painting a picture of an administration more influenced by fringe ideologies than by sound, rational judgment.

The Bigger Picture

The revelation from Matthews brings to light a broader issue within Trump's circle and potentially his administration: the alarming ease with which far-right figures and conspiracy theorists gain access and influence. This isn't just a question of poor judgment or a lack of a robust advisory framework; it's a glaring red flag about the very principles that could underpin decision-making in a Trump-led government.

A Scary Precedent

The influence of individuals like Laura Loomer, as described by Matthews, sets a scary precedent for what a second Trump term could look like. The thought of far-right conspiracy theorists having direct lines to the Oval Office and shaping policy is a chilling prospect for any observer concerned with the integrity and effectiveness of governance.

In conclusion, Matthews' insights on MSNBC aren't just a peek behind the curtain; they're a blaring siren warning of the potential dangers lying ahead should Trump find his way back to the presidency. The influence of figures like Laura Loomer isn't just concerning; it's a clarion call for vigilance and opposition to ensure that the highest office in the land isn't compromised by the lowest standards of advice and influence.
