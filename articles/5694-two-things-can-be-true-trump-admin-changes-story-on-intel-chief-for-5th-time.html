'Two things can be true': Trump admin changes story on intel chief for 5th time

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 6, 20264 min read

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt speaks during a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 5, 2026. REUTERS/Al Drago

President Donald Trump’s administration has once again changed the reason Director of National Intelligence (DNI) Tulsi Gabbard was on site for the FBI’s raid at the Fulton County Election Hub and Operation Center in Georgia last month. The story has now changed five times, and three times in the past 24 hours.

CBS News reported Thursday that Gabbard’s spokesperson clarified the message after Trump told the National Prayer Breakfast that Attorney General Pam Bondi tasked Gabbard with going to Georgia. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt also argued Trump put election security under the DNI’s purview.

“As the president said, he asked for Director Gabbard to be there,” Gabbard spokesperson Alexa Henning wrote on X. “Attorney General Bondi also asked for her to be there. Two things can be true at the same time.”

As of publication, the president has not said he ordered it.

Trump told “NBC Nightly News“ anchor Tom Llamas about his conspiracy theory that he had not losts the 2020 election. Llamas asked Trump point-blank, “Why is Tulsi Gabbard there?”

“I don’t know,” Trump answered. “But a lot of the cheating comes from, it’s international cheating.”

Gabbard was the one who claimed Trump ordered.

In a letter to Democratic lawmakers Monday, Gabbard said her presence “was requested by the president.”

Less than 24 hours after Trump’s interview, the story changed again.

While he was speaking at the National Prayer Breakfast, Trump gushed over Gabbard for attending the raid.

“She took a lot of heat two days ago because she went in at Pam’s insistence,” the president said, referring to Attorney General Pam Bondi.

“They say, ‘why is she doing it’… because Pam wanted her to do it. And you know why? Because she’s smart,” Trump added.

The story has changed radically in the past week. The first to comment on Gabbard was Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, who told reporters that Gabbard just happened to be in Atlanta when the raid unfolded.

Our Analysis:

The Ever-Changing Narrative: Trump Administration’s Shifting Story on Gabbard’s Presence

In what can only be described as a political circus, the Trump administration’s explanation for Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard’s presence at the FBI’s raid in Georgia has morphed more times than a chameleon in a kaleidoscope. The storyline has not just shifted; it has practically performed acrobatics, changing five times, with three of those changes happening in the past 24 hours alone. If you’re feeling dizzy trying to keep up, you’re not alone.

The Whirlwind of Explanations

CBS News brought to light the latest twist in this saga, with Gabbard’s spokesperson attempting to clarify the muddy waters after Trump’s comments at the National Prayer Breakfast. Initially, it was Attorney General Pam Bondi who supposedly requested Gabbard’s attendance in Georgia. Then, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt chimed in, saying Trump had placed election security within the DNI’s responsibilities. According to Gabbard’s spokesperson, Alexa Henning, “Two things can be true at the same time.” However, this attempt at clarification only muddies the water further.

In a bewildering turn of events, Trump, in an interview with “NBC Nightly News,” claimed ignorance about Gabbard’s reasons for attending the raid, instead spiraling into a conspiracy theory about international election cheating. Yet, less than a day earlier, Gabbard had asserted in a letter to Democratic lawmakers that her presence was at the president’s request.

The National Prayer Breakfast Confession

Trump’s remarks at the National Prayer Breakfast added yet another layer of confusion. Praising Gabbard, he attributed her involvement to Pam Bondi’s insistence, contradicting previous statements. It’s a classic move in the Trump playbook: when caught in a bind, shift the narrative and hope it sticks.

The Initial Comment

Let’s not forget the origin of this narrative rollercoaster. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche initially stated that Gabbard was merely in Atlanta coincidentally when the raid happened. This casual remark has since spiraled into an ever-evolving story that the administration can’t seem to keep straight.

Analysis

What we’re witnessing is not just a failure in communication within the Trump administration; it’s a glaring display of political improvisation. With every contradictory statement, the administration undermines its credibility, leaving the public to wonder if there’s any truth to be found in this chaos. This isn’t just about Gabbard’s presence at a raid; it’s about an administration’s apparent inability to coordinate a consistent narrative.

The shifting stories reflect a deeper issue: a lack of transparency and accountability. It’s one thing for an administration to adjust its messaging based on new information; it’s another to rewrite the narrative so frequently that the truth becomes unrecognizable. This isn’t just poor communication; it’s a strategic blurring of facts and fiction.

In conclusion, the Trump administration’s handling of the Tulsi Gabbard saga is a masterclass in obfuscation and misdirection. As the story continues to evolve, one thing remains clear: in this administration, the truth is as elusive as ever. What’s needed is not another explanation but a commitment to honesty and clarity—qualities sorely missing in this convoluted narrative.
