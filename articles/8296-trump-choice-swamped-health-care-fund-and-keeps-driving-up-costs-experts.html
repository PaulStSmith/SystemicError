Trump choice 'swamped' health care fund and keeps driving up costs: experts

The Trump administration has missed the “root cause” of ongoing health care costs, an economics expert warned.

Vivian Ho, the chair of health economics at Rice University’s Baker Institute for Public Policy, suggested costs are running so high in health care because of policies brought in by President Donald Trump and his team. Speaking with Politico, Ho suggested there’s “been no break” for health care to recover from the turbulent economy under the Trump administration.

“It’s just accumulated over time, and there’s been no break,” Ho said. “The Trump economy is not focusing on the root causes of why health care costs are so high in this country.”

Amandu Chu, who wrote the Politico piece, shared that Trump’s administrative choices had led to a spike in financial assistance from the HealthWell Foundation. It marks a 23 percent rise in year on year requests. Chu wrote, “Requests swamped the fund it launched this month to help consumers offset higher Obamacare premiums that are likely if the subsidies expire, prompting it to stop taking new applicants after just two days.”

“The Colorectal Cancer Alliance has seen a 26 percent increase in requests year-over-year, and CancerCare, another charity, has seen a 10 percent increase in year-over-year requests.”

HealthWell chief executive Michael Heimall has said these spikes will increase in future. He said, “I don’t see the demand curve flattening out in the foreseeable future.”

“As economic things tighten, your donors have to make decisions about if they are going to donate to a charity or are they going to invest in some of the things they need to invest in?”

White House spokesperson Kush Desai has blamed the Democrats for higher health care costs. Desai said, “Democrats’ push to maintain these high prices by giving more money to insurance companies is not a real solution for President Trump.”

“The President has instead focused on lowering prescription drug costs by hammering out deals with pharmaceutical companies, as well as taking on waste, fraud and abuse in the system to deliver results for patients.”

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Blame Game and the Real Culprits Behind Skyrocketing Healthcare Costs

In an era where the blame game seems to be the default strategy for political mishaps, the Trump administration’s handling of healthcare costs is a textbook example of how not to address a systemic issue. The recent article by Amandu Chu, citing economics expert Vivian Ho, sheds light on the fundamental misunderstandings and misdirections that have characterized the administration’s approach to healthcare.

The Root Cause Ignorance

First off, Vivian Ho’s assertion that the Trump administration has missed the “root cause” of ongoing health care costs is a glaring indictment of their policy direction. It’s not just about missing the target; it’s about not even aiming for the right one. The continuous escalation of healthcare costs under Trump’s watch is not a symptom of temporary economic turbulence but a consequence of deep-seated policy errors.

The Trump Economy and Healthcare: A Toxic Relationship

The article points out the turbulent economy under the Trump administration as a contributing factor to the healthcare crisis. But let’s be clear: the economy didn’t just become turbulent on its own. The policies enacted, or in many cases, dismantled by Trump and his team have directly contributed to this instability. The notion that there’s “been no break” for healthcare to recover is not just an observation; it’s a scathing critique of a failure to provide any semblance of relief or direction.

The Spike in Financial Assistance Requests: A Symptom of a Larger Disease

Chu’s observations regarding the spike in financial assistance requests, with the HealthWell Foundation and other charities experiencing significant increases, are particularly damning. It’s a direct consequence of policies that have made healthcare unaffordable for many. This isn’t just about numbers; it’s about people being forced to seek charity for essential healthcare needs because the system has failed them. The response from HealthWell chief executive Michael Heimall, anticipating no flattening of the demand curve, is a bleak forecast for the future of American healthcare.

The White House’s Deflection Strategy

Kush Desai’s comments blaming the Democrats for higher healthcare costs are a classic deflection. It’s akin to blaming the fire department for water damage while ignoring the arsonist. The claim that Democrats’ efforts to provide more money to insurance companies are the problem is not just misleading; it’s an outright distortion of the broader policy failures at play. The Trump administration’s focus on lowering prescription drug costs, while commendable, does not address the systemic issues that drive healthcare costs up.

Conclusion: Misdirection and Missed Opportunities

The Trump administration’s handling of healthcare is a case study in misdirection and missed opportunities. Instead of addressing the root causes of high healthcare costs, such as inefficiencies, lack of regulation, and the need for systemic reform, the administration has chosen to blame others and focus on narrow policy areas that, while important, do not tackle the systemic nature of the problem. The real solution lies in comprehensive reform that addresses the systemic inefficiencies, not in the simplistic blaming of political opponents or minor policy tweaks.

As we dissect the failures and misdirections of the Trump administration’s healthcare policies, it’s crucial to remember that the stakes are incredibly high. This is not just about political points; it’s about the health and well-being of millions of Americans. The time for blame games is over. What’s needed is a bold, systemic approach to healthcare reform that truly addresses the root causes of high costs and ensures that all Americans have access to the care they need.
