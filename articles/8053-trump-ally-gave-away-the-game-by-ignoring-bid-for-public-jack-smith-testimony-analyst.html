Trump ally 'gave away the game' by ignoring bid for public Jack Smith testimony: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of Transparency: A Study in Irony

Jack Smith (image from Wikimedia Creative Commons)

House Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan (R-OH) “prefers secrecy to sunlight” when it comes to his probe of former counsel Jack Smith, writes MS NOW’s Steve Benen.

House Republicans, led by Jordan, subpoenaed Smith for a closed-door deposition on December 17, 2025, as part of their oversight investigation into his probes into President Donald Trump’s efforts to overturn the 2020 election and his handling of classified documents.

Smith’s lawyers have stated he volunteered to appear for an open, public hearing but will comply with the deposition request.

On Capitol Hill, GOP lawmakers remain eager to treat Smith like a “punching bag,” Benen writes.

“Rep. Michael Rulli of Ohio recently accused the prosecutor of acts that he said bordered on ‘treason[,]’ Sen. Marsha Blackburn referred Smith to the Justice Department for a misconduct investigation, based on misguided allegations the Tennessee Republican did not appear to understand[,] and several GOP members have even pushed for Smith’s disbarment,” he notes.

Jordan sent a letter to Smith to demand his closed-door testimony and NBC news reports that Smith’s lawyer Peter Koski said he’s “disappointed ... Republicans rejected the Trump prosecutor’s offer to provide public testimony.”

“Jack looks forward to meeting with the committee later this month to discuss his work and clarify the various misconceptions about his investigation,” Koski tells NBC.

Benen says that “Smith clearly wanted more transparency, but he wasn’t in a position to dictate the terms.”

The New York Times reported that House Republicans “have been reluctant to give [Smith] a prime public platform out of concern that he could embarrass Trump by making a compelling case for the indictments over the president’s attempt to overturn the 2020 election and his retention of classified documents.”

These concerns, Benen writes, “gave away the game,” adding that “Smith is an experienced, credible and capable prosecutor who’s familiar with Trump’s criminal cases at a granular level.”

“The more Republicans drag him back into the spotlight, the more Smith is positioned to remind the public not only of the variety of alleged presidential felonies, but also of evidence the party would prefer to forget,” Benen says.

While Trump says he, too, would rather see Smith testify in public, Jordan isn’t keen on it at all.

“Jordan will likely ignore the White House’s preference, even as the president probably ought to be careful what he wishes for,” he concludes.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of Transparency: A Study in Irony

House Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan’s latest maneuver in the political chess game against former counsel Jack Smith is nothing short of a master class in ironic governance. Preferring the shadows to the spotlight, Jordan’s insistence on a closed-door deposition, as highlighted by MS NOW’s Steve Benen, starkly contradicts the often-touted Republican mantra of transparency and accountability.

Secrecy Over Sunlight

At the heart of this contradiction is the subpoena for Smith to appear in a setting far removed from public scrutiny on December 17, 2025. Despite Smith’s own lawyers stating his willingness to testify in an open hearing, the House Republicans, under Jordan’s lead, have opted for opacity. The reason? It appears that the GOP harbors fears that Smith, with his deep understanding and involvement in the investigations into Donald Trump’s actions post-2020 election, might just air too much dirty laundry.

“Punching Bag” Politics

The GOP’s strategy, as Benen astutely points out, seems to revolve around using Smith as a political punching bag. This approach is underscored by various attacks and accusations from GOP members, ranging from claims of “treason” by Rep. Michael Rulli to misguided calls for misconduct investigations by Sen. Marsha Blackburn. Such actions suggest a desperate attempt to discredit Smith before he has a chance to publicly articulate his findings and insights.

The Fear of Transparency

The irony thickens with the revelation from The New York Times that GOP reluctance to grant Smith a public platform is fueled by fear of embarrassment for Trump. This fear inadvertently acknowledges the potential strength and credibility of Smith’s case against the former president. It’s a telling sign when a party’s strategy hinges on suppression of information rather than the robust defense of its figurehead.

The Trump Dilemma

Adding another layer of irony is Trump’s own preference for Smith’s public testimony, a stance seemingly at odds with Jordan’s. This divergence hints at a complex calculus within the Republican camp, balancing the desire for public vindication against the risk of damning revelations.

Conclusion: The Cost of Secrecy

In the final analysis, Jordan’s preference for a closed-door deposition over an open public hearing speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse. It reflects a preference for narrative control over genuine transparency, revealing a troubling discomfort with the very principles of accountability and open governance that many politicians are quick to champion but slow to practice.

As this saga unfolds, it serves as a potent reminder of the power of transparency to both clarify and convict. The GOP’s maneuvering around Smith’s testimony not only undermines their own stated values but also deprives the public of a critical opportunity to witness, firsthand, the workings of justice and accountability in their government. The question then becomes: What are they so afraid of? The answer, it seems, lies in the shadows they so zealously guard.
