'Challenging situation for Trump': Marjorie Taylor Greene may run for another major office

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Political Chessboard: Greene, Trump, and Georgia's Gubernatorial Race

U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) looks on, ahead of President Donald Trump delivering remarks on tariffs, in the Rose Garden at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 2, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

Despite officially pulling out of Georgia's Republican U.S. Senate primary this week, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) may still seek a higher office next year — and some Republicans believe President Donald Trump may feel the need to intervene.

Semafor reported Friday that Greene may throw her hat in the ring in next year's gubernatorial race in the Peach State, as current Republican Governor Brian Kemp is term-limited and will have to leave office at the end of next year. One unnamed Republican strategist told the outlet that this "could create a challenging situation for Trump," given that he was apparently involved in making sure she didn't follow through on her talk of running for Senate.

“All the polling shows I blow out a primary for governor or Senate,” Greene said after abandoning her Senate plans. “I think it’s smart for me to consider that, having so much support in the state of Georgia.”

While Greene's MAGA bonafides are likely sufficient to win a Republican primary, that could also be her undoing in a general election – particularly in a purple state like Georgia, which in 2021 elected two Democratic U.S. senators for the first time in decades. A different Republican operative told Semafor that they "still think [Greene] would have significant liabilities in the general election" in Georgia.

The Wall Street Journal reported this week that Trump pollster Tony Fabrizio commissioned the poll showing that Greene would lose a hypothetical 2026 general election matchup against Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-Ga.) by 18 points. And before Greene announced she wasn't running for Senate, Gov. Kemp was reportedly planning to meet with Trump at the White House to discuss who to unite behind in order to make sure Greene wouldn't advance to the general election.

Greene is apparently not worried about whether Trump ultimately endorses her, should she run for governor next year.

“I don’t make my decisions that way,” the far-right Georgia Republican told Semafor. “I didn’t have the president’s endorsement the first time I ran, and I beat eight men in the primary and a neurosurgeon in the runoff.”

Our Analysis:

The Political Chessboard: Greene, Trump, and Georgia's Gubernatorial Race

In a move that shocks no one but alarms many, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) has once again teased the possibility of seeking higher office, this time setting her sights on the gubernatorial throne of Georgia. After bowing out of the state's Republican U.S. Senate primary, Greene's political ambitions remain undeterred, hinting at a gubernatorial run in a state that's increasingly becoming a battleground. But the plot thickens with former President Donald Trump's potential intervention, creating a scenario ripe for political analysis.

Trump's Dilemma: The former president finds himself in a political quagmire. Having reportedly played a role in dissuading Greene from a Senate run, Trump's influence over Greene's gubernatorial aspirations remains to be seen. His endorsement—or lack thereof—could be pivotal, yet Greene's defiance in basing her decisions on Trump's support signals a complicated relationship. This dynamic underscores the fractured nature of Republican politics, where allegiance to Trump is both a badge of honor and a potential albatross.

Greene's Delusional Confidence: Greene's assertion that all polling indicates she would "blow out" a primary for governor or Senate is a masterclass in political hubris. Despite her confidence, the reality on the ground tells a different story. A poll commissioned by Trump pollster Tony Fabrizio paints a grim picture for Greene, showing her losing a hypothetical general election matchup against Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-Ga.) by a staggering 18 points. This disconnect between Greene's self-assessment and empirical data is a hallmark of her political career, defined by controversy and detachment from reality.

The MAGA Albatross: While Greene's staunch MAGA credentials might endear her to the Republican base, they're a double-edged sword in a general election, especially in a purple state like Georgia. The state's political landscape has shifted, evidenced by the election of two Democratic U.S. senators in 2021. Greene's far-right positions and incendiary rhetoric, while galvanizing a segment of the GOP, are liabilities in a broader electorate. This dichotomy between primary success and general election viability is a recurring dilemma for the Republican Party, with Greene as its latest embodiment.

Kemp's Preemptive Strike: The reported meeting between Gov. Brian Kemp and Trump, intended to unite behind a candidate to block Greene's advancement, illustrates the strategic calculations within the GOP. Kemp's maneuvering highlights the party's internal struggle to balance extremist elements with broader electoral appeal. This episode is a microcosm of the larger Republican quandary: how to navigate the Trump era's aftermath without alienating the base or the middle ground.

In conclusion, Greene's potential gubernatorial bid is more than just political theater—it's a litmus test for the Republican Party's direction in post-Trump America. Her candidacy would not only challenge Trump's sway over the party but also force a confrontation with the GOP's identity crisis. As Georgia becomes a focal point in this turbulent saga, the stakes couldn't be higher. The outcome will reverberate far beyond the Peach State, signaling whether the Republican Party can chart a course that reconciles its internal factions or if it will continue to be ensnared by the divisive politics of figures like Greene and Trump.
