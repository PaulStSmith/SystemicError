Rebel Republican claims billionaire in Epstein's 'black book' is targeting him

Republican Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) plans to take steps Tuesday to push for a vote to release the Jeffrey Epstein files — and suggested a shadowy billionaire in the famous "black book" is out to get him.

Lawmakers are back from summer recess, and Massie and Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) were expected to file a discharge petition aimed at compelling the Department of Justice to fully release all files related to the disgraced financier's case. The bipartisan effort is underway from the House Oversight Committee to uncover the files despite pushback from Republican leaders.

"Jobs, the economy, those are always the big issues, but you really can't solve any of that if this place is corrupt," Massie told CNN.

CNN reporter Manu Raju asked, "What do you mean, if this place is corrupt?"

"Well, I mean, if they're going to cover up for rich and powerful and politically connected people, which is still what is happening right now here," Massie said.

Massie, who has faced off with House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and the Trump administration over calling for the release of the files, continues to face attacks.

"Look, I got three billionaires right now running ads against me in Kentucky, and one of them is in Epstein's black book. So I'm not worried about it," Massie said. "I've already poked the hornet's nest here, and, you know, once you're in for a penny, you're in for a pound. We're going to get these files released."

Anti-Massie super PAC MAGA KY has drawn $2 million from donors through June and the end of August, Politico reports. That includes $1 million from Paul Singer, $250,000 from hedge fund manager John Paulson, and $750,000 from Preserve America PAC, another super PAC connected to Miriam Adelson that backed Trump in the election.

At least five Republicans, plus all Democrats, are needed to vote in favor of releasing the Epstein files, which could push the measure forward.

Massie believes there will be enough votes — and that considerable pressure is coming from the White House for other GOP members not to side with him on the move.

The House Oversight Committee is scheduled to meet on Tuesday with victims of the disgraced financier who died in 2019 while awaiting trial on human-trafficking charges and is alleged to have run a blackmail operation targeting powerful figures.

Survivors have indicated that they want the files released, Massie said.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of the Epstein Files Release Initiative

In an era where political divisions cut deep, the bipartisan effort led by Republican Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) to compel the Department of Justice to release all files related to Jeffrey Epstein's case is a striking anomaly. This initiative, however, is not just a quest for transparency; it's a testament to the murky waters of political corruption, power plays, and the lengths some will go to keep the skeletons in their closets.

The Unlikely Alliance and the Shadow of Corruption

At first glance, the collaboration between Massie, a Republican, and Khanna, a Democrat, is commendable. Bipartisanship is a rare gem in today's political climate, yet their united front underscores a grim reality: the battle against corruption transcends party lines. Massie’s statement to CNN, “Jobs, the economy, those are always the big issues, but you really can't solve any of that if this place is corrupt,” hits the nail on the head. It’s a stark reminder that economic policies and job creation efforts are moot if the core of the political system is rotten.

The Billionaire's Shadow

Massie's assertion that three billionaires, including one listed in Epstein's infamous "black book," are funding campaigns against him, is a chilling revelation of how money can be weaponized in politics. The involvement of individuals like Paul Singer and entities such as the MAGA KY super PAC, which amassed $2 million for anti-Massie efforts, exemplifies the dark underbelly of political financing. It begs the question: What are they afraid of? The answer, it seems, is transparency.

The Political Roadblocks

Despite the bipartisan push, the effort faces resistance from within the Republican leadership, with considerable pressure reportedly coming from the White House itself. This resistance is not just a hurdle; it's a glaring red flag. It signals a profound fear among the political elite about what the Epstein files might reveal. The fact that survivors of Epstein's alleged human-trafficking ring want the files released only adds a layer of moral urgency to the initiative.

The Heart of the Matter

The scheduled House Oversight Committee meeting with Epstein’s victims is not just procedural; it’s symbolic. It represents a sliver of hope for justice in a saga that has long been shrouded in secrecy and speculation. The potential release of the Epstein files is about more than just unveiling the illicit activities of a disgraced financier; it’s about exposing the systemic corruption that allowed his crimes to go unchecked for so long.

In Conclusion

The effort to release the Jeffrey Epstein files is a pivotal moment in the ongoing struggle against political corruption. It's a battle that pits integrity against influence, transparency against secrecy. The bipartisan nature of this initiative is a beacon of hope, suggesting that some issues are so fundamentally about right and wrong that they can unite even the most divided of political adversaries.

Yet, as Massie and Khanna prepare to push forward, they do so against a backdrop of formidable opposition, funded by deep pockets and vested interests. The resistance they face is not just a barrier to transparency; it's a testament to the power dynamics that govern our political system. The outcome of this effort will not only determine the fate of the Epstein files but could also serve as a litmus test for the health of American democracy. Will we allow the rich and powerful to dictate the flow of information, or will we demand the accountability and transparency that is the bedrock of a truly democratic society? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the fight for the Epstein files is a fight for the soul of American politics.
