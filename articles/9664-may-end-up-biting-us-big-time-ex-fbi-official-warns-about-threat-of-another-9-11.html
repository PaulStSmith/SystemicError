'May end up biting us big time': Ex-FBI official warns about threat of another 9/11

Reacting to a new report that the FBI under Director Kash Patel is shifting a massive amount of manpower to helping the Department of Homeland Security track down undocumented immigrants, two former bureau officials claimed there is now an increased risk of a terrorist attack in the US.

According to a report that was issued by Sen. Mark Warner (R-VA), who sits on the Senate Intelligence committee, which he shared with the Guardian, an estimated 45 percent of FBI agents in the country’s 25 largest field offices are now working on immigrant roundups, and 23 percent of the nearly 13,000 total agents are assisting Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

After the Guardian’s Sam Levin shared those numbers with former FBI official Kenneth Gray, who now teaches in the University of New Haven’s criminal justice department, the FBI veteran expressed alarm that important investigations that the FBI is normally tasked with are unmanned and therefore posing a threat to national safety.

The 24-year veteran of the agency explained, “The bureau can withstand a temporary change in its priorities, but in the long-term, if agents are continuing to work immigration matters, as opposed to counterterrorism, foreign counterintelligence or cybercrimes, that may end up biting us big time.”

He added, “The next 9/11 might happen if agents who were working on counter-terrorism have been diverted.”

According to the Guardian report, “Mike German, a former FBI agent and civil liberties advocate, said it was unprecedented for the bureau to redirect this many agents to a mission that is not part of the FBI’s mandate.”

German, now an FBI whistleblower, also said there may be an ulterior motive to the shift of manpower.

“Part of the reason FBI leadership would be doing this at such scale is to separate those two – to identify who are the loyalists and who are potential impediments to the administration’s goals,” he explained.

He also added that the agents being reassigned are not trained for their current mission under the Donald Trump administration.

“Just jumping in an SUV with a bunch of armed men and rolling around the streets until you see someone running away is inherently a more dangerous type of activity they are not very well trained for,” he suggested.

The Guardian’s Levin added, “There has been extensive documentation of the violent and indiscriminate nature of the Ice raids, which have often been carried out by masked men and have at times led to the detention of US citizens, and adding FBI personnel to the mix could exacerbate the chaos and potential for abuses, the former agent said.”

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Misallocation of FBI Resources

In a startling revelation, a recent report indicates the FBI, under Director Kash Patel, has diverted a staggering proportion of its manpower towards supporting the Department of Homeland Security in tracking down undocumented immigrants. This shift, as detailed by Sen. Mark Warner (R-VA) and reported by the Guardian, illustrates not just a misdirection of the FBI’s core mission but a potential peril to national security.

A Stark Transformation

The figures are alarming: 45 percent of FBI agents in the country’s 25 largest field offices are now embroiled in immigrant roundups, with 23 percent of the nearly 13,000 total agents assisting Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). This isn’t just a minor reallocation of resources; it’s a wholesale transformation of the FBI’s day-to-day operations, redirecting a significant portion of its workforce away from its foundational roles in counterterrorism, foreign counterintelligence, and cybercrimes.

The Cost of Misplaced Priorities

The consequences of this shift could be dire. Former FBI official Kenneth Gray, a 24-year veteran of the agency, didn’t mince words when he suggested that this could lead to the next major terrorist attack on U.S. soil. The logic is straightforward and chilling: if the FBI’s best and brightest are chasing down undocumented immigrants instead of thwarting potential threats, who’s guarding the gate?

A Question of Motive

The reassignment of such a large number of agents to tasks outside their mandate raises eyebrows, not just about strategy but about underlying motives. Mike German, a former FBI agent and now a whistleblower, suggests an insidious goal: to sift through the ranks for loyalists and weed out those who might oppose the administration’s goals. This is a dark insinuation, implying a level of politicization within the FBI that should give pause to any observer of American democracy.

Untrained for the Task at Hand

Moreover, these agents, trained in the intricacies of federal law enforcement and counterintelligence, are now being asked to perform duties for which they are not adequately prepared. The image of FBI agents, untrained in immigration enforcement, participating in potentially violent and chaotic ICE raids is not just incongruous; it’s inherently dangerous.

The Risk of Indiscriminate Action

The Guardian’s reporting adds another layer of concern, highlighting the violent and often indiscriminate nature of ICE raids. The involvement of FBI personnel, many of whom may lack the training for such operations, could only amplify the chaos and potential for rights abuses. The detention of U.S. citizens during these raids is a glaring testament to their indiscriminate nature, raising serious questions about the legality and morality of such operations.

Conclusion

The redirection of FBI resources towards immigration enforcement is not just a shift in priorities; it’s a dangerous gamble with the nation’s security. The potential consequences of sidelining crucial counterterrorism and counterintelligence work cannot be overstated. Moreover, the implications of using such reassignments to gauge loyalty within the ranks of the FBI are deeply troubling, suggesting a politicization at odds with the bureau’s mission to uphold the law impartially.

As the details of this strategy unfold, one must question: at what cost does this redirection come, not only in terms of national security but to the very fabric of American democratic institutions? The answer, it seems, is one that we cannot afford.
