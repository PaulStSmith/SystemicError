'The president is in a hell of a pickle right now' with little way out: Axios founder

Former U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to an audience at the “American Freedom Tour” event in Memphis, Tennessee, U.S., June 18, 2022. REUTERS/Karen Pulfer Focht

During a break from discussing the Donald Trump administration's growing battle with the judiciary, Axios founder Jim VandeHei reminded the panel on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" that the president has a lot on his plate right now with problems of his own making.

According to the Washington insider, Trump's tariff war has done what appears to be irreparable damage to the reputation of the U.S. and he has put himself into a box he can't escape from to get the economy back on track.

Noting Trump has been facing an extraordinary amount of push back from Wall Street, VandeHei explained, "I think the president blinked in a pretty substantial way last week because of this pressure," he began. "It's pressure from CEOs. It's pressure from investors."

"Listen, if you're running a business and someone who does run a business the last thing you want is unpredictability, right?" he continued. "And there's a tremendous amount of unpredictability. So when companies start to say we're going to freeze hiring or they have to wait to figure out what's happening at the port with shipments of goods, that has a tremendous effect on that company and ultimately on the consumer."

"I think that the president is in a hell of a pickle right now, and I don't actually know how he's going to solve it," he asserted. "The Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessett's theory now would be the best possible outcome is that you use a very messy situation to leverage European countries and South Korea and Japan and others to create almost a free trade zone with no tariffs between all of us in exchange for not doing business with China."

"Okay, in theory, that sounds like a great idea," he added skeptically. "Except for if you're a European company or you're a European country, or you're South Korea or you're Vietnam, do you trust that the United States now is a more trustworthy partner?"

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tango: A Dance of Desperation

In a riveting episode of MSNBC's "Morning Joe", Axios founder Jim VandeHei cuts to the chase, illuminating the self-inflicted chaos reigning supreme in the Trump administration, particularly focusing on the president's disastrous tariff war. This isn't just any opinion piece; it's a surgical dissection of a policy blunder with far-reaching consequences. Trump's trade maneuvers, described by VandeHei, have not only tarnished the U.S.'s reputation but also ensnared the economy in a labyrinthine mess from which escape seems like a Herculean task.

The Self-Made Quagmire

Let's start with the basics: Trump's tariff war is a classic case of shooting oneself in the foot, then wondering why it's difficult to run. VandeHei hits the nail on the head when he mentions the "irreparable damage" to the U.S.'s standing. It's not just about economics; it's about the global perception of the U.S. as a reliable, stable partner in international trade. The damage done isn't superficial; it's deep, possibly long-lasting, and most definitely self-inflicted.

A President Cornered by His Own Policies

VandeHei captures the essence of Trump's predicament with the phrase, "a lot on his plate right now with problems of his own making." The imagery is apt. Trump, seemingly, has bitten off more than he can chew, and now, the indigestion of his own policies is causing discomfort not just within his administration but also among the broader U.S. populace and its economy.

Wall Street Says, "Enough!"

The reaction from Wall Street, as highlighted by VandeHei, speaks volumes. It's not just a whisper of dissent; it's a clamorous rejection of unpredictability and chaos. When CEOs and investors are pushing back hard, it's a clear sign that the president's policies are not in the nation's best economic interest. The freeze on hiring and the anxiety over shipments of goods are not mere inconveniences; they are symptoms of a deeper economic malaise induced by erratic policy decisions.

Theoretical Solutions and Real Distrust

VandeHei's skepticism towards Treasury Secretary Scott Bessett's proposed solution—a messy leverage play to create a tariff-free zone excluding China—is not just warranted, it's necessary. The proposal, while theoretically appealing, ignores a fundamental flaw in Trump's foreign policy approach: trust. After the capriciousness displayed by the Trump administration, expecting European countries, South Korea, Vietnam, or any other nation to view the U.S. as a "trustworthy partner" is, to put it mildly, a stretch.

Conclusion: A Dance of Desperation

In essence, VandeHei's analysis on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" isn't just a critique; it's a cautionary tale of what happens when short-sighted policies collide with the complex realities of global economics. Trump's tariff war, portrayed as a bold move by its architects, is unraveling as a dance of desperation. The steps are misaligned, the rhythm is off, and the global audience is not amused. The question that looms large, as VandeHei aptly puts it, is not just how Trump plans to solve this conundrum, but whether he can do so without further jeopardizing the U.S.'s economic and diplomatic standing.
