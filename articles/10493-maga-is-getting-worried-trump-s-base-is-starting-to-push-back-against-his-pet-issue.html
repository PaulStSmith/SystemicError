'MAGA is getting worried': Trump's 'base is starting to push back' against his pet issue

Donald Trump's "populist base is starting to push back" against him, according to new reporting.

In an article called "Trump loves AI, and the MAGA world is getting worried," Politico reported, "President Donald Trump’s AI action plan has set off a backlash from some of the biggest figures in the America First movement — a rift expected to shape the next round of arguments in Congress about how to turbocharge the technology."

"Trump’s rush toward AI is exposing an important faultline in the Republican coalition: Many of its voters and leaders deeply mistrust the power of Big Tech, but Trump himself has worked closely with industry CEOs to deliver on their priorities," the report states.

It's true that Trump has been a more bold ally to the tech titans than many of those in his MAGA base would like. Some prominent critics of Big Tech within MAGA include Laura Loomer and Marjorie Taylor Greene, the latter of whom was mentioned in the piece by Politico.

According to the outlet, "Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) fired off a tirade on X, complaining that AI could create mass poverty by replacing human jobs, and giant AI data centers could have potentially devastating effects on the environment and water supply."

"In the days that followed, GOP strategist Steven Bannon chimed in, comparing the pursuit of AI superintelligence to 'summoning the demon.' And since then, think-tankers and populist conservative outlets have continued to stoke worries about federal policies that turbocharge AI development," according to Politico's reporting.

But that's not all, according to the report.

"On stage at the National Conservatism conference in Washington in early September, Sen. Josh Hawley (R-Mo.) criticized the AI revolution as a leap towards transhumanism — a human-machine future that he said is currently against 'the working man' and the teachings of the Bible, as well as installing 'a rich and powerful elite,'" it states.

Our Analysis:

Trump's AI Ambitions vs. MAGA's Big Tech Distrust

In a fascinating turn of events, Donald Trump’s enthusiastic charge towards artificial intelligence (AI) development is causing a significant rift within his own populist base. Politico's coverage, under the title "Trump loves AI, and the MAGA world is getting worried," lays bare the internal contradictions of the Trump coalition, providing a textbook example of the complex interplay between political leadership and base expectations.

The MAGA Base Pushes Back

It's clear that the MAGA movement, which has often prided itself on its skepticism of mainstream and technocratic elites, finds itself at odds with Trump's AI action plan. This plan, it seems, is too cozy by half with the very tech titans that many in the MAGA crowd love to hate. Figures like Laura Loomer and Marjorie Taylor Greene have become the vocal critics of this initiative, with Greene particularly highlighting potential socio-economic and environmental catastrophes as byproducts of unchecked AI development.

Steven Bannon's comparison of pursuing AI superintelligence to "summoning the demon" encapsulates the apocalyptic tone of the criticism. This isn't just opposition; it's a fundamentally different vision of the future, one that views AI not as a tool for progress but as a harbinger of societal collapse.

The Faultline Exposed

Trump's rush towards AI is not just a policy decision; it's a revelation of a deep-seated faultline within the Republican coalition. On one side, we have the tech-optimistic Trump, willing to work closely with Silicon Valley to push the boundaries of AI. On the other, a faction within his base deeply mistrusts Big Tech's power and intentions. This rift underscores a broader tension between populist rhetoric and the realities of governing in an increasingly digital world.

The Economic and Ethical Dimensions

Critics like Greene and Hawley don't just see potential economic disruptions from AI, such as mass unemployment; they're also raising ethical and philosophical concerns. Hawley's remarks at the National Conservatism conference about AI leading towards transhumanism and favoring "a rich and powerful elite" touch on deeper fears about the loss of human dignity and autonomy.

Conclusion: A Political Quagmire

Trump's AI enthusiasm places him in a political quagmire, caught between advancing a technology that he believes will propel America forward and placating a base that sees this very advancement as a threat to their values and livelihoods. This situation is emblematic of a larger issue within contemporary conservatism: the struggle to reconcile a desire for technological and economic dominance with a populist ethos that distrusts the mechanisms required to achieve it.

Politico's reporting on this internal conflict within the MAGA world is not just a snapshot of a moment in Republican politics; it's a window into the ongoing tension between modernity and tradition, between the future and the past. It remains to be seen how Trump navigates this rift, but one thing is clear: the road ahead is fraught with ideological landmines.
