Elon Musk panics as DOGE-cut bodies start piling up: 'He's in damage control mode'

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 24, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Billionaire Power, Human Cost

The Power Was Not Abstract

This story is not about a noisy billionaire having an online tantrum. It is about Elon Musk exercising real institutional power inside the Trump administration through DOGE, then trying to rewrite the record after the consequences became harder to bury. He oversaw roughly $60 billion in foreign aid cuts and helped leave USAID with only a handful of surviving projects. That is not commentary. That is administrative force with casualties attached.

The Decision Was the Crime

The crucial fact is not that Musk bragged about “feeding USAID to the woodchipper.” It is that the cuts were made abruptly, at scale, and without assessing what harms would follow. Jeremy Konyndyk says DOGE clear-cut more than 80% of USAID programs and that staff who tried to flag the damage were forced out. That is how institutions turn cruelty into procedure: they silence the people who can still see the consequences.

Blame Management Is the Follow-Up

Musk’s response tells you everything about the moral structure of the operation. Faced with reporting that his cuts may contribute to millions of preventable deaths by 2030, he did not defend the policy on the merits. He attacked Rep. Ro Khanna, called him a liar, threatened prison, and declared that there was not “even a single dead child.” This is not accountability. It is a familiar tactic: deny the harm, insult the messenger, and demand that reality conform to the rich man’s timeline.

The Article Soft-Pedals the Power Grab

The source does better than most coverage by naming the cuts and the deaths, but the framing still risks treating this as a scandal of online excess rather than a case of concentrated power doing irreversible damage. Musk was not some outside agitator riffing on the sidelines. He was inside the machinery, coordinating with a president’s administration, and using state capacity to dismantle aid programs that served vulnerable people. That distinction matters because it assigns responsibility where it belongs.

What This Pattern Reveals

This is what oligarchic governance looks like in practice: a wealthy operator seizes a public agency, strips it for ideological spectacle, forces out internal dissent, and then performs outrage when the body count appears in reporting. The language of efficiency is the cover; the real product is abandonment. When elite power can destroy lifesaving systems, then deny the destruction with a straight face, the issue is not confusion. It is institutionalized impunity.
