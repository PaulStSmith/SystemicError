Far-right figure posts photo with Trump to prove president isn't 'slipping and near death'

Trump is not “slipping and near death” and one photo proves it, according to a controversial far-right activist.

Roger Stone, a longtime political figure and friend/adviser to Trump, sought over the weekend to push back against claims that Trump has been slipping, both physically and in his mind. Recently, a member of Trump’s own family tied the president’s failing mental health to his purportedly weakening grip on the Republican Party.

According to Stone, who was convicted of lying to Congress but received a pardon from Trump, the president hasn’t changed in the 50 years that they have known each other.

Taking to social media first thing Sunday morning, Stone posted a photo of the two together and wrote that it is “entertaining that those who lied so convincingly about the fact that Joe Biden was a veritable incoherent vegetable when he was president now insist Trump” is the one suffering decline.

Stone went on to reject those who say that the president, “who I saw only days ago and who is no different than he has been in the 50 years I have known him, is slipping and near death.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Farce: Roger Stone’s Defense of Trump

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as the theatrics of the absurd, Roger Stone, a figure whose reputation is as tarnished as a penny lying in a puddle of vinegar, has come forth to declare that Donald Trump is not “slipping and near death.” This declaration, based on a single photo and the word of a man convicted of lying to Congress, is as flimsy as a house of cards in a hurricane.

The Photo that Proves Nothing

Let’s dissect the claim that one photo can dispel concerns about a person’s physical and mental health. The idea that a snapshot—a moment frozen in time, likely curated for public consumption—can negate legitimate concerns about Trump’s health is laughable. It’s akin to saying that seeing a picture of a clean room proves that the house isn’t falling apart. The superficiality of this evidence is staggering.

Roger Stone: A Questionable Source

Roger Stone, a man pardoned by Trump after being convicted of lying to Congress, is hardly a paragon of truth. His assertion that Trump hasn’t changed in the 50 years they’ve known each other is meant to instill confidence. However, this statement comes from someone whose credibility is less than zero. Using Stone as a character witness for Trump’s health is like asking a fox to guard the henhouse—both ludicrous and revealing.

The Irony of Stone’s Accusation

Stone’s attempt to deflect by criticizing those who questioned Joe Biden’s mental acuity is a classic case of whataboutism. The irony of Stone calling others liars, given his own conviction for lying, is so thick you could cut it with a knife. This tactic is a transparent attempt to shift focus and should be seen for what it is: a desperate ploy to protect Trump.

The Underlying Issue

Beyond the farcical defense put forth by Stone lies a more serious issue: the manipulation of public perception. By claiming that a single photo and the word of a convicted liar are enough to dispel concerns, Stone is attempting to rewrite the narrative. This isn’t just about Trump’s health; it’s about how facts are increasingly disregarded in favor of convenient narratives.

Conclusion: A Picture is Worth a Thousand Denials

In sum, Roger Stone’s defense of Trump is a masterclass in obfuscation and misinformation. The claim that a photo disproves concerns about Trump’s health is as credible as a chocolate teapot. The reliance on Stone, a figure with a dubious relationship with the truth, only undermines the argument further.

What this episode reveals is the lengths to which some will go to protect Trump, regardless of the facts. It’s a stark reminder of the era of post-truth politics we find ourselves in, where a photo and the word of a known liar are presented as definitive proof. Let’s not be fooled by the smoke and mirrors. The health of a public figure, especially one as polarizing as Trump, is a legitimate concern, and it deserves more than a flippant dismissal based on a photo op.
