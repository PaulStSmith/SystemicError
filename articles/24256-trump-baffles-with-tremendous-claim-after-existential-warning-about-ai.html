Trump baffles with 'tremendous' claim after existential warning about AI

Based on an article by Erik De La Garza

Paulo SantosSeptember 30, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

President Donald Trump said Tuesday that artificial intelligence should be subject to “tremendous self-regulation,” while also claiming that the Justice Department and FBI already provide some form of regulation. RawStory reported that he made the remarks after a White House summit with technology executives, when asked whether government guardrails were necessary.

Days earlier, Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates had told NBC’s “Meet the Press” that government oversight was necessary and warned that AI could “cause a billion deaths.” Trump did not address that scenario directly. Instead, he praised a group including Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, and Jeff Bezos as extraordinarily capable and celebrated their work.

Power without a policy

The federal government holds the power to impose enforceable rules. Trump held the presidency and therefore substantial influence over whether executive agencies would develop, apply, or advocate such safeguards. The executives in the room controlled companies with immense capital and technological reach, but they were also the prospective subjects of regulation.

Trump’s answer blurred those distinct roles. The Justice Department and FBI enforce laws and investigate crimes; invoking them did not explain what AI standards existed, what conduct they covered, or how catastrophic risks would be prevented. Calling that arrangement “automatic” regulation substituted institutional names for an actual policy.

The regulated become the regulators

Self-regulation places the central judgment in the hands of companies whose incentives include expansion, market dominance, and speed. It asks those building and commercializing the technology to decide which constraints should slow them down.

The problem is structural, not a referendum on whether Musk, Zuckerberg, Bezos, or their peers are “brilliant.” Technical accomplishment does not eliminate conflicts of interest. Praise for executives answers a different question from the one reporters asked: not whether these men are talented, but whether the public should depend on their voluntary restraint.

Praise as misdirection

The most revealing move was the pivot from risk to prestige. Confronted with a question about enforceable guardrails, Trump praised the people who would face those guardrails. That framing recasts oversight as a matter of confidence in exceptional individuals rather than a question of public authority and accountability.

RawStory’s emphasis on Trump appearing “unfazed” captures the contrast with Gates’s warning, but bafflement is not the core issue. The statement’s political meaning is clearer than its policy content: governmental responsibility was displaced onto private actors while government agencies were cited vaguely enough to suggest that protection already existed.

Catastrophic stakes, voluntary limits

Gates’s billion-death scenario was his warning, not an established forecast in the reporting. But even as a hypothetical, it exposes the inadequacy of Trump’s response. Risks described as potentially catastrophic cannot coherently be met with admiration for the industry and an undefined appeal to self-policing.

This is a recurring pattern of concentrated power: corporations retain operational control, political leaders decline to specify binding limits, and the public is told that elite competence will substitute for enforceable accountability. If harm follows, responsibility becomes easy to scatter among developers, users, agencies, and an allegedly unpredictable technology.

The system behind the shrug

The deeper failure is not that Trump sounded confused. It is that his answer treated public protection as optional while preserving maximum discretion for powerful private institutions.

When government officials answer demands for rules by praising the regulated, they are making a political choice even if they never articulate a policy. The state keeps its enforcement vocabulary, industry keeps its freedom of action, and everyone outside the room inherits the consequences.
