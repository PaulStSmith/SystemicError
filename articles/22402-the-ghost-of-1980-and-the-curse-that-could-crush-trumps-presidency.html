The ghost of 1980 and the 'curse' that could crush Trump’s presidency

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Trump Put Himself in the Trap

The Power Is His

The source story is trying to dress this up as a geopolitical chess match, but the basic fact is simpler: Donald Trump made the decision to drag the United States into war with Iran, alongside Israel, and now he owns the consequences. Iran is reacting to an American president who reached for escalation first and is now pretending he can control the fallout with threats and rallies.

That is the center of the story. Not “mysterious” Iranian leverage. Not tragic fate. Trump holds the office, commands the war machine, and chose the path that produced the damage.

One Short Summary

The article compares Trump’s Iran problem to Jimmy Carter’s hostage crisis, arguing that both presidents were trapped by events they could not easily reverse. It says Trump is facing political blowback from a war he started, with polls showing voters feel worse off and with Iran holding strategic leverage after a collapsed ceasefire and the closure of Hormuz.

Misdirection by Comparison

The Carter comparison only works if you flatten the difference between a president caught in a crisis and a president who helped create one. Carter inherited the hostage crisis. Trump authored his. That distinction matters because journalism often uses historical analogy to blur responsibility. It turns decision into atmosphere and agency into misfortune.

This framing makes Trump sound like a man swept up in forces beyond him. That is false comfort for a political arsonist. He is not “embroiled” in a mess. He lit it.

Iran’s Leverage, Trump’s Failure

The article is clearest when it describes Iran’s leverage over U.S. politics. If Iranian officials are determined not to let Trump control the “ebb and flow” of the war, that is not a sideshow. It is the strategic penalty for reckless American escalation. Iran is using the midterms as a pressure point because Trump turned foreign policy into domestic political exposure.

That does not make Iran the moral lead of the story. It means Trump handed an adversary a clean line into his own electorate. The consequence of that decision is not abstract. It is inflation, gas prices, the risk of more American deaths, and a president looking weaker because he insisted on looking strong.

The Real Cost of Bluster

Trump’s threats to “bomb the s--- out of” Oman if it obstructs his war only underline the same point: he reaches for spectacle when he has lost control. That posture is not strength. It is a panic response from an executive who mistakes noise for power.

The article almost gets this right, but it still flirts with the mythology that bluster is a political strategy rather than a symptom of collapse. When a president can only threaten escalation to salvage authority, he has already admitted the weakness beneath the performance.

The Pattern

This is the recurring authoritarian pattern in miniature: initiate harm, deny ownership, then demand credit for the damage you caused. Trump’s war politics work the same way his domestic politics do. He breaks institutions, inflames crisis, and then tries to survive the blowback by shouting louder than the consequences.

The deeper lesson is not about Iran alone. It is about a political class willing to convert state power into a personal gamble and then ask voters to absorb the losses. That is not leadership. It is liability with a flag pinned to it.
