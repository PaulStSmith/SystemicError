'Guess what, buddy?': CNN panel laughs off Trump's latest bid to make history

By Paulo Santos

The panel on CNN's "Table for Five" laughed off Donald Trump's habit of making bold –– and distracting –– proposals when things are not going well for him and noted they all seem to be designed to raise his profile among all presidents.

Speaking with host Abby Phillip, longtime D.C. insider Chuck Todd noted that the president was doing all he could earlier in the week after he created a firestorm by telling an NBC host he wasn't sure if he was supposed to follow the Constitution.

That, in turn, led the embattled president to propose re-opening the Alcatraz prison despite the fact that is has been falling into disrepair for decades.

Pointing out that the NBC interview with host Kirsten Welker "didn't go well," Todd added, "Suddenly, instead of that being the thing, 'Oh, let me throw up this Alcatraz thing.'"

"I do think sometimes –– I’m with you guys –– it’s just, what do they call it, flak? You know, to distract the fighter jets, what are you shooting at? We cannot take the bait and I completely agree. But he’s obsessed with being in history. He wants, maybe he really is afraid that he is not going to be more than one page in the history book."

He then pointed out, "And I promise you, guess what buddy? You probably maybe will be two because you’re impeached twice," before adding, "No president ever lives as long in the history books as they think they will."

"You’re so right about that, because maybe it’s all these things at once, right? Trump wants to be a historical figure. And in his mind, it’s acquiring territory. It’s renaming things," host Phillip contributed. "He really would love to be on the Mount Rushmore. I think he’s maybe realized that that’s like a little bit tough of a sell. But this is important to him."

Our Analysis:

Distracting with the Absurd: Trump's Alcatraz Gambit

In a recent episode of CNN's "Table for Five," the panel, including host Abby Phillip and seasoned D.C. insider Chuck Todd, dissected former President Donald Trump's latest antics with a mix of amusement and incredulity. At the heart of their discussion was Trump's seemingly out-of-left-field proposal to reopen the dilapidated Alcatraz prison—a move that smacks of desperation and distraction.

Trump's Mastery of Distraction Through Absurdity

Trump's suggestion to revive Alcatraz, a facility infamous for its historical significance and current state of disrepair, is emblematic of his well-documented strategy: when under fire, deflect with something more sensational. This tactic was employed following a tumultuous NBC interview in which Trump ambiguously questioned his obligation to adhere to the Constitution. Rather than confront the fallout, Trump pivoted to the Alcatraz proposal, effectively shifting the conversation.

A Transparent Bid for Historical Significance

The panelists on "Table for Five" rightly noted Trump's obsession with his historical legacy. His fear of being a mere footnote in history propels him towards grandiose and often implausible propositions. Chuck Todd's pointed remark, "And I promise you, guess what buddy? You probably maybe will be two [pages] because you’re impeached twice," underscores the irony of Trump's quest for greatness resulting in infamy rather than acclaim.

The Folly of Alcatraz as a Distraction

Reopening Alcatraz as a serious policy proposal is ludicrous, not only due to the logistical and financial impracticalities but also because it reflects a profound misunderstanding of what constitutes meaningful presidential achievement. This move, intended to dazzle and distract, instead reveals a desperation to be perceived as consequential.

The Panel's Collective Eye-Roll

The reaction of the "Table for Five" panel—ranging from laughter to dismissive agreement—mirrors the broader public's fatigue with Trump's tactics. The consensus that these antics should not detract from substantive critiques of his presidency and behavior is a call to focus on the issues rather than the spectacle.

Conclusion: The Spectacle Over Substance Strategy

Trump's Alcatraz proposal is symptomatic of a broader strategy that prioritizes spectacle over substance. While it may momentarily capture the public's imagination, it fails to address any real issues or offer viable solutions. This approach, though momentarily diverting, underscores a presidency more concerned with headlines than with historical integrity. As the "Table for Five" panel concisely articulated, Trump's legacy, despite his best efforts, may ultimately be defined more by his impeachments and theatrics than by any substantive contributions to the nation's fabric.
