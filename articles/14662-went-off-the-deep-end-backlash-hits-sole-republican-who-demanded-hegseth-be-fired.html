'Went off the deep end': Backlash hits sole Republican who demanded Hegseth be fired

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Lone Voice of Dissent: Congressman Don Bacon's Stand Against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth closes his eyes as he stands by U.S. President Donald Trump (not pictures), in the Oval Office at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 21, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

Congressman Don Bacon (R-NE) still remains the only GOP lawmaker who called for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to be ousted from his position — and it could be costing him support, according to a Washington Post report.

“He went off the deep end,” Tom Widhalm, an Air Force veteran, told the outlet. “He doesn’t back the military like he should.”

And colleagues in his party have warned him to "be careful," the Post reported.

Bacon, a retired Air Force general, has split his constituents with his stance — though he stands firmly outside of his party's line on Hegseth.

His former deputy chief of staff, Felix Ungerman, said current and former military personnel are “kind of split down the middle" on the Defense Secretary.

“You’ve got people who really don’t like this administration and what they’re doing,” Ungerman told the Post. “But then you’ve also got another view that ‘this is a long time coming’” — that the disruption ordered by Trump and executed by Hegseth “has been needed for some time.”

Bacon is an Air Force veteran who has previously said his lack of support for Hegseth “had nothing to do with anything political … It’s just what’s right.”

His calls to remove Hegseth began after the secretary used an unclassified messaging app to share “highly sensitive details of a planned U.S. assault on Yemeni militants.” The chat included a journalist and later became known as “Signalgate.”

“I could have forgiven it if he had said, ‘I was wrong and I won’t do it again,’” Bacon said. “People make mistakes.” But instead, Hegseth “tried to deny it was a problem,” and he sought to shift the blame to the journalist, he said.

Bacon has said Hegseth's behavior “does not garner credibility in the armed forces.” He later added that if he had done the same thing “as a lieutenant, captain, major, lieutenant colonel, colonel, you name it, I would have been fired.”

A spokesman for Hegseth did not respond to the Washington Post's request for comment.

The Lone Voice of Dissent: Congressman Don Bacon's Stand Against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

In a political climate that often rewards conformity and punishes dissent, Congressman Don Bacon (R-NE) emerges as a rare figure of principled opposition within the GOP, specifically regarding his call for the ousting of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. This stance, as reported by the Washington Post, has not only isolated him within his party but also appears to be a double-edged sword among his constituents and colleagues. Let's dissect the layers of this intriguing political drama.

The Cost of Principle Over Party

Bacon, a retired Air Force general, has broken ranks with his party over Hegseth's handling of sensitive military operations. Specifically, his uproar was against Hegseth's use of an unclassified messaging app to share "highly sensitive details of a planned U.S. assault on Yemeni militants," an incident now infamously dubbed as "Signalgate." Bacon's critique is not just a whisper of disagreement but a loud call for accountability, stating that such actions "do not garner credibility in the armed forces."

What's striking here is Bacon's emphasis on principle over politics. He explicitly stated that his lack of support for Hegseth "had nothing to do with anything political ... It's just what's right." This is a refreshing, albeit risky, stance in today's hyper-partisan environment, where loyalty often trumps integrity.

The Fallout: Isolation and Warning

The reaction from his party and constituents has been mixed, to say the least. On one hand, Bacon has been warned by his colleagues to "be careful," a veiled caution that reeks of political intimidation. On the other hand, his constituency is divided, with some, like Air Force veteran Tom Widhalm, accusing him of not backing the military sufficiently. This criticism seems misdirected, considering Bacon's military background and his critique's focus on maintaining operational security and credibility, pillars of military effectiveness and honor.

Felix Ungerman, Bacon's former deputy chief of staff, offers a nuanced view, suggesting that the military community is "split down the middle" on Hegseth. This division reflects broader national debates on military strategy and leadership, exacerbated by the polarizing nature of Trump-era appointments and decisions.

The Bigger Picture: Accountability in Leadership

At the heart of this controversy is a critical question of leadership accountability. Bacon's insistence that Hegseth's behavior warrants dismissal is grounded in a fundamental military and governmental tenet: that leaders must be held to the highest standards of conduct, especially when handling sensitive information. His assertion that he would have been fired for similar actions at any rank underscores a perceived erosion of these standards at the highest levels of defense leadership.

Conclusion: A Test of Political Integrity

Congressman Don Bacon's predicament is emblematic of the broader challenges facing individuals in government who seek to prioritize ethical standards and integrity over party loyalty. While his stance may cost him politically in the short term, it also cements his reputation as a figure of principle. Whether this will benefit or hinder his career remains to be seen, but it undoubtedly adds a layer of complexity to the ongoing discussions about military leadership, operational security, and the political dimensions of defense.

In a political era marked by division and scandal, Bacon's actions pose a critical question: can integrity and principle pave a viable path through the murky waters of contemporary politics? Only time will tell, but for now, Don Bacon stands as a lone voice crying out for accountability and ethical governance in a sea of partisan conformity.
