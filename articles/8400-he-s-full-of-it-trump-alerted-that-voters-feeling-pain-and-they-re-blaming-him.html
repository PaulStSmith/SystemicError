'He's full of it': Trump alerted that voters feeling 'pain' — and they're blaming him

President Donald Trump and his top officials insist the economy is humming, but CNN’s Harry Enten found that Americans aren’t buying their assurances.

The president claims prices are coming down and there’s “almost no inflation,” which stands in stark contrast to what solid majorities are telling pollsters about their views on the economy.

“They think prices are rising, rising, rising, and it’s across different [categories],” Enten said. “Look at this: I mean, just your costs are up versus a year ago. Groceries, 85 percent, utilities, 78 percent, health care, 67 percent, housing, 66 percent, gasoline, 54 percent. The bottom line is this Americans feel like prices are rising in each and every single part of their lives, rising, ever climbing, and they just don’t feel like that they can catch a break.”

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued at the weekend that tariffs actually help consumers, saying that prices would be coming down in the weeks and months ahead, but Enten said the public disagrees.

“They think he’s full of it, that’s exactly what they think,” Enten said. “I mean, just take a look here: Tariffs rising raising prices or raising prices ... 71 percent, 84 percent of Democrats, 74 percent of independents and even a majority of Republicans. When you can get a majority of Republicans to agree with majority of Democrats, you know that the pain is being felt by the American public right now. The treasury secretary can try and tell the people something, but they can feel in their pocketbooks. They, simply put, don’t buy what he and this administration is selling when it comes to tariffs.”

Trump and his top officials blame any economic woes on former President Joe Biden, but Enten said that isn’t a widely shared view.

“No, no, no,” he said. “You know what, I want to quote Judge Judy, but I’m not going to, I’m merely going to try and say it’s like they’re trying to sell ice up at the North Pole when it comes to this. More responsible for the current economy, 62 percecnt of Americans say it’s Donald Trump, just 32 percent say it’s Joe Biden. Look, Donald Trump did, in fact, inherit an economy from Joe Biden, but now we are nearly a year into the Donald Trump administration. If the Trump administration is trying to push the book over there. Simply put, the American folks aren’t buying it. They say at this point, it is Donald Trump’s economy and what is happening in terms of inflation is his responsibility.”

“If you want the power, you get the power,” Enten added. “But with great power comes great responsibility.”

Our Analysis:

The Great Disconnect: Trump’s Economic Fantasies vs. The American Reality

In a classic display of political dissonance, President Donald Trump and his top officials are painting a picture of the U.S. economy that is as detached from the public’s perception as it is from reality. Despite their insistent proclamations of a booming economy and negligible inflation, the American populace is not only skeptical but downright disbelieving of this administration’s economic narrative.

The Inflation Illusion: Trump vs. The American Wallet

Trump’s assertion that prices are on a downward trend and that inflation is “almost non-existent” flies in the face of what a significant majority of Americans are experiencing firsthand. According to CNN’s Harry Enten, the numbers speak louder than the administration’s assurances. A staggering 85% of Americans report an increase in grocery prices, 78% in utilities, 67% in healthcare, 66% in housing, and 54% in gasoline compared to just a year ago. This isn’t just a matter of perception; it’s the harsh reality impacting every facet of the average American’s life, leaving them feeling trapped in an ever-rising tide of costs with no relief in sight.

Tariff Tensions and the Treasury’s Tall Tales

In a bewildering display of economic gymnastics, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent claims that tariffs are actually beneficial to consumers and predicts a decrease in prices in the coming weeks and months. However, this optimistic forecast is met with widespread public skepticism. Enten highlights that a vast majority, including 71% of the general populace, 84% of Democrats, 74% of independents, and even a majority of Republicans, believe that tariffs are exacerbating the situation, contributing to rising prices. This bipartisan consensus underscores a glaring disconnect between the administration’s rhetoric and the tangible financial strain felt by Americans.

The Blame Game: Trump vs. Biden

In a rather predictable move, Trump and his officials have attempted to deflect responsibility for the economic woes onto former President Joe Biden. Yet, this blame-shifting strategy seems to fall flat with the American public. Enten points out that 62% of Americans hold Trump accountable for the current economic conditions, compared to just 32% who point the finger at Biden. Nearly a year into his administration, the attempt to pass the buck to his predecessor is not only unconvincing but also indicative of a refusal to acknowledge the consequences of their own economic policies.

Conclusion: Accountability in the Age of Disinformation

The disconnect between the Trump administration’s economic narrative and the reality experienced by Americans is not just a matter of differing perspectives; it’s a glaring testament to the administration’s attempt to craft a favorable reality through sheer force of rhetoric. However, as the data and public sentiment clearly demonstrate, the American people are far from hoodwinked.

The administration’s failure to acknowledge and address the real economic pressures facing Americans not only undermines their credibility but also exacerbates the challenges of those struggling to keep up with rising costs. In the end, leadership comes with responsibility, and it is high time the Trump administration faced this undeniable truth. As Enten aptly summarizes, echoing the timeless words of wisdom, “With great power comes great responsibility.” It’s a lesson this administration would do well to learn and embody.
