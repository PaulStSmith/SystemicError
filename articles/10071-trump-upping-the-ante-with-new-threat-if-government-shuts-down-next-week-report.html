Trump 'upping the ante' with new 'threat' if government shuts down next week: report

By Paulo Santos

Congressional leaders have until the end of the month to agree on a deal to keep government agencies funded. But according to a new report, President Donald Trump’s administration is using the possibility of a government shutdown to carry out its plans of permanently hollowing out federal agencies.

On Wednesday, Politico’s Sophia Cai reported that agency heads have been handed memos from the White House Office of Management and Budget (OMB) instructing them to submit reduction-in-force (RIF) plans in the event of a federal government shutdown. This effectively means that the administration is planning to carry out mass firings of federal workers, even though federal employees typically come back to work after Congress agrees on a funding bill.

“The move marks a significant break from how shutdowns have been handled in recent decades, when most furloughs were temporary and employees were brought back once Congress voted to reopen government and funding was restored,” Cai wrote. “This time, OMB Director Russ Vought is using the threat of permanent job cuts as leverage, upping the ante in the standoff with Democrats in Congress over government spending.”

According to the OMB memo, agencies have been ordered to compile lists of “programs, projects, and activities” that will run out of discretionary funding on October 1, where there is no alternative funding source available. Politico reported that the OMB’s plan would amount to “permanently eliminating jobs in programs not consistent with President Donald Trump’s priorities.”

If the administration followed through on this threat to unilaterally and permanently eliminate positions created by congressional appropriations, it would seemingly run afoul of Article I, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution, which explicitly grants the power of the purse to Congress. One unnamed OMB official told Politico that agencies that rely on mandatory (versus discretionary) spending are exempted from the order, including Social Security, Medicare and the Department of Veterans’ Affairs. And agencies carrying out Trump’s priorities, like U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Customs and Border Protection, will be exempted from the RIF order.

Bobby Kogan, who is the senior director of federal budget policy at the left-leaning Center for American Progress, wrote on Bluesky: “Setting aside the question of legality, this would be an action of enormous self-harm inflicted on the nation, needlessly ridding the country of talent and expertise.”

“It’s also extortive. ‘Give us what we want in a funding fight, or we’ll hurt the country,’” he added.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Trump Administration’s Shutdown Strategy—A Dangerous Game of Chicken with Federal Employment

The recent report by Politico’s Sophia Cai unveils a rather alarming strategy by the Trump administration, which appears to be leveraging the threat of a government shutdown to orchestrate what can only be described as a draconian cull of federal employees. This maneuver is not just a departure from standard protocol; it’s a headlong dive into uncharted and perilous waters, with President Donald Trump’s Office of Management and Budget (OMB) at the helm, steering towards a potentially unconstitutional confrontation.

The OMB’s Bold Maneuver

At the core of this controversy is a memo from the OMB, directing agency heads to prepare reduction-in-force (RIF) plans in anticipation of a government shutdown. This directive isn’t merely about tightening belts; it’s about fundamentally reshaping the federal workforce in alignment with Trump’s priorities—by force. The memo essentially weaponizes the shutdown, transforming it from a temporary hiccup in federal operations into a means of permanently excising programs and positions that don’t align with the administration’s agenda.

Constitutional Conundrums

The notion of using a government shutdown to enforce such drastic workforce reductions not only deviates from decades of precedent but also raises serious constitutional questions. The U.S. Constitution clearly vests the power of the purse in Congress, not the Executive Branch. By unilaterally deciding which positions and programs to eliminate, the Trump administration is treading dangerously close to usurping a power that the Constitution explicitly reserves for the legislative body. This not only challenges the separation of powers but also undermines the foundational principles of American governance.

Selective Exemptions and Political Posturing

It’s noteworthy that this draconian RIF order conveniently exempts agencies like Social Security, Medicare, and the Department of Veterans’ Affairs—areas of mandatory spending that are politically sensitive and less vulnerable to discretionary cuts. Moreover, agencies critical to Trump’s priorities, such as U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Customs and Border Protection, are also shielded from the order. This selective culling process reveals a disturbing willingness to sacrifice the broader federal workforce on the altar of political expediency, prioritizing partisan objectives over the effective functioning of the government.

The Cost of Playing Chicken with Federal Employment

Bobby Kogan of the Center for American Progress hits the nail on the head, describing the OMB’s strategy as both “extortive” and an act of “enormous self-harm.” The potential fallout from implementing such RIF plans extends far beyond the immediate job losses. It threatens to erode the federal government’s institutional knowledge and capacity, inflicting long-term damage on the nation’s ability to serve its citizens. Furthermore, it sets a dangerous precedent, suggesting that any future administration could sideline Congress and manipulate federal employment based on the whims of the Executive Branch.

Conclusion

The Trump administration’s gamble with federal employment is a high-stakes game that risks not only the livelihoods of countless federal workers but also the very principles upon which the U.S. government is built. Leveraging a government shutdown to enforce permanent job cuts not only flirts with unconstitutionality but also undermines the nation’s trust in its government to act as a stable employer and a faithful steward of the public interest. It’s a strategy that reeks of short-term political gain at the cost of long-term national harm, a trade-off that is as shortsighted as it is reckless.
