'Wow': Onlookers stunned by GOP congressman's '100% correct' Trump admin criticism

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 1, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Political observers and experts pounced on Saturday as a Republican lawmaker offered a stinging rejection of reported conduct by Donald Trump’s administration.

The Wall Street Journal’s piece ahead of the weekend, Make Money Not War: Trump’s Real Plan for Peace in Ukraine, claims that, “The Kremlin pitched the White House on peace through business. To Europe’s dismay, the president and his envoy are on board.” It caught the attention of Rep. Don Bacon, who recently issued an unrelated warning to Trump.

Bacon chimed in on the report over the weekend. The lawmaker said, “WSJ today. Multi-billion $ business deals between Russian & American billionaires that forces Ukraine to give Russia more land, & make Ukraine a Russian vassal state. Greed trumps Ukrainian sovereignty. Churchill & Reagan are turning in their graves.”

Bacon’s response brought in numerous high-profile replies of its own.

Conservative pollster Sarah Longwell said, “Go caucus with the democrats. This Republican Party does not deserve your affiliation. You have the power to do something.”

Ex-GOP lawmaker Denver Lee Riggleman III said, “Yep. This is about oligarchs working together to destroy Ukraine. Pretty obvious.”

Stephen Sestanovich said, “Two reactions: 1) WSJ did great reporting here, and 2) is anyone really surprised? Witkoff hasn’t exactly been hiding the sleaze…”

Michael Weiss added, “Right now Ukraine is being more transparent about corruption at the highest levels of government than the United States is…”

Former GOP insider Tara Setmayer weighed in with, “This WSJ piece is a MUST read. And Rep. Don Bacon is 100% correct here.”

Robert McCartney added, “GOP Congressman (Nebraska), a retired Air Force General, isn’t happy that Trump wants to sell out Ukraine so his family and cronies can make big bucks.”

Journalist Andrew Hall said, “This poster is a Republican Congressman. WOW.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Unsettling Intersection of Politics and Profit

The recent Wall Street Journal article, “Make Money Not War: Trump’s Real Plan for Peace in Ukraine,” has sparked a significant political uproar, laying bare the uncomfortable nexus between geopolitical decisions and personal financial interests. At the heart of this controversy is a proposal that seems to redefine diplomacy through the lens of business deals, prioritizing profit over the sovereignty and welfare of nations.

Greed Over Governance

Rep. Don Bacon’s vehement repudiation of the reported conduct by Donald Trump’s administration underscores a fundamental betrayal of democratic values and international norms. The proposal, as Bacon outlines, to entice peace by essentially selling out Ukraine to Russian interests is not only morally reprehensible but a strategic blunder of epic proportions. It’s a clear case of greed trumping sovereignty, a notion so outlandish that one could scarcely believe it wasn’t conjured up in a dystopian novel.

Echoes of Discontent

The backlash from political observers and experts alike is telling. Figures from across the political spectrum, including conservative pollster Sarah Longwell and former GOP lawmaker Denver Lee Riggleman III, have expressed their disdain and disbelief. Their responses highlight a growing chasm within the Republican Party, one that pits principled conservatism against a transactional, profit-driven approach to foreign policy.

Michael Weiss’s observation that Ukraine is displaying greater transparency regarding corruption than the United States is particularly damning. It suggests a profound reversal of roles, wherein the U.S., once a beacon of democratic principles and governance, is now being outshone by a country historically grappling with corruption at the highest levels.

The Unsurprising Sleaze

Stephen Sestanovich’s remark that nobody should be surprised by the revelations, given the individual involved, points to a deeper, systemic issue within certain corridors of power. The acceptance of sleaze as a norm rather than an aberration is a dire indictment of the current state of affairs. Yet, the commendation of the WSJ’s reporting signifies a silver lining, demonstrating the enduring value of investigative journalism in holding power to account.

Conclusion: A Call for Integrity

The article serves as a crucial reminder of the importance of integrity in politics and governance. The notion that peace can be brokered at the expense of a sovereign nation for personal gain is antithetical to the principles upon which democratic societies are built. It’s a stark warning that when the pursuit of profit overshadows ethical considerations and international solidarity, the very fabric of global diplomacy is at risk.

As the dust settles, one thing remains clear: the need for a principled stand against the commodification of conflict resolution. The outcry from political figures like Rep. Don Bacon and the broader community reflects a deep-seated belief in the sanctity of national sovereignty and the inherent value of ethical governance. In a world increasingly defined by transactions, these voices serve as a vital counterpoint, advocating for a return to a diplomacy rooted in mutual respect and democratic values.
