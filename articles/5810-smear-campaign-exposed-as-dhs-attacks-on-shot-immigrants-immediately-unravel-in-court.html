'Smear campaign' exposed as DHS attacks on shot immigrants immediately unravel in court

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 4, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

DHS Secretary Kristi Noem (Twitter screen capture)

After a US Border Patrol Agent shot two Venezuelan immigrants in Portland, Oregon, in January, the Department of Homeland Security claimed that the two victims were “vicious Tren de Aragua gang members” who “weaponized their vehicle” against federal agents, who had no choice but to open fire in self-defense.

However, court records obtained by the Guardian reveal that a Department of Justice prosecutor subsequently told a judge the government was “not suggesting” that one of the victims, Luis Niño-Moncada, was a gang member.

The Guardian also obtained an FBI affidavit contradicting DHS claims about the second victim, Yorlenys Zambrano-Contreras, being “involved” in a shooting in Portland last year, when in reality she was a “reported victim of sexual assault and robbery.”

Attorneys representing Niño-Moncada and Zambrano-Contreras, who both survived the shooting and were subsequently hospitalized, told the Guardian that neither of them have any prior criminal convictions.

Legal experts who spoke with the Guardian about the shooting said it appeared that DHS was waging a “smear campaign” against the victims.

Sergio Perez, a civil rights lawyer and former US prosecutor, noted in an interview that prosecutors filed criminal charges against Niño-Moncada and Zambrano-Contreras just two days after they were shot, even before it had obtained crucial video evidence of the incident.

“This government needs to go back to the practice of slow and thorough investigations,” he told the Guardian, “rather than what we consistently see in immigration enforcement activities—which is a rush to smear individuals.”

Carley Palmer, a former federal prosecutor, told the Guardian that the court records obtained by the paper don’t show DOJ presenting any of the usual evidence that prosecutors use to establish defendants’ alleged gang membership.

“What’s interesting about the filings is that you don’t see evidence of gang association,” said Palmer. “It just feels like a dirtying up of the defendant.”

DHS in recent months has made a number of claims about people who have been shot or killed by federal immigration officers that have not held up to scrutiny.

Most recently, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem claimed that slain Minneapolis intensive care nurse Alex Pretti was a “domestic terrorist” intent on inflicting “maximum damage” on federal agents, when video clearly showed that Pretti was swarmed by multiple federal agents and was disarmed before two agents opened fire and killed him.

Noem also openly lied about the circumstances and actions that resulted in the shooting death of Renee Nicole Good by a federal agent weeks earlier.

In November, federal prosecutors abruptly dropped charges against Marimar Martinez, a woman who was shot multiple times by a US Border Patrol agent in October in Chicago’s Brighton Park neighborhood.

In the indictment filed against Martinez, prosecutors said that the Border Patrol agent who shot her had been acting in self-defense, and that he had only opened fire after Martinez’s car collided with his vehicle.

However, uncovered text messages showed the Border Patrol agent apparently bragging about shooting Martinez, as he boasted that he “fired five rounds and she had seven holes” in a message sent to fellow agents.

An attorney representing Martinez also claimed that he had seen body camera footage that directly undermined DHS claims about how the shooting unfolded.

No explanation was provided for why charges against Martinez were dropped.

Our Analysis:

The DHS’s Dangerous Disinformation Campaign

The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has found itself embroiled in a series of controversial shootings involving its agents and Venezuelan immigrants. The narrative spun by the DHS in the aftermath of these incidents has been called into question, revealing a troubling pattern of misinformation and potential smear campaigns against the victims. This analysis delves into the discrepancies between DHS claims and the emerging facts, shedding light on the systemic issues within the agency and the broader implications for justice and accountability.

The Portland Shooting: A Case Study in Misinformation

In January, a US Border Patrol agent shot two Venezuelan immigrants in Portland, Oregon. The DHS was quick to label the victims as “vicious Tren de Aragua gang members” who “weaponized their vehicle” against federal agents. This narrative, however, began to crumble under scrutiny. Court records and an FBI affidavit revealed a starkly different story: one victim was falsely accused of gang membership, and the other was actually a victim of sexual assault and robbery. The rush to criminalize these individuals without solid evidence is not just alarming; it’s a blatant miscarriage of justice.

Legal Experts Cry Foul

The actions of the DHS have drawn severe criticism from legal experts and civil rights attorneys. The filing of criminal charges against the victims before obtaining crucial video evidence of the incident is a clear departure from standard investigative procedures. This eagerness to smear individuals, as noted by former US prosecutor Sergio Perez, underscores a worrying trend in immigration enforcement activities. The lack of evidence presented to support claims of gang association further emphasizes the agency’s reckless disregard for the truth.

A Pattern of Deception

This incident is not an isolated case. The DHS has made several unsubstantiated claims about individuals shot or killed by federal immigration officers. From falsely labeling a slain Minneapolis nurse as a “domestic terrorist” to lying about the circumstances leading to another shooting death, the pattern of deception is unmistakable. Additionally, the case of Marimar Martinez, who was shot by a Border Patrol agent in Chicago, highlights the brazenness of these agents, with uncovered text messages showing the agent bragging about the shooting.

The Urgent Need for Accountability

The discrepancies between the DHS’s claims and the facts are more than just errors; they represent a systemic issue within the agency. The rush to criminalize and smear victims of shootings by federal agents not only undermines the credibility of the DHS but also erodes public trust in law enforcement. This dangerous disinformation campaign has real-world consequences, affecting the lives of the individuals involved and their families.

The DHS’s actions necessitate a thorough and independent investigation to ensure accountability. The agency must review its protocols to prevent the premature criminalization of individuals and the dissemination of false information. Transparency in these investigations is crucial to restoring public trust and ensuring that justice is served.

In conclusion, the DHS’s handling of these shootings, characterized by misinformation and a lack of due diligence, raises serious concerns about the agency’s commitment to justice and transparency. The victims of these shootings deserve a fair and unbiased examination of the facts, not a rush to judgment. It’s time for the DHS to confront its systemic errors and make the necessary reforms to ensure that such miscarriages of justice are not repeated.
