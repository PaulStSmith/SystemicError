'Drawing more attention': Trump's 'scathing' age defense is reportedly backfiring on him

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump’s “scathing” Truth Social rant in defense of his age and physical capabilities just made his problems worse, according to an analyst.

Journalist and political analyst Hayes Brown appeared on MSNOW over the weekend, where he flagged a “chaotic and unprecedented” power play Democrats might be able to try. After that, he was asked about a recent New York Times piece aboutTrumpand his aging.

Of Trump’s defenses to the aging report, the host asked, “Butyou say all this does is provethe point. How so?”

He replied, “I think that if you are pushing 80 and unwilling to admit, yes, I am aging. Yes, I am slowing down. Yes, some things may take me longer, but I’m doing it the best I can, that I think, and just pretending it’s not happening, that is a hit to your credibility.”

He continued:

“Like the I know that the mythos that he’s putting out there, this this aura of invincibility that he is eternal, that he is so powerful, so strong, the utmost of masculinity. I know that that is what he puts out there to his base. That’s the image he wants to project, and that aging undercuts that. So he wants to try to ignore it. But the problem is, aging is a reality. Like I feel like in this country especially, we fetishize youth and aging is a taboo. And so in the emphasis of the former and vitality and strength, we want to not even look at the latter.”

Brown went on to tie it all together, saying Trump is the victim and the cause of his own version of a “Streisand effect.”

“So in trying to deny the fact that he is getting older, Trump is I feel like drawing more attention to those moments where it’s obvious, like it’s almost a Streisand effect, you know what I’m saying?” he said Saturday. “Like where even trying to deny a story and put something down, you’re drawing more attention to it.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Age and The Art of Making Things Worse

Donald Trump’s attempt to defy the universal law of aging through a so-called “scathing” rant on Truth Social has once again spotlighted his uncanny skill in exacerbating his own controversies. Political analyst Hayes Brown’s commentary, as featured on MSNOW, slices through Trump’s denial with the precision of a scalpel, revealing a deeply flawed strategy that only emphasizes his vulnerabilities.

The Irony of Invincibility

Trump’s effort to cast himself as an ageless titan is nothing short of delusional. As Hayes Brown astutely points out, Trump’s refusal to acknowledge the natural process of aging doesn’t project strength; it highlights a fear of weakness. This is a man who, in his desperation to maintain the façade of “the utmost of masculinity,” ends up undermining his own credibility. It’s a classic case of a self-inflicted wound, where the more he tries to suppress the truth, the more glaringly obvious it becomes.

The Fetishization of Youth in Politics

Brown’s commentary also touches on a broader societal issue—the obsession with youth and the stigmatization of aging. Trump’s denial is symptomatic of a larger cultural problem where aging is seen as a defect rather than a natural progression of life. This is particularly relevant in the context of politics, where experience should be valued over the superficial appeal of vitality. Trump’s antics are a stark reminder of the unhealthy standards we’ve come to accept in our leaders.

The Streisand Effect in Full Force

The mention of the Streisand Effect—where attempting to suppress information only leads to more attention being drawn to it—is a fitting metaphor for Trump’s predicament. His vehement denials and attempts to assert his physical capabilities have only served to spotlight his age-related vulnerabilities. It’s a self-defeating strategy that turns every attempt at damage control into a spectacle, inviting even more scrutiny.

Conclusion: Age is Just a Number, but Denial is a Red Flag

Trump’s handling of the discourse around his age is a textbook example of how not to address personal vulnerabilities. In his quest to appear invulnerable, he has inadvertently shone a spotlight on his incapacity to confront reality. This isn’t just about age or physical capability; it’s about the ability to acknowledge and adapt to one’s limitations. A leader who can’t face the truth about themselves is hardly equipped to face the truths of the world they seek to govern.

The real issue at hand isn’t Trump’s advancing years; it’s his advancing detachment from reality. As Hayes Brown insightfully analyzes, the former president’s denialism does him no favors, only serving to underscore his insecurities and inability to accept the inevitable. In politics, as in life, the greatest strength often lies in facing our vulnerabilities, not in denying them.
