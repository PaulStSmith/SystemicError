The real problem isn't Trump — it's the dead enders who keep supporting him

Based on an article by D. Earl Stephens

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20263 min read

Racism Is the Product, But Power Is the Distributor

The Base Is Not the Whole Machine

The source article centers a grotesque White House spectacle: a wrestler thanked Trump, then lobbed a racist insult at Michelle Obama on public property, and Trump smiled instead of rejecting it. That is the evidence. The larger political point is not that one ugly crowd appeared and embarrassed itself. It is that the presidency, the White House stage, and the party apparatus around it turned open racial contempt into approved performance.

Who Holds Power

The people with actual institutional power are not the loud supporters being cursed at in the article. They are Trump, the White House operation, and the political infrastructure that decides what is invited, rewarded, tolerated, and normalized. A supporter can shout. A president can host, bless, and launder the shouting through federal ceremony. Those are not equivalent acts.

The source gets closest to the truth when it notes that Trump knew exactly what this base wanted. That is the real relationship: not a confused leader dragged along by racist followers, but a leader who recognizes that racist grievance is a usable political asset and builds a career around feeding it.

The Deliberate Part

The article is right to reject the comforting lie that this is accidental or merely “tone.” The insult to Michelle Obama was not a misunderstanding. Trump’s smile was not confusion. The event was not a breakdown in civility; it was a signal that public racism can be hosted, tolerated, and enjoyed at the highest level of government.

That matters because deliberate harm is often dressed up as populist spontaneity. The source refuses that cover, and correctly so. But it still leaves too much of the burden on the supporters alone, as if the crowd manufactured the system from scratch. They did not. They were invited into it, courted by it, and used by it.

The Blame Shift

Here the article’s frame weakens. It says the country is racist because of the people who support Trump, and that’s too neat. It makes racism sound like a moral flaw located mostly in followers, when the more useful political truth is structural: elites, candidates, media ecosystems, and institutions convert prejudice into power.

Yes, the supporters are complicit. Yes, they are responsible for what they endorse. But the operative force is not just their ugliness. It is the machinery that turns their ugliness into leverage. That machinery includes a president who smiles at public bigotry, a White House that hosts it, and a political culture that treats the whole thing as another day’s content.

The Pattern

This is the familiar authoritarian trick: degrade public life, then present the degradation as proof that opponents are hysterical. Scapegoat the weak, flatter the resentful, and convert cruelty into identity. The article names the racial contempt clearly. What it also reveals is the method: make domination feel like belonging.

That is why appeals to decency fail when they stop at embarrassment. Embarrassment is not the point. Power is. The point is to make institutions absorb the insult, normalize it, and keep operating. Once that happens, the insult is no longer an accident. It is governance.

The Deeper Lesson

The story is not that one man attracted bad people. It is that political power found a reliable way to organize bad faith, racial grievance, and institutional cowardice into a durable coalition. Trump is not the whole explanation. He is the brand name on a much older operation.

That is the system: racism as strategy, spectatorship as cover, and public office as the stage on which contempt is rewarded instead of punished.
