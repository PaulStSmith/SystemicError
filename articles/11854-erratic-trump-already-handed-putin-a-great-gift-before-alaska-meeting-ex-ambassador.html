'Erratic' Trump already handed Putin a 'great gift' before Alaska meeting: ex-ambassador

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Diplomatic Surrender: Trump's Alaskan Rendezvous with PutinThe Perils of PersonalizationThe Alaskan Gambit: A Concession Too Far?

Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump's agreement to meet with President Vladimir Putin in Alaska next week is the culmination of a series of wins the president is handing the Russian strongman, claimed former Ambassador Michael McFaul.

Appearing on MSNBC's "The Weekend," the diplomat would took great pains to explain he is worried about what Trump will agree to during with regard to Ukraine and after the meeting, explaining, "I'm also worried about the erratic nature of the president of the United States today. So he did say all those things. and then his special envoy goes to Moscow a few days ago, and suddenly the tone has changed. So I hope he'll remember his frustration there and i hope he won't roll into the meeting with Putin, you know, thinking that he's got a new relationship with him."

"I would just say this generally, the president personalizes bilateral relations with the rest of the world all the time, and he thinks that a good meeting is some kind of outcome," he elaborated. "A good meeting is a means to some other end that is good for the American people, and it's good for the free world. and oftentimes President Trump gets that mixed up. I hope he doesn't get that mixed up in Alaska."

As for Putin he added, "I've watched him. 'I've been in the room with him, he is good at this and President Trump better prepare. He better not just wing it and think that it's all going to work out, because it will not. This, he needs to take very seriously and he's already in the hole. I want to emphasize: he's already in the hole, he already has given Putin this great gift of inviting him to the United States of America and standing next to him, so he better be thinking hard about what we get in return."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Diplomatic Surrender: Trump's Alaskan Rendezvous with Putin

In what reads like a plot twist from a Cold War-era spy novel, Donald Trump's decision to meet with Vladimir Putin in Alaska is less a diplomatic chess move and more a bewildering capitulation to the Kremlin's long game. Former Ambassador Michael McFaul's recent appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend" offers a chilling insight into this latest episode of Trump's foreign policy escapades, which could be generously described as improvisational theater at best, and at worst, a series of unforced errors that weaken the United States' stance on the global stage.

Putin: The Chess Master vs. Trump: The Gamble

McFaul, with the pained expression of a man witnessing a slow-motion car crash, articulates a concern that resonates with anyone paying the slightest attention to international politics. Trump's erratic behavior and his tendency to personalize diplomatic relations have already set the stage for a meeting that seems skewed in Putin's favor. The former ambassador's observations underscore a critical point: Trump is walking into a high-stakes game with a player known for his strategic acumen, armed with little more than a hope for a 'new relationship'.

The Perils of Personalization

Trump's approach to foreign policy, as McFaul rightly points out, often conflates personal rapport with diplomatic success. This misunderstanding is not just a simple faux pas; it's a fundamental misreading of how global politics operates. Diplomacy is not a reality TV show where a handshake and a smile can resolve complex international conflicts. It requires preparation, strategic thinking, and a clear understanding of what's at stake — qualities that Trump seems to either disregard or misunderstand entirely.

The Alaskan Gambit: A Concession Too Far?

The decision to invite Putin to the United States is emblematic of Trump's misguided belief in the power of his personal diplomacy. McFaul's assertion that Trump is "already in the hole" with this invitation speaks volumes. It's not just a meeting; it's a symbolic victory for Putin, a tacit acknowledgment of his influence and a boon to his standing both domestically and internationally. The optics of this encounter alone are a gift to the Russian strongman, and one must wonder, as McFaul does, what the United States is getting in return.

The Bottom Line

As the world watches this unfolding drama, the questions raised by McFaul cannot be ignored. Trump's predilection for unpredictability and his apparent lack of preparation for a meeting of this magnitude are not just worrying; they're potentially dangerous. The Alaska meeting is not just a diplomatic engagement; it's a litmus test for the United States' current foreign policy direction under Trump's stewardship.

In the grand scheme of things, one can only hope that the president takes McFaul's warnings to heart and realizes the gravity of the situation. However, if past behavior is any indicator of future performance, we might be witnessing a significant misstep in real-time. The stakes are high, and the consequences of failure are not limited to diplomatic embarrassment — they could shape international relations for years to come.

In conclusion, as Trump prepares to meet Putin in Alaska, it is imperative that he does so with a clear strategy and a firm grasp of the larger geopolitical picture. Anything less would not only be a disservice to the office he holds but also a strategic blunder that could cost the United States dearly. The world is watching, and history will not forget.
