America's unmet promise — and most shameful secret

The Number Behind the Myth

The source gives the United States what its political class hates most: a comparison point. In a country with a $32 trillion economy, the basic measures of health, food, work, and education are not lagging because of scarcity. They are lagging because the system does not convert wealth into broad human security. That is the whole story, and it is not subtle.

Power, Not Pity

Actual institutional power sits with lawmakers, presidents, and the policy machinery that turns national income into private gain or public provision. The article makes that plain enough in the places where it matters: the Trump-signed 2025 tax and spending package is projected to strip health coverage from millions, and it already helped cut food assistance for millions more. That is not drift. That is governance.

The decision-makers were not confused about the consequences. They chose them.

The Cleanest Form of Misdirection

The language of “underperformance” is too polite for what the data describe. It suggests a system failing to meet an objective it accepted in good faith. But the evidence points the other way: the state is delivering exactly what powerful actors have built it to deliver. The gains in wealth have been skewed toward the richest Americans, while wages, food security, and access to care have been pushed downward for everyone else.

Calling this a shortfall softens responsibility. It turns policy into weather.

Harm Disguised as Normal

The article is strongest when it stops pretending the U.S. merely “could do better” and names the actual mechanism: cuts to health insurance, cuts to food aid, a federal minimum wage too low to protect dignity, and an economy that lets prosperity pool at the top while basic life gets harder below. The result is not an unfortunate mismatch between ideals and reality. It is a distributional settlement enforced through law.

Even the improvements cited in the past were tied to policy choices, like expanded insurance under the Affordable Care Act. When coverage rose, health scores rose. When the political system reversed course, the scores fell. That is not a mystery. It is causation.

The Founding Promise as Cover Story

Invoking “the general welfare” in a republic that leaves millions without reliable food or care is not a noble reminder. It is an indictment of a political order that keeps reciting its own civic scripture while violating it in practice. The U.S. does not lack resources. It lacks will, and more specifically, it lacks institutions willing to treat human survival as a binding obligation rather than an optional benefit.

The Systemic Error

The deeper pattern is simple: American power is structured to protect wealth first and redistribute pain downward second. That is why the richest country in the dataset can rank near the bottom on basic human outcomes. The public is told this is complexity, inertia, or hard tradeoffs. In reality, it is a durable political design that converts abundance into inequality and then calls the result unavoidable.
