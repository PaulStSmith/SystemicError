Republican kills governor bid over fears 'family squabble' would tear apart GOP

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Dave Jost. (Courtesty of Dave Jost for Governor)

Ohio Attorney General Dave Yost announced the suspension of his gubernatorial campaign in an email to supporters on Friday.

Yost said he has enjoyed traveling around the state engaging with Ohioans in his bid to succeed term-limited Governor Mike DeWine, but “it is also apparent that a steep climb to the nomination for governor has become a vertical cliff.”

“I do not wish to divide my political party or my state with a quixotic battle over the small differences between my vision and that of my opponent,” Yost wrote, seeming to refer to fellow candidate Vivek Ramaswamy.

“I am simply not that important.” Ramaswamy, an entrepreneur who was also the co-leader with Elon Musk of the Trump-created Department of Government Efficiency, received the Ohio Republican Party’s gubernatorial endorsement on May 9.

Yost said the state “has challenges ahead” and Columbus “needs re-engineering, not demolition.” He warned of a “comeback attempt” by former U.S. Sen. Sherrod Brown that could “unwind all of the good that the last 15 years of Republican leadership has brought.”

“This is a time to protect Ohio, not a time for a family squabble,” Yost wrote.

Brown has not formally announced any bid for elected office, but former Ohio Department of Health director Amy Acton has already thrown her hat in the ring to vie for the governor’s seat as a Democrat.

Yost, whose term as state attorney general runs until Jan. 2027, plans to “continue to fight for Ohio and Ohioans” for the rest of his tenure, but “I suspect that this is not my final chapter,” he told supporters.

Our Analysis:

The Surrender of Dave Yost: A Tactical Retreat or Political Cowardice?

In a move that surprised few but disappointed many, Ohio Attorney General Dave Yost announced the suspension of his gubernatorial campaign with a flourish of rhetoric that barely conceals the bitter taste of defeat. Yost, in his email to supporters, casts his withdrawal not as a capitulation but as a noble sacrifice to avoid dividing the GOP and the state of Ohio. However, a closer examination reveals a narrative rife with contradictions, fear of competition, and a thinly veiled attempt to remain politically relevant.

Yost's Admission: The Vertical Cliff

Yost's admission that his path to the gubernatorial nomination has turned from a "steep climb to a vertical cliff" is a stark acknowledgment of his campaign's failure to gain traction among Ohio's Republican base. This metaphor, while poetic, speaks volumes about the insurmountable odds Yost faced against his primary opponent, Vivek Ramaswamy. The latter, an entrepreneur and a figure who stood alongside Elon Musk in the Trump-created Department of Government Efficiency, secured the Ohio Republican Party’s gubernatorial endorsement on May 9. This endorsement, a critical blow to Yost's aspirations, signifies not just a preference for Ramaswamy's vision but a repudiation of Yost's political viability within his party.

The Quixotic Battle and the Illusion of Small Differences

Yost's assertion that he does not wish to divide his party or state over "small differences" between his vision and that of Ramaswamy's is both ironic and disingenuous. Political battles, especially primaries, are inherently about highlighting differences, no matter how small, to provide the electorate with a clear choice. By downplaying these differences, Yost not only undermines the democratic process but also insults the intelligence of Ohio's voters. This statement reeks of a politician trying to save face by pretending his retreat is a strategic move rather than an acknowledgment of his impending defeat.

The Boogeyman of Sherrod Brown's Comeback

Yost's warning about a "comeback attempt" by former U.S. Sen. Sherrod Brown to "unwind all of the good that the last 15 years of Republican leadership has brought" is a classic fear-mongering tactic. It's a desperate attempt to rally the Republican base by invoking the specter of a Democratic resurgence under Brown, who has not even formally announced any bid for elected office. This maneuver is transparent in its intent to distract from Yost's own campaign shortcomings by shifting focus to an imaginary threat.

The Future of Dave Yost

Yost's parting note to his supporters, hinting that "this is not my final chapter," is a clear indication that he intends to remain in the political arena, likely eyeing future opportunities. This ambition, while understandable, does not absolve him of the present failure. His decision to bow out of the race, while pragmatic, leaves many questions unanswered about his commitment to addressing the "challenges ahead" for Ohio.

In conclusion, Dave Yost's suspension of his gubernatorial campaign is a tale of political realism clashing with personal ambition. His attempt to frame his withdrawal as a selfless act is undermined by the clear political calculus at play. Ohioans, and indeed the GOP, deserve a more forthright explanation for his retreat. Yost's political future may be uncertain, but this chapter of his career will be remembered not for its grace, but for its glaring avoidance of a fight he deemed unwinnable.
