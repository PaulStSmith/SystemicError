'He's officially exiled!' Don Jr throws tantrum as Pentagon insider dishes on Hegseth

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump Jr., son of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump, speaks during a rally for Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump at Madison Square Garden, in New York, U.S., October 27, 2024. REUTERS/Andrew Kelly

Following yet another development in the "Signalgate" scandal, John Ullyot, a longtime Donald Trump insider who resigned as a top Pentagon adviser last week, warned that Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth is perliously close to losing control of the Pentagon, if not losing his job outright — but none of this sat well with Donald Trump Jr. who took to X on Monday to declare him excommunicated from MAGA.

"This guy is not America First," he wrote. "I’ve been hearing for years that he works his a-- off to subvert my father’s agenda. That ends today. He’s officially exiled from our movement."

As some commenters were swift to point out, Ullyot is in fact a longtime Trump loyalist who went to bat for him in bold and controversial ways.

For example, noted Aaron Blake of The Washington Post, "Ullyot in 2018 sought to rein in a VA diversity officer who wanted to condemn the racist violent [protest] in Charlottesville more, because it was at-odds with Trump's message." At the time, the president infamously triggered outrage by saying there were "very fine people on both sides" of the neo-Nazi demonstration, although in recent years Trump has tried to deny he ever said this.

Now, Ullyot — despite his history of sticking by Trump — is warning in a Politico article that Hegseth's repeated use of Signal group chats to share highly sensitive military plans with people not cleared to learn them, most recently his wife and brother, has thrown top Pentagon leadership into complete disarray.

He said the problem has been exacerbated by his firing top officials from the Pentagon.

“It’s been a month of total chaos at the Pentagon,” wrote Ullyot in Politico. “From leaks of sensitive operational plans to mass firings, the dysfunction is now a major distraction for the president.” He added that, "President Donald Trump has a strong record of holding his top officials to account. Given that, it’s hard to see Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth remaining in his role for much longer."

Our Analysis:

The Tangled Webs of "Signalgate" and MAGA's Internal Strife

In the latest installment of the political circus that could be dubbed the Trumpian era, we find ourselves dissecting yet another scandal, affectionately termed "Signalgate." This saga involves John Ullyot, a now-former top Pentagon adviser and a figure previously known for his unwavering loyalty to Donald Trump, issuing a dire warning about the state of the Pentagon under Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth's stewardship. Ullyot's cautionary tale, however, has landed him in hot water with none other than Donald Trump Jr., leading to a public excommunication from the MAGA movement on X (formerly Twitter).

The Irony of Loyalty

Let's start with the delicious irony that is Ullyot's fall from grace. Here we have a man who, as Aaron Blake of The Washington Post pointed out, once sought to silence a VA diversity officer from condemning the racist violence in Charlottesville, aligning himself with Trump's infamous "very fine people on both sides" comment. One could argue that Ullyot's actions epitomized the MAGA creed: loyalty to Trump above all else, even at the expense of moral decency. Yet, this very loyalty seems to have bought him nothing but a one-way ticket out of the MAGA movement when he dared to criticize the current state of affairs under Hegseth's leadership.

The Hypocrisy of Excommunication

Donald Trump Jr.'s swift move to "excommunicate" Ullyot from the MAGA movement highlights a broader issue within political movements that preach loyalty but practice a form of conditional allegiance. The idea that Ullyot, a "longtime Trump loyalist," could suddenly be deemed not "America First" by the younger Trump for raising valid concerns about national security risks, is as laughable as it is hypocritical. This knee-jerk reaction to silence and ostracize a once-valued insider for airing dirty laundry in public is emblematic of the MAGA movement's inability to tolerate dissent, however well-founded it may be.

The Grave Implications of "Signalgate"

Moving beyond the MAGA drama, the core issue at hand is the alleged mishandling of sensitive military information by Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. According to Ullyot, Hegseth's use of Signal group chats to share highly sensitive military plans with individuals not cleared to receive them has thrown the Pentagon into "total chaos." This isn't just a petty internal squabble; it's a matter of national security. The casual dissemination of operational plans, if true, not only undermines the integrity of the Pentagon but also poses a significant risk to the safety and security of the United States and its allies.

The Trumpian Accountability Paradox

Ullyot's claim that "President Donald Trump has a strong record of holding his top officials to account" would almost be comedic if the stakes weren't so high. The Trump administration's track record of accountability is, at best, inconsistent, marked by a revolving door of officials who have been fired or resigned under less-than-clear circumstances. The notion that Hegseth might be removed for his alleged indiscretions feels more like wishful thinking than a probable outcome, given the administration's history of erratic responses to controversy and scandal.

In conclusion, "Signalgate" is more than just another Trump-era scandal; it's a revealing look into the practices and priorities of the MAGA movement and the Trump administration. The excommunication of John Ullyot for speaking out against what he perceives as dangerous practices within the Pentagon underscores the movement's intolerance for criticism and its penchant for loyalty over logic. Meanwhile, the allegations against Hegseth, if true, highlight a disturbing disregard for national security protocols, further complicating an already chaotic administration's legacy.
