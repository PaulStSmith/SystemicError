'Lunatic': Fox News urged to 'bench' host over vow to 'avenge' Charlie Kirk’s death

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Fox News’ Jesse Watters in Palm Beach, Florida in 2018 (Gage Skidmore)

After MAGA activist and Turning Point USA founder Charlie Kirk was fatally shot in Utah on Wednesday afternoon, a long list of centrist Democrats, liberals and progressives wasted no time wholeheartedly condemning the attack — from California Gov. Gavin Newsom to former President Joe Biden to Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vermont) and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez.

Former MSNBC host Mehdi Hasan, who is originally from the U.K., tweeted, "Charlie Kirk called me a 'lunatic' and a 'prostitute' and demanded I be deported. Nothing, nothing, justifies killing him, or robbing his kids of their dad. We don't know the identity or motive of the shooter but murder can never be the response to political disagreements."

On Fox News, however, far-right host Jesse Watters had a threatening tone when he called for retaliation against Kirk's critics.

Watters told viewers, "They are at war with us, whether we want to accept it or not. And what are we going to do about it? Charlie would want us to put as much pressure on these people as possible….. This is unacceptable, and it has to stop. And it has to stop now. And everybody's accountable. And we're watching what they're saying on television and who's saying what. The politicians, the media, and all these rats out there. This can never happen again. It ends now…. This is a turning point."

Watters called for the right to "avenge Charlie's death" — a comment that journalist Pete Twinklage found especially disturbing.

On X, formerly Twitter, Twinklage posted, "this is spectacularly dangerous rhetoric. the Murdochs need to bench Watters immediately. vowing to 'avenge' Kirk's horrific assassination instead of calling for calm is beyond irresponsible. the country is a tinderbox and this lunatic is juggling dynamite and matches."

Watters' comments are generating a lot of discussion on X.

New Jersey-based Kim Hurdman tweeted, "Why would the Murdoch’s bench him? They installed him. The entire network promotes this mindset and ideology. Telling people their political opponents are enemy[.] They spent 40 yrs getting fabulously wealthy telling older white pp who to be afraid of and who to hate. Get real."

Journalist Jessica Durkin posted, "Meanwhile MSNBC is apologizing up and down because Matthew Dowd said the truth on that channel that Kirk speaks hate."

U.S. Marines veteran Gene Frenkel tweeted, "MSNBC fired Dowd.. Watters deserves the same."

Another U.S. Marines veteran, Paul Nun, commented, "Jesse Watters made an entire career around stoking division. He failed to mention the Republican terrorist who murdered Democratic politicians a few weeks ago."

X user Martin Judd remarked, "Murdochs have just announced the most radical far-right son will run the place. So he is not going to be of any help."

Journalist/author John L. Valentine wrote, "This punk isn't going to do anything but run his mouth. He's a TV warrior. Brave behind his desk. He'll leave the violence he's trying to incite to others. His call for more violence won't end the violence. It'll only exacerbate it."

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Rhetoric of Jesse Watters: A Call to Sanity Amidst Political Violence

The recent assassination of MAGA activist and Turning Point USA founder Charlie Kirk has plunged the nation into a deep reflection on the state of political discourse and violence. While a chorus of voices from across the political spectrum, including prominent figures like California Gov. Gavin Newsom, former President Joe Biden, Sen. Bernie Sanders, and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, have rightly condemned this heinous act, Fox News host Jesse Watters has chosen a path fraught with peril and irresponsibility.

Jesse Watters' Call for Retaliation: A Dangerous Escalation

In a tone that can only be described as incendiary, Watters has called for retaliation against Kirk's critics, painting a target on the backs of those who dare to disagree politically. His words, "They are at war with us, whether we want to accept it or not. And what are we going to do about it? Charlie would want us to put as much pressure on these people as possible…This is unacceptable, and it has to stop. And it has to stop now," are not just a call to action but a battle cry that risks further violence.

The Irresponsibility of Avenge Rhetoric

Watters' suggestion that the right should "avenge Charlie's death" is not only spectacularly dangerous, as journalist Pete Twinklage noted, but it is also a stark departure from the calls for calm and unity that we desperately need. This rhetoric does nothing but fan the flames of division, pushing the nation closer to the brink of a conflict that nobody wants. It is the kind of speech that has no place in responsible media, especially in a country already grappling with political violence.

The Murdoch Conundrum

The reaction to Watters' comments on X, formerly Twitter, highlights a broader issue with the Murdoch-owned Fox News. As user Kim Hurdman pointed out, the network has long promoted a divisive and fearmongering ideology. The decision by the Murdochs to escalate this rhetoric by potentially backing Watters' statements with inaction speaks volumes about the dangerous path Fox News is willing to tread for viewership and profit.

A Double Standard in Media Accountability

The contrast between the treatment of Matthew Dowd at MSNBC for speaking the truth about Kirk and the apparent lack of repercussions for Watters at Fox News underscores a glaring double standard in media accountability. While Dowd was fired for his comments, Watters' call for violence seems to have been met with silence from his employers, raising serious questions about the role of media outlets in perpetuating a cycle of violence and division.

Conclusion: The Need for Responsible Journalism

In these turbulent times, the role of journalists and media personalities is more critical than ever. They have the power to influence public opinion and shape the discourse surrounding events of national significance. Jesse Watters' reckless and dangerous comments serve as a reminder of the responsibility that comes with this power. It is a responsibility to foster informed and civil dialogue, not to incite violence or advocate for retaliation. The path forward must be paved with a commitment to truth, integrity, and the pursuit of unity. Anything less is not just a disservice to the audience but a threat to the very fabric of our society.
