'Propaganda hire': Analysts admonish MAGA-aligned economist Trump could pick to lead BLS

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Reports indicate President Donald Trump is interviewing candidates for the vacant Bureau of Labor Statistics Commissioner position, and analysts admonished one potential candidate on Sunday.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump is considering hiring the Heritage Foundation's chief economist, E.J. Antoni, to lead the BLS. The report indicates that Antoni has been critical of the Trump administration and has also voiced concerns about revisions to jobs data.

Trump used a significant revision to the most recent job numbers as a pretext to fire the former BLS Commissioner, Erika McEntarfer.

Antoni has support from MAGA firebrand Steve Bannon as well, according to WSJ.

Economists and analysts took to social media to share their thoughts about Antoni being in line to lead the BLS.

"Propaganda hire," journalist and author Mark Chadbourn posted on Bluesky.

"There are so many well qualified (sic) conservative labor economists that he can put in this position, but he won’t because they won’t cook the books," Scott Imberman, professor of economics and education at Michigan State University, posted on Bluesky.

"This administration rewards those who do not care for the truth," Alex Nowrasteh, vice president of economics and social policy at The Cato Institute, posted on X.

Susan Dynarski, economist and professor at Harvard University, wrote on Bluesky that Antoni's dissertation only has one citation. For comparison, McEntarfer published 47 peer-reviewed papers and had more than 1,300 citations, according to Dynarski.

Bobby Kogan, senior director of federal budget policy for the Center for American Progress, wrote on Bluesky that Antoni's economic analysis, which he shared with a Congressional committee, was flawed.

"When I testified before the House next to this guy, in his *prepared* testimony on rising costs, he double-counted inflation," Kogan posted on Bluesky.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Controversial Pick for BLS Commissioner: A Deep Dive

In a move that's sparked widespread controversy, President Donald Trump appears to be considering E.J. Antoni, the Heritage Foundation's chief economist, for the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Commissioner role. This choice is contentious, not just for Antoni's credentials, but for the broader implications it spells out for the administration's approach to economic data and policy.

Criticisms and Concerns

First off, let's tackle the elephant in the room: Antoni's qualifications—or perceived lack thereof. According to a report by The Wall Street Journal, Antoni's candidacy has raised eyebrows, with critics labeling him a "propaganda hire." This is a stark accusation, but not without its reasons.

E.J. Antoni vs. Erika McEntarfer: The comparison here is telling. McEntarfer, the former BLS Commissioner, was ousted following a significant revision to job numbers, which Trump used as a pretext for her firing. McEntarfer's credentials included 47 peer-reviewed papers and over 1,300 citations. Antoni, on the other hand, has a dissertation with just one citation. This stark disparity in academic contributions cannot be overstated. It raises critical questions about Antoni's suitability for a role that is fundamentally grounded in rigorous statistical analysis and integrity.

Concerns Over Integrity and Expertise: Critics, including Scott Imberman and Alex Nowrasteh, have voiced concerns that Trump's potential pick is less about expertise and more about loyalty. The fear is that Antoni would be more inclined to "cook the books" rather than uphold the rigorous, unbiased standards expected of the BLS. This is a damning indictment of the administration's approach to crucial economic data, suggesting a preference for propaganda over professionalism.

Flawed Economic Analysis: Bobby Kogan's critique of Antoni's economic analysis further underscores concerns about the latter's competence. According to Kogan, Antoni's prepared testimony before the House on rising costs contained significant errors, such as double-counting inflation. For a role as critical as the BLS Commissioner, where precision and accuracy are paramount, such mistakes are not just troubling; they are unacceptable.

The Role of Ideology

The support from Steve Bannon, a MAGA firebrand, is particularly revealing. It suggests that Antoni's potential nomination is as much about furthering a specific political ideology as it is about his qualifications. This intertwines the BLS with the administration's political agenda, potentially compromising the bureau's integrity and independence.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The consideration of E.J. Antoni for the BLS Commissioner role sets a dangerous precedent. It signals a willingness to prioritize loyalty and ideological alignment over expertise and integrity. Such a move not only undermines the credibility of the BLS but also jeopardizes the reliability of economic data that policymakers, businesses, and the American public depend on.

In a role that should epitomize the pinnacle of economic analysis and integrity, the choice of Antoni—if it comes to pass—would be a stark deviation from these standards. It's a systemic error that could have far-reaching consequences for the credibility of the Trump administration and the economic well-being of the country.
