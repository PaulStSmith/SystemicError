'Completely ridiculous': GOP's 'shameful' Jeffrey Epstein stunt torn to shreds on CNN

Comer’s Epstein Theater Is Designed to Fail

The Power Is in the Process

The relevant power here is not the former Barclays CEO trying to talk his way through a committee interview. It is House Oversight Chairman James Comer, who controlled the terms of the interview and chose not to put witnesses under oath. That is the decision that shaped the outcome. If the committee is serious, it sets rules that force truth. If it is unserious, it stages a hearing and calls it oversight.

Rep. Robert Garcia’s complaint is blunt for a reason: when the chair strips the interview of oath-backed accountability, he is not investigating so much as curating deniable appearances.

Unsurprising Dishonesty, Enabled

Garcia says Jes Staley denied having a close relationship with Jeffrey Epstein even though the committee has thousands of pages of documents and exchanges describing a “best friendship.” That is the substance. The larger point is simpler: untruthful witnesses are not the mystery here. A process built to permit evasiveness produces evasiveness.

Comer did not just fail to catch a liar. He created a setting in which lying carried little immediate cost. That is not oversight. It is permission.

The Misdirection

The familiar move in these Republican spectacles is to treat the witness’s dishonesty as the central scandal while obscuring the institutional choice that made dishonesty easy. The story is not that one executive was slippery. The story is that the committee chair structured the interview in a way that weakened accountability before it began.

That is the misdirection: attention flows toward the evasive witness, while responsibility rests with the lawmaker who refused basic enforcement tools. When the question is whether powerful people can answer for their conduct, “not under oath” is not a technical detail. It is the point.

Epstein as a Test of Democratic Seriousness

Epstein-related oversight is supposed to expose networks of complicity, not protect them with procedural cosplay. If lawmakers possess thousands of pages showing a close relationship, then the burden is on the committee to extract truthful testimony, not produce another round of sanitized non-answers.

Garcia’s insistence that witnesses be brought back under oath is the bare minimum. The fact that this even needs saying tells you how low the bar has been set by Comer’s leadership.

The Pattern

This is the broader political pattern: perform the appearance of accountability while avoiding the mechanisms that make accountability real. Republicans keep proving they understand spectacle better than oversight. They know how to hold a camera-ready hearing. They do not want the harder thing, which is to compel testimony that might implicate the powerful.

That is how institutional cowardice works. It turns serious abuse into managed theater, lets the chair escape responsibility behind process, and leaves the public with the illusion of investigation instead of the substance of it.
