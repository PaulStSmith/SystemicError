'Hand it back to the president': Top Trump aide asks Nobel winner to cede prize

Roger Stone, a longtime adviser to Donald Trump, called on Nobel Peace Prize winner Maria Corina Machado to relinquish the award to the president.

Following a ceasefire deal between Israel and Gaza, Stone told Real America’s Voice host Jack Posobiec that he was “not happy that the Norwegian-based Nobel Peace Prize Committee elected to give their award to Maria Corina Machado.”

“The right thing to do would be for her to ask or maybe have Marco Rubio ask her if President Trump can give her the award,” he insisted. “And then she should hand it back to the president.”

“He deserves this award, even though I thought those elitist globalist freaks at the Nobel Peace Prize Committee would likely not give it to him.”

Stone suggested that former President Barack Obama should not have received the award.

“I really think the president deserves this award,” he remarked. “And I would hope that Maria Corina Machado would consider accepting it and giving it to the president, bestowing it to the president.”

“Yeah, Roger, that’s an incredible idea,” Posobiec agreed. “So she’ll get it there and then I suppose you’d have to bring it to, he could invite her to the White House perhaps and have the ceremony there and invite the Nobel Committee as well.”

Our Analysis:

Roger Stone’s Nobel Peace Prize Fantasy: A Study in Delusion

In what can only be described as a bizarre episode of political theater, Roger Stone, a longtime adviser to Donald Trump, has embarked on a quixotic campaign urging Nobel Peace Prize winner Maria Corina Machado to hand over her award to the former president. This spectacle unfolded on Real America’s Voice, a platform that has become a haven for right-wing conspiracy theories and unfiltered Trumpian propaganda.

Let’s dissect the absurdity of Stone’s proposition and the underlying motives that might be driving such an outlandish suggestion.

The Illusion of Deservedness

Stone’s assertion that Donald Trump deserves the Nobel Peace Prize for orchestrating a ceasefire deal between Israel and Gaza is a masterclass in selective memory. It conveniently ignores the broader context of Trump’s foreign policy, which was often characterized by its unpredictability, antagonism, and a notable lack of lasting peace achievements on the global stage. This attempt to rewrite history and paint Trump as a peacemaker is not just disingenuous; it’s a distortion of reality.

The Nobel Peace Prize: Not a Game of Hot Potato

The Nobel Peace Prize, despite its controversies, remains one of the most prestigious accolades in the world, recognized for its intention to honor those who have made significant contributions to peace. Roger Stone’s suggestion that Maria Corina Machado—a figure celebrated for her advocacy for democracy and human rights—should simply hand over her award to Trump is not just disrespectful; it fundamentally misunderstands the nature of the prize. It’s not a trophy to be passed around at the whims of political allies or re-allocated as a gesture of partisan loyalty.

The Obama Obsession

Stone’s swipe at former President Barack Obama, suggesting that he too did not deserve the Nobel Peace Prize, is a tired refrain within certain conservative circles. It echoes a longstanding obsession with delegitimizing Obama’s achievements and, by extension, any recognition that the Nobel Committee has given to individuals who do not align with their political views. This fixation reveals less about the perceived flaws in the Nobel’s selection process and more about the relentless drive to undermine any legacy or institution that does not serve their narrow political interests.

The Art of the Impossible Demand

Asking Maria Corina Machado to “bestow” her Nobel Peace Prize on Donald Trump is not just ludicrous; it’s a strategic play in the theater of the absurd. Stone and his cohort are fully aware that such a request is impossible and impractical. Yet, by making it, they accomplish several objectives: they reinsert Trump into the global conversation about peace and leadership, they attempt to delegitimize the Nobel Committee, and they rally their base around a perceived injustice against their leader.

Conclusion: A Distraction, Not a Debate

Roger Stone’s comments should not be seen as a serious critique of the Nobel Peace Prize or its recipients. Instead, it’s a calculated attempt to distract from the real issues and controversies surrounding Trump’s presidency and to stir up the base with an impossible proposal. It’s political theater designed to entertain, enrage, and distract—not to contribute to any meaningful discussion about peace or leadership on the global stage.

In the end, Stone’s suggestion is nothing more than a fantasy—a political sideshow meant to keep the Trump mythology alive and well in the minds of his supporters. It’s not just an incredible idea; it’s an incredibly bad idea, rooted in delusion and partisan pettiness.
