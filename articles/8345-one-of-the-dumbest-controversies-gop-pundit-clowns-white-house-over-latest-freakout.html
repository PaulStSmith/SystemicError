'One of the dumbest controversies': GOP pundit clowns White House over latest freakout

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media while on his way to board Marine One to depart for Joint Base Andrews, from the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 22, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

After President Donald Trump threatened to execute several Democratic lawmakers who urged service members to defy illegal orders, one conservative commentator is pushing back on the administration for what he labeled as “one of the dumbest controversies” he’d seen.

“I really wish both sides would take it down a notch,” said Andy McCarthy, a conservative commentator and former assistant U.S. attorney, appearing on Fox News Tuesday.

“I think the Democrats are wrong to have pitched this the way they did; the reaction to it is a way, way overreaction, and we’d be better off if both sides just took it down about 11 notches.”

Last week, a handful of Democratic lawmakers published a video in which they urged active duty service members to “refuse illegal orders,” sparking an immediate reaction from Trump: “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH” he wrote on social media.

And on Monday, the Pentagon announced that it was investigating Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) – a former Navy pilot – for his participation in the video, an investigation that could lead to a potential court-martial.

Asked if the Justice Department “or anybody else” had a legitimate case against the handful of Democratic lawmakers, McCarthy dismissed the allegations against them out of hand.

“The Democrats, if they want to complain about lawless orders, should specify what orders they’re talking about, that’s perfectly reasonable. But in a country with the First Amendment, they’re allowed to make even incorrect statements!” McCarthy said.

“As it happens, it’s a correct statement of law to say that troops don’t have to follow illegal orders, and you’d never be able to make a case out of that. At the most, what you would have – if they were truly interfering with the carrying out of lawful orders – you’d have insubordination, you wouldn’t have anything like sedition or insurrection, which requires a violent uprising.”

Our Analysis:

The Absurd Theatre of Political Controversy: A Critical Analysis

In a spectacle that mirrors the absurdity often found in the darkest comedies, President Donald Trump’s reaction to Democratic lawmakers urging service members to defy illegal orders has catapulted the political arena into a frenzy, marking what could easily be dubbed as “one of the dumbest controversies” of recent times. This scenario, as relayed in a recent news article, offers a perfect storm of overreaction, misinterpretation, and a fundamental misunderstanding of legal principles and constitutional rights.

The Misguided Missile of Accusation

At the heart of this farce is Trump’s hyperbolic response to the Democratic lawmakers’ video, which, in essence, called for service members to adhere to the law by refusing illegal orders. Trump’s branding of this act as “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH” on social media is not only a gross overreaction but a dangerous misrepresentation of what sedition actually entails. Sedition, by its very definition, requires an incitement to rebellion against the authority of a state, not a call to uphold the legal and moral obligations of its military.

The Pentagon’s Perplexing Participation

Adding to the theater is the Pentagon’s decision to investigate Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) for his role in the video, a move that could potentially lead to a court-martial. This investigation, ostensibly for encouraging the refusal of illegal orders, ironically underscores the administration’s own misunderstanding of military law and the First Amendment. The fact that a former Navy pilot and current senator is under scrutiny for advocating lawful conduct within the military is not just ironic—it borders on the Kafkaesque.

The Voice of Reason in a Sea of Absurdity

Enter Andy McCarthy, a conservative commentator and former assistant U.S. attorney, who appears to be one of the few voices of reason in this scenario. McCarthy’s plea for both sides to “take it down about 11 notches” reflects the sentiment of many who view this controversy as a needless escalation of political theatre. His assertion that the Democrats’ call for refusing illegal orders is not only protected by the First Amendment but is also a correct statement of law, highlights the baselessness of the accusations of sedition and insurrection.

McCarthy’s dismissal of the potential for these actions to be construed as sedition or insurrection is grounded in a clear understanding of the law. As he aptly notes, at most, the Democrats’ actions could be seen as insubordination if they were actually interfering with the carrying out of lawful orders. However, the call to refuse illegal orders does not, by any stretch of the imagination, constitute insubordination, let alone sedition or insurrection.

Conclusion: The Need for a Return to Sanity

This controversy, if it can even be called that, is a stark reminder of the current state of political discourse in the United States. The escalation from a call to uphold legal and moral obligations to accusations of sedition punishable by death is not just an overreaction; it’s a dangerous slide into the realms of authoritarian rhetoric. McCarthy’s call for de-escalation is not just sensible; it’s necessary for the preservation of rational discourse and the principles of democracy.

The real question we should be asking is not whether the Democrats’ video constituted seditious behavior but why the administration is so quick to label it as such. In a healthy democracy, urging adherence to the law, especially within the military, should be commended—not condemned. As this controversy unfolds, one can only hope for a collective return to reason, legal principle, and the constitutional rights that form the bedrock of American society.
