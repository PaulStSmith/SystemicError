Valentine's Day prices soar to  'crisis levels' under Trump: analyst

President Donald Trump‘s tariffs have hit an important product ahead of Valentine’s Day — chocolate.

CNN reporter Matt Egan described why chocolate prices have soared more than 14% higher than the more than 7% price hike compared to 2025. But it’s not only chocolate prices that are climbing as a result of Trump’s tariffs and inflation, plus other international factors.

“The problem started with a shortage of the main ingredient in chocolate cocoa beans, extreme weather in West Africa, where most of the cocoa on the planet comes from, caused bad harvests,” Egan said.

“So you had a situation where supply was crashing, and demand was not, and prices had nowhere to go but up,” Egan explained. “Cocoa prices. They went from around $2,500 a metric ton to almost 13,000, a Hershey executive told CNN’s Kate Bolduan earlier this week that the company experienced unprecedented cocoa inflation. And the problem is that a lot of the chocolate on the store shelves right now, they are still priced at or near those crisis levels now, the good news, however, is that prices have started to come down for cocoa, and analysts are hopeful that prices will start to flatten out, hopefully by Easter and ideally start to come down in time for Halloween.”

Other potential Valentine’s Day gifts are also seeing price jumps.

“Now, as you mentioned, it’s not just chocolate that’s gotten more expensive,” Egan told CNN anchor Boris Sanchez. “Some other go-to gift ideas for Valentine’s Day also going up in price. If you look at flowers and indoor plants today’s inflation report showed a 6% year over year increase. Their jewelry up by 7%, and that likely reflects those steep U.S. tariffs on imports.”

Although it might not be all bad news, Egan added.

“But I do want to leave you with some good news and some possible gift-buying ideas if you’re looking at cosmetics and perfume, those prices are only up by 1%,” he said. “That’s less than the overall rate of inflation, and wine is actually cheaper than this point. Last year it’s down by 1%, so Boris, maybe you want to go the red wine and perfume route this year because those prices at least are doing a little bit better than chocolate.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tariffs and the Bittersweet Reality of Valentine’s Day

As we delve into the confectionery chaos that has unfolded ahead of Valentine’s Day, it’s impossible not to notice the bitter aftertaste left by President Donald Trump’s economic policies. CNN’s Matt Egan has shed light on a phenomenon that’s as perplexing as it is frustrating: a significant spike in chocolate prices, soaring a whopping 14% higher than the already steep 7% hike in 2025. But, as we unwrap the layers of this issue, it becomes evident that chocolate is merely the tip of the iceberg.

The Cocoa Crisis: A Recipe for Disaster

At the heart of this sugary saga lies a critical shortage of cocoa beans, the lifeblood of chocolate. Extreme weather in West Africa, responsible for the lion’s share of the globe’s cocoa, has devastated harvests. Egan poignantly captures the essence of the dilemma: supply plummeting while demand stubbornly remains, creating a perfect storm for prices to skyrocket. Cocoa prices have catapulted from around $2,500 a metric ton to an almost inconceivable $13,000. The repercussions? Companies like Hershey are grappling with “unprecedented cocoa inflation,” leading to chocolate products that linger at crisis-level prices on store shelves.

A Valentine’s Day Villain: Trump’s Tariffs

While Mother Nature certainly played her part, the exacerbating factor here cannot be ignored: Trump’s tariffs. These economic barriers have not only inflamed the cost of chocolate but have also spread their fiery grip to other Valentine’s Day staples. Flowers and indoor plants have seen a 6% price increase, jewelry 7%, all bearing the scars of steep U.S. tariffs on imports. It’s a stark reminder of how broad-spectrum policy decisions can trickle down, affecting the minutiae of our daily lives and special occasions.

A Silver Lining?

In an attempt to sweeten the bitter pill, Egan points out that not all is doom and gloom. Cosmetics and perfume prices have risen by a modest 1%, under the overall rate of inflation, and, surprisingly, wine prices have dipped by 1%. It’s a small consolation for those looking to celebrate love without breaking the bank.

Analysis: The Bigger Picture

What this narrative underscores is the intricate web of cause and effect that global and domestic policies weave around our everyday experiences. The direct impact of tariffs on consumer goods highlights a fundamental truth: economic policies are not just abstract concepts debated in the halls of power; they have tangible impacts on the ground. The chocolate price surge ahead of Valentine’s Day is a poignant example of how policies can transform from numbers on a page to a significant dent in our wallets.

Moreover, it raises critical questions about the sustainability of such tariffs and their long-term effects on global trade, consumer prices, and even cultural traditions like Valentine’s Day. As we navigate these choppy economic waters, it becomes increasingly crucial to question and scrutinize the decisions made by those in power and their real-world ramifications.

In essence, as we seek to celebrate love and connection, let’s also remember the broader economic landscapes and the systemic errors that can sometimes make that celebration a little less sweet.
