Trump underestimated Iran’s resilience — and now there is only one way out: scholar

The Real Story Behind the US-Iran-Israel Conflict

The current military debacle involving the United States, Israel, and Iran is a potent illustration of misguided decisions at the highest levels of power. As the conflict unfolds, it’s crucial to dissect the roles and decisions of those in power, namely US President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, whose initial military aggression has spiraled into a complex, enduring war with no clear end.

Misjudged Military Might

Trump and Netanyahu’s decision to initiate hostilities was ostensibly based on the presumption that a display of overwhelming military strength would rapidly subdue the Iranian regime, fostering an environment ripe for regime change. This expectation has proven to be a critical miscalculation. Despite facing significant internal and external pressures, Iran has mounted a surprisingly robust defense, leveraging asymmetrical warfare tactics that have effectively exploited US and Israeli vulnerabilities.

The Iranian response has included sophisticated strikes on US bases across the Persian Gulf and significant attacks within Israel. More strategically disruptive has been Iran’s decision to partially close the Strait of Hormuz, a vital artery for global oil shipments, precipitating a crisis in the global energy supply and economic repercussions far beyond the immediate theater of conflict.

Institutional Power and Responsibility

It is imperative to focus on who holds the institutional power and who made the decisions leading to the current situation. The narrative that the Iranian regime’s resilience is solely due to external support (e.g., from Russia and China) and an internal rallying ‘around the flag’ is an oversimplification that neglects the significant agency and strategic acumen of the Iranian government itself.

The real oversight lies with the US and Israeli leadership, who underestimated the complexities of Iranian politics and military capabilities. The decision by Trump and Netanyahu to opt for war over continued diplomatic engagement — especially following indications from Iranian negotiations that a peaceful resolution might have been viable — marks a pivotal failure in leadership and strategic judgment.

The Path to Peace

With military victory increasingly elusive, the onus is indeed on Trump, as suggested, to pivot towards diplomacy. However, the framing that Trump needs to “force Netanyahu to comply” simplifies the intricate dance of international diplomacy and overstates the US president’s ability to dictate terms to an allied sovereign nation. Both leaders are caught in their own cycles of aggressive posturing and domestic political pressures, with Netanyahu notably resistant to stepping back from his hardline stance against the Iranian regime.

The path to peace, obscured by the fog of war and political ambitions, involves a nuanced understanding of the power dynamics and genuine diplomatic engagement by all parties. Critics and analysts suggesting that Iran is “prevailing” merely by enduring overlook the broader strategic and humanitarian costs of such endurance. The conflict’s extension serves no party’s interests and primarily harms the ordinary citizens of the involved nations.

Conclusion

As the conflict drags on, it becomes increasingly clear that a negotiated settlement is the only plausible solution that can prevent further escalation and potential regional destabilization. The simplistic narrative of military might translating directly to political victory is flawed and dangerous. Real leadership will be shown by a courageous pivot towards diplomacy and a willingness to compromise for peace and stability in the region. This requires a clear articulation of goals, a genuine appreciation of the adversary’s capabilities and motivations, and, most critically, the humility to admit initial miscalculations.

In sum, the narrative of this conflict needs a reevaluation of its foundational assumptions — about power, about strategy, and about the efficacy of military force in achieving complex political objectives. The lessons here are not just for Trump or Netanyahu, but for all global leaders navigating the treacherous waters of international conflicts.
