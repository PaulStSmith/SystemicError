President Donald Trump must address a challenge with one group of voters, a CNN analyst said Friday.

As midterms approach, Trump needs to focus on independents, the group most divided on the joint U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, political commentator and CNN host Michael Smerconish told anchor Wolf Blitzer.

“I see that largely it’s a referendum on the commander in chief,” Smerconish said. “Republicans overwhelmingly supportive of the efforts, supportive of the president; Democrats in equal numbers, in opposition. The tie breakers are the independents, and that’s why, by a relatively slim margin, I see the war effort as being underwater.”

He argued that the upcoming elections would reveal how Americans support both the president and the conflict in the Middle East, something that the Trump administration should focus its messaging around. He also suggested the White House needs to shift its approach toward explaining why the military strikes were happening.

“It needs to be sold more about policy than the personality of the president,” Smerconish said. “I think what they need to say is, look, for half a century religious fanatics who rule in another country have sworn our destruction. ‘Death to America, death to Israel.’ We’re just not going to let them have a nuclear weapon. And I’d repeat it and repeat it and repeat it.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Narrative: The U.S.-Israeli War in Iran and the Independent Voter Dilemma

The recent CNN analysis by political commentator Michael Smerconish places the spotlight on President Donald Trump’s handling of the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, particularly focusing on the political implications for independent voters as midterm elections loom. Smerconish’s commentary, as detailed in the article, emphasizes the partisan divide on the war and the critical role of independents in the upcoming elections. This analysis aims to dissect the framing of the situation, identify where institutional power and decision-making responsibility truly lie, and assess any misdirection in responsibility.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

1. Institutional Power: The primary institutional power in the context of military actions, such as the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, rests with the executive branch of the U.S. government, specifically with the President, who serves as the Commander in Chief of the armed forces. This role grants the President significant authority over military operations, although the decision to go to war ideally involves consultation with and, in certain cases, authorization from Congress.

2. Decision-Making: The decision to engage in military action in Iran, as described, would have been made by President Trump, possibly in consultation with his national security team and other advisors. While the legislative branch (Congress) can play a role in such decisions, especially regarding funding and long-term military authorization, the immediate decision-making power resides with the President and the executive branch.

Article’s Framing and Responsibility

The framing of the article focuses significantly on the political implications of the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, particularly on how it might affect independent voters in the midterm elections. This perspective, while relevant for understanding the political landscape, potentially misdirects responsibility in several ways:

1. Misdirected Responsibility: By emphasizing the electoral consequences and the need for the Trump administration to “sell” the war more effectively to independents, the article arguably diverts attention from the more substantive issues at stake, such as the legal and moral justifications for the war, the strategic objectives, and the potential consequences for both the U.S. and the wider Middle East.

2. Limited Control Actors: The article implies that the key to political success regarding the war effort lies in messaging and persuasion, particularly targeting independent voters. This perspective overlooks the broader dynamics at play, including international law, diplomatic relations, and the inherent unpredictability of military conflict. Independent voters’ views on the war are likely shaped by a complex mix of factors, not just the administration’s messaging.

Conclusion: A Call for Deeper Analysis

The CNN analysis, as presented, places undue emphasis on the electoral mechanics surrounding the U.S.-Israeli war in Iran, at the expense of a deeper examination of the war’s justification, objectives, and potential impact. While understanding the political landscape is important, it is critical not to lose sight of the fact that the decision to engage in military action carries profound implications far beyond the next election cycle.

Critique: The article’s focus on how the Trump administration can better “sell” the war effort to independent voters is a simplification of a vastly more complex issue. It’s essential to question the underlying premises of the military action, the processes by which such decisions are made, and the broader consequences of these actions. Independent voters, like all citizens, deserve a nuanced analysis of the situation, one that goes beyond electoral considerations to address the fundamental issues of governance, accountability, and international peace and security.

In this context, the narrative would benefit from a shift towards more critical issues such as assessing the legality and morality of the war, the strategic objectives behind it, and the potential repercussions for global stability. This approach would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the situation, beyond the binary lens of electoral politics.
