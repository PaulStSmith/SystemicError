MAGA Republicans take unlikely stance in rebellion against House bill: columnist

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Marjorie Taylor Greene on Facebook.

MAGA Republicans led by Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) took a surprising turn as they denounced a House bill meant to protect Trump ally Benjamin Netanyahu's Israel from boycotts.

The bipartisan bill, HR 867, "would have amended the Anti-Boycott Act of 2018 by criminalizing compliance with boycotts called for by international government organizations, such as the United Nations — with potential penalties including prison time," according to a new article on MSNBC.com.

"A vote on the bill had been set for Monday, but it was canceled by House GOP leadership after Greene and other MAGA Republicans — including Rep. Anna Paulina Luna and former Rep. Matt Gaetz, both of Florida — denounced it," wrote MSNBC opinion writer Ja'han Jones.

Greene posted to X her reason for opposing the bill: "It is my job to defend American’s rights to buy or boycott whomever they choose without the government harshly fining them or imprisoning them."

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) wrote that he agreed with Greene and would also vote 'no' on the bill.

After word came down that the bill was being pulled from consideration, Massie wrote, "Apparently HR 867 has been pulled from the schedule for this week. Thank you for your vocal opposition on this platform. It was a ridiculous bill that our leadership should have never scheduled for a vote."

Jones cited a recent Pew Research survey that found "53% of Americans hold negative views of Israel — up from 42% about three years ago," before the Hamas attack of Oct. 7, 2023 and Israel's retaliation that has decimated Gaza.

In addition, Americans' opinion of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu also remains low at 32%, according to the survey conducted in March.

Jones concluded, "With that broadening discontent in mind, along with uncertainty over its passage, it’s easy to understand why the House bill was pulled."

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Conundrum: Opposing Protection for Netanyahu's Israel

In a twist that could only emerge from the labyrinthine politics of our times, MAGA Republicans, spearheaded by the ever-controversial Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), have taken a stand that might momentarily align them with those on the far left. They have voiced their opposition to HR 867, a bill that sought to extend protections to Benjamin Netanyahu's Israel against boycotts. This move is as surprising as it is perplexing, given the usual staunch support Israel receives from conservative factions in the United States.

The Bill in Question

HR 867 wasn't a light piece of legislation. It aimed to amend the Anti-Boycott Act of 2018 by introducing criminal penalties for complying with international government organization-led boycotts against Israel. We're talking about potential prison time for those who align with such boycotts. Yes, prison time for exercising what could be construed as a form of free speech or protest.

The bill's cancellation, prompted by Greene and her MAGA compatriots, including Rep. Anna Paulina Luna and the infamous former Rep. Matt Gaetz, both from Florida, signals a rare departure from the GOP's typically unyielding support of Israeli policies.

Greene took to X (formerly Twitter) to justify her stance: "It is my job to defend American’s rights to buy or boycott whomever they choose without the government harshly fining them or imprisoning them." Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) chimed in with agreement, heralding the bill's withdrawal as a victory against what he deemed a "ridiculous" legislative effort.

The Undercurrents of Discontent

The decision to pull the bill might also be seen as a reflection of shifting American sentiments towards Israel, particularly in the wake of recent escalations in Gaza. A Pew Research survey highlighted a significant uptick in negative views of Israel among Americans, jumping from 42% to 53% in a span of roughly three years. Moreover, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's popularity remains low at 32%, suggesting a broader disenchantment that extends beyond the confines of domestic Israeli politics.

The MAGA Paradox

This scenario presents a fascinating paradox. On one hand, Greene and her MAGA allies have positioned themselves as champions of individual freedoms, opposing government overreach into citizens' rights to protest and boycott. On the other hand, this stance starkly contrasts with their usual hardline, often punitive positions on freedom of speech and protest domestically. It's a contradiction that lays bare the inconsistent application of principles when it suits political narratives.

In Conclusion:

The cancellation of HR 867 is a significant occurrence, not just for its immediate implications, but for what it reveals about the complex web of politics, freedoms, and international relations. The MAGA Republicans' opposition to the bill might be lauded as a defense of free speech, but it also prompts a closer examination of their broader agenda and ideological consistency. As always, actions speak louder than words, and in this case, the actions are echoing through the halls of Congress with a resonance that demands attention.
