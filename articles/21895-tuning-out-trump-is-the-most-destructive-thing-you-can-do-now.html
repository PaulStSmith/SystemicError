Tuning out Trump is the most destructive thing you can do now

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republicans Reward the Enforcer, Then Hunt the Scientist

The Power Map

The story is not confusion. It is power using institutions to protect itself. Senate Republicans confirmed Todd Blanche, a man the source says carried out illegal presidential orders, and moved against Anthony Fauci, an 85-year-old retired public health official. That is the actual structure here: upward immunity for loyal operators, downward punishment for a convenient target.

Blanche Was the Point

Blanche is not being elevated despite the conduct described in the source. He is being elevated because of it. The Senate’s message is blunt: if you help a president misuse the state, the state will still reward you. The article even notes the unresolved danger that Trump could use the $8 billion slush fund Blanche created, along with IRS immunity, to bury evidence of criminal enterprise. That is not normal governance. It is institutional laundering.

Fauci as Decoy

The contempt vote against Fauci is the kind of political theater that reveals weakness. Republicans are not prosecuting a crisis; they are manufacturing one. The source says the pandemic is old news, the public does not care, and Fauci is retired. Exactly. That is why he is useful. He is old, unguarded, and easy to turn into a symbol for a party that has no defensible record left to run on.

Misdirection as Strategy

The article correctly identifies the racket: Republicans have backed Trump’s disasters on foreign policy, Iran, the economy, immigration, healthcare, and the environment, while also voting through tax cuts for the rich paid for in part by cuts to Medicare, Medicaid, and food stamps. When a party has sold out every governing claim it once pretended to have, it needs a scapegoat. Fauci is not the issue. He is cover.

Democrats Cannot Pretend This Is Symmetric

The source briefly blames Democratic timidity, and that complaint is fair as far as it goes. But there is a limit to the useful self-flagellation. Democrats can be slow, cautious, and often pathetic. Republicans, in this story, are something else: collaborators in an open project of impunity, demagoguery, and retaliation. Treating those failures as equivalent is how people miss the difference between weakness and organized political sabotage.

What This Story Really Shows

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in an advanced stage: loyalists promoted, experts harassed, accountability inverted, and public attention dragged toward fake enemies while real power is protected. The pattern is not accidental. It is the operating system. Republicans are not merely failing to govern; they are using government to shield a shrinking, corrupt coalition and to punish anyone who can still name what they have done.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
