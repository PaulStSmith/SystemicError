“Jimmy Kimmel Live!” returned to some local ABC airwaves on Tuesday night following an “indefinite” suspension handed down last week by Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair Broadcast Group. The two Trump-aligned companies suspended Kimmel’s show after the comedian made comments about the killer of conservative activist Charlie Kirk that were deemed “insensitive.”

Disney, which owns ABC, announced on Monday that Kimmel’s eponymous show would return in most markets. Nexstar said in a statement that it won’t air the program and will instead work toward “fostering an environment of respectful, constructive dialogue in the markets we serve.” Sinclair is also planning to preempt the airing of Kimmel’s show, according to media reports.

Trump responded to the news in a social media post on Tuesday night.

“I can’t believe ABC Fake News gave Jimmy Kimmel his job back,” Trump’s wrote in a post on Truth Social late Tuesday night. “The White House was told by ABC that his Show was cancelled!”

“I think we’re going to test ABC out on this,” the post continues. “Let’s see how we do. Last time I went after them, they gave me $16 Million Dollars.”

Political analysts and observers responded to Trump’s threat on social media.

“Fats needs to just take the L here,” commentator Keith Olbermann posted on X.

“The President of the United States threatening a television network,” immigration lawyer Aaron Reichlin-Melnick posted on X. “Just background noise in our broken politics.”

“What would be the basis of the lawsuit, Trump doesn’t like Kimmel’s jokes?” Dean Baker, senior economist for the Center for Economic &Policy Research, posted on X.

“A lot of people were like ‘Jimmy Kimmel being back on PROVES there was no government coercion against free speech’ and then Trump just posted he’s going to sue ABC for putting Jimmy Kimmel back on,” podcaster Armand Domalewski posted on X.

“Trump is crying because Jimmy Kimmel is back on the air,” media strategist Vince Monroy posted on Bluesky.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Threat to ABC: A Deep Dive into Absurdity

In a striking display of brazen audacity, former President Donald Trump lashed out late Tuesday night at ABC for reinstating “Jimmy Kimmel Live!” on its network. This move comes after a brief “indefinite” suspension by Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair Broadcast Group, entities known for their alignment with Trump. The suspension itself was a knee-jerk reaction to Kimmel’s comments on the killer of conservative activist Charlie Kirk, which these companies deemed “insensitive.” However, Disney, owning ABC, decided to reinstate Kimmel’s show in most markets, sparking Trump’s ire.

The Spectacle of Threats

Trump’s response, a social media post on Truth Social, encapsulates the former president’s penchant for bluster and intimidation. “I can’t believe ABC Fake News gave Jimmy Kimmel his job back,” Trump wrote, later threatening, “I think we’re going to test ABC out on this. Let’s see how we do. Last time I went after them, they gave me $16 Million Dollars.” This statement is not just a testament to Trump’s ongoing war against what he perceives as ‘fake news’ but also an alarming indication of his readiness to leverage his influence for seemingly personal vendettas.

Observers’ Reactions: From Outrage to Mockery

Observers and political analysts have met Trump’s threats with a mixture of outrage and mockery. Keith Olbermann’s advice for Trump to “just take the L here” speaks volumes of the absurdity of the situation. Meanwhile, immigration lawyer Aaron Reichlin-Melnick’s comment, “The President of the United States threatening a television network,” underscores the bizarre normalization of such behavior in our current political climate. Dean Baker, a senior economist, questions the legal basis of Trump’s potential lawsuit against ABC, highlighting the frivolity of Trump’s grievances.

The Irony of Crying Foul on Free Speech

Perhaps the most striking aspect of this debacle is the irony inherent in Trump’s actions. For someone who has vehemently criticized the alleged suppression of free speech, threatening a television network for reinstating a comedic show is hypocritical, to say the least. Podcaster Armand Domalewski’s observation that “Jimmy Kimmel being back on PROVES there was no government coercion against free speech” followed by Trump’s threat only serves to underscore this hypocrisy.

Conclusion: A Disturbing Glimpse into Trump’s Vision of Press Freedom

Trump’s threat against ABC is more than just a disgruntled reaction to a television show; it’s a disturbing glimpse into the former president’s vision of press freedom and the lengths he might go to silence criticism. In a democracy, the freedom of the press is sacrosanct, serving as a pillar for free speech and a watchdog against power abuse. When a political figure of Trump’s stature threatens this freedom, it’s not just “background noise”; it’s a clarion call to defend the principles that underpin our society.

In essence, Trump’s reaction to Jimmy Kimmel’s reinstatement by ABC is emblematic of a broader issue plaguing our political discourse: the weaponization of power to suppress dissenting voices. As media strategist Vince Monroy succinctly puts it, “Trump is crying because Jimmy Kimmel is back on the air.” But the implications of this tantrum extend far beyond late-night comedy—it’s a stark reminder of the fragility of our freedoms in the face of authoritarian tendencies.
