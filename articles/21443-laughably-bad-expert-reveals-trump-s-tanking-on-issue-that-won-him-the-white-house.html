'Laughably bad:' Expert reveals Trump's tanking on issue that won him the White House

Trump’s Price-Check Problem Is Not a Mystery

The Promise Was the Point

CNN’s Harry Enten is pointing at a brutal fact: Trump’s standing on inflation, the issue that helped put him back in the White House, has collapsed into historical ugliness. He is now 50 points underwater on net approval in CNN’s polling, and 70 percent of Americans say the country is on the wrong track on cost of living. The damage is not abstract. It is political recoil from a campaign built on the claim that he, unlike his predecessors, would make prices behave.

Power Sits in the Oval Office

This is the part the coverage should not blur: Trump holds the institutional power. He is the president. He ran on affordability, inherited the authority to act, and is now being judged on whether the public can feel any results. When 78 percent of Americans say he has not focused enough on lowering prices, that is not a communications problem. It is a verdict on the gap between promised control and actual delivery.

The article’s framing leans on surprise, but the deeper story is accountability. Voters did not elect a pundit. They elected the person who claimed he could fix their bills.

Republicans Are Not Confused

The most revealing number is not just the overall disapproval. It is that half of Republicans now say the country is headed in the wrong direction on cost of living, and a majority of Republicans, 58 percent, say Trump is not doing enough to lower prices. That matters because it strips away one of the favorite excuses around Trumpism: that the base is blindly loyal because it is misinformed.

No. They can see grocery receipts. They can see rent. They can see that slogans do not reduce prices. When even his own party registers disappointment, the failure is not ideological drift. It is lived experience.

The Usual Misdirection Won’t Hold

Trump’s political method has always depended on relocating blame downward. Prices become someone else’s fault. Economic pain gets dressed up as a cultural insult. Failure gets narrated as sabotage by bureaucrats, outsiders, or invisible enemies. But inflation is a material issue, and material issues eventually puncture propaganda.

That is why this story matters beyond the polling. It shows the limits of authoritarian marketing when the household budget keeps refusing to cooperate. The public can be battered by noise for a while. It cannot be indefinitely convinced that expensive life is victory.

The Pattern Behind the Collapse

This is the basic Trump pattern in office: promise control, seize power, produce spectacle, then hope resentment substitutes for results. That works until the numbers stop moving in your favor. On inflation, they have moved hard in the wrong direction. The polling does not show confusion. It shows punishment.

The larger lesson is not that one politician is having a bad week. It is that right-wing populism is structurally dependent on performance without accountability. It needs a permanent enemy because it cannot reliably deliver the material relief it sells. Once that gap becomes visible, the spell weakens.
