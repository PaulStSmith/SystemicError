Colossal wager predicts Trump will bomb Iran by end of day — triggering insider fears

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 10, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts while preparing to sign an executive order classifying fentanyl as ‘weapon of mass destruction’ during a Mexican Border Defense Medal presentation in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 15, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

An anonymous online trader placed a “huge” wager Monday on the Trump administration launching a military strike against Iran by the end of the day Monday, a bet so colossal that the payout could reach around $4 million, sparking speculation that the individual might have insider knowledge.

The online trader joined the cryptocurrency-based prediction market platform Polymarket last November, and has since made only two wagers; an unsuccessful $75,000 bet against Trump winning the 2024 election, and a nearly $100,000 bet that the United States would strike Iran by the end of Monday.

“A potential U.S. Gov insider has been spotted. A wallet just placed $100K on the U.S. striking Iran by [end of day] today at 2% odds,” wrote Haeju Jeong, senior software engineer for META and Polymarket trader, in a social media post Monday on X.

“If it hits, the payout is $4M. The wallet was inactive for 460 days and has only participated in one other market ever. Very unusual behavior. Definitely a wallet to watch and see how this plays out”

Trump has increasingly threatened military action against Iran amid its crackdown on protesters, threats he walked back last month but that have continued to be discussed within his administration.

Both wagers on online prediction markets and cryptocurrency purchases have fueled speculation in the past about individuals using insider knowledge of the Trump administration for profit.

For instance, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt abruptly ended a press conference last month just seconds shy of it reaching 65 minutes, a move that unexpectedly earned a select-few prediction market traders who had bet on the 2% odds of the presser lasting under 65 minutes a small fortune.

Another eyebrowsing trade occurred last October when an anonymous trader opened an account and made a lofty bet on the cryptocurrency Bitcoin crashing just 30 minutes ahead of a major tariff announcement from President Donald Trump, a bet that paid off to the tune of $88 million.

“This is the weirdest thing I’ve seen on Polymarket lately,” said another predictions market trader, X user “Lirrato,” to their more than 4,000 followers in asocial media poston X Monday. “I don’t know how this ends, but I think I’m going to be watching this all day.

Our Analysis:

The Dark Betting Game: Unraveling the Shadows of Insider Trading in Political Predictions

In the murky waters of online prediction markets, an alarming incident has surfaced, casting a long shadow over the integrity of cryptocurrency-based betting platforms and potentially unveiling a sinister intersection of insider knowledge and speculative gambling. An anonymous trader, with the audacity of a high-stakes gambler, placed a “huge” wager on the Trump administration launching a military strike against Iran by the end of a single day. This bet, amounting to nearly $100,000 with the possibility of a $4 million payout, isn’t just a gamble; it’s a stark revelation of how the lines between informed speculation and insider trading are not just blurred but perhaps obliterated.

The Specter of Insider Knowledge

The trader, a ghostly figure in the digital expanse of Polymarket, has emerged with only two bets to their name. Their return from a 460-day hiatus to place a bet of such magnitude on a specific geopolitical event screams not of chance, but of confidence—misplaced or otherwise—in privileged information. The audacity to wager on the volatile whims of international politics, particularly an event as catastrophic as a military strike, with such precision is alarming. It begs a significant question: Are we witnessing a bold gambler at play, or is this the tip of an iceberg, revealing a network of individuals with access to classified information leveraging it for financial gain?

The Problem with Prediction Markets

Prediction markets, especially those based on cryptocurrency, operate in a regulatory gray area. They are the digital age’s oracle, offering a platform for forecasting events ranging from the mundane to the monumental. Yet, this incident underscores a fundamental flaw in their design: the potential for exploitation by those with insider knowledge. The anonymity and lack of oversight that define these platforms create a fertile ground for unethical conduct. The case of the anonymous trader isn’t an anomaly; it’s symptomatic of a broader issue where the allure of astronomical gains tempts individuals to cross ethical lines, leveraging sensitive information for personal gain.

A Pattern of Suspicion

This is not an isolated incident. The abrupt termination of a press conference by White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, manipulated to benefit a select few, and the predictive bet on Bitcoin’s crash minutes before a major tariff announcement, highlight a disturbing trend. These instances aren’t just lucky guesses; they are calculated moves, possibly predicated on insider information. The patterns here are too conspicuous to ignore, suggesting a clandestine use of privileged information within the realms of prediction markets and cryptocurrency speculation.

The Implications

The implications of such actions are profound. Beyond the immediate financial ramifications, they erode public trust in the transparency and fairness of both government actions and financial markets. When the specter of insider trading looms large, it casts a pall over the integrity of political and economic systems, potentially manipulating markets and geopolitical events for personal gain.

The Way Forward

Addressing this challenge requires a multifaceted approach. Regulatory bodies must evolve to keep pace with the digital transformation of financial markets, ensuring robust oversight and transparency. This incident should serve as a clarion call for stricter regulations and the implementation of mechanisms to detect and deter the misuse of insider information.

In conclusion, the audacious bet by an anonymous trader on Polymarket is more than a gamble; it’s a glaring spotlight on the dark intersections of insider knowledge, cryptocurrency markets, and political predictions. It’s a wake-up call for regulators, platforms, and participants alike to reevaluate and reinforce the ethical and legal boundaries of betting in the shadowy digital age.
