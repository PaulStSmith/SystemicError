The longtime Texas senator "is boasting a 99% voting record with President Trump in a new memo provided first to Axios," said the report. This comes as Cornyn "and his firebrand primary challenger, state Attorney General Ken Paxton, are battling to win Trump's coveted endorsement in what is quickly shaping up to be the most dramatic and expensive GOP primary of the cycle."

"Cornyn's team is using fellow Texas Sen. Ted Cruz (R) as a foil, tallying the votes to tout that Cruz has voted against Trump more often than Cornyn," said the report. "Over Trump's first term and so far in his second, Cornyn voted in line with Trump 99.2% of the time while Cruz voted with him 96.6% of the time, according to the data, which removed duplicative procedural votes."

Paxton is an extreme-right state law enforcement official who has had his hands in everything from purging abortion rights from the state, to signing onto Trump's 2020 efforts to try to overturn that presidential election. He has a long record of legal controversy, facing a yearslong indictment for securities fraud only resolved last year, and was impeached by the Texas House over a bribery and retaliation scandal within his office, but fended off conviction in the Senate and has tried to position himself as the true heir to MAGA in Texas.

Cornyn has sought to tarnish Paxton for his legal problems, at one point responding to the latter's attacks on him over insufficient fervor on border issues by replying, "Your criminal defense lawyers are calling."

For his part, Cruz is staying out of endorsing in the race, and Senate GOP leadership is fighting to protect Cornyn, privately urging Trump to give the incumbent senator his endorsement.

A group backing Paxton has also courted Trump by airing an ad in support of the attorney general in Palm Beach, Florida, where Trump's country club is located.

Our Analysis:

The Texas GOP Primary: A Theater of the Absurd

In the latest act of political gymnastics, Senator John Cornyn (R-TX) is strutting his nearly impeccable alignment with President Trump as a badge of honor—or perhaps, more accurately, as a desperate plea for political survival. Courtesy of Axios, we've been given a front-row seat to this spectacle. Cornyn, it seems, is fighting tooth and nail against his primary challenger, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, in a bid for the golden Trump endorsement. This isn't just any political showdown; it's shaping up to be the pièce de résistance of GOP primaries this cycle.

Cornyn's Trump Loyalty: A Double-Edged Sword

Let's dissect the narrative that Cornyn is painting: boasting a 99% voting record in line with Trump's policies. On the surface, this might appear as an unwavering dedication to the party's recent figurehead. However, beneath this veneer of loyalty lies a stark revelation—a willingness to toe the line, irrespective of the broader implications on democracy, ethics, and governance. It's a staggering admission of priority: allegiance to Trump over a nuanced assessment of policy impact.

The Paxton Problem

Enter Ken Paxton, a figure so embroiled in controversy that it's a wonder he can see through the fog of his own legal battles. Paxton's resume reads like a thriller novel: purging abortion rights, attempting to overturn presidential election results, and navigating a labyrinth of legal issues, including a securities fraud indictment and a bribery scandal. Yet, here he stands, vying for the MAGA crown in Texas, seemingly undeterred by the skeletons dancing in his closet.

The Cruz Calculation

As if this primary couldn't get more convoluted, Senator Ted Cruz emerges as an unlikely benchmark. Cornyn's camp is keen to highlight that, compared to Cruz, Cornyn is more in sync with Trump. This is a fascinating strategy, leveraging Cruz's occasional dissents as a foil to enhance Cornyn's pro-Trump image. It's a clear sign of the times: in the current GOP landscape, your value is often measured by your loyalty to Trump, rather than your legislative achievements or policy positions.

The Trump Endorsement: The Ultimate Prize

Amid this chaos, Trump's endorsement emerges as the holy grail. Both Cornyn and Paxton are pulling out all stops, with Paxton's supporters even targeting Trump's Floridian stronghold with ads. It's a surreal scenario: Senate GOP leadership whispering in Trump's ear to favor Cornyn, while Paxton's camp wages a media campaign to capture Trump's attention.

Conclusion: A Damning Reflection

This entire affair—Cornyn's boastfulness of his pro-Trump voting record, Paxton's contentious bid, and the almost comical reliance on Trump's endorsement—paints a damning portrait of the current state of the Republican Party. It's a party seemingly caught in a vortex of its own making, where fealty to a single figure overshadows substantive policy discussions and where the quest for endorsements trumps ethical considerations.

In the end, the Texas GOP primary isn't just a political contest; it's a vivid illustration of a party at a crossroads, struggling with its identity and values. Whether this will lead to a reckoning or a further descent into the politics of personality remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: the soul of the Republican Party in Texas, and perhaps beyond, is up for grabs.
