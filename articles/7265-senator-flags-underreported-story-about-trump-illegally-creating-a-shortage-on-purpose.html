Donald Trump is “illegally” creating a shortage in the U.S., and the media isn’t noticing, a senator said on Saturday.

Brian Schatz, Dem of Hawaii, flagged the story the weekend after Christmas. Taking to social media, the senator said, “One of the most underreported stories of 2025 is that Trump is the first President ever to create electricity shortages on purpose.”

“He is illegally canceling solar and wind, causing layoffs and price spikes, and the media is like ‘wow what a showman that guy is!’” he added Saturday.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of a Political Circus

In an era where the boundaries between political maneuvering and reality TV blur, the recent drama unfolding between Senator Brian Schatz of Hawaii and former President Donald Trump over alleged intentional electricity shortages is a spectacle that demands scrutiny. Schatz accuses Trump of “illegally” orchestrating a shortage of electricity in the U.S. by undermining solar and wind energy, paired with a critique towards the media’s enamorment with Trump’s showmanship over substantive policy implications. This narrative, while captivating, is fraught with complexities and contradictions that merit a closer examination.

Trump’s Alleged Energy Sabotage

Firstly, the accusation that Trump is intentionally creating electricity shortages by canceling solar and wind projects is a bold one. It suggests a level of malevolence and strategic manipulation of the energy market that is not only unprecedented but deeply concerning. If true, such actions could indeed exacerbate layoffs in renewable energy sectors and spike energy prices, affecting millions of Americans. However, the lack of substantial evidence provided in the public domain to support these claims makes this a difficult pill to swallow without skepticism.

The Media’s Role: Enabler or Critic?

Senator Schatz’s critique of the media’s response—or lack thereof—to Trump’s actions raises another critical issue. The portrayal of Trump as a charismatic “showman” rather than a figure whose policies may have detrimental impacts on the nation’s energy security and environmental sustainability is indicative of a broader problem in political journalism. The fascination with personality over policy contributes to a misinformed public, undermining the gravity of the decisions being made in the corridors of power.

However, the senator’s remarks also expose a troubling tendency to dismiss any media critique of Trump’s energy policies as insufficient or cheerleading. This binary view oversimplifies the nuanced and diverse responses from the media landscape, not all of which shy away from holding Trump accountable.

The Senator’s Social Media Strategy: Help or Hindrance?

Senator Schatz’s choice to air his grievances and political strategies on social media is emblematic of contemporary political discourse. Yet, one must question the efficacy and sincerity of such an approach. Sarcastic retorts to legitimate concerns about the effectiveness of social media advocacy do little to advance the cause of climate action or reassure constituents of a proactive approach to policy change.

Moreover, Schatz’s commitment to “devote his professional life to climate action” in response to a critique on X (formerly known as Twitter) underscores a performative element that plagues modern politics. It’s a promise that, while noble, begs for evidence of action beyond the digital echo chambers.

Conclusion: Beyond the Spectacle

The exchange between Senator Schatz and critics, set against the backdrop of Trump’s controversial energy policies, encapsulates the chaotic nature of current political discourse. Substance is often sacrificed at the altar of spectacle, and meaningful dialogue is drowned out by the cacophony of social media banter.

As denizens of a democratic society, it is incumbent upon us to demand more from our leaders and the media. This means insisting on evidence-based critiques, fostering nuanced conversations over policy, and prioritizing the planet’s well-being over political point-scoring. The issues at hand are too critical to be reduced to mere social media fodder. In the end, the real showman is the one who can turn words into action, transforming the political circus into a forum for genuine progress.
