What Jefferson and Madison would have thought about ‘rededicating’ the US to God

Rededicating Misdirection: The Strategic Blurring of Church and State

Political Theater on the National Mall

On May 17, 2026, the National Mall witnessed a spectacle named “Rededicate 250,” ostensibly a prayer rally celebrating the U.S. approaching its 250th birthday. The U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson and a cohort of conservative leaders orchestrated this event, blurring historical lines that demarcate the separation of church and state. This isn’t an innocent celebration but a calculated political maneuver. Johnson’s invocation to “rededicate the United States of America as one nation under God” reveals an intent to redefine national identity aligning with specific religious values, directly challenging the foundational secular principles of the American republic.

Misuse of Power and Historical Revisionism

The event, spearheaded by figures with significant political power, including the Speaker of the House, underscores a deliberate attempt to influence public policy through religious rhetoric. This isn’t just a rally; it’s a strategic exertion of power aiming to reshape public perception about the role of religion in government. Johnson’s previous remarks in 2023, dismissing the separation of church and state as a “misnomer,” further expose a concerted effort to undermine a core democratic principle through historical revisionism. Such statements are not only misleading but are also an attempt to legitimize a closer relationship between church and state, countering established legal interpretations and the majority public opinion that favors separation.

Institutional Cowardice and the Supreme Court’s Role

This rally didn’t occur in a vacuum. It reflects a broader ideological push that has found sympathizers within the highest echelons of the American judiciary. Recent Supreme Court decisions, deviating from decades of precedent upholding the separation principle, signal a judicial endorsement of this ideological shift. These decisions, permitting taxpayer dollars to flow to religious schools and other similar measures, represent a tangible manifestation of this shift, abetted by the Court. The critique by Justice Sonia Sotomayor in 2022, lamenting the transformation of church-state separation from a “constitutional commitment” to a “constitutional violation,” highlights the gravity of this judicial pivot and its implications for American secularism.

The Disservice to Foundational Principles

This rally and the rhetoric surrounding it do a disservice to the nuanced understanding of religious freedom as envisioned by foundational figures like Jefferson and Madison. These figures advocated for a robust separation to prevent government entanglement in religion, ensuring freedom of conscience for all. The misrepresentation of their views by contemporary political leaders not only distorts historical facts but also jeopardizes the very liberties—religious and secular—that these founders fought to protect.

A Chilling Reflection of Authoritarian Tendencies

“Rededicate 250” is not merely a religious observance but a reflection of an authoritarian inclination to merge political power with religious orthodoxy, marginalizing those who believe in a secular governance model. This merging threatens to alienate a substantial portion of the American populace that does not subscribe to the religious ideologies being promoted at such events.

Beyond the Rally: A Call for Vigilance

The event on the National Mall should not be dismissed as a standalone occurrence but seen as part of a troubling trend towards religious nationalism. It is a stark reminder of the need for continual vigilance and resistance against any form of governance that seeks to undermine the secular, inclusive principles upon which the United States was founded. As the nation nears its 250th year, the true dedication should be to those principles of freedom and equality, not to a revisionist agenda that seeks to dismantle them.
