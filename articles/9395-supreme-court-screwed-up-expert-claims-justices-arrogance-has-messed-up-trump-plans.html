'Supreme Court screwed up': Expert claims justices' 'arrogance' has messed up Trump plans

The U.S. Supreme Court’s “arrogance” is threatening to stall some of President Donald Trump’s central plans, a legal expert said Monday.

A federal appeals court refused to halt a district court order last week against the president’s mobilization of the National Guard in Illinois, undercutting his efforts to flood Chicago with troops, and Slate’s Dahlia Lithwick and Mark Joseph Stern discussed how lower courts have been setting themselves against a Supreme Court that has largely been shaped by the president.

“We’re looking at judges across the ideological spectrum — Trump judges, Obama judges, Federalist Society judges, young judges, old and distinguished judges — who have never picked up their pen to speak out on political matters,” Lithwick said.

“And across the board, they’re in agreement about what is happening. They’re naming it. They’re saying: I refuse to be gaslit today. And they’re calling it unlawful. I guess I’m wondering if it was an unforced error by the U.S. Supreme Court to treat lower courts — their findings of fact, their efforts to do hard doctrinal work under unbelievably challenging circumstances — with hostility or overt contempt.”

“I find myself wondering if the court, particularly over the summer, made a lot of enemies by using dismissive rhetoric, and now in a lot of ways emboldening judges to say: Not today, Satan. Don’t lie to me,” she added.

Stern agreed, saying that even some conservative lower court judges – such as Trump appointee Amy St. Eve – seem to be crafting their orders as checks against the higher court.

“It seems to me that the Supreme Court has fomented an ‘us vs. them’ dynamic with the lower courts,” Stern said. “It’s presenting itself as the final arbiter of all facts on the ground, ignoring its obligation to defer to what the district court believes is happening in the real world. The Supreme Court has decided: We know everything, we have a crystal ball, we are omnipresent and omnipotent.

“That arrogance doesn’t just offend the lower courts’ egos — it undercuts the work they see as their duty, especially the careful establishment of facts. So when the Supreme Court treats fact-finding as optional, maybe judges like St. Eve respond by making it decisive. They’re not just fact-checking, they’re reality-checking.”

“That, to me, shows that maybe the Supreme Court has screwed up here,” Stern added. “Yes, the Trump administration might still win at SCOTUS. But it will lose weeks in the process. Those will be weeks during the appeal when the administration can’t send the National Guard into Chicago at this moment because lower courts are standing in its way. Maybe if the Supreme Court had shown more respect for the lower courts, then judges like St. Eve wouldn’t be pointedly deferring to a district court’s factual findings that essentially call Trump a liar.

“That’s just my guess. But if lower court judges really are turning against the Supreme Court, I think it does hurt the administration’s ability to run these shock-and-awe campaigns at a moment’s notice like they want to. Because lower courts can freeze them in their tracks.”

Our Analysis:

Supreme Court’s “Arrogance” Undermines Trump’s Plans: A Critical Analysis

In a recent unfolding of events, the U.S. Supreme Court’s alleged arrogance has been spotlighted for potentially derailing President Donald Trump’s key agendas, particularly the deployment of the National Guard in Illinois to overwhelm Chicago with troops. This analysis dives into the assertions made by legal experts, emphasizing how the Supreme Court’s demeanor may be fostering an unprecedented judicial resistance that transcends ideological lines.

The Court’s Contempt for Lower Judiciaries

It’s profoundly telling when legal commentators like Dahlia Lithwick and Mark Joseph Stern highlight the burgeoning defiance among lower court judges against a Supreme Court shaped significantly by Trump. The narrative isn’t just about disagreement; it’s about a collective refusal to be gaslit or to let the law be contorted by political whims. This isn’t a mere squabble over legal interpretations; it’s a fundamental clash over the essence of judicial integrity and the factual grounding of legal decisions. When Lithwick speaks of judges from across the ideological spectrum uniting in their stance, it underscores a critical juncture where the judiciary seems to be saying, enough is enough.

The Supreme Court’s “Omnipotence”

Mark Joseph Stern’s observations further peel the layers off the Supreme Court’s perceived omnipotence. This isn’t just about arrogance; it’s about a dangerous precedent where the Supreme Court acts with a disregard for the meticulous fact-finding missions undertaken by lower courts. By sidelining these efforts, the Supreme Court isn’t merely asserting its dominance; it’s engaging in a reality-denial that challenges the very foundation of judicial process. When judges like Amy St. Eve pivot to make fact-finding “decisive,” they’re not just pushing back against the higher court’s disdain; they’re safeguarding the judicial duty to establish truth.

The Real-World Implications

The ramifications of this judicial tug-of-war are far-reaching. Stern aptly points out that, while the Trump administration might still secure victories at the Supreme Court, the battle scars will be significant. The administration’s inability to dispatch the National Guard into Chicago “at a moment’s notice” underscores a broader truth: judicial checks are alive and well, and they serve as crucial barriers against the whims of executive power. This isn’t merely about bureaucratic delays; it’s about a judiciary that refuses to be a rubber stamp for the administration’s controversial tactics.

Conclusion: A Judiciary at a Crossroads

The discourse surrounding the Supreme Court’s alleged arrogance and its implications for Trump’s plans isn’t just legal punditry; it’s a reflection of a judiciary at a crossroads. The lower courts’ emerging defiance signals a critical moment in the American judicial landscape, one where the guardians of the law are increasingly unwilling to compromise on the sanctity of facts and the principled application of justice. The Supreme Court’s perceived detachment from these values not only breeds resistance among lower courts but also raises profound questions about the Court’s role in an era of deep political divisions.

As this saga unfolds, one thing becomes clear: the judiciary’s commitment to truth and integrity might just be the bulwark that preserves the democratic fabric in tumultuous times. The Supreme Court, in its lofty perch, would do well to remember that its power is not derived from an assumed infallibility but from its fidelity to the law and its principles. In the end, the Court’s legacy will be defined not by its ability to impose its will but by its willingness to listen, learn, and adapt in the face of changing societal norms and expectations.
