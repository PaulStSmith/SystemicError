Hookup site leak spurs denial from 'family values' GOP candidate

Based on an article by Nicole Charky-Chami

Paulo SantosJuly 3, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Who Actually Holds PowerThe Campaign’s Useful DistractionMoral Panic as Campaign StrategyThe Campaign Knows What It Is DoingThe Pattern Underneath

The Problem Isn’t the Leak. It’s the Power Grab.

Derek Wulf, the Iowa Republican running for lieutenant governor alongside Zach Lahn, has been tied by leaked data to an Adult Friend Finder account associated with his email address. He denies setting it up, the campaign says someone else could have used the email, and the underlying breach means the account trail is not proof of authorship. That is the narrow scandal. The larger one is that these men are campaigning to govern other people’s lives while selling themselves as guardians of “traditional family values.”

Who Actually Holds Power

This story is not really about a hookup site. It is about people seeking state power while preaching a politics of sexual discipline, religious authority, and punishment. Wulf and Lahn are not private citizens making private mistakes. They are candidates for executive office, and they have already told voters what kind of power they want: the power to define “values,” to posture as defenders of “the foundation of our country,” and to treat judicial equality as “tyranny.”

That is the real institutional fact here. If they win, they do not just get to moralize. They get access to the machinery of state government.

The Campaign’s Useful Distraction

The source article invites a familiar cheap thrill: a conservative anti-“non-traditional relationships” candidate linked to a site for casual sex. That is the bait. It is also the escape hatch. It shifts attention from public program to private embarrassment, from coercive politics to tabloid irony.

But the important question is not whether Wulf personally used the account. The important question is why this brand of politics keeps trying to turn the state into an enforcement arm for one narrow religious-sexual order. If the campaign’s answer is just “someone else could have used the email,” that does not address the political hypocrisy. It only tries to make the scandal unprovable enough to ignore.

Moral Panic as Campaign Strategy

Lahn and Wulf have been explicit. They talk about “restoring Iowa’s values and way of life.” Wulf says the nation’s foundation is based on Jesus Christ. Lahn called the Iowa Supreme Court’s same-sex marriage ruling “judicial tyranny.” This is not accidental language. It is the old authoritarian move: define one group’s rights as social decay, then present yourself as the only legitimate defender of order.

That makes the Adult Friend Finder angle politically revealing, but not because of prudery. It exposes how much of this movement runs on projection. Private vice is not the core issue. The core issue is public cruelty paired with private immunity: condemn other people’s relationships, then demand your own contradictions be treated as noise.

The Campaign Knows What It Is Doing

The response from the campaign was not transparency. It was intimidation. Strategist Luke Thompson did not just deny the allegation; he warned Raw Story that pursuing the matter “will change the nature of our relationship considerably.” That is not confidence. That is a small power move from a campaign accustomed to bullying coverage instead of answering questions.

The article’s framing is weak wherever it treats that posture as ordinary spin. It is not ordinary. It is a warning shot from people who want the benefits of public scrutiny only when it flatters them.

The Pattern Underneath

The deeper pattern is familiar: anti-democratic moralism wrapped in farm-state populism. Lahn’s announcement praised Wulf as an “Iowa First fighter” for “Right to Repair,” “private property rights,” and “stopping corporate overreach.” That language is the camouflage. It gives the ticket a working-class varnish while the cultural core remains reactionary control over gender, sexuality, and legitimacy.

This is how the modern right keeps moving: it pairs kitchen-table slogans with a punitive worldview, then asks voters to separate the two. Don’t. The private scandal matters less than the public agenda. The public agenda is the point.
