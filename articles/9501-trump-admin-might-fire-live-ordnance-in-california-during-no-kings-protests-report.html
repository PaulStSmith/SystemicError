Trump admin might fire live ordnance in California during No Kings protests: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump points a finger from behind a bulletproof glass during a Navy 250 Celebration in Norfolk, Virginia, U.S. October 5, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

The Trump administration might fire live ordnance during a military celebration while the No Kings protests are occurring, prompting Gov. Gavin Newsom, a Democrat, to consider shutting down one of the state’s main interstates, according to a new report.

The Los Angeles Times reported on Wednesday that Newsom’s office “received, but not confirmed,” reports that the Trump administration would fire live rounds over an interstate from an offshore vessel during an event commemorating the Marine Corps’ 250th anniversary. The event is scheduled to take place on Saturday, when hundreds of “No Kings” protests are planned across the country.

Vice President JD Vance is expected to attend the ceremony, which is titled “Sea to Shore — A Review of Amphibious Strength,” according to the report.

“California always honors our Marines — but this isn’t the right way to go about it,” a Newsom spokesperson told The Times. “The White House should focus on paying their military, lowering grocery prices and honoring these soldiers for their service instead of pompous displays of power. The lack of coordination and communication from the federal government on this event — and the overall impact to our society and economy — is evident of the larger disarray that is the Trump Administration.”

Our Analysis:

A Spectacle of Misguided Priorities and Dangerous Decisions

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a dystopian novel, the Trump administration apparently contemplates turning a military celebration into a live-fire exercise, dangerously close to civilian protests. This alarming development, as reported by The Los Angeles Times, underscores not just the administration’s brazen disregard for public safety, but also its predilection for grandiose displays of military might over addressing pressing national concerns.

“Firing Live Rounds Over an Interstate” - Let that sink in. The notion that live ordnance could be discharged over a major interstate during a public event is not just ludicrous; it’s borderline negligent. The Trump administration’s plan to commemorate the Marine Corps’ 250th anniversary with such a hazardous act, especially in proximity to the “No Kings” protests, is a recipe for disaster. It’s a stark illustration of how militaristic pomp seems to trump (pun intended) public safety and civil liberties under this administration.

Governor Gavin Newsom’s Reaction - In a warranted response, Governor Newsom is considering shutting down one of the state’s main interstates to presumably protect the public from this potential hazard. Newsom’s office has been put in a position where they must scramble to safeguard Californians from their own federal government’s recklessness. The spokesperson’s statement captures the essence of frustration felt by many: “California always honors our Marines — but this isn’t the right way to go about it.” Indeed, honoring the military shouldn’t entail endangering the public.

Misplaced Priorities - The critique offered by Newsom’s spokesperson hits the nail on the head, highlighting a grotesque misallocation of attention and resources. In a time when the nation grapples with economic instability, the administration’s focus on a “pompous displays of power” is not just tone-deaf; it’s a dereliction of duty. The military, a venerable institution deserving of respect and celebration, is being used as a prop in a dangerous spectacle, rather than being supported in more substantive, meaningful ways.

A Reflection of Disarray - The lack of coordination and communication from the federal government, as pointed out by the spokesperson, is symptomatic of a larger issue within the Trump administration. This isn’t merely about a single event; it’s indicative of a systemic problem of governance where spectacle often supersedes substance. The potential live-fire event, juxtaposed with the “No Kings” protests, could be seen as a metaphor for the administration’s broader disconnect with the American populace.

In conclusion, this proposed military celebration, with its potential risks and blatant disregard for public welfare, is emblematic of an administration more concerned with displays of power than the actual well-being of its citizens. The fact that state officials have to contemplate drastic measures to protect the public from their own government’s actions is a sad indictment of the current state of affairs. As this situation unfolds, one can only hope that reason will prevail over recklessness. However, given the track record, one might be advised not to hold their breath.
