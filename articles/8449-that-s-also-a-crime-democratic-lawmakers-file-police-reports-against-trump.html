'That's also a crime': Democratic lawmakers file police reports against Trump

By Paulo Santos

Several Democratic members of the House of Representatives have filed official complaints with the U.S. Capitol Police Department after President Donald Trump endorsed a call to have them hanged.

Politico reported Friday that Reps. Jason Crow (D-Colo.), Chris DeLuzio (D-Pa.) and Chrissy Houlahan (D-Pa.) contacted Capitol Police to file reports about the threat, which Trump issued after they appeared in a video encouraging military service members to defy illegal orders. The video also featured Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-N.H.), along with Sens. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) and Elissa Slotkin. (D-Mich.)

Following the video’s release, Trump posted to his Truth Social account that their comments should be seen as “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by death!“ He also reposted a supporter’s comment calling for the six Democrats to be hanged.

“Each one of these traitors to our Country should be Arrested and put on trial,” Trump wrote. “Their words cannot be allowed to stand – We won’t have a Country anymore!!! An example must be set.”

Houlahan’s office told Politico that the congresswoman treated Trump’s threat no different than “all threats targeting Representative Houlahan” and reported it to police. Two of her district offices received bomb threats following Trump’s post, and her office was reportedly barraged with “thousands” of angry phone calls and email messages.

“If that’s not a threat to violence I don’t know what is,” Houlahan told The New Republic’s Greg Sargent. “Even if he for whatever reason didn’t mean it or doesn’t act on it, he’s just unleashed a lot of people who are not necessarily well on me and my team and my family, and that’s also a crime.”

The six Democrats noted in their video that they were all veterans of either the U.S. military or intelligence agencies. And their call to disobey illegal orders comes from the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ). That document stipulates that members of the military are duty-bound to obey “any lawful general order or regulation” lest they be subjected to a court-martial, but the provision of the UCMJ pertaining to the court-martial process stipulates that the duty to obey orders “does not apply to a patently illegal order, such as one that directs the commission of a crime.”

Our Analysis:

An Analysis of Trump’s Endorsement of Violence Against Democratic Lawmakers

In a move that should shock no one for its brazen disregard for democratic norms and the rule of law, Donald Trump has once again crossed a line that should be inviolable in any society that claims to cherish freedom and civil discourse. By endorsing a call for the hanging of several Democratic members of the House of Representatives, Trump has not only shown a flagrant disrespect for the lives and safety of elected officials but has also illuminated the dangerous underbelly of his political ethos.

The Facts

According to Politico, Representatives Jason Crow, Chris DeLuzio, and Chrissy Houlahan, among others, found themselves in the crosshairs of Trump’s ire after participating in a video encouraging military service members to adhere to the Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) and refuse illegal orders. This stance, rooted in the very fabric of military law and ethics, was twisted by Trump into a narrative of sedition, culminating in his call for their execution.

Trump’s message, disseminated through his Truth Social account, declared their actions as “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by death!” and further shared a supporter’s message advocating for their hanging. This act of incitement is not only reprehensible but also indicative of Trump’s penchant for authoritarian rhetoric.

The Response

The targeted lawmakers, understandably concerned for their safety and that of their staff and families, reported the threats to the U.S. Capitol Police Department. Following Trump’s incitement, Rep. Houlahan’s office became the target of bomb threats and a deluge of harassing communications. This escalation of hostility is a direct consequence of Trump’s rhetoric, highlighting the tangible dangers of political figures inciting violence against their opponents.

The Analysis

This incident is not an isolated one. It fits into a larger pattern of behavior exhibited by Trump, where he has repeatedly shown a willingness to undermine democratic principles and norms to protect his own interests and attack his perceived enemies. His endorsement of violence against elected officials for performing their duty is a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy when faced with demagoguery.

The lawmakers in question were advocating for a principle enshrined in military law: the refusal to follow unlawful orders. This principle is fundamental to preventing abuses of power and safeguarding the rule of law within the military. By framing this advocacy as sedition, Trump not only misrepresents the law but also attacks the very foundations of lawful military conduct.

The Implications

Trump’s actions have serious implications for the safety of public officials and the integrity of political discourse in the United States. His willingness to incite violence against his political opponents undermines the democratic process and emboldens individuals with violent tendencies. This is not merely a matter of political disagreement; it is a direct threat to the lives of elected representatives and a challenge to the rule of law.

Conclusion

In a functional democracy, there should be no place for rhetoric that endangers the lives of those who serve the public. Trump’s endorsement of violence against Democratic lawmakers is a chilling demonstration of his disregard for democratic norms and the safety of those who oppose him. It is imperative that such actions are met with unequivocal condemnation and accountability. The silence or complicity of his party and supporters only serves to normalize this dangerous behavior and further endanger the principles upon which the United States was founded.
