'More cruel than they wanted': Joe Rogan breaking with Trump on ICE stuns ex-GOP analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Joe Rogan’s Break with Trump: A Turning Point or Mere Rhetoric?

FILE PHOTO: US media personality Joe Rogan stands for a benediction after President Donald Trump was sworn in as the 47th US President in the US Capitol Rotunda in Washington, DC, on January 20, 2025. SAUL LOEB/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo

Popular podcaster Joe Rogan appears to have broken with President Donald Trump on one of the president’s key policy platforms during a recent episode, a move that stunned one former GOP analyst.

During a recent episode of “The Joe Rogan Experience,” Rogan said Trump’s effort to send people to deport immigrants to foreign countries “shows no heart.”

“That shows no heart, and that’s the problem,” Rogan said. “You’re not going to get any reasonable people to want to go along with that. Any kind person would look at that and go, ‘This can’t be the only way to do this.’”

Tim Miller, a former Republican strategist, said on a new episode of “Bulwark Takes” on Thursday that Rogan’s comments show he’s becoming a “stalwart” on the issue for parts of the right wing.

“It’s helping wake up a lot of people who might not be consuming whatever left media or even mainstream media,” Miller said. “A lot of people who aren’t reading the New York Times or watching the nightly news and who are getting their information from Rogan... maybe some of them will also be woken up to the fact that holy s--- they signed up for something that was a lot more cruel than they wanted.”

Our Analysis:

Joe Rogan’s Break with Trump: A Turning Point or Mere Rhetoric?

In a recent turn of events that can only be described as startling, Joe Rogan, the popular podcaster and erstwhile ally to conservative talking points, seems to have veered off the Trumpian path. During an episode of “The Joe Rogan Experience,” Rogan critiqued President Donald Trump’s immigration policies, particularly the efforts to deport immigrants, as lacking “heart.” This marks a significant departure from the echo chamber of right-wing rhetoric, revealing a chasm in what was once thought to be a monolithic base.

“That shows no heart, and that’s the problem,” Rogan stated, cutting through the usual political jargon with a blunt instrument of truth. This isn’t just a critique of a policy; it’s an admonishment of a philosophy, a rebuke of a system that views human beings as mere chess pieces in a grand political game. Rogan’s assertion that “You’re not going to get any reasonable people to want to go along with that” is not just a statement; it’s a reality check for those who have been riding the wave of populist rhetoric without considering its human cost.

Tim Miller, a former Republican strategist, seems to think Rogan’s comments could signal a shift, calling him a “stalwart” on the issue for parts of the right wing. Miller’s optimism, as featured in “Bulwark Takes,” suggests that Rogan’s influence might awaken those who have been insulated from alternative viewpoints, particularly those “who aren’t reading the New York Times or watching the nightly news.” The suggestion here is that Rogan’s platform reaches corners of the public sphere that traditional media cannot, acting as a potential catalyst for change among those who might have uncritically accepted Trump’s immigration policies.

However, before we herald Rogan as the harbinger of a more compassionate conservatism, let’s not leap without looking. While it’s refreshing to see a figure like Rogan challenge Trump’s more draconian policies, we must ask: Is this a genuine pivot to a new way of thinking, or merely a momentary lapse in the usual programming? Rogan’s platform, after all, is known for its chameleonic nature, often shaped by the whims of its host or the perspectives of its guests.

Moreover, Miller’s comments, though optimistic, highlight a disturbing reality about the consumption of political discourse in today’s society. The idea that Rogan’s audience could be “woken up” to the cruelty of policies they previously supported suggests a troubling passivity in our engagement with politics. It speaks to a broader issue of political alignment and the sources from which people are willing to receive their information.

In essence, while Rogan’s break from Trump on immigration is noteworthy, it raises more questions than it answers. Can a single statement on a podcast undo the years of hardline rhetoric that have dominated the immigration debate? More importantly, does this signify a genuine shift in the conversation, or is it merely a drop in the ocean of political discourse?

As we navigate these tumultuous political waters, let’s not be too quick to celebrate what could simply be a fleeting moment of dissent. Instead, let’s demand more consistent and meaningful engagement from public figures on issues of human dignity and rights. After all, in the grand scheme of things, it will take more than a podcast episode to dismantle the systemic injustices that underpin policies like those championed by Trump.
