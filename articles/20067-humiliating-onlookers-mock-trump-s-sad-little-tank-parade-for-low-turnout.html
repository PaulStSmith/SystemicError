'Humiliating': Onlookers mock Trump's 'sad little tank parade' for low turnout

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump was ridiculed on Saturday for a "sad little tank parade" with a purportedly low turnout rate.

The New York Times was one of the first major outlets to flag what could be considered signs of failure for the MAGA event. One reporter noted that "hordes" of people were trying to leave before Trump even spoke, while another called the parade and crowd "listless."

But the Times wasn't the only one to get in on the action.

On social media, several commentators and observers circulated images of the less-than-expected MAGA crowd.

Comedian and writer Mike Drucker shared a video from the event, and wrote, "This is genuinely humiliating to see."

Drucker in a separate post added a photo of Trump, and said, "It’s your birthday. It’s Saturday night. But you made one critical mistake: You threw a $45 million parade for yourself without realizing you’d be bored out of your mind."

Democratic strategist Mike Nellis said, "America's army won WWI and WWII, and this is the best they could f------ do with $45 million for this parade?"

"I get the weather, but this is embarrassing," he added.

NBC reporter Mark Segraves said, "The #Army250 parade starting 30 minutes early due to incoming weather. Crowd nowhere near the 200,000 expected," to which former GOP lawmaker Barbara Comstock said, "Newsmax is saying about 10,000 there. That’s small for some protest rallies today and a huge waste of our military $$$ when the world is on fire."

Conservative Tim Miller gave his "main takeaway" from the event.

"Lame and embarrassing Trump having a sad little tank parade while Israel is wrecking the entire IRGC with precision strikes just underscores our decline," the strategist added.

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Diminishing Returns: Trump's "Sad Little Tank Parade"

In what can only be described as a farcical display of vanity, Donald Trump's latest attempt to muster MAGA fervor—a "sad little tank parade"—has been met with widespread ridicule and embarrassment. With a turnout that barely scratched the surface of expectations, the event has become a symbol of a waning influence, a microcosm of the former president's current political standing.

The Hype Versus Reality

Promised as a grandiose display of patriotism and military might, the event was anything but. The New York Times, never one to shy away from pointing out the emperor's new clothes, was among the first to highlight the glaring discrepancies between expectation and reality. Reports of "hordes" of attendees attempting to flee the scene before Trump's oration only serve to underscore the event's failure to captivate even the most ardent of MAGA supporters.

A Parade of Discontent

Observers, from comedians to political strategists, have been quick to lampoon the event's dismal execution. Mike Drucker's commentary cuts to the heart of the matter, encapsulating the sheer absurdity of throwing a $45 million self-aggrandizing parade that fails to entertain, let alone inspire. Meanwhile, Democratic strategist Mike Nellis's comparison of the event's lackluster display to the historical might and victory of America's military forces during the World Wars is both a biting indictment and a poignant reflection on misallocated resources and misplaced priorities.

The Cost of Misguided Spectacle

Perhaps most telling is the criticism coming from within Trump's own camp. Former GOP lawmaker Barbara Comstock's observation of the event's paltry attendance numbers—compared unfavorably even to modest protest rallies—highlights not just a failure of showmanship but a profound waste of military funding at a time of global instability. Tim Miller's remarks further illustrate the juxtaposition between Trump's feeble display of power and the current international military engagements, like Israel's precision strikes on the IRGC, underscoring a perceived decline in American leadership and strength.

Conclusion: A Parade to Nowhere

Trump's "sad little tank parade" serves as a stark reminder of the dangers of conflating leadership with spectacle. In his quest to reaffirm his relevance and might, Trump has instead laid bare the cracks in his armor, revealing a spectacle of diminishing returns. The event, far from showcasing strength, has only highlighted a desperate cling to a past era of influence that is swiftly fading into the annals of history.

In a world fraught with real challenges and conflicts, the spectacle at the heart of this parade serves as a misguided distraction from the substantive leadership and vision needed. As the dust settles on this ill-fated parade, one can only hope for a return to genuine discourse and action that addresses the pressing issues facing the nation and the world, rather than indulging in the empty theatrics of a bygone era.
