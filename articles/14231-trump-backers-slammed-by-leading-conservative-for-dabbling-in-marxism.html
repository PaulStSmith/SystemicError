Trump backers slammed by leading conservative for 'dabbling in Marxism'

Longtime right-wing strategist and commentator Erick Erickson accused President Donald Trump's supporters on Friday of effectively embracing what he called a core trait of Marxism in order to pass their agenda.

"One of the hallmarks of Marxism is a redefinition of words and control of language," wrote Erickson on X. "Marxists believe if you control language, you control reality and thereby acquire power. Some on the right are dabbling in Marxism, now insisting tariffs are not tax."

"When you embrace Marxism as a political tool, don't be surprised to wake up one day a communist," he warned.

Marxism is an economic and political theory primarily based on rejecting capitalism and free enterprise, and promoting collective ownership of capital. "Control of language" is not inherently a plank of Marxism although, in practice, many countries whose political systems were founded on Marxist ideology ended up repressive of speech, which often had societal consequences even after these countries abandoned their systems.

Trump's aggressive worldwide tariff policy, which was struck down by a federal court earlier this week but is currently still in effect while an appellate court reviews the issue, has been widely criticized by economists and even some conservative politicians for imposing steep taxes that will ultimately be paid by consumers.

The administration has denied this is the case and even put political pressure on retail giants like Walmart not to raise their prices in response to the tariffs.

Erickson, who previously worked on the George W. Bush presidential campaign and advised several GOP politicians in Georgia, has had a fraught relationship with Trump and the MAGA movement, at times endorsing it, but on many other occasions calling out elements of Trump's agenda and inner circle he believes are harmful to the country or the Republican Party.

Our Analysis:

Erickson's Accusation: A Dive into Alleged Marxism Among Trump Supporters

Erick Erickson, a figure well-versed in the conservative political landscape, has lobbed a dramatic accusation against supporters of President Donald Trump, effectively charging them with embracing Marxism. This claim, centered on their stance regarding tariffs, is not just a casual comment but a profound critique that demands a closer examination.

The Core Argument: Tariffs and Linguistic Control

Erickson posits that a significant number of Trump's followers are engaging in what he describes as a "core trait of Marxism" by redefining tariffs as something other than taxes. This, according to Erickson, mirrors the Marxist approach to controlling language as a means to control reality and, by extension, power. The irony here is palpable; those who often vehemently denounce Marxism are accused of employing one of its tactics. Erickson's warning is stark: "When you embrace Marxism as a political tool, don't be surprised to wake up one day a communist."

The Misinterpretation of Marxism

However, Erickson's argument treads on shaky ground when he simplifies Marxism to a mere manipulation of language. While it's true that repressive regimes, often inspired by Marxist ideology, have historically sought to control speech, it is an oversimplification to reduce Marxism to this aspect alone. Marxism, at its core, is an economic and political theory that advocates for the collective ownership of capital, a detail Erickson lightly touches upon but fails to explore in depth. His critique, while sharp, seems more concerned with the political weaponization of language rather than engaging with the full breadth of Marxist theory.

Trump's Tariff Policy: A Misguided Adventure?

Erickson's commentary is anchored in the broader context of Trump's "aggressive worldwide tariff policy," a strategy that has not only drawn criticism from economists but also from within conservative circles. This policy, recently hindered by a federal court decision, operates under the controversial belief that tariffs are not a direct tax on consumers—a stance the Trump administration staunchly defends, despite evidence to the contrary.

The administration's denial and its pressure on entities like Walmart to absorb these costs rather than pass them on to consumers speak volumes about the manipulation of economic realities to suit a political narrative. It is here that Erickson's accusation finds some grounding, albeit through a convoluted path.

Erickson's Fraught Relationship with Trump

Erick Erickson's critique cannot be fully appreciated without acknowledging his complex relationship with Trump and the MAGA movement. Having oscillated between endorsement and criticism, Erickson emerges as a figure attempting to navigate the tumultuous waters of contemporary conservative politics. His willingness to call out what he perceives as detrimental elements within Trump's agenda and inner circle indicates a broader struggle within the Republican Party—a struggle between traditional conservative values and the disruptive force of Trumpism.

Conclusion: A Scathing Critique or a Misdirected Attack?

Erickson's accusation of Marxism among Trump supporters is a provocative charge that, while flawed in its simplification of Marxist theory, highlights a real concern regarding the manipulation of language and reality in political discourse. His critique, however, might benefit from a more nuanced understanding of Marxism and a deeper exploration of the economic implications of Trump's tariff policy.

In the end, Erickson's commentary serves as a reminder of the ongoing ideological battles within the conservative movement, raising questions about the future direction of the Republican Party and the principles it stands to uphold.
