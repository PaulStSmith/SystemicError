Top Trump ally concedes Gavin Newsom's major social blitz is starting to sting

California Gov. Gavin Newsom has been on a social media frenzy over the past several days trolling President Donald Trump by evoking his writing style in several posts, and while the strategy has been mocked by some conservative critics, even some MAGA heavyweights are admitting the social media blitz is starting to sting.

“He’s no Trump, but if you look at the Democratic Party, he's at least getting up there, and he's trying to imitate a Trumpian vision of fighting,” said Steve Bannon, former White House chief of staff under Trump and host of the alt-right podcast War Room, speaking with Politico Wednesday.

“He looks like the only person in the Democratic Party who is organizing a fight that they feel they can win. People in the MAGA movement and the America First movement should start paying attention to this, because it’s not going to go away, they’re only going to get more intense.”

Newsom’s posts have been varied, but generally target Trump and his administration, and in a manner mirroring Trump’s syntax and capitalization style. Among Newsom’s most viral recent posts include the reposting of an image depicting him on Mount Rushmore, and the posting of an image generated with generative artificial intelligence depicting him praying while surrounded by pro-Trump figures Tucker Carlson, Kid Rock and the late Hulk Hogan.

One particularly viral post of Newsom’s saw the governor mock Trump for a social media blunder in which he shared a one-word social media post that read “bela.”

“DONALD (TINY HANDS), HAS WRITTEN HIS AUTOBIOGRAPHY THIS MORNING – UNFORTUNATELY (LOW IQ) HE SPELLED IT WRONG – ‘BETA,’ Newsom’s account posted. “SOON YOU WILL BE A ‘FIRED’ BETA BECAUSE OF MY PERFECT, ‘BEAUTIFUL MAPS.’ THANK YOU FOR YOUR ATTENTION TO THIS MATTER! – GCN.”

The White House even fired back at Newsom Wednesday in a response to Politico, a response that included only a single image, an original meme referencing a scene from the AMC series Mad Men depicting Trump disregarding a person representing Newsom’s X account.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Trolling: Newsom vs. Trump

In a world where political discourse increasingly resembles a circus, California Governor Gavin Newsom has decided to not just buy a ticket but to dance under the big top. His recent foray into social media trolling, particularly targeting President Donald Trump, has sparked a mix of amusement, criticism, and, interestingly, some grudging respect from unexpected quarters.

The Strategy: Mimicry or Mockery?

Newsom's approach is not just about trolling for the sake of online banter. It's a calculated move, employing Trump's own distinctive social media style against him—complete with the hallmark syntax and capitalization. This isn't mimicry; it's strategic mockery. By adopting Trump's communication method, Newsom is not only engaging in psychological warfare but also signaling his readiness to dominate the digital arena, a battleground Trump has undeniably mastered.

The Bannon Seal of Approval

It's not every day that Steve Bannon, a figure synonymous with the alt-right and Trump's chaotic administration, doles out compliments to Democrats. Yet, Bannon's acknowledgment of Newsom's tactics as a genuine threat to the MAGA movement is both telling and significant. Bannon's comments underline a crucial point: Newsom's social media onslaught is not a mere political stunt but a potent strategy that's resonating beyond echo chambers.

The Content: Viral and Visceral

The content of Newsom's posts, ranging from an AI-generated image of him praying surrounded by pro-Trump figures to mocking Trump's typos, straddles the line between creative and cringeworthy. Yet, the virality of these posts cannot be ignored. They serve not just as entertainment but as sharp political tools, cutting through the noise to highlight the absurdity and flaws of Trump's administration.

The Response: A Meme War

The White House's response, a meme referencing a scene from Mad Men, is emblematic of the current state of political discourse. While it attempts to dismiss Newsom's jabs, it also tacitly acknowledges the effectiveness of his strategy. This back-and-forth, played out in memes and tweets, might seem trivial but is a reflection of the broader battle for public opinion and narrative control.

Conclusion: The Double-Edged Sword

Newsom's strategy, while attention-grabbing, is not without risks. Engaging in a social media tit-for-tat with Trump could elevate Newsom's profile but also potentially reduce serious political discourse to mere entertainment. Furthermore, it risks alienating voters tired of political theatrics.

In the end, Newsom's social media frenzy against Trump is a fascinating case study in modern political strategy. It highlights the power of digital platforms in shaping public perception and the importance of narrative control. Whether this approach will yield long-term political dividends for Newsom remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the lines between politics and entertainment are not just blurred but virtually nonexistent.
