'Something is deeply wrong': Ex-Trump aide flags new evidence of mental decline

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 18, 20254 min read

Donald Trump made the "big mistake" of attacking his own followers in part because he is "unraveling," according to a former operative.

Lev Parnas, a former associate of Trump who worked with the White House team on behind-the-scenes matters, has recently been speaking out about Trump's actions on Substack.

In a piece dated on Thursday, Parnas summarized a bit of what Trump is doing regarding lobbing insults at his own MAGA followers.

"I know a lot of you have been wondering what Trump’s next move might be. But as I’ve been telling you, the real story isn’t about what he will do—it’s about what he’s already doing right now. The cover-up is intensifying. And this morning, Donald Trump took a sledgehammer to what remained of his MAGA base," Parnas wrote. "In a Truth Social post, he didn’t just call out his supporters—he disemboweled them. The very people who built his movement and who defended him through every scandal were called irrelevant. Weaklings."

Later in the day, according to Parnas, "he doubled down while taking questions in the Oval. He called MAGA foolish, stupid, and duped by the Democrats—specifically about what he now calls the 'Epstein hoax.'"

According to Parnas, this unforced error was the result of Trump's mental health.

"This was a big mistake. Because something is happening that the media is not reporting," according to Parnas. "But I’ve been telling you for months, and now even Trump’s closest allies are starting to admit it: the man is unraveling."

Parnas continued:

"You could call it desperation, cognitive decline, or just plain delusion. But when Donald Trump can’t remember that he himself nominated Jerome Powell to the Fed—and then blames Joe Biden for it—you know something is deeply wrong. He’s so used to blaming Biden for everything, it’s now automatic. The economy? Biden’s fault. The Epstein files? Biden planted them."

Regarding Trump's polling, Parnas says, "Quinnipiac’s bombshell poll just dropped, and it shows what Trump fears most: his own voters are done being lied to. 63% of all voters disapprove of Trump’s Epstein cover-up. But here’s the kicker: 40% of Republicans are rejecting his stonewalling too. That’s not a political hiccup—that’s a full-blown revolt inside the base."

"And the response from Trump? Rage. Projection. Attacks," Parnas wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Self-Sabotage: A Critical Analysis

In a revelatory piece by Lev Parnas, a former associate of Donald Trump who had a front-row seat to the inner workings of Trump's White House, we are given a stark depiction of a man in the throes of what can only be described as a self-destructive spiral. Parnas, utilizing the platform Substack, doesn't just highlight Trump's recent tirades against his own MAGA base; he delves into the deeper, more troubling implications of these actions.

The Unraveling of Donald Trump

Parnas's observations suggest a shocking turn of events where Trump, in a move of bewildering strategy, decides to alienate the very supporters who have stood by him through thick and thin. Describing Trump's actions as taking a "sledgehammer" to his MAGA base is no small statement. It's a vivid portrayal of a man seemingly determined to destroy his own legacy, branding his followers as "irrelevant," "weaklings," and even "duped by the Democrats." Such insults are not just unwise; they are political suicide.

The notion that this self-sabotage is indicative of Trump's mental unraveling is a bold claim, yet Parnas supports it with compelling evidence. Trump's apparent cognitive decline, desperation, or delusion—whichever label one prefers—manifests in his inability to recall his own decisions (such as nominating Jerome Powell to the Fed) and his incessant, misplaced blame on Joe Biden for everything wrong, including the ludicrous "Epstein hoax."

The Fallout: A MAGA Revolt

The response from Trump's base to his antics, as reported by Parnas, is particularly telling. Citing a "bombshell" Quinnipiac poll, Parnas points out a significant shift: a notable portion of Trump's own Republican followers are now rejecting his narratives, with 40% disapproving of his handling of the so-called Epstein cover-up. This isn't merely a "political hiccup;" it's indicative of a profound and growing rift within the MAGA movement itself.

Trump's reaction to this internal dissent? Predictably, rage, projection, and further attacks. This behavior doesn't just highlight a man out of touch with his base; it showcases a leader incapable of accepting responsibility or adapting to critique. Such characteristics are dangerous in any leader, particularly one who still wields significant influence over American political discourse.

Conclusion: The Self-Destructive Path of a Flailing Leader

What we're witnessing, as detailed by Parnas, is not just the political decline of Donald Trump, but a cautionary tale about the perils of hubris, denial, and the refusal to engage with reality. Trump's attacks on his own followers mark a new low in his presidential legacy, suggesting a man who, in his desperation, has lost sight of the very people who propelled him to power.

In his piece, Parnas doesn't just offer a critique; he provides a window into the tragic unraveling of a figure who, once considered the unshakeable leader of the MAGA movement, now seems determined to undermine everything he built. This isn't just about Trump's fall from grace; it's a stark reminder of the fragility of political loyalty and the volatile nature of populist movements.

As observers and participants in the political landscape, we must ask ourselves: What are the broader implications of this internal conflict? And what does this mean for the future of American politics? The answers to these questions are still unfolding, but one thing is clear—Trump's self-inflicted wounds may very well be the most damning indictment of his presidency.
