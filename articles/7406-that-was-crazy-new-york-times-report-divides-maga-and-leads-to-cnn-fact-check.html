'That was crazy': New York Times report divides MAGA — and leads to CNN fact check

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 23, 20255 min read

A recent New York Times article about dark money in American politics led one outgoing mayor to lob allegations against a former presidential candidate, leading to division among MAGA and a fact check.

The NYT recently published an article called “Nearly $900 Million Flowed in Secret to Help Harris and Trump in 2024.” One of the authors of the piece, Teddy Schleifer, took to social media, writing, “One donor gave $97.5 million to a nonprofit that gave $250 million to Kamala Harris’s super PAC.”

“You’ll never know who the donor is. Hello to our campaign-finance system!” he wrote, attaching the article stating that the donor was anonymous and that millions were sent in by “Future Forward USA Action, the so-called dark-money group of the main super PAC backing” Joe Biden and Harris.

That led to the former Democrat Eric Adams, once the popular mayor of New York, to lash out against Harris herself:

“Her Justice Department wanted to lock me up for more than 30 years for taking an upgraded airline seat 10 years ago,” he claimed, misrepresenting the charges against him.

That caused a division among allies of Trump, with the president’s confidant Roger Stone saying, “No, it was the $100,000 in illegal contribution you took from the Turks that you then laundered through New York City straw donors then applied for matching funds. in return you let the Turks have an occupancy permit for a building that was not safe. Crook.”

Criminal defense attorney Peter Cheese, Esq. said, “You keep spinning this yarn, Eric, and it doesn’t get any more believable.”

Elon Musk, however, still an ally to Trump, agreed with Adams. The billionaire wrote, “That was crazy. There has been too much abuse of our legal system.”

Another right-wing tech CEO, Reza Chowdhury, asked, “Why did Kamala and the autopen administration dump a quarter of million illegal entrants to the US into NYC without any meaningful support?”

Journalist Henry Goldman said to Adams, “The press corps never adequately covered your mental health.”

“If I were still in City Hall it’s a story I would have wanted to do,” he then added.

CNN’s Edward-Isaac Dovere provided a fact check:

“ Kamala Harris, never having been president, was never in charge of the Justice Department — and the charges against the outgoing mayor were supported by prosecutors with a range of political persuasions.”

Union political director Kevin Elkins said it was “bribery.”

“And then you were about to get indicted again for destroying documents before Daddy Trump saved you,” Elkins then concluded

Our Analysis:

Dark Money and Divisive Politics: A Closer Look

The recent New York Times article shedding light on the nearly $900 million of dark money flowing into American politics, particularly favoring Harris and Trump for the 2024 elections, is a testament to the opaque and murky waters of political financing in the United States. Teddy Schleifer’s revelation on social media about a single donor’s $97.5 million contribution to a nonprofit, which then funneled $250 million to Kamala Harris’s super PAC, underscores a disturbing reality: our campaign-finance system is a breeding ground for anonymity and influence-peddling.

Eric Adams’s Reaction: A Misdirection?
Former Mayor Eric Adams’s response to the article, however, veers into the realm of personal vendetta and misrepresentation. Claiming that Harris’s Justice Department sought to imprison him for an upgraded airline seat is not only factually incorrect—as Harris was never president and thus never in charge of the Justice Department—but it also detracts from the real issue at hand: the influence of dark money in politics. Adams’s allegations appear to be a smokescreen, diverting attention from the systemic flaws of political financing to individual grievances.

The Fallout: Division and Deflection
The article has evidently stirred the pot among Trump’s allies, with figures like Roger Stone and Elon Musk chiming in with their own takes, further muddling the discourse. Stone’s accusation against Adams and Musk’s vague lament about legal system abuses do little to address the core problem of dark money. Instead, they serve to deepen divisions and distract from the need for campaign finance reform.

Reza Chowdhury’s question about Kamala Harris and the “autopen administration’s” handling of immigration is another example of how discussions about dark money quickly devolve into unrelated political grievances, failing to stay focused on the systemic issues that allow such vast sums of anonymous money to influence American politics.

The Media’s Role: Inadequate Coverage or Misdirection?
Journalist Henry Goldman’s remark about the inadequate coverage of Adams’s mental health, while personal and somewhat out of place, highlights another significant issue: the media’s role in political discourse. Are we, as a society, focusing on the right issues? Or are sensational allegations and personal attacks overshadowing the fundamental problems with our political system?

Fact Check: A Beacon of Clarity
CNN’s Edward-Isaac Dovere provides a necessary fact check, reminding us that Kamala Harris was never in a position to use the Justice Department against Adams. This fact check serves as a beacon of clarity in an otherwise murky discussion, emphasizing the importance of grounding political debates in reality, rather than unfounded allegations and personal animosities.

Conclusion: The Real Issue at Hand
The uproar following the New York Times article reflects the chaotic state of American political discourse, where substantive discussions about campaign finance reform are overshadowed by personal vendettas, unrelated political grievances, and a lack of focus on systemic issues. As we navigate these turbulent waters, it’s crucial to remember the underlying problem: a campaign-finance system that allows anonymous donors to wield disproportionate influence, undermining the democratic process. It’s time to refocus the conversation on meaningful reform, rather than getting lost in the noise of divisive politics.
