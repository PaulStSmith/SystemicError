America's oil industry in serious decline — thanks to Trump: WSJ

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20254 min read

Donald Trump attends the America First Policy Institute (AFPI) gala at Mar-A-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., November 14, 2024. REUTERS/Carlos Barria TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

The collective stock value of America's oil and gas firms reportedly declined by over $280 billion following the announcement of President Donald Trump's controversial tariff policy.

The Wall Street Journal reported Friday that this drop took place between April 2, the day Trump announced the tariff measures, and this Monday.

This amount exceeds the market capitalization of Chevron, the second-largest oil company in the United States. The oil industry's total market value is said to have fallen more than any other major sector.

"Among the biggest U.S. oil companies, Exxon and Chevron shares have declined 11% and 18%, respectively. Oil-field services giant Schlumberger and its top rivals, Halliburton and Baker Hughes, have fallen between 21% and 23%. Those companies are bellwethers for activity in the U.S. oil patch," noted the WSJ report, written by Benoît Morenne, Collin Eaton and Andrew Mollica.

The report further said U.S. oil prices dropped 19 percent in April to $58.21 a barrel. This is the lowest level to which the prices have been in over four years.

Several economists expect that tariffs imposed by Trump will trigger a worldwide economic slowdown, leading to decreased demand for crude oil. The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) and its partners plan to increase their production by over 400,000 barrels daily starting in May, according to the report.

"Before Trump came into office, oil production in most U.S. crude regions was set to decline because of maturing fields and dwindling sweet spots. Oil executives expect Trump’s policies to accelerate this trend, including in the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico, the largest oil field in the country," the report adds.

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Fallout: A Sharp Dive into America's Oil and Gas Sector

The announcement of President Donald Trump's tariff policy has sent shockwaves through the American economy, particularly hitting the oil and gas sector with the force of a sledgehammer. According to a recent report by The Wall Street Journal, the collective stock value of America's oil and gas firms plummeted by an astounding $280 billion following the tariff announcement. This financial hemorrhage not only underscores the immediate impact of the policy but also raises critical questions about the long-term health and stability of the U.S. energy sector.

The Numbers Don't Lie

The magnitude of this decline cannot be overstated. To put it into perspective, the loss in market capitalization exceeds that of Chevron, the second-largest oil company in the United States. This isn't just a dip; it's a nosedive. Exxon and Chevron, giants in the industry, saw their shares decline by 11% and 18%, respectively. Meanwhile, oil-field services behemoths like Schlumberger, Halliburton, and Baker Hughes experienced drops between 21% and 23%. These aren't marginal decreases. These are significant, potentially crippling decreases that signal a profound lack of confidence in the sector's future profitability and stability.

A Barrel of Woes

Further compounding the industry's woes is the precipitous drop in U.S. oil prices, which fell 19 percent in April to $58.21 a barrel—the lowest level in over four years. This isn't just a reflection of market jitters; it's indicative of a potentially seismic shift in the global energy market. The tariffs, intended or not, seem poised to catalyze a worldwide economic slowdown. The logic is simple yet stark: slower economic growth leads to reduced demand for crude oil. This is Economics 101, and it appears the administration either ignored or underestimated these fundamental principles.

OPEC's Countermove

In what can be seen as a direct countermeasure to the United States' flailing oil sector, the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) and its partners are planning to increase their production by over 400,000 barrels daily starting in May. This move, while seemingly a boon for global oil supply, places additional pressure on U.S. producers. It's akin to adding fuel to a fire—the fire being the already declining profitability and viability of the U.S. oil and gas sector.

The Trump Effect

Before Trump's tenure, U.S. oil production was predicted to decline due to natural attrition—maturing fields and dwindling sweet spots. However, the report suggests that Trump’s policies could accelerate this trend, particularly in critical areas like the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico, the largest oil field in the country. This is not merely a setback; it's potentially a death knell for American dominance in the global oil market.

Final Thoughts

The Wall Street Journal's report paints a bleak picture of the immediate and possibly lasting consequences of President Trump's tariff policy on the American oil and gas industry. The significant loss in market value, the drop in oil prices, and the strategic moves by OPEC all point to a sector under siege. It's a stark reminder that policy decisions have real, often immediate, impacts on our economy. The administration's gamble appears to have backfired, leaving the U.S. oil and gas sector—and by extension, the American economy—in a precarious position.

To the Dems and naysayers: the evidence is mounting. The Trump administration's policies are not just contentious; they are actively undermining the bedrock of America's energy sector. It's time to stop questioning whether Trump will attempt to "destroy us" and start addressing the systemic errors his policies are exacerbating. The clock isn't just ticking; it's about to strike midnight.
