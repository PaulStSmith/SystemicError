'No job can last forever': Dem urged to step aside​ in impassioned plea from 'dear friend'

By Paulo Santos

Former Democratic National Committee chair Donna Brazile penned a tender plea in The Washington Post to Washington, D.C.'s congressional delegate, Eleanor Holmes Norton, to abstain from seeking re-election and clear the way for a new generation of representation for the district in a turbulent time.

Brazile, who previously served as Norton's campaign manager and describes herself as a "dear friend" who sees Norton as a "role model," released her article amid reporting that the 88-year-old delegate, now the oldest member in Congress, has grown too tired to do the duties of her office.

"I’ve known my dear friend Eleanor for 44 years, since I came to Washington after graduating from college and worked with her and other civil rights leaders to win enactment of a federal holiday honoring the Rev. Martin Luther King Jr," she wrote, noting that even though she has no vote on the full floor of Congress, she has achieved numerous policy wins for D.C. including scholarships, homeowner tax credits, and a long list of civil rights victories. Nonetheless, "She is no longer the dynamo she once was ... It’s in her best interest, and the interest of D.C., for her to serve her current term but then end her extraordinary service in Congress and not seek reelection next year."

The fact is, Brazile continued, this is a critical moment when D.C. needs clear-headed, energetic leadership to fight against Trump's attempts to bend the city to submission.

"D.C. is under attack as at no other time in recent history, and we need a new champion to defend us. President Donald Trump is treating the District like a colonial possession he can rule as a dictator, rather than a city governed by leaders who are elected by voters," wrote Brazile. "Trump has sent National Guard members and federal law enforcement officers into D.C. to round up undocumented immigrants — many of whom have jobs and are valued residents of our community ... Washington is a city under occupation."

This year has seen a large number of elder statesmen in both parties, from Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) to Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY), announce their retirement, said Brazile — all of whom are younger than Norton. Even former President Joe Biden is younger.

"Norton should follow the example of other older elected officials who have retired after many years of service," she concluded. "After doing so much for D.C. for so long, it’s understandable that she wants to remain in Congress. Public service is her life. But no job can last forever, and no person is irreplaceable. As I’ve told her in person, retirement from Congress is the right next chapter for her — and for the District."

Our Analysis:

The Call for Change: A Dissection of Brazile’s Plea

In an article that oscillates between a tender farewell and a stark wake-up call, Donna Brazile publicly urges Eleanor Holmes Norton, Washington D.C.'s non-voting congressional delegate, to step aside for the sake of ushering in a new era of leadership. Brazile's piece, draped in the robes of friendship and respect, navigates through the delicate terrain of political retirement with the grace of a seasoned diplomat yet lands punches where they're due.

The Heartfelt Prelude

Let's recognize the emotional labor that Brazile pours into her plea. She begins with a walk down memory lane, establishing her deep, personal connections with Norton that span over four decades. This is not merely a political maneuver but a heartfelt appeal from someone who has been in the trenches with Norton. However, the nostalgia is quickly overshadowed by the gravity of the situation at hand. Norton, at 88, is portrayed as a beloved yet diminished force, a relic of a bygone era struggling to keep pace with the demands of a district under siege.

The Stark Reality

Brazile doesn't mince words in depicting the current state of affairs. D.C. is under attack, she asserts, with Trump treating the district as "a colonial possession." The imagery is potent, invoking scenes of unwelcome occupation and the erosion of local governance by federal overreach. This is where Brazile's argument pivots from personal to political, framing Norton's continued service not as a testament to her resilience but as a liability in the face of existential threats to D.C.'s autonomy.

The Unspoken Truth

While Brazile's public call for Norton's retirement is cloaked in layers of respect and admiration, it also unearths an uncomfortable truth about the lifecycle of political careers. The mention of other elder statesmen stepping down is not incidental; it's a calculated reminder that there is dignity in knowing when to pass the torch. The comparison to figures like Jerry Nadler and Mitch McConnell subtly underscores the broader issue of age and capability, without resorting to ageism.

The Underlying Message

Beneath the surface of Brazile's plea is a deeper commentary on the state of American politics—a system increasingly characterized by gerontocracy, where the old guard clings to power at the expense of generational renewal. By singling out Norton, Brazile inadvertently spotlights a systemic issue that transcends individual cases.

Conclusion: Between the Lines

Brazile’s article, while a personal appeal to a long-time colleague, serves as a microcosm of a larger, pressing dialogue on leadership, aging, and the necessity for political evolution. Her call for Norton to retire is not merely about making way for younger leaders; it's a clarion call for a reinvigorated defense of D.C. against the encroachments of Trumpian politics.

In framing her argument, Brazile navigates the delicate balance between honoring a legacy and recognizing the imperative for change. Her plea is a testament to the complex interplay of loyalty, legacy, and the relentless march of time—elements that, when left unchecked, can stifle progress and leave even the most storied careers as cautionary tales of overstaying one's welcome.

In this light, Brazile's article is not just a plea but a wake-up call—a reminder that the fight for what is right often requires tough choices, including the courage to step aside when the time comes.
