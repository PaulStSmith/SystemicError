Ukrainian official predicts disastrous Trump-Zelensky meeting: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskiy and U.S. President Donald Trump meet, while they attend the funeral of Pope Francis, at the Vatican April 26, 2025. Ukrainian Presidential Press Service/Handout via Reuters

With President Donald Trump’s meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy set to kick off Monday afternoon at the White House, one Ukrainian official is predicting summit will go much like the two’s previous meetup back in February, which many critics labeled as a disaster due to Trump’s antagonistic and confrontational demeanor.

“We should expect another cluster---- meeting in the Oval Office,” a Ukrainian official told Politico Monday, speaking on the condition of anonymity.

The meeting is the second Trump summit regarding the president’s efforts to end the war in Ukraine, coming just days after his high-stakes meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in Alaska on Friday. Trump’s “affinities with Russia” and “admiration for Putin” has sparked fear among European leaders, who worry that the president will cave to some of Putin’s demands in negotiations.

Heading into today’s meeting, Trump has placed the onus on Zelenskyy to end the war by agreeing to Putin’s terms, which include giving up a sizable share of Ukrainian land, according to two European government officials speaking with The New York Times.

Other Ukrainian officials told Politico that Zelenskyy would be open to several "serious concessions,” though ceded Ukrainian territory that had yet to be occupied by Russia was not one of them.

Rather, the officials told Politico that they believed Zelenskyy would be open to “freezing the war where the battle lines currently stand,” but no more.

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Zelenskyy Summit: A Predictable Fiasco?

The impending summit between President Donald Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, following their previous disastrous encounter, is already setting off alarms. An anonymous Ukrainian official's prediction of it being another "cluster---- meeting" does not bode well for diplomatic progress, particularly in the sensitive context of ending the war in Ukraine. This prediction, steeped in the bitterness of past experiences, sets a grim tone for the anticipated discussion.

Trump's Allegiance: A Cause for European Alarm

Trump's well-documented "affinities with Russia" and his "admiration for Putin" have rightly alarmed European leaders. These sentiments are not just problematic; they're dangerous. The fear that Trump might yield to Putin's demands in negotiations is not unfounded but a reflection of a disturbing pattern in Trump's approach to foreign policy. The notion that he has placed the responsibility on Zelenskyy to end the war by capitulating to Putin's egregious demands, including surrendering substantial Ukrainian territory, is alarming. It's not just a failure of diplomacy; it's a potential betrayal of Ukraine's sovereignty and a stark illustration of Trump's misguided foreign policy priorities.

The Ukrainian Stance: Between a Rock and a Hard Place

Ukrainian officials' hinted willingness to make "serious concessions" speaks volumes about the desperate situation Ukraine finds itself in, sandwiched between an aggressive Russia and a U.S. president whose loyalties seem misplaced. The proposal to "freeze the war where the battle lines currently stand" may seem pragmatic, but it's a concession that comes with its own set of problems, potentially legitimizing territorial gains made by force.

Analysis: A Summit Doomed from the Start?

This summit, then, appears less an opportunity for meaningful progress and more a stage for Trump to further demonstrate a foreign policy approach that is both confrontational and, paradoxically, submissive to Russian interests. The expectation of another "cluster---- meeting" suggests that little has changed since the last Trump-Zelenskyy encounter, with the U.S. president seemingly unwilling or unable to play a constructive role in resolving the Ukraine conflict.

The implications of Trump's stance are dire, not just for Ukraine, but for the principles of sovereignty and territorial integrity. If Trump indeed presses Zelenskyy to agree to Putin's terms, it would represent a significant setback for international norms and a victory for autocratic aggression.

In conclusion, the article paints a bleak picture of the upcoming summit, driven by Trump's problematic affinities and a clear disregard for the complexities of the Ukrainian crisis. The anticipation of failure is not just a reflection of pessimism but a realistic expectation based on a history of misguided diplomacy. As the summit approaches, one can only hope for a deviation from this expected trajectory, though based on the evidence at hand, such hope appears slim.
