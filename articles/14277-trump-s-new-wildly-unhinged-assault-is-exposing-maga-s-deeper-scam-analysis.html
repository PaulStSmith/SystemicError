President Donald Trump’s assault on Harvard “is a wildly unhinged abuse of power in every way," according to The New Republic’s writer Greg Sargent.

Railing against the Trump administration's move to revoke Harvard’s ability to accept international students because of a lack of information about the students, Sargent wrote, “That demand was itself absurdly intrusive, and seemed designed not to be met, creating a pretext for Trump to broaden his attack.”

A federal judge later blocked Trump’s move. “At this point, there’s no need to pretend there’s a genuine public-interest rationale at work here,” Sargent said. “Everyone knows it’s all about getting universities to surrender to flatter Trump, or about executing a broader hostile MAGA takeover of liberal institutions.”

Sargent, who also is host of The Daily Blast podcast, added, “There’s something particularly ghoulish about Trump’s suggestion that his blockade on international students is about helping American kids who are unfairly displaced by them. That’s because the ‘big, beautiful bill’ that House Republicans passed last week, which Trump has urged Senate Republicans to adopt, could make attending college harder for countless such kids.”

Referencing a piece in The New Republic by Monica Potts, Sargent claims the bill “Would make it harder for full-time students to qualify for Pell Grants, bump off large numbers of part-time students, and restrict access to the program and other financial assistance for higher education in numerous other ways.”

Trump’s administration is attempting to propel many people into trade schools; however, Sargent believes this is “laudable” because supporting trade schools has “been a mainstay of Democratic proposals—sometimes with GOP support—for years.”

Sargent believes this points to a “deeper scam.” He said, “We are often told Trump’s success is fueled by a backlash against the failures of meritocracy. Meritocratic elites, in this story, game our system to help their own, destroying broader bonds of social trust.”

He added, “Trump is the tribune of those who have been left behind by those self-dealing elites. This gives Trump’s attacks on elite institutions like Harvard enduring political appeal.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's War on Education: A Closer Look

In a recent escalation of President Donald Trump's contentious relationship with higher education, Greg Sargent of The New Republic delivers a blistering critique of Trump's attempt to revoke Harvard's ability to accept international students, labeling it a "wildly unhinged abuse of power". This move, however, is merely the tip of the iceberg in what appears to be a broader offensive against the academic institutions that dare to defy him.

Sargent's analysis reveals the Trump administration's demands for information on international students as absurdly intrusive, designed to be impossible to satisfy and thus serving as a pretext for a wider assault on liberal institutions. This is not just a bureaucratic overreach; it's a calculated attack under the guise of national interest, which a federal judge rightly blocked.

The Real Motive: Political Retribution and Control

Sargent cuts through the administration's thin veil of public interest, exposing the true motives behind these actions: to compel universities to bend to Trump's will and to further a MAGA-driven agenda aimed at reshaping the landscape of American education. This strategy is part of a larger pattern of targeting institutions that Trump perceives as oppositional, leveraging federal power to coerce compliance or silence dissent.

Exploiting American Students: A Twisted Justification

Trump's justification for blocking international students, ostensibly to protect American students from being "unfairly displaced," is especially cynical when juxtaposed with legislative efforts that would, in fact, make higher education less accessible to those very students. The so-called "big, beautiful bill" that Trump champions would severely restrict access to Pell Grants and other financial aid, disproportionately affecting full-time and part-time students alike. This is not just hypocritical; it's a direct assault on the educational aspirations of countless Americans.

The Deeper Scam: A Misguided Meritocracy

Sargent doesn't stop at critiquing the immediate implications of Trump's policies; he delves into the deeper scam at play. By positioning himself as the champion of those "left behind" by a flawed meritocracy, Trump has crafted a narrative of populist revolt against elitist institutions. However, Sargent astutely observes that this narrative is a smokescreen for policies that ultimately serve to entrench inequalities rather than dismantle them. The attack on Harvard and the broader educational system is not about correcting the failures of meritocracy but about consolidating power and undermining the foundations of a critical and free-thinking society.

A Laudable Idea Tainted by Political Machinations

Interestingly, Sargent acknowledges the merit in supporting trade schools—an idea that has enjoyed bipartisan support and could genuinely benefit the American workforce. Yet, in Trump's hands, even this commendable goal is tainted by the broader agenda to devalue traditional higher education and impose a singular vision of what American education should look like.

Conclusion: The Battle for America's Educational Soul

Greg Sargent's analysis is a scathing indictment of Trump's attempts to manipulate the educational system for political gain. It's a reminder that the battle over policies like these is not just about international students or financial aid but about the very soul of American education. In Trump's America, institutions of higher learning are under siege, forced into a corner where they must fight to preserve their independence, integrity, and the right to think critically and freely. This is not just a policy debate; it's a defining moment for the country's future.
