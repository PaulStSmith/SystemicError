A Donald Trump insider has been revealed to have been in “regular contact” with the late child sex abuser Jeffrey Epstein, including in one email that says “miss u,” according to the latest DOJ release.

CBS News reported on the Epstein files release on Saturday in an article called, “Trump insider Tom Barrack kept in regular contact with Jeffrey Epstein for years, files show.” Barrack is also an administration ambassador to Turkey.

“President Trump’s longtime confidant Thomas Barrack, now serving as U.S. ambassador to Turkey and special envoy to Syria, was in regular, close contact with Jeffrey Epstein for years after Epstein’s 2008 conviction for soliciting a minor, a CBS News analysis of over 100 texts and email exchanges from the newly released Justice Department documents shows,” according to CBS.

The outlet further reported, “The correspondence places Barrack, a globe-trotting billionaire, among a circle of wealthy and influential figures who maintained social contact with Epstein even as his criminal history became widely known. Their relationship continued even after Barrack became a prolific fundraiser for Mr. Trump’s 2016 campaign, and later, led his inaugural committee and became a frequent presence in the White House.”

The article states Epstein may have thought he could use Barrack to get to Trump.

“At times, Epstein appeared to regard Barrack as a potential conduit for passing information to Mr. Trump, though available records show the communication going only in one direction. There’s no indication Epstein’s communications were passed on to Mr. Trump or that Mr. Trump communicated to Epstein through Barrack,” it reports. “There’s also no evidence to suggest Barrack participated in or had knowledge of any ongoing criminal conduct by Epstein. Instead, the messages document continuing personal and professional contact during a period when Epstein was attempting to reestablish himself within elite social and financial circles.”

While “the exchanges include invitations to private residences, introductions to diplomats and investors, and discussions of business opportunities,” one particular email stood out to conservative political analyst Bill Kristol:

“In August 2011, Barrack emailed Epstein and said ‘just saw Sultan who likes you a lot! I told him you are the best that you are a first class brain inside of a first class human being! Miss u.’”

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Dance of Power and Influence: A Closer Look at the Epstein-Barrack Correspondence

In the latest revelation that feels like a sinister echo from a past we can’t seem to shake, CBS News reports on the intimate and ongoing relationship between Thomas Barrack, a Donald Trump insider, and the late Jeffrey Epstein. This connection isn’t just a fleeting acquaintance but a sustained dialogue spanning years, punctuated by an email from Barrack to Epstein saying “miss u.” This isn’t just troubling; it’s a stark illustration of how the rich and powerful operate in a parallel moral universe.

The Uncomfortable Truth

Let’s dissect the core of the issue. Thomas Barrack, not just a Trump confidant but also a U.S. ambassador to Turkey and special envoy to Syria, maintained regular contact with Epstein post his 2008 conviction for soliciting a minor. This relationship wasn’t casual. It was deep, involved, and persisted even as Barrack ascended the echelons of political power, becoming a key figure in Trump’s 2016 campaign and later, a White House regular.

The Circle of Influence

The CBS report paints a picture of a world where connections trump morality. Barrack and Epstein’s exchanges weren’t just friendly banter; they were gateways to potentially influential introductions and business opportunities. This wasn’t just two friends catching up; it was a networking operation, with Epstein possibly viewing Barrack as a conduit to Trump, albeit with no evidence suggesting a direct line to the then-president.

A Disturbing Complacency

What’s perhaps most alarming about this revelation is the nonchalant continuation of a relationship with a convicted sex offender, aiming to reintegrate into the very circles that should have ostracized him. The communications reveal a disturbing complacency, a willingness to overlook Epstein’s criminal past for the sake of maintaining beneficial connections.

The Missing Accountability

While the article points out that there’s no evidence of Barrack participating in or having knowledge of Epstein’s ongoing criminal activities, it does raise questions about the moral responsibility of those in power. The fact that Barrack’s interactions with Epstein continued unfazed, even flourished, post-conviction is a damning commentary on the ethical blind spots of the elite.

The Lingering Questions

Bill Kristol’s highlighting of the email where Barrack expressed missing Epstein isn’t just a curiosity; it’s a window into the personal rapport between these figures. It begs the question: How deep did these connections go, and what did they facilitate? While the report clarifies that there’s no direct link to Trump or evidence of Barrack aiding Epstein’s illegal endeavors, the very existence of this relationship is a troubling testament to the values, or lack thereof, in these circles of influence.

In summary, the Epstein-Barrack correspondence isn’t merely a scandalous revelation; it’s a symptomatic glimpse into the systemic rot within the corridors of power. It underscores a world where the wealthy and influential operate with impunity, shielded by their status, and connected by a web of relationships that seems impervious to moral and legal standards. As these stories unfold, they reveal not just individual failings but a systemic error in how power, influence, and morality intersect in our society.
