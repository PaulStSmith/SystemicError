Religion forecast has Christian conservatives panicking: 'It's the babies'

Based on an article by Roxanne Cooper

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A demographic forecast becomes an immigration weaponThe answer is birth rates, not conquestChristianity is growing, just not where reactionaries want

Pew Research Center’s forecast does not describe a Muslim “takeover.” It describes two measurable demographic forces: faster secularization in Western countries and higher birth rates in Muslim populations. Yet conservative politicians and commentators responded by blaming immigration, casting Muslim growth as a civilizational threat, and urging Christians to produce more children. The panic reveals less about Christianity’s global prospects—which remain robust—than about a political movement that treats religious dominance as an entitlement.

Pew projects that Muslims will number 2.57 billion worldwide by 2035, slightly exceeding the projected 2.52 billion Christians. That crossover would arrive 15 years earlier than previously estimated. In the United States, Christians are projected to fall from 62 percent of adults to below half by 2065. Europe’s Christian population could contract by one-third by 2070.

Those figures prompted what Gina Zurlo, a Harvard Divinity School researcher specializing in religious demography, described to the Associated Press as growing “angst.” The old “clash of civilizations” theory, she said, continues to shape how some communities interpret Islam’s growth. Pew religion research director Alan Cooperman warned against presenting the forecast as a zero-sum competition between Muslims and Christians. Much of the political right immediately did exactly that.

A demographic forecast becomes an immigration weapon

On the day Pew released its report, Republican Rep. Keith Self of Texas asked the Department of Homeland Security to investigate three Muslim leaders in his area over alleged Muslim Brotherhood ties. Self explicitly connected the demographic projections to what he called “unfettered mass immigration.”

That linkage supplied a politically useful culprit, but it did not explain Pew’s findings. Demographers cited in the reporting attributed the projected religious crossover primarily to birth rates and Western secularization—not mass conversion and not immigration. Self nevertheless used a global population forecast to intensify suspicion of identifiable Muslim leaders at home.

The institutional imbalance is plain. Muslims were discussed as an expanding menace, while a member of Congress called upon a federal security agency to scrutinize Muslim figures over alleged organizational connections. This was not merely online anxiety. It was an elected official attempting to translate demographic fear into state attention directed at members of a religious minority.

Middle East Eye reported that other conservative figures and organizations used the projections to promote tighter immigration restrictions, encourage Christians to have more children, and warn that American culture and heritage faced an imminent threat. American Greatness emphasized projected Christian losses in North America. Human rights lawyer Qasim Rashid catalogued far-right pundits treating the forecast as a civilizational defeat. On X, Facebook, and Reddit, some self-described Christians dismissed Pew’s work as propaganda or fake news, while others demanded missionary mobilization against a supposed cultural takeover.

The responses were contradictory but politically coherent: reject the figures as fraudulent while using them to demand coercive policy. The data mattered only insofar as it could furnish a new rationale for an established project—restricting immigration, stigmatizing Muslims, and defining American identity through Christian numerical control.

The answer is birth rates, not conquest

Pew senior demographer Conrad Hackett gave the Associated Press the blunt explanation: “It’s the babies.” Between 2020 and 2025, Muslim women had an average fertility rate of 3.1 children, compared with 2.5 among Christian women. Population age and fertility can change the relative size of religions without anyone invading, converting, or conspiring.

Religious switching also matters, particularly in Western countries. Christianity is projected to lose nearly 105 million people worldwide to religiously unaffiliated status between 2020 and 2070. Islam’s projected net loss from switching is 7.7 million. Religion News Service reported that church affiliation declined faster than expected in the United States and United Kingdom, moving Pew’s projected crossover forward by more than a decade.

That is the part of the story the immigration panic obscures. In North America and Europe, Christianity’s demographic position is weakening substantially because people are leaving churches or declining to affiliate with them. Blaming Muslim immigrants redirects attention from institutions that have failed to retain their own adherents. It also converts an internal crisis of religious credibility into an external enemy.

The proposed remedy—Christians should have more children to preserve demographic supremacy—reduces family life to political arithmetic. Women become instruments in a competition for cultural control, and children become future entries in a religious head count. The language may invoke faith, but the objective is power: maintaining a majority and the privileges presumed to accompany it.

Christianity is growing, just not where reactionaries want

The forecast does not show Christianity disappearing worldwide. Hackett said its global population is expected to grow by 30 percent, reaching roughly 3 billion adherents by 2070. Much of that growth will occur in sub-Saharan Africa.

The panic therefore depends on a selective definition of Christian success. Christianity can add hundreds of millions of followers and still be narrated as losing if its numerical center moves away from white-majority countries in Europe and North America. The problem, for those sounding the alarm, is not simply fewer Christians. It is declining Western ownership of Christianity and the prospect that Muslim populations may become larger without remaining politically subordinate.

Pew’s forecast is a projection, not a declaration of victory by one faith over another. Conservative actors supplied the conquest narrative themselves. They took fertility, disaffiliation, and geographic change and recast them as an immigration emergency requiring surveillance and exclusion. That misdirection protects Christian institutions from scrutiny while placing Muslims under it—and Rep. Self’s appeal to Homeland Security shows how quickly demographic resentment can acquire the machinery of the state.
