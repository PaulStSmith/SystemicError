GOP is making Susan Collins miserable — and risks losing Senate control: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 17, 20254 min read

Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) speaks during a Senate Appropriations Subcommittee on Commerce, Justice, Science, and Related Agencies hearing at the Dirksen Senate Office building in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 9, 2021. Stefani Reynolds/Pool via REUTERS

Despite being firmly ensconced as chair of the powerful Senate Appropriations Committee, Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) is reportedly unhappy that the GOP leadership is blowing off her concerns about the recently passed massive budget bill.

According to a report from Politico's Jordain Carney, Jennifer Scholtes and Katherine Tully-McManus, Republicans are playing with fire by not taking the Maine Republican seriously — and that could cost them a seat in the Senate if she retires in frustration.

Although Collins has indicated she plans to run for re-election, there are growing concerns that, should she change her mind, that seat will likely go to a Democrat.

According to Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), who will be stepping down from his seat in 2026 after futilely battling with President Donald Trump, "The one thing I am certain of is if Susan Collins is not running for re-election, then that state is even more at risk than North Carolina."

According to the Politico report, "Democrats have a steep road back to the majority, needing to flip an unlikely four seats while also holding onto their own swing seats in Georgia and Michigan. But they view Maine as a top pick-up opportunity, and they would unquestionably have an easier time without Collins on the ballot, potentially allowing them to pour more resources into tougher races."

Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) echoed those comments, adding, “She represents … a very blue state. She has to run for reelection this year. She’s the only Republican that can win. And so, you know, she sees the world through a different lens, and she’s always very upfront about what she’s going to do.”

The report notes that Democrats believe that Collins — increasingly "out-of-step" with her party — may decide it is not worth it after the GOP-controlled Senate passed the budget bill late Tuesday, over her objections, with Vice President J.D. Vance casting the tie-breaking vote.

The report also notes that Trump could play a wild card when it comes to her electability, with one GOP lawmaker joking, "He’s very irritated at Susan — very, I can tell. But she doesn’t care, because the more Trump gets irritated with her, the better it is for her politics back home.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Collins Conundrum: Analysis of the Politico Report

In a political landscape that increasingly resembles a high-stakes game of chicken, Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) finds herself at a peculiar crossroads. The Politico report paints a vivid picture of internal strife and potential electoral consequences that could ripple through the Republican Party. Let's dissect the core elements of this unfolding drama.

The Crux of Collins' Discontent

At the heart of this political tempest is Collins' reported dissatisfaction with how GOP leadership has disregarded her concerns regarding the massive budget bill recently passed. This isn't just a minor spat within the party; it's emblematic of a deeper rift. Collins, with her pivotal role as chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, is not someone whose concerns can be easily dismissed without consequence. Yet, that appears to be exactly what's happening.

The Electoral Domino Effect

The stakes of this internal conflict are high, not just for Collins but for the GOP's control in the Senate. The report suggests that Collins, despite her current plans to run for re-election, might reconsider if her frustrations continue to be sidelined. This isn't mere speculation; the implications are grounded in electoral reality. Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) is quoted expressing a concern that mirrors what many Republicans might be feeling: without Collins, Maine could easily swing to the Democrats, amplifying the GOP's challenges in maintaining Senate control.

The Democratic Perspective

From the Democrats' viewpoint, Maine represents a golden opportunity, a sentiment underscored by the Politico report. The possibility of Collins stepping down would not only make Maine more attainable but could also allow Democrats to redirect resources to other battleground states. This scenario highlights the precarious balance of power in the Senate and how individual decisions can have far-reaching impacts on national politics.

Trump: The Wild Card

The report touches on an intriguing aspect of Collins' political saga: her relationship with Donald Trump. It's no secret that Trump's influence within the GOP can sway electoral fortunes. The notion that Trump's irritation might actually bolster Collins' standing with her constituents back home is a fascinating twist. It suggests that, in certain contexts, Trump's endorsements or criticisms can have a counterintuitive effect, complicating the GOP's internal dynamics even further.

Conclusion: A Brewing Storm

The Politico report sheds light on a brewing storm within the Republican Party, centered around Sen. Susan Collins. The GOP's apparent willingness to sideline one of its own, especially one as influential as Collins, speaks volumes about the party's current priorities and internal divisions. As the article suggests, this could lead to significant electoral repercussions, particularly in a state as pivotal as Maine.

The GOP's strategy—or lack thereof—regarding Collins and the broader implications of internal dissent, raises critical questions about party unity and electoral viability. As Collins navigates these turbulent waters, her decisions could very well reshape the GOP's fortunes, for better or worse. The party would do well to remember the old adage: ignore your allies at your peril.
