Red state Republicans increasingly vulnerable thanks to 'scandal and weak fundraising'

By Paulo Santos

Democrats remain bullish about the chances for major gains in the 2026 midterms, and according to a report from NOTUS, two seats in deep-red states are now viewed as vulnerable due to “weak fundraising and scandals.”

Cory Mills is a Republican congressman representing Florida’s 7th District, where he won reelection in 2024 by 13 points, and Donald Trump bested Kamala Harris by the same margin. Despite that showing, Democrats are emboldened about their chances to unseat him this year due to “allegations of stolen valor, domestic violence and shady business dealings this campaign cycle.”

Mills’ 2003 receipt of a Bronze Star for delivering “life-saving” aid to fellow soldiers under fire in Iraq has been brought into question, as five individuals who served with him said that they did not remember him being on the scene. Last February, officials with the Metropolitan Police Department in Washington, D.C., confirmed to NOTUS that Mills was under investigation for simple assault, though the congressman denied any wrongdoing.

According NOTUS’s new report, “those issues appear to have deteriorated his campaign’s ability to bring in cash.”

“Last fall, between October and December, Mills raised a mere $61,000 — incredibly low numbers from a candidate who had never struggled to raise well over $100,000, often more than $200,000, every quarter since his election in 2022,” the outlet detailed. “In that same time period, Bale Dalton, his Democratic challenger, a Navy veteran and former NASA chief of staff, raised more than $343,000.”

Dalton is campaigning against Mills on a platform based on affordability, a message that led Democrats to resounding successes in elections elsewhere last year.

“My message is: Life is too expensive, and Cory Mills is too corrupt to fix it,” Dalton told the outlet in a prior interview.

Though not dogged by scandal like Mills, Democrats see a similar opening against. Rep. Andy Ogles, a Republican representing Tennessee’s 5th District. Ogles was reelected in 2024 by a margin of 17 points in 2024, while Trump carried the district over Harris by 18. Despite that, he has suffered a severe fundraising deficit against his Democratic challenger, Chaz Molder, the mayor of Columbia, Tennessee.

“Although the district also historically votes Republican, Molder raised well over $1 million in the last two quarters of 2025, compared to Ogles’ $209,000 in that same period, as the congressman attempts to fend off an investigation into alleged campaign finance violations,” NOTUS reported.

Mills and Ogles are still generally favored to win reelection later this year, but these issues led the Cook Political Report to downgrade their odds from “solid” to “likely.”

Our Analysis:

Democratic Opportunism Amidst Republican Scandals: A Closer Look

In a political landscape that often feels more like a battlefield littered with scandals and accusations than a forum for public service, the Democrats’ bullish stance towards the 2026 midterms, as reported by NOTUS, presents a fascinating case study in opportunism and, perhaps, a glimmer of hope for change. But let’s not get ahead of ourselves. The thought of flipping seats in deep-red states might feel like a political fever dream, but it’s grounded in the tangible weaknesses of the incumbent Republicans: Cory Mills in Florida and Andy Ogles in Tennessee.

The Cory Mills Saga: A Textbook Case of Political Vulnerability

Cory Mills, a Republican congressman, seems to be embroiled in controversies that read like a thriller novel - stolen valor, domestic violence, and shady business dealings. These are heavy accusations, ones that not only tarnish the personal reputation of Mills but, more critically, erode the trust between him and his constituents. The allegations surrounding his Bronze Star, a recognition meant to spotlight bravery and service, being called into question by his fellow soldiers is particularly damning. It’s not just a matter of public perception; it’s a direct challenge to the very narrative that likely helped him secure his seat.

And then there’s the money trail - or, more accurately, the lack thereof. Mills’ fundraising efforts, as described by NOTUS, have been abysmal. Dropping from over $100,000 per quarter to a “mere $61,000” is not just a red flag; it’s a glaring neon sign of potential downfall. In contrast, his Democratic challenger, Bale Dalton, a figure with a commendable background in the Navy and at NASA, has outpaced Mills significantly, with a war chest exceeding $343,000 in the same period. This financial disparity is critical, as it not only reflects Dalton’s appeal but also suggests a shifting sentiment among the electorate. Dalton’s message, focusing on affordability and painting Mills as too corrupt to address it, is both strategic and scathing. It’s a narrative that, given the context, could resonate well with voters tired of scandal-ridden representatives.

Andy Ogles: Another Republican in the Hot Seat

Ogles’ situation mirrors that of Mills, albeit with less sensationalism. His fundraising struggles are a stark indicator of vulnerability, especially when juxtaposed with his Democratic challenger, Chaz Molder’s, impressive $1 million haul. Campaign finance violations, the specter haunting Ogles, may not carry the same weight as allegations of stolen valor or domestic violence, but they are serious nonetheless. They feed into a narrative of incompetence and, potentially, corruption.

The Bigger Picture

While both Mills and Ogles are still favored to win, the downgrade of their odds from “solid” to “likely” by the Cook Political Report is not to be understated. In the volatile world of politics, such shifts can be harbingers of change, or at least of a more competitive race than previously anticipated.

However, let’s temper our optimism with realism. The Democratic Party’s strategy, while seemingly effective in capitalizing on Republican vulnerabilities, must not solely rely on the missteps of their opponents. The risk of focusing too much on the scandals of Mills and Ogles is that it can overshadow the substantive issues at stake. Voters need to believe in a positive vision for the future, not just a lesser of two evils.

Conclusion

The unfolding drama ahead of the 2026 midterms is a reminder of the ever-present dance between scandal and opportunity in American politics. For the Democrats targeting seats in deep-red states, the path to victory is lined with the pitfalls of their opponents. But victory, if it comes, should be seen not just as a win against flawed candidates but as a mandate to address the pressing issues facing their constituents - affordability, integrity, and accountability. Let’s watch closely as this narrative unfolds, but let’s also demand more than just a successful exploitation of scandal. The electorate deserves it.
