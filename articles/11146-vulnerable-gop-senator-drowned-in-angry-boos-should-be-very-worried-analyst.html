Vulnerable GOP senator drowned in angry boos should be 'very worried': analyst

U.S. Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) speaks on behalf of one of U.S. President Donald Trump’s judicial nominees during a Senate Judiciary Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 30, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

For many years, Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) was among the most popular Republicans in New England. First elected to the U.S. Senate in 1996, Collins was reelected by 7 percent in 2002, 23 percent in 2008, and 37 percent in 2014. Conservative-leaning but not far-right, Collins attracted a lot of swing voters, moderates, independents and Democrats.

But her image suffered when she voted to confirm now-Justice Brett Kavanaugh to the U.S. Supreme Court in 2018 despite warnings that doing so would help to doom Roe v. Wade — which is exactly what happened when the High Court voted to overturn Roe in 2022 and Kavanaugh was among the "yes" votes. Collins was reelected by 8 percent in 2020, which was a lot less than her 37 percent victory six years earlier.

Collins was reminded how much her image had suffered when she was booed during a ribbon-cutting ceremony in Maine on Tuesday, August 26.

The New Republic's Malcolm Ferguson, in an article published the following day, reports, "This was Collins' first public, press conference-style event in her home state in nearly a decade. Video shows the room erupting in boos as she approached the front of the room to cut the ribbon for a new Main Street in Seaport, Maine. The boos eventually turned into chants of 'Shame! Shame! Shame!' Collins smiled and cut the ribbon as if her angry constituents weren't even there. The crowd continued to shout her down."

Collins voted "no" on President Donald Trump's "big, beautiful bill," a piece of legislation that some of the attendees were angry about. But other people in the crowd were still angry about her "yes" vote on Kavanaugh.

One of them shouted, "Your votes destroyed our Supreme Court."

"This all comes as progressive populist Democrat Graham Platner announced his bid to unseat Collins last week in a now viral video," Ferguson explains. "His candidacy serves as a foil to Collins on almost every issue, and Maine residents are starting to notice."

Collins is running for reelection in 2026 and is seeking a sixth term. 2026 will be her second reelection campaign since voting "yes" on Kavanaugh, but only her first since the High Court overturned Roe in its unpopular Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization ruling.

"Collins' chances of winning have been precarious for some time now," Ferguson observes. "Her dedication to a spineless centrist conservatism has frustrated Maine voters, especially in a state won by Kamala Harris in the 2024 general election. That, combined with Collins' icy reception at her own event, and Platner's current surge, should make the longtime senator very worried."

The Fall of Susan Collins: A Study in Political Miscalculation

For years, Sen. Susan Collins (R-Maine) floated above the tumultuous seas of American politics with the grace of a seasoned navigator. Her journey, marked by significant victories and wide margins, seemed to be guided by an unerring compass towards the hearts of New England’s electorate. Yet, in what can only be described as a catastrophic miscalculation, Collins' ship hit the iceberg named Brett Kavanaugh. The fallout from this decision has been nothing short of a Shakespearean tragedy, unraveling the senator's carefully curated image as a moderate Republican.

The Brett Kavanaugh Debacle

In 2018, Collins cast a pivotal vote confirming Brett Kavanaugh to the U.S. Supreme Court, a move that was met with immediate backlash. Critics warned that Kavanaugh's confirmation would lead to the overturning of Roe v. Wade, a prediction that tragically came to fruition in 2022. This moment was not just a blip in Collins' political career but a seismic shift that realigned her public perception from a centrist maverick to an enabler of the far-right's agenda.

The repercussions of this decision were palpable in Collins' 2020 reelection campaign. Her victory margin plummeted from a staggering 37 percent in 2014 to a mere 8 percent. This stark decline is a testament to the deep disillusionment among her constituents, a sentiment that was vividly displayed during a ribbon-cutting ceremony in Maine. The event, which marked Collins' first public appearance in her home state in nearly a decade, descended into chaos as the crowd erupted in boos and chants of "Shame! Shame! Shame!"

The Icy Reception at Home

This moment of public rebuke is emblematic of Collins' eroding support base. The article describes how Collins attempted to navigate the uproar with a smile, a gesture that reeks of political tone-deafness. Her constituents' anger, stemming not just from the Kavanaugh vote but also from her broader legislative decisions, underscores a growing disconnect between the senator and the people she is supposed to represent.

The presence of Graham Platner, a progressive populist Democrat, in the political arena adds a new layer of urgency to Collins' predicament. Platner's viral candidacy announcement has resonated with Maine residents, signaling a palpable shift in the political winds. As Collins eyes a sixth term in 2026, she finds herself at a crossroads, with her past decisions casting long shadows over her political future.

A Centrist's Dilemma

Collins' brand of “spineless centrist conservatism,” as Ferguson aptly puts it, has left her in a precarious position. The senator's attempt to straddle the line between conservative principles and moderate stances has ultimately satisfied no one. In a state that swung for Kamala Harris in the 2024 general election, Collins’ icy reception and the rise of a charismatic challenger should indeed be a source of worry for her campaign.

Conclusion

The tale of Susan Collins is a cautionary one, highlighting the risks inherent in political miscalculations. Her story reflects a broader narrative about the limitations of centrist conservatism in an era marked by polarizing figures and ideologies. As Collins prepares for her 2026 reelection campaign, one can't help but wonder whether she can navigate her way back into the favor of Maine's voters, or if her ship has already sailed. The chorus of boos that greeted her in Seaport, Maine, may very well be the harbinger of a political career on the brink.
