Drowning in Hypocrisy: How Texas Politicians Defund Science and Blame the Forecast

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In the aftermath of yet another climate-related disaster, Texas officials are once again pointing fingers at the National Weather Service for "failing" to predict the rain that caused massive flooding. It's a tragically familiar pattern: Republican lawmakers, who have held a stranglehold on Texas politics for decades, routinely undermine scientific institutions—and then demand miracles from the very agencies they’ve starved of funding.

This isn’t just a matter of political posturing. It’s a systemic problem that gets people killed.

The National Weather Service (NWS), a division of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), is tasked with forecasting and warning the public about severe weather. They rely on a vast network of satellites, radars, supercomputers, and trained meteorologists to do this job. And yet, year after year, Texas lawmakers vote to slash NOAA’s budget in Congress. They do this under the banner of "fiscal conservatism," but the consequences are anything but conservative.

February 2021: The Disaster That Should Have Been a Wake-Up Call

Remember Winter Storm Uri? The 2021 deep freeze that left over 200 Texans dead, millions without power, and the state's infrastructure in ruins? It was a catastrophe not just of nature but of negligence. ERCOT, the agency that manages Texas’s uniquely deregulated electric grid, had ignored repeated warnings to winterize its infrastructure. The grid was intentionally kept isolated from the rest of the nation’s power systems in a libertarian fever dream to avoid federal oversight.

When the grid failed and the suffering became undeniable, Texas officials once again looked for scapegoats. Wind turbines were blamed. Green energy was demonized. The "Green New Deal," which isn’t even law, was bizarrely held responsible.

And, of course, Democrats were blamed. Despite the fact that Republicans have controlled every branch of Texas government since the 1990s.

Science Can’t Function on Wishes and Lies

Here’s the truth: you don’t get a top-tier weather forecasting system by gutting science agencies and ignoring expert warnings. NOAA has faced constant budget cuts, hiring freezes, and political interference. When you cut funding for satellite upgrades, delay the replacement of aging radar, and leave meteorologist positions unfilled, you're gambling with lives.

Weather forecasting is data-intensive. Accurate models require computing power, global monitoring, and skilled interpretation. When politicians defund these capabilities, they are deliberately blinding the nation’s eyes in the face of oncoming storms. But when floods destroy homes or a blizzard causes blackouts, those same politicians show up on TV, demanding to know why the warnings came too late.

It’s not just disingenuous. It’s lethal.

The GOP's Political Theater of Blame

How does the GOP get away with this? Partly because they’ve mastered the art of blame deflection. Fox News and right-wing media churn out narratives that always cast the GOP as the saviors fighting against some liberal boogeyman. The science community, coastal elites, immigrants, LGBTQ+ people, or the mythical "woke agenda" are easier targets than Republican-led deregulation.

And partly because voters have been conditioned to value identity over evidence. Many Texans vote not on policy outcomes, but on party branding. The GOP sells rugged individualism, anti-federal rhetoric, and cultural nostalgia. Even when those very policies cause harm, the marketing machine turns the story into another culture war skirmish.

Gerrymandering and the Death of Accountability

The GOP's grip on Texas is not just cultural; it's structural. Through gerrymandering and targeted voter suppression, the Texas legislature has created electoral maps that all but guarantee Republican dominance, even when public opinion starts to shift. Entire communities are carved up and diluted to maintain the illusion of a mandate.

So, even as climate disasters become more frequent and deadly, there is no mechanism within the state to hold the ruling party accountable. Elections are won before the first vote is cast. And while people die in floods, freeze in their homes, or suffer from drought-induced power outages, GOP leaders smile for photo ops and spin the narrative.

The Cost of Manufactured Ignorance

This isn’t just about Texas. It’s a national cautionary tale.

When you let ideology override evidence, and propaganda replace public policy, you end up with a state where billionaires pay no taxes while public services collapse. Where the government that promises to "get out of the way" ends up standing between people and survival. Where officials ask, "Why didn’t the forecast warn us?" while holding the knife that cut the agency's funding.

Climate change is accelerating. Weather patterns are growing more extreme. We need more science, more funding, more infrastructure resilience. What we don’t need is another performative press conference blaming the very agencies being systematically dismantled by the people doing the blaming.

If Texas wants better forecasts, safer infrastructure, and fewer preventable deaths, there’s a simple solution:

Stop voting for people who hate government, then act shocked when the government doesn’t work.

Until then, Texans aren’t just braving storms. They’re drowning in hypocrisy.
