MAGA may turn 'violent' if Trump can't pull off his next 'trick': biographer

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump wears a ‘Make America Great Again’ (MAGA) hat as he attends the commencement ceremony at West Point Military Academy in West Point, New York, U.S., May 24, 2025. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

One of President Donald Trump's biographers warned on Saturday that the president's MAGA base could become violent if Trump is implicated in the Jeffrey Epstein scandal.

Michael Wolff, who has authored four books on Trump, made the comments on The Daily Beast's eponymous podcast. The comments come at a time when the Trump administration is seeking a way to control the Epstein narrative, which has consumed the presidency for more than two weeks.

"Michael, if it turns out that Ghislaine Maxwell does have compromising material on the president and Todd Blance advises Donald Trump to give her a pardon or commute her sentence in some way, what is the MAGA base reaction?" host Joanna Coles, The Daily Beast's chief content officer, asked Wolff.

"Violent," the author said solemnly. "That's going to be a major issue. He has a choice whether to protect himself or deal with the base."

"Somehow he has to bring those two together," Wolff continued. "He has to get the base to realize that without him, there is no MAGA."

The MAGA base's response to the Epstein scandal has been fractured from the start. About half of the base wants Trump to release the files, as he promised to do on the campaign trail. The other half have taken to defending the president against claims of wrongdoing.

"This is a really complicated trick. Can he pull that off? I'm not sure," Wolff said.

The Epstein Scandal and Trump's MAGA Base: A Powder Keg Waiting to Ignite?

In a recent episode of The Daily Beast's eponymous podcast, a statement by one of President Donald Trump's biographers, Michael Wolff, has sent ripples through the political sphere. Wolff, having penned four books on Trump, does not mince words when it comes to forecasting the potential reaction of Trump’s MAGA base if the former president is implicated in the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. According to Wolff, the word is "Violent." This single word, heavy with ominous undertones, opens a Pandora's box of concerns regarding the volatile intersection of politics, personal loyalty, and public safety.

The Trump Administration's Epstein Narrative Quagmire

The article highlights the Trump administration's scramble to control the narrative surrounding the Epstein scandal, which has been a thorn in its side for over two weeks. This isn't merely a political inconvenience; it's a full-blown crisis that threatens to undermine Trump's image and authority. The administration's efforts to contain the fallout reveal a deep-seated fear of the potential implications—both legally and politically.

A Base Divided

Wolff's insights into the MAGA base's fractured response to the Epstein scandal underscore a significant predicament for Trump. On one hand, half of his base clamors for transparency and the fulfillment of Trump's campaign promise to release the files connected to Epstein. On the other, a fiercely loyal faction leaps to his defense, refusing to entertain any suggestion of wrongdoing. This divide not only highlights the complexity of the situation but also the almost cult-like dichotomy within the MAGA movement.

The Pardon Hypothetical: A Litmus Test for Loyalty

The discussion takes a particularly intriguing turn when the possibility of Trump pardoning Ghislaine Maxwell is broached. Wolff's prediction of violence as a response from the MAGA base to such an action brings to light the delicate balance Trump must maintain. This hypothetical scenario is emblematic of a broader dilemma: the conflict between self-preservation and the maintenance of a fervent, loyal base. Wolff rightly points out that Trump's challenge lies in reconciling these opposing imperatives. He must convince his base that their identity and cause are inextricably linked to his leadership—without him, there is no MAGA.

Analysis: Navigating a Minefield

Wolff's commentary provides a stark reminder of the volatility inherent in populist movements. The MAGA base, with its passionate and sometimes extreme devotion to Trump, represents a double-edged sword. While their unwavering support has bolstered Trump's political resilience, it also poses a significant risk of violence and unrest, should they feel betrayed or misled.

The article sheds light on a critical aspect of contemporary American politics: the intertwining of personal loyalty with political ideology. In this context, Trump's handling of the Epstein scandal is more than just a test of political acumen; it's a litmus test for the stability and unity of his base. The potential for violence, as Wolff warns, is not merely a hypothetical outcome but a stark reality that underscores the fragility of the political landscape.

In conclusion, the discussion on The Daily Beast's podcast with Michael Wolff illuminates the precarious position in which Trump and his administration find themselves. As they navigate the treacherous waters of the Epstein scandal, the reactions of the MAGA base loom large. Wolff’s analysis, though speculative, is grounded in a sobering reality: the potential for violence is real, and the stakes for Trump, both personally and politically, are astronomically high.
