'Unhinged!' New Trump DHS pick melted down at gym for 'bullying' her over MAGA hat

A MAGA Fox News commentator who was reportedly selected to join the Department of Homeland Security as a spokesperson was outraged just days ago after she claimed her gym bullied her for wearing a Trump hat.

Katie Zacharia, an adviser to Richard Grenell, the current interim head of the Trump-Kennedy Center and former U.S. Ambassador to Germany, has been hired by DHS following the announcement that top DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin was leaving the Trump administration, a source told Axios in a report published Tuesday. Zacharia will join the agency as deputy assistant secretary for the Office of Public Affairs.

Zacharia penned an opinion piece on Sunday for the California Post, the New York Post’s new West Coast operation, saying she was unfairly treated by people at her California kickboxing gym after wearing a particular hat and referred to “MAGA bullies.”

“But last week, my ‘Team Trump 2024’ hat — earned from hard work helping flip Michigan red in Dearborn — triggered an unhinged meltdown,” Zacharia claimed, who said one woman confronted her in a workout class and two others wrote emails to complain about her hat.

“Yet the gym caved to these liberal bullies, punishing a lawful viewpoint to appease outrage,” Zacharia wrote.

She also blamed the owner and vowed to fight for her right to wear the hat, arguing it’s her First Amendment right.

“The hypocrisy runs deeper than any Hollywood script,” she added. “The owner has also quietly warned others to ditch MAGA gear to avoid ‘offending’ people. He has appointed himself ideological gatekeeper—an arbiter of acceptable speech—while profiting from the silenced majority.”

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Katie Zacharia: A Symptom of a Larger Malady

In a twist that reads more like a satirical skit than a slice of political reality, Katie Zacharia, a MAGA Fox News commentator and now a Department of Homeland Security spokesperson appointee, cries foul over alleged gym bullying for donning a Trump hat. This incident, as reported, is not just a personal anecdote of perceived victimhood but a glaring illustration of the hypocrisy and melodrama that often characterizes the political discourse in today’s America.

The Incident

Zacharia claims she was subjected to unfair treatment at her California kickboxing gym after wearing her “Team Trump 2024” hat, a token of her efforts in Michigan. According to her, this led to a confrontational episode with another gym-goer and subsequent complaints from others, which in turn prompted the gym to take action against her. She cries injustice, alleging that the gym’s response was a capitulation to “liberal bullies.”

The Hypocrisy

Let’s dissect the irony and hypocrisy Zacharia points out, albeit from a different angle than she likely intended. She lambasts the gym owner for acting as an “ideological gatekeeper,” yet fails to see the irony in her own position. As someone who has now been hired by the DHS and previously advised Richard Grenell, she is, by proxy, part of an administration that has been criticized for its own forms of gatekeeping and suppression of dissenting views.

The First Amendment Right: Zacharia’s invocation of the First Amendment is a classic move, yet it’s fundamentally flawed in this context. The First Amendment protects individuals from government censorship, not from private entities or individuals expressing disapproval or setting their own rules within their properties. Her failure to grasp this basic concept of constitutional rights is startling, especially for someone about to hold a significant position in the DHS.

The Deeper Issue

Zacharia’s outcry over a personal grievance is a microcosm of a larger, more troubling trend: the weaponization of victimhood in political narratives. It’s a tactic used by both sides of the aisle, but in this instance, it’s particularly rich coming from a figure associated with a movement that often derides others for perceived sensitivity.

The Real Victims?: In a world where genuine instances of oppression and rights violations occur daily, the focus on a gym dispute over a hat seems not just trivial but grotesquely so. It diverts attention from substantial issues — such as the systemic problems within DHS itself, immigration policies, and human rights concerns — to personal slights.

Conclusion

Katie Zacharia’s tale of gym “bullying” is not just a personal anecdote; it’s a reflection of the current state of political discourse. It underscores a pernicious shift towards framing every disagreement as an attack, every policy enforcement as oppression, and every personal inconvenience as a constitutional crisis.

As she steps into her role at the DHS, one can only hope that Zacharia will apply a more nuanced understanding of rights, freedoms, and the true meaning of public service. However, based on this episode, we might be better off bracing for more melodrama than meaningful discourse. The real hypocrisy and ideology policing, it seems, comes not from the owner of a kickboxing gym but from those who claim to champion free speech and personal liberties only when it suits them.
