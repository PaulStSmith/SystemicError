Far-right couple accused of looting nonprofit to fund lavish castle life

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

New York Attorney General Letitia James. (Shutterstock)

New York Attorney General Letitia James has filed a lawsuit against the anti-immigration group VDARE, saying that the couple who ran the group used donations to fund a medieval-style castle in West Virginia and then moved into it.

Peter and Lydia Brimelow, known for being long-time immigration foes, have "become a bridge between mainstream Republicans and the more overtly racist elements of the far right," wrote New York Times reporters David A. Fahrenthold and Alan Feuer.

The lawsuit is part of James’s ongoing efforts to regulate and hold nonprofits accountable in New York and comes amid her prominent record of taking on controversial organizations, including the Trump Organization and the National Rifle Association.

The core of the lawsuit alleges that the Brimelows used over $1 million of donor money not just for luxury expenditures and overseas travel, but for renovating and maintaining an extravagant castle purchased as a private residence, as well as club memberships and payments benefiting themselves and their family.

James’s office emphasized that charities are meant "to serve the public good," not fund lavish lifestyles for their leaders. She is seeking a court order to compel the Brimelows to repay the alleged misappropriated funds, impose additional penalties, shutter VDARE, and redistribute any remaining assets to other reputable charities. James called the charity a "personal piggy bank" for the couple.

The "castle" is a whopping 9,300 square feet in the resort town of Berkeley Springs, West Virginia, according to a Redfin listing. It was built in the 1880s and features a turret and battlements. The Brimelows didn't ask the group's board for permission to live in the home, James's suit said.

Her court filing asks the judge to force repayment of the funds, plus penalties, and to dissolve the organization, giving any assets to legitimate charities.

The group is named after a white supremacist icon, Virginia Dare, who was purportedly the first white person born in the United States to English settlers.

VDARE has already been sued by James once in 2022, as part of her investigation.

"A state judge later found the nonprofit in contempt of court because of its slow cooperation, imposing tens of thousands of dollars in penalties that Ms. James said the group had not paid," said the Times.

Brimelow announced in a video in July 2024 that he was closing VDARE, blaming James.

“VDARE has been murdered by the New York state attorney general, Letitia James,” he claimed. “She’s not charged us with anything — it’s important to know. She’s simply battered us to death with an enormous, ongoing, intrusive, quote-unquote investigation."

Our Analysis:

The Castle of Corruption: Unveiling the VDARE Scandal

In an audacious display of brazenness and greed, the anti-immigration group VDARE, steered by Peter and Lydia Brimelow, has been dragged into the limelight, not for its controversial stance on immigration, but for the opulent and utterly indefensible misuse of donations to fund a medieval-style castle. This bombshell lawsuit filed by New York Attorney General Letitia James is not just another item on the docket; it's a revealing snapshot of how thinly veiled charitable endeavors can serve as nothing more than personal piggy banks for those at the helm.

The Lavish Castle of Deceit

At the heart of this scandal lies a 9,300 square-foot castle in Berkeley Springs, West Virginia, complete with turrets and battlements, seemingly ripped right from a fairy-tale if not for its foundation built on exploitation and deceit. The Brimelows, known for their divisive anti-immigration rhetoric, have allegedly siphoned over $1 million from VDARE's coffers—money intended to fuel their cause—towards maintaining and enjoying this extravagant residence, along with other luxuries including club memberships and overseas travel. Such flagrant misuse of charitable funds not only betrays the trust of every donor but mocks the very concept of charity itself.

A Bridge to Nowhere: Exploiting the Mainstream

The Brimelows have been described as a bridge between mainstream Republicans and the more overtly racist elements of the far right. This lawsuit peels back the layers of their operation, revealing that the only thing they've been bridging is the gap between deceptive fundraising and personal enrichment. This behavior is not pioneering; rather, it's a textbook example of the exploitation of political divisions and the generosity of individuals for personal gain.

Regulatory Retribution

Attorney General Letitia James's lawsuit is not merely a punitive measure; it's a clarion call for accountability and integrity within the nonprofit sector. Her efforts aim to ensure that charities are indeed serving the public good rather than lining the pockets of their stewards. By seeking to dissolve VDARE, compel the repayment of misappropriated funds, impose penalties, and redistribute any remaining assets to reputable charities, James is not only seeking justice for the donors but safeguarding the integrity of charitable organizations at large.

The Irony of Victimhood

Peter Brimelow's claim that VDARE has been "murdered" by the Attorney General's actions flips the script of victimhood in a way that almost distracts from the core issue: the alleged systematic plundering of a nonprofit's resources. His lamentations about being battered by an "enormous, ongoing, intrusive" investigation sidestep the gravity of the accusations: that he and his wife treated charitable donations as their personal treasure trove. This defensive stance seems to be a last-ditch effort to garner sympathy where there is little to be found.

Conclusion: A Beacon of Greed Extinguished

Letitia James's lawsuit against VDARE and the Brimelows is more than a legal battle; it's a stark reminder of the perils of unchecked power and greed. The misuse of charitable funds for personal luxury, especially under the guise of a cause as divisive and sensitive as immigration, is a betrayal of public trust and the principles of charity. As this case unfolds, it should serve as a cautionary tale for nonprofits and their leaders: the public's goodwill is not a commodity to be exploited, but a sacred trust to be upheld.

In the grand scheme, the dissolution of VDARE may signify the end of one organization but, more importantly, it heralds a necessary step towards cleansing the nonprofit sector of exploitation and ensuring that charitable contributions genuinely serve the public good. The medieval castle, rather than a symbol of success, stands as a monument to greed—a tangible representation of what happens when the lines between charity and personal enrichment blur beyond recognition.
