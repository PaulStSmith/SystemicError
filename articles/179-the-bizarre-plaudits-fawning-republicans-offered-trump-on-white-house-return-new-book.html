The bizarre plaudits fawning Republicans offered Trump on White House return: new book

Based on an article by Alexandria Jacobson, Investigative Reporter

Congress Did Not Check Trump; It Curated His Court

The Real Power Was Not Hidden

The reporting, drawn from Regime Change, describes a simple political fact with embarrassing clarity: Trump returned to the White House and House Republicans did not treat that as a constitutional problem. They treated it as an opportunity for tribute. After the same party briefly recoiled when he lost in 2020 and was impeached a second time, they came back in 2024 and endorsed him as a bloc. The institution with leverage was not the man alone. It was the Republican congressional caucus, and it chose submission.

Tribute Is a Form of Governance

The book’s examples are not random flattery. They are legislative performances of obedience: Mount Rushmore fantasies, Flag Day renamed for Trump’s birthday, airports and transit systems relabeled in his honor, a federal study of “Trump Derangement Syndrome,” and bills to put his face on currency. This is not policymaking. It is ritualized deference dressed up as lawmaking. When elected officials spend their energy decorating a leader’s ego, they are not representing constituents. They are proving access.

The Misleading Part Is the Theater of “Shock”

The article frames these gestures as something that would “shock students of history,” and yes, they are grotesque. But the deeper story is not surprise. It is consent. Trump did not somehow hypnotize a passive Congress into doing this. Republicans enabled him because it was useful: loyalty became a survival strategy, and cowardice became party discipline. That distinction matters. The harm is not confusion. The harm is institutional collaboration.

Tech Billionaires Knew the Script Too

The book also says Trump enjoyed tech leaders ingratiating themselves to him. That detail matters because it shows the same pattern outside Congress: powerful people bending before power they helped elevate. This is not just personality cult behavior. It is elite rationalization. When business and political actors decide that access matters more than restraint, they feed the same machine from different doors.

Dictatorship as a Style Choice

Trump comparing himself to Hitler, Mao, and Stalin, as the book reports, fits the larger political function here: domination as self-mythology. Republicans did not respond by defending institutional limits. They responded by laundering the image. That is how authoritarian culture spreads in plain sight. Not through tanks in the street, but through lawmakers proposing symbolic gifts to a leader who already understands the value of humiliation as proof of loyalty.

The Pattern Is Deeper Than Trump

The core lesson is not that Trump is unusually vain. It is that a major party can decide that servility is cheaper than accountability. Once that happens, the legislature stops acting like a check on power and starts acting like a stage for it. The tributes are absurd, but the system underneath them is familiar: ambition, fear, and the steady conversion of public office into private homage.
