The Trump administration is actively working to “destabilize” the 2026 midterm elections in a plot to expand its executive power, election law expert David Becker warned in a New York Times report Saturday.

That plot, Becker argued, was made evident by the FBI’s unprecedented raid on a Georgia election center last week, where agents – controversially accompanied by National Intelligence Director Tulsi Gabbard – seized hundreds of boxes of election materials in Fulton County, the heart of President Donald Trump’s false claims that the 2020 election was “rigged.”

Becker, who leads the Center for Election Innovation and Research as its executive director, told the Times that it was “very unlikely” that the FBI raid was sparked by any new evidence. And, in the absence of any new evidence of election fraud, the motivation behind the raid was clear, Becker argued.

“So if that’s not the purpose, then the purpose must be to create a false narrative around election security that’s designed to destabilize the 2026 election, and what they might use that narrative to justify in terms of executive power in the states,” Becker said.

Trump’s recent comments also give credence to Becker’s warning. On Monday, Trump told his former FBI deputy director Dan Bongino that Republicans should take control of the election process in “at least 15 places,” citing his false theories about systemic voter fraud in the 2020 election.

Republican lawmakers have struggled to defend Trump’s comments about expanding the federal government’s control over the election process, with Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) openly objecting to Trump’s pitch to federalize elections.

Thune was joined in his opposition by Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), whoarguedthat Trump’s pitch to federalize elections ran at direct odds with the Constitution. Others took a more evasive approach, including Rep. Tony Gonzalez (R-TX), whorepeatedly dodged questionsabout Trump’s proposal during aCNNappearance this week.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration’s Alarming Maneuvers: A Closer Look

In what reads like a political thriller, but unfortunately is not, the Trump administration has been accused of actively working to “destabilize” the upcoming 2026 midterm elections. This claim, as reported by the New York Times, is supported by election law expert David Becker, whose insights paint a grim picture of the current state of American democracy.

The Georgia Election Center Raid: A Red Flag? The FBI’s raid on a Georgia election center, accompanied by National Intelligence Director Tulsi Gabbard, is a scene right out of an authoritarian playbook. The seizure of hundreds of boxes of election materials in Fulton County — the epicenter of President Donald Trump’s baseless claims of a “rigged” 2020 election — is not just shocking but deeply troubling. Becker’s assertion that this raid likely wasn’t sparked by any new evidence only adds to the suspicion that its true purpose was to sow discord and undermine faith in the electoral process.

Creating a False Narrative Around Election Security Becker’s analysis suggests that the raid’s underlying intention was to craft a false narrative about election security, thereby “destabilizing” the 2026 election. This strategy isn’t just underhanded; it’s a direct assault on the democratic process. By attempting to justify an expansion of executive power in the states through manufactured chaos, the Trump administration is playing with fire, risking the very foundations of American democracy.

Trump’s Troubling Comments and the GOP’s Response Trump’s recent remarks to his former FBI deputy director, advocating for Republicans to take control of the election process in “at least 15 places,” reeks of authoritarianism. This, coupled with his continued propagation of debunked theories about systemic voter fraud, should alarm not only every American citizen but also those within his party. Yet, the response from Republican lawmakers has been mixed, at best. While Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) and Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) have voiced their opposition, citing constitutional concerns, others like Rep. Tony Gonzalez (R-TX) seem to be dodging the issue altogether. This lack of a unified stance against such dangerous rhetoric is not just disappointing; it’s a failure of leadership.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The events and comments outlined in the New York Times report are not mere political maneuvering; they are indicative of a dangerous trend towards undermining the electoral process and, with it, the very essence of democracy. The raid on the Georgia election center, under the pretext of investigating election fraud without new evidence, and the push to federalize elections, against constitutional principles, should serve as a wake-up call.

This situation demands more than just passive observation or political posturing. It requires a vigilant, active defense of democratic principles from all quarters, especially from those within Trump’s party who still value the constitution they’ve sworn to protect. The dangers of complacency cannot be overstated; the very fabric of American democracy is at stake.
