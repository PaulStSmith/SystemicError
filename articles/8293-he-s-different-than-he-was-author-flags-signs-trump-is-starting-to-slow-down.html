'He's different than he was': Author flags signs Trump is 'starting to slow down'

An author who has written about President Donald Trump for more than a decade flagged signs that the president is “starting to slow down” during a podcast interview on Wednesday.

Katy Tur, who covered Trump for MS NOW and wrote a book about covering his 2016 campaign called “Unbelievable,” discussed the signs that Trump is aging on a new episode of “The Daily Beast Podcast” with Joana Coles, the outlet’s chief content officer.

Tur revealed that Trump is watching less television than he did during his first administration. She recalled that people in the first administration would joke that Trump was working while he watched TV, but that doesn’t appear to be happening as much during the second term.

She added that Trump is also calling into his favorite shows less frequently.

“He is different than he was,” Tur said. “He doesn’t have the same amount of energy. He’s not as coherent as he used to be. He’s always been somewhat coherent, but it’s different now. He’s slower. His speech is slower. You can see him falling asleep sometimes, or appearing to fall and fall asleep in the Oval Office.”

Tur said she’s noticed Trump’s energy has decreased and his workload appears to have lessened compared to his first administration.

Questions about Trump’s age have been swirling since he took office. The 79-year-old president has been seen falling asleep during press conferences in the Oval Office, and has admitted to taking cognitive exams that have made experts wonder whether he is exhibiting signs of dementia.

Our Analysis:

Aging in the Oval: Trump’s Diminishing Vigor

In a recent episode of “The Daily Beast Podcast,” author Katy Tur, known for her extensive coverage of Donald Trump over more than a decade, sheds light on a subject that’s been simmering beneath the surface of political discourse: the apparent decline in Trump’s physical and cognitive abilities. Tur’s observations are not just passing comments but are grounded in her long-term observation of a man who once dominated the media landscape with his relentless energy and unscripted bravado. Let’s dissect this with the unflinching scrutiny it deserves.

The Diminishing Screen Time

First off, Trump’s reduced television watching is a startling revelation for a figure whose presidency was so closely intertwined with media consumption and manipulation. The man who famously consumed hours of cable news daily and used it as a barometer for his administration’s policies and direction is now tuning out. This isn’t merely a change in habit; it’s a telling sign of a shift in engagement with the world around him. When Tur points out that Trump is “watching less television than he did during his first administration,” it’s not just about his media diet; it’s an indicator of a broader withdrawal.

Less Engagement with Favorite Shows

Moreover, Trump’s decreased frequency in calling into his favored shows is another red flag. For a president who utilized direct communication channels to bypass traditional media gatekeepers and connect with his base, stepping back from this strategy suggests a loss of interest or perhaps a diminished capacity to engage as he once did. This is a significant departure from the Trump we knew, who thrived on the immediacy and control provided by live television and radio appearances.

Energy and Coherence on the Decline

Tur’s observations about Trump’s energy levels and coherence are perhaps the most damning. A president known for his rambling but robust public appearances now showing signs of fatigue and reduced coherence raises serious questions about his ability to perform the duties of the office. The mention of Trump “falling asleep sometimes, or appearing to fall and fall asleep in the Oval Office” is not just a side note; it’s a profoundly worrying indicator of a leader not fully present.

The Age Question

Finally, the age question cannot be ignored. At 79, Trump’s physical and cognitive health is not just a personal issue but a matter of national concern. The admission of taking cognitive exams and the public instances of falling asleep during press conferences fuel speculation about his fitness for office. While aging is a natural process, the demands of the presidency do not relent for the tolls of time. Questions about Trump’s age and health are not mere political fodder; they are critical considerations for the viability of his leadership.

In conclusion, Katy Tur’s insights provide a compelling narrative of a leader seemingly in decline. This isn’t about partisan politics; it’s about recognizing the signs of a president who might not be operating at full capacity. The implications are vast, touching on everything from policy-making to international relations. As observers, critics, and citizens, we must ask ourselves: what does it mean for the country if its leader is “starting to slow down”? The answer, though complex, is crucial for the health of the nation and its democratic processes.
