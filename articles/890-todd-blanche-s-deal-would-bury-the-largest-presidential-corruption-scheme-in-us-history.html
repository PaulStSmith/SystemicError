Todd Blanche's deal would bury the largest presidential corruption scheme in US history

Based on an article by Robert Reich

A President Building a Legal Firewall Around Himself

Power Wears a Suit

The source story is simple enough: Todd Blanche, now up for attorney general, previously served as Trump’s private lawyer and helped craft a May 19 deal that would bar future prosecutions and inquiries into Trump and his family. That is the relevant fact pattern. Everything else is varnish.

The real question is not whether Blanche is “experienced.” It is who he served, what he built, and what that structure is designed to prevent. The answer is blunt: he used public law to protect private power.

The Client Was Never the Country

The Justice Department is supposed to answer to the United States. Blanche, by the article’s account, still behaves as though Trump is the client and the republic is the obstacle. That is not a neutral bureaucratic error. It is a loyalty test disguised as legal expertise.

The Senate is being asked to confirm a man who allegedly treated the department like a private law firm and then signed off on a settlement that immunizes Trump and his family from a sweeping range of future claims, examinations, and requests for relief. That is not ordinary legal housekeeping. It is institutional sabotage with letterhead.

The Settlement Is the Story

The article’s strongest point is also its ugliest: the so-called settlement is not a resolution, it is a shield. Its breadth matters. According to the source, it protects Trump, his children, and affiliated individuals from future federal scrutiny over matters tied to “lawfare” and “weaponization,” including things that “could have been” asserted by the United States.

That language is doing political work. It recasts oversight as persecution before any oversight can even begin. It transforms possible investigation into illegitimacy in advance. If that deal stands, the government does not merely lose a case. It loses the right to ask the question.

Misdirection as Strategy

The weaker actors in this story are not the ones exercising power. They are the ones being used to absorb blame, distraction, or procedural theater. The Senate gets invited to debate confirmation as if the issue were temperament or qualifications. The public gets fed the familiar pageant of appointees, hearings, and norms.

But the source points to a much larger fact: the president’s lawyer allegedly helped construct a legal quarantine around presidential conduct, family enrichment, and possible corruption. That is not confusion. That is design.

The article also refuses the lazy fiction that this is just about “Trump being Trump.” It names the enabling layer: Blanche, the Senate, the legal machinery, and the immunity deal itself. The harm is not accidental. It is administered.

The Money Trail Is the Point

The source piles up examples for a reason. Crypto ventures, federal financing, mining interests, defense contracts, foreign meetings, market-sensitive announcements, clemency for fraudsters. The pattern is not ideological governance. It is monetization.

Trump and his family, according to the article, have converted the presidency into a channel for enrichment while using deregulatory treatment, federal loans, contracts, and pardons as instruments of value transfer. That is the governing model. The presidency becomes a lever; the family becomes a beneficiary class.

And once you see that pattern, Blanche’s immunity deal stops looking like an isolated legal maneuver. It looks like infrastructure for impunity. If the deal remains in force, the source warns, the government may never learn the full extent of the wrongdoing because the mechanism itself blocks inquiry.

What This Reveals

This is what authoritarian corruption looks like when it is done in daylight and wrapped in procedure. It does not always arrive in uniforms or tanks. Sometimes it arrives as a settlement, a nomination, and a claim that the law is being “weaponized” against the person who is weaponizing it.

The governing pattern here is not just self-dealing. It is the conversion of state power into personal insulation. Trump allegedly profits; Blanche allegedly codifies the protection; the Senate is asked to pretend this is a routine confirmation. That pretence is the scandal.

If the account in the source is accurate, the issue is not merely that Todd Blanche should not be attorney general. It is that the office he seeks has already been treated as a tool for burying evidence, laundering loyalty, and locking the door behind a corrupt presidency. That is not a personnel problem. It is a regime logic.
