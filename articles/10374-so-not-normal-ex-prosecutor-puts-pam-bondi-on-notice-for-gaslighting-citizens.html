Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance late Tuesday weighed in on Bondi's recent comments regarding hate speech.

"There’s free speech and then there’s hate speech," Bondi told Stephen Miller’s wife Katie during an episode of her podcast. "We will absolutely target you, go after you, if you are targeting anyone with hate speech.”

According to Vance, "It has been a day where things are so not normal that you might feel a little bit gaslit."

"Among other things, in the wake of Charlie Kirk’s murder, Attorney General Pam Bondi has been saying the Justice Department will 'absolutely target' hate speech. Presumably, she would be the one who gets to define what qualifies," Vance wrote. "But that’s most definitely not what the law permits."

Vance goes on to say what the First Amendment protects and doesn't protect.

"The First Amendment protects hateful speech. That’s the reason Nazis have been able to march on the streets in Skokie, Illinois, where many Holocaust survivors lived out their lives after the war," the ex-prosecutor wrote. "The First Amendment matters the most when it comes to the speech we like the least, protecting people’s right to air their views, no matter how extreme, because in this country, we make decisions in a free marketplace of ideas, and not one where the morality police get to decide what is and isn’t acceptable. We tolerate speech we disagree with, even speech that we find horrific, in order to protect our own right to speak."

Then, Vance talked about the "good news."

"Here’s the good news: Bondi’s comments provoked outrage. And not just from people like me. Texas Senator Ted Cruz said, 'The First Amendment absolutely protects speech. It absolutely protects hate speech. It protects vile speech. It protects horrible speech. What does that mean? It means you cannot be prosecuted for speech, even if it is evil and bigoted and wrong.' That’s a correct statement of the law, and it doesn’t matter whether it comes from the political left, the political right, or anywhere else in our society," according to Vance.

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Bondi's Gaslighting Saga

In what can only be described as a stunning display of legal malpractice, U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi's recent comments about hate speech not only muddy the waters of legal understanding but set a dangerous precedent for how the Justice Department perceives and aims to regulate speech. Joyce Vance, a former federal prosecutor, calls this out as "gaslighting," and right she is. Let's dissect this mess with the precision it desperately needs.

Bondi's Bizarre Take on Hate Speech

Bondi's assertion, made during a cozy podcast chat, that "There’s free speech and then there’s hate speech," and her promise to target those engaging in the latter, is a textbook example of a government official flirting dangerously close with authoritarianism. The audacity to suggest that the Justice Department will "absolutely target you, go after you, if you are targeting anyone with hate speech,” is not just troubling; it's a flagrant misunderstanding—or worse, a willful ignorance—of the First Amendment's protections.

The First Amendment: A Reminder from Vance

Joyce Vance swoops in with a much-needed constitutional reality check. Her reminder that the First Amendment protects even the most hateful of speech—yes, including that which allows Nazis to march in Skokie, Illinois—is a cold shower for Bondi's fiery, misguided rhetoric. Vance's point that "The First Amendment matters the most when it comes to the speech we like the least" is a succinct summation of a principle that seems to have escaped Bondi's grasp entirely. In a country that prides itself on a "free marketplace of ideas," the notion of targeting hate speech as Bondi suggests is not just impractical; it's antithetical to the very fabric of American democracy.

Unlikely Allies and the "Good News"

Perhaps the most striking aspect of this entire debacle is the source of some of the backlash. Texas Senator Ted Cruz, not exactly a poster child for progressive values, echoes Vance's defense of the First Amendment, stating unequivocally that it "absolutely protects hate speech." This alignment across the political spectrum highlights the universal importance and acceptance of free speech principles, suggesting that Bondi's views are not just controversial, but fringe.

The Verdict

Bondi's comments represent a dangerous flirtation with the erosion of free speech under the guise of combating hate. While her intentions may stem from a desire to protect, the path she suggests is fraught with legal and ethical landmines. Vance's analysis is a beacon of reason in the murky waters of Bondi's rhetoric, reminding us of the importance of vigilance in the face of attempts to dilute our fundamental rights.

In essence, Bondi's approach to hate speech, as critiqued by Vance, is a stark reminder of why we must guard against the overreach of those in power, especially when it comes to the delicate balance between protecting sensibilities and preserving freedom. Let's not be gaslit into believing that the erosion of our rights is a necessary sacrifice for security or civility. The stakes are simply too high.
