'Dagger into the heart': Trump’s EPA head brags he just killed climate change fight

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20255 min read

Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Administrator Lee Zeldin speaks during the signing of the memorandum of understanding between U.S. and Mexico to achieve a permanent solution to the decades-old Tijuana River sewage crisis, in Mexico City, Mexico July 24, 2025. REUTERS/Raquel Cunha

The Environmental Protection Agency, led by longtime ally to President Donald Trump Lee Zeldin, announced Tuesday that the agency is moving to end all regulations that limit greenhouse gas emissions, and in effect, drive “a dagger into the heart of the climate change religion,” CNN reported.

Specifically, the EPA announced its intention to reverse the 2009 “endangerment finding,” a legal opinion that serves as the basis for regulations limiting the emissions of greenhouse gases. By reversing the standard, experts warn that the effects could be devastating for the environment.

“This is not just an attack on science but on common sense,” said Zealan Hoover, a former senior advisor with the EPA, speaking with The Washington Post.

“The National Climate Assessment provides over 2,000 pages of detailed evidence that climate change harms our health and welfare, but you can also ask the millions of Americans who have lost their homes and livelihoods to extreme fires, floods, and storms that are only getting worse.”

Speaking at an event in Indianapolis on Tuesday, Zeldin called the announcement the “largest deregulatory action" in the history of the United States. He also discounted the scientific consensus around climate change as “oversimplified” and “inaccurate” while appearing on the conservative podcast “Ruthless” the same day.

Many business leaders agree with Zeldin, such as Diana Furchtgott-Roth with the conservative think tank the Heritage Foundation, who told The Washington Post on Tuesday that the endangerment finding standard was “a very expensive" regulation. John Bozzella, president of the Alliance for Automotive Innovation, a trade group that represents automakers, said that eliminating the standard would help “keep the auto industry in America competitive.”

Were the EPA to follow through with its proposal, automakers and energy producers would be largely unrestricted in the level of emissions their products and facilities produce, something that some experts warn could be catastrophic, particularly for Americans in more disaster-prone areas.

“You’re asking the American people who are living through wildfires, floods, hurricanes, heat domes and so on, not to believe what they’re going through, not to believe their own eyes,” said David Doniger, senior attorney at the advocacy group Natural Resources Defense Council, speaking with The Washington Post.

“At some point what they’re claiming is going to appear to people to be mind bogglingly false, and out of touch.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Assault on Climate Regulations: A Dangerous Game

In a move that can only be described as both brazenly audacious and recklessly dangerous, the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), under the stewardship of Lee Zeldin—a figure whose allegiance to former President Donald Trump is as well-known as it is concerning—has declared war on the environment. The agency's recent announcement to end all regulations limiting greenhouse gas emissions effectively thrusts a sword, not a dagger, into the very heart of rational environmental stewardship and, indeed, the global fight against climate change.

The Reversal of Common Sense

The EPA's intention to reverse the 2009 “endangerment finding” is not merely a policy shift; it's an outright denial of scientific consensus and empirical evidence. This legal opinion, which the agency now seeks to obliterate, forms the foundation upon which regulations of greenhouse gas emissions are built. Its reversal signifies more than a step backward; it represents a leap into a chasm of willful ignorance. Zealan Hoover, a former senior advisor with the EPA, hit the nail on the head when he termed this move an “attack on science and on common sense.” Indeed, how else can one describe the dismissal of over 2,000 pages of detailed evidence and the lived experiences of millions of Americans facing the wrath of climate change firsthand?

The Deregulatory Mirage

Lee Zeldin's proclamation of this initiative as the “largest deregulatory action” in U.S. history is not a badge of honor but a mark of infamy. To tout the undermining of foundational environmental protections as a triumph is to celebrate the acceleration towards ecological and societal peril. Furthermore, Zeldin's dismissal of the scientific consensus on climate change as “oversimplified” and “inaccurate” during his appearance on the conservative podcast “Ruthless” is not just misleading—it's dangerously false.

Economic Fallacies and Environmental Fallout

The notion peddled by business leaders like Diana Furchtgott-Roth of the Heritage Foundation, that the endangerment finding standard is “a very expensive regulation,” is a classic example of prioritizing short-term economic gains over long-term environmental and societal health. Similarly, John Bozzella's argument that eliminating this standard would bolster the competitiveness of the American auto industry is a shortsighted view that neglects the broader implications of unchecked emissions on global markets, not to mention the planet.

The Reality of Experience vs. Political Rhetoric

David Doniger's remarks underscore the dissonance between the EPA's actions and the reality experienced by Americans across the country. By asking people to disregard the evidence of their own experiences with wildfires, floods, hurricanes, and heat domes, the EPA is not just gambling with public opinion—it's gambling with lives. The assertion that the ramifications of this policy reversal could be "catastrophic" is not hyperbole; it's a forewarning of the tangible and tragic outcomes that such willful ignorance invites.

Conclusion: The Path Forward

The EPA's current trajectory under Lee Zeldin is not merely misguided; it's a direct threat to the well-being of the planet and its inhabitants. This is not about politics; it's about preservation. Preservation of our environment, our communities, and our future. As this dangerous game unfolds, one can only hope that rationality, science, and common sense will prevail over the reckless abandon that currently guides the agency's policies. The stakes could not be higher, and the outcome affects not just the present generation, but all those that follow.
