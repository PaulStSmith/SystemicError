This massive corporate bonus reveals everything about Trump's America

Tesla announced on Monday that it’s granting additional shares to Elon Musk worth around $29 billion. Tesla’s board describes it as a “first step, ‘good faith’ payment” to Musk — even as Tesla continues to battle in court over reinstating an even bigger pay package that a Delaware judge struck down.

Why is this giant pay package necessary, you might ask, when Musk already holds 13 percent of the company, worth hundreds of billions?

It’s not as if Tesla is thriving and Musk has contributed to its profitability. In fact, Tesla’s sales and profit are falling and it’s losing market share. Tesla’s stock is now down about 20 percent for the year. The company hasn’t reported an increase in quarterly earnings since the third quarter of 2024.

Tesla’s downward profit spiral is mainly due to Musk’s involvement in right-wing politics, which has alienated many car buyers. Although Musk has officially left the Trump administration, he is still nosing around politics. He’s even talking about starting a third party.

And let’s be clear: His political power comes directly from his wealth. Tesla’s making him $29 billion wealthier arguably makes American politics $29 billion dirtier.

It’s not as if Musk needs the additional money. He’s already the world’s richest person, worth about $350 billion.

So why is Tesla’s board giving him a $29 billion raise?

Because Musk hinted last month that he wanted more shares in Tesla to prevent his ouster by “activist” shareholders. It was a “major concern,” he said on an earnings call with analysts.

But this excuse begs the question of why activist shareholders would want him ousted if he were doing such a good job at Tesla. The answer is he’s obviously not doing a good job, and he knows it.

Tesla’s directors aren’t exerting better control over Musk because the board is packed with Musk’s close friends and his brother. This is called a conflict of interest, people.

In fact, what Musk is doing to Tesla is a smaller version of what Trump is doing to America: fleecing it while running it into the ground.

And Tesla’s board’s response is a miniature version of the way congressional Republicans are responding to Trump: rubber-stamping whatever he wants.

Many Tesla shareholders, meanwhile, resemble Trump’s MAGA base. They’ve made a cult out of Musk and applaud anything that keeps him at Tesla despite his breathtakingly irresponsible performance as CEO.

Call it authoritarian capitalism.

Our Analysis:

Tesla's Billion-Dollar Boondoggle: Musk's Monstrous Reward

In a move that reeks of corporate nepotism and raises eyebrows across Wall Street and Main Street alike, Tesla announced a staggering $29 billion share grant to Elon Musk, a decision that seems more like a parody of corporate excess than a sound business strategy. This so-called "first step, ‘good faith’ payment" is not just a slap in the face to Tesla shareholders but a grotesque illustration of the widening chasm between corporate elites and the principles of responsible governance and fiscal sanity.

Musk's Unmerited Bonanza

The rationale behind this gargantuan grant is as baffling as it is infuriating. Musk, already sitting on a personal fortune of approximately $350 billion, hardly needs a financial incentive to remain at the helm of Tesla. Yet, Tesla’s board, in what can only be described as a sycophantic display of loyalty, has decided to enrich him further, ostensibly to ward off "activist" shareholders concerned with Musk's performance. This reasoning is so flimsy it wouldn't hold water in a sieve.

Tesla's Troubling Trajectory

Let's not mince words: Tesla is in trouble. The company’s sales and profits are tumbling, and its market share is eroding. This decline cannot be disentangled from Musk's controversial foray into right-wing politics, a venture that has not only tarnished his and Tesla's image but has alienated a significant segment of the car-buying public. Despite these glaring red flags, Tesla's board, packed with Musk's cronies and his own brother, has shown more backbone in a jellyfish, rubber-stamping this ludicrous pay package without a second thought to the company’s or shareholders' best interests.

The Political Pollution

Musk's political machinations, from flirting with the idea of creating a third party to his unsettling coziness with the Trump administration, are not just personal idiosyncrasies—they're liabilities. His political influence, powered by his immense wealth, is a contaminant in the American political landscape. The additional $29 billion in his pocket thanks to Tesla’s largesse does nothing but amplify this influence, making the waters of American politics even murkier.

A Call to Action for Tesla Shareholders

This latest episode in the Musk saga should not just be a cause for concern but a clarion call for Tesla shareholders. The company’s direction, under the unchecked guidance of Musk and his board of enablers, is veering into dangerous territory. Shareholders must demand accountability, transparency, and most importantly, a leadership that prioritizes the company’s long-term health over the whims of its CEO.

Conclusion

What we are witnessing at Tesla is nothing short of authoritarian capitalism—a system where the rules are bent, twisted, and sometimes broken to cater to the whims of a single individual, consequences be damned. The $29 billion share grant to Musk is not just a financial folly; it's a symbol of everything that is wrong with corporate governance today. It’s time for those with a stake in Tesla’s future to stand up and demand change, lest they find themselves complicit in this systemic error.
