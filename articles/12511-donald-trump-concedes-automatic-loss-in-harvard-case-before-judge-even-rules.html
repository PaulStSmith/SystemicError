Donald Trump concedes 'automatic loss' in Harvard case before judge even rules

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Tirade: A Masterclass in Prejudgment and Misdirection

U.S President Donald Trump looks on on the day he signs the HALT Fentanyl Act, in the East Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 16, 2025. REUTERS/Umit Bektas

President Donald Trump conceded an "automatic loss" in the Harvard federal funding case before Judge Allison D. Burroughs even ruled on Monday.

Trump posted to social media, "The Harvard case was just tried in Massachusetts before an Obama appointed Judge. She is a TOTAL DISASTER, which I say even before hearing her Ruling. She has systematically taken over the various Harvard cases, and is an automatic 'loss' for the People of our Country!"

Harvard sued the Trump administration for withholding $2.6 billion in federal funds as punishment for allegedly allowing antisemitism to run rampant on campus.

NBC News reported Monday afternoon that the judge did, in fact, appear to "lean in favor of Harvard University" during oral arguments.

Trump's post continued, "Harvard has $52 Billion Dollars sitting in the Bank, and yet they are anti-Semitic, anti-Christian, and anti-America. Much of this money comes from the U.S.A., all to the detriment of other Schools, Colleges, and Institutions, and we are not going to allow this unfair situation to happen any longer.

"How did this Trump-hating Judge get these cases? When she rules against us, we will IMMEDIATELY appeal, and WIN. Also, the Government will stop the practice of giving many Billions of Dollars to Harvard, much of which had been given without explanation. It is a longtime commitment to Fairness in Funding Education, and the Trump Administration will not stop until there is VICTORY. Thank you for your attention to this matter!"

Our Analysis:

The Trump Tirade: A Masterclass in Prejudgment and Misdirection

In a stunning display of preemptive defeatism, President Donald Trump has managed to epitomize the art of political bluster with his recent social media outburst regarding the Harvard federal funding case. Before Judge Allison D. Burroughs could even issue her ruling, Trump took to the digital stage to declare an "automatic loss" due to the judge's Obama-era appointment and her perceived disastrous influence over the Harvard cases. This maneuver is nothing short of a spectacular misfire in the realm of public discourse, a blend of prejudgment and misinformation that demands a closer examination.

Trump's Premature Concession: A Strategy or a Spectacle?

Trump's declaration of defeat ahead of the court's decision is a curious strategy, to say the least. By announcing an "automatic loss," the President not only undermines the judicial process but also positions himself as a martyr for the so-called "People of our Country." This preemptive strike against Judge Burroughs is emblematic of Trump's broader disdain for the judiciary, particularly when presided over by appointees from the opposing political camp. His words, "She is a TOTAL DISASTER," lack substance and serve only to discredit the judge based on her appointment rather than her judicial record or the merits of the case at hand.

The Antisemitism Allegation: A Cloak for Financial Strong-arming?

Central to this saga is the Trump administration's withholding of $2.6 billion in federal funds from Harvard University, ostensibly as punishment for allowing antisemitism to "run rampant" on campus. This is a grave accusation, one that NBC News notes was met with skepticism by Judge Burroughs during oral arguments. Trump's narrative conveniently omits the complexity of such allegations, opting instead for a broad-brush condemnation of one of America's oldest institutions. His claim that Harvard is "anti-Semitic, anti-Christian, and anti-America" is a bold and unfounded assertion that seems more aimed at rallying his base than addressing the nuances of hate speech and academic freedom.

Harvard's Wealth: A Red Herring?

Trump's fixation on Harvard's $52 billion endowment is a misleading grievance. The President insinuates that because Harvard has significant financial reserves, it should not receive federal funds, ignoring the fact that these funds often support research and financial aid that benefits the wider American and global community. His promise to "stop the practice of giving many Billions of Dollars to Harvard" is a populist platitude that oversimplifies the complex ecosystem of federal funding for higher education. It is a dangerous precedent to suggest that the government should withhold funds based on a university's perceived political stance or the wealth of its endowment.

The Trump Administration's Quest for "VICTORY"

In his concluding remarks, Trump vows to appeal any unfavorable ruling and to continue the fight until there is "VICTORY." This language, militaristic and absolute, reveals a concerning perspective on governance and justice. It is not the role of the presidency to declare war on academic institutions or to seek victory over the free exchange of ideas. This stance is antithetical to the principles of academic freedom and the pursuit of knowledge, cornerstones upon which the American educational system is built.

In summary, Trump's premature condemnation of the Harvard funding case is a masterclass in misdirection and misinformation. It reveals a profound misunderstanding of, or disregard for, the principles of justice, academic freedom, and the role of government in supporting higher education. This saga is less about the legal merits of the case and more about the ongoing battle between Trump's administration and the institutions that dare to challenge it.
