Sam Stein, managing editor of The Bulwark, said on the podcast “Bulwark Takes” that Trump’s vaccine policies seem to be too extreme for Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who has received significant criticism from medical professionals over his own stance on the issue.

Stein cited reporting from the Wall Street Journal that says Trump “feels emboldened to go further after feeling as though he has faced surprisingly little resistance to his ambitious agenda in the first nine months.”

One agenda item that Trump pursued in recent weeks is changing the childhood vaccine schedule, according to the report. RFK Jr. tried to talk Trump out of “making such broad proclamations” about childhood vaccines, but Trump ignored him, the report says.

“So, he’s so crazy,” Stein said of Trump. “He’s too crazy even for Robert F. Kennedy Jr.Kennedy Jr. on vaccines.”

Sarah Longwell, publisher of The Bulwark, seemed stunned by the news.

“It also smacks to me of the president of the United States, who’s basically taken unilateral authority,” Longwell said. “He ought to be able to get a couple of things done that aren’t terrible.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of the Unhinged: Trump vs. RFK Jr.

In a political theater that often mirrors the absurd, the latest act features President Donald Trump and Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. in roles that defy even the most imaginative scriptwriters. According to Sam Stein, managing editor of The Bulwark, Trump’s stance on childhood vaccines has managed to alienate Kennedy Jr.—a scenario as bewildering as it is alarming.

Trump’s Vaccine Gambit: Alienating Allies in Broad Daylight

Trump’s approach to childhood vaccines, as reported, is so radical that it has pushed away RFK Jr., a figure already controversial for his own vaccine skepticism. This revelation is not just a mere disagreement within the ranks; it is a glaring spotlight on Trump’s willingness to push beyond the bounds of reason and expert advice. The report from the Wall Street Journal that Trump “feels emboldened to go further after feeling as though he has faced surprisingly little resistance to his ambitious agenda in the first nine months” reveals a president unmoored from the gravity of his office’s responsibility.

RFK Jr.’s Desperate Plea Ignored

RFK Jr.’s attempt to temper Trump’s broad proclamations on childhood vaccines underscores a desperate bid to inject a semblance of sanity into an administration increasingly detached from reality. The fact that a figure like Kennedy—who has faced significant criticism from the medical community for his stance on vaccines—finds Trump’s policies too extreme, speaks volumes about the reckless direction in which Trump is steering his administration.

The Shock and Awe of Unilateral Authority

Sarah Longwell’s reaction to Trump’s unilateral moves on vaccine policy points to a broader concern: the erosion of collaborative governance and the rise of autocratic decision-making. When Longwell expresses shock at the president taking unilateral authority to the extent of making decisions that are potentially harmful to public health, it underscores a fundamental breakdown in the system of checks and balances that is supposed to safeguard against such overreach.

Conclusion: The Absurdity of the Situation

The situation is as ridiculous as it is dangerous. The idea that Trump’s policies could be considered too extreme for RFK Jr., a well-known vaccine skeptic, would be laughable if it weren’t so deeply concerning. This episode is yet another illustration of how far Trump is willing to go in pursuit of his own agenda, without regard for expert advice, scientific consensus, or the potential repercussions on public health. It’s a stark reminder of the need for resistance against such unhinged policymaking and the importance of holding leaders accountable for their actions. In the grand scheme of things, this isn’t just about vaccines—it’s about the very principles of governance and the safeguarding of public welfare.
