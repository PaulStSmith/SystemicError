Trump singles out MAGA senator in late-night attack: 'People are sick and tired'

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosOctober 3, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedPower sits in the SenateTrump chose a targetA slogan in place of evidenceManufactured urgencyThe system behind the scapegoat

What happened

RawStory reports that President Donald Trump used a late-night Truth Social post to blame Republican Sen. Tom Cotton of Arkansas for blocking the Sunshine Protection Act, which would make daylight saving time permanent. Trump said Cotton was holding up the bill “for reasons unknown” and declared that people were tired of changing their clocks twice a year.

The measure had passed the House but remained stalled in the Senate. Trump issued his demand after senators had already left for a recess lasting until after the midterm elections, so his post could not produce the immediate Senate action he requested.

Trump also claimed that permanent daylight saving time would improve health, safety, family life and several industries. RawStory reports those claims but supplies no supporting studies or response from Cotton explaining his position.

Power sits in the Senate

The Senate has the institutional power to advance or bury the legislation. Its procedures, agenda and leadership determine whether the House-passed bill receives consideration. Cotton may possess enough influence to impede it, but Trump’s post alone does not establish how he did so or whether he was solely responsible.

That missing mechanism matters. Reducing Senate inaction to one obstructionist senator turns an institutional outcome into a personality dispute. It leaves leadership decisions, procedural barriers and the conduct of other senators largely outside the frame.

Trump chose a target

Trump did not explain Cotton’s objection. He attached Cotton’s name to public frustration and described the reason for the delay as unknown. That is political pressure, not an account of the legislative process.

The asymmetry is clear: the president commands a national platform and can direct supporters’ anger toward an individual lawmaker. Cotton is hardly powerless—he is a senator—but Trump’s framing still substitutes denunciation for evidence. If Cotton blocked the bill, readers need to know what action he took. RawStory does not provide that detail.

A slogan in place of evidence

Trump presented a sweeping catalogue of promised benefits, ranging from fewer murders and traffic deaths to lower cardiac risk and energy bills. The breadth of the list creates an impression of settled authority, but the report neither identifies the studies Trump invoked nor evaluates whether they support permanent daylight saving time specifically.

That distinction is essential. Evidence that changing clocks causes disruption would not, by itself, establish that permanent daylight saving time is preferable to permanent standard time. Trump’s post compresses those separate questions into one politically convenient conclusion.

Manufactured urgency

The timing exposes the performance. Trump demanded immediate action after the Senate had already recessed. Whatever his preference on clock policy, the reported intervention arrived when senators could not deliver the result he publicly demanded.

That allows a familiar maneuver: issue an impossible deadline, identify a culprit and claim alignment with an exhausted public. Legislative strategy disappears. The spectacle of command replaces the work required to move a bill.

The system behind the scapegoat

This episode shows how institutional failure is repackaged as personal drama. A stalled bill involving the House, Senate procedure and legislative leadership becomes a feud between two Republicans, while unsupported policy claims pass through as a list of supposed facts.

The result is political misdirection with a practical consequence: readers learn whom Trump wants blamed, but not who controlled the schedule, what Cotton actually did, or why the Senate failed to act. When coverage adopts that frame, power escapes scrutiny precisely by pointing at a target.
