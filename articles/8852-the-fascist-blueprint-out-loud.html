The Fascist Blueprint, Out Loud

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 10, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Language of DesperationA Blueprint, Not a RantThe Cult of UrgencyThe Hypocrisy of “Seizing”The Authoritarian’s CalculusBannon’s Enemies ListPreparing the Ground for ViolenceThe Banality of the GriftThe StakesWhat Comes Next


Steve Bannon is once again saying the quiet part with a megaphone. The disgraced architect of Trumpism—the self-styled revolutionary who sees democracy as an inconvenience and authoritarianism as destiny—stood before a crowd of would-be loyalists this week and told them to “seize the institutions.” Not metaphorically. Literally.

Bannon’s speech wasn’t a slip of the tongue or an off-the-cuff provocation. It was a field manual for the next American coup, delivered with the casual swagger of a man who knows impunity when he tastes it. “As God as my witness, if we lose the midterms and we lose 2028, some in this room are going to prison,” he said, confessing the central truth of the MAGA movement: this isn’t about ideology, governance, or the people. It’s about power, and survival after guilt.

The threat embedded in Bannon’s words is two-fold. First, it’s a rallying cry for political extremism masquerading as “urgency.” Second, it’s a psychological conditioning campaign—one that prepares the faithful to justify lawlessness when the next crisis arrives.

The Language of Desperation

Bannon frames his crusade as existential: lose power, go to jail. That framing is not paranoia; it’s projection. He and his cohort should fear prison, because they’ve already dragged the rule of law through the mud—defying subpoenas, laundering donations, and conspiring to defraud the very “patriots” they pretend to represent.

By couching his agenda in the language of self-preservation, Bannon recasts accountability as persecution. To his followers, the possibility of facing justice becomes proof of martyrdom. Every indictment, every conviction, every banishment from polite society is rebranded as evidence of courage. It’s the fascist alchemy that turns crooks into saints and mobs into “movements.”

A Blueprint, Not a Rant

For years, analysts dismissed Bannon’s podcast tirades as theater—a grifter’s performance art for the paranoid and the gullible. But what he’s doing now is more calculated. “Seize the institutions,” he says, “codify what Trump has done by executive order.” That’s not random rhetoric. That’s an operational directive.

He’s calling for a metastasis of authoritarian control through legal and bureaucratic means. Don’t just stack the courts—weaponize them. Don’t just defy the Justice Department—own it. Don’t just govern by decree—transform those decrees into law before the public realizes what’s been lost.

It’s precisely how every authoritarian project consolidates itself: use democracy’s own mechanisms as the knife against its throat.

The Cult of Urgency

Bannon’s refrain—“We’re burning daylight”—is the heartbeat of this movement. Fascism thrives on the illusion of a closing window. There’s always a last chance, a ticking clock, a final battle to “save” the nation from imaginary enemies. The narrative demands permanent mobilization, because reflection breeds doubt, and doubt kills zeal.

This is why Bannon invokes apocalypse when speaking of electoral defeat. “If we lose 2028, it’s over,” he warns. Over for what? Not for the republic. For him. For Trump. For their ecosystem of corruption that depends on keeping chaos profitable.

The Hypocrisy of “Seizing”

Bannon couches his war cry in the language of “freedom” and “constitutionalism,” even as he advocates dismantling the very system that protects those ideas. He derides “structural barriers” like the filibuster, checks and balances, and civil service independence—features designed to restrain executive overreach—and frames them as enemies of progress.

The irony, of course, is that Bannon and his ilk once screamed about “tyranny” when Democrats used executive power for healthcare reform or climate policy. But tyranny, in their lexicon, is always what others do. When they demand total control, it’s “restoring order.” When their opponents govern, it’s “socialism.”

The Authoritarian’s Calculus

Bannon’s contempt for democracy is not ideological—it’s tactical. He’s an opportunist who recognizes that permanent minority rule can only be sustained by suppressing dissent and rewriting the rules mid-game. His real genius lies in persuading the disaffected to fight for their own disenfranchisement, to see their servitude as empowerment.

The far right’s mythology of victimhood is the glue binding the movement together. They are forever “under siege”—by immigrants, feminists, scientists, teachers, journalists, and anyone capable of critical thought. This perpetual siege narrative justifies everything: the lies, the violence, the authoritarian impulse.

Bannon’s Enemies List

His venom toward New York’s newly elected mayor, Zohran Mamdani, exposes the racist subtext underlying his strategy. Calling for the deportation of a naturalized citizen for daring to champion working-class policies isn’t just demagoguery—it’s a fascist purity test. Citizenship itself becomes conditional on political loyalty.

That’s not America. That’s the rhetoric of regimes that burn books and build camps.

Preparing the Ground for Violence

As one critic noted, Bannon is “preparing his audience to see violence and institutional takeover as necessary.” That’s exactly right. The MAGA movement is being conditioned to believe that legality is irrelevant when “the enemy” controls the courts, the media, or the vote. Once that seed is planted, the leap from ballot box to bullet is no longer unthinkable—it’s patriotic.

This is how democracy dies in the 21st century: not with a coup d’état in the streets, but with a million people convinced the coup already happened to them.

The Banality of the Grift

It’s easy to forget, amid all the revolutionary bombast, that Bannon is fundamentally a hustler. He monetizes outrage. Every apocalyptic broadcast, every “urgent” appeal, every new ideological crusade is a fundraising hook. He’s selling a subscription to fascism, with tiered benefits: emotional validation, scapegoats, and the illusion of power.

But the grift is contagious. Other Republicans—those “institutionalists” like Josh Hawley and Lindsey Graham, whom Bannon claims are “coming around”—see which way the wind is blowing. They’re calculating that silence or complicity might spare them when the knives come out. History says otherwise. The revolution always eats its own.

The Stakes

When Bannon warns that “you’re never going to have another Trump,” he’s right—but not in the way he thinks. The next demagogue won’t need to mimic Trump’s chaos. They’ll inherit the infrastructure of control that Trump and Bannon built—the loyal judges, the gutted agencies, the cowed media ecosystem—and they’ll use it with precision.

That’s the danger of normalizing Bannon’s rhetoric: it’s not just words. It’s the roadmap for a future where elections still occur but outcomes are predetermined, where courts still exist but justice is performative, where the flag still waves but means nothing.

What Comes Next

The proper response to Bannon’s call isn’t pearl-clutching or fact-checking his latest lie—it’s mobilization. The institutions he wants to seize don’t belong to him; they belong to the people. But ownership without vigilance is an invitation to theft.

Democracy survives only when its defenders stop assuming someone else will defend it. That’s the uncomfortable truth for moderates who still think politics is a spectator sport.

The lesson of the last decade should be carved in granite by now: fascism doesn’t fade on its own. It metastasizes when ignored.

Bannon isn’t just a symptom. He’s a strategist. And his followers aren’t fringe—they’re foot soldiers embedded in school boards, state legislatures, and corporate media. Their goal is not to “win” elections, but to make losing impossible.

So, yes, he’s “burning daylight.” But so are we. The clock isn’t counting down to their next conquest—it’s counting down to our next chance to stop them.

If America wants to remain a democracy, it must treat Steve Bannon’s words as what they are: an open declaration of intent from a man who’s already tried once to overthrow the government and is now outlining the sequel.

And if history has taught us anything, it’s that fascists don’t warn—they announce.
