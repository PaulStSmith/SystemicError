'That is not true': Trump hit with blunt fact check after spreading Supreme Court lie

President Donald Trump has been making false claims about Supreme Court approval for his latest round of tariffs, according to reporting from Politico that reveals the president is misrepresenting the high court’s actual ruling.

Trump has been “repeatedly claiming that the same Supreme Court went ahead and blessed his use of other authorities, like the so-called Section 122 tariffs he’s turned to as a short-term fix.” However, Politico reports this characterization is inaccurate.

“That is not true,” according to the outlet.

While three justices who dissented from the Supreme Court’s recent tariff decision did cite Section 122 as a potential tool Trump could use, the court’s six-justice majority explicitly rejected that position. Chief Justice John Roberts wrote in the majority opinion: “We do not speculate on hypothetical cases not before us.”

The distinction matters significantly for Trump’s legal strategy. The president is now relying on Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 to justify his 10 percent tariffs imposed in February, claiming the U.S. faces a “large and serious balance-of-payments deficit.”

Legal experts acknowledge Trump’s new tariffs are on “probably stronger legal footing” than his previous “Liberation Day” taxes that the Supreme Court struck down. However, challengers including Democratic attorneys general from 24 states are already filing lawsuits challenging the legitimacy of Trump’s statutory interpretation and the sweeping exemptions his proclamation includes.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Trump’s Tariff Claims

Misrepresentation in the Oval Office: President Donald Trump’s recent declarations concerning Supreme Court backing for his tariff strategy starkly illustrate a troubling trend of misinformation emanating from the highest echelons of power. According to Politico, Trump has been inaccurately asserting that the Supreme Court endorsed his use of Section 122 tariffs as a legitimate tool for addressing economic concerns, a claim that directly conflicts with the Court’s actual stance.

Who Holds the Power?

First and foremost, it’s essential to pinpoint where the institutional power lies in this scenario. The President of the United States, wielding executive authority, undoubtedly has significant influence over trade policy and national economic strategy. However, the Supreme Court, as the judicial branch, holds the power to interpret the legality of these actions. The interaction between these branches is central to the unfolding narrative.

Decision-makers and Outcomes

The decision to implement tariffs under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 was made solely by President Trump and his administration, purportedly in response to a “large and serious balance-of-payments deficit.” This move, and the justification for it, squarely places Trump at the heart of the decision-making process. Conversely, the Supreme Court’s majority opinion, led by Chief Justice John Roberts, explicitly refrained from endorsing this approach, emphasizing a reluctance to speculate on hypotheticals not currently before the court.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article’s framing correctly attributes responsibility to President Trump for both the decision to impose the tariffs and the misleading portrayal of the Supreme Court’s stance. There’s no evidence of misdirection towards actors with limited control over the situation. Instead, Politico’s reporting shines a light on the factual inaccuracies propagated by the Trump administration, challenging the narrative put forth by the President.

Conclusion: A Clear Misrepresentation

The heart of this issue lies not in the legal complexities of tariff imposition but in the deliberate misrepresentation of the Supreme Court’s position by President Trump. By claiming unwarranted judicial support, Trump seeks to legitimize his controversial trade policies and mitigate backlash. This tactic, however, stands on shaky ground—a fact made evident by Politico’s investigative efforts.

The distinction drawn by the Supreme Court, particularly its majority’s decision to distance itself from speculative endorsements of Section 122 tariffs, is crucial. It underscores the judiciary’s role as an interpreter of law, not an enabler of executive policy. The forthcoming challenges, led by Democratic attorneys general, further highlight the contentious nature of Trump’s tariff strategy and its questionable legal foundation.

In dissecting this narrative, it becomes clear that criticism of President Trump’s misrepresentations is well-placed. The responsibility for both the decision to implement the tariffs and the subsequent dissemination of misleading information about the Supreme Court’s involvement rests solely with the Trump administration. Politico’s reporting serves as a vital corrective, ensuring accountability remains a cornerstone of our democratic discourse.
