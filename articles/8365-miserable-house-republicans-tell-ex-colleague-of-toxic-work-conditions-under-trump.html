An ex-GOP lawmaker came clean on what it’s like to work for “toxic” President Donald Trump — and what he believes is happening behind the scenes among Republicans.

Former Rep. David Trott (R-MI), in an opinion piece published in The Daily Beast on Tuesday, wrote about why the Republican majority is falling apart — and the difficult position Trump has put it in.

“Republicans in particular have an untenable choice—stand up to President Trump’s unconstitutional actions or fall in line and accomplish nothing,” Trott wrote.

Trott described his experience in Congress during Trump’s first term 10 years ago.

“Most of my peers were more capable and intelligent than the media portrayed them, although the place was completely dysfunctional. It still is. And it’s gotten worse. More toxic. The focus is on winning—i.e., beating the other party—and playing the blame game, not solving problems,” Trott wrote.

He now sees a different problem for his Republican colleagues.

“Members I have kept in touch with are miserable, and the deplorable state of affairs in our Capitol today is certainly a driving factor behind many recent lawmakers’ resignations,” he wrote. “One senior Republican recently told Punchbowl that ‘his entire White House team has treated ALL members like garbage... And Mike Johnson has let it happen because he wanted it to happen.’”

Referring to the president as “King Trump,” Trott reveals how the situation has worsened.

“I was there during some of his first term, yes, but Trump was not as adept at abusing his power back then—I can’t imagine serving today just waiting for him to tell me what to do or how to vote,” Trott explained.

Bipartisanship and focusing on results could help lawmakers get out of the situation, but Trott does not expect it to improve anytime soon, especially while the focus for GOP leaders appears to be job security and not serving the American public.

“Let’s be clear, Trump’s executive branch is being run by a bunch of sycophants, felons, and idiots. (Well, former felons—they’ve all been pardoned.) Break the law, go to prison, cut a check, and become a special advisor to the president—what a great country we live in! The Senate confirmed Trump’s picks for key Cabinet roles, ambassadorships, and many more positions of power with blind loyalty being the only criterion,” Trott added.

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Trump Conundrum: A Former Lawmaker’s Scathing Exposé

The Republican Party’s internal struggle with the legacy and leadership of Donald Trump has never been more starkly illuminated than in the recent opinion piece by former Rep. David Trott (R-MI) published in The Daily Beast. Trott’s detailed account not only sheds light on the toxicity that pervades the corridors of power but also on the untenable choices facing Republicans: to either defy Trump’s unconstitutional demands or to acquiesce and achieve nothing of substance. Let’s dissect Trott’s revelations and the broader implications for the GOP and American democracy.

The Cult of Winning Over Governing

Trott’s reflection on his time in Congress during Trump’s first term is a damning indictment of the shift from governance to gamesmanship. The obsession with winning—at the expense of problem-solving and bipartisanship—is a poison running through the veins of Capitol Hill. The fact that lawmakers, according to Trott, are more capable and intelligent than portrayed by the media, yet choose to engage in a dysfunctional and toxic political environment, speaks volumes about the priorities that have come to dominate the Republican Party.

Miserable Lawmakers and the Exodus from Capitol Hill

The deplorable state of affairs in Washington D.C. is not just a matter of ideological clashes or policy disagreements. It’s about a work environment so toxic that it’s driving lawmakers to resign. Trott’s conversations with his former colleagues paint a picture of widespread misery and disillusionment. When senior Republicans describe Trump’s team treating members like “garbage,” it’s clear that respect and collegiality have been supplanted by contempt and coercion. This is not a healthy environment for democracy.

“King Trump” and the Abuse of Power

Referring to Trump as “King Trump,” Trott highlights the evolution of Trump’s authoritarian tendencies. The former president, it seems, has become more adept at leveraging the powers of his office to compel obedience and conformity among GOP lawmakers. The notion that serving in Congress today involves waiting for Trump’s directives on how to vote or what to do is a chilling testament to the erosion of legislative independence and integrity.

The Sycophantic Executive Branch

Trott does not mince words when describing Trump’s executive branch: “a bunch of sycophants, felons, and idiots.” This characterization, while harsh, is backed by a pattern of behavior that prioritizes loyalty over competence and legal adherence. The pardoning of felons who then become special advisors to the president is emblematic of a broader culture of impunity and cronyism. This, according to Trott, is facilitated by a Senate too willing to confirm Trump’s picks with “blind loyalty” as the primary qualification.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

David Trott’s exposé is more than just a personal account of his time in Congress; it’s a clarion call for a reevaluation of the Republican Party’s values and priorities. The GOP finds itself at a crossroads, caught between allegiance to a figure who demands unconditional loyalty and the foundational principles of democracy and good governance. As Trott bleakly assesses, there’s little hope for improvement so long as job security remains the GOP leadership’s paramount concern over serving the American public.

In the end, Trott’s piece isn’t just an indictment of Trump or even the Republican Party; it’s a warning about the fragility of democratic institutions when leaders prioritize power over principles. The GOP’s Trump dilemma is, at its core, America’s dilemma: will we choose leaders who aspire to serve and unite, or will we succumb to the toxic allure of authoritarianism and division?
