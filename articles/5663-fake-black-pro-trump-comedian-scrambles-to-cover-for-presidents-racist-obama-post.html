'Fake!' Black pro-Trump comedian scrambles to cover for president’s racist Obama post

By Paulo Santos

Terrance Williams, a Black comedian, MAGA influencer and loyal supporter of President Donald Trump, scrambled Friday to cover for the president’s racist social media post that included depictions of Barack and Michelle Obama as primates, and was promptly dogpiled by critics.

Early Friday morning, Trump shared a video on social media that included racist depictions of the Obamas as primates in a jungle, a post that stunned critics, media figures and onlookers alike. As a fierce defender of Trump, however, Williams falsely claimed that the outrage was manufactured, and that Trump never shared the racist clip.

“FAKE NEWS ALERT! Trump Didn’t post this monkey video of the Obamas,” Williams wrote Friday in a social media post on X. “Lying Democrats. It’s not on his page. Fake screenshot.”

Williams’ post was immediately hit with X’s crowd-sourced fact-checking service known as “Community Notes” flagging his statement as false. Williams also faced swift pushback from critics, many of whom pointed to the irony of a Black comedian defending a president for amplifying an anti-Black racist trope with roots stretching back centuries.

“Yes he did,” wrote journalist and former MSNBC host Mehdi Hasan, refuting Williams’ claim that Trump did not share the video in question. “He is happy to depict Black people as apes and you’re still pathetically and shamelessly willing to shill for him.”

With the evidence clear that Trump did, in fact, share the video, some critics called on Williams to make his position clear.

“Now that you know he did, will you say why you were so adamant about trying to deny it?” wrote Ron Filipkowski, the editor-in-chief of MeidasTouch. “Obviously you recognize that this isn’t cool, which is why you posted the denial. Maybe you can do a NEWS ALERT on why you wanted to deny this.”

Trump’s social media post was so shocking that some figures who frequently lavish praise on the president, such as journalist Batya Ungar-Sargon, assumed that the post must have been a mistake.

“This is gross and racist. The President should delete it and apologize,” Ungar-Sargon wrote in a social media post on X.

“The clip with the Obamas is the last 2 seconds of a minute long clip on voting machines being hacked, so maybe Trump didn’t watch it til the end. Maybe he didn’t even post it himself. If that’s the case, he should say so.

Our Analysis:

The Unforgivable Defense of the Indefensible

In an era where the boundaries of political discourse are tested daily, the recent incident involving former President Donald Trump’s social media activity marks a new low. Trump’s decision to share a video depicting Barack and Michelle Obama as primates is not just a mere lapse in judgment; it is a blatant display of racism. Yet, what’s equally troubling is the response from Terrance Williams, a Black comedian and MAGA influencer, whose attempt to cover for Trump’s action reveals a disturbing level of denial and obfuscation prevalent among some of Trump’s most loyal supporters.

The Manufactured Defense

Williams’ claim that the outrage over Trump’s post was “manufactured” and that the video was a product of “fake news” is not just factually incorrect; it is an insult to the intelligence of the public. The use of X’s crowd-sourced fact-checking service to debunk Williams’ statement underscores the reality that truth is not a matter of opinion. It’s a reflection of how platforms are battling to contain the wildfire of misinformation, yet individuals like Williams seem intent on fanning the flames.

The Irony of the Situation

The irony of a Black comedian defending a racist trope that dehumanizes Black people cannot be overstated. It’s a sad testament to the lengths some will go to remain in the good graces of power, even if it means turning a blind eye to the most egregious expressions of racism. Mehdi Hasan’s rebuke of Williams highlights a critical point: allegiance to Trump should not supersede basic human decency or the acknowledgment of racism when it is so blatantly displayed.

The Question of Accountability

Critics’ calls for Williams to explain his initial denial following the incontrovertible evidence that Trump did share the video is a demand for accountability. It’s not enough to backtrack silently or ignore one’s role in spreading falsehoods. The expectation for Williams to address his misleading defense is about more than just clarifying the truth; it’s about acknowledging the harm that such defenses do to the communities affected by the racism being dismissed.

The Excuse of Ignorance

Attempts by some Trump supporters to excuse the president’s action by suggesting he might not have watched the video until the end or that he didn’t post it himself are pathetic at best. This line of reasoning is an insult to the concept of leadership and responsibility. If Trump or his team posted the video without fully reviewing its content, it speaks volumes about their negligence and disregard for the impact of their actions. The suggestion that Trump should simply delete the post and apologize, as made by journalist Batya Ungar-Sargon, while a step in the right direction, fails to address the underlying issue of repeatedly engaging in and amplifying racist rhetoric.

Conclusion: The Unbearable Lightness of Being Wrong

This incident is not just about a racist social media post; it’s about the systematic failure of some individuals to recognize, admit, and correct their errors, especially when those errors perpetuate racism. It’s a reflection of how deep the roots of denial and defensiveness go in the face of undeniable racism. Williams’ scramble to defend Trump, and the subsequent backlash, serves as a reminder of the importance of standing against racism, no matter who commits it. In an age where truth is under constant assault, the responsibility lies with each of us to confront falsehoods and demand accountability, lest we all be complicit in the systemic perpetuation of racism.
