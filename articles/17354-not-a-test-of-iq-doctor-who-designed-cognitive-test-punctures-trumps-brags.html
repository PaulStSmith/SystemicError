'Not a test of IQ': Doctor who designed cognitive test punctures Trump’s brags

Distraction by Design: Trump’s Misuse of Cognitive Tests

The Misguided Boast

Former President Donald Trump has repeatedly touted his seemingly impressive results on routine cognitive tests as proof of his intellectual prowess. This boast is fundamentally misleading. The Montreal Cognitive Test, the instrument in question, is not a measure of intelligence or exceptional mental agility; it is a basic screening tool designed to detect cognitive impairment. Neurologist Ziad Nasreddine, who designed the test, has clarified that it is intended to assess normal cognitive function—not intellectual superiority.

A Tool Used Wrong

Trump’s frequent references to his test scores, particularly amidst a backdrop of public concern about his mental sharpness, reveal a strategic misdirection. Rather than addressing genuine public concerns about his cognitive abilities, Trump has manipulated these test results to construct an image of robust health and exceptional intelligence. This tactic diverts attention from more pressing questions about his fitness for office.

Public Perception and Political Reality

Recent polling data indicates a decline in public confidence regarding Trump’s mental acuity. Despite this, Trump’s persistent focus on his cognitive test scores seems to be an attempt to stabilize his image among voters. This manipulation of medical assessments for political gain is a troubling reflection of Trump’s approach to leadership—prioritizing personal image over genuine transparency.

The Larger Pattern of Misdirection

Trump’s handling of his cognitive test results is emblematic of his broader political strategy: redirect, boast, and confuse. Instead of engaging substantively with critiques or concerns, he often opts to create distractions or to reinterpret facts to his advantage. This pattern of behavior is not just troubling in its immediate effects but corrosive to democratic discourse, as it prioritizes personal victory over public clarity.

Systemic Insight: Weaponizing Information

Trump’s use of his cognitive test results as a shield against criticism is a microcosm of a larger political ailment: the weaponization of information. By boasting about passing a basic cognitive assessment, Trump transforms a low bar into a trophy, misleading the public about the nature and purpose of the test. This tactic undermines informed public discourse, replacing it with a distorted narrative that serves his personal and political goals.

In conclusion, Trump’s boast about his cognitive test results is a strategic misdirection that speaks volumes about his approach to leadership and information. It’s a clear instance of using technical truths—such as the ability to pass a simple cognitive test—to weave a misleading narrative about one’s capabilities and health. This is not just a misrepresentation; it’s an active manipulation of public perception, revealing deeper issues about accountability and transparency in leadership.
