The Fox News host fielded questions on her podcast, “Right View With Lara Trump,” and one listener asked who she would like to see perform at Turning Point USA’s alternative to pop superstar Bad Bunny’s concert at the NFL championship game, reported The Daily Beast.

“I know a lot of owners of NFL teams, a lot of people are upset about that,” Trump said. “I don’t know what the heck the NFL was thinking … We don’t need the NFL, we don’t need any of these woke losers to perform, we can have our own performance.”

Conservatives have objected loudly to the Puerto Rican rapper’s selection to perform at the Super Bowl because has spoken out against Immigration and Customs Enforcement crackdowns, and he has refused to add concerts in the U.S. because he fears federal agents will target his fans.

The 43-year-old broadcaster, who served less than a year as co-chair of the Republican National Committee and has floated her name for open Senate seats, rattled off several conservative-coded musicians, including country star Chris Stapleton and Canadian MAGA rapper Tom MacDonald, before landing on herself.

“You know what? You can throw Lara Trump in there,” she said, throwing her head back and laughing uproariously. “Yeah, that’s right.”

Eric Trump’s wife has tried to conjure up a singing career to mixed reviews, with 10,000 monthly followers on Spotify, but her latest song, “Eyes of God,” has been streamed only 2,731 times, and her most popular song, a cover of Tom Petty’s “I Won’t Back Down,” was widely panned and prompted a cease-and-desist order from the rock legend’s family.

“Trump was in no way authorized to use this song to further a campaign that leaves too many Americans and common sense behind,” Petty’s family said in a statement after she performed the song at a 2020 rally in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

Her recently released song, “Hero,” garnered brutal reviews online.

Our Analysis:

A Spectacle of Delusion: Lara Trump’s Super Bowl Fantasy

In a recent episode that seems ripped from the pages of The Twilight Zone, Lara Trump, the daughter-in-law of President Donald Trump, has floated the idea of herself as a performer in an alternative conservative counterprogramming to the Super Bowl halftime show. This suggestion came about during her podcast, “Right View With Lara Trump,” after a listener inquired about her choice for a conservative rebuttal to Bad Bunny’s anticipated performance at the NFL championship game. The Daily Beast reports that Trump expressed befuddlement over the NFL’s decision, criticizing it for aligning with “woke losers” and suggesting that conservatives don’t need the NFL for entertainment.

Waging War on “Wokeness”

Once again, we see the conservative machine’s gears grinding against the specter of “wokeness,” a term so amorphously defined that it has become a catch-all for any entity or individual that dares to deviate from a certain ideological line. The selection of Bad Bunny, a Puerto Rican rapper known for his outspoken stance against ICE crackdowns and his solidarity with his fans over fear of federal targeting, has apparently ruffled enough feathers to warrant this bizarre counterproposal.

The Lara Trump Show?

Lara Trump’s self-nomination as a halftime performer is as bewildering as it is amusing. Known more for her role as a Fox News host and a brief stint as co-chair of the Republican National Committee, her musical aspirations seem to be a footnote in her career—albeit a footnote she is keen to expand upon. Despite a paltry following on Spotify and a track record that includes a widely panned cover of Tom Petty’s “I Won’t Back Down” (which prompted a cease-and-desist from Petty’s family), Trump appears undeterred, suggesting herself alongside conservative-coded musicians like Chris Stapleton and Canadian MAGA rapper Tom MacDonald.

A Tone-Deaf Proposition

What is striking about this proposition is not just its audacity, but its profound tone-deafness (both musically and socially). Lara Trump’s musical ventures have not been met with acclaim; her latest song, “Hero,” has faced brutal online reviews, and her attempt to cover Tom Petty was met with legal pushback and public disdain. Political commentator Travis Akers’s quip that those exposed to her singing “might be entitled to compensation” underscores the general reception of her musical output.

The Bigger Picture

This episode encapsulates the current state of political and cultural discourse in the United States—a landscape where ideological battles overshadow artistic merit, and where the Super Bowl halftime show, a cultural institution, becomes another front in the culture wars. It’s a manifestation of the politics of grievance, where the selection of a halftime performer is not just a choice but a statement, warranting counterprogramming rooted not in cultural contribution but in cultural combat.

In proposing herself as a performer, Lara Trump may have intended to make a bold statement against what she perceives as the “woke” culture permeating the NFL. Instead, she has unwittingly highlighted the absurdity of politicizing every aspect of American life, down to the entertainment at one of the country’s most unifying events. The Super Bowl, with its spectacle of athleticism, music, and commercials, has long been a rare common ground in an increasingly polarized society. Perhaps it’s time to leave the halftime show out of the culture wars and let music be music.
