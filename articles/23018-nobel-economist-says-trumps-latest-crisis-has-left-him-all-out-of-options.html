Trump Turned Fuel Into a Moral Alibi

The Setup

Paul Krugman’s column, as described in the source, starts with a simple fact pattern: Trump promised cheaper fuel, failed to deliver, and then tried to recast higher prices as patriotic sacrifice. That is the whole trick. The promise was economic; the explanation is now ideological.

Power Is the Point

The relevant power here sits with the White House and Treasury, not with consumers, not with Ukraine, and not with the market fairy tale Trump likes to invoke when it suits him. Trump made the promise. Trump set the frame. Trump is now the one laundering his failure through foreign policy theater and pretending the public should absorb the cost as a gesture of loyalty.

This is not confusion. It is a deliberate conversion of accountability into nationalism.

Blame Goes Downhill

Bessent’s Fox appearance follows the usual pattern of elite misdirection: find a complicated geopolitical event, strip out the context, and present the downstream price effect as if it appeared from nowhere. Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil infrastructure may affect supply, especially diesel. That is real. But the source also makes clear why Ukraine is hitting Russian energy assets in the first place: it is fighting an invasion and trying to weaken the machinery sustaining it.

The administration wants the price impact without the war context. That way, it can imply that Ukrainian resistance, rather than Russian aggression and Trump’s own failures, is the culprit.

The Colonial Logic

The rare-earth proposal is the more revealing detail. Demanding a massive share of Ukraine’s future mineral revenues in exchange for little security or aid is not alliance-building. It is resource extraction with diplomatic stationery. The reported language around Zelenskyy only sharpens the picture: contempt, not strategy, drives the relationship.

Trump and Bessent are not behaving like statesmen managing a hard bargain. They are acting like extraction agents who assume weaker nations exist to pay for their errors.

The Cards They Don’t Hold

The Oval Office humiliation of Zelenskyy, with Trump bragging that Ukraine “doesn’t have the cards,” now reads like a preview of this energy mess. The real problem for Trump is that he also does not have the cards. He cannot command Ukraine to stop defending itself. He cannot command global oil markets into obedience. He cannot turn his broken promise into a win by renaming the failure.

So he does what weak men in power always do: he shifts the story, recruits a scapegoat, and asks the public to mistake damage control for leadership.

What This Story Really Shows

This is the governing pattern of contemporary authoritarian politics: promise abundance, fail, then demand sacrifice while blaming someone less powerful. The point is never competence. The point is submission. If prices rise, the administration wants obedience, not explanation. If the war continues, it wants a bargaining chip, not a policy.

The deeper lesson is brutally simple. When leaders cannot deliver material results, they try to govern through resentment, debt, and manufactured enemies.
