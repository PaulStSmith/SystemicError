The Dragons of the Book Hoard: Librarians in the Age of Censorship

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Once upon a time, in a land not far away, knowledge was sacred. Its guardians were not knights or kings, but quiet figures cloaked in cardigans and ink-dusted spectacles. These were librarians, and they presided over vast hoards of treasure—not gold, but books. Ideas. Histories. Futures.

In Old English, the word for library was bōchord — a "book hoard." It evokes a visceral image: a cavern filled with tomes, manuscripts, and scrolls, each one a sliver of human thought and imagination. By this poetic logic, librarians are the dragons, fierce custodians of civilization's intellectual wealth. And in the United States of 2025, those dragons are under siege.

The GOP and its ideological vassals have weaponized moral panic into a full-blown crusade against libraries. Under the guise of “protecting children,” they're banning books, gutting library budgets, and terrorizing educators. In many states, librarians have become targets of harassment campaigns, with some even facing criminal charges simply for refusing to remove books that challenge heteronormative, whitewashed, patriarchal narratives.

It is not hyperbole to call this a cultural purge. Like all authoritarian movements, the goal isn't just to silence dissent — it's to obliterate the very possibility of dissent. A library, by its nature, is pluralistic. It contains contradictions. It forces readers to engage with complexity. That terrifies the right-wing project, which thrives on binary thinking: us vs. them, good vs. evil, man vs. woman, white vs. other.

To maintain that brittle worldview, the GOP must reduce the library to a curated echo chamber. Books that mention LGBTQ+ identities? “Pornographic.” Novels that explore racism? “Divisive.” History that includes indigenous genocide or slavery? “Un-American.” Every excuse is a lie, and every lie is a stepping stone toward a more obedient, less educated population.

Let us be clear: this is not about protecting children. Children are not harmed by seeing themselves represented in literature. They are harmed by being erased from it. The harm does not come from books about racism, but from the systemic racism that those books expose. The danger is not in reading about gender fluidity, but in living in a society that punishes you for existing outside a binary.

The people pushing these bans know this. They're not stupid. They're strategic. They understand that if you control what people can read, you can control what they think. You can shape memory. Rewrite the past. Corral the future.

And that is why the librarians — the dragons — matter more than ever.

These aren’t just clerks stamping due dates anymore. They are frontline defenders of democracy. They are standing between inquisitive minds and the flames of ignorance. They are housing the banned. Protecting the vulnerable. Telling the truth when it’s dangerous to do so.

Consider the sheer bravery it takes now for a small-town librarian to put Gender Queer or Stamped or The Hate U Give on a shelf, knowing full well that a swarm of pitchforked ideologues might descend upon them. It is the moral courage of these dragons that keeps our hoards alive.

We must support them. That means more than polite applause or wistful memes. It means attending school board meetings. Donating to libraries. Calling out censorship when we see it, not just in Texas or Florida, but everywhere. Because the fire is spreading. And apathy is an accelerant.

We must also reclaim the narrative. If the right can paint librarians as villains, then we must paint them back as what they are: heroes. Mythic ones, even. Let the children grow up believing that to work in a library is to guard the gate to knowledge, to wield the sword of reason, to fly on wings of curiosity.

Let them know that some dragons don’t sit on treasure. They organize it, catalog it, and lend it freely to anyone brave enough to seek it.

The book burners are loud. But the dragons are many. And we remember our history.

When they burned books in Berlin, Brecht asked: what crimes did the authors commit, if any? And what of the librarians who kept copies hidden?

Today, they are hiding books again. In backpacks. In private collections. In Little Free Libraries and encrypted PDFs.

The hoard lives. The dragons endure.

And when the smoke clears, as it always does, it is the knowledge that will remain—scorched perhaps, but not destroyed.

Let them come for the libraries. Let them try.

Because, here, there be dragons.
