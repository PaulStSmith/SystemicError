'A joke!' Lindsey Graham scrambles after urging Trump to trash the Constitution

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 25, 20254 min read

U.S. Senator Lindsey Graham (R-SC) speaks to the media, after a Senate GOP lunch, as Republican lawmakers struggle to pass U.S. President Donald Trump’s sweeping spending and tax bill, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 28, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

International leaders and diplomats gasped and were seen shaking their heads as US President Donald Trump gave his address at the United Nations General Assembly—attacking the UN itself, migration, and climate action—but Sen. Lindsey Graham gave the speech an obsequious review on Fox News Tuesday night, going so far as to say Trump’s performance made a compelling case for him to run for an illegal third term.

“Trump 2028,” Graham (R-SC) told Sean Hannity. “I hope this never ends.”

He repeated Trump’s baseless claim that he has ended seven “unendable” wars since taking office in January and praised the president for “standing up in the UN and telling the world the way it is.”

“We don’t have to live this way,” said Graham, before emphasizing, “I hope he runs again.”

Graham’s comments represented “Republicans just openly calling for unconstitutional, lawless behavior” from the president, said journalist Mehdi Hasan.

Trump is barred from running for a third term by the 22nd Amendment of the US Constitution, which states: “No person shall be elected to the office of the president more than twice.”

Graham has previously called for the president to run for a third term in 2028, saying on the social media platform X, “Trump 2028!” after praising Trump’s address to Congress in March.

The comment was “a joke,” Graham later said.

The president has also made comments about attempting to serve a third term, and Rep. Andy Ogles (R-Tenn.) introduced a resolution days after Trump was sworn in to amend the Constitution, aiming to clear the way for a 2028 presidential run.

Economist Dean Baker of the Center for Economic and Policy Research said the latest comments from Graham—who loudly denounced Trump at the beginning of his political career—suggested that “Trump must have some big-time dirt” on the senator.

“He used to have pretenses of being a serious person,” said Baker.

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Alliance: Trump, Graham, and the Mockery of Constitutional Boundaries

In a recent spectacle at the United Nations General Assembly, US President Donald Trump unleashed a tirade that not only attacked the foundational principles of the UN itself but also took aim at migration and climate action efforts. This brazen display was met with wide-eyed astonishment and disbelief among international leaders and diplomats. Yet, amidst this global dismay, Senator Lindsey Graham found the performance not just acceptable but laudable, giving it an obsequious review on Fox News.

Trump 2028: A Dangerous Fantasy

Graham’s endorsement of Trump for an illegal third term is not only a stark deviation from constitutional fidelity but a glimpse into a dystopian political fantasy. “Trump 2028,” Graham proclaimed on Sean Hannity’s show, a statement that toes the line of authoritarian aspirations with unsettling casualness. This isn’t the first time Graham has floated this unconstitutional balloon. His remarks on the social media platform X, along with a subsequent ‘joke’ defense, reveal a troubling pattern of normalizing lawless suggestions.

The Baseless Bravado

Echoing Trump’s baseless claims of ending seven “unendable” wars, Graham’s praise for Trump’s UN address underscores a detachment from reality that is both dangerous and misleading. The assertion not only distorts Trump’s actual foreign policy achievements but also disrespects the complex, ongoing conflicts that continue to afflict the global community.

The Constitutional Crisis

At the heart of Graham’s endorsement lies a blatant disregard for the 22nd Amendment of the US Constitution, which unequivocally bars any president from being elected more than twice. This advocacy for unconstitutional behavior, as journalist Mehdi Hasan rightly puts it, is an open call for lawlessness at the highest levels of American political discourse.

The Graham Transformation

Economist Dean Baker’s commentary on Graham’s transformation from a Trump critic to a fervent supporter suggests a sinister undercurrent of political blackmail or coercion. Once perceived as a serious political figure, Graham’s recent antics and alignment with Trump’s controversial stances highlight a dramatic, and concerning, ideological shift.

Conclusion: A Mockery of Democracy

The dialogue between Trump, Graham, and their supporters, flirting with the idea of a third Trump term, is not just a challenge to constitutional norms but a mockery of democratic principles. It’s a stark reminder of the fragile state of America’s political integrity, where the whims of a few seek to undermine the foundational laws that govern the republic.

In an era where the truth is often dismissed as ‘fake news,’ and constitutional boundaries are treated as mere suggestions, the voices of reason and accountability are more crucial than ever. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, it is incumbent upon us to challenge these dangerous narratives and defend the constitutional order that underpins American democracy. The specter of a Trump 2028 campaign, whether in jest or earnest, is a dire warning of the systemic erosion of democratic norms and a call to action for all who value the principles upon which the United States was founded.
