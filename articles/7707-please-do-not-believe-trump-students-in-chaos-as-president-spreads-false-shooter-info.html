'Please do not believe Trump': Students in 'chaos' as president spreads false shooter info

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 14, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Donald Trump on Saturday was forced to issue a correction for his own public statement after the president falsely reported information about an active shooter, but the damage may have already been done, according to numerous observers.

An active shooter was reported this weekend at Brown University in Providence, Rhode Island, and news outlets suggested there are multiple victims. CNN also reported the news, publishing an article called, “No suspect in custody after reports of a shooting at Brown University, officials say.”

CNN and MS NOW both reported that no suspect was in custody, despite there being an initial report of an arrest, but Trump posted outdated information on his Truth Social account suggesting there was someone in custody.

Many people warned about Trump causing risks to students on scene, including some students themselves.

PatriotTakes, which purports to track right-wing extremism, said, “At 5:44, Trump posted that the Brown University shooting suspect was in custody 8 minutes later at 5:52, Brown University said the ‘situation remains ongoing. Continue to shelter in place.’”

The account added, “Careless misinformation like this during a school shooting can get people killed.”

“Schools will now have to update their procedures to tell students to ignore Trump’s posts during school shootings,” they wrote. “Hopefully no one was harmed due to Trump’s sharing of false information.”

A user on X, Danny, said, “I am at brown university they have not confirmed a shooter in custody please do not believe trump and stay inside.”

MeidasTouch wrote, “Trump is causing mass confusion right now amid an active shooter situation at Brown University.”

“Trump posted that the suspect was in custody, but Brown University and media reports say that is not true and that the situation remains active,” the account added.

Young Dem influencer Harry Sisson said, “Trump at 5:44 pm tonight ‘I have been briefed on the shooting that took place at Brown University… the suspect is in custody’ Trump at 6:03 pm tonight ‘The suspect is NOT in custody.’ Why is he posting misinformation about this tragedy?”

“People could get killed because of it,” Sisson added.

Self-described “policy wonk” Maine said, “Trump contradicting Brown University and Providence PD: Suspect in Custody. Absolute Chaos.

Our Analysis:

The Reckless Dissemination of Misinformation by Donald Trump: A Critical Analysis

In a world where the rapid spread of information is both a boon and a bane, the responsibility of public figures in disseminating accurate information cannot be overstated. The recent debacle involving Donald Trump and his premature announcement regarding an active shooter situation at Brown University highlights a dangerous penchant for carelessness that could have had dire consequences.

The Faux Pas

Donald Trump, on his Truth Social account, prematurely claimed that a suspect in the Brown University shooting was in custody, a statement that proved to be grossly inaccurate. This misinformation spread quickly, setting off a chain reaction of confusion and potential risk to those on the ground, including students sheltering in place, awaiting clear instructions from credible sources.

The Correction and Its Implications

Trump was forced to issue a correction, stating that the suspect was not in custody, a stark turnaround from his initial claim. This incident raises serious concerns about the former president’s credibility and the potential harm his posts can cause in critical situations. It’s not merely about the embarrassment of having to retract a statement; it’s about the real danger that such misinformation can pose to individuals caught in life-threatening scenarios.

The Response from Observers

Observers and critics were quick to point out the ramifications of Trump’s actions. Accounts like PatriotTakes highlighted how misinformation during a school shooting could have fatal consequences. The suggestion that schools might need to update their procedures to include ignoring Trump’s posts during such crises is both astonishing and deeply troubling. It underscores the gravity of the misinformation issue and the unique challenges posed by social media in times of crisis.

The Broader Context

This incident is symptomatic of a larger issue at play: the recklessness of influential figures in handling sensitive information. Trump’s actions, whether borne out of a desire to be the first to report or a gross oversight, underscore a flagrant disregard for the principles of responsible communication. In an era where fake news and misinformation can spread like wildfire, the onus is on leaders and public figures to verify information before sharing it.

Conclusion

The incident at Brown University serves as a stark reminder of the potential consequences of irresponsible information dissemination. Donald Trump’s premature announcement not only caused confusion and panic but also highlighted the inherent risks associated with high-profile individuals carelessly wielding their influence. As we move forward, it is imperative that all individuals, especially those in positions of power, exercise due diligence in their communications, especially during crises. The spread of misinformation, particularly in times of emergency, is not just a faux pas—it’s a dangerous act that could have irreparable consequences.

In a world fraught with uncertainty and peril, the least we can do is strive for accuracy and truth, especially when lives could be at stake.
