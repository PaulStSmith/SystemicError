'More expensive': Trump’s economic policies are giving this Upstate NY farmer a rude awakening

By Paulo Santos

Canada has a long history of being one of the United States' closest allies and a major trading partner, but U.S. President Donald Trump's steep tariffs are creating considerable friction between the two countries. And economists are warning that the tariffs will be a huge expense for homeowners and the real estate industry, as the U.S. gets a lot of building materials from Canada.

American farmers are feeling the pinch as well, including Nicholas Gilbert in Upstate New York.

Gilbert, according to Syracuse.com reporter Geoff Herbert, owns Adon Farms — a dairy farm in Potsdam, New York, which is about 250 miles from Toronto — and has about 1400 cows. The farmer gets his livestock feed from Ontario, Canada to his north, and he was shocked to find out that a recent order of feed cost him $2200 more than he had been paying.

Gibert told The Atlantic, "I'm not even sure it's legal. We contracted for the price on delivery. If your price of fuel goes up or your truck breaks down, that's not my problem. That's what the contract's for."

But Herbert notes that the tariff was, in fact, legal, and that it is Gilbert's "responsibility" to pay the additional cost.

"Tariffs are paid by domestic importers, not foreign exporters, despite Trump's frequent claims otherwise," Herbert reports in a Syracuse.com article published on April 16. "And according to The Atlantic, Adon Farms is doubly stuck with the added cost because the price of the milk Gilbert sells is set by a local co-op and there are no U.S. suppliers nearby. Buying feed somewhere else would be more expensive, he said."

Herbert adds, "It's the latest in a growing number of businesses caught in Trump's trade war. A small packing and shipping business in Washington State announced, last week, it was shutting down because Canadian customers stopped using it due to tariffs and Trump's repeated comments about annexing Canada…. Gilbert said some larger companies may be able to pass costs on to consumers by raising prices. But he can't, and it's only going to get harder with levies on fertilizer or farm equipment."

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Tangle: A Closer Look at Trump's Trade War and Its Victims

The recent article under scrutiny brings to light an often overlooked aspect of international trade policies: the very real and immediate impact of tariffs on small business owners and, by extension, on the broader economy. With President Donald Trump's imposition of steep tariffs creating a rift between the United States and Canada—a nation long considered one of the U.S.'s closest allies—the consequences have begun to ripple through sectors that many might not initially consider. Among those hit hardest are homeowners, the real estate industry, and American farmers, with the case of Nicholas Gilbert, a dairy farmer in Upstate New York, serving as a poignant example.

The Real Cost of Tariffs

Gilbert's narrative is not just a story about increased operational costs; it's a testament to the broader implications of a trade policy that seems to be more of a blunt instrument than a surgical tool. The fact that Gilbert was taken aback by a sudden $2200 increase in his feed order from Canada speaks volumes about the disconnect between policy decisions made at the highest levels of government and their repercussions on the ground.

Trump's tariffs, while perhaps intended to bolster American industry by making foreign goods more expensive and thus less attractive, have instead painted a target on the backs of domestic importers. The article rightly points out that, contrary to Trump's claims, it's these importers who bear the brunt of the costs, not the foreign exporters. This distinction is crucial and undermines the administration's narrative that tariffs are a weapon against foreign economies.

The Legal Quagmire and Economic Incompetence

Gilbert's assertion that the price hike might be illegal because it contradicts the terms of his delivery contract, while understandable from a business owner's perspective, misses the mark legally. As noted, tariffs are indeed a responsibility of the domestic importer. However, this legal reality does not absolve the policy of its shortsightedness. It's a stark example of what the article refers to as Trump's "naked economic incompetence," a phrase that, while harsh, is not without merit given the circumstances.

The Bigger Picture

Beyond the individual story of Gilbert and his dairy farm, the article sheds light on the broader economic fallout from such tariffs. The closure of a small packing and shipping business in Washington State, due to lost Canadian customers, is a microcosm of the potential for widespread economic damage. The ripple effects of these policies can lead to job losses, increased consumer prices, and strained international relations—not just with Canada, but globally.

Moreover, Gilbert's predicament illustrates a critical flaw in the tariff strategy. Smaller businesses, which lack the financial resilience or market power to adjust prices or absorb costs, find themselves in an untenable position. This is especially true in industries like agriculture, where the price of goods is often not determined by market forces but by co-ops or regulatory bodies.

Conclusion: A Misguided Policy with Real Victims

In sum, the article offers a compelling glimpse into the unintended consequences of Trump's trade tariffs. By focusing on the plight of Nicholas Gilbert and others in similar situations, it highlights the gap between the theoretical benefits of protective tariffs and the harsh realities they impose on American businesses and consumers.

The discussion around tariffs and trade policies must consider these on-the-ground impacts. Economics is not just numbers on a paper; it's about people, livelihoods, and communities. As such, policies need to be crafted with a nuanced understanding of their potential effects, rather than as broad strokes painted with the brush of nationalism or economic isolationism. The story of Adon Farms is a cautionary tale, one that should prompt a reevaluation of current trade policies and a move towards more thoughtful, targeted approaches that do not sacrifice the many for the supposed benefit of the few.
