'A confession': Onlookers stunned by new Trump military move that 'makes no sense'

Trump over the weekend took the time to brag about a military strike, saying it was an “honor” while announcing that two were killed in the strike on a submarine. The New York Times also released a report focusing on the two survivors of the strike on the purported drug vessel, who were not arrested and were instead reportedly returned to their home countries.

It’s that report that baffled political and international law experts.

Conservative attorney and anti-Trump activist George Conway said Saturday, “This is essentially a confession by administration that it committed murder.”

Immigration policy expert Aaron Reichlin-Melnick chimed in: “So the people on these boats are ‘terrorists’ who can be killed without any due process, but if they don’t die in the initial strike, they just get released and go home with a warning? Seriously?”

Former Obama staffer Tommy Vietor said, “So the administration’s argument is that these men are dangerous narco-terrorists waging war against the US and thus deserved to be executed, but also that the US should send them home rather than to try prosecute them in court. Makes sense.”

Political science professor Christopher Clary also added, “Enough evidence to try and kill them but not enough evidence to prosecute… what are we doing here exactly?”

Political attorney Robert Kelner also weighed in, saying, “This makes no sense, if we are supposed to believe this was a drug trafficking ship and the attack on the ship was legal.”

“If that were so, why wouldn’t we detain the evil drug traffickers and prosecute them?” Kelner asked. “Congress needs to exercise its oversight authority. And reporters need to dig into this story.”

Our Analysis:

A Stark Contrast in Actions and Logic

The recent decision by Donald Trump’s administration not to arrest two suspected peddlers of fentanyl, juxtaposed with the administration’s willingness to brag about a military strike on a submarine, which resulted in two deaths, is a glaring example of the chaotic and often contradictory approach that characterizes much of Trump’s tenure. This decision has not only shocked observers but has also raised substantial concerns among political, legal, and international law experts.

The Confusion Unraveled

The New York Times’ revelation about the survivors of the strike, who were not arrested but instead reportedly sent back to their home countries, is baffling, to say the least. It underlines a disconcerting dissonance in the administration’s approach to what it perceives as threats. On one hand, there’s a display of military might against supposed narco-terrorists; on the other, a bewildering leniency that contradicts the administration’s own narrative of danger and justice.

Expert Opinions Highlight the Absurdity

The comments from figures like George Conway, Aaron Reichlin-Melnick, Tommy Vietor, Christopher Clary, and Robert Kelner underscore the bafflement and outrage that this incident has sparked. The consensus among these observers points to a stark inconsistency: the notion that these individuals were dangerous enough to warrant a lethal strike, yet not dangerous enough to arrest and prosecute.

  • George Conway’s assertion that this amounts to a confession of murder by the administration is particularly damning. It strips down the action to its most basic and horrifying implication: that the administration believes it can act as judge, jury, and executioner, without the due process guaranteed by law.

  • Aaron Reichlin-Melnick and Tommy Vietor raise critical points about the supposed logic (or lack thereof) behind treating these individuals as “terrorists” worthy of execution without trial, but then deeming them harmless enough to send home with merely a “warning.”

  • Christopher Clary and Robert Kelner focus on the legal and procedural absurdities. Clary’s remark on the lack of evidence for prosecution, despite sufficient evidence to attempt killing, and Kelner’s question regarding the rationale behind not detaining and prosecuting the alleged traffickers, highlight a bewildering inconsistency in policy and practice.

The Larger Picture

This incident is emblematic of the larger issues plaguing the Trump administration: a penchant for theatrical displays of force, a disregard for international law and norms, and a troubling inclination to sidestep the judicial process. It raises serious questions about accountability, the value placed on human life, and the integrity of the United States’ justice system.

The Need for Oversight and Accountability

Robert Kelner’s call for Congress to exercise its oversight authority and for reporters to dig deeper into this story is a crucial reminder of the need for transparency and accountability in government actions. This incident should not be swept under the rug or lost amid the daily tumult of news. It demands scrutiny, and it requires answers.

In a functioning democracy, actions taken in the name of national security must be balanced with respect for human rights and due process. The administration’s decision in this case is a stark reminder of the dangers posed when those in power are allowed to circumvent those principles without consequence.
