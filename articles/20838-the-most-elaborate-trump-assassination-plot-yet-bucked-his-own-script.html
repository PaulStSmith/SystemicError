The most elaborate Trump assassination plot yet bucked his own script

Based on an article by Jordan Green, Investigative Reporter

Paulo SantosJuly 15, 20263 min read

Trump’s Terror Map Is Built to Mislead

The Source Material

The reporting describes a real federal case: eight men were indicted over an alleged plot involving drones, rooftops, and planned killings of high-profile targets, while other people caught in the investigation faced arrests or raids for adjacent online activity. It also shows the Trump White House pushing a counterterrorism strategy that elevates “violent left-wing extremists” while leaving white supremacist violence out of the frame.

Power Writes the Threat List

The central fact is not the chaos of the defendants’ online beliefs. It is that the White House and its political appointees decide what the federal government says counts as a terror threat. Trump ordered the FBI to investigate antifascists. Sebastian Gorka’s strategy then formalized the insult to reality by centering imagined left-wing menace and erasing the long, documented history of racial and ethnically motivated violence.

That is not an analytical mistake. It is a political decision. When the administration chooses to spotlight antifascists while skipping white supremacist violence, it is not being confused. It is choosing a narrative that protects its own movement from scrutiny.

The Actual Pattern Is Far-Right, Anti-Government Violence

The people described in the reporting do not fit the administration’s cartoon. Their grievances are a mix of anti-government rage, anti-elite paranoia, antisemitism, anti-Israel fixation, religious extremism, and conspiracy culture. That is not a left-right blur. It is the old American far-right ecosystem mutating in real time.

The source even says this plainly through expert analysis: anti-government extremists have long targeted both Democratic and Republican administrations, and the MAGA world is now part of the establishment they once claimed to oppose. That matters. Once a movement captures state power, its own militants stop seeing the president’s party as an outside force. They see it as compromised, corrupt, and punishable.

ICE Is Not a Fixable Institution

The reporting also shows how the state uses ICE as a magnet for grievance and then acts shocked when violence follows. A defendant’s bio included “F--- ICE.” Another message referred to strikes on “ICE camps.” The federal government’s answer is not to question the legitimacy of a deportation machine that invites this politics. It is to widen surveillance and criminalize more people around the edges.

That should not be read as a problem of training or oversight. ICE is an enforcement arm built to terrorize vulnerable communities, normalize cruelty, and turn human beings into targets. It should be abolished. Anything less keeps the machinery intact and preserves the same grievance-producing state violence under a cleaner public-relations wrapper.

The Dragnet Is the Point

The most revealing part of the story is how quickly an alleged plot becomes a permission structure for broad repression. The FBI’s device searches, the widening inquiry, the arrest of people only tangentially connected, the raid on the “armed leftist” TikTok user, the offer of informant money, the use of social media scraps as guilt-adjacent evidence: this is how the security state expands. It does not need precision if it can get fear.

The administration gets two benefits at once. It can pretend it is confronting domestic extremism while keeping its ideological enemies centered in public rhetoric. And it can use the investigation to build a larger file on anyone inconvenient enough to fit an online profile. That is not neutral law enforcement. It is political sorting with guns, warrants, and federal databases.

The Real Story Is State Misdirection

The deepest scandal here is not that extremists are incoherent. Extremists always are. It is that the federal government is coherent enough to know better and dishonest enough to say otherwise. It knows the far-right remains the more durable source of domestic terror. It knows anti-government conspiracism keeps flowing through Republican-aligned politics and into violence. It knows the MAGA movement is not a victim of this ecosystem; it is one of its main outlets.

But the White House chose propaganda over honesty. It pointed federal machinery toward antifascists, erased white supremacist violence from its own strategy, and then acted as if the resulting contradiction were an innocent oversight. It was not. It was an attempt to define reality in a way that protects power, launders the movement’s own extremism, and keeps the state pointed at the wrong enemies.
