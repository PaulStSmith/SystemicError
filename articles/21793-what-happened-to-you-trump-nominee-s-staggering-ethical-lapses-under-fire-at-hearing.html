'What happened to you?' Trump nominee's 'staggering' ethical lapses under fire at hearing

When the Attorney General Becomes the Client’s Cleanup Crew

Power Moved, Fast

Todd Blanche is not some detached caretaker of the public interest. He is Donald Trump’s former lawyer sitting in the Justice Department, now defending a settlement that insulated Trump, his family, and his businesses from IRS scrutiny. That is the power structure here: the president’s personal legal world has been folded into the machinery of the state, and the machinery was asked to protect him.

The Settlement Was the Story

The core fact is simple. In May, Blanche signed a deal that permanently barred the IRS from auditing Trump or pursuing past tax claims against him, his family, or his businesses. A federal judge in Miami voided it on Monday and referred Blanche to the New York State Bar. That is not an innocent paperwork dispute. It is a court saying the agreement was a sham.

Misdirection as a Shield

Blanche’s defense in the hearing did what this kind of defense always does: it narrowed the issue until the abuse looked technical. When Sen. Adam Schiff pressed him on whether the settlement also helped shield Trump from liability tied to his criminal conviction for falsifying business records, Blanche tried to hide behind a fake distinction about what Trump was convicted of. But Schiff was pointing at the larger truth: if Trump’s network used falsified records to cheat on taxes, then a blanket IRS shield is not neutral administration. It is interference.

The Personal Becomes Institutional

This is the corruption pattern in plain view. Blanche was asked to represent the IRS and the Justice Department, but he had already represented Trump personally. Schiff called the deal what it was: self-dealing. Blanche’s response was the standard bureaucratic lie of captured institutions, the claim that “we made the decision to settle the case” as if collective procedure disinfects a decision designed to advantage one man.

The Old Prosecutor Is Not the Point

Schiff’s reference to Blanche’s past as a respected federal prosecutor matters less as a morality tale than as evidence of institutional decay. The issue is not whether Blanche is “the same person.” The issue is what happens when a former Trump lawyer is installed in a position where his loyalty can be tested against the public interest. The answer, in this case, was immediate: he chose Trump.

Conclusion: State Power in Private Hands

This story is not about a bad settlement in isolation. It is about how authoritarian politics works when it gets inside the state: loyalists convert public authority into personal protection, then dress the result up as legal normalcy. The court voided the deal, but the deeper damage is already visible. When the Justice Department can be used as a refuge for a president’s liabilities, the problem is not one ethical lapse. It is a captured institution pretending to still be a public one.
