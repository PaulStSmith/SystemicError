Trump's top negotiator called on the carpet for baffling Russia claim: 'They lie'

Steve Witkoff on CNBC (MS NOW screenshot)

A stumbling answer by Donald Trump’s lead international negotiator on Tuesday was the subject of both amusement and disbelief on MS NOW, with the entire panel wondering if the Trump hire, a commercial real estate investor, is way over his head.

During an appearance on CNBC, Steve Witkoff, who is the lead White House diplomat in negotiations dealing with both the war in Ukraine and the US war on Iran, was asked about alarming reports that Russia is giving assistance to Iran on targeting US servicemembers.

Witkoff replied, “I can tell you that yesterday, on the call with [President Trump], the Russians said they have not been sharing,” paused and added, “That’s what they said. So, we can take them at their word, but they did say that.”

He then curiously continued, “So, I, you know, that’s a better question for the intel people, but let’s hope that they’re not sharing.”

After playing the clip on “Morning Joe,” an amused Joe Scarborough immediately pounced, looked at the camera and lectured, “You know, diplomats, you got to work with both sides. But I will just say, if Steve is watching, Steve, they lie. This is what Russians do. They think it’s the smart move to always lie, lie. That’s the very nature of it. They are disruptors. They count on American suckers to think that they’re going to win them over by force of personality. It never happens.”

“Steve Witkoff just said, I have to take Vladimir Putin at his word. I have to take the KGB agent at his word. And remember, Steve Witkoff is the lead negotiator in the Russia-Ukraine talks, among many other places,” he added.

Co-host Willie Geist offered, “Just remember, as we think about why, perhaps we’re not giving as much aid as we should be to Ukraine in the eyes of many people. The guy leading the negotiations says you have to take Russia at their word.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Witkoff Fiasco: Naivety or a Dangerous Game?

In a recent spectacle that has both amused and alarmed many, Steve Witkoff, a figure whose experience is rooted in commercial real estate rather than international diplomacy, stumbled through his role as the Trump administration’s lead negotiator on critical international conflicts. This incident, showcased on MS NOW, reveals not just a personal failure but a systemic flaw in the administration’s approach to international relations and conflict negotiation.

A Commercial Realist in a Diplomat’s Shoes

Witkoff’s background as a commercial real estate investor is a bizarre foundation for his current role in negotiating intricate international matters, such as the war in Ukraine and US-Iran tensions. His lack of experience was glaringly evident in a recent CNBC interview, where he awkwardly attempted to address questions about Russia’s alleged support to Iran in targeting US servicemembers. His response was not only hesitant but alarmingly naive, suggesting a reliance on Russia’s word amidst a complex geopolitical conflict.

The Dangers of Taking Russia “At Their Word”

Witkoff’s assertion that “the Russians said they have not been sharing” intelligence with Iran is not just a stumble; it’s a fall into a dangerous pit of wishful thinking. As Joe Scarborough rightly pointed out, relying on the word of a nation known for its strategic disinformation campaigns is not just foolish; it’s perilous. Russia’s history of deceit and disruption in international affairs is well-documented, making Witkoff’s trust in their assurances a clear indication of his unsuitability for the role.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection of Trump’s Administration

This fiasco is not merely about Witkoff’s ineptitude; it’s a reflection of a broader issue within the Trump administration: the appointment of individuals to critical roles for which they are blatantly unqualified. The decision to entrust complex international negotiations to a commercial real estate investor with no diplomatic experience is a systemic error that undermines the United States’ position and credibility on the world stage.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Competence

Witkoff’s bumbling attempt to address serious geopolitical issues serves as a stark reminder of the importance of experience and expertise in diplomacy. The Trump administration’s habit of placing unqualified individuals in high-stakes roles poses a significant threat to national and global security. It’s imperative for the administration to reassess its criteria for such appointments, prioritizing competence and experience over loyalty or business acumen. The world is not a real estate deal, and it’s high time the administration recognized that fact.
