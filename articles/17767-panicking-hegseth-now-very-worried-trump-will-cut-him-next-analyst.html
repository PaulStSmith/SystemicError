Panicking Hegseth now 'very worried' Trump will cut him next: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Politics of Paranoia in the Pentagon

The Power Play Behind the Scenes

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s recent actions, including the firing of Navy Secretary John Phelan, reveal a deeper narrative of fear-driven power consolidation within the highest echelons of the United States defense establishment. Hegseth, according to analyst Tom Nichols, is motivated by a concern that President Donald Trump might dismiss him. His strategy has involved surrounding himself with loyalists who would oppose his removal. This not only solidifies his position but also reflects a broader, troubling trend of leadership based on loyalty rather than competence or integrity.

Manufacturing Loyalty through Fear

Hegseth’s actions are a textbook example of creating a climate of fear to engender loyalty. By repopulating the Pentagon with figures who are indebted to him for their positions, Hegseth ensures a buffer against potential threats to his tenure. This tactic is indicative of a leader using fear as a tool to maintain control, a strategy that can destabilize the very institution he is meant to lead. The focus on loyalty over merit risks compromising the effectiveness and moral compass of the Pentagon, potentially impacting national security.

Weaponizing “Anti-Woke” Rhetoric

Coupled with his loyalty campaign is Hegseth’s push of an “anti-woke” agenda. This is not merely a cultural war issue but a strategic maneuver to align the Pentagon more closely with the ideological preferences of the current administration, thereby securing his position. By labeling opposing views as “woke,” Hegseth delegitimizes dissent and masks his authoritarian approach as a fight against political correctness. This misdirection serves to consolidate power by dismissing and discrediting opposition.

Palace Politics as Usual?

Nichols describes Hegseth’s approach as typical “palace politics,” a term that evokes images of historical monarchies rather than a modern democratic institution. This analogy is apt as it highlights the regression to a form of governance where personal allegiance to a leader trumps allegiance to the principles the institution stands for. In this environment, fear and favor are currencies more valuable than expertise and ethical governance.

Consequences of Cultist Leadership

The immediate effect of Hegseth’s purge and paranoia is a more insular Pentagon, less responsive to genuine threats and more concerned with internal loyalty tests. Long term, this could erode institutional integrity, lower morale, and diminish the global standing of the United States as a leader in military professionalism and ethical conduct. Hegseth’s actions may protect his position temporarily, but they undermine the very foundations of effective defense leadership.

Systemic Insight: A Broader Pattern of Decay

Hegseth’s tenure at the Pentagon is symptomatic of a broader systemic problem in American politics: the prioritization of personal power over public service. This pattern of decay threatens to transform robust institutions into hollow echo chambers of a single leader’s fears and ambitions. The real danger here is not just the instability of Hegseth’s position, but the instability his leadership style introduces into one of the most critical institutions in the United States. As this trend continues, the resilience of American democratic institutions is steadily undermined, replaced by the whims of those in power who act not out of duty, but out of fear and self-preservation.
