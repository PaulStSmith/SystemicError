The 'elite' team protecting Trump keeps getting smaller and smaller

Trump’s Defense Team Is Not Shrinking. It Is Rotating Into Power.

The source material, briefly

Politico reports that Sullivan & Cromwell’s Trump defense team has been whittled down to one partner, after four lawyers left for rival firms or were elevated into Trump’s administration or judiciary. The remaining team is handling appeals tied to the E. Jean Carroll defamation verdict, the Manhattan felony conviction, and the New York fraud case against the Trump Organization.

The real holder of power

The interesting fact is not that Trump’s legal team is unstable. The interesting fact is that Trump can turn elite legal service into a pipeline for appointments, status, and leverage. Lawyers do not simply “leave” Trump’s orbit in this story. They are rewarded by it. One becomes Manhattan U.S. attorney. Another gets a lifetime appellate judgeship. Others land at a rival firm with their reputations intact.

That is power working exactly as designed for the powerful. Trump remains the client, but he also remains the dispenser of career gravity. The legal world treats proximity to him as both contamination and opportunity, which is why it keeps circling back.

Who enabled what

The institutions doing the enabling are not confused. Sullivan & Cromwell gave Trump a high-prestige legal shield in civil and criminal matters. Trump’s administration and judicial appointments then absorbed some of the same lawyers who had been defending him. That is not an accident of staffing. It is the mechanism.

The story makes the lawyers’ movement look like a personnel issue. It is really an accountability issue. The same elite legal ecosystem that files briefs to overturn Trump’s judgments also produces the officials and judges who will help shape the terrain around him. The revolving door is not a metaphor here. It is the operating system.

The framing problem

The softer version of this story would suggest Trump’s legal troubles are just generating turnover, as if the issue were workload, burnout, or firm politics. That framing is too polite for what is happening. These are not neutral professional transitions. They are strategic alignments around a man who has been found liable and convicted, and who still attracts institutional deference from firms eager for access and lawyers eager for advancement.

Even the reported backlash inside the firm matters less as scandal than as evidence of how normalized this arrangement has become. The discomfort is internal. The costs remain external, borne by the rule of law that gets treated like a prestige market.

The pattern underneath

This is the Trump era in miniature: elite institutions absorb democratic damage by wrapping it in professionalism. Law firms sell legitimacy. Courts absorb pressure. Appointees move between private service and public power. The public is asked to see all of this as routine, when it is really the conversion of institutional capacity into personal insulation.

Trump does not need loyalty from every lawyer. He only needs enough ambitious people to keep treating his defense as a career opportunity. That is how authoritarian politics is normalized inside respectable institutions: not only by zealots, but by the talented people who decide the rules are flexible when the rewards are large enough.

The deeper lesson

The story is not that Trump’s defense team is thinning. It is that the legal and political class around him remains porous to him. The system keeps producing professionals who can defend the man, serve the man, and then call it ordinary. That is the corruption worth naming: not chaos, but accommodation.
