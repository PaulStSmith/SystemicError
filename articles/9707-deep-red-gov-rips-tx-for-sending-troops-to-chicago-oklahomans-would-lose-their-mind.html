Deep red gov rips TX for sending troops to Chicago: 'Oklahomans would lose their mind'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Pot Calling the Kettle Black: A Deep Dive Into Hypocrisy and State Rights

Republican Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt said “Oklahomans would lose their mind” if Illinois sent National Guard troops to his state, marking the first time a Republican governor has questioned the interstate deployment of guard troops. (Photo: Screen capture)

A deep red state governor slammed Texas for sending National Guard troops to Chicago, saying “Oklahomans would lose their mind” if Illinois were to do the same to his state.

Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt, chair of the National Governors Association, said the move is overstepping state rights in a New York Times interview on Thursday. His comments “marked the first time a Republican governor has questioned the interstate deployment of National Guard troops over a governor’s objections.”

“We believe in the federalist system — that’s states’ rights,” he told The Times, “Oklahomans would lose their mind if Pritzker in Illinois sent troops down to Oklahoma during the Biden administration.”

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker has urged other Democratic and Republican governors to oppose the deployment of guard troops.

Stitt said he supports President Donald Trump’s move to protect ICE agents and restore “law and order” to cities. But he also indicates he worries that these decisions to bring guard troops could carry a precedent — or be carried out in the future by a president from another party.

In his interview with The Times, Stitt said, “Trump should have moved to federalize the troops in Illinois first.”

“I was surprised that [Texas Republican Gov. Greg] Abbott sent troops from Texas to Illinois,” Stitt said. “Abbott and I sued the Biden administration when the shoe was on the other foot and the Biden administration was trying to force us to vaccinate all of our soldiers and force masks across the country.”

He said he hasn’t spoken with Abbott about the deployment; however, they would both be at the University of Oklahoma and the University of Texas football game this weekend.

“As a federalist believer, one governor against another governor, I don’t think that’s the right way to approach this,” he said.

Pritzker and California Gov. Gavin Newsom have threatened to leave the 100-year-old bipartisan National Governors Association if the organization does not speak up against the Texas guard deployment in the Windy City.

The association has not commented yet, and Stitt said that it’s not his role to come out against the Trump administration and the Texas governor’s decision.

“The N.G.A. is an educational organization under I.R.S. code,” Stitt said. “And so we’re not going to be weighing into the politics. That’s not our lane.”

Our Analysis:

The Pot Calling the Kettle Black: A Deep Dive Into Hypocrisy and State Rights

In a recent development that can only be described as theatre of the absurd, Oklahoma Governor Kevin Stitt, a staunch Republican and chair of the National Governors Association, has voiced his concern over Texas sending National Guard troops to Chicago. This move, according to Stitt, is a gross overstepping of state rights, a stance that would be laudable if it weren’t dripping with hypocrisy.

Overstepping State Rights or Political Theatre?

Stitt’s outcry, as reported by The New York Times, marks the first instance of a Republican governor questioning the interstate deployment of National Guard troops over another governor’s objections. The irony is thick here, considering Stitt’s past endorsement of policies under the Trump administration that were equally invasive of state autonomy. His sudden defense of federalism, stating, “Oklahomans would lose their mind if Pritzker in Illinois sent troops down to Oklahoma during the Biden administration,” is a classic case of political convenience.

Selective Federalism

Stitt’s selective embrace of federalism is most apparent when he mentions his support for President Donald Trump’s move to protect ICE agents and restore “law and order” to cities. This selective federalism only applies when the actions align with his political beliefs, exposing a blatant double standard. The concern that these decisions “to bring guard troops could carry a precedent” is valid but laughably late. The precedent was set long ago, with Stitt and his allies often on the side of federal overreach when it suited their purposes.

A Misguided Approach to Bipartisanship

Further complicating this farce, Stitt mentions his surprise at Texas Governor Greg Abbott’s decision to send troops to Illinois, referencing their past legal battles against the Biden administration’s health mandates. This comparison is not only apples to oranges; it’s deceitful. It attempts to equate public health measures with the militarization of state disputes, a dangerous conflation.

Moreover, Stitt’s admission of not having discussed the deployment with Abbott, yet planning to meet at a football game, trivializes the gravity of the situation. It portrays a lack of serious dialogue and coordination among Republican governors on matters of national security and state autonomy.

Hiding Behind the National Governors Association

Stitt’s assertion that the National Governors Association (N.G.A.), being an educational organization, should not weigh into politics is perhaps the most disingenuous of all his claims. The N.G.A., by virtue of its composition and influence, is inherently political. For Stitt to hide behind its tax status to avoid condemning an unprecedented and controversial action speaks volumes of his, and by extension, the N.G.A.’s, moral and political cowardice.

Conclusion

Governor Kevin Stitt’s criticisms of Texas’ decision to send National Guard troops to Chicago under the guise of defending state rights is a stark reminder of the hypocrisy that pervades political discourse today. His selective federalism, coupled with a refusal to engage in meaningful conversation or critique, underscores a broader issue of political expediency over principled governance. As Illinois Governor JB Pritzker and California Governor Gavin Newsom threaten to leave the N.G.A., one must wonder if this organization can or will ever take a stand on matters that profoundly affect the very fabric of our federalist system.

In the end, Stitt’s comments not only fail to address the root issues at hand but also expose a dangerous precedent for future interstate relations and the integrity of state sovereignty. The real question we should be asking is not whether states have the right to send troops to one another, but why our political leaders are resorting to such drastic measures in the first place.
