Trump admin is about to make Iran's 'escalation trap' much more dangerous: expert

By Paulo Santos

A political scientist said on Saturday that the Trump administration’s “stunning” gaps in its Iran strategy just made Iran’s “escalation trap” much more dangerous.

Robert Pape, a political scientist at the University of Chicago, argued during a recent interview on MS NOW’s “Alex Witt Reports” that if the Trump administration decides to deploy ground troops in Iran, then the regime will start killing civilians in other countries like France and Germany with their long-range missiles. Pape said Iran has been telegraphing this move with its posture, and it isn’t clear whether the Trump administration has picked up on this signal yet. Pape described these steps in a recent Substack essay as Iran’s “escalation trap.”

“What you’re seeing is Iran is shifting,” Pape said on Saturday. “The first bombs killed leaders, but hardened the regime. Then Iran has shifted to horizontal escalation, taking the Strait of Hormuz, as we’re now about to launch operations to take it back, they’re telling us they’re going to start killing civilians.”

Pape’s comments come at a time when the Trump administration is weighing “several options” for dealing with the fallout of the Iran war, which includes sending ground troops to the country, NBC News reported. He argued that it is “really stunning” that the Trump administration appeared unprepared for Iran’s current escalation, which suggests further escalations may be more deadly.

“We will likely lose many more [people] than the 13 military personnel that we’ve lost so far,” Pape said about a hypothetical decision by the Trump administration to deploy ground troops. “But that’s focusing on our side. From Iran’s perspective, they will fight those tactical battles, but they’re showing, and they’re telling. Watch out for those malls. Watch out for Western tourists and the hotels.”

Pape added that Iran’s recent strike on a joint U.S.-U.K. base in Diego Garcia shows that the regime is capable of escalating the conflict.

“There are many more like that that can hit Rome, can hit Paris, and can hit Berlin,” Pape said. “So, they are explaining very clearly with their behavior, not just their words, that if we do ground force operations in stage three, they’ve got another plan for stage three, and that is indiscriminate casualties on civilians.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Escalation Trap

The narrative surrounding the Trump administration’s handling of the Iran situation, as discussed by political scientist Robert Pape, requires a careful unpacking to identify where responsibility and power truly lie, and to address the potential misdirection of blame.

Identifying Institutional Power

First, it’s crucial to highlight that the institutional power in the situation described revolves around the executive branch of the U.S. government, specifically under the Trump administration. The decision-making authority concerning foreign policy, military action, and strategies to address international conflicts rests with the President, advised by his cabinet and military advisors. This setup places the Trump administration at the center of the decisions leading to the current state of escalated tensions with Iran.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

The decision to potentially deploy ground troops in Iran, as discussed in the article, is a significant strategic move that would have been made by the Trump administration. According to Pape, this decision appears to be made without fully considering or understanding Iran’s signaled responses, including the severe threat of attacking civilians in third countries via long-range missiles. Pape’s critique points to a gap in strategy and preparedness on the part of the Trump administration, suggesting a lack of comprehension of Iran’s “escalation trap.”

Framing and Misdirection of Responsibility

The article frames the situation as a failure of the Trump administration to anticipate and prepare for Iran’s strategic shift. While it’s vital to scrutinize the actions and decisions of those in power, it’s equally important to consider the broader context of the U.S.-Iran relationship, historical interventions in the region, and the complex geopolitical dynamics at play. However, the critique seems well-placed in this instance. The responsibility for military strategy, understanding the enemy’s capabilities and intentions, and protecting civilian lives—both domestic and foreign—undeniably rests with the administration in power. The framing does not misdirect responsibility; it squarely places it on the Trump administration’s decision-makers who hold the institutional power to act and react to international threats.

Conclusion

The article’s focus on the Trump administration’s alleged unpreparedness for Iran’s strategic escalation is not misplaced. Those in positions of power within the administration are responsible for the decisions that lead to military action and must anticipate the consequences of those actions, including potential retaliatory measures by other nations. The critique by Robert Pape underscores a critical gap in strategic planning and threat assessment that, if accurate, points to a significant oversight by the administration. This scenario is a stark reminder of the importance of comprehensive strategic planning in foreign policy and military engagement, where understanding the adversary’s potential actions is key to preventing unnecessary escalation and loss of civilian lives.

In the realm of international conflict and military strategy, the responsibility lies heavily with those who command the military and shape the nation’s foreign policy. The Trump administration, as the decision-maker with the institutional power to lead and direct U.S. actions regarding Iran, must bear the scrutiny for its strategies and their outcomes. False narratives or attempts to shift blame to entities with limited control over such situations should be rejected in favor of a clear-eyed assessment of where responsibility truly rests.
