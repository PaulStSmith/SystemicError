Tariff Tantrums and Billion-Dollar Profits: The GM Sympathy Scam

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Let’s get one thing straight from the start: Trump’s tariffs were, are, and continue to be a masterclass in self-inflicted economic sabotage. They masquerade as bold protectionism but function in practice as a hidden tax on American businesses and consumers. General Motors (GM), in its latest quarterly report, is singing that tune loudly, reporting a $1.1 billion hit directly attributed to tariff policies. Cue the corporate sob story. But before we all break out the tiny violins, let’s look at the rest of the story GM doesn’t want you to focus on.

Despite this so-called devastating hit, GM still managed to pull in $3 billion in profit. In one quarter. That’s roughly $33 million per day, or over $1.3 million every hour. Think about that. They took a punch and kept walking, just a little slower. This isn’t a company in distress. This is a company that made slightly less than its usual mountain of cash.

The headlines scream “Profits drop!” but they rarely finish the sentence: “...to a number most companies would sacrifice their firstborn to achieve.”

The Real Strategy Behind the Whining

Corporate America has turned strategic whining into an art form. Here’s how it works:

  1. Suffer a visible, headline-grabbing cost (like tariffs).

  2. Publicize the pain loudly and dramatically.

  3. Use that publicity to:

    • Push for regulatory rollbacks

    • Pressure for subsidies or tax breaks

    • Justify layoffs, outsourcing, or price hikes

GM is following the playbook to the letter. They took a tariff hit? Sure. But they also want the public and policymakers to think they’re on the ropes, gasping for air, needing relief. What they really need is for no one to look at the rest of their balance sheet.

$1.1 Billion Loss, $3 Billion Profit — Perspective Matters

Let’s do the math:

  • Profit fell 32%, landing at $3 billion.

  • That means their pre-tariff baseline was somewhere north of $4.4 billion.

So even with that $1.1 billion gouge, GM is still stacking billions. What GM calls a financial setback, most of the world would call a miracle quarter. This isn’t insolvency. This is the equivalent of Jeff Bezos losing a $20 bill.

It’s important to keep that perspective when companies try to blame policy for all their woes. Because often, these companies aren’t in pain — they’re just not achieving record-breaking profits. And in the grotesque world of corporate capitalism, if you're not breaking records every quarter, the sky must be falling.

Let’s Talk About That Profit

What GM and other automakers rarely highlight is what they do with that profit. Spoiler alert: it's not about reinvesting in American workers or bolstering long-term resilience. Here's what we've seen instead:

  • $10 billion in stock buybacks in recent years

  • Executive compensation ballooning while line workers fight for basic contract renewals

  • Factory closures in the U.S. paired with expansion in Mexico and China

  • Aggressive union-busting tactics during contract negotiations

If tariffs were truly crippling the company, would they still have money to burn on enriching shareholders and executives? Of course not. They just wouldn’t. But here we are.

Tariffs Are Still Dumb. But So Is This Grift.

Let’s be clear: Trump’s tariffs were never sound policy. They were performative, punitive, and economically counterproductive. They targeted allies more than adversaries, disrupted supply chains, and caused American companies to pay more for everything from steel to semiconductors. No economic adviser with a functioning frontal lobe thought it was a smart idea.

But GM isn’t collapsing because of them. They’re not even bleeding. They’re just bleeding less gold. And they want a bailout — not in name, but in policy favor, regulatory capture, and public sympathy.

We need to resist that manipulation.

The Public Pays Either Way

Whether it's tariffs driving up the cost of imported parts, or GM using those costs as an excuse to hike car prices, the average American loses. That’s the dirty truth. Tariffs don’t punish foreign producers — they punish domestic businesses and consumers. Companies like GM pass along those costs, protect their margins, and still rake in billions.

And when they cry poor, they’re setting the stage for:

  • Price hikes

  • Job cuts

  • More automation

  • Plant relocations

  • Political pressure for more tax incentives

All while the C-suite cashes out and shareholders get their buybacks.

Stop Falling for the Billionaire Victim Narrative

GM is not the victim. They're the shark complaining about the ocean temperature.

Yes, the system needs fixing. Yes, tariffs need to go. But let’s not pretend that corporations pulling in $3 billion a quarter are fragile. They’re not. They just want more. And more. And more.

It’s time we stop treating them like they need our pity. They need our scrutiny. They need our regulation. They need to pay their damn taxes.

Until then, the next time a multi-billion-dollar corporation whines about a policy setback, ask them this:

"How's life with only three billion in profit? Holding up okay?"

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
