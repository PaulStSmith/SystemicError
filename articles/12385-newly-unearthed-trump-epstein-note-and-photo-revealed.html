A new note has been unearthed that President Donald Trump reportedly sent to Jeffrey Epstein, the deceased sex trafficker and billionaire financier.

The New York Times reported Thursday evening that it confirmed Trump's name appeared on a contributor list for Epstein’s 50th birthday book.

The report also revealed a previously undisclosed note that Trump sent Epstein in 1997. The message was an inscription in a copy of Trump's book, "Trump: The Art of the Comeback," that Epstein owned.

“To Jeff — You are the greatest!”

The message was signed “Donald” and dated “Oct ’97,” when the book was released.

Additionally, the Times published a previously unreleased photo of Trump, Epstein, and superstar artist James Brown, in which the three pose together with Brown in the center. It wasn't immediately known where the photo was taken.

The report comes after Democrats forced a key House committee to vote to subpoena the Justice Department's Epstein files. It also comes after the Wall Street Journal reported Thursday evening that former President Bill Clinton

White House spokesman Steven Cheung has insisted that Trump kicked Epstein out of Mar-a-Lago for being a "creep."

Our Analysis:

The Unearthed Note: Trump's Dubious Praise for Epstein

A recently discovered note from Donald Trump to Jeffrey Epstein, the now-deceased financier and convicted sex trafficker, adds another layer of controversy to Trump's already tumultuous legacy. According to a report by The New York Times, Trump's name was found on a contributor list for Epstein's 50th birthday book, alongside a note that Trump had penned in 1997 in a copy of his book, "Trump: The Art of the Comeback." The message, “To Jeff — You are the greatest !” signed “Donald” and dated “Oct ’97,” raises eyebrows, not for its content alone, but for the disconcerting camaraderie it implies between Trump and Epstein.

This revelation is particularly significant in light of the Democrats' recent push in a key House committee to subpoena the Justice Department's files on Epstein. The timing is curious, coinciding with reports from the Wall Street Journal that attempt to distance Trump from Epstein, citing a former President Bill Clinton White House spokesman, Steven Cheung, who claimed Trump ousted Epstein from Mar-a-Lago for being a "creep."

Analyzing the Note and Its Implications

The Content: The message itself, while seemingly benign in isolation, gains a sinister edge when contextualized with Epstein's heinous crimes. Praising Epstein as "the greatest" now reads as a grotesque miscalculation at best, and at worst, a chilling endorsement of a man whose criminal activities were hidden in plain sight.

The Timing: The date of the note, 1997, is crucial. It predates Epstein's public exposure and subsequent legal battles, suggesting that Trump's association with Epstein occurred during a period when Epstein's predatory behavior was rumored, if not widely acknowledged. It throws into sharp relief the judgment (or lack thereof) of those who chose to associate with Epstein during this time.

The Discrepancy with Public Statements

The emergence of this note starkly contrasts the narrative spun by Trump's defenders, notably the claim that Trump recognized Epstein's true nature early on and distanced himself accordingly. This note does not just challenge that claim; it obliterates it, revealing a relationship that—at least at one point—was not just cordial but seemingly admiring.

The Broader Context

The photo featuring Trump, Epstein, and James Brown, though lacking in details regarding its origin, adds a visual confirmation to the ties between Trump and Epstein. It's a tangible link that further complicates efforts to sever the historical connection between the two men.

Conclusion: The Uncomfortable Questions Remain

This new evidence does not just cast a shadow on Trump's past associations; it blares a spotlight on them. It forces us to confront uncomfortable questions about the nature of their relationship, what Trump knew of Epstein's activities, and why these ties were maintained as long as they were.

In the grand scheme, the note is but a piece of a much larger, more disturbing puzzle. It's a reminder of the often too-late scrutiny applied to the powerful and their networks, and a call to action for continuous, vigilant examination of those in high places. The saga of Trump and Epstein is far from over, and each new discovery serves as a grim reminder of the systemic failures that allowed such relationships to flourish unchecked.
