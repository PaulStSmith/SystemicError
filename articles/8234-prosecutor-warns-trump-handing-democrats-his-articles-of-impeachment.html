Prosecutor warns Trump 'handing Democrats' his articles of impeachment

Former prosecutor Andrew McCarthy has no love for the “craven video” Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) and five Democrats released to the public advising military members to ignore illegal orders. But he said President Donald Trump’s executive power abuses in reacting to it represent a whole “new level” of threat.

McCarthy tells the National Review that he partially blames Democrats for Trump ordering the Pentagon to demote Kelly and cut his benefits because “Kelly knows, when Democrats poke another hole in another norm, the president’s MO is to drive a truck through it.”

The author and National Review Institute senior fellow also notes Trump is howling “sedition” like he knows what it means.

“What is truly bizarre is to find the president, who likes to remind us that he is the nation’s chief law enforcement official, grossly misstating the law while claiming that the ‘Seditionist Six’ are dangerously misstating the law (when in fact they’ve accurately stated the law),” McCarthy said. “As one of the few current or former prosecutors in the United States to have actually charged and convicted people for seditious conspiracy, I’m here to tell you that the heart of any sedition offense is the use of force against the nation or its government.”

Section 2384 of federal criminal law defines the crime as conspiring to levy war against the United States or to forcibly (1) destroy the government, (2) prevent execution of the laws, or (3) seize government property. The military law definition, said McCarthy, is even more narrow: One must join in the creation of a “revolt, violence, or other disturbance against” government authority, “with intent to cause the overthrow or destruction of” that authority.

“Nothing on the Democrats’ video comes close to urging or promoting violence. Indeed, in comparison to Trump’s fiery Ellipse speech prior to the Capitol riot … the lawmakers’ video is vanilla,” said McCarthy. “If Kelly had been urging his fellow military members to disobey lawful orders, that would be insubordination, not sedition. But he wasn’t.”

“As the president watches his poll numbers plummet, it either doesn’t dawn on him or he just doesn’t care that he is in office, in part, because the voting public was unnerved by Democratic lawfare.” Said McCarthy. “Clearly, Trump’s statist mismanagement of the economy is his biggest problem, as it was Biden’s. But lawfare … is a bigger problem for Trump.”

“Trump and his minions revel in lawfare,” said McCarthy, which “further normalizes the noxious practice, potentially entrenching it.”

“Trump is also handing Democrats the articles of impeachment they will swiftly enact if, as seems increasingly likely, his erratic governance hands them back the House next year — and maybe even the Senate the way things are going,” McCarthy warned. “… Incorporating the Pentagon into the lawfare campaign against political enemies raises the abuse of executive power to a new level.”

“For the president to begin pulling the military he commands into his ongoing, punitive use of government processes against his partisan opponents is a red line,” said McCarthy. “Justice Department lawfare is bad, but the courts are equipped to handle it. Politicization of the military is a different, more threatening beast.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Misuse of Executive Power and the Politicization of the Military

Former prosecutor Andrew McCarthy’s recent comments to the National Review about President Donald Trump’s reaction to a video released by Senator Mark Kelly and five other Democrats highlight a stark misuse of executive power and a worrying trend towards the politicization of the military. McCarthy, no stranger to the intricacies of legal definitions and the consequences of their misuse, lays bare the dangerous ground Trump is treading by misinterpreting sedition and unjustly targeting political opponents.

The Sedition Smokescreen

At the heart of McCarthy’s critique is Trump’s gross misinterpretation of sedition to attack his political adversaries. Trump’s labeling of the “Seditionist Six” for advising military members to refuse illegal orders showcases a fundamental misunderstanding (or intentional misrepresentation) of what constitutes sedition. McCarthy, with his background as a prosecutor who has actually charged individuals with seditious conspiracy, points out that sedition involves the use of force against the nation or its government. The Democrats’ video, advocating for the refusal of illegal orders, clearly does not advocate violence or fulfill any criteria of sedition. This misuse of legal terminology by the President not only muddies the waters of legal discourse but also dangerously misleads the public on what constitutes a threat to the nation.

Executive Overreach and Military Politicization

Trump’s directive to the Pentagon to demote Kelly and cut his benefits, as McCarthy suggests, is a vindictive reaction that blurs the lines between lawful oversight of the military and partisan retribution. This action, according to McCarthy, elevates the abuse of executive power to a new level, especially concerning because it drags the military into the sphere of political gamesmanship. The military, traditionally and ideally, stands apart from political squabbles, serving the nation and obeying lawful orders regardless of the political winds. Trump’s actions, however, threaten to undermine this impartiality, potentially dragging the military into a quagmire of partisanship that could have long-lasting implications for its role in democracy.

The Bigger Picture: Lawfare and Its Consequences

McCarthy’s assertion that Trump is “handing Democrats the articles of impeachment” through his actions speaks to a broader issue of lawfare—the use of legal systems and principles as a means to achieve a political goal. While lawfare can be a tool for accountability, its misuse, especially involving the military, is deeply problematic. The normalization of such practices, as seen in Trump’s administration according to McCarthy, risks entrenching a cycle of retribution and misuse of legal norms that could have devastating effects on the fabric of American governance.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Andrew McCarthy’s analysis underscores a critical and concerning shift in the use of executive power under Trump. The misuse of legal concepts like sedition, the retaliatory targeting of political opponents through military demotions, and the broader trend of lawfare represent a threat to the core principles of democracy and the rule of law. Such actions set a dangerous precedent, not just for the present administration, but for the future of American political and military governance. As McCarthy warns, the consequences of these actions, if left unchecked, could extend far beyond the tenure of any single president, eroding trust in key institutions and undermining the very foundations of the nation.
