'How will history remember you?' Ex-MSNBC host lays into Republicans' 'disturbing' remarks

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

(Screengrab via CNN)

Two Republicans got more than they bargained for during a heated CNN segment Wednesday night, as a former MSNBC host called their remarks "disturbing" and asked them to consider how they'll be remembered in the annals of history.

The fiery segment occurred on CNN's "NewsNight" during a discussion over due process and whether the administration should send suspected gang members to El Salvador without a court hearing.

Former Wisconsin Lt. Governor Mandela Barnes went toe to toe with GOP strategist Shermichael Singleton, noting the Trump administration isn't only looking to deport undocumented immigrants, they're looking into "homegrowns" as well, meaning American citizens who commit crimes.

"Homegrowns — people who were born here, people who have citizenship. They've already floated the idea of revoking people's citizenship," noted Barnes. "So for the people you talk to, that you mentioned, that say, 'This may not apply to me'—I guarantee you, there will be something that applies to you."

Barnes compared the deportation of people to prisons without due process to World War II-era actions.

"That is essentially sending people to internment camps, like what we did in World War II," he said.

Abel Maldonado, a Republican former California lieutenant governor, asserted that Kilmar Abrego Garcia, the Maryland father mistakenly sent to El Salvador, was denied political asylum, and a deportation order for him was paused.

"Now he has an El Salvador problem — he doesn't have a Trump USA problem," Maldonado insisted, prompting former MSNBC host Tiffany Cross to shout back, "He does have a Trump USA problem!"

"He's not coming back because they're not bringing him back!" Cross later added.

Maldonado attacked Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD) for not "talk[ing] to the woman here who lost her daughter in Maryland," but "go[ing] down to El Salvador to try to bring an amnesty process back to America."

And Cross wasn't having any of it.

"What does that have to," she began, cutting him off. "OK can I just say. It is disturbing that you are sitting here —"

As she tried to interject, Maldanado talked over her.

"It is disturbing, you are sitting here defending the lack of due process to have the government disappear people and take them to El Salvador," she railed.

As the two continued talking over each other Cross demanded to know from the Republicans, "For you and you who are defending this — how do you think history will remember you?"

"You mean, how do you think — when we look back at this time — when the government is disappearing people, and you two sat on this stage and said, “Oh, well, that's okay.”

But Maldonado didn't budge.

"This is a gentleman who obviously has had issues. We’re finding out more and more and more. And the Democrat Party and Van Hollen — I think they’re on the wrong side of this. There was a mistake made. He’s in El Salvador. He should get a lawyer in El Salvador to get out of the prison. But he’s not coming to the USA."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Disturbing Defense of Due Process Erosion

In an era where political discourse frequently descends into the abyss of the absurd, a recent CNN segment has emerged as a glaring testament to the lengths some are willing to go to defend the indefensible. The segment, featuring a clash between former MSNBC host Tiffany Cross and two Republican figures, Shermichael Singleton and Abel Maldonado, served as a chilling reminder of the dangerous path some seek to tread concerning due process and citizenship rights.

The Underlying Issue: A Disregard for Due Process

At the heart of this fiery exchange lies a profoundly disturbing proposition by the Trump administration: the potential deportation of suspected gang members to El Salvador without a court hearing. This is not merely a policy discussion about undocumented immigrants but a deeply troubling inclination towards stripping American citizens—referred to as "homegrowns" by former Wisconsin Lt. Governor Mandela Barnes—of their citizenship and rights.

A Historical Parallel of Shame

Barnes's comparison of the proposed deportations to World War II-era internment camps is not an exaggeration to be dismissed lightly. It is a pointed reminder of one of the darkest chapters in American history, where due process was obliterated under the guise of national security. The idea that the administration is even considering such measures should send shivers down the spine of anyone who values civil liberties.

The Maldonado Deflection

Abel Maldonado's attempt to minimize the issue by focusing on the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia—a Maryland father mistakenly sent to El Salvador—is a masterclass in missing the point. Maldonado's assertion that Garcia "doesn't have a Trump USA problem" but rather an "El Salvador problem" is a staggering display of cognitive dissonance. The fact that Garcia was deported because of a flawed system in the United States directly contradicts Maldonado's attempt to absolve the administration of responsibility.

Cross's Condemnation and the Question of Legacy

Tiffany Cross's outrage is not only justified but necessary. Her confrontation with the Republicans on the panel, demanding they consider how history will remember their defense of such policies, cuts to the very core of the issue. The defense of policies that can lead to the disappearance of people without due process is not something to be taken lightly. Maldonado's and Singleton's apparent comfort with such prospects is a stark illustration of how far the Overton window has shifted in favor of authoritarianism under the guise of law and order.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Our Times

This CNN segment serves as a microcosm of the broader political and moral battles raging in the United States. The defense of the indefensible—whether it be the erosion of due process, the stripping of citizenship, or the defense of flawed deportation policies—reflects a disturbing trend towards authoritarianism. The willingness of some to sidestep fundamental rights and liberties in pursuit of political goals is a dangerous precedent.

As we navigate these tumultuous times, it is imperative to remember that the defense of our most basic rights and principles is not a partisan issue but a fundamental cornerstone of democracy. The question posed by Tiffany Cross about how history will remember those who choose to defend such policies is one that all involved in this debate would do well to consider seriously.
