On Friday, Trump threatened to hit China with 100% tariffs, a move that sent the stock market tumbling. The tech-heavy NASDAQ Composite Index fell by 3.6%; the S&P 500 lost 2.6% and the DOW Jones lost 1.9%.

Trump tried to assuage investors’ concerns on Sunday in a Truth Social post.

“Don’t worry about China, it will all be fine!” Trump posted on Truth Social. “Highly respected President Xi just had a bad moment. He doesn’t want Depression for his country, and neither do I. The U.S.A. wants to help China, not hurt it!!!”

The president’s words seemed to have the opposite impact on investors. Several observers shared their thoughts about the move on social media.

“TACO strikes again,” Peter Schiff, chief economist at Europac Asset Management, posted on X. “JD Vance walked back Trump’s 100% tariffs threat by saying it’s just a negotiating tactic. It’s not the best negotiation strategy to let your opponent know that you’re bluffing. Trump also posted about his great relationship with China’s wonderful president.”

“Taco time,” Spencer Hakimian, founder of Tolou Capital Management, posted on X.

“The Trump-China trade relationship reminds me of my middle school love life: On one week, off the week, high drama, passing notes, heartache, renewal, gossip, and we did it all again the next week,” University of Michigan economist Justin Wolfers posted on X. “The reality, of course, is that despite the drama, no one was making long-term commitments.”

“When you fire all the China experts, you end up with stupid remarks just like this,” military veteran John Jackson posted on X. “China is patient, methodical, deliberate and ruthless. No it won’t be ok, Mr. President, and you can’t wish away that which you alone have wrought.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tariff Tantrums: A Masterclass in How Not to Do Diplomacy

In what can only be described as a political circus, President Donald Trump’s latest maneuver in the ongoing saga with China has left observers, economists, and investors alike with their heads spinning. The President’s threat to impose a staggering 100% tariffs on China has not only sent the stock market into a nosedive but has also sparked a chorus of criticism from various quarters, each more incredulous than the last.

Economic Fallout: Playing with Fire

First, let’s dissect the immediate fallout. Following Trump’s announcement, the NASDAQ Composite Index plummeted by 3.6%, the S&P 500 by 2.6%, and the DOW Jones by 1.9%. These aren’t mere numbers; they represent billions in lost market value, evaporating in the heat of a tweet. Yet, in a move that would be comical if it weren’t so tragic, Trump attempted to soothe the markets with a post on Truth Social, asserting, “Don’t worry about China, it will all be fine!” This statement, far from assuaging fears, likely only further convinced investors of the chaotic unpredictability at the helm.

The Art of the Bluff: A Negotiation Strategy?

Observers, including Peter Schiff and JD Vance, were quick to point out the folly in Trump’s approach. Vance’s attempt to walk back the 100% tariffs threat by painting it as a mere “negotiating tactic” does little to inspire confidence. Not only does it highlight a lack of coherence in the administration’s strategy, but it also begs the question: Is publicly broadcasting a bluff really a masterstroke in negotiation, or is it, as Schiff suggests, a surefire way to undermine one’s own position?

The Drama of International Relations

Justin Wolfers’ comparison of the Trump-China trade relationship to a tumultuous middle school romance is both hilarious and apt. The on-again, off-again nature of the negotiations, complete with high drama and empty promises, does little to foster stability or long-term commitments. It’s a stark reminder that global economics and international relations are not a game to be played on a whim, but rather a complex, nuanced field that requires careful, considered strategy.

Dismissing Expertise: A Dangerous Game

John Jackson’s critique that firing all the China experts has led to “stupid remarks” is a damning indictment of Trump’s approach to governance. China, as Jackson astutely points out, is “patient, methodical, deliberate and ruthless.” To navigate such a landscape requires expertise, tact, and a deep understanding of the intricate dynamics at play. Trump’s dismissal of such expertise in favor of impulsive decision-making is not just reckless; it’s a dereliction of duty.

Conclusion: A Call for Sanity

In summary, Trump’s handling of the China situation is a case study in how not to conduct international relations. The implications of his actions go far beyond the immediate economic ramifications, threatening long-term stability and diplomatic relations. It serves as a stark reminder of the importance of expertise, strategic thinking, and coherence in governance - qualities that seem to be in short supply in the current administration. As the world watches on, one can only hope for a return to sanity, before the high-stakes game of tariff roulette causes irreparable damage.
