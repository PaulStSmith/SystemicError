Backlash brews against Trump as voters reject assumption people 'enjoy suffering'

By Paulo Santos

“Daily Blast” podcaster Greg Sargent spoke with The American Prospect senior writer Paul Waldman about the backlash brewing among American voters against MAGA and Trump-style cruelty.

Sabrina Carpenter recently condemned the White House for using her song in a video of people getting arrested, pinned to the ground and handcuffed, which Carpenter called “evil and disgusting.” The White House replied with a statement blasting Carter for defending “these sick monsters” and suggested she “must be stupid, or is it slow?”

“It’s intentionally juvenile,” said Waldman, who added that administration spokespeople had “abandoned” principles of earlier administrations by handing out responses that basically say: “Your mom.”

“Their attitude is ‘no, we’re just going to be like the worst 4chan troll because we think that’s funny and we think people will respond to it and like it and our base will like it when we’re being abusive to people. And when it comes to people like immigrants — that we’re being literally sadistic to them — that we want people to enjoy their suffering,’” Waldman said.

But Americans appear to be turning on the childish sadism, said Sargent.

“You could … argue that they’re losing the war over social media spectacle because all the polls are showing very clearly that there’s widespread revulsion and rejection of the mass deportations,” Sargent said. “Trump has completely thrown away whatever advantage he had on the broader immigration issue. Thank you, [White House advisor] Stephen Miller, for doing that. [Trump] has really wrecked his standing on the issue with all these deportation raids and so forth. And I think they almost weren’t prepared for it in one critical sense, which is funny because they sort of pose as very savvy in terms of social media and the cultural stuff.”

All over the country, ICE is becoming a pariah agency, said Sargent. “People are taking out their phones, filming these things,” suggesting that the Trump administration is losing the “spectacle wars” they started by filming agents abusing children and family members.

“I think maybe they are,” said Waldman. “The theory at the heart of what the administration is doing and what Trump’s entire career is based on is that we should all be our worst selves, that our kind of darkest impulses should be the ones that reign. We should be the most bigoted, the most corrupt, the most angry and hateful — that that’s our truest self is our worst self.”

“And if you look at who is in this administration, it is a collection of the worst people from top to bottom,” Waldman continued. “And the implicit argument is that we should all be that way. And we should cheer when people get brutalized. And we should laugh when we see the corruption because everybody is corrupt and everybody is sadistic, and that’s who we ought to be,” said Waldman. “But the truth is that that’s not who most people want to be.”

Our Analysis:

The Cruel Spectacle: Analyzing the Trump Administration’s Tactics

In an eye-opening discussion on the “Daily Blast” podcast, Greg Sargent and Paul Waldman dissect the repugnant underbelly of MAGA and Trump-style governance, exposing a strategy rooted in cruelty and sadism. This analysis delves into their conversation, highlighting the critical points where the Trump administration’s approach not only fails morally but also politically.

The Unholy Alliance of Politics and Cruelty

The article begins with an unsettling instance involving Sabrina Carpenter, whose song was used in a White House video depicting arrests in a manner she condemned as “evil and disgusting.” The White House’s retort, dismissing Carpenter’s concerns with derogatory insinuations about her intelligence, epitomizes a juvenile approach to criticism. This isn’t just about poor taste; it’s a calculated embrace of 4chan-level trolling, aiming to amuse a base that revels in the denigration of others, particularly immigrants.

Waldman’s remarks point to a sinister strategy: promoting the worst of human impulses — bigotry, corruption, anger, and hatred — as virtues. This isn’t governance; it’s a descent into barbarism, where the suffering of the vulnerable is not just tolerated but celebrated.

Losing the War of Public Opinion

Despite the administration’s belief in their social media savvy, Sargent observes a critical miscalculation. The public’s reaction to the mass deportations and ICE’s increasingly notorious reputation suggests a widespread revulsion against the administration’s tactics. The intended spectacle of power has backfired, revealing a spectacle of cruelty that most Americans find repulsive.

Stephen Miller, often credited with spearheading the Trump administration’s immigration policies, is singled out as instrumental in this failure. The attempt to weaponize immigration as a political tool has not only failed but has also significantly damaged Trump’s standing on the issue.

The Spectacle Wars: A Losing Battle

The concept of “spectacle wars” is particularly poignant. The Trump administration, which prided itself on dominating the narrative through shock and awe, is losing ground. The ubiquity of smartphones and social media has turned their attempts at displaying strength into exhibitions of brutality. The public, armed with nothing more than their phones, is documenting and exposing the administration’s actions for what they are: abuses of power.

The Core of Trumpism: Celebrating Our Worst Selves

Waldman’s analysis of Trumpism’s core — encouraging society’s worst impulses — is a damning indictment of not just the administration but also its enablers and supporters. This isn’t merely a political strategy; it’s a moral crisis. The administration, as Waldman notes, is a “collection of the worst people,” advocating for a world where cruelty is not only acceptable but applauded.

Conclusion: A Rejection of Sadism

The article, and the podcast it covers, serve as a crucial reminder of the battle not just for the soul of America, but for the very essence of humanity itself. The Trump administration’s reliance on cruelty, bigotry, and sadism has not gone unnoticed, and the public’s rejection of these values is a testament to the resilience of empathy, kindness, and decency.

In the end, it’s not just about political ideologies but about what kind of people we choose to be. Waldman and Sargent’s conversation underscores a pivotal moment in American politics: a moment where rejecting the administration’s tactics is also an affirmation of our collective humanity.
