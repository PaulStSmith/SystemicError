'Never satisfied': Trump and Epstein's ex lawyer attacks Marjorie Taylor Greene

David Schoen, a lawyer who represented President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein, blamed Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) for refusing to drop a scandal concerning his former clients.

During a Monday interview on Newsmax, host Katrina Szish asked Schoen about Trump's ongoing Epstein scandal.

"Just explain this to me because you, again, you're a former Epstein lawyer," Szish said. "Why, then, are people so obsessed? And I'm not just talking about one side of the party or on one side of party lines or others. Overall, people are obsessed. What will it take to get this story — I'm just going to say to go away because it doesn't seem like there's anything there?"

Schoen argued that the scandal was being driven by "competing agendas" on the left and the right.

"On the left, New York Times and the Democratic Party, the hypocrisy is outrageous, as I've said, but they want to continue to sow as much dissension as they can and distract from the accomplishments of the Trump administration," he said.

"On the other side, you've got, you know, Tucker Carlson, Marjorie Taylor Greene, conspiracy theorists, who will never be satisfied by anything," Schoen added. "If there were a smoking gun, Maxwell says, for example, that some young woman was with, fill in the blank, some famous and wealthy person. They'll say, well, there must be more. That can't be all of it."

"So it's being driven, I think, by financial reasons and other reasons by both sides."

Earlier this month, Greene warned that Trump could lose his MAGA base over the scandal.

"If you tell the base of people, who support you, of deep state treasonous crimes, election interference, blackmail, and rich powerful elite evil cabals, then you must take down every enemy of The People," the lawmaker wrote on X. "The base will turn and there's no going back."

"Dangling bits of red meat no longer satisfies," she remarked.

Our Analysis:

The Scapegoating Spectacle: Unpacking David Schoen’s Commentary

In a recent Newsmax interview, David Schoen, a lawyer with the quite distinctive resume of having represented both President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein, took to the airwaves to weave a narrative that is as perplexing as it is predictable. Schoen attempts to deflect from the Epstein scandal, surrounding Trump, by casting blame far and wide, with a particular spotlight on Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA). Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a cynic.

The Art of Deflection

Schoen's argument is essentially a masterclass in deflection. He suggests that the "obsession" with the Epstein scandal is merely the result of "competing agendas" from both sides of the political spectrum. On the left, he accuses the New York Times and the Democratic Party of hypocrisy, asserting they aim to "sow as much dissension as they can and distract from the accomplishments of the Trump administration." This claim is a classic move in the political playbook: when in doubt, blame the opposition for distraction and dissension.

However, Schoen doesn't stop there. He turns his gaze to the right, singling out figures like Tucker Carlson and Marjorie Taylor Greene, labeling them as "conspiracy theorists" who are never satisfied with the evidence presented to them. This is a fascinating twist, considering Greene's own penchant for conspiracy theories has been a defining characteristic of her political persona. It's almost as if Schoen is attempting a precarious balancing act, trying to appease both sides by acknowledging their grievances while simultaneously dismissing them as unfounded.

The Role of Marjorie Taylor Greene

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene's warning that Trump could lose his MAGA base over the scandal is a pivotal moment that Schoen conveniently glosses over. Greene's statement, "If you tell the base of people, who support you, of deep state treasonous crimes, election interference, blackmail, and rich powerful elite evil cabals, then you must take down every enemy of The People," is emblematic of the high stakes in maintaining the MAGA movement's support. Her caution that "dangling bits of red meat no longer satisfies" is not just a critique; it's a prophecy of the potential unraveling of Trump's base if perceived inaction continues.

Schoen's attempt to downplay the significance of Greene's critique and the broader implications for Trump's support base is, at best, an oversight and, at worst, a deliberate obfuscation. The fact that a figure as influential within the MAGA movement as Greene is sounding the alarm should not be understated or dismissed as mere conspiracy theory fodder.

The Financial Motivations

Schoen's assertion that the scandal is being driven by "financial reasons and other reasons by both sides" is a vague and nebulous claim that warrants scrutiny. While it's no secret that sensational stories drive clicks and viewership, which in turn can translate to financial gain for media outlets, Schoen's framing lacks nuance and specificity. It's a sweeping generalization that serves to muddy the waters rather than clarify the dynamics at play.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

David Schoen's interview is a microcosm of the broader systemic issues plaguing our political discourse. The use of deflection, the vilification of the opposition, and the reduction of complex scandals to mere political footballs are all symptoms of a larger problem. In a world where accountability seems increasingly elusive, figures like Schoen thrive on the confusion and polarization they perpetuate.

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene's warnings, whether one agrees with her politics or not, highlight a fundamental truth: the base's loyalty is not infinite, and perceived betrayals or inconsistencies can have real consequences. Schoen's attempt to brush aside the Epstein scandal as a mere distraction fails to reckon with the gravity and complexity of the issues at hand.

In the final analysis, the interview leaves us with more questions than answers, a testament to the tangled web of politics, power, and public perception. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, it is our duty to seek clarity, demand accountability, and remain ever vigilant against the systemic errors that threaten to undermine the very fabric of our society.
