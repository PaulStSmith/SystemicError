“Because Being Mean Makes Me Feel Bad”: A Tumblr User Accidentally Solves Moral Philosophy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Inner CompassNo Sky-Dad RequiredGoodness for Its Own Sake

There’s a meme floating around the internet—like most profound things these days—that distills over two millennia of moral philosophy into a three-line Tumblr post.

It starts with a prompt:

“In a game with no consequences, why are you still playing the ‘Good’ side?”

A fair question. In digital worlds where nothing matters, why not let your inner villain loose? Burn the village. Kick the chicken. Betray your allies.

Then comes the reply:

“Because being mean makes me feel bad.”

And finally, the gut-punch of a follow-up:

“23 fucking hundred years of philosophy and this mother fucker on tumblr gets it in a meme.”

And they’re right. Plato, Aristotle, Kant, Hume, Mill, and an entire conga line of moral theorists have spilled oceans of ink trying to answer this very question: Why be good? Is it duty? Utility? Social contract? Divine command?

But what if it’s something simpler?

What if it’s just that being kind makes you feel like a better version of yourself—and being cruel doesn’t?

The Inner Compass

There’s a quiet elegance to this answer. No theological scaffolding. No metaphysical gymnastics. Just a gut-check.

You spare the townsfolk in Fallout, not because God is watching, but because your conscience is. That imaginary pang of guilt you feel when you break a digital NPC’s heart? That’s your brain reminding you: Hey, we’re the kind of person who gives a damn. Even when nobody else is watching.

And this isn’t new. Philosophers like David Hume argued that morality flows not from logic, but from sentiment. Our emotional reactions to suffering, fairness, kindness—they are the foundation of our ethics. Hume didn’t need heaven to make a case for compassion. Neither did the Buddha. Neither, apparently, does Tumblr.

No Sky-Dad Required

Let’s go one step further: if your morality evaporates the second divine punishment is off the table, was it ever yours to begin with?

Morality that depends on a deity’s gaze is obedience. What we’re talking about here—what this Tumblr user captured—is empathy. The internalized sense of right and wrong. It’s what makes you hesitate before breaking the heart of a virtual character made of polygons and subroutines. Because on some level, you know what it’s like to hurt.

We don’t need a burning bush to tell us that cruelty is wrong. We can feel it in our bones. And that’s what makes it real.

Goodness for Its Own Sake

Maybe that’s why the “good” side in games feels more satisfying. Not because it’s easy. Not because it comes with extra loot. But because it aligns with who we are—or who we want to be.

Choosing kindness in a consequence-free environment is arguably the purest form of morality. No fear of hellfire. No hope of reward. Just the quiet, unshakable sense that empathy is worth something. That being good... feels good.

So yes, philosophers may continue to debate the nature of ethics. But one anonymous Tumblr user? They nailed it.

Because being mean makes me feel bad.
And that’s reason enough.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
