'You're a fool!' Trump's 'Hollywood ambassador' rages at Newsom after they meet

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20254 min read

Conservative actor Jon Voight fulminated against California Gov. Gavin Newsom – whom he called a "lying dog" and a "fool" – just days after meeting with him in his role as president Donald Trump's "Hollywood ambassador."

The 86-year-old actor posted the blistering harangue late Tuesday, after Newsom sharply criticized the president's escalation of the situation in Los Angeles by activating National Guard troops and U.S. Marines without the governor's approval, and Voight defended Trump in a two-minute video posted on X, reported The Daily Beast.

“You’re a fool blaming Trump,” Voight said, talking before an American flag backdrop and calling Trump “the greatest president since Abraham Lincoln."

Voight has met with Newsom privately last Thursday to discuss their shared goal of bringing back film and television productions to California from other states that offer tax incentives, but he had strong words for the governor and even stronger words for the protesters.

“What are you doing for this destruction, these animals destroying Los Angeles,” Voight said. “Are you there? Talking calmly with them? You fool. They would burn you down like they’re burning the cars and the American flag, with no regard for humanity.”

Some of the demonstrators have set fire to self-driving taxis during the protests against the Trump administration's crackdown on immigrants, and Voight – who was named in January as one of Trump’s “special ambassadors” for Hollywood, along with Sylvester Stallone and Mel Gibson – said the military escalation was necessary.

The troops are needed to “help with these destructive, barbaric riots," Voight said, adding that the governor had caused "chaos for the people. You’re a disgrace.”

Our Analysis:

The Blazing Rhetoric of Jon Voight: A Critical Analysis

In an era where political discourse frequently descends into the realm of the theatrical, conservative actor Jon Voight's recent tirade against California Governor Gavin Newsom stands as a vivid illustration of this phenomenon. Voight, donning the mantle of President Donald Trump's "Hollywood ambassador," unleashed a vitriolic critique of Newsom, whom he labeled a "lying dog" and a "fool." This outburst came in the wake of Newsom's condemnation of Trump's decision to deploy National Guard troops and U.S. Marines to Los Angeles without the governor's consent, a move that further inflamed tensions amidst already heated protests.

Voight's Venom: More Than Mere Words

Voight's assault was not limited to mere name-calling. In a video posted on X, set against the backdrop of an American flag, he defended Trump with zeal, declaring him "the greatest president since Abraham Lincoln." Such a comparison, hyperbolic at best, not only elevates Trump to an unwarranted pedestal but also trivializes the complex and transformative leadership of Lincoln. Voight's rhetoric, steeped in idolization, seems detached from a balanced assessment of historical presidencies.

Further, Voight's condemnation of the protesters as "animals" destroying Los Angeles reveals a disturbing dehumanization of those exercising their right to protest. His words, charged with disdain, fail to engage with the underlying causes of the protests — primarily, a widespread outcry against systemic injustices. Instead, Voight opts for a narrative that paints military intervention as not only necessary but heroic, ignoring the potential for escalation that such actions could provoke.

The Irony of Jon Voight's Position

The irony of Voight's stance cannot be overstated. Just days before his incendiary critique, he had met with Newsom to discuss the mutual aim of revitalizing California's film and television production industry. This sudden pivot from diplomacy to hostility underscores a volatile approach to political engagement that does more to inflame than to illuminate.

Moreover, Voight's appointment as one of Trump's "special ambassadors" for Hollywood, alongside figures like Sylvester Stallone and Mel Gibson, speaks to a broader strategy of leveraging celebrity influence for political ends. However, the effectiveness of such a strategy is questionable when the messages conveyed are rife with division and disdain.

A Disgrace to Discourse

Voight's closing remarks, branding Newsom a "disgrace," ironically reflect more on the nature of his own contribution to public discourse. In his rush to vilify and delegitimize, Voight bypasses the opportunity for constructive criticism and meaningful dialogue. His approach, marked by a stark us-vs-them mentality, serves only to deepen divisions and distract from the pressing issues at hand.

In sum, Jon Voight's recent outburst is emblematic of a troubling trend in political expression — one characterized by vitriol, hyperbole, and a profound lack of empathy. Such rhetoric does not enlighten but rather enshrouds, contributing to a polarized and less informed public sphere. As we navigate these turbulent times, the need for thoughtful, respectful, and fact-based discourse has never been greater. Voight's words, unfortunately, move us further from this ideal.
