National Guard in LA left hanging with no pay and no support from locals: report

By Paulo Santos

Members of the California National Guard stand guard, as a demonstration against federal immigration sweeps takes place, outside the Edward R. Roybal federal building, after their deployment by President Trump, in Los Angeles, June 8. REUTERS/Mike Blake

In the Donald Trump administration's haste to federalize California National Guard troops to hit the streets of Los Angeles to protect ICE agents grabbing immigrants, the Pentagon has dropped the ball on providing the paperwork that would get them paid and activate their benefits, according to a report.

In interviews with more than a dozen of those deployed, Military.com found frustration that the Department of Defense, under Secretary Pete Hegseth, is not upholding its obligation to provide their "formal activation orders, the critical paperwork that not only authorizes their duty status, but also unlocks pay, Tricare health benefits and eligibility for Department of Veterans Affairs benefits."

The report notes that the hasty move to send the Guard to LA, bypassing a request from Gov. Gavin Newsom, is a contributing factor to the snafu.

Add to that, the unpopularity of the militarized occupation of an American city has put off local merchants who are normally supportive of the military.

According to the report, the failure of the administration is "... indicative of a slapdash mission where the welfare of troops has not been a priority. Several service members described inadequate living conditions at staging areas such as the military facility at Naval Weapons Station Seal Beach, on the outskirts of Los Angeles, where soldiers are sleeping outside on cots due to a shortage of space. Others cited intermittent issues with food and fuel supplies."

The report adds that traditionally, when the National Guard is deployed to assist in distressed communities, they are welcomed with open arms. But soldiers are getting the cold shoulder this time.

"One midlevel officer said their unit would receive 'extremely generous' help when deployed to combat wildfires, including water, sports drinks and food, which played a significant role in morale and tightened the Guard-community relationship," the report notes, before adding one officer conceded, "This is a situation [where] we're against the community."

Our Analysis:

A Blatant Disregard for Troop Welfare and Civil Liberties

In what can only be described as a chaotic and deeply troubling mismanagement by the Donald Trump administration, the decision to federalize California National Guard troops for an aggressive immigration enforcement action in Los Angeles has unfolded into a fiasco. The Military.com report highlights not just the logistical nightmare but a startling indifference to the well-being of the troops and a blatant disrespect for the civil liberties of the community involved.

Rushed and Reckless: The Administration's Approach

The administration's decision to bypass Governor Gavin Newsom's request and deploy the National Guard to Los Angeles to protect ICE agents was not only rushed but recklessly executed. The Pentagon, under Secretary Pete Hegseth, failed to provide the necessary paperwork for the deployed troops, leaving them without pay, healthcare benefits through Tricare, and eligibility for Department of Veterans Affairs benefits. This oversight is not just administrative—it's inhumane.

The Fallout: Troop Welfare and Community Relations

The consequences of this slapdash operation are twofold. Firstly, the welfare of the troops, who have been sent into a contentious situation without the basic support structures of pay and health benefits, is disgracefully overlooked. The report outlines "inadequate living conditions" and "intermittent issues with food and fuel supplies", which speaks volumes about the administration's disregard for the very individuals it deployed.

Secondly, the militarized occupation of an American city has eroded the relationship between the National Guard and the local community—a relationship that is vital for the success of any deployment within U.S. borders. The contrast between the warm reception troops usually receive when aiding in natural disasters and the cold shoulder they're getting now couldn't be starker. This shift not only affects operational morale but also deeply damages the long-standing goodwill between the military and civilian populations.

Unpopular, Unprepared, and Unacceptable

The unpopularity of the mission—described by one officer as a situation where they're "against the community"—highlights a fundamental flaw in the administration's decision-making process. The use of military forces within U.S. cities for immigration enforcement is a deeply divisive and distressing strategy, raising serious questions about the erosion of civil liberties and the militarization of civilian spaces.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Misplaced Priorities

This debacle is indicative of a broader pattern of misplaced priorities within the Trump administration, where the spectacle of power takes precedence over the well-being of service members and the rights of communities. The failure to provide for the National Guard troops, the disregard for democratic processes, and the erosion of community trust are symptomatic of an administration that acts first and thinks later—if at all.

The situation demands not just immediate corrective action to support the deployed troops but also a profound reevaluation of the policies that led to this moment. The welfare of military personnel and the protection of civil liberties must be non-negotiable priorities for any administration, without exception.
