'No MAGA left behind': Outrage as Trump pardon chief issues new vow

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Closer Look at the Controversial Pardon Politics Under Trump

Interim U.S, Attorney Ed Martin on February 12, 2025 (Image: Screengrab via ABC 7 News WJLA / YouTube)

President Donald Trump's highly controversial head of the Department of Justice's Office of the Pardon Attorney appeared to suggest on social media that all Trump supporters could be eligible for pardons.

Ed Martin, who has represented January 6 defendants and was an organizer for Trump's "Stop the Steal" rallies that falsely alleged widespread fraud in the 2020 election, was installed as Interim U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia on Trump's first day in office this year. However, his background was so controversial that his nomination to fill the position permanently was scuttled by Republican Senators joining with Democrats.

The Associated Press had reported that Martin's nomination was pulled after "concerns of partisanship" and concerns "about the conservative activist’s modest legal experience, divisive politics and support for Jan. 6 rioters."

On Monday, Martin thanked President Donald Trump for giving an unconditional pardon to a Virginia Sheriff who had been convicted on federal conspiracy, fraud, and bribery charges.

"Trump praised Jenkins as 'a wonderful person, who was persecuted by the Radical Left 'monsters,' and 'left for dead,'" according to ABC News.

"This is why I, as President of the United States, see fit to end his unfair sentence, and grant Sheriff Jenkins a FULL and Unconditional Pardon. He will NOT be going to jail tomorrow, but instead will have a wonderful and productive life."

Acting U.S. Attorney Zachary T. Lee had called Jenkins' actions a "cash-for-badges scheme."

Martin, who also serves as Trump's weaponization of government czar, posted a thank-you note on social media on Monday: "Thank you, President Trump! I am thrilled that Sheriff Jenkins is the first pardon since I became your Pardon Attorney. Congratulations, Sheriff Jenkins: Do great things and make us proud."

He followed that up with a second note: "Thank you, @potus Trump, for pardoning Sheriff Jenkins!"

And then, this vow: "No MAGA left behind."

Critics blasted Martin.

"Hey Ed!!" wrote Lawfare senior editor Anna Bower. "Aren’t you the guy who is investigating the 'politicization' of DOJ? And you’re also the pardon attorney? And you said 'No MAGA left behind' in reference to a pardon the President issued?"

"Do you think that might be perceived as politicization of the pardon process?" she asked.

Eric Columbus, who served as the special litigation counsel for the U.S. House of Representatives and in the DOJ during the Obama administration, declared Martin is "already announcing that it’s all just politics."

Media Matters' senior fellow Matthew Gertz wrote, "Ed Martin on the case, doling out pardons to every MAGA crank who enjoys crimes."

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at the Controversial Pardon Politics Under Trump

In the latest chapter of what can only be described as the theatrics of governance under President Donald Trump, Ed Martin, the head of the Department of Justice's Office of the Pardon Attorney, has once again stirred the pot of controversy with his recent social media antics. Martin, a figure already marred by his associations with the January 6 defendants and the "Stop the Steal" rallies, has taken his polarizing position to new heights, or perhaps more aptly, new lows, by suggesting that Trump supporters could be eligible for pardons en masse.

The Background: Installed as Interim U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia on Trump's inauguration day this year, Martin's nomination for a permanent position was torpedoed by an unlikely coalition of Republican Senators and Democrats. The reasons? A cocktail of "concerns of partisanship", a "modest legal experience" not befitting the role, and his "divisive politics and support for Jan. 6 rioters", as reported by The Associated Press. It's a background that reads less like a resume and more like a rap sheet for someone tasked with overseeing the integrity of the pardon process.

The Pardon in Question: The recent unconditional pardon of a Virginia Sheriff, convicted on charges including federal conspiracy, fraud, and bribery, serves as a prime example of Martin's and by extension, Trump's, approach to the pardon power. Described by Trump as a "wonderful person, who was persecuted by the Radical Left 'monsters,'" the pardon of Sheriff Jenkins is a disturbing testament to the politicization of what should be a measured and just process. Jenkins' actions, labeled a "cash-for-badges scheme" by Acting U.S. Attorney Zachary T. Lee, hardly seem to merit the label of political persecution.

"No MAGA Left Behind": Martin's declaration on social media, a vow of "No MAGA left behind" in reference to pardons, is as blatant an admission of politicization as one might find. Critics, rightly, have blasted Martin for this stance. Anna Bower of Lawfare and Eric Columbus, with his background in the DOJ during the Obama administration, both point out the glaring politicization at play. Matthew Gertz of Media Matters succinctly captures the essence of Martin's role: "doling out pardons to every MAGA crank who enjoys crimes."

Analysis: What we're witnessing is not just the weaponization of the pardon power, but a broader strategy of governance by loyalty rather than law. The implications of such a stance are deeply troubling. It suggests a future where legal accountability is contingent on political allegiance, undermining the very foundation of our democratic system. The message sent by Martin's actions and Trump's endorsements is clear: allegiance to Trump and his ideology may well be a get-out-of-jail-free card in today's America.

In a democracy, the pardon power is a critical tool for mercy and justice, meant to correct wrongs, not perpetuate them. What Martin, under Trump’s direction, seems to be advocating for is a perversion of this power, transforming it into a partisan tool rather than a constitutional safeguard.

Conclusion: The politicization of the pardon process under Trump, with Martin as its cheerleader, is not just an aberration; it's a stark warning. The integrity of our justice system, the impartiality of our institutions, and the very principles of our democracy are at stake. The scenario unfolding is a systemic error that demands correction, lest we find ourselves in a reality where legal guilt or innocence is overshadowed by political loyalty. This is not just about pardons; it's about the soul of American governance.
