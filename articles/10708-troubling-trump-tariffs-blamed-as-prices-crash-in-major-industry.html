'Troubling': Trump tariffs blamed as prices 'crash' in major industry

Crashing lumber prices are sending a "troubling" warning sign for the U.S. economy following uncertainty over President Donald Trump's tariffs and a "deteriorating housing market," the Wall Street Journal reported Monday.

"Crashing wood prices are troubling because they have been a reliable leading indicator on the direction of the housing market as well as broader economic activity," according to the Journal.

Two U.S. sawyers last week said they will decelerate production and "curtail output, slowing the decline."

Trump tariffs and import taxes have caused unpredictable impacts on supply chains.

"During the Covid-19 lockdown, two-by-four prices nearly tripled the prepandemic record, an early sign of the inflation and broken supply chains that would bedevil the economic reopening," the Journal noted. "When the Federal Reserve began raising interest rates in 2022 to curb inflation, lumber was among the first assets to decline in value. Now, prices are signaling caution again."

Lumber prices have had a turbulent road. In anticipation of Trump's threatened higher duties on Canadian imports and tariffs on wood, a surplus of wood was set aside in the United States.

Wood prices climbed in the spring when the White House claimed "it was investigating national security aspects of imported lumber and Trump threatened steep tariffs on all Canadian goods."

The price plummeted when Trump let down tariff talk on Canada, and then prices surged again in May when buyers were looking ahead.

"In May, they started surging again as buyers began stocking up ahead of the scheduled hike in existing Canadian lumber duties and Trump’s threatened tariffs," according to WSJ.

The Trump administration is reportedly considering more tariffs — as it did with aluminum, steel and copper products — on imported wood, citing national security concerns.

Producers plan to continue cutting back on production.

In July, residential building permits slipped to just 1.4 million units, a seasonally adjusted annual rate, and the fewest units in construction since June 2020.

Construction spending in the U.S. dropped by 3.4% in July compared to the record amount set in May 2024.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Lumbering Warning Signs in the U.S. Economy

The latest piece from the Wall Street Journal paints a grim picture of the U.S. economy, attributing part of its woes to crashing lumber prices. This isn't just a minor hiccup in an otherwise robust economic framework; it's a "troubling" warning sign, signaling deeper issues at play, particularly in the housing market and broader economic activity. Let's dissect the key points and the underlying causes with a critical eye.

The Trump Tariff Tango

At the heart of this economic conundrum are President Donald Trump's tariffs and import taxes, which have sent shockwaves through supply chains. The unpredictability these policies have introduced is nothing short of chaotic. It's a classic case of political maneuvering having real-world consequences, and not the kind that fosters stability or growth.

The Lumber Rollercoaster

Lumber prices have been on what can only be described as a rollercoaster ride, influenced heavily by the anticipation of, and actual implementation of, Trump's tariffs, especially those targeting Canadian imports. This price volatility isn't just a market anomaly; it's a reflection of deeper systemic issues. When the White House throws around threats of tariffs under the guise of national security, only to back down later, it creates an environment of uncertainty and speculation that benefits no one.

A Symptom of Broader Economic Malaise

The WSJ correctly points out that lumber prices are a "reliable leading indicator" of housing market health and broader economic trends. The deterioration of this market, coupled with the Federal Reserve's interest rate hikes in 2022 to curb inflation, has put the economy in a precarious position. It's a cautionary tale of how interconnected and sensitive our economic system is to both policy changes and market speculation.

The Fallout

What's particularly alarming is the downward spiral this has triggered: decreased construction spending, a slump in residential building permits, and producers cutting back on production. This isn't merely an academic concern; it's a tangible slowdown that affects jobs, housing availability, and the overall economic outlook.

The Bigger Picture

The fluctuation in lumber prices and the subsequent impact on the U.S. economy is a microcosm of a larger issue: the need for a more stable and predictable policy environment. The current approach, characterized by threats and reversals, does nothing but harm. It's time for a policy framework that considers the long-term implications on all sectors of the economy, rather than short-term political gains.

In conclusion, the "troubling" warning signs flagged by the Wall Street Journal are a wake-up call. They underscore the need for a more thoughtful and consistent approach to economic policy, one that avoids unnecessary turbulence and fosters a stable environment for growth. The lumber market, with its ups and downs, serves as a stark reminder of the broader economic vulnerabilities we face and the urgent need for corrective action.
