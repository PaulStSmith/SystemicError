Epstein victims 'who have not spoken before' to fly to DC to confront Mike Johnson

Victims of Jeffrey Epstein “who have not spoken [publicly] before” are poised to arrive in Washington, D.C. next week to put pressure on lawmakers amid fears that House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) may use his authority to block an effort to force the release of files related to Epstein.

The Epstein victims will be speaking at a press conference sometime next week, Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) announced this week during an appearance on “Breaking Points,” a follow-up to the explosive presser held early last month, and is set to coincide with a potential vote on a bill that would compel the Justice Department to release all of its files on Epstein.

“We have a press conference that Thomas Massie (R-KY), Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) and I are planning next week, and a number of survivors from around the country are going to be flying in, some who have not spoken out before,” Khanna said.

“Mike Johnson keeps thinking ‘okay, if you just shut down Congress long enough, people are going to forget,’ but he doesn’t realize that this story has gripped the American people. They know there were horrific acts that were committed, and I think when they hear from these women again – these brave women – next week, it’s going to make sure that we have action and an overwhelming vote in the House.

Khanna and Massie have introduced what’s known as a discharge petition, which, with 218 signatures, would force a vote on a particular bill – in this case, a bill that would force the DOJ’s hand in releasing files on Epstein. The petition secured its 218 signature this week after Rep. Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ) was sworn in, 52 days after she was elected to Congress.

And yet, despite the discharge petition being specifically designed to circumvent House leadership and force a vote on a particular measure, Johnson could still very well attempt to block it, Khanna warned.

“Johnson has dozens of procedural motions he can try to obstruct it; we could spend the whole day talking about the tools he has,” Khanna continued.

“The confidence I have though is that there are a lot of Republicans who do not want the discharge petition tool to be rendered useless, they want to use it… to get votes on other reforms, so I’m hopeful the coalition will hold to say you’ve got to bring this for a vote. If Johnson stops the petition from getting a vote, that will hurt any Republican who wants to bring any bill using that mechanism.”

Khanna’s concerns have been echoed by Massie, who in July warned that Johnson may “try to pull a fast one” and rewrite House rules in an effort to block their discharge petition.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Saga: A Battle for Transparency and Accountability

In what reads like a political thriller but is, unfortunately, the grim reality of our times, victims of Jeffrey Epstein are set to descend upon Washington, D.C., in a poignant act of bravery and a desperate bid for justice. Their aim? To pressure lawmakers into releasing files related to Epstein, fearing that House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) may deploy his authority to obstruct this effort. This scenario underscores not just the enduring impact of Epstein’s heinous acts, but also a significant test of political will and integrity at the highest levels of power.

The Press Conference: A Call for Action

Announced by Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) during an appearance on “Breaking Points,” the upcoming press conference is a strategic move to reignite public and legislative interest in the Epstein case. The involvement of victims, particularly those who have remained silent until now, adds a deeply personal and compelling dimension to the call for transparency. It’s a stark reminder that behind the legal and political maneuverings are real human lives irreparably damaged by Epstein’s crimes. Khanna’s assertion that the story has “gripped the American people” is not just rhetoric; it’s a fact. The public’s fascination and horror at the Epstein saga signal a collective yearning for justice and answers.

The Discharge Petition: A Test of Democracy

The discharge petition, spearheaded by Khanna and Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), represents a rare procedural tactic designed to bypass the usual legislative gatekeeping by House leadership. The fact that this petition has garnered the necessary 218 signatures to force a vote is a significant achievement. It symbolizes not merely a procedural victory but a moral one, signaling a bipartisan desire to confront the Epstein case head-on.

However, the shadow of potential obstruction by House Speaker Mike Johnson looms large. Khanna’s warning about Johnson’s capacity to deploy “dozens of procedural motions” to thwart the petition’s progress is a chilling reminder of the political machinations that can undermine democratic processes. Johnson’s resistance, as Khanna suggests, does not just threaten the Epstein-related bill but jeopardizes the very utility of the discharge petition as a tool for legislative action. This is not just a matter of political strategy; it’s a question of ethical governance.

The Republican Dilemma: A Question of Integrity

The situation places Republican lawmakers in a particularly precarious position. On one hand, supporting the discharge petition aligns them with a push for transparency and justice, values that resonate deeply with the public. On the other, opposing it—or allowing Johnson to neutralize the petition—risks portraying them as complicit in the silencing of victims and the shielding of potentially explosive truths.

Rep. Thomas Massie’s warning that Johnson might “try to pull a fast one” by rewriting House rules to block the petition is an alarming prospect. It suggests a willingness to manipulate the legislative framework to serve individual or partisan interests, rather than those of justice and transparency.

Conclusion: The Road Ahead

As the victims of Jeffrey Epstein prepare to make their voices heard in Washington, D.C., the political arena is faced with a critical test. Will lawmakers rise to the occasion and uphold the principles of transparency, accountability, and justice? Or will procedural obstacles and political maneuvering prevail, leaving the public’s demand for truth unmet?

This episode is not just a chapter in the sordid tale of Jeffrey Epstein. It’s a litmus test for American democracy, revealing the extent to which it can confront and address the darkest aspects of its society. The outcome of this battle will speak volumes about the state of our political system and the moral compass of those who navigate it.
