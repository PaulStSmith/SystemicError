Stephen Miller rails against ‘monstrous’ wind farms: ‘Visual assault on God’s creation’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Stephen Miller’s Windmill Tirade Is Not Energy Policy. It Is Grievance Politics With a Federal Seal.

The Real Power Here

Stephen Miller is not a random pundit posting into the void. He is the White House deputy chief of staff, speaking from inside the machinery of federal power and broadcasting to more than 1.7 million followers. That matters. When an official like that attacks wind power, he is not merely expressing taste. He is signaling what the administration wants its base to hate and what it wants the public to stop scrutinizing.

This is what institutional power looks like when it drops the pretense of governing and starts performing resentment.

The Source Material

Miller reacted to a photo of wind turbines off Virginia Beach by calling them “monstrous” and a “visual assault on God’s creation.” He claimed they exist only because of federal subsidy, weaken the grid, damage marine ecosystems, and force reliance on foreign fuel. The problem for his little sermon is that the Trump administration’s own Energy Department publicly says wind power is cost-effective and strengthens energy reserves.

That is the whole story in miniature: one arm of the government promotes a fossil-fuel fantasy while another quietly contradicts it with basic facts.

Who Is Misdirecting Whom

Miller frames wind turbines as an offense against nature and national strength, but the evidence in the source points the other way. The deliberate distortion is not confusion. It is a choice. He is laundering a political preference for fossil fuels through culture-war language, then dressing it up as concern about the grid and the environment.

This is classic blame inversion. The technology is treated as the problem, while the real beneficiaries of the attack remain invisible: entrenched fuel interests, political patrons of extraction, and officials who would rather posture than govern on evidence.

What He Actually Said

The rhetoric matters because it reveals the method. “God’s creation,” “monstrous,” “visual assault” are not policy arguments. They are emotional triggers designed to turn infrastructure into an enemy. Once the public is trained to see turbines as ugly intrusions, it becomes easier to defend the older order that poisons the air, destabilizes the climate, and keeps power concentrated in familiar hands.

Miller’s post is not an analysis of energy systems. It is an act of political conditioning.

The Pattern

This is the broader pattern: authoritarian-adjacent politics substitutes spectacle for administration and grievance for analysis. It uses official authority to amplify false certainty, then treats institutional contradiction as irrelevant. Even when the government’s own Energy Department says the opposite, the propaganda can still travel faster than the correction.

That is the danger here. Not just that one senior official dislikes wind farms, but that he uses the prestige of office to normalize contempt for evidence and to steer public feeling toward a fossil-fuel hierarchy he helps protect.
