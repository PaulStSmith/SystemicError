Trump's top advisers are 'deeply frustrated' over Tulsi Gabbard's latest 'blunder': report

Director of National Intelligence (DNI) Tulsi Gabbard looks on, on the day she testifies before a Senate Intelligence Committee hearing on worldwide threats, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 25, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Donald Trump's Director of National Intelligence (DNI) Tulsi Gabbard is in hot water with the president's top advisers, according to new reporting.

Gabbard, a former Democrat who was accepted into the White House fold, left Trump's chief aides completely in the dark when it came to certain security clearances, according to the Guardian's weekend report.

In an exclusive called "Tulsi Gabbard did not alert White House before revoking 37 security clearances," the outlet reported, "Tulsi Gabbard, the director of national intelligence, did not inform the White House that her office was revoking the security clearances of 37 people – including top deputies to the CIA director, John Ratcliffe – before it happened last month, according to three people familiar with matter."

The article continues, stating, "The move caused consternation because it resulted in the White House not having an opportunity to closely vet the list before it became public and there appeared to be no paper trail from the president directing the effort, the people said."

"As a result, officials only realized after the fact that Gabbard had managed to pull the security clearances of career CIA officials, at least one of whom was a top adviser to Ratcliffe and had worked on some of the US’s most sensitive military operations, the people said," according to the report. "The list also included two Democratic congressional staffers – Maher Bitar, the national security adviser to senator Adam Schiff, and Thomas West, an aide on the Senate foreign relations committee – prompting fears the administration would be thrust into a messy separation-of-powers issue."

But things reportedly didn't end there, as now some close to Trump are apparently holding a grudge.

"Weeks later, several of Trump’s top advisers remain deeply frustrated with Gabbard and view the episode as a blunder that comes as Trump is skeptical of the intelligence community and has suggested dismantling the office of the director of national intelligence (ODNI)," according to The Guardian's reporting.

Our Analysis:

Tulsi Gabbard's Security Clearance Fiasco: A Closer Look

In what reads like a plot from a political thriller, Tulsi Gabbard, Donald Trump's Director of National Intelligence (DNI), has apparently decided to play rogue agent within the administration. According to a weekend report by The Guardian, Gabbard left Trump's chief aides out in the cold by revoking the security clearances of 37 individuals, including key players within the CIA, without so much as a heads-up to the White House. The title "Tulsi Gabbard did not alert White House before revoking 37 security clearances" barely scratches the surface of the debacle that unfolded.

The Unsanctioned Move

Gabbard's action is not just a breach of protocol; it's a stark display of unilateral decision-making in a realm where collaboration and communication are supposed to be paramount. The revocation included top deputies to the CIA director, John Ratcliffe, which by itself could fuel a few conspiracy theories. But the lack of a paper trail from the president directing this effort is what turns this situation from concerning to alarming.

The Fallout

The fallout from this move is multifaceted. Firstly, the White House was blindsided, unable to vet the list before it became public knowledge. This lack of oversight is troubling, given the sensitive nature of the roles involved. Among those whose clearances were pulled were career CIA officials and at least one top adviser to Ratcliffe, who had been involved in the U.S.'s most sensitive military operations. The inclusion of two Democratic congressional staffers, Maher Bitar and Thomas West, adds a layer of political intrigue and hints at potential separation-of-powers issues.

The Underlying Issues

The article points to a deeper malaise within the Trump administration: a distrust of the intelligence community. Trump's skepticism and thoughts on dismantling the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) are not new. However, Gabbard's actions, whether intended or not, play directly into this narrative, potentially undermining the very foundations of the U.S. intelligence apparatus.

The Grudge

The reaction from Trump's top advisers speaks volumes. Their frustration with Gabbard, weeks after the incident, underscores the severity of the blunder. The term "blunder," however, might be too generous. This was not a minor oversight; it was a significant operational decision made in isolation. The implications are far-reaching, affecting not just internal dynamics within the administration but also the perception of the U.S. intelligence community both domestically and abroad.

Conclusion

Tulsi Gabbard's unilateral decision to revoke the security clearances of 37 individuals without notifying the White House is more than a faux pas; it's a systemic error that highlights the fragile state of affairs within the Trump administration. The lack of communication, oversight, and apparent disregard for established protocols raises serious questions about the administration's approach to national security and intelligence. As the dust settles, one thing is clear: this incident is a symptom of a larger problem, and unless addressed, it could have lasting implications for the U.S.'s ability to protect its interests and maintain its integrity on the world stage.
