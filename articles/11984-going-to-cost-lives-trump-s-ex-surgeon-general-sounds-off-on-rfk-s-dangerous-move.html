'Going to cost lives': Trump's ex-surgeon general sounds off on RFK's 'dangerous' move

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 7, 20255 min read

The decision Tuesday night by Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the U.S. Health Secretary, to cut nearly $500 million in federal funding for the development of mRNA vaccines, was too far even for a former Trump surgeon general.

Kennedy halted 22 projects primarily targeting respiratory viruses like COVID-19 and influenza. Federal contracts and proposals with leading pharmaceutical companies — including Pfizer, Moderna, and Sanofi — were affected. Investments managed by the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority, or BARDA, were also pulled back.

Kennedy, a vaccine conspiracy theorist, claimed that mRNA technology doesn’t “perform well against viruses that infect the upper respiratory tract," and baselessly claimed they “can actually prolong pandemics as the virus constantly mutates to escape the protective effects of the vaccine.”

“We’re prioritizing the development of safer, broader vaccine strategies like whole virus vaccines and novel platforms that don’t collapse when viruses mutate,” Kennedy said, according to Rolling Stone.

Jerome Adams, who served as surgeon general much of President Donald Trump’s first term, tore into Kennedy's decision in a post on X.

"I’ve tried to be objective & non-alarmist in response to current HHS actions – but quite frankly this move is going to cost lives. mRNA technology has uses that go far beyond vaccines… and the vaccine they helped develop in record time is credited with saving millions," he said.

In a separate post, Adams agreed with infectious diseases doctor Jake Scott, who said the idea that mRNA vaccines "failed" because they didn't block all respiratory infections "reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of immunology."

"No vaccine for flu, RSV, or COVID has ever done that. The goal is preventing severe disease, and mRNA vaccines delivered," said Scott. "They saved millions of lives and gave us a platform that can be rapidly updated as new threats emerge. Walking away from that undermines our ability to respond to the next pandemic and puts lives at risk."

Adams agreed with Scott's assessment and added, "We must also acknowledge that part of the blame lies in (often politicized) messaging that this was a 'pandemic of the unvaccinated,' and that 'if you get this vaccine you won’t get Covid.'"

In another thread, Adams touted mRNA research for helping develop COVID vaccines in "record time" and saving millions of lives.

"Cutting half a billion in mRNA funding, as HHS Sec. Kennedy proposes, isn’t just bad policy - it’s dangerous," he said.

Adams warned that attacking mRNA research means "turning our backs on more than just COVID vaccines."

"This platform is being used to develop vaccines and therapies for: Flu RSV HIV Zika Cancer (yes, cancer) Autoimmune diseases And more... Ending and/or politicizing support risks ALL of that."

Our Analysis:

The Fatal Flaw in Kennedy's Cutting Crusade

An Unfounded Assault on mRNA

In a move that can only be described as both breathtakingly ignorant and dangerously shortsighted, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the U.S. Health Secretary, has taken a sledgehammer to nearly $500 million in federal funding earmarked for the development of mRNA vaccines. This isn't just a policy shift; it's a full-blown assault on modern science and public health.

Kennedy's decision to halt 22 projects—projects that are at the forefront of combating respiratory viruses like COVID-19 and influenza—reveals a staggering disregard for the potential these vaccines hold. The affected entities aren't obscure labs operating on the fringes of medical science; we're talking about pharmaceutical titans like Pfizer, Moderna, and Sanofi. This isn't just pulling the plug; it's effectively dynamiting the dam while the village downstream sleeps.

The Conspiracy Theorist at the Helm

Kennedy, who has long been marred by his association with vaccine conspiracy theories, bases his decision on claims so unfounded they would be laughable were they not so tragic. The assertion that mRNA technology doesn’t “perform well against viruses that infect the upper respiratory tract" flies in the face of the global scientific consensus that mRNA vaccines have been a game-changer in the fight against COVID-19. His baseless claim that such vaccines “can actually prolong pandemics as the virus constantly mutates to escape the protective effects of the vaccine” is, frankly, a fabrication that undermines public health.

The Voices of Reason in the Wilderness

Enter Jerome Adams, former Trump surgeon general, who has emerged as a voice of reason against Kennedy's folly. Adams' critique is not just a polite disagreement; it's a blistering condemnation of a policy that, in his words, “is going to cost lives.” Adams highlights the multifaceted utility of mRNA technology—its applications extending far beyond mere vaccines to encompass treatments for diseases from flu to cancer.

Adams finds an ally in Jake Scott, an infectious diseases doctor, who succinctly points out that expecting vaccines to block all respiratory infections is a “fundamental misunderstanding of immunology.” The real victory of mRNA vaccines has been their ability to prevent severe disease, a fact blatantly ignored by Kennedy’s crusade.

The Real Cost of Kennedy's Decision

This isn’t merely about halting funding; it’s about actively dismantling our defense against future pandemics. Kennedy’s misguided policy doesn’t just threaten the advancement in combating COVID-19; it jeopardizes research into vaccines and therapies for a spectrum of diseases, including HIV, Zika, and even cancer.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Gamble

Kennedy’s decision to slash funding for mRNA vaccine development is not just bad policy—it’s a perilous gamble with public health. It’s a decision that flies in the face of science, endangers lives, and sets a disastrous precedent for the management of future health crises. The voices of reason, like Adams and Scott, are not merely critics; they are the beacons of sanity in a policy landscape gone mad. As this debacle unfolds, one can only hope that reason will prevail over recklessness. But until then, we stand on the precipice of a self-inflicted wound whose scars will be felt for generations to come.
