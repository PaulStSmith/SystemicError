America faces a 'Weekend at Bernie's' situation with declining Trump: ex-GOP lawmaker

By Paulo Santos

A former House Republican warned Friday that President Donald Trump‘s cognitive decline reveals “the most dangerous reality” as the president expresses concerns about his own mortality while his “powerful advisers pursue their own agendas” — and that the United States could end up with a “Weekend at Bernie’s president.”

Former Republican lawmaker and Air National Guard member Adam Kinzinger questioned when this apparent cognitive decline could worsen and what those around him plan to do in a Substack essay.

“The question here is, is this an act, is he really losing his mind, is it both?” Kinzinger said in a Substack video.

“You don’t know anymore what’s an act, what’s real, but I mean, it feels like he’s even descended since the beginning of his term,” Kinzinger said. “Three years left of this and that’s the question so when he does, let’s say he does hit a point where he’s completely out of it, would there be anybody that had the courage to do the 25th Amendment, so you may ultimately end up with a ‘Weekend at Bernie’s’ president... I just think it’s something we need to look at, consider, and think about, having an insane president — I think we have an insane president — but having an insane president that actually can’t think because he’s lost it.”

Kinzinger details the president’s most recent comments about heaven.

“How do we know he’s worried? Recently, he’s started talking about his own mortality,” Kinzinger writes. “’I want to try and get to heaven, if possible,’ he said. ‘I’m hearing I’m not doing well. I’m really at the bottom of the totem pole.’ On another occasion: ‘I don’t think there’s anything going to get me in heaven. I really don’t. I think I’m not maybe heaven-bound.’ He’s also begun warning that one day he’ll fall down — the same kind of stumble he once mocked Biden for.”

The president might say he’s fine, but he actually isn’t, said Kinzinger.

“ Trump shows visible signs of age-related circulatory issues known as venous insufficiency, which causes swelling in the ankles and bruising on the hands. For a man who’s long claimed to be immune to aging, the visible evidence must be unsettling,” Kinzinger wrote.

Trump might be signaling he’s aware of what’s happening, while he falls deeper into QAnon and conspiratorial territory, including his recent Truth Social post with a fake “med bed.”

“These references to heaven and falling suggest an awareness of his own fragility,” Kinzinger writes. “The physical decline may not be as worrying as the mental one. His father, Fred Trump Sr., displayed clear signs of dementia years before being diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease — and heredity is a known risk factor. Preventing or slowing such decline requires habits Trump has always resisted: a healthy diet, exercise, and humility.”

It also raises questions about the people around him — and how vicious they could be, he adds.

“What does it mean to have a president in visible decline? It means we must watch carefully and hope those around him are competent. In the case of the Israeli–Hamas peace effort, skilled negotiators from the U.S. and abroad did the heavy lifting while Trump played a ceremonial role. A similar pattern is unfolding domestically, where powerful advisers pursue their own agendas.” Kinzinger wrote. “Stephen Miller drives the crackdown on immigrants and the push to use the National Guard. Robert F. Kennedy Jr. is leading the assault on public health. Russell Vought is overseeing the mass firing of thousands of federal workers.”

It’s unclear what those around him will ultimately do.

“The truth is, what Donald Trump says matters less than what those behind him do. And that may be the most dangerous reality of all,” Kinzinger argues.

Our Analysis:

The Alleged Decline of a President: A Critical Analysis

In a recent blast from the political cannon, Adam Kinzinger, a former House Republican and Air National Guard member, has launched a scathing critique against President Donald Trump, focusing on what he perceives to be the president’s cognitive decline. This isn’t just idle gossip from the political sidelines; it’s a serious accusation that, if true, could have profound implications for the United States. Let’s dissect Kinzinger’s arguments with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned analyst.

The Core of Kinzinger’s Critique

Kinzinger’s primary concern seems to be the president’s mental fitness, a topic that is as sensitive as it is controversial. He posits that Trump’s cognitive abilities might be deteriorating, citing the president’s own musings about mortality and heaven as evidence of his existential dread—or perhaps an acknowledgment of his aging and its inevitable toll. Kinzinger goes so far as to speculate about the potential for invoking the 25th Amendment, a measure reserved for circumstances in which a president is unable to fulfill the duties of the office. This is no light suggestion and signals Kinzinger’s deep concerns—or perhaps, his political motivations.

Evidence or Conjecture?

Kinzinger points to Trump’s visible signs of aging and his recent preoccupation with topics of mortality as indicators of his decline. However, one must ask: Are these signs truly indicative of cognitive decline, or are they the natural manifestations of aging? Aging, after all, is not selective in its targets, affecting every human being, president or not. Moreover, Kinzinger’s reference to Trump’s father’s dementia as a hereditary risk factor, while medically sound, seems to tread dangerously close to speculation without concrete evidence of Trump’s mental state.

Power Plays and Presidential Puppetry

The former congressman doesn’t stop at Trump’s health; he delves into the dynamics of power surrounding the president. He suggests that Trump’s “powerful advisers” might be the ones truly running the show, pursuing their own agendas under the guise of Trump’s presidency. This assertion, if true, paints a troubling picture of a figurehead president manipulated by those in the shadows. Kinzinger names Stephen Miller, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and Russell Vought as key figures driving their own policies, from immigration crackdowns to public health assaults and federal worker firings.

The Dangerous Reality?

Kinzinger concludes with a stark warning: The real danger lies not in what Trump says, but in what his advisers do behind the scenes. This raises questions about the true locus of power within the Trump administration and the potential consequences of a presidency that might be more figurehead than leader.

Analysis and Commentary

Kinzinger’s essay is a provocative piece that invites scrutiny not just of Trump but of the political ecosystem that surrounds him. However, it’s crucial to distinguish between legitimate concerns about a leader’s ability to govern and politically motivated attacks. Kinzinger, a known critic of Trump within the Republican Party, may have valid points, but his critique must be balanced against the evidence—or lack thereof.

The implications of Kinzinger’s accusations are grave, suggesting a presidency in peril, either because of the president’s health or the machinations of those around him. Yet, one must approach such claims with caution, recognizing the fine line between observation and alarmism.

In the end, the health of a president is a matter of public interest, but it must be discussed with care, respect, and a staunch commitment to factual accuracy. Kinzinger’s concerns may well be genuine, but they also serve as a reminder of the deep divisions within American politics and the need for a more nuanced understanding of leadership and its challenges in the modern age.
