'Gutter racist': Outrage swamps Hegseth as news that he snubbed Black colonel spreads

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 29, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real Story Behind the Scandal

In an unusually contentious display of power within the U.S. Department of Defense, Secretary Pete Hegseth has vetoed the promotions of two Black and two female colonels to one-star generals. This decision has triggered a significant backlash, with accusations of racism and sexism swirling around an already polarized administration. But to understand the full scope and implications of this decision, it’s vital to dissect the layers of power, decision-making, and accountability at play.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

At the heart of this controversy is Secretary Hegseth, a figure appointed by President Donald Trump, whose past statements and policies have often been criticized as racially and sexually biased. Hegseth has not only the institutional power but also the presidential backing to influence military promotions—a process typically guided by merit and the recommendations of senior military officials, in this case, Army Secretary Daniel Driscoll.

Despite Driscoll’s support for the colonels, based on their “decadeslong records of exemplary service,” Hegseth’s blockage speaks volumes about the true nature of power dynamics within the military’s leadership. It’s crucial to note here that the decision to block these promotions was Hegseth’s, influenced perhaps by the broader administrative ethos but ultimately signed off by him.

Misdirection and Responsibility

The narrative surrounding this issue has been charged with allegations of animus toward Black individuals and women. This is compounded by an alleged comment from Hegseth’s chief of staff, Lt. Col. Ricky Buria, who reportedly stated that President Trump would prefer not to be seen with Major General Antoinette Gant, a Black female officer. This comment, while officially denied, aligns uncomfortably well with the administration’s track record and public statements, adding a layer of credibility to the accusations of racial and gender bias.

However, the responsibility for the promotion blockages rests squarely on Hegseth’s shoulders. Any attempt to shift this responsibility onto lower-ranking officials or even onto President Trump himself is a clear misdirection. While Trump’s preferences and prejudices may influence the atmosphere and general policy direction, the institutional power to promote or block rests with Hegseth.

The Broader Impact

This decision does not exist in a vacuum. It reflects and possibly exacerbates ongoing issues within the U.S. military regarding diversity and inclusion. Hegseth’s own public disdain for what he calls “whores to wokesters” suggests a deep-seated aversion to recent efforts aimed at enhancing diversity within the military ranks. This stance not only affects the individuals involved but could have broader implications for military cohesion and the perception of the U.S. armed forces both domestically and internationally.

The overwhelming composition of military leadership—predominantly white and male, despite the diverse nature of the troops—speaks to systemic issues that go beyond individual decisions. Yet, individual decisions like those made by Hegseth play a crucial role in either challenging or perpetuating these disparities.

Conclusion

The blocking of these promotions by Secretary Hegseth is a clear demonstration of how individual biases and systemic structures interact to reinforce each other. While it’s easy to point fingers at the wider administration or societal prejudices, the fact remains that Hegseth, in his capacity as Secretary of Defense, made a decision that directly impacts the careers of deserving officers and the broader ethos of the U.S. military. It is a decision that should be scrutinized, criticized, and remembered as a deliberate act of exclusion, reflective of a broader agenda rather than an isolated incident of poor judgment.
