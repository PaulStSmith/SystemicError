'They had to use a saw': Trump reportedly burying Epstein drives pried from safe

Donald Trump's administration says it has released all the info it has on Jeffrey Epstein, but there is additional "evidence" being held back, according to a former prosecutor on Saturday.

Former State Attorney for Palm Beach County Dave Aronberg appeared on MSNBC this weekend, where he was asked about the recent controversial announcement from the Department of Justice that there is no Epstein "client list," which has become the subject of conspiracy theories among the MAGA faithful.

While "the right wing conspiracy theorists want to see a client list," there "is no client list," according to Aronberg, who added, "But there is evidence of third parties who were involved in this."

"After all, what was Ghislaine Maxwell prosecuted for if there was no third party who was getting these young girls trafficked to them? And so there is evidence," he said before flagging a raid.

"In 2019, there was a raid of Jeffrey Epstein's New York mansion, and there was a safe, and they had to use a saw to get in the safe. And there were hard drives or thumb drives in there. What's on the thumb drives? We really still don't know," he said. "So I think there's a lot of images. I think there are ties to individuals. And perhaps the DOJ thinks there's not enough evidence to file a lawsuit, especially when you saw that big celebrities like Diddy just got away with human trafficking, allegedly."

He continued, saying the decision came down to "a calculus."

"But yes, I do think that people have a right to ask questions because I don't think the evidence shows that it's a buttoned up case. There are individuals out there. It's just not a client list like the right wing believe. This is not a cabal of Democrats who had a big sex trafficking ring in the basement of a pizza shop, so they need to get over that."

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Conundrum: Unraveling the Web of Secrecy

In a recent MSNBC interview, former State Attorney for Palm Beach County, Dave Aronberg, made some startling comments regarding the ongoing Jeffrey Epstein saga. Despite the Trump administration's claims of transparency, Aronberg suggests that not all cards are on the table. This raises a glaring question: What is being hidden, and why?

The Myth of the "Client List"

Aronberg attempts to dispel the notion of a so-called Epstein "client list," a concept that has fueled countless conspiracy theories, particularly among MAGA supporters. But let's cut through the fog here. The absence of a "client list" does not equate to an absence of guilt or association. As Aronberg rightly points out, Ghislaine Maxwell's prosecution for trafficking hinges on the presence of third parties—the very individuals who remain shrouded in mystery.

The Safe, The Drives, and The Silence

The raid on Epstein's New York mansion in 2019 unearthed hard and thumb drives in a safe—a safe that had to be forcibly opened. The contents of these drives? Still a question mark. This is where the narrative veers into the realm of the absurd. The implication that there are "ties to individuals" but possibly not enough evidence to pursue legal action is a convenient conclusion, especially considering the high-profile nature of some alleged associates. This isn't about lacking evidence; it's about lacking the will to pursue justice.

A Calculus of Convenience

Aronberg's reference to a "calculus" behind the DOJ's decision-making process is particularly telling. It suggests a weighing of outcomes, where the scales of justice are tipped not by evidence but by potential fallout. This isn't justice; it's political and social expediency. The mention of celebrities like Diddy "getting away" with alleged crimes only adds fuel to the fire of systemic failure.

The Bigger Picture

Let's not mince words. The Epstein case is symptomatic of a larger, systemic issue that transcends political lines. The insinuation that this is merely a fixation of "right-wing conspiracy theorists" is a red herring. Yes, the outrageous claims about a "cabal of Democrats" and pizza shop basements are ludicrous. But let's not allow such extremes to blind us to the very real, very bipartisan nature of corruption and influence-peddling that allows figures like Epstein to exist—and to flourish.

In Conclusion

The Epstein saga is far from over. As Aronberg's interview suggests, there are still untold stories, hidden evidence, and powerful individuals who have yet to be held accountable. This isn't about partisan witch hunts; it's about piercing the veil of secrecy that protects the elite at the expense of the vulnerable.

In a just world, those hard drives would be cracked open for all to see. But we don't live in a just world. We live in a world where the calculus of power decides what truths are revealed and what sins remain hidden. And until that changes, the systemic error at the heart of our society will persist, unchallenged and unchecked.
