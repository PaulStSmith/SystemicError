DOGE could be headed for a 'new, more dangerous phase' as Musk steps away: analyst

Elon Musk walks on the day of a meeting with House Republicans to discuss the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 5, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A decision by billionaire Elon Musk to restrict his involvement in the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) that he created so that he can tend to his ailing Tesla automobile company does not mean that the damage being done by the DOGE staffers will be coming to an end.

According to Guardian journalist David Smith, their work is not done and, with Musk out of the picture as the face of DOGE, things could get a lot worse.

Conceding there is a feeling among some that the "lull" that will follow Musk's department is reason for relief, Smith wrote instead it "presages a new, more dangerous phase."

ALSO READ:'We’ve made a mistake': Trump’s trade war sends GOP into frenzy

Case in point, he notes a CNN report from CNN stating, "Doge is building a master database to accelerate deportations of undocumented immigrants by combining sensitive data from across the federal government."

That led Judd Legum, of the DOGE-tracking Popular Information, to predict, "As they realize there’s nothing left to cut that they want to cut or can cut, they’ll probably turn to more of those more policy-oriented initiatives.”

According to the Guardian's Smith, "CNN reported that Doge is developing a comprehensive database to enhance immigration enforcement. The initiative aims to consolidate sensitive information from various federal agencies, including the IRS and Social Security Administration. Its goal is to create 'targeting lists' for Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to expedite the identification and deportation of undocumented immigrants," adding, "Palantir, a data analytics company, is assisting in building this system, CNN said. Concerns have been raised by congressional Democrats and privacy advocates regarding the legality and potential for misuse of centralised data. A former senior IRS employee with knowledge of the plans was quoted by CNN as saying: 'If they are designing a deportation machine, they will be able to do that.'"

Democratic strategist Chris Morris warned the public should not let its guard down.

“Unfortunately, with anything with this administration and what it started, even when it dies out for a little bit, I don’t take it as they have quit. They retool and they reassess their approach. They feel like this is something that’s very effective. They’re reassessing how to go about it in their next phase," he cautioned.

Our Analysis:

The Sinister Shift in DOGE's Strategy: A Critical Analysis

In a recent turn of events that should alarm any individual with a semblance of concern for privacy and human rights, the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), under the erstwhile stewardship of billionaire Elon Musk, has taken a dark turn. Despite Musk stepping back to focus on his beleaguered Tesla company, the shadow of DOGE's nefarious activities looms larger than ever, hinting at a future fraught with peril for undocumented immigrants and, indeed, the very fabric of democracy.

Musk's Departure: A Red Herring

The narrative that Musk's reduced involvement in DOGE might signal a slowdown in the department's activities is, according to David Smith of The Guardian, a dangerous misconception. Far from being reassured, we should be on high alert. The "lull" that some anticipate could very well be the calm before a storm of unprecedented policy aggressions. Smith's insights lay bare the unsettling truth: DOGE's operations are morphing into something far more sinister.

The Master Database: A Tool for Accelerated Deportation

Central to DOGE's new phase is the development of a master database—a digital Panopticon designed to streamline the deportation of undocumented immigrants by amalgamating sensitive data from a plethora of federal agencies. This initiative, reportedly aided by data analytics giant Palantir, is not just a breach of privacy; it's a full-scale assault on the rights of countless individuals. The creation of "targeting lists" for Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is a chilling reminder of how technology can be weaponized against vulnerable populations.

The legality and ethics of such a centralized database are, unsurprisingly, under scrutiny. Concerns from congressional Democrats and privacy advocates highlight the ominous potential for misuse. A former senior IRS employee's comment to CNN about designing a "deportation machine" is a stark warning of the dystopian direction in which we are headed.

The Dangerous Complacency

Democratic strategist Chris Morris hits the nail on the head: the danger is far from over. The administration's strategy is one of adaptation and recalibration—not surrender. The belief that DOGE's initiatives may temporarily recede only to resurface in a more refined and potentially more devastating form is a realistic and terrifying prospect.

The Verdict: A Call to Vigilance

What we are witnessing with DOGE's evolution is not just an isolated policy shift but a symptomatic manifestation of a broader, more systemic threat to civil liberties and human dignity. The construction of a database to expedite deportations under the guise of government efficiency is a stark illustration of how technological advancements and data analytics can be perverted to serve inhumane ends.

The narrative propagated by those who downplay the significance of Musk's departure from DOGE misses the forest for the trees. It's not just about who is at the helm; it's about the direction in which the ship is sailing. As Smith and Morris articulate, the real danger lies in underestimating the administration's commitment to its draconian policies.

In the end, the creation of DOGE's master database for deportations is a testament to the administration's chilling proficiency in combining technology, data, and policy to further its agenda. It's a wake-up call for all who value human rights and democracy to remain vigilant and resist the normalization of such dystopian measures. The fight for privacy, dignity, and justice must continue with renewed fervor, lest we find ourselves in a world where efficiency trumps empathy, and databases determine destinies.
