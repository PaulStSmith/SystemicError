GOP candidate in ruby red state backtracks from Trump amid panic of Dem victory

A mayoral race in Nebraska's "blue dot" could be a test of voter dissatisfaction with Donald Trump's second presidency — and a bellwether for next year's midterm elections.

Democrats in the liberal enclave in a conservative state are hoping to unseat Omaha's three-term Republican mayor, Jean Stothert, in a technically nonpartisan election. But anger over Trump's agenda has fueled closer-than-expected results in special elections for GOP-held seats in Florida and a decisive victory for Democrats in Wisconsin's Supreme Court race, reported the Washington Post.

“What I’ve been working to do here in this election is capture the momentum of all of the different blue dot groups and get them to be as focused on this election as they are on national elections, reminding them that the local elections actually have more impact on their lives,” said challenger John Ewing Jr., who would become the city's first Black mayor. “So we’ve been able to be very successful with that.”

The race between Ewing, the Douglas County treasurer and a former deputy police chief, and Stothert, who made history as the first woman mayor in 2013, has largely focused on local issues. But Trump and GOP culture war issues have loomed large.

"Ewing has run ads critical of Stothert for backing Trump in 2024," the Times reported. "The Republican mayor, in turn, has put out ads suggesting Ewing 'stands with radicals' on the issue of 'boys in girls’ bathrooms and sports' — leading his campaign to demand a retraction."

Stothert, a former intensive care nurse, has raised nearly twice as much as Ewing, but Democrats have a 39 percent-to-32 percent registration advantage in Nebraska’s 2nd Congressional District, and the GOP incumbent mayor has distanced herself a bit from Trump – whom she says she supported because she wanted a more secure border and a better economy.

“I can honestly say as a Republican, I don’t like everything he’s doing and decisions he’s making," Stothert said in a recent interview. "I wish he’d slow down on a lot of these decisions he’s making. I don’t advise the president.”

Nebraska is one of only two states that split electoral votes among statewide and congressional district winners, and Trump and other conservative leaders intensely pushed the state's legislators to change the rules for awarding electoral votes. But that effort proved unsuccessful and the district was one of only three in the country that split its vote between Kamala Harris and a House Republican – moderate Rep. Don Bacon.

“Not only did we fight like heck and build a coalition to protect the blue dot from being erased by the governor and the legislature, but we then won that blue dot,” said Nebraska Democratic Party Chair Jane Kleeb.

Our Analysis:

Nebraska's Mayoral Race: A Microcosm of National Dissatisfaction

In the heart of Nebraska, a state predominantly painted in broad strokes of Republican red, lies a "blue dot" that's currently the stage for a political drama with implications far beyond its borders. The mayoral race in Omaha is not just a local skirmish but a litmus test for the national mood, specifically the enduring shadow of Donald Trump's divisive politics. Democrats, sensing an opportunity, are rallying behind John Ewing Jr., aiming to unseat the three-term Republican mayor, Jean Stothert. The stakes? More than just local governance — this is about signaling a shift in voter sentiment as we inch towards the midterm elections.

Trump's Influence: A Double-Edged Sword

The article astutely highlights the paradox of Trump's influence. On one hand, his presence energizes the GOP base; on the other, it galvanizes opposition, even in conservative strongholds. Stothert's attempt to distance herself from Trump is telling. Her assertion, "I can honestly say as a Republican, I don’t like everything he’s doing," speaks volumes about the tightrope Republicans must walk — aligning with Trump for base support while disavowing his more controversial actions to appeal to moderates.

Cultural Wars as Campaign Fodder

The descent into cultural war issues, such as the rights of transgender individuals, is a predictable yet disappointing tactic. Stothert's campaign ads accusing Ewing of standing "with radicals" on gender issues are a classic example of fearmongering. It's a diversion, a smokescreen to obscure more pressing local issues. Ewing's response, demanding a retraction, is the right move, but it underscores a larger problem: the nationalization of local politics, where local issues are overshadowed by the specter of national debates.

The Blue Dot Strategy

The Democratic strategy, as articulated by Ewing, to rally the "blue dot" groups highlights a crucial understanding: local elections matter immensely. It's a refreshing perspective in an era where national politics often consume the lion's share of attention. However, one must question, is rallying the base enough? Or should the Democrats also focus on winning over independents and moderate Republicans disillusioned with Trump's GOP?

Economic and Security Justifications

Stothert's support for Trump, justified by a desire for a more secure border and a better economy, is a common refrain among Republicans. Yet, it's essential to dissect these justifications. How do Trump's policies align with Omaha's needs? Is there a direct correlation, or is this an attempt to align with broader GOP themes that may not necessarily reflect local interests?

The Electoral Vote Conundrum

Nebraska's unique electoral vote distribution system, allowing for a split vote, is a double-edged sword. It offers a glimmer of hope for Democrats in conservative states but also makes Nebraska a battleground for electoral manipulation efforts. The unsuccessful push by conservative leaders to change this system underscores the ongoing battle for electoral fairness and representation.

Conclusion: Beyond Omaha

The Omaha mayoral race is more than just a local election; it's a bellwether for the national political climate. The dynamics at play — from Trump's lingering influence to the exploitation of cultural wars and the strategic importance of local elections — reflect broader trends that will undoubtedly impact the upcoming midterms. As Omaha goes, so might the nation, making this race one to watch for anyone invested in the future direction of American politics.
