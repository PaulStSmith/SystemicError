Ken Paxton flees to Fiji with mistress to decompress as divorce finalizes

Based on an article by Brenda Alexander

Paulo SantosNovember 14, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A marriage ends after the political protection holdsTexas Republicans had repeated chances to actThe getaway comes after voters did what institutions would not

Ken Paxton was reportedly headed to Fiji with Christian influencer Tracy Duhon after losing his 2026 statewide race and finalizing his divorce from state Sen. Angela Paxton. TMZ spotted the pair at San Francisco International Airport, dressed for a tropical trip and discussing whether their luggage would reach the island. The vacation is gossip. The political story is that Paxton reached this point only after Texas institutions repeatedly absorbed allegations of misconduct that would have ended a less protected official’s career.

His defeat made him the first Republican in more than three decades to lose a statewide election in Texas, according to AlterNet. But voters acted only after years in which Paxton’s party preserved his power through criminal accusations, corruption investigations and an impeachment trial that publicly exposed his extramarital affair.

A marriage ends after the political protection holds

Angela Paxton alleged adultery when she filed for divorce in 2025, ending a 38-year marriage. She said she acted on “biblical grounds.” Paxton’s lawyer, Jared Julian, said the settlement terms would remain private and described the former spouses as sharing “a mutual respect for one another” while anticipating “the next chapters of their lives,” according to the Houston Chronicle.

That carefully managed language followed years of unusually public scandal. The affair surfaced during Paxton’s 2023 impeachment, but Angela Paxton had remained politically loyal through his legal and institutional crises. Less than a month before the 2026 election, she appeared in an advertisement urging support for the Republican ticket without naming her former husband.

“It’s not as much about the individual players on the team,” she said. “It’s about getting the whole team across the finish line.”

The remark distilled the governing bargain more clearly than any campaign slogan could. Individual conduct became secondary to partisan control. The team needed protection; accountability could wait.

Texas Republicans had repeated chances to act

Paxton’s troubles did not begin with his divorce or electoral defeat. He was indicted in 2015 for allegedly misleading investors. That case ended in 2024 with an agreement requiring restitution and community service, rather than a trial.

In 2020, eight former aides accused him of abusing the attorney general’s office to assist Nate Paul, a wealthy donor and real-estate developer. Their allegations prompted an FBI investigation and ultimately helped produce Paxton’s historic impeachment in 2023. The Texas Senate acquitted him.

That acquittal was an exercise of institutional power, not an absence of agency. Senators had the authority to remove Paxton and chose not to. Whatever private calculations drove individual votes, the public result was unmistakable: a Republican-controlled chamber returned a Republican attorney general to office after his own former aides accused him of using public power for a benefactor.

The affair mattered personally and politically, especially once it became part of the impeachment record. But adultery was never the most consequential allegation surrounding Paxton. Treating the Fiji trip as the culmination of the story risks shrinking a record of alleged abuse of office into celebrity scandal. Duhon’s straw hat and Paxton’s baby-blue hoodie are vivid details; they are not the reason Texans should care how long he remained powerful.

The getaway comes after voters did what institutions would not

Paxton and Duhon reportedly worried aloud about their bags making it to Fiji. Texas Republicans spent years managing heavier baggage on his behalf.

Angela Paxton built a career of her own as a former teacher and school counselor who has represented Texas’ 8th Senate District since 2019. Yet her final campaign intervention reduced political judgment to moving the Republican roster across the finish line. That logic asks voters to treat candidates as interchangeable containers for party power, even when an individual officeholder’s conduct is precisely what requires scrutiny.

Paxton’s electoral loss finally imposed a consequence his party’s governing machinery had repeatedly declined to deliver. His marriage is over, his statewide winning streak is over, and he was reportedly leaving for a tropical vacation with the woman AlterNet identified as his former mistress. The revealing image is not that he fled accountability. It is that, for years, the people with the authority to impose it kept carrying his bags.
