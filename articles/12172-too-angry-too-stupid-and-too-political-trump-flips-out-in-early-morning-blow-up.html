'Too angry, too stupid and too political': Trump flips out in early morning blow-up

President Donald Trump went after Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell early Thursday in yet another rant over his continued refusal to drop interest rates, calling him a “total loser” and blaming him for “costing our country trillions of dollars.”

“Jerome ‘too late’ Powell has done it again!” Trump wrote in a post on his social media platform Truth Social.

“He is too late, and actually, too angry, too stupid and too political to have the job of Fed chair. He is costing our Country trillions of dollars, in addition to one of the most incompetent, or corrupt, renovations of a building(s) in the history of construction! Put another way, ‘too late’ is a total loser, and our country is paying the price!”

Trump has long called for Powell to lower interest rates, which currently sit at around 4.33%, and has floated the idea of replacing him in his spot leading the Federal Reserve. After receiving some blowback, however, Trump has walked that idea back, and instead has shifted to launching an investigation into Powell over a series of renovations to the central bank headquarters in Washington, D.C.

Trump has also labeled Powell as a partisan actor, pointing to the interest rate cuts he and the Federal Reserve implemented under former President Joe Biden.

Experts warn, however, that should Trump ultimately replace Powell with someone willing to cut interest rates to his desired rate of 1%, real-world interest rates and prices couldincrease significantly, causing investor panic and a loss of credibility in the United States.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against Powell: A Masterclass in Misdirection

President Donald Trump's latest outburst against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell is a textbook example of his approach to criticism and accountability: deflect, denigrate, and distort. In a recent rant on his social media platform, Truth Social, Trump lambasted Powell as a "total loser," blaming him for a litany of economic woes and even the cost overruns of a renovation project. This tirade is not just a personal attack; it's a strategic move to shift blame and reshape the narrative around the economy.

The Art of the Blame Game

Trump's accusations against Powell range from incompetence to political bias, claims that are as baseless as they are bold. Calling Powell "too late, and actually, too angry, too stupid and too political" is not just an attack on Powell's character but an attempt to undermine the Federal Reserve's independence. This independence is crucial for maintaining economic stability, free from the whims of political cycles. Yet, Trump's narrative conveniently ignores this, focusing instead on a simplistic solution: lower interest rates.

The Economic Illusionist

Trump's fixation on lowering interest rates to around 1% reveals a profound misunderstanding of or disregard for economic policy and its consequences. Interest rates are a tool to manage economic growth and inflation, not a lever to be pulled for immediate political gain. Experts caution that such drastic cuts could lead to real-world increases in interest rates and prices, sparking investor panic and undermining the United States' credibility. Yet, Trump's narrative sidesteps these nuances in favor of a populist appeal that sounds appealing at first blush but is dangerously simplistic upon closer examination.

The Renovation Red Herring

The attack on Powell over the Federal Reserve building renovations is particularly perplexing. Trump's attempt to link Powell to the supposed "incompetence, or corrupt, renovations" is a clear example of misdirection. This issue, largely administrative and logistical, is twisted into a personal failing of Powell's, diverting attention from more substantive economic discussions. It's a classic Trump move: find a scapegoat for broader systemic issues.

A Partisan Smokescreen

Labeling Powell as a partisan actor serves a dual purpose for Trump. Firstly, it attempts to erode trust in the Federal Reserve, suggesting that its decisions are politically motivated rather than based on economic analysis. Secondly, it deflects from Trump's own history of attempting to politicize the Federal Reserve, as he has long sought to influence its policies to align with his economic agenda.

Conclusion: Trump's Economic Misdirection

Trump's tirade against Jerome Powell is not just an attack on an individual but an assault on the principles of economic governance and the independence of the Federal Reserve. By framing Powell as the villain in his economic narrative, Trump seeks to divert attention from his own policy shortcomings and to undermine public confidence in one of the nation's most critical economic institutions. It's a dangerous game of economic misdirection, with stakes far higher than Trump seems to realize.
