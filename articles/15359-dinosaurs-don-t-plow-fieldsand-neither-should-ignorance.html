Dinosaurs Don't Plow Fields—and Neither Should Ignorance

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 2, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There's a meme making the rounds online that surgically slices into one of the great tensions of our time: the ongoing clash between willful ignorance and scientific literacy. It features an image so absurd it demands a double-take: a man plowing a field with a dinosaur, paired with the biting caption:

"You are perfectly free to teach your kids that the Earth is only 6000 years old, and evolution isn't real, but my kids probably won't hire yours."

It’s funny—sure. But it’s also deadly serious. In those few lines, it delivers a razor-sharp indictment of a cultural rot: the enduring fantasy that facts are optional, that science is just another opinion, and that belief in ancient fables should be equated with—or worse, replace—real education.

Let’s be clear: in a free society, you can teach your kids whatever you want. You can homeschool them in Noah's Ark logistics and flood geology. You can handwave away radiometric dating, dismiss the fossil record, and cast Charles Darwin as a villain in a cosmic morality play. That’s your legal right. You can even put them in private schools that reinforce this worldview, surrounding them with others who echo the same dogma. Fine.

But what you cannot do is expect the rest of us to pretend it’s valid. Because while your children are learning to distrust evidence and reject reality, ours are being equipped to build the world you’ll be living in tomorrow. They’re writing code, running experiments, launching startups, and solving problems. And when the time comes to hire, collaborate, or innovate—they’re not going to bet on someone who thinks dinosaurs lived alongside shepherds.

The cost of anti-science indoctrination is subtle but compounding. It doesn’t just block access to academic and professional fields; it locks young minds into a box, where curiosity is a threat and questioning is heresy. And that’s not just a disservice to them—it’s a ticking time bomb for a society that needs all hands on deck to tackle climate change, public health, space exploration, and AI ethics.

This isn’t an attack on religion. It’s an appeal to responsibility. You can have faith and still teach your kids to understand the scientific method. Plenty of religious scientists do just that. But the line gets crossed when faith becomes an excuse to inject falsehood into science classrooms and hobble a generation's ability to think critically.

Rejecting evolution isn’t a harmless eccentricity; it’s a deliberate rejection of the evidence-based framework that underpins biology, medicine, and genetics. Pretending the Earth is 6000 years old requires ignoring not just geology but physics, astronomy, and archaeology too. It’s not one harmless belief—it’s a wholesale abdication of reason.

And let’s not forget who benefits. It’s not the children. It's the culture warriors, grifters, and political operatives who peddle ignorance for power. They’re the ones selling tickets to the Creation Museum while quietly sending their kids to STEM academies. They’re the ones demonizing public schools while profiting off charter scams. Your kids? They’re just cannon fodder in a war against modernity.

Science is not infallible. But it’s the best tool we have to understand the world, test our assumptions, and push the boundaries of what's possible. It’s not a belief system. It’s a method. And rejecting that method out of fear, pride, or ideology is a choice—a choice that has consequences.

So when you teach your kids that evolution is a lie and that humans rode dinosaurs like cattle, just know this: you're not raising free thinkers. You're raising adults who will struggle to navigate a world built by people who didn’t choose ignorance.

The meme isn’t just a joke. It’s a prophecy. In a future where literacy in science and technology determines opportunity, the divide between fact and fable becomes a chasm. And the kids raised on fairy tales will find themselves on the wrong side of it.

So yes, teach them the Earth is 6000 years old if you must. Just don’t act surprised when the people running the labs, writing the code, and driving the future leave them behind. Because no matter how many times you tell the story, dinosaurs never pulled plows.
