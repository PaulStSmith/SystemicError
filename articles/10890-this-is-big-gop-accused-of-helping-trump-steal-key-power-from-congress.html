'This is big': GOP accused of helping Trump 'steal' key power from Congress

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Financial Maneuvering: A Brazen Power Grab?

Donald Trump addresses a joint session of Congress. Win McNamee/Pool via REUTERS

President Donald Trump has been trying to rob Congress of its centuries-old powers of the purse, according to legal challenges filed by Democrats against the administration, and that fight will intensify as the executive and legislative branches try to avert a government shutdown.

The administration complicated spending talks by proposing to cancel $4.9 billion of foreign aid using a “pocket rescission” that Democrats and the top Republican Senate appropriator have said is illegal, and congressional Democrats have added that figure to the estimated $425 billion that lawmakers have approved but the president and his officials want to claw back, reported CNN.

“This is a big deal," said Rep. Rosa DeLauro (D-NJ), the top Democrat on the House Appropriations panel. "[Trump] has concentrated all this power in his hands. They are running around illegally stealing taxpayer money, and it is a unilateral partisan act that excludes any cooperation with Democrats.”

Democrats have won some legal challenges forcing budget chief Russ Vought to provide more transparency about what he's doing with legislative spending, but the Supreme Court and federal courts have allowed the administration to cut some foreign aid and research grants at the National Institutes of Health.

“Republicans should not accept Russ Vought’s brazen attempt to usurp their own power," said Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA). "No president has a line-item veto — and certainly not a retroactive line-item veto."

Some congressional Republicans have grumbled about Trump moving to grab funding lawmakers approved, with Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) calling the moves “a clear violation of the law,” and deficit hawk Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican congressman, questioned the pocket recission.

“It looks like they’ve done it intentionally," Massie said. "I think they are trying to be too cute by half by doing it this late. It’s also a risky move. The courts could tie it up. If it’s such a good idea, we have a majority in the House and Senate, why not let us vote on it?”

Trump campaigned on grabbing spending power from Congress so he could “squeeze the bloated federal bureaucracy for massive savings," and the White House budget office defended its own moves as fully legal.

“We are on very firm legal footing and we will be making that case and I think the courts, if they do consider this, will decide along the lines of what we’ve articulated,” a White House official said.

The courts so far have been the primary check on Trump's power grab, with House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) pushing the administration's plans to pause congressionally approved spending to help sell a full-year continuing resolution back in March, but Democrats have challenged some of the budget office's moves that were baked into that bill.

"The full-year continuing resolution, which locks prior year funding levels in place through September 30, included $3 billion in emergency funding that OMB would not allocate, which Democrats argued was illegal," CNN reported.

"OMB went a step further to obscure spending by taking down a public website that discloses what are known as apportionments, which is how agencies are specifically doling out funds," the network added. "In a rare instance of bipartisan pushback against the White House, the four leaders of the appropriations committees sent a letter telling the White House to restore the website."

A federal judge ordered the administration to put that website back online last month, and Democrats insist they will again try to limit Trump's attempts to get around the appropriations process during budget negotiations this month, but they acknowledged that some Republicans had to join their fight.

“We tried to do that last March,” said DeLauro said, adding that Republicans argued that Congress had not authority to rein in the president. “That’s bull----, excuse me. Republicans have been silent. They refuse to stand up and fight back.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Financial Maneuvering: A Brazen Power Grab?

In a move that can only be described as audacious, President Donald Trump appears to have launched a full-scale assault on Congress’s constitutional power of the purse. This isn't just a minor skirmish over budgetary line items; it's a comprehensive effort to shift the balance of power squarely into the hands of the executive branch. The administration’s attempt to rescind $4.9 billion of foreign aid through a "pocket rescission" has ignited a firestorm of criticism from both sides of the aisle, with top Democrats and even the Senate’s leading Republican appropriator crying foul over what they see as an illegal power grab.

The Illegal Rescission Ruse

Let's break down the "pocket rescission" tactic. Essentially, it's an end-run around Congress, an attempt to unilaterally claw back funds that lawmakers have already approved. This isn't just pushing the envelope of executive authority; it's tearing it to shreds. Congressional Democrats have rightly added this figure to the staggering estimated $425 billion that the president and his officials are attempting to reel back, setting the stage for a monumental clash over constitutional prerogatives.

Rep. Rosa DeLauro’s (D-NJ) remarks encapsulate the gravity of the situation, painting a picture of a presidency run amok, concentrating power in a manner that sidelines any pretense of bipartisan cooperation. The administration’s actions aren’t just problematic; they're portrayed as outright theft of taxpayer money, executed with a brazen disregard for legal and procedural norms.

Legal Challenges and Republican Discontent

While some legal challenges have borne fruit, forcing Budget Chief Russ Vought to offer a modicum of transparency, the courts have been a mixed bag, allowing certain cuts to foreign aid and research grants. This piecemeal judicial pushback underscores the precarious nature of relying on the courts to arbitrate such fundamental disputes over the separation of powers.

Notably, some Republicans have broken ranks, voicing their disapproval. Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) and Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) have expressed concerns, with Massie’s critique of the timing and legality of the pocket rescission highlighting internal GOP divisions over the administration’s tactics. Yet, this flicker of bipartisan unease has yet to coalesce into a unified front capable of curbing the administration’s ambitions.

The White House’s Defense and the Ongoing Battle

The White House, unsurprisingly, defends its actions as entirely within the legal bounds, a claim that stands in stark contrast to the outcry from both Democrats and a handful of Republicans. This defense not only challenges the established norms of congressional oversight but also sets the stage for a potential constitutional crisis over the allocation and management of federal funds.

Even more troubling is the administration’s decision to obscure spending by dismantling a public website that tracks apportionments—a move that, thankfully, was reversed by a federal judge’s order. This attempt to shroud the budgetary process in secrecy is emblematic of a broader disdain for transparency and accountability.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

As negotiations over the budget loom, the administration’s maneuvering sets a dangerous precedent, threatening to permanently alter the delicate balance of power between the branches of government. The silence and complicity of many Republicans, as pointed out by DeLauro, only serve to embolden these overreaches, raising profound questions about the future of congressional authority and the integrity of the appropriations process.

This isn't just a partisan issue; it's a fundamental challenge to the mechanisms of American governance. As this battle unfolds, one thing is clear: the stakes could not be higher, both for the current administration and for the future of democratic oversight and fiscal responsibility.
