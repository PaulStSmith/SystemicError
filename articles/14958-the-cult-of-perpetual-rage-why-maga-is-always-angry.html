The Cult of Perpetual Rage: Why MAGA Is Always Angry

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 13, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Winning Isn’t the PointA Rage Cult, Not a Political MovementThe Manufactured VictimhoodThe Grift Behind the FuryWhat Happens When the Rage Burns Out?Not Bizarre—Deliberate

This article was inspired by a question on Quora that asked a seemingly simple question: "Why does MAGA always sound so angry and triggered when they answer questions? Shouldn’t they be happy they are ‘winning’?" One answer went on to list a litany of grievances MAGA adherents still rage about, years after they supposedly achieved victory. It’s a question worth unpacking—not because the answer is elusive, but because it exposes the rotting foundation beneath the MAGA movement.

Winning Isn’t the Point

To outsiders, the paradox is puzzling: Donald Trump won in 2016. Republicans had control of the House, the Senate, and the presidency. They stacked the Supreme Court with ultra-conservative justices. So why does it feel like they’re angrier now than ever before?

Because the MAGA movement was never about policy victories, governance, or national unity. It was about anger. Trump didn’t promise a better America for everyone—he promised a return to a time when his base felt dominant. He didn’t appeal to hope; he stoked resentment.

The red hat wasn’t a symbol of change. It was a battle flag.

A Rage Cult, Not a Political Movement

MAGA is a rage cult disguised as a political ideology. Its followers feed off a diet of perpetual grievance, seeking out new targets for their disdain: immigrants, LGBTQ+ communities, the media, academia, multinational corporations, beer cans, cartoon characters, and yes—even rainbows.

When your identity is built around opposition, peace becomes a threat. You can’t allow yourself to be content because then the war is over—and MAGA can’t exist without a war. That’s why they’re still mad about Obama. Still mad about Hillary. Still mad about a 2020 election they falsely believe was stolen. Still mad at Bud Light. Still mad at M&Ms. Still mad at anything that reflects a world moving on without them.

It’s performative outrage, yes—but also existential. It’s the howl of a movement that only feels alive when it’s under siege.

The Manufactured Victimhood

The key to understanding MAGA rage is understanding that victimhood is a feature, not a bug. Trump weaponized this perfectly. He told his followers they were being silenced while giving them a louder megaphone than they’d ever had. He told them they were under attack while unleashing their fury on the most vulnerable among us.

Victimhood gave them purpose. It gave them a scapegoat for every failure. And crucially, it absolved them of introspection.

Why didn’t coal come back? Why didn’t the wall get built? Why didn’t Trump repeal and replace Obamacare?

Easy answer: the Deep State. The RINOs. The socialists. The media. The Clintons. George Soros. Take your pick.

When you’re always the victim, nothing is ever your fault. You never have to confront that maybe—just maybe—you were conned.

The Grift Behind the Fury

Trump’s con was never about running the country. It was about turning the presidency into a personal ATM. Every tantrum, every tweet, every conspiracy theory was chum in the water for a base trained to take offense at everything. And while they were busy screaming at drag queens and fact-checkers, Trump and his cronies were raking in donations, steering federal funds to friends, and dodging accountability.

He gave them someone to blame. They gave him their wallets.

The movement became a business model. Outrage was monetized. Media networks, merch vendors, and influencers realized there’s money to be made in selling rage by the ounce.

What Happens When the Rage Burns Out?

Here’s the terrifying truth: if the anger ever stops, the MAGA identity collapses. If a Trump supporter wakes up and finds they’re not furious about a blue beer can or a Sesame Street character, they might begin to wonder why their lives haven’t improved.

They might question why, after years of Republican control, wages haven’t gone up, jobs haven’t returned, schools haven’t improved, and health care hasn’t gotten cheaper.

They might start looking for answers instead of enemies.

And that would be the death of MAGA.

Not Bizarre—Deliberate

So no, the rage isn’t bizarre. It’s the point. It’s the glue that binds the cult. It’s the shield against facts, empathy, and progress. It’s the fire that keeps the grift going.

MAGA is not a movement on the brink of victory—it’s a movement terrified of peace. Because peace requires reflection. And that’s something it cannot afford.
