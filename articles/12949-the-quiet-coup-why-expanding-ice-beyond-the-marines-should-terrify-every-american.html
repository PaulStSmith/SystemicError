The Quiet Coup: Why Expanding ICE Beyond the Marines Should Terrify Every American

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20254 min read

In the long, dark tradition of authoritarian creep, one tactic has always stood out: expand internal enforcement powers while cloaking the buildup in the language of law and order. Today, in the United States, that tactic wears the badge of Immigration and Customs Enforcement — ICE — and it’s being given a budget and mandate that dwarfs anything we’ve seen in modern domestic policy. Bigger than the Marines? That’s not a typo. That’s a five-alarm fire.

Let’s be brutally clear: you don’t make ICE bigger than the Marines unless you’re planning to do some really bad shit. And if recent history is any indication, “bad” doesn’t even begin to describe what’s coming.

ICE: A Mechanism of Cruelty by Design

ICE wasn’t born out of a commitment to justice. It was forged in the aftermath of 9/11, when fear was currency and paranoia a national virtue. Its original mandate was immigration enforcement — but it rapidly became a sprawling, opaque, militarized bureaucracy with carte blanche to operate within American communities with little oversight.

Under various administrations, ICE has repeatedly shown that its purpose is not to protect but to punish. It has locked up children. Torn families apart. Operated detention centers so abhorrent that watchdogs have compared them to concentration camps. It has raided homes, workplaces, hospitals, even courthouses — with tactics that wouldn’t be out of place in a military occupation.

But ICE is not the military. It’s worse — because it operates inside the borders and outside the checks and balances that typically restrain military force.

A Coup by Other Means

Let’s return to the meme that sparked this discussion: “You don’t make ICE bigger than the Marines if you aren’t about to do some really bad shit.” That is not hyperbole. That’s a blueprint.

Why would any administration seek to supercharge a domestic enforcement agency beyond the scale of a global military force? Simple: because the military is constrained by law. You can’t legally send tanks into polling places. You can’t deploy Marines to round up dissidents. Posse Comitatus stands in the way. But ICE? ICE is a workaround. It’s a Trojan horse for authoritarian ambition.

As Quora user Keldon Alleyne put it: “It’s to get around limits on using the military against voters. It is a coup.” That’s not conjecture. That’s a historical pattern. Strongmen don’t always wear medals. Sometimes, they wear suits and appoint loyalists to build paramilitary forces under the guise of “public safety.”

Who Will Fill ICE's Ranks?

Let’s talk personnel. ICE already has a recruitment problem — not in numbers, but in standards. Background checks have been loosened. Accusations of racism, abuse, and corruption plague the agency. These aren’t isolated incidents. They’re systemic failures.

Now imagine what happens when you inject billions more dollars and a greenlight to ”enforce immigration law” during a time of rising authoritarian rhetoric. You’re not building a law enforcement agency — you’re assembling a political militia.

As Benjamin Muller pointed out in that same Quora thread: “The biggest sociopathic traitors America has to offer.” It’s a chilling but accurate forecast.

This isn’t speculative fiction. It’s happened before — from the SS in Nazi Germany to Pinochet’s death squads in Chile. Each time, it started with a government arming a domestic force loyal not to the people, but to power.

History's Warning: Leah McCreery’s Stark Reminder

In the same thread, Leah McCreery dropped the mic: “Back in the 40s, my uncle and a couple million other Americans went to Europe to join our allies in shooting men like that, and we call them heroes.”

She’s right. Americans fought and died to stop regimes that built internal paramilitary machines. We honored them not just for their courage, but for recognizing evil early and confronting it directly.

So what are we doing now?

Looking away while ICE builds an empire?

Funding it like it’s a branch of the military?

Staffing it with the ideologically loyal and morally vacant?

If you’re not angry yet, you’re not paying attention.

ICE Is Not Reformable. It Must Be Abolished.

Let’s stop pretending this is about national security. Let’s stop clinging to the delusion that ICE is a flawed but fixable institution. It’s not.

ICE is functioning exactly as designed: to intimidate, detain, deport, and disappear. It’s a hammer looking for nails, and in the hands of an aspiring autocrat, every opponent becomes a nail.

Calls for "reform" are like trying to repaint a guillotine. The structure is intact. The purpose remains. The outcome is predictable.

Abolishing ICE isn’t radical. Funding it at Marine Corps levels is.

This Is the Test. Will We Pass It?

History will not look kindly on those who stood by. And no one will be able to say they weren’t warned. The writing is on the wall. Hell, it’s glowing neon:

You don’t make ICE bigger than the Marines unless you’re about to do some really bad shit.

America, this is the part in the movie where the protagonist wakes up. Where the audience is screaming at the screen. Where we realize the villain has been setting the stage all along.

The only question left is: will we act?

Dismantle ICE. Defund the machinery of domestic tyranny. Before it turns on you.
