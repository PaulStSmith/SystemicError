'Writing is on the wall' as demoralized GOP lawmakers expect to become minority: insider

By Paulo Santos

Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (Reuters)

The former chief aide to ex-House Speaker Kevin McCarthy (R-CA) contends that the GOP caucus under current Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) is demoralized and angry as members head home for the holidays, facing the prospect of losing House control in 2026.

During an appearance on MS NOW’s “Way Too Early” with host Ali Vitali, Brendan Buck noted that while internal fractures have existed within the Republican majority, conditions have deteriorated significantly under Johnson’s leadership. The Speaker faces an escalating revolt from his own members.

Buck pointed to several instances where GOP House lawmakers joined with Democrats to pass discharge petitions against the Speaker’s wishes. He expressed pessimism about improved conditions when lawmakers return after the holiday recess.

“I wonder if you think that it’s overblown when you hear about, oh, Republicans are nervous about the midterms. They feel like the issues are getting away from them,” host Vitali prompted her guest. “The generic ballot looks difficult. Is it overblown? Is it spot on? How do you think about this?”

“It shouldn’t be overblown,” he replied. “I mean, I think one of the signs of that is the number of retirements you’ve already started to see — and I think you’re going to see a lot usually when they go home for the holidays and come back, because when you see a lot of retirements, I would expect you’re going to get a lot.”

“Some of it is just again, we’re not doing a whole lot, but the writing is very much on the wall that you’re going to be in the minority next year and that’s not a very fun life,” he observed. “I think a lot of people are just sort of tired of having to deal with the whole [Donald] Trump circus as well. You’re in Congress, you’re not accomplishing much, you may be in the minority soon. You’re constantly having to respond to whatever Donald Trump did — It’s not a very fun existence.”

“I think that is another part of why you’re seeing all these people sort of lashing out in a way that makes it very hard to govern,” he added.

Our Analysis:

GOP Caucus: A Symphony of Discord and Despair?

The recent remarks by Brendan Buck, a former chief aide to ex-House Speaker Kevin McCarthy, paint a grim picture of the current state of the Republican caucus under Speaker Mike Johnson. The portrait isn’t just somber; it’s akin to a storm brewing on the horizon, threatening to engulf the GOP in turmoil and potential defeat come 2026. Let’s dissect Buck’s observations and the implications they hold for the Republican Party and its ability to govern.

The Leadership Crisis

Mike Johnson’s tenure is marked not by unity and strength but by internal fracturing and rebellion. When members of your own party are so discontent that they join forces with the opposition to override the Speaker’s wishes, it’s not just a crack in the facade; it’s a structural failure. The GOP under Johnson seems more like a loosely held confederation on the brink of collapse rather than a united front. The fact that this is happening under the watch of a Speaker is both telling and damning.

The Trump Albatross

The mention of the “whole Trump circus” by Buck is a critical point that cannot be overstated. The GOP’s identity and strategy have been inextricably linked to Donald Trump since his rise in 2016. While this association has galvanized the base at times, it has also become a Sisyphean burden for many within the party. The constant need to react, defend, or distance themselves from Trump’s actions and statements has created a political quagmire that impedes legislative progress and strategic coherence.

The Exodus Indicator

Buck’s mention of increasing retirements within the GOP as a harbinger of doom is particularly striking. This isn’t just about individuals seeking to exit a sinking ship; it’s about the demoralization and disillusionment permeating the ranks. When the prospect of becoming the minority looms large, coupled with the relentless chaos associated with Trump, it’s no wonder many are opting to hang up their hats. This trend should alarm the GOP leadership, as it signals a loss of faith in the party’s direction and prospects.

Governance in Peril

The overarching theme of Buck’s commentary is the ineffectiveness and stagnation plaguing the GOP. The inability to govern effectively, as evidenced by the reliance on discharge petitions and the constant distraction of Trump-related controversies, undermines the very purpose of holding office. If the Republican Party is to recover and present a formidable challenge in future elections, it must first address these fundamental flaws in leadership, unity, and strategic direction.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The GOP stands at a critical juncture. Will it continue down the path of internal discord and reactionary politics, or will it seize this moment to redefine its values and goals? The answers to these questions will not only shape the party’s future but also the political landscape of the United States. As 2026 approaches, the Republican Party must undertake a serious introspection if it wishes to avoid the fate foretold by Brendan Buck. The clock is ticking, and the world is watching.
