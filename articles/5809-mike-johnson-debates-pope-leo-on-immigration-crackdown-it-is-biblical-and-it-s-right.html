Mike Johnson debates Pope Leo on immigration crackdown: 'It is biblical and it's right'

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) offered a Bible lesson on immigration after Pope Leo XIV advised policymakers to engage in “deep reflection” about the treatment of immigrants.

After the pope cited the book of Matthew to ensure that migrants’ “spiritual rights” were respected, a reporter asked Johnson to provide his own interpretation of the Bible.

“How would you respond to Pope Leo in scripture?” the reporter wondered.

“So you want me to give you a theological dissertation?” Johnson replied. “Borders and walls are biblical. From the Old Testament to the New, God has allowed us to set up our civil societies and have separate nations. Immigration is not something that’s frowned upon in Scripture. In fact, it’s welcome. We’re to welcome the sojourner and love our neighbor as ourselves.”

“But what’s also important in the Bible is that assimilation is expected and anticipated and proper,” he continued. “When people cite passages out of the Old Testament, they say, well, you’re supposed to take care of the sojourner and the neighbor, treat them as yourself, welcome them in. Yes, but that is an admonition to individuals, not to the civil authorities.”

“The civil authorities are given authority under Scripture to maintain order. Romans 13 says that the civil authorities are God’s agents of wrath to bring punishment upon the wrongdoer. And it says, if you do right, you have no fear of the civil authorities, but those civil authorities are necessary.”

Johnson said he saw the Bible as “a calling to maintain order in society.”

“Sovereign borders are biblical and good and right, and they’re just because — it’s not because we hate the people on the outside, it’s because we love the people on the inside,” the speaker argued. “We should love our neighbors ourselves as individuals, but as a civil authority, and the government has to maintain the law.”

“And that is biblical, and it’s right, and it’s just. And I’m happy to have this lengthy debate with anybody and at the time they want to.”

Our Analysis:

A Divine Divide: House Speaker Mike Johnson’s Biblical Justification for Immigration Policies

In a recent and rather audacious move, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has taken it upon himself to offer a Bible lesson on immigration, responding to Pope Leo XIV’s call for policymakers to reflect deeply on the treatment of immigrants. This incident reveals not just a clash of interpretations but a fundamental misunderstanding—or perhaps a deliberate misrepresentation—of biblical texts to justify stringent immigration policies.

The Biblical Battleground

Johnson’s rebuttal to the Pope’s invocation of Matthew’s book is nothing short of a theological tightrope walk. He asserts that “borders and walls are biblical,” a statement that at first glance might seem to carry the weight of divine authority. However, a closer examination reveals a troubling conflation of ancient texts with modern geopolitical concerns. The leap from biblical times to contemporary border policy is vast and fraught with misinterpretations.

Selective Scripture Reading

Johnson’s argument rests on a selective reading of scripture, particularly his emphasis on assimilation and the role of civil authorities as outlined in Romans 13. While it’s true that the Bible discusses the roles of authorities, Johnson’s interpretation conveniently ignores the broader biblical call to compassion, hospitality, and justice for the stranger among us—a recurrent theme from Genesis to the New Testament.

The Assimilation Argument: Johnson’s insistence on assimilation as a biblical mandate is particularly problematic. This notion not only oversimplifies the biblical text but also imposes a modern, nationalist framework on ancient, communal societies. The Bible’s admonitions to care for the sojourner and love one’s neighbor are presented as universal, moral obligations, not conditional upon the sojourner’s assimilation.

Romans 13 and the Authority of Civil Authorities: Johnson weaponizes Romans 13 to justify stringent immigration controls, suggesting that civil authorities are divinely sanctioned to enforce order at the expense of compassion. This interpretation is not only a narrow reading of Paul’s letter but also neglects the broader biblical witness that challenges unjust laws and calls for radical hospitality.

A Dangerous Conflation

Johnson’s theological dissertation on immigration policy is a dangerous conflation of faith and politics. By asserting that “sovereign borders are biblical and good and right,” he not only misconstrues biblical texts but also lends a veneer of divine approval to policies that often marginalize and harm the very individuals the Bible calls us to protect.

Love for the Inside vs. the Outside: The dichotomy Johnson presents between loving “the people on the inside” and maintaining sovereign borders to protect them from “the people on the outside” is a stark deviation from the biblical call to universal love and compassion. This us-vs-them mentality is antithetical to the gospel’s inclusive message.

Conclusion: A Misguided Theological Debate

Johnson’s readiness to engage in a “lengthy debate” on the biblical justification for current immigration policies misses the point entirely. The issue at hand is not whether the Bible can be interpreted to support sovereign borders, but whether our policies reflect the core biblical values of compassion, justice, and hospitality.

In his attempt to defend the indefensible, Johnson not only misinterprets scripture but also undermines the very principles of love and compassion that are central to the Christian faith. It’s a stark reminder that when politics seeks to co-opt religion to justify exclusion and division, it’s a clear sign of a systemic error in our moral and ethical compass.
