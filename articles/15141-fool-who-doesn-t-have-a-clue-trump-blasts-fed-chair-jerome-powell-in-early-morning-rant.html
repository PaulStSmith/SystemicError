'Fool who doesn't have a clue': Trump blasts Fed Chair Jerome Powell in early morning rant

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as Jerome Powell, his nominee to become chairman of the U.S. Federal Reserve, speaks at the White House in Washington, U.S., November 2, 2017. REUTERS/Carlos Barria/File Photo

President Donald Trump woke up Wednesday morning and took aim at Federal Reserve chair Jerome Powell.

“‘Too Late’ Jerome Powell is a FOOL, who doesn’t have a clue. Other than that, I like him very much! Oil and Energy way down, almost all costs (groceries and ‘eggs’) down, virtually NO INFLATION, Tariff Money Pouring Into the U.S. — THE EXACT OPPOSITE OF ‘TOO LATE!’ ENJOY!” He posted on Truth Social.

ALSO READ: ‘Pain. Grief. Anger’: Families heartbroken as Trump backlash smashes adoption dreams

The early morning post came after Powell declined to lower interest rates on Tuesday. The Fed's benchmark interest rate was held steady at 4.25% to 4.5%. The rate was reached at the end of 2024 after cutting rates by a full percentage point last fall.

Powell has consistently pushed back on pressure from the White House, to lower interest rates. Powell told reporters on Tuesday that President Trump "doesn't affect our doing our job at all" and that "I've never asked for a meeting with any president, and I never will."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against Powell: A Misguided Missile?

President Donald Trump's latest outburst on Truth Social, targeting Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, is a spectacle of misplaced priorities and a fundamental misunderstanding of economic policy—or, at the very least, a willful misrepresentation of it. Trump's tirade, branding Powell as "‘Too Late’ Jerome Powell" and a "FOOL," would be laughable if it weren't so deeply concerning coming from a figure of his influence.

"Other than that, I like him very much!" Trump quips, after a barrage of insults, in a tone that oscillates between mockery and bitter sarcasm. He proceeds to paint a rosy picture of the economic landscape, citing "Oil and Energy way down, almost all costs (groceries and ‘eggs’) down, virtually NO INFLATION, Tariff Money Pouring Into the U.S." This portrayal not only oversimplifies complex economic dynamics but also blatantly ignores the nuances of inflation, trade policies, and their impacts on the average citizen.

Economic Illusions and Tariff Confusions

Let's dissect Trump's claims with a scalpel of truth. For starters, the assertion of "virtually NO INFLATION" flies in the face of economic indicators and the lived experience of ordinary Americans. While specific sectors may experience fluctuations, the broader economic landscape tells a story of ongoing challenges, where the Federal Reserve's mandate to control inflation often requires difficult decisions—decisions that Trump seems to neither understand nor respect.

Moreover, the boast about "Tariff Money Pouring Into the U.S." is a gross misrepresentation. Economists have long argued that tariffs are a double-edged sword, often hurting domestic consumers and businesses as much as, if not more than, they penalize foreign exporters. The notion that this constitutes some form of economic victory is at best a misunderstanding of basic economics, and at worst, deliberate disinformation.

Powell's Stoic Stance

In contrast to Trump's bombastic rhetoric, Jerome Powell's response is a study in steadfast commitment to duty. Powell's stance, "President Trump "doesn't affect our doing our job at all" and "I've never asked for a meeting with any president, and I never will," reflects a dedication to the Federal Reserve's independence—a cornerstone principle that ensures monetary policy remains insulated from political whims and pressures.

The decision to maintain the benchmark interest rate steady at 4.25% to 4.5% is a calculated move aimed at balancing growth with inflation control—a tightrope walk that seems lost on Trump. Powell's refusal to bow to White House pressure exemplifies the kind of principled leadership necessary to navigate the complexities of modern economies.

The Verdict

Trump's early morning rant is more than just a former president venting on social media; it's a manifestation of a broader disregard for economic expertise and a penchant for simplistic solutions to complex problems. The attack on Powell is not only unjustified but also misinformed, reflecting a troubling trend of populist leaders undermining expert opinion for political gain.

As we sift through Trump's claims, the conclusion is inescapable: this is not just about interest rates or inflation—it's about the integrity of economic policymaking in the face of political interference. Powell, in adhering to his mandate amidst such turbulence, demonstrates a resilience and commitment to principle that deserves not scorn, but respect.

In the end, Trump's tirade serves as a reminder of the dangers posed by leaders who choose to sideline expertise in favor of expedience. As observers and participants in this ongoing economic saga, we must remain vigilant, separating fact from fiction with diligence and discernment.
