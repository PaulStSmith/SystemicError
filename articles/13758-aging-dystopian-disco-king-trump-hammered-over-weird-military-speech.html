'Aging dystopian disco king' Trump hammered over 'weird' military speech

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump prepares to deliver remarks during a visit to Fort Bragg to mark the U.S. Army anniversary, in North Carolina, U.S., June 10, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

On Tuesday Donald Trump decided to use his speech at Ft. Bragg, which was planned as a tribute to the 250th birthday of the U.S. Army, to instead spiral into another rehash of his "American carnage" themes as he painted a portrait of a country under siege from forces within.

That led Guardian columnist Robert Tait to label what he watched as some "weird s--- indeed."

In his speech postmortem, Tait noted that the president's MAGA rally style before the assembled Ft. Bragg soldiers was heavy on attacks on California and light on praise for the mission of the U.S. Army –– which was compounded by Trump's choice of music for his walk-off.

Noting the president went dark again with his attacks on U.S. citizens and the political leadership of California who are balking at his heavy-handed military approach to occupying the state in search of immigrants, Tait wrote, "It was the same discordant melody he had gone off on in his memorably dark first inauguration speech of January 2017, prompting George W Bush – who has kept an otherwise sphinx-like silence on things Trumpian in recent years – to murmur that it was 'some weird s---'."

"Given the martial setting, it would have been worthier, though unquestionably duller, to hum a tune of virtue and valor," the columnist suggested before adding, "It was a tour of Trump’s darkest horizons – all the bleaker for being leavened with a comical parting serenade."

That "comical parting serenade" he noted was the Village People's disco chestnut "YMCA."

Then he waddled off stage, like some aging dystopian disco king."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Fort Bragg Speech: A Dive into the "American Carnage" Redux

In a move that veers into the realm of the absurd, Donald Trump hijacked what was meant to be a tribute to the U.S. Army's 250th birthday at Fort Bragg, spiraling instead into a tirade that resurrected his infamous "American carnage" rhetoric. This peculiar choice of theme and setting did not go unnoticed, with Guardian columnist Robert Tait aptly dubbing the spectacle "some weird s--- indeed." The event, rather than honoring the valor and history of the U.S. Army, morphed into a MAGA rally, replete with attacks on California and a scarce nod to the military's mission.

Trump's Dissonant Melody: Trump's penchant for dark, divisive rhetoric is hardly news. Yet, his decision to unleash a barrage of attacks on U.S. citizens and the political leadership of California, all under the guise of a military operation against immigrants, is a stark reminder of his administration's confrontational stance. Tait's observation that this speech mirrored the "discordant melody" of Trump's grim inauguration address is spot-on. Even George W Bush, known for his reticence on Trumpian matters, couldn't help but remark on the oddity of it all.

A Misplaced Tribute?: The setting of Fort Bragg, meant to honor the U.S. Army, instead served as the backdrop for Trump's bleak vision of America—a vision that Tait suggests would have been more appropriately replaced with "a tune of virtue and valor." The irony of Trump's "tour of [...] darkest horizons" in such a context cannot be overstated. It's a distortion of what should have been an apolitical celebration of military service and sacrifice.

The "Aging Dystopian Disco King": The conclusion of Trump's speech, marked by the blaring of the Village People's "YMCA" and Trump's awkward dance, would be comical if it weren't so tragically misplaced. Tait's description of Trump as "some aging dystopian disco king" as he "waddled off stage" is both vivid and vexing. This imagery underscores the surreal juxtaposition of the event's purported solemnity against the reality of its execution.

Conclusion

Trump's speech at Fort Bragg was a missed opportunity to genuinely honor the U.S. Army on its 250th birthday. Instead, what unfolded was a spectacle that was as bewildering as it was inappropriate. The decision to veer into "American carnage" territory, the scant acknowledgment of the Army's purpose, and the bizarre exit to the tunes of "YMCA" collectively paint a picture of a presidency that often blurs the lines between leadership and spectacle.

In dissecting Trump's speech, we are reminded of the profound responsibility leaders have in choosing their words and their moments. The distortion of a military tribute into a platform for political grievance and divisive rhetoric is a stark example of leadership gone awry. As observers and analysts, it's crucial to call out these missteps, not just for the sake of critique, but to underscore the importance of honor, respect, and appropriateness in presidential conduct—a standard to which we must hold all our leaders, regardless of party affiliation.
