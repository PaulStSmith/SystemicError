Trump pushed Zelenskyy in vulgar 'shouting match' to cede land or be 'destroyed': report

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump meets with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy during the 80th United Nations General Assembly, in New York City, New York, U.S., September 23, 2025. (REUTERS/Al Drago)

In a private meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Friday, President Donald Trump urged him to concede a significant amount of territory to Russia or face destruction, a meeting that devolved into a “shouting match” with Trump “cursing all the time,” according to insiders who spoke with the Financial Times in its report Sunday.

“If [Putin] wants it, he will destroy you,” Trump reportedly told Zelenskyy in the closed-door meeting, according to who the Financial Times described as a “European official with knowledge of the meeting,” speaking with the outlet on the condition of anonymity.

According to the insiders who spoke with the Financial Times, Trump “threw Ukraine’s maps of the battlefield” during the tense meeting, urging Zelenskyy to surrender parts of the eastern Donbas region which still remain under Ukrainian control. The concession Trump proposed would be in exchange for Russia ceding small regions near the southern and southeastern Ukrainian cities of Kherson and Zaporizhzhia, respectively – the former now under Ukrainian control and the latter under Russian control.

But for some Ukrainian officials, surrendering the Donbas region was a nonstarter.

“To give [the Donbas] to Russia without a fight is unacceptable for Ukrainian society, and [Russian President Vladimir] Putin knows that,” said Oleksandr Merezhko, who chairs the Ukrainian parliament’s foreign affairs committee, speaking with the Financial Times.

An unnamed official told the outlet that Zelenskyy was “very negative” following the meeting, while noting that European leaders were “not optimistic but pragmatic with planning next steps.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Diplomatic Arm-twisting: A Closer Look at Trump’s Meeting with Zelenskyy

In what reads like a script ripped straight from a Cold War thriller, a recent article by the Financial Times exposes the underbelly of international diplomacy, revealing a high-stakes confrontation between President Donald Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. According to the report, Trump not only engaged in a verbal tirade but also pressured Zelenskyy to cede significant territory to Russia, essentially strong-arming Ukraine into a corner with the threat of annihilation.

Trump’s Ultimatum: Concede or Be Destroyed

The audacity of Trump’s directive—”If [Putin] wants it, he will destroy you”—is not just a mere suggestion; it’s an ultimatum laced with dire consequences. Trump, by leveraging the existential threat that Putin represents to Ukraine, essentially positioned himself as the harbinger of doom, should his advice go unheeded. This isn’t diplomacy; it’s coercion on an international scale.

The Tossing of Maps: A Symbol of Disdain

Trump’s reported action of “throwing Ukraine’s maps of the battlefield“ speaks volumes, symbolizing not just a disregard for the sanctity of Ukrainian territory but also an alarming disrespect for the gravity of the situation. This act, if true, is emblematic of a broader issue: a failure to appreciate the complexities of the Ukrainian struggle and a cavalier attitude towards sovereign nations’ territorial integrity.

The Unyielding Stance of Ukraine

The response from Ukrainian officials, particularly Oleksandr Merezhko’s assertion that conceding the Donbas region to Russia is “unacceptable,” underscores a fundamental misreading by Trump of the Ukrainian spirit. The notion, as suggested by Trump, to trade parts of the Donbas region for marginal gains elsewhere, not only insults the sacrifices made by Ukrainians but also demonstrates a lack of understanding of the conflict’s core dynamics.

A Meeting Met with Despair and Pragmatism

The aftermath of the meeting, as described, paints a bleak picture. Zelenskyy’s “very negative” disposition and the tempered pragmatism of European leaders signal a diplomatic encounter that left more scars than solutions. It’s a telling indication of the despair and frustration that often shrouds negotiations involving volatile geopolitical conflicts.

In Conclusion

The Financial Times’ report sheds light on a troubling episode of international relations, marked by high pressure, intimidation, and a blatant disregard for the principles of sovereignty and self-determination. Trump’s approach, as detailed in the article, is not just a departure from diplomatic norms but a dive into the murky waters of geopolitical bullying.

As observers and analysts, we must question the long-term implications of such tactics on the global stage. The preservation of territorial integrity and the respect for sovereign decisions must remain paramount in international discourse. Anything less is a disservice to the principles upon which global peace and security are founded.
