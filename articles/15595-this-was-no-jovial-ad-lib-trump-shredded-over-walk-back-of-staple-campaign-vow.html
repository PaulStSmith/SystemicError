'This was no jovial ad-lib': Trump shredded over walk-back of 'staple' campaign vow

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of the 'Joke': Deciphering Trump's Claims on Ending the Ukraine WarConclusion: The Danger of Disinformation

Donald Trump (Reuters)

President Donald Trump is trying to walk back his promise to end the Russian invasion of Ukraine on "day one" by passing it off as a joke — but that's simply not true, CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale wrote in an analysis published Friday.

Trump attempted the walkback in his wide-ranging interview with Time Magazine conducted this week.

"I said that figuratively, and I said that as an exaggeration, because to make a point, and you know, it gets, of course, by the fake news," Trump said when reminded of his words. "Obviously, people know that when I said that, it was said in jest, but it was also said that it will be ended."

But it wasn't at all "in jest," wrote Dale, who has covered lies and false claims from the president extensively.

"A Friday search of the Roll Call Factbase database that catalogues Trump’s public remarks turned up at least 53 examples of Trump making such comments," wrote Dale. "It’s sometimes hard to determine the intent of a politician’s one-time ad-libs, but this was no jovial ad-lib. Rather, the promise of a rapid end to the war was a sober staple of Trump’s pre-written rally remarks. He framed the promise as a key component of his second-term agenda, and he justified it with claims about his 'credibility' as a leader, his history as a 'peacemaker,' and his knowledge of Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky."

For instance, during a speech to the 2023 Conservative Political Action Conference, Trump said, “Before I even arrive at the Oval Office, I will have the disastrous war between Russia and Ukraine settled. It will be settled quickly. Quickly. I will get the problem solved and I will get it solved in rapid order and it will take me no longer than one day. I know exactly what to say to each of them.”

In May of last year, at a town hall in New Hampshire, Trump used similarly specific and literal phrasing.

“If I’m president, I will have that war settled in one day, 24 hours. First, I’ll meet with Putin, I’ll meet with Zelensky. They both have weaknesses and they both have strengths. And within 24 hours, that war will be settled. It will be over. It will be absolutely over.”

As recently as last October, Trump was saying at a rally in Wisconsin, “I will settle the war in Ukraine before I even take office; I’ll settle it as president-elect. I met with President Zelensky the other day. I know President Putin very well. I’ll get it settled.”

Indeed, Dale noted, "Trump sometimes emphasized that he was speaking literally, scoffing at critics who said he couldn’t end the war that fast."

With months of no progress on resolving the war, Trump has forcefully demanded that Ukraine and Russia reach a deal, and has drafted proposals that would effectively give Russia everything it wants by ceding critical occupied territory.

Our Analysis:

The Art of the 'Joke': Deciphering Trump's Claims on Ending the Ukraine War

In a world where political promises often stretch the bounds of believability, former President Donald Trump’s claim that he could end the Russian invasion of Ukraine on "day one" of his potential second term stands out for its audacity. However, the subsequent attempt to rebrand this as a mere jest does little to mask the irresponsibility of such statements. CNN's Daniel Dale, a fact-checker with a keen eye for political hyperbole, has once again punctured the bloated claims of Trump, revealing a pattern of misleading rhetoric.

Trump's Walkback: A Thinly Veiled Attempt to Dodge Accountability

Trump's attempt to walk back his bold promise during an interview with Time Magazine—claiming it was said "figuratively" and "as an exaggeration" to make a point—is a classic maneuver in the Trump playbook. However, to anyone paying attention, this explanation falls flat. Dale's analysis, grounded in a meticulous review of Trump’s public remarks, including at least 53 instances of similar claims, illustrates that Trump’s assertion was far from a light-hearted jest. It was a repeated pledge, delivered with the seriousness of a cornerstone campaign promise.

The Illusion of a Swift Resolution

The notion that Trump could single-handedly resolve the complex and deeply entrenched conflict between Russia and Ukraine within 24 hours is, to put it mildly, fantastical. His claims, made on several occasions and in various formats, including pre-written rally remarks and public speeches, were framed as a testament to his leadership, peacemaking history, and personal relationships with Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelensky.

During his speech at the 2023 Conservative Political Action Conference, Trump did not merely hint at a rapid resolution; he promised it. His language was unequivocal, claiming a "disastrous war" would be "settled quickly. Quickly." Further, his assertion of having "exactly what to say" to both leaders not only oversimplifies international diplomacy to a dangerous degree but also grossly underestimates the complexities of geopolitical conflicts.

From Promises to Proposals: Giving Away the Store?

More alarming than the fantastical promises is Trump's approach to conflict resolution, as critiqued by Dale. Trump's proposed strategy, seemingly ready to concede "everything Russia wants" by ceding critical occupied territory, raises serious questions about the implications for Ukrainian sovereignty and international law. Such proposals, far from being a masterstroke of diplomacy, appear to capitulate to Russian aggression, undermining the principles of national integrity and self-determination.

Conclusion: The Danger of Disinformation

Trump's attempt to recast his bold claims as a joke might have been intended to deflect criticism or to backtrack from an untenable position. However, it underscores a more troubling trend of disinformation and manipulation. The ease with which significant campaign promises can be dismissed as exaggerations or jokes not only insults the intelligence of the electorate but also erodes the very fabric of political accountability.

Daniel Dale's analysis serves as a necessary corrective to the narrative spun by Trump, reminding us of the importance of holding leaders to account for their words. In the realm of international conflict, where lives hang in the balance, the stakes are too high for empty promises and irresponsible rhetoric. As voters and citizens, it is our responsibility to sift through the noise, challenge the veracity of grandiose claims, and demand more from those who seek to lead.
