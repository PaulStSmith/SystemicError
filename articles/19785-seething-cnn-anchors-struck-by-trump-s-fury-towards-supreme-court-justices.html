'Seething!' CNN anchors struck by Trump's fury towards Supreme Court justices

Anchors Boris Sanchez and Brianna Keilar spoke with senior White House correspondent Kristen Holmes about President Donald Trump’s furious response to the Supreme Court’s ruling on his tariffs Friday. (CNN/Screenshot)

CNN hosts were taken aback by how angry President Donald Trump was Friday after the Supreme Court struck down his tariffs.

Anchors Boris Sanchez and Brianna Keilar were talking to senior White House correspondent Kristen Holmes about Trump’s reaction to the Supreme Court’s ruling — and how noticeably upset he appeared to be during it. Trump called the move “deeply disappointing” during a press conference and his first public reaction to the high court’s decision. He also said that the SCOTUS justices who voted against his tariffs are “barely” invited to his State of the Union address next week, saying, “I couldn’t care less if they come.”

“Yeah, he is clearly angry,” Holmes said. “He’s been seething about this decision. This is the real core tenet of not just his economic agenda, but really his foreign policy agenda as well. He has used these tariffs as leverage, and he said specifically yes, he is going this alternative route. Yes, he is going to be invoking this 10% global tariffs by using the section 122. We know that they are looking into also using section 301. Those are the things those trade law that they’re talking about to get this done. But that being said, the reason that they had gone this route initially was because this was quicker. They wanted this to be done quicker. They wanted to be able to instate this quicker. And that is why you’re seeing this frustration from President Trump.”

Trump was vocal and expressed his annoyance that he wanted his tariffs to continue despite the high court’s decision. He also refused to answer CNN questions during the press briefing, calling the network “fake news.”

“And I will say there were several interesting things he said. One, he was asked specifically about the two justices that he appointed to the Supreme Court,” Holmes said.

Trump had a scathing comment to the justices he had appointed who voted down his tariffs.

“The question that I had also tried to ask, which is whether or not he regretted it, he wouldn’t answer that, but he said it was an embarrassment,” Holmes said. “This decision to rule against the tariffs was an embarrassment to their families. We know that he has ranted in the past about Supreme Court justices, particularly those he has appointed, who don’t rule in his favor, but it was very clear here today that he was incredibly angry; he was angry at the court. He was angry at the people that he put into place. And he said so much, saying that they should be ashamed of themselves and of this decision that they made.”

Trump praised the three justices who dissented from the decision, including Justice Brett Kavanaugh, who issued a 63-page dissent.

“Now, one of the things we also saw him do was walk through the dissent from Kavanaugh, who obviously ruled in favor of keeping the tariffs or against them being illegal and said that he that Kavanaugh had sort of set up a roadmap for what they were going to be doing now to instate these tariffs and praised Kavanaugh as well also mentioning that all the Supreme Court justices are still invited to the State of the Union, but barely but again, you could see how angry he was,” Holmes said. “This is a core part of what they do what he has been doing, both in terms of economic policy, paying for different programs, saying that these tariffs are going to various different programs and bailouts, as well as when he goes into meetings with these foreign leaders using the tariffs as an enormous amount of leverage and really doing so carte blanche until now.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tariff Tantrum: A Scathing Analysis

In a display of petulance that would make a spoiled toddler blush, President Donald Trump’s reaction to the Supreme Court’s ruling against his tariffs was nothing short of a spectacle. The ruling, which struck down Trump’s tariffs, sent him into a fury, proving once again that when the going gets tough, Trump gets going... off the deep end.

CNN hosts Boris Sanchez and Brianna Keilar, along with senior White House correspondent Kristen Holmes, provided a front-row seat to Trump’s meltdown. His response to the Supreme Court’s decision was to call it “deeply disappointing,” a phrase that barely scratches the surface of the volcanic anger bubbling beneath. Trump’s threat to essentially uninvite the justices from the State of the Union address—because, of course, that’s the mature response—was as laughable as it was petty. “I couldn’t care less if they come,” he declared, in a tone that screamed the exact opposite.

The Core of Trump’s Ire

Holmes rightly points out that tariffs aren’t just a part of Trump’s economic agenda; they’re a cornerstone of his foreign policy. Trump’s reliance on tariffs as a form of leverage is well-documented, but his understanding of their implications is as shallow as a kiddie pool. His decision to invoke a 10% global tariff, citing sections 122 and 301, was a desperate grasp at straws, a move that underscores his preference for quick fixes over sustainable solutions.

The fact that Trump wanted these tariffs instated quickly—to bully and leverage, no doubt—reveals the impulsive nature of his decision-making. It’s not about what’s best for the country; it’s about what’s best for Trump’s ego.

The Embarrassment of Supreme Proportions

Trump’s scathing comments towards the justices he appointed, who dared not to kowtow to his whims, were particularly telling. Calling the decision an “embarrassment to their families” is a new low, even for Trump. It’s clear that loyalty, in Trump’s world, is a one-way street. If you’re not with him, you’re against him, and therefore, a target for his boundless wrath.

Praising Justice Brett Kavanaugh for his dissent, Trump clung to the 63-page document like a life raft, hoping it would guide him to his next disastrous policy decision. Kavanaugh’s dissent, according to Trump, provided a “roadmap” for reinstating tariffs—a roadmap that likely leads off a cliff if Trump’s previous decisions are any indication.

A Temper Tantrum of International Proportions

Trump’s use of tariffs as a “carte blanche” tool in negotiations with foreign leaders is a dangerous game. It’s akin to playing poker with a hand full of jokers; eventually, the other players catch on and the game is up. The Supreme Court’s ruling was a reality check for Trump, a reminder that there are limits to his power, a concept he seems to struggle with.

The Supreme Court’s decision against Trump’s tariffs is not just a blow to his policy agenda; it’s a stark exposure of his inability to handle defeat with any semblance of grace or maturity. His reaction was not just deeply disappointing; it was deeply revealing. It showed a president more concerned with his own pride than with the economic and diplomatic ramifications of his actions—a president who views the highest court in the land as another entity to bully and berate when things don’t go his way.

In conclusion, Trump’s reaction to the Supreme Court’s ruling is a microcosm of his presidency: impulsive, vengeful, and overwhelmingly shortsighted. As the dust settles, one can only hope that the lesson here isn’t lost on him. But, if history is any indication, Trump is more likely to double down on his defiance, consequences be damned.
