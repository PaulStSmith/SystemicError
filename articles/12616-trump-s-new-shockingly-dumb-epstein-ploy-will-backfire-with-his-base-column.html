Trump's new 'shockingly dumb' Epstein ploy will backfire with his base: column

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Unraveling Trump's Latest Gambit: A Desperate Bid for Transparency or a Calculated Distraction?

Alexander Willis / Raw Story

In the wake of the bombshell Wall Street Journal report that revealed new details about the relationship between President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein, the president has ordered the release of grand jury transcripts in an effort to exonerate himself, a ploy that one columnist described as “shocking dumb,” and one that will likely backfire.

“None of it makes sense,” writes The New Republic staff writer Greg Sargent in a column published Friday.

“Now that Trump wants the grand jury testimony, why won’t this only whet MAGA’s appetite more? Recall that the administration’s initial release of Epstein materials, which contained mostly publicly known stuff, only fueled more MAGA screams of a cover up.”

Trump has been under fire from some of his most dedicated supporters after last week, the Justice Department shut down further investigation into Epstein, alleged to have operated a human trafficking ring and maintained a client list of powerful individuals for blackmail purposes. Many MAGA faithful have been further enraged by Trump’s response to the outrage, who dismissed theories around Epstein as a “hoax” concocted by Democrats.

After the WSJ report, however, Trump has given the all clear for Attorney General Pam Bondi to release select transcripts from a grand jury, though Sargent called the ploy “laughable,” given the trove of Epstein-related documents the DOJ is currently sitting on.

“As many pointed out, the files on Epstein, a sex offender who died in prison in 2019, contain all sorts of other materials, like videos, recordings, FBI witness interviews, and more that could incriminate Trump (or others) if he’s in there,” Sargent wrote.

“The new story is being manufactured before our eyes: Trump is for full disclosure, and his demand is totally owning Democrats (in Trump’s new mythology, Democrats created the files to manufacture a fake story against Trump). If the grand jury testimony doesn’t incriminate Trump (which it likely won’t), he will have been proven right.”

The issue, Sargent writes, is that Trump’s most diehard supporters, many of whom are deeply unsatisfied with Trump’s handling of the Epstein affair, will only grow hungrier for further releases, should Trump follow through on his pledge to release grand jury transcripts.

“If the testimony comes out and paints an unsatisfyingly incomplete picture, why wouldn’t the same anger erupt inside MAGA-world, perhaps more forcefully, what does Trump say at that point?” Sargent writes.

“Trump’s only way out of this is to seek full release of the Epstein files. But he won’t. And he can’t explain why he won’t. The more he calls for meaningless ‘releases’ that don’t provide real transparency, the more he will stoke conspiracism – and the more he’ll appear in on the elite coverup.”

Our Analysis:

Unraveling Trump's Latest Gambit: A Desperate Bid for Transparency or a Calculated Distraction?

In a move that can only be described as paradoxically brazen yet predictably transparent, President Donald Trump has once again found himself embroiled in controversy, this time due to his associations with the notorious Jeffrey Epstein. According to a Wall Street Journal report, Trump has ordered the release of grand jury transcripts in a feeble attempt to clear his name. This decision, lambasted by critics as "shockingly dumb," reeks of desperation and is poised to backfire spectacularly.

Let's dissect the absurdity piece by piece.

Firstly, the notion that releasing grand jury testimony could somehow exonerate Trump is, to put it mildly, laughable. Greg Sargent of The New Republic rightly points out the inherent flaw in Trump's logic. The initial release of Epstein materials, which unearthed nothing the public wasn't already aware of, did little but fan the flames of conspiracy among Trump's most ardent supporters. This begs the question: Why would further releases quell the MAGA mob's thirst for 'truth'?

It's evident that Trump's underestimation of his base's appetite for conspiracy is a gross miscalculation. His dismissal of the Epstein theories as a "hoax" crafted by Democrats has only served to alienate and enrage his followers further. And now, in a desperate bid to regain their favor, Trump has sanctioned Attorney General Pam Bondi to release selected transcripts from a grand jury—an act that screams of selective transparency and manipulation.

Sargent's analysis hits the nail on the head; the DOJ is sitting on a veritable mountain of Epstein-related documents, including videos, recordings, and FBI witness interviews. The materials could potentially be damning not just for Trump but for other high-profile individuals entangled in Epstein's web. Trump's call for the release of grand jury testimony is nothing more than a smokescreen, an attempt to appear as the champion of full disclosure while strategically concealing the bulk of the evidence.

The irony is palpable. Trump's maneuver to release grand jury transcripts is a thinly-veiled attempt to manipulate the narrative, portraying himself as a victim of a Democrat-orchestrated witch hunt. However, this strategy is fundamentally flawed. Even if the transcripts fail to incriminate Trump directly, the omission of the broader array of evidence will only fuel further speculation and discontent among his supporters and critics alike.

Sargent's conclusion that Trump's insistence on meaningless "releases" will only exacerbate conspiratorial thinking and solidify his role in the alleged elite cover-up is spot on. The move is a masterclass in how not to handle scandal management, demonstrating a profound lack of understanding of the very base Trump seeks to appease.

In essence, Trump's latest ploy is a testament to his desperation and inability to navigate the political quagmire he's entrenched himself in. By choosing selective transparency over full disclosure, Trump not only fails to exonerate himself but also risks alienating his base further, proving once and for all that his presidency is more concerned with appearance than substance. The verdict? A spectacularly orchestrated failure, designed to distract, that will inevitably backfire.
