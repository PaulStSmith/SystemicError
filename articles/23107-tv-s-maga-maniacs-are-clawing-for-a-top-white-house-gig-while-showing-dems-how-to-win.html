TV's MAGA maniacs are clawing for a top White House gig — while showing Dems how to win

Based on an article by Tara Dublin

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Press Shop Runs on Loyalty, Not Information

Maria Bartiromo’s departure from Fox News and the scramble over who might replace Karoline Leavitt matter less as personnel gossip than as a window into how Trump’s communications machine works. The source reports a White House press operation drifting through temporary stand-ins, with names like Bartiromo and Scott Jennings floated for the job, while the House is recessed to block an Epstein Files vote and Trump’s “midterm RNC” is reportedly bleeding attendance. That is the context. The point is the machinery underneath it.

The Job Is Power

The White House press secretary is not a ceremonial mouthpiece. The role shapes what the public is told about policy before policy becomes visible in practice. The source is right about that much: whoever gets the job helps decide which facts are emphasized, which are buried, and which are laundered into talking points. In a system like Trump’s, that makes the office a control point, not a communications nicety.

That is why the speculation matters. Bartiromo and Jennings are not being discussed because they are especially qualified to clarify policy. They are being discussed because they already perform obedience for power on camera. The standard is not competence. It is loyalty.

Misdirection Is the Product

The article frames the succession mess as a personality drama around Leavitt, Harp, Bartiromo, Jennings, and Cheung. That framing is convenient because it keeps attention on the actors instead of the structure. The actual structure is simple: Trump wants a press system that never corrects him, never disciplines him, and never treats lies as liabilities.

That is why the revolving door of briefers matters. Mehmet Oz, JD Vance, and whoever else is temporarily stationed at the podium are not there to inform the public. They are there to absorb the day’s contradictions and keep the message intact. The confusion is not accidental. It is a method of governance.

Leavitt Is Not the Story

Leavitt’s reported departure, whatever the private reasons, should not be treated as the central drama. The larger fact is that the White House did not build a durable press operation around truth or even basic administrative seriousness. It built one around performance and grievance. If a press secretary can be replaced by a cable personality, a vice president, or a communications director improvising day to day, that tells you the office is functioning as a loyalty relay, not a governing institution.

The source also notes Trump’s hostility toward pregnancy and postpartum care, but that material mostly serves to expose the moral rot around him. The more important point is not his personal ugliness, though that is abundant. It is that this is the kind of political environment where women’s bodies, labor, and timing are subordinate to the demands of a cult of personality.

Democrats Should Stop Leaving the Field Empty

The strongest part of the source is also its most useful strategic insight: Democrats need a counter-briefing operation. If Trump’s people are using the White House podium as a daily disinformation funnel, then the opposition cannot behave like a passive audience waiting for headlines to mature on their own.

A Democratic press secretary would not need to imitate the White House. It would need to interrupt it. Daily briefings, rapid response, plain-language rebuttal, and consistent exposure of what the administration is doing would force the information fight into the open. The source is correct that Republicans are in disarray. Letting that disarray go unanswered is political malpractice.

The Pattern Is Bigger Than the Podium

This story is not really about Bartiromo, Jennings, Leavitt, or Cheung. It is about a governing style that treats public communication as a weapon, not a duty. It is about institutions hollowed out until they can only reproduce loyalty and denial. It is about a press system where lying is not a bug but the point.

That is the larger lesson. When power captures the language of government, the fight is no longer only over policy. It is over whether the public is allowed to know what is being done in its name.
