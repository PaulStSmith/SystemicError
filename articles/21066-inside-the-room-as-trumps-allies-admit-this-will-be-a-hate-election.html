Inside the room as Trump’s allies admit this will be a 'hate election'

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 12, 20263 min read

Fear Is the Business Model

The polling detail is the least important part of this story. The real fact is that a large share of Americans now experience politics not as representation but as a source of harm, and that fear is not accidental. It is manufactured, priced, and repeated by the people who benefit from keeping the public disoriented.

The Poll Is the Symptom

One in ten Americans telling a global risk survey that politics is their biggest personal danger is not a cultural mood. It is evidence that political power in the United States has become visibly punitive. The article points to concrete threats: Medicaid cuts, Social Security sabotage, privatization schemes, culture-war panic, and a media ecosystem built to keep those threats blurred behind noise.

That is the key context. People are not merely anxious. They are reacting to a governing bloc that has learned to turn public office into a machine for extraction.

Who Built This

The institutional power sits with Republicans, billionaire patrons, and the media apparatus that launders their agenda. The article is blunt about that, and the bluntness is warranted. These are not random legislative failures or ideological misunderstandings. They are decisions: to cut benefits, to weaken public systems, to hand regulatory control to fossil fuels, chemicals, crypto, private health contractors, and every other industry that treats the state as a prize.

The point is not that Republicans are bad at governing. The point is that they are governing as designed, for clients above citizens.

The Misdirection Is the Message

The central trick is not policy. It is scapegoating. When Republicans tell voters to fear migrants, trans people, socialists, Muslims, or whoever is next in line, they are not making an argument. They are covering the tracks of material theft.

That is why the article’s attack on hate-mongering matters. Fear is being used to distract from the actual authors of decline: the elected officials who vote for cuts, the donors who finance them, and the media figures who dress the damage up as common sense. When the public is told to panic about the person mowing the lawn, it is being diverted from the people emptying the house.

The Democratic Record They Want Erased

The article’s most important corrective is historical. The country’s major public goods did not appear by magic, and they did not come from the Republican Party. Social Security, labor rights, Medicare, Medicaid, civil rights enforcement, food assistance, workplace protections, environmental rules, consumer protections, Pell Grants, and the rest were built through Democratic power and organized democratic pressure.

That is not party worship. It is institutional memory. The only serious national coalition that has repeatedly built durable public capacity is the democratic side of American politics. Republicans spent decades trying to break that capacity, privatize its remains, and convince voters that the wreckage was proof government never worked.

What This Reveals

This story is not really about one poll or one election cycle. It is about an authoritarian pattern hiding inside normal politics: concentrate wealth, capture institutions, starve public goods, then sell resentment as identity. That pattern depends on amnesia. It depends on people forgetting who built the protections they now take for granted, and who is still trying to destroy them.

The article is right about the stakes. Democratic politics is not failing because it is too ambitious. It is under siege because it remains the only institutional barrier to a governing project that treats fear as a substitute for legitimacy and cruelty as a substitute for policy.
