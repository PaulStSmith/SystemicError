'As serious as Watergate': Trump just put nightmare scenario on 'edge of becoming reality'

Paulo SantosSeptember 22, 20254 min read

President Donald J. Trump speaking at his campaign rally in Des Moines, Iowa at Drake University’s Knapp Center. (Aspects and Angles / Shutterstock.com)

Donald Trump's latest move just put a nightmare scenario within reach, according to an ex-prosecutor.

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance wrote an essay in which she addressed Trump's latest efforts to indict his perceived political enemies. Specifically, she noted how the president signaled his willingness to replace prosecutors who don't go along with his goals.

"If you’ve been paying attention, it’s not exactly news that this is Donald Trump’s revenge presidency. It’s not even news that Donald Trump is trying to use the criminal justice system to harm people he considers to be his enemies. Investigations launched against Letitia James, Adam Schiff, Jim Comey, and others fully substantiate that, despite the under oath promises by Bondi and FBI Director Kash Patel that there would be no enemy lists when they stood for confirmation," Vance wrote. "But now it’s clear that there are. And Trump will install a U.S. Attorney in Virginia who will guarantee that. If she deviates from the plan because she is principled, she, too, will likely be replaced with someone more compliant. Perhaps the administration will want to let go of this messy situation, let a lengthy re-investigation forestall additional controversy, rather than seek a bad indictment and have a grand jury reject it, or get a marginal indictment and face dismissal from a judge or a failure to convict by a trial jury. But Trump seems extremely focused on getting revenge."

According to Vance, "It feels like it should be unnecessary to state the obvious here, that there’s not much of a case if the only way to get it prosecuted is by firing an experienced prosecutor to get an inexperienced one (or no prosecutor at all) to bring it. So let’s be clear about what Trump wants. He wants to turn us into a banana republic where the ability to prosecute people becomes a political tool in the hands of the president. That means he wants to exercise the ultimate power to put down any opposition to his rule."

The ex-prosecutor then added, "It’s precisely what I’ve been discussing with you on television and in writing since the start of Trump’s first administration. It has always been a risk, now it’s on the edge of becoming a reality. Any president who would behave this way is a president in name only; in truth, they are well down the road to becoming a dictator. This is a moment as serious, if not more, as the one the country faced during Watergate."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Descent into Despotism: A Critical Analysis

In a recent essay that reads like a clarion call to defend the very soul of American democracy, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance lays bare the dangerous trajectory of Donald Trump's presidency. Vance's essay, addressing Trump's overt attempts to manipulate the justice system to target his political adversaries, is not just a cautionary tale—it's a red alert for anyone with even a cursory understanding of democratic principles.

The Revenge Presidency

It's no secret that Donald Trump's tenure in the Oval Office has been marked by vendettas and vindictiveness, but Vance's analysis elevates the conversation to the realm of the terrifyingly tangible. Trump's willingness to replace prosecutors who resist his directives is an affront to the independence of the judicial system, a cornerstone of democratic governance.

Vance elucidates on the investigations launched against figures such as Letitia James, Adam Schiff, and Jim Comey, painting a picture of a president obsessed with retribution. The mention of under-oath promises by Bondi and FBI Director Kash Patel that there would be no enemy lists—now proven to be empty reassurances—underscores the deceit at the heart of Trump's administration.

The Banana Republic Blueprint

Perhaps the most chilling aspect of Vance's essay is the depiction of Trump's ultimate goal: transforming the United States into a "banana republic" where prosecutorial power is wielded as a political weapon. This isn't just an abuse of power; it's a systematic dismantling of the checks and balances that protect American freedom.

Vance's assertion that Trump's actions are pushing the country to the brink of dictatorship isn't hyperbolic—it's a logical conclusion. The comparison to Watergate, a scandal that rocked the nation and led to the resignation of President Nixon, serves as a stark reminder of the stakes involved.

A Dictator in All but Name

The ex-prosecutor's final warning—that any president who behaves in such a manner is a president in name only, veering dangerously close to dictatorship—is a potent summary of the existential threat Trump poses. It underscores the necessity of recognizing and resisting the erosion of democratic norms and institutions.

In Conclusion

Joyce Vance's essay isn't just an analysis; it's a battle cry for all who value democracy and the rule of law. Her insights reveal the extent of Trump's attempts to subvert the justice system for personal vendettas, warning of the dire consequences should he succeed.

In a world where the truth often gets buried under a barrage of misinformation, Vance's clarity is both refreshing and necessary. It serves as a reminder that the fight for democracy is ongoing, and complacency is a luxury we can ill afford. As the United States teeters on the edge of a precipice, the choice between democratic principles and dictatorial desires has never been clearer.
