The SEAL Raid That Exposed Trump’s Unfitness for Command

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 5, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The New York Times has dropped a bombshell that rips apart the carefully cultivated myths of Donald Trump’s supposed “deal-making genius” and exposes him for what he really was: a reckless showman who treated international diplomacy like a reality TV plot twist and played soldier with other people’s lives. In early 2019, while publicly flattering Kim Jong-un and mugging for cameras, Trump greenlit one of the riskiest covert operations in decades: sending SEAL Team 6 into North Korean territory to plant a listening device.

The goal was to tap directly into Kim’s communications, supposedly solving an intelligence blind spot that decades of sanctions, negotiations, and threats had failed to overcome. The plan called for submarines, mini-subs, underwater insertions, and perfect timing. It also called for secrecy so tight that even Congress — the body constitutionally charged with oversight of covert operations — was kept in the dark. And when the plan went wrong, it went wrong in exactly the way experts have been warning about for years: civilians died, diplomacy collapsed, and the United States was left more vulnerable than before.

A Mission of Hubris

Even for America’s most elite operators, this mission was a nightmare scenario. The SEALs had to ride in freezing water aboard “wet subs” for hours, crawl ashore in darkness, plant delicate electronics, and then slip away undetected from one of the most heavily militarized regimes on earth. They had no drones to provide live coverage. Their communication was restricted to near-silence. The plan depended entirely on guesswork about fishing patterns and local traffic.

And then reality intruded. The SEALs surfaced on what they believed was a deserted shore. Instead, they encountered a North Korean boat. Believing themselves discovered, they opened fire. Within moments, every person on the boat — likely civilian fishermen — was dead. Their bodies were punctured and sunk to avoid discovery. The listening device was never planted. The team fled, calling in emergency extraction from a nuclear submarine that risked detection by moving into shallow coastal waters. What could have been catastrophic — capture, escalation, or war — was averted only by luck.

Civilian Casualties, Military Lies

The civilians who died that night paid the price of Trump’s “boldness.” Fishermen, not soldiers, were slaughtered by the most elite unit in the U.S. arsenal. This was not a heroic tale of American strength. It was a botched operation, steeped in arrogance and sealed by poor judgment. And instead of accountability, the Trump administration buried the story. Key congressional leaders were never informed. No public acknowledgment was made. The operators were promoted. The mission was scrubbed from memory.

This is the problem with the cult of SEAL Team 6. To the public, they are mythic warriors, known for killing Osama bin Laden and dramatized in films. But their real track record is far more complicated: failed missions, civilian deaths, cover-ups, and propaganda that hides failure behind a wall of secrecy. Trump leaned into that myth because it served his ego. He wanted the glory of decisive action without the responsibility of oversight or the honesty of accountability.

Diplomacy by Photo-Op

The grotesque irony is that while SEALs were swimming toward disaster, Trump was still playing the part of statesman. His on-again, off-again romance with Kim Jong-un swung from “fire and fury” to “love letters.” Summits were staged in Singapore, Vietnam, and the DMZ. Trump’s base applauded these images as proof that he could achieve what no president before him had managed.

But beneath the smiles and handshakes, Trump was authorizing operations that, if exposed, could have triggered exactly the war those summits were meant to prevent. He was undermining his own diplomacy with shadow play. And when the inevitable collapse came — when talks ended in failure, when North Korea ramped up its missile tests, when their nuclear arsenal swelled to fifty warheads — the United States was left weaker, not stronger.

A Pattern of Recklessness

This was not an isolated incident. Trump’s tenure was littered with examples of recklessness masquerading as boldness:

  • The Yemen raid (2017): Greenlit days into his term, bypassing deliberation, the mission killed 30 villagers, including women and children, and a Navy SEAL. A $75 million aircraft was lost. Trump called it a success.

  • North Korea diplomacy: Trump made himself the first U.S. president to step onto North Korean soil — a TV-ready stunt that achieved nothing strategically.

  • Abandoning oversight: By cutting deliberation processes and sidelining Congress, Trump turned America’s most sensitive operations into extensions of his political ego.

Time and again, Trump showed that he was willing to gamble with lives and global stability for headlines and ratings. He was not a commander-in-chief; he was a gambler playing with nuclear stakes.

The Human Cost of Ego

The true obscenity of this story is not just the risk of war. It is the civilians — poor, unarmed North Koreans — killed by American commandos because Donald Trump wanted leverage at a negotiating table. Their deaths will never be acknowledged by the U.S. government. Their families will never know what happened. They are ghosts erased by secrecy, collateral damage to a president’s vanity project.

It should be searing to every American conscience that in our name, and with our tax dollars, fishermen were slaughtered on a dark beach, their bodies mutilated to sink into the sea. All for an operation that achieved nothing. That is the legacy of Trump’s version of “strength.”

Garbage Collector in Chief

Let’s be blunt: Donald Trump was not fit to be president. He was not fit to command troops. He was not fit to make decisions that could end or save lives. He was not fit to carry the nuclear codes. He is not fit to be a garbage collector — and we say that with the deepest respect for garbage collectors, who perform their essential work with discipline, integrity, and accountability. Unlike Trump, they show up and take responsibility for what they do.

A garbage collector who fails in his duties faces consequences. Trump presided over a string of reckless failures with no accountability, shielded by partisanship and secrecy. In the end, the garbage men of America have more honor than the man who occupied the Oval Office.

Lessons Ignored

The Biden administration reviewed the mission and briefed Congress in 2021. But the findings remain classified. The military has quietly moved on, burying another failure under the rubble of “national security.” The public is left with sanitized myths, as if SEAL Team 6 is infallible and presidents always act wisely.

But the truth matters. Not because it will bring back the dead, but because it exposes the rot of a system where secrecy shields failure and presidents play at war like children. If Americans don’t learn this lesson, if they continue to buy the myth of Trump the strongman, then we are doomed to repeat these disasters.

Conclusion

The 2019 SEAL raid in North Korea was not just a military failure. It was a moral failure, a diplomatic failure, and an indictment of a president who saw the world as a stage and human lives as props. It shows what happens when ego overrides judgment, when oversight is ignored, when secrecy smothers accountability.

Donald Trump did not make America safer. He made the world more dangerous. He left corpses in his wake and handed adversaries more incentive to arm themselves. His legacy is not one of strength but of chaos, cowardice, and blood in the water.

History must record this honestly: Donald Trump was not fit to lead. He was not fit to decide life and death. He was not fit to command even a garbage truck, much less the United States military.
