The real reason Trump is attacking Canada over a bridge

On Monday, President Donald Trump learned a new bridge between Michigan and Ontario was set to save U.S. and Canadian residents the cost of using one aging toll bridge owned by one of his billionaire GOP donors.

Atlantic writer Janathan Chait reports Trump “decided this could not stand.”

Trump attacked Canada over the new bridge, which would give drivers an alternative to the $10 to $20 Ambassador Bridge, announcing on social media: “I will not allow this bridge to open until the United States is fully compensated for everything we have given them, and also, importantly, Canada treats the United States with the Fairness and Respect that we deserve.”

“On the surface, this looked to be just one more Trumpian tantrum, the kind that regularly pops up when he sees something distressing on television or is spoken to by a woman without the self-abasement he demands,” Chait wrote. “But subsequent reporting suggests that this was something even worse: an episode that sums up Trumpian economics in all its stupidity and atavistic sleaze.”

Hours before Trump’s post, according to The New York Times, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick (an outed associate of convicted sex-trafficker Jeffrey Epstein) met with competing bridge owner Matthew Moroun. He then called Trump.

“You might wonder why a major international bridge has an owner when such things are ordinarily in public hands. The answer is that the Ambassador Bridge was privately constructed, and for decades has stood as the sole trucking link from Detroit to Windsor,” said Chait, adding that the Ambassador Bridge gets clogged with traffic but is still the sole connection to Canada because “Moroun’s family has spent decades and millions of dollars trying to keep things that way, relentlessly lobbying to block construction of a second bridge desired by drivers and merchants on both sides of the Detroit River.”

The new Gordie Howe International Bridge, unlike Moroun’s bridge, has a dedicated lane for bicycles and pedestrians. And Chait says the new bridge would “unlock billions of dollars in savings for consumers and businesses,” but the sole loser is Moroun, “a billionaire whose fortune rests on rent seeking.”

“Now that the bridge construction is essentially complete and set to finally open, Moroun has gone to the administration, and Trump has shut down his competition,” Chait said.

“This episode is a prototypical demonstration of Trump’s economic worldview. Faced with a policy choice that pits the interests of millions of people against the wealth of a single rent seeker, Trump has intervened in a way that benefits the billionaire,” Chait continued. “The insight that the free exchange of goods and services has positive-sum benefits — a core tenet of market economics — has always eluded Trump. His instincts are not capitalistic but pre-capitalistic. He has the mentality of a Renaissance baron, hoarding power and collecting tribute rather than innovating and creating wealth.”

The president, he said, “is shrinking the pie, while delivering a larger slice of it to a Trumpian oligarch.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Bridge Controversy: A Tale of Greed Over Public Good

In what appears to be a clear exhibition of crony capitalism at its worst, President Donald Trump’s recent intervention to halt the opening of the new Gordie Howe International Bridge between Michigan and Ontario starkly highlights a troubling preference for the interests of a wealthy few over the welfare of millions. The details, as reported by Janathan Chait in The Atlantic and further investigation by The New York Times, reveal a narrative so deeply infused with self-interest and disregard for economic principles that it borders on the absurd.

The Heart of the Matter

The crux of this debacle lies in Trump’s decision to block the opening of the Gordie Howe International Bridge—a decision apparently made after a meeting between Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Matthew Moroun, the owner of the competing Ambassador Bridge, and a subsequent call to Trump. This move blatantly favors Moroun, a billionaire GOP donor, over the clear economic and logistical benefits the new bridge would provide to countless U.S. and Canadian residents.

Trumpian Economics: A Study in Stupidity and Sleaze

Chait’s critique of Trump’s action as a “prototypical demonstration of Trump’s economic worldview” is both scathing and spot-on. It’s a worldview where the interests of the many are sacrificed on the altar of the wealth of the few—a billionaire whose fortune, as Chait aptly notes, rests on rent-seeking. This is not capitalism; it’s a feudalistic hoarding of resources and power. Trump, in his actions, seems to embody the mentality of a “Renaissance baron,” far removed from the principles of market economics that champion the free exchange of goods and services for the benefit of all.

A Bridge Too Far

The Gordie Howe International Bridge, with its dedicated lane for bicycles and pedestrians and the promise of unlocking billions in savings for consumers and businesses, stands as a beacon of modern infrastructure and economic progress. Yet, it has been thwarted by the very administration that claims to champion economic growth and innovation. The sole loser in this scenario, Moroun, is ironically portrayed as the victim, while millions of actual victims—drivers, merchants, and consumers on both sides of the Detroit River—are left to contend with the congested, outdated Ambassador Bridge.

The Real Losers

It is the public who loses in this egregious display of power misuse and economic mismanagement. Trump’s actions do not just “shrink the pie,” as Chait argues, but actively poison it, prioritizing a larger slice for a “Trumpian oligarch” over the economic health and welfare of two nations. This incident is a stark reminder of the dangers of allowing the wealth and influence of a few to dictate policy and progress at the expense of the many.

In Conclusion

The Gordie Howe International Bridge controversy is not just a story about a bridge. It’s a damning indictment of an administration that chooses personal loyalties and self-interest over public good and economic sense. As we dissect this episode, we see not just a failure of policy, but a failure of principle—a retreat from the ideals of free market capitalism to the dark ages of economic feudalism. In this tale of greed over public good, it is clear that the Trump administration’s economic policies are not just misguided; they are morally bankrupt.
