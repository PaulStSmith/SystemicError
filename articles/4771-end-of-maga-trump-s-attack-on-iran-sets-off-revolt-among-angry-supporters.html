'End of MAGA': Trump's attack on Iran sets off revolt among angry supporters

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 1, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Stark Betrayal: Trump’s Iranian Assault

Donald Trump speaks during the signing of executive orders in the Oval Office. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

A decision by Donald Trump to launch an assault on Iran with the help of Israel has set off a firestorm among longtime supporters who helped propel him to a second term based, in part, on his promise of no foreign military interventions.

In the wee hours of the night in the US, jets pounded Tehran, with smoke being seen near the presidential palace.

The New York Times reported, “The American-led attack appeared to herald a much broader regional crisis. Iranian news media reported that Iran had targeted at least four U.S. military bases across the Persian Gulf — including in the United Arab Emirates and Qatar, which said they had come under attack.”

On social media, the response among conservatives was condemnatory with a former House supporter of Trump declaring, “End of MAGA.”

Conservative podcaster Meghan McCain wrote, “Remember when every maga personality screamed at the top of their lungs that anyone with the last name McCain was a blood thirsty neocon warmonger who would bomb Iran and had no place in the Republican Party? Yea I didn’t forget that either.”

Gateway Pundit writer Cassandra MacDonald reposted a post from the late Charlie Kirk who had written, after an earlier attack, “This is insane. Regime change will result in a bloody civil war, killing hundreds of thousands and creating another massive Muslim refugee crisis. Topping (sic) a leader is NEVER as easy you think. It almost always results in further involvement, a civil war, and chaos. Resist this!”

Trump ally Andrew Tate was even more blunt, writing, “NOBODY WANTS THIS WAR,” before adding, “War transfers wealth from the working class to the banking class. So does fraud and inflation. The working class man simply cant catch a break and is endlessly wrecked. This is The Matrix - extraction from the populace to feed a machine minded host. Heartless.”

A day before the assault, Ex-Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) predicted the attack and added it to her list of Trump betrayals by posting a clip of Trump previously railing against wars and then writing, “This is the Trump I supported, the man who called out the truth about the WMD in Iraq and declared NO MORE FOREIGN WARS. Now for some unknown reason, Trump has joined the neocons and will soon go to war against Iran with the same BS excuses. End of MAGA.”

On Saturday ,Taylor Greene added, “I did not campaign for this. I did not donate money for this. I did not vote for this, in elections or Congress. This is heartbreaking and tragic. And how many more innocent will die? What about our own military? This is not what we thought MAGA was supposed to be. Shame!“

“Hold our country in prayer right now. Hold our service members and their families in prayer right now. Hold the innocent people of Iran in prayer right now,” lamented Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL), an Air Force veteran.

One of her followers wrote, “I’m afraid the GOP is going to lose the Senate as well because of this war.”

Conservative pundit Matt Lewis chipped in, “I was told that if I didn’t vote for Trump, we’d keep doing these forever wars. And they were right …?”

“I voted for Trump in 2016 and 2020. You can change your mind when new information is presented to you. I did that well before the 2024 election. When he lied about DeSantis and bragged about Warp Speed, I knew I was out. Now he is starting a very dangerous war. It’s not too late to withdraw your support of a President who has betrayed the American people,” accused Nicholas J. Stelzner.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Betrayal: Trump’s Iranian Assault

The Unraveling of MAGA Promises

In a move that has sent shockwaves through his once unshakeable base, Donald Trump’s decision to launch an assault on Iran, in collaboration with Israel, has sparked a monumental backlash from his staunchest supporters. This bold military action directly contradicts Trump’s loud campaign promises of avoiding foreign military entanglements, promises that were instrumental in securing him a second term. As the smoke rises from Tehran, so does the ire of the conservatives who once championed his cause, marking a pivotal moment of disillusionment and betrayal.

The Hypocrisy of War

The narratives spun by Trump and his allies about avoiding the quagmires of foreign conflicts have come crashing down, exposing a glaring hypocrisy that has not gone unnoticed. Meghan McCain’s scathing reminder of the MAGA movement’s previous branding of the McCain family as warmongers underscores the irony of Trump’s current warpath. This volte-face from peacetime promises to aggressive military action starkly highlights the inconsistency and opportunism rife within Trump’s administration.

A Chorus of Condemnation

The outcry from conservative circles has been both loud and clear. Figures like Andrew Tate and the late Charlie Kirk, previously in lockstep with Trump’s policies, have voiced their strong opposition, underscoring the war’s detrimental impact on the working class and the potential for chaos and further conflict it brings. The sentiments expressed by these conservatives reveal a deep-seated concern for the ramifications of such unilateral military actions, not just abroad but also for the American people.

The Betrayal Felt by Supporters

Perhaps the most poignant expressions of betrayal come from the likes of Marjorie Taylor Greene and her ilk, who have been ardent supporters of Trump’s ‘America First’ doctrine. Greene’s lament over Trump’s abandonment of his non-interventionist stance serves as a bitter pill for those who once saw in him a bulwark against the neoconservative agenda. The disillusionment within Trump’s base is palpable, with figures like Nicholas J. Stelzner publicly revoking their support in light of these recent actions. This sense of betrayal is not just about the assault on Iran; it’s a reflection of a broader disenchantment with a leader who promised to be different but ended up walking a path all too familiar.

A Divided Base and the Fallout

The reactions from Trump’s conservative base illuminate a deep and potentially irreparable rift. The condemnation from figures like Rep. Anna Paulina Luna and the fear of political repercussions expressed by her followers hint at the broader political implications of Trump’s decision. This isn’t merely about a single military action; it’s about the erosion of trust and the potential for significant shifts in political loyalties.

Conclusion: The Echo of Broken Promises

The assault on Iran by the Trump administration, with its consequential backlash from within his own ranks, serves as a stark reminder of the perils of political inconsistency and the hollow nature of campaign promises. As the dust settles, the MAGA movement finds itself at a crossroads, torn between allegiance to a figure who once symbolized their hopes and the harsh reality of his actions. The fallout from this decision will undoubtedly continue to reverberate, prompting a reevaluation of what it truly means to put America first.
