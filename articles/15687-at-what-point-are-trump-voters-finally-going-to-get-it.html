At what point are Trump voters finally going to get it?

By Paulo Santos

Almost 100 days into the most loathsome authoritarian regime in American history, and there’s still uncertainty about if and when we call it a dictatorship.

Most Republicans prefer to believe that Trump is merely fulfilling campaign pledges that a majority of voters endorsed. Many Democrats and independents are outraged but prefer to believe we still live in a democracy, albeit one that Trump is testing.

The question I keep asking politicians, political scientists, pundits, and pollsters is what event will trigger their view that we are no longer living in a democracy. To put the question another way: What’s their definition of a dictatorship?

Let me list below the major responses I’ve received. All appear to be growing more probable by the day. Please register your own view in the poll below.

Which of these triggering events would most clearly turn America into a dictatorship?

1. Trump openly and directly defies the Supreme Court. He does what JD Vance has suggested — tells the court that although it has made its decision, it cannot enforce it. This may occur if and when the court pushes the regime to have Kilmar Abrego Garcia returned from the harsh prison in El Salvador to which he was illegally and unconstitutionally sent. Or it could be triggered by a court decision on birthright citizenship with which Trump disagrees.

2. The Trump regime openly abducts American citizens whom the regime alleges are dangerous, without any hearing. ICE has already done this on several occasions, but it has released them once their citizenship has been established. Yet we seem to be getting close to the point when allegedly “dangerous” American citizens are not released, at least not for months or years.

3. The Trump regime launches criminal investigations of Americans against whom Trump seeks vengeance — people such as former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi, former special counsel for the Department of Justice Jack Smith, former President Joe Biden, or anyone else who tried to hold Trump accountable. Trump has threatened such criminal investigations, but none has occurred as yet.

4. The Trump regime directly penalizes individuals or organizations that have criticized it, or explicitly urges Trump supporters to punish any such critic. Here again, Trump and his regime have come close to doing both — consider the penalties imposed on law firms and media corporations that have crossed Trump, or the provocative tweets Trump, Vance, and Musk have posted against perceived enemies — but so far, their efforts have been indirect or made to appear justified by their target’s other alleged malfeasance.

It seems highly likely that at least one of these will tip America into a Trumpian dictatorship. Although some observers believe we’re already in it, most say we’re not there yet but easily could be.

Hence, today’s Office Hours question: What event tips us into a dictatorship?

Our Analysis:

The Precipice of Authoritarianism: Analyzing the Trumpian Threat

The article presents a chilling narrative, one that paints the current U.S. political landscape under the Trump administration as teetering on the brink of outright dictatorship. The author, Robert Reich, not only outlines the potential triggers that could plunge the United States into authoritarian rule but also implicitly critiques the complacency and denial pervasive in American political discourse. Let's dissect the key points and the underlying implications of this compelling analysis.

The Criteria for Dictatorship

Reich identifies four critical events that, if realized, could signify the United States' descent into dictatorship under Trump:

  1. Defying the Supreme Court: The notion that the President could simply ignore the highest court in the land is a flagrant violation of the checks and balances principle fundamental to American democracy. This scenario, inspired by JD Vance's comments, would represent a clear move towards autocracy.

  2. Abducting American Citizens: The actions of ICE, as described, already hint at a government operating with impunity. The evolution from temporary detention to indefinite holding of American citizens, under the guise of them being "dangerous," is a hallmark of dictatorial regimes.

  3. Launching Vengeance-Driven Criminal Investigations: The use of state power to persecute political enemies is a tactic as old as dictatorship itself. Should Trump initiate such investigations, it would mark a disturbing shift from democratic norms to tyrannical vendettas.

  4. Penalizing Critics: The final criterion involves directly targeting individuals or organizations that criticize the regime. While the Trump administration has come close to this, a move to outright punishment would cross a definitive line into authoritarian behavior.

The Veil of Justification

A striking aspect of Reich's analysis is the emphasis on the thin veil of justification that the Trump regime has used to mask its near-authoritarian actions. By framing punitive measures as responses to "malfeasance" or protecting the nation from "dangerous" individuals, the administration maintains a facade of legitimacy. This tactic is disturbingly effective, allowing many to dismiss signs of authoritarianism as mere policy disagreements or necessary security measures.

Complacency and Denial

Equally concerning is the portrayal of a nation in denial. Reich highlights the reluctance of both Republicans and Democrats to fully acknowledge the authoritarian direction in which they are heading. This complacency, fueled by a mix of disbelief, party loyalty, and perhaps fear, sets the stage for the erosion of democratic norms. The question posed by Reich, "What event tips us into a dictatorship?" serves as a stark reminder that the threshold for such a transition may be far more nebulous and closer than many dare to admit.

Conclusion

Robert Reich's article is a clarion call to vigilance. It outlines not just potential future scenarios but current realities that edge perilously close to authoritarian rule. By framing these developments not as distant possibilities but as ongoing processes, Reich challenges the reader to reconsider their perceptions of democracy's resilience.

In a political climate rife with division and escalating tensions, the value of such analyses cannot be overstated. As co-authors of 'Systemic Error', it is our duty to amplify these warnings, dissect the veneer of normalcy, and confront the systemic threats to democracy with unwavering clarity and resolve. The path forward requires not just acknowledgment of the risks outlined by Reich but collective action to ensure they remain hypothetical rather than historical realities.
