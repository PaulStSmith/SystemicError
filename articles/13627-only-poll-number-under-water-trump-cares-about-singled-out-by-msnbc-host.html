MSNBC's Jonathan Capehart noted Donald Trump's plummeting poll numbers early Saturday morning and pointed out there is only one metric that could make him change the course he has put the country on.

On the day when Trump is using the 250th Anniversary of the U.S. Army to throw a parade aimed more at celebrating his birthday, Capehart pointed out the president is swiftly falling out of favor with voters.

From his spot on MSNBC's "The Weekend," Capehart explained to his co-hosts and guests, "You know, on immigration, the president is underwater. There's a new poll, this poll shows that Trump's approval rating is at 38% percent among registered voters, which is a three-point drop from April."

"And then there's another, this is from Politico, and on immigration, Trump's approval rating dropped five points from April to 43 percent," he continued. "Now, I put these poll numbers up there, and I bring this up because there's another thing that people need to keep in mind when looking at any polls that talk about President Trump's ratings, approval ratings overall or on any particular issue: the president couldn't care less about his overall approval rating. "

"What he cares about is where he stands among Republicans. And as long as his approval rating among Republicans is north of 50 percent, he is fine."

"Right," former Washington Post columnist Eugene Robinson agreed before elaborating, "Because he's got the Republican majorities in Congress he can get his agenda through as long as Republicans broadly support him. As that wanes, if that wanes, you know, as Lincoln said, public opinion is everything really you need to have that sort of support."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Approval Ratings: A Dive into the Depths

MSNBC's Jonathan Capehart shed light on a situation that many have sensed but few have articulated with such clarity: Donald Trump's plummeting approval ratings. In an early Saturday morning revelation, Capehart didn't just point out the decline; he underscored the singular metric that might force Trump to reconsider the divisive path he has charted for the country. Yet, in a twist that surprises no one, Trump's focus remains unwaveringly narrow, prioritizing the approval of his Republican base above all else.

The Parade of Ego

On a day that should have been about commemorating the 250th Anniversary of the U.S. Army, Trump instead seemingly hijacked the event to serve as a backdrop for a self-celebratory parade. This move, emblematic of Trump's presidency, puts his ego on full display, overshadowing what should have been a moment of national pride and reflection.

Sinking Ship or Steady in Choppy Waters?

Capehart draws attention to a concerning trend for the Trump administration: a significant drop in approval ratings. With a three-point drop since April among registered voters, plummeting to a dismal 38%, and a five-point drop on the immigration issue to 43%, the numbers paint a bleak picture. However, as Capehart astutely points out, these figures seem to barely register on Trump's radar. Why? Because his approval rating among Republicans remains above the 50% threshold, giving him little to no incentive to change course.

The Republican Safety Net

Echoing Capehart's analysis, former Washington Post columnist Eugene Robinson highlights a crucial point: as long as Trump maintains support within his party, he retains the ability to push his agenda through Congress. This is where the real danger lies. Trump's disregard for broader public opinion and his laser focus on maintaining Republican support underscore a disturbing reality of his presidency: as long as he has the backing of his party, broader disapproval remains inconsequential to him.

The Fatal Flaw in Trump's Armor

What this analysis brings to light is not just the declining approval ratings of a president deeply entrenched in controversy and division. It exposes a fundamental flaw in Trump's approach to leadership: a complete disregard for the wider electorate. In a democracy, such a stance is not only dangerous; it's unsustainable. While Trump may revel in the safety net his party currently provides, history has shown us that public opinion, though slow to mobilize, is a force to be reckoned with.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

Capehart's insights serve as a stark reminder of the precarious path Trump is walking. With a focus so narrowly cast on maintaining party approval, Trump risks alienating not just the wider voter base, but the very principles of democratic leadership. The declining poll numbers should not just be a wake-up call for Trump, but for all who support him, signaling a need to recalibrate or face the consequences at the ballot box.

As we digest Capehart's analysis, let's remember: in a democracy, leaders ignore public opinion at their own peril. Trump's current trajectory is a dangerous gamble, one that threatens to not only undermine his presidency but erode the very foundations of our democratic institutions.
