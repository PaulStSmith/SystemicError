“It’s not,” the legal analyst Rubin clapped back. “But that doesn’t mean it can’t happen.”

Rubin on Wednesday flagged Leavitt’s comments she made at a daily news briefing this week for readers of his MSNBC legal blog – which the former prosecutor said were forewarned by Justice Sonia Sotomayor just a day earlier.

“Dissenting from the Supreme Court’s decision to grant emergency relief to the government in a case about deportations, Sotomayor wrote that the implications of the Trump administration’s legal stance is that ‘not only noncitizens but also United States citizens could be taken off the streets, forced onto planes, and confined to foreign prisons with no opportunity for redress if judicial review is denied unlawfully before removal,”' according to Rubin.

That legal battle is currently pending before the high court, the MSNBC analyst said.

“Leavitt’s comments thus reinforce the importance of the court’s forthcoming decision in Abrego Garcia’s case, whose consequences could inform just how far this administration will go,” Rubin concluded.

Our Analysis:

The Unfathomable Suggestion: Deporting American Citizens

In a world where the lines between absurdity and official policy proposals blur, MSNBC's Jordan Rubin delivers a chilling analysis of White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt's recent remarks that flirt with the dystopian idea of deporting American citizens who are "violent" repeat offenders. The audacity of such a suggestion, aside from being blatantly illegal, illustrates a worrying trend within the current administration's approach to law and order.

"It's not legal, but that doesn't mean it can't happen," Rubin starkly warns. This statement alone should send shivers down the spine of anyone who values civil liberties and the rule of law. The mere implication that the U.S. government could, even theoretically, entertain the idea of deporting its own citizens is a grotesque distortion of justice and a flagrant violation of constitutional rights.

The Warning from Justice Sotomayor

Rubin astutely connects Leavitt's comments to a prescient warning from Justice Sonia Sotomayor. The day before Leavitt's comments, Sotomayor dissented from the Supreme Court's decision to grant emergency relief in a deportation case. She highlighted the terrifying implications of the Trump administration's legal stance: the possibility that not only noncitizens but also U.S. citizens could be "taken off the streets, forced onto planes, and confined to foreign prisons" without legal recourse if judicial review is bypassed unlawfully.

The Case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia

The potential reality of Leavitt's suggestion is brought into sharp focus with the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland father mistakenly sent to an El Salvadoran mega-prison by the Trump administration. This case, currently pending before the Supreme Court, underscores the real-life consequences of a government that plays fast and loose with the law. Rubin emphasizes that the outcome of Abrego Garcia's case could set a precedent, determining just how far this administration is willing to go in its pursuit of a draconian law and order agenda.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Path Forward

Rubin's analysis is a stark reminder of the dangerous path the current administration seems all too willing to tread. The suggestion of deporting American citizens, "if it's legal," represents a fundamental misunderstanding of, or perhaps a willful ignorance to, the principles upon which the United States was founded. The rights to due process and protection from exile are bedrocks of the Constitution and are not to be trifled with or discarded on a whim.

The fact that such a suggestion was even floated by a White House press secretary is alarming. It signifies a potential willingness to explore avenues that are not only unconstitutional but deeply authoritarian in nature. The judiciary, as Rubin eloquently highlights through the words of Justice Sotomayor and the pending case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, remains a critical bulwark against such overreach.

In Rubin's sharp analysis, we are reminded of the importance of vigilance and the need to push back against any attempt to undermine the foundational liberties that define American democracy.
