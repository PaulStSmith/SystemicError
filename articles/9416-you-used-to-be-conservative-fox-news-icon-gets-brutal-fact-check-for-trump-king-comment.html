'You used to be conservative': Fox News icon gets brutal fact check for Trump king comment

Fox News political analyst Brit Hume was hit with outrage on Saturday after appearing to reject the premise of the “No Kings” protests this weekend.

Hume took to social media to defend the president. He wrote, “The ‘No Kings’ rallies are mostly a protest against a man who sought power 3 times through democratic elections and enacted his major agenda item by majority vote of democratically elected members of Congress.”

“Some king,” he then added.

But he was quickly corrected.

Ex-GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger replied directly to Hume, writing, “Come on. You know what this means.”

“Of course he’s not a literal king because we won’t let him be. But our nation was founded on anti-corruption, disseminating power to the people, holding all equal under the law,” he wrote Saturday. “Trump tried to overthrow a free election, pardoned 1000 criminals, and has placed congress on their knees. Oh not to mention the Epstein files, and his 400 million dollar free jet that we will pay 1 billion to upgrade and give to him. Gold palaces and ballrooms and massive arches are not the works of someone who seeks humble power.”

Political scientist Norman Ornstein had a list of his own:

“Executive actions, illegal acts, including ‘pocket’ rescissions, firing people and eliminating agencies, bragging about killing fisherman in violation of international law. Weaponized DOJ and FBI in an open campaign of retribution,” he wrote. “Masked thugs without identification assaulting children and American citizens, throwing them into detention without their constitutional rights being respected. Federal judges nominated by every modern president trying to block imperial actions. Brit, you used to be a conservative.”

He continued: “Let’s talk about selling pardons, violating emoluments clauses by enriching himself and his family through his office, including a $400 million jet bribe from Qatar, and Trump resorts in various countries, not done out of largesse but knowing they can extract something in return.”

Ornstein concluded, “If you don’t see something troubling in all of this, Brit, you have replaced your integrity with fealty to the cult. Which is very, very sad.”

Our Analysis:

Brit Hume’s Misguided Defense: A Tale of Willful Ignorance

In a display of what can only be described as a baffling attempt to redefine reality, Fox News political analyst Brit Hume has sparked a firestorm with his comments on the “No Kings” protests. His assertion, suggesting these demonstrations merely oppose a president who utilized democratic mechanisms, is not just simplistic; it’s dangerously misleading.

Hume’s stance is a glaring example of selective interpretation, a skill that seems to be finely honed among certain political commentators. To claim that these protests are an overreaction to a leader who “sought power 3 times through democratic elections” and “enacted his major agenda item by majority vote” is to ignore the elephant in the room. And that elephant is not just in the room; it’s trampling over the very principles of democracy and the rule of law.

Adam Kinzinger’s rebuttal to Hume couldn’t be more on point: “Of course he’s not a literal king because we won’t let him be.” This statement cuts to the heart of the matter. The protests aren’t about opposing a monarch in the literal sense but resisting a tide of corruption, abuse of power, and a flagrant disregard for democratic norms. Trump’s efforts to overturn a free election, the pardoning of criminals, and the bending of Congress to his will are not the actions of a humble servant of the people. They are the maneuvers of someone who aspires to the untouchable status of a king, if not in title, then in power and impunity.

The litany of accusations laid out by political scientist Norman Ornstein further dismantles Hume’s argument. From executive overreach, illegal acts, and weaponization of federal agencies for personal vendettas, to the grotesque spectacle of masked agents detaining citizens without due process, the picture painted is one of a democracy under siege from within.

Ornstein’s poignant observation that Hume has “replaced your integrity with fealty to the cult” strikes a chord. It’s a sad commentary on the state of political discourse when a once-respected conservative voice can no longer see—or perhaps chooses not to see—the forest for the trees. The issue here transcends partisan politics; it’s about the fundamental values that underpin American democracy.

The “No Kings” protests are a manifestation of a populace pushed to the brink, a collective outcry against the erosion of democratic safeguards and the concentration of power in the hands of the few. To dismiss these concerns as mere dissatisfaction with a leader who played the game of democracy is not just missing the point; it’s an act of intellectual dishonesty.

In the end, Brit Hume’s comments serve as a stark reminder of the deep divisions and the dangerous game of selective interpretation plaguing our political landscape. The real question we should be asking isn’t whether the protests are a reaction to a “king” in the literal sense but whether we are willing to stand by as the principles of our democracy are chipped away, piece by piece. The answer, resoundingly echoed in the streets and on social media, seems to be a defiant “no.”
