The Republican Party vs. Democracy: A Battle in Broad Daylight

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In what can only be described as a stunningly honest move, the Republican National Committee (RNC) has intervened in federal court to defend an anti-voting executive order introduced by Donald Trump. This order, which seeks to restrict access to mail-in ballots and tighten voter roll purges, has been widely criticized as a blatant voter suppression tactic. But the RNC isn’t hiding its intent behind the usual smokescreen of “election integrity.” No, this time, they said the quiet part out loud: more accessible voting hurts Republican candidates.

Let that sink in. One of America’s two major political parties is now on record in court asserting that it is disadvantaged when more citizens can vote. That isn’t a political strategy. That’s an admission of systemic decay. It’s a confession that the GOP’s grip on power can only be maintained by limiting participation, not by persuading the electorate.

The Executive Order: Voter Suppression Repackaged

Trump’s executive order purports to protect the sanctity of elections by imposing measures like:

  • Requiring proof of citizenship to register to vote,

  • Limiting acceptance of mail-in ballots to only those received by Election Day,

  • Mandating voter roll purges to eliminate “ineligible” voters.

Each of these has a veneer of legitimacy. Who wouldn’t want secure elections, after all? But scratch beneath the surface and it’s clear that these policies are designed not to prevent fraud, but to shrink the electorate — disproportionately affecting low-income, minority, disabled, and elderly voters who are more likely to vote by mail or lack immediate access to identity documents.

These aren’t new tactics. Voter ID laws, polling place closures, gerrymandering, and felony disenfranchisement have long been weapons in the GOP’s anti-democratic arsenal. What makes this moment uniquely disturbing is the RNC’s legal argument: that the very act of making voting easier is a partisan threat.

The Legal Argument: A Cynical Confession

The RNC’s court filing amounts to this: if more people vote, we lose. It’s not an exaggeration. Their legal defense claims that broader voter access harms Republican electoral prospects, which they argue justifies restrictive voting policies.

That should be the end of the conversation. A political party that cannot win free and fair elections has no business wielding power in a democracy. And yet, here we are, watching the GOP fight to roll back civil rights that were hard-won through generations of struggle, blood, and activism.

Imagine if a business said, “We can only succeed if fewer people can shop at our competitor.” We’d call that anti-competitive and unethical. When a political party makes the same case, we should call it what it is: authoritarianism.

The Myth of Voter Fraud

To justify these restrictions, Republicans continue to trot out the myth of widespread voter fraud. It is a myth that has been debunked time and time again. Even Trump’s own commission on voter fraud found no evidence of systemic issues. Courts have repeatedly rejected fraud claims as baseless.

But facts don’t matter in the GOP’s post-truth politics. What matters is narrative. What matters is power. The fraud narrative provides a convenient cover for policies that are designed to suppress votes.

The Real Fraud: A Rigged System

If anything is fraudulent, it’s the GOP’s rigged game. Gerrymandered districts that dilute Black and Latino votes. Laws that reduce polling places in urban neighborhoods. Purges that remove legitimate voters. Intimidation tactics aimed at discouraging participation.

And now, a full-throated legal argument that democracy itself is the enemy.

This is not about preventing fraud. This is about preventing democracy from functioning. The Republican Party is telling us, without shame, that they can only win when the game is rigged. That’s not a party. That’s an occupying force.

The Broader Implications

This case should alarm everyone who believes in democratic norms. Because once a party justifies suppressing votes to gain power, what’s to stop them from suppressing speech, press, or protest? The rot spreads. And it’s spreading quickly.

The implications are not limited to one election cycle. This is about the long-term survival of representative democracy in America. If the GOP succeeds, the precedent will be that votes are optional, power is permanent, and elections are performative.

What Comes Next

This lawsuit is a clarifying moment. It’s an unmasking of motives and methods. The courts may yet uphold voting rights. But the American public must wake up to the threat posed by a political movement that openly fears its own citizens.

The solution is as obvious as it is urgent:

  • Pass national voting rights protections like the John Lewis Voting Rights Act.

  • End gerrymandering through independent commissions.

  • Expand access to mail-in voting, early voting, and same-day registration.

  • Confront the GOP’s anti-democratic agenda head-on, in the courts, in Congress, and at the ballot box.

Conclusion

The RNC’s court filing is not just a legal document. It is a manifesto of minority rule. It declares that a functioning democracy is a threat to Republican power. And in doing so, it makes one thing perfectly clear:

The Republican Party isn’t trying to win elections anymore. It’s trying to end them.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
