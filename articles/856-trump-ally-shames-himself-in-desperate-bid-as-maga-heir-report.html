Trump ally shames himself in desperate bid as MAGA heir: report

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

Rubio’s Performance Is the Point

The Setup

The reporting here is simple enough: Marco Rubio is using a White House UFC rollout to audition for a post-Trump future, even as the office he holds sits inside a foreign-policy collapse of his own making. The hook is not the spectacle itself. It is the willingness of a senior official to spend political capital on cosplay while the machinery under him keeps producing damage.

The Power He Actually Holds

Rubio is not some ornamental bystander. He is the secretary of state, and, as the source notes, he also served as acting administrator of USAID. That means he sits inside the institutional chain for the very decisions and failures now defining Trump’s second term abroad. The article is right to say he probably did not personally author every worst move. But “not the man making the decision” is not innocence. It is the defense of a man who stayed in position while those decisions happened directly under his watch.

Clown Work as Loyalty

Rubio’s UFC theatrics are not accidental embarrassment. They are political labor. The comparison of a combat-sports partnership to the moon landing is not diplomacy; it is abasement dressed up as patriotism. Rubio is signaling that he will flatten himself into whatever shape Trump’s politics require, because loyalty now matters more than competence, and performance matters more than authority.

The Real Misdirection

The article correctly resists the lazy temptation to treat Rubio as the lone culprit. The deeper problem is the system that lets a cabinet secretary become a props department for a president who profits from spectacle. The UFC partnership itself is not merely silly branding. It carries the stench of conflict of interest, especially with Trump’s reported stock purchase in UFC’s parent company. That is the real structure here: power arranged so that personal enrichment, public office, and partisan theater blur into one another while responsibility gets diffused downward.

Institutional Cowardice

Rubio was once marketed as the “grown-up in the room.” That was always a convenient fiction, and the fiction is now spent. What remains is the familiar Washington trick of laundering degradation through a respectable résumé. The Senate’s 99-0 confirmation vote did not produce competence; it produced a permission slip for collapse. He was installed as a heavyweight and has chosen to behave like a court jester because it protects his ambitions and flatters the boss.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics of the soft variety works: senior officials keep their titles, surrender their seriousness, and then ask to be taken seriously later. The joke is not that Rubio looks foolish. The joke is that a man charged with America’s diplomacy can help preside over its deterioration, pose for UFC marketing, and still be treated as a plausible future leader. That is not a personal failure alone. It is a political system that rewards submission, converts humiliation into résumé value, and treats national office as a stage for loyalty rituals rather than governance.
