Trump's 'credibility is shot' as he gets cold shoulder from massive industry execs: report

Trump Is Threatening the Market Because He Cannot Control It

The Setup

Trump tried to bully oil companies into lowering gas prices after market conditions pushed costs up during the Iran war. The companies ignored him, crude only dipped slightly, gas prices stayed flat, and Politico’s reporting makes the basic fact unavoidable: the president’s words are not moving the machinery that actually sets prices.

Power Sits Elsewhere

The central power in this story is not the Oval Office. It is the oil industry, the market, and the geopolitical disruption Trump inherited and helped magnify through his own war messaging. He can complain about profits all he wants; he cannot command corporations to erase scarcity, and he cannot speak inflation away once voters have already felt it at the pump.

A Credibility Problem, Not a Messaging Problem

The source is blunt that Trump has damaged his own credibility by flip-flopping on the Iran war and promising a deal that never appears. That matters because his economic theatrics depend on one thing: the belief that he can still impose order. When his own adviser says he has only himself to blame, that is not a hostile leak. It is an admission that this is a self-inflicted collapse of authority.

The Real Misdirection

Trump is trying to turn an outcome of war, supply shock, and corporate pricing into a performance issue, as if louder threats can substitute for policy. That is the usual con: redirect anger toward visible villains while obscuring the structure producing the pain. Oil companies are not innocent, but the larger deception is pretending the president is a captive critic rather than a participant in the conditions driving the crisis.

Republicans Are Eating Their Own Cost-of-Living Story

The political damage here falls squarely on Republicans, not because the system is fair, but because the party has fused itself to Trump’s volatility and incompetence. Three months before the midterms, voters are being asked to believe that the same crew that cannot stabilize a war, cannot control prices, and cannot project competence deserves more power. That is not a messaging challenge. It is a governing failure.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics degrades into farce: spectacle in place of leverage, blame in place of accountability, and empty threats in place of state capacity. Trump does not look strong here. He looks like a man shouting at markets that no longer pretend to listen. That is not a glitch in the system. It is the system showing exactly who it serves, and who it ignores.
