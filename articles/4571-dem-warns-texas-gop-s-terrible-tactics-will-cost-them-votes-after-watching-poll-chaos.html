Dem warns Texas GOP's 'terrible tactics' will cost them votes after watching poll chaos

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20264 min read

Democratic U.S. Senate candidate for Texas, U.S. Representative Jasmine Crockett takes the stage to speak during the primary election night party in Dallas, Texas, U.S., March 3, 2026. REUTERS/Richard Rodriguez

WASHINGTON — Following her high-stakes Senate primary loss, Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) delivered a defiant statement, doubling down on allegations of voter suppression while pointedly refusing to fade into the political background.

Speaking to Raw Story on Wednesday in the immediate aftermath of her loss to state Rep. James Talarico, Crockett pivoted away from her primary setback to focus fire on Republicans, claiming their tactics were actively undermining Democratic chances in the general election.

“Listen, I think that it was an exciting election. I think it’s clear that the trends are that we can win in November, and so we just got to keep the energy up and make sure the terrible tactics of the Republicans don’t cost us votes and disenfranchise voters as we saw in Dallas County,” she said.

Crockett added: “Obviously, I know you’re very well aware of everything that took place. I don’t know where the party is going with the litigation, because they were the ones who had been pending litigation, but this is something that needs to be fixed.”

When asked what comes next for her political future, Crockett replied, “I am going to continue to serve.”

Dallas and Williamson County Republican Parties opted not to hold joint primaries, which, under Texas law, forced both parties to abandon countywide vote centers on Election Day and return to assigned precinct polling places.

Many Dallas voters, accustomed to countywide centers since 2019, showed up at their usual or convenient locations and were turned away or redirected, leading to mass confusion, long lines, and reports that large shares of voters initially went to the wrong place. Dallas County Democrats have said this amounted to voter suppression and sought emergency relief to keep polls open longer for their primary.

A Dallas judge ordered Democratic polling places in the county to stay open two extra hours, until 9 p.m., with ballots cast after 7 p.m. to be counted as provisional. The state Supreme Court quickly issued a stay, instructing that ballots cast by voters not in line by 7 p.m. be separated while it decides whether those late votes are valid. The saga threw the final tally into chaos.

Our Analysis:

Voter Suppression: A Persistent Shadow Over Democracy

In an era where the integrity and accessibility of the electoral process should be beyond reproach, the recent Senate primary in Texas casts a long, dark shadow of doubt. Rep. Jasmine Crockett’s loss is not just a tale of political defeat but a stark illustration of the machinations that threaten the very core of American democracy: voter suppression.

Following her defeat, Crockett’s refusal to quietly disappear from the political scene is commendable. Her allegations of voter suppression are not to be taken lightly, especially given the context in which they arise. The decision by the Dallas and Williamson County Republican Parties to eschew joint primaries, thus reverting to assigned precinct polling places, is a move that reeks of strategic disenfranchisement. This shift, which upended the voting process for countless individuals accustomed to the convenience of countywide vote centers since 2019, is a stark illustration of the lengths to which some entities will go to hinder voter turnout.

The chaos that ensued—voters turned away or redirected, the resultant long lines, and the mass confusion—is a scenario that should alarm any observer of democratic processes. It’s not merely an inconvenience; it’s a calculated obstruction. The Dallas County Democrats’ characterization of these events as voter suppression is not hyperbolic; it’s a factual statement of the barriers erected to dilute the power of the vote.

The legal battle that unfolded, with a Dallas judge extending polling hours only for the state Supreme Court to intervene, adds another layer of complexity and concern. The instruction to separate ballots cast by voters not in line by 7 p.m. as the court decides their validity is a microcosm of the broader battle for voting rights in America. It demonstrates a judicial system entangled in the political machinations of voter suppression, where the right to vote is caught in the crossfire of legal skirmishes.

Crockett’s forward stance, focusing on the Republican tactics that threaten to disenfranchise voters, is a narrative that needs more airtime. Her determination to continue serving despite her primary loss is not just a personal decision but a political stance against the systemic efforts to undermine democratic processes.

The unfolding situation in Texas is a glaring reminder of the fragility of democracy and the constant vigilance required to protect it from those who seek to manipulate its mechanisms for partisan advantage. Voter suppression, in any form, is an affront to the democratic ideals that America purports to champion. The saga of Crockett’s primary loss is not just a story of electoral defeat; it’s a cautionary tale of the pernicious effects of undermining the electoral process.

In a country that prides itself on being a beacon of democracy, the events in Dallas County serve as a stark reminder that the battle for fair and free elections is far from over. It’s a battle that requires not just the vigilance of politicians like Crockett but the active engagement of every American who believes in the sanctity of the vote.
