'People are terrified': Trump insiders using 'crazy' MAGA influencer to knife each other

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Loomer Factor: A Symptom of Systemic Dysfunction

Far-right activist Laura Loomer has become such a close confidant of President Donald Trump and has become so influential over his hirings and firings that White House insiders are covertly recruiting her to go after each other and ruin their rivals' chances of promotions, The Free Press reported on Thursday.

In recent months, Loomer, an avowed conspiracy theorist and 9/11 truther who describes herself as a "proud Islamophobe" and a white nationalist sympathizer, has personally been responsible, through her social media rants, for some of Trump's nominees being ousted or withdrawn, with a key example being a surgeon general pick she proclaimed had fabricated medical credentials and was too pro-COVID vaccine.

The latest case appears to be Dr. Vinay Prasad, who in May was named director of the Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research, an FDA office responsible for regulating gene therapy and vaccines. Per Gabe Kaminsky, Loomer "said in an article on her website that he was a 'lifelong progressive liberal,' citing Prasad’s support in 2020 for Bernie Sanders, abortion, and gay marriage. And she brought attention to a portion of a 2021 podcast episode in which she claimed that Prasad said he stabbed a Trump voodoo doll to curse him. (Prasad was making a joke about who to blame for the pandemic.)"

Shortly after this, Prasad left the administration, with a Health and Human Services spokesperson saying he didn't want to be a "distraction."

But it goes deeper than Loomer sabotaging careers from the outside, said the report: she's actually being weaponized by certain Trump administration officials to go after others.

“People here are terrified of her. Every time she gets brought up, you can feel the air in the room drop by like 15 degrees. A lot of people are worried about being ‘Loomered,’” said one White House official. Another said, “She talks to the president about stuff, and she knows how to characterize things and successfully move the needle on issues. I think she is f------ crazy, but she is also influential.”

Yet another "claimed it’s not uncommon for current officials to contact Loomer for her to go after their rivals in the government," creating an "annoyance" for everyone.

All of this comes even though some Trump allies in Congress raise alarms about Loomer having so much influence; as far back as 2023, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) was adamant the president shouldn't hire Loomer for his campaign.

Our Analysis:

The Loomer Factor: A Symptom of Systemic Dysfunction

In the latest revelation from the political circus that has become synonymous with the Trump administration, the rise of far-right activist Laura Loomer as a shadowy figure of influence paints a troubling picture of the inner workings of the White House. The report from The Free Press that details Loomer's ascendancy to the role of an unofficial kingmaker—or more aptly, kingbreaker—within the Trump administration is not just a story about one individual's influence. It's a glaring symptom of a much deeper malaise affecting the political and ethical fabric of the governing body.

Loomer's Controversial Persona and Influence

Laura Loomer, known for her avowed conspiracy theories, her self-proclaimed status as a "proud Islamophobe," and sympathies toward white nationalism, is not the typical influencer one might expect to wield power in the hallowed halls of the White House. Yet, according to insiders, her word has become gospel to some, guiding the hiring and firing decisions of President Donald Trump himself. This is not just troubling; it's outright dangerous.

The case of Dr. Vinay Prasad serves as a stark example. A respected professional appointed as the director of the Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research at the FDA, Prasad's career was derailed by Loomer's baseless allegations, including the absurd claim of cursing Trump with a voodoo doll—a joke taken entirely out of context. The fact that such frivolous, unverified accusations can influence career-defining decisions at the highest levels of government is a mockery of due process and rational decision-making.

Weaponization of Influence

Even more alarming is the revelation that Loomer is being "weaponized" by officials within the Trump administration to settle personal scores. The very idea that government officials are engaging in this sort of backdoor infighting, leveraging a far-right activist's social media clout to "Loomer" their rivals, is a testament to the toxic, cutthroat culture that seems to have taken root. This isn't just petty office politics; it's a dangerous game that threatens to undermine the integrity and functionality of the administration.

A Climate of Fear

The climate of fear Loomer instills, as described by White House officials, is indicative of a broader issue. When officials are more concerned about avoiding being "Loomered" than about making sound, ethical decisions, the governance process is fundamentally flawed. This environment not only stifles dissent and critical debate but also encourages a culture of sycophancy and opportunism.

The Bigger Picture

The real issue at hand goes beyond Loomer's individual actions or her relationship with Trump. It's about what her rise to influence signifies—a political ecosystem where sensationalism, fear-mongering, and personal vendettas can dictate the course of national policy and careers. It's a system where meritocracy takes a backseat to loyalty and where critical voices are silenced by the threat of public vilification.

Even voices within the Republican Party, like Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, have expressed concern over Loomer's influence, signaling a recognition of the problem. Yet, the continued reliance on such figures for political maneuvering reflects a willful disregard for ethical governance.

Conclusion

The saga of Laura Loomer's influence within the Trump administration is not just a story of one individual's rise to power. It's a cautionary tale of what happens when the lines between sound governance and political theatre are blurred beyond recognition. As we navigate these revelations, the question becomes not just about how to mitigate Loomer's influence, but how to address the systemic errors that allowed her to gain such a foothold in the first place.
