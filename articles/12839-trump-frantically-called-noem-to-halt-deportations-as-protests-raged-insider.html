Trump frantically called Noem to halt deportations as protests raged: insider

A panicked Donald Trump phoned Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem to slam the brakes on his coast-to-coast immigration raids after furious street protests erupted across the country, according to a new report.

The bombshell revelation from Reuters comes after White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller set an aggressive quota of 3,000 immigration arrests per day in late May, following through on Trump's campaign promise to carry out what he called the "largest domestic deportation operation" in U.S. history.

But the massive crackdown quickly backfired, sparking nationwide protests in June that started in Los Angeles, where Trump dispatched 4,000 National Guard troops that he said were needed to quell demonstrations—over the fierce objections of California Governor Gavin Newsom.

As his poll numbers plummeted and political fallout mounted, Trump frantically called Noem to put a pause on his signature policy.

"He said: 'We're going to do this targeted,'" one former official who heard the mid-June call told Reuters, describing Trump's growing panic that indiscriminate arrests of farmhands and hotel maids were becoming a political nightmare.

The episode exposed a crack in the alliance between Trump and Miller, with Immigration and Customs Enforcement quietly freezing operations at farms, restaurants, and meat-packing plants within hours of Trump's desperate call, the report stated.

Senior ICE official Tatum King immediately told field offices to ease up on the raids, according to Reuters, but the stand-down lasted only days before Miller and Noem rescinded the order.

Trump's administration has doubled overall arrests, slapped travel bans on 19 countries, and earmarked a staggering $170 billion for immigration enforcement under the so-called "One Big Beautiful Bill" passed in July.

But the crackdown is now facing serious pushback from federal courts, which have moved to stop multiple attempted deportations.

A White House official told Reuters there was "no daylight between Miller and Trump."

The official also claimed that "the initial ICE directive pausing raids had not been authorized by top administration leaders."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fiasco: Trump’s Immigration Crackdown Unravels

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a political thriller, the recent Reuters report sheds light on the chaotic unraveling of President Donald Trump’s aggressive immigration crackdown. This debacle, fueled by the fervent ideologies of Trump and his Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, has exposed the deep fissures within an administration that prides itself on a tough stance against immigration. However, the reality of their actions—marked by panic, political fallout, and a stark reversal of policy—reveals a narrative of disarray and desperation.

The Ill-Conceived Plan

At the heart of this turmoil was an ambitious and inhumane quota set by Stephen Miller: 3,000 immigration arrests per day. This directive was not just a fulfillment of Trump’s campaign promise; it was an attempt to orchestrate what he proudly termed the "largest domestic deportation operation" in U.S. history. Yet, such aggressive posturing quickly backfired. The massive crackdown did not go unnoticed or unchallenged by the public. Furious street protests erupted across the nation, beginning in Los Angeles and rapidly spreading, as communities pushed back against the administration's draconian measures.

The Fallout

As the protests gained momentum, Trump’s bravado faltered. His decision to dispatch 4,000 National Guard troops to Los Angeles, supposedly to quell demonstrations, faced fierce objections from California Governor Gavin Newsom. This militaristic response to civic unrest did little but fan the flames of public dissent, leading to a precipitous drop in Trump’s poll numbers and mounting political fallout.

In a moment of panic, Trump, realizing the indiscriminate nature of the arrests—targeting not only the so-called "criminals" but also farmhands and hotel maids—made a desperate call to Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem to slam the brakes on the operation. This call, described by a former official, underscores the chaos and lack of foresight that characterized the administration's approach to immigration enforcement.

The Brief Reprieve and Its End

Following Trump’s frantic directive, Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) momentarily froze operations in sectors that directly impact the American economy—farms, restaurants, and meat-packing plants. This stand-down, however, was short-lived. Senior ICE official Tatum King’s instruction to ease up on the raids was quickly rescinded, as Miller and Noem aligned to continue their stringent policies.

The Bigger Picture

Trump’s administration, in its relentless pursuit of stringent immigration control, has not only doubled overall arrests but also imposed travel bans on 19 countries and earmarked a staggering $170 billion for immigration enforcement under the ominously named "One Big Beautiful Bill." These actions have not gone unchecked. Federal courts have begun to push back, moving to stop multiple attempted deportations and challenging the administration's aggressive policies.

Conclusion: The Unraveling of a Hardline Stance

The revelation of Trump's panicked call and the subsequent policy flip-flop exposes the administration's immigration crackdown for what it truly is—a political nightmare born out of a toxic blend of ambition and xenophobia, poorly executed and even more poorly retracted. The claim by a White House official that there was "no daylight between Miller and Trump" and that the initial pause on raids was unauthorized does little to salvage the credibility of an administration mired in its own chaos.

As the nation watches this drama unfold, it becomes increasingly clear that the administration's hardline stance on immigration is not only unsustainable but also deeply un-American. The fabric of the country, woven from the threads of diversity and the strength of its immigrant communities, cannot be torn asunder by the whims of a panicked presidency. In the end, the true measure of a nation’s greatness lies not in its ability to exclude and deport, but in its capacity to embrace and integrate.
