U.S. President Donald Trump reacts while hosting a Kennedy Center Board dinner at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 19, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump had a sympathetic crowd laughing as he uttered a vulgar take on his election at a Kennedy Center board dinner Monday.

Trump, who purged the board at the prestigious performing arts center in D.C. in February and made himself chairman, was there for the unveiling of the coming season’s shows.

But during his speech, he celebrated his own part in securing the 2028 Olympics and the 2026 soccer World Cup for the USA, HuffPost reported.

His role would have been forgotten if Joe Biden hadn’t stolen the 2020 election from him, he said — repeating his often-used lies — as he would already be out of office.

“And then they rigged the election, and then I said, ‘You know what I’ll do? I’ll run again and I’ll shove it up their a..,’” he said.

The audience roared with laughter.

“So if they would’ve left us alone, and wouldn’t have cheated on the election, and wouldn’t have rigged it, I would’ve been retired right now,” he went on.

“I would’ve been happily doing something else, and instead they have me for four more years, can you believe that?”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Unabashed Vulgarity and Unfounded Claims at Kennedy Center Board Dinner

In a spectacle that can only be described as a blend of the absurd and the distressing, President Donald Trump regaled a crowd with a vulgar take on his election loss during a dinner at the Kennedy Center. This event, far from being a dignified affair befitting the venue, was turned into a platform for Trump's baseless allegations and self-aggrandizement.

Self-Appointed Chairman and Self-Celebrated Hero

Trump, in a move that reeks of nepotism and authoritarian overreach, purged the Kennedy Center board in February, installing himself as chairman. His actions are a stark illustration of his tendency to place personal interests above cultural or national considerations. At an event intended to unveil the coming season’s shows, Trump instead chose to highlight his own supposed contributions to securing the 2028 Olympics and the 2026 soccer World Cup for the USA—a claim that bears scrutiny.

Repeating Unfounded Allegations

In his speech, Trump reiterated the often-used lies about the 2020 election being stolen from him. This claim, despite being debunked by numerous courts, recounts, and audits, continues to be a dangerous narrative that undermines the democratic process. By saying, "And then they rigged the election, and then I said, ‘You know what I’ll do? I’ll run again and I’ll shove it up their a..,’" Trump not only displayed a shocking lack of decorum but also a deliberate intent to sow discord and division.

The Audience's Reaction

The audience's reaction, roaring with laughter at Trump's vulgarities, is a concerning reflection of how normalized his behavior has become among his supporters. It's a stark reminder of the erosion of public discourse and the lowering of standards for presidential conduct.

The Irony of Trump's Lament

Trump's lament that he would have been retired and "happily doing something else" if not for the alleged election rigging is rich with irony. It ignores the fact that his continued insistence on these baseless claims is a choice—a choice to prioritize personal grievances over the country's well-being. His statement, "So if they would’ve left us alone, and wouldn’t have cheated on the election, and wouldn’t have rigged it, I would’ve been retired right now," sidesteps the reality of the 2020 election's outcome and his role in perpetuating falsehoods.

Conclusion

Trump's speech at the Kennedy Center board dinner was not just a display of vulgarity and unfounded claims; it was a manifestation of his ongoing assault on democratic norms and values. The laughter and applause that met his remarks are a sobering reminder of the divisions he continues to stoke. As observers and as citizens, we must remain vigilant against such attempts to rewrite history and erode the foundations of our democracy. The spectacle at the Kennedy Center is not just an isolated incident—it's a symptom of a much larger systemic error that threatens the very core of our democratic society.
