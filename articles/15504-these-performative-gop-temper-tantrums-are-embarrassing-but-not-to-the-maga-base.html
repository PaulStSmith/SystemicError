Discussing the history of conservatism in the United States, veteran Washington Post columnist George Will used the word "bookish" to describe Republicans of the past. And Will, who is very much a Never Trump conservative, meant that in a good way.

Will was applauding those conservatives of yesterday for, as he saw it, their detailed focus on policy. And he is no fan of the performative theatrics of the MAGA movement.

Salon's Amanda Marcotte cites three recent examples of such theatrics in an article published on April 28, calling out "performative temper tantrums" by Rep. Nancy Mace (R-Georgia), Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Trump White House adviser Stephen Miller.

"If there were an Oscar for the category 'hard to watch,'" Marcotte argues, "I'd have to nominate the video of Rep. Nancy Mace, R-S.C., barking expletives at a constituent after he asked her if she would have a town hall soon. It's produced in a beauty supply store instead of a movie studio, but in a brief minute and 42 seconds, the video finds its place in the canon of horror films shot from the villain's perspective…. Most adults who act like Mace in public immediately wish to disappear off the face of the Earth in shame. But not our Nancy! No, she's the one who posted this video online, proud of her emotional incontinence."

Marcotte adds, "She even offered a homophobic gay panic defense, by describing the man as 'wearing daisy dukes, at a makeup store'….. To people outside the MAGA bubble, it's a baffling choice. She's not even a fun villain."

Marcotte laments that "what most adults find embarrassing, the MAGA base will eat right up."

"Despite this effort, Mace didn't even come close to nabbing last week's gold star for the most histrionic MAGA performance," Marcotte observes. "She was outdone by Stephen Miller, whose usual register on TV is 'verge of a nervous breakdown,' but got so shrill on Fox News Tuesday that Lauren Tousignant at Jezebel worried she'd soon have to 'look at Stephen Miller's face as he pops a dozen blood vessels as his brain explodes'…. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth turned in two performances that would cause Al Pacino to tell him to settle down."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: The MAGA Movement's Theatrical Antics

In a recent exposé, Amanda Marcotte of Salon delves into the performative temper tantrums that seem to be the hallmark of certain MAGA-aligned political figures. The analysis provided by Marcotte is not only a scathing critique of these antics but also a laser-focused observation on how they serve to distract from the lack of substantive policy discussion. Let's dissect these instances with the same fierce clarity and biting commentary they deserve.

Theatrics over Substance

George Will's nostalgic recount of a "bookish" Republican party underscores a stark contrast to today's MAGA movement, characterized by its preference for performance over policy. This shift towards performative theatrics is not just a departure from traditional conservatism but a strategy to engage a base more interested in spectacle than substance.

The Canon of Horror: Nancy Mace's Public Outburst

Rep. Nancy Mace's decision to post a video of herself hurling expletives at a constituent is emblematic of this new performative trend. The video, described by Marcotte with a vivid horror film analogy, is not just appalling for its content but also for Mace's proud display of what most would consider a moment of deep embarrassment. This incident, coupled with a homophobic defense, shows a troubling descent into incivility, seemingly applauded by her base.

Outdoing Each Other in Histrionics

Stephen Miller and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's contributions to this circus of histrionics further demonstrate the MAGA movement's drift towards emotional incontinence over reasoned debate. Miller's near apoplectic appearance on Fox News and Hegseth's voice-cracking rant during the White House Easter egg roll are not accidental slips but calculated performances. These outbursts are not just for show; they serve to obfuscate the lack of coherent policy discussion.

The Pragmatic Purpose Behind the Pandemonium

Marcotte astutely points out that all this "yelling and bellyaching" is not without its purpose. It is a deliberate distraction from the substance—or the lack thereof—of their arguments. The louder and more outrageous the performance, the less attention is paid to the actual policies being proposed or enacted. This tactic, while effective in galvanizing a certain base, is detrimental to the broader political discourse.

The Baffling Choice of Villainy

The choice to embrace these theatrics, as Marcotte notes, is baffling to those outside the MAGA bubble. It represents a troubling evolution of political engagement where emotional incontinence is celebrated over intellectual discourse and policy analysis. This shift not only impoverishes the political landscape but also elevates figures more suited for reality television than the halls of governance.

Conclusion: The Distracting Art of Performative Politics

In conclusion, the incidents highlighted by Marcotte are not mere aberrations but symptomatic of a larger trend within the MAGA movement—a trend that prioritizes spectacle over substance, theatrics over thoughtful discussion. This strategy, while perhaps politically expedient in the short term, undermines the very fabric of democratic dialogue and policy-making. As observers and participants in this democratic process, it is incumbent upon us to demand more from our political figures than mere performance. The stakes are too high, and the issues too important, for anything less.
