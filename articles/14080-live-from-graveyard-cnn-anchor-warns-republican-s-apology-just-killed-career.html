'Live from graveyard': CNN anchor warns Republican's apology just killed career

CNN Anchor Audie Cornish on Sen Joni Ernst comments (CNN Screenshot) 2025-06-02

CNN anchor Audie Cornish slammed Senator Joni Ernst (R-IA) Monday morning, calling a sarcastic apology the lawmaker issued to voters over the weekend a “head-shaking” moment.

Cornish began the segment by replaying a clip from last week of Ernst defending cuts to Medicaid.

The clip showed Ernst arguing with voters at a town hall about Medicaid fraud. One member of the crowd yelled about people affected by Medicaid cuts included in a budget bill the GOP is currently trying to pass. “They are all going to die," the crowd member yelled.

To which Ernst replied, “We all are going to die.”

The statement caused outrage and Ernst issued an apology for the interaction. “I made an incorrect assumption that everyone in the auditorium understood that yes, we are all going to perish from this earth,” Ernst said in the video, “So, I apologize, and I'm really, really glad that i did not have to bring up the subject of the tooth fairy as well.”

Cornish chimed in, saying, “I think that was live from a graveyard just to underscore the point. Sarcasm is my love language, but it feels like this is not the moment.”

The apology was delivered while Ernst was actually in a graveyard — which Cornish suggested was symbolic.

The anchor turned to her guests, seemingly at a loss for words, “Like, it's kind of a — you guys should – just like – head shaking.” The panel burst into laughter before Cornish asked, “What's head shaking about this in this moment for this bill?”

“So two things,” Spectrum News NY1 Washington Correspondent Kevin Frey said. “One, this seems to be maybe symptomatic of kind of the Trump iteration of the Republican Party that you never apologize for anything. That's clearly what was going on there.”

He added, “Two, this kind of only underscores what clearly is a reality of this bill that the Republican Party is just not quite sure of how to navigate in terms of a messaging standpoint, because we know Democrats are eager to exploit this Medicaid aspect of this.”

Frey added, “We know Democrats spoke over the weekend, Schumer and Jeffries on Sunday. We know that the House Democrats are going to go over to the Senate this week to kind of show ways to exploit this bill. Medicaid is going to be front and center on that.”

Our Analysis:

The Graveyard Apology: A Symptom of Political Tone-Deafness

In what can only be described as a bizarre and macabre twist to political apologies, Senator Joni Ernst (R-IA) managed to amplify her initial insensitivity with a sarcastic mea culpa delivered, of all places, from a graveyard. CNN anchor Audie Cornish's incredulity mirrors our own, as we unpack the layers of this head-shaking moment in contemporary political discourse.

Ernst's Initial Faux Pas

The controversy began when Ernst, in a moment of what can only be assumed was intended to be levity, responded to legitimate concerns about the life-and-death implications of Medicaid cuts with a flippant, “We all are going to die.” This remark, understandably, sparked outrage. It's one thing to engage in policy debate; it's another entirely to dismiss constituents' fears about their very survival with cavalier indifference.

The Apology... If We Can Call It That

Ernst's "apology" was as tone-deaf as her original comment, if not more so. The choice of a graveyard as the setting for this apology is so on the nose, it veers into the realm of parody. Yet, this was no Saturday Night Live sketch; this was a sitting U.S. senator addressing a deeply contentious issue. Ernst's attempt to lighten the mood with a reference to the tooth fairy only adds insult to injury, trivializing the genuine concerns of those who rely on Medicaid for their healthcare needs.

The Bigger Picture: GOP Messaging Woes

Kevin Frey's analysis hits the nail on the head. This incident is symptomatic of a broader issue within the Republican Party — an apparent allergy to genuine apology or admission of fault, possibly inherited from the Trump playbook. The inability to navigate the Medicaid cuts messaging is telling. It exposes not just a communication failure but a fundamental misunderstanding of or disregard for the concerns of the most vulnerable populations.

As Democrats rally to highlight the potential impacts of these cuts, the GOP's floundering response serves to further alienate those directly affected by such legislative actions. The optics of a senator joking about death in a graveyard, while discussing policies that could have life-or-death consequences, are as stark as they are disturbing.

Conclusion: A Moment of Reflection Required

The graveyard apology serves as a grim metaphor for the current state of political discourse. It's a moment that should prompt serious reflection within the GOP about its approach to policy-making and public engagement. Sarcasm and belittlement are poor substitutes for empathy and understanding in political leadership.

Ernst's comments and subsequent apology underscore a critical disconnect between some legislators and the realities faced by their constituents. In the end, the graveyard setting was perhaps an unwittingly apt choice, symbolizing the political and moral burial of what should have been a moment of earnest apology and reconnection with voters.
