'Rattled' Trump taking new approach to keep his base from turning on him: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Tenuous Tightrope: Evading Epstein Enquiries

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump is finding out the hard way that anything he says about teen sex abuser Jeffrey Epstein and the notorious files the DOJ is sitting on, which are at the center of a brutal fight in Congress, is not helping his case with his base so he is being forced to change his normal strategy as he tries to rally them to his defense.

According to a report from MS NOW’s Zeeshan Aleem, the president is now visibly “rattled” that he can’t make the controversy go away, so in recent days he has been keeping his distance from reporters because his off-the-cuff answers have been blowing up in his face.

As Aleem wrote, “in the past few days, Trump has been uncharacteristically reticent around journalists,” adding that The Atlantic’s Jonathan Lemire agreed, writing, “White House held a signing ceremony for the president to officially end the longest federal-government shutdown in history, the reporters present were quickly whisked out of the Oval Office.”

Pointing out that Trump surprisingly held no public events on Friday where shouting reporters would be present, Aleem suggested the president doesn’t want to be put on the spot with unanticipated questions, so he is taking his fight to Truth Social where he has more control.

“Trump may well resume taking reporters’ questions with his usual bravado in the coming days, but it’s telling that he appears hesitant to do his usual extemporaneous riffing,” the MS NOW reporter observed before adding, “The reason is simple: Questions surrounding Trump’s relationship with Epstein and Epstein’s sexual offenses is one of the few issues over which segments of Trump’s base openly question him.”

As MS NOW contributor Phillip Bump pointed out, the president has good reason to want to avoid enraging his base further.

Bump wrote, “In polling conducted in October by Ipsos for Reuters, 9 in 10 Republicans expressed approval of Trump’s presidency. But only 4 in 10 indicated that they approved of how he was handling the Epstein files. Quinnipiac University polling conducted this summer found a similar split: 84% of Republicans approved of Trump’s presidency, but only 44% approved of how he was handling Epstein.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tenuous Tightrope: Evading Epstein Enquiries

Donald Trump’s dalliance with disaster dances on as he desperately dodges the damning discourse surrounding his dubious dealings—or rather, his verbal volleys—regarding teen sex abuser Jeffrey Epstein. The tendrils of this controversy are tightly twined around the files the Department of Justice (DOJ) is ominously sitting on, files that find themselves fiercely fought over in Congress. This isn’t just political theatre; it’s a spectacle of seismic proportions that even Trump’s base, typically as unwavering as the walls of Jericho before the trumpets blew, is scrutinizing with increasing discomfort.

Trump Retreats into the Shadows

In an uncharacteristic move that screams volumes louder than his usual bravado-laden bellowing, Trump is retreating from the limelight. Zeeshan Aleem of MS NOW paints a picture of a president who, for once, finds the ground under his feet quaking with the uncertainty of public opinion. It appears the Teflon is wearing thin, the non-stick surface scarred from too many self-inflicted wounds.

Aleem’s observation, supported by The Atlantic’s Jonathan Lemire, underscores a strategic withdrawal from the battlefield of public opinion. Trump’s silence—his absence from the usual arenas where he parries with the press—is telling. His decision to instead wield Truth Social as his weapon of choice, a platform over which he exercises more control, is a testament to his current vulnerability. The man known for his ‘shoot from the hip’ approach to communication is now holstering his weapon, perhaps recognizing that every misfire chips away at his fortress.

A Base Betrayed?

The crux of this conundrum isn’t just Trump’s reticence; it’s the reason behind it. The association with Epstein and the swirling storm of sexual offenses is a bitter pill too large for even the most ardent supporter to swallow without a grimace. Phillip Bump of MS NOW brings the numbers into stark relief—while Trump’s presidency enjoys robust approval within his party, his handling of the Epstein saga is a glaring blemish. The polls from Ipsos/Reuters and Quinnipiac University reveal a chasm between general approval of his presidency and approval of his handling of the Epstein files.

This isn’t merely a matter of political inconvenience; it’s a potential Achilles’ heel. For a base that has rallied behind Trump through countless controversies, this issue cuts closer to the bone, challenging their loyalty and forcing them to confront uncomfortable truths about the man they support.

The Unanswered Question

What remains glaringly absent in this entire debacle is a clear, unequivocal repudiation or explanation from Trump regarding his relationship with Epstein and the infamous files. The silence is as deafening as it is damning. It raises more questions than it answers, and each day that passes without a coherent response from Trump is a day his credibility erodes further.

In the end, this episode is yet another chapter in the saga of a presidency and post-presidency mired in controversy. But it’s more than that—it’s a litmus test for Trump’s base, a moment of reckoning that may well redefine the contours of his support. Will they continue to stand by their man, or will this be the straw that breaks the camel’s back? Only time will tell, but as the saying goes, “You can ignore reality, but you cannot ignore the consequences of ignoring reality.” Trump’s reality, it seems, is catching up with him at last.
