Top MAGA fighter slams 'Epstein list' White House event in Trump snub

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump participate in the annual ceremony to pardon the national Thanksgiving turkeys, Tuesday, November 25, 2025, in the White House Rose Garden. (Official White House Photo by Daniel Torok)

Fighter and Donald Trump fan Sean Strickland is skipping the president’s upcoming White House Ultimate Fighting Championship event — because he doesn’t want to hang out with “the Epstein list.”

Strickland, once a vocal Trump supporter who voted for him and took public photos with him, has significantly distanced himself from Trump’s orbit in recent months. During a workout, when asked about participating in the White House event, Strickland responded: “If there was some kind of inclusion for fans I’d be more [inclined], but just to go hang out with the Epstein list? I’m good dog. I’m good, dude.”

The UFC event is planned as part of celebrations marking America’s 250th anniversary. Trump announced: “On Flag Day, we will have a one-of-a-kind UFC event here at the White House. It’ll be the greatest champion fighters in the world, all fighting that same night. The great Dana White is hosting, and it’s going to be something special.”

White House spokesperson Steven Cheung called it “one of the greatest and most historic sports events in history” and “a testament to his vision to celebrate America’s monumental 250th anniversary.”

Strickland’s principal objection concerns limited public access. He stated: “I’d be down, dude… if there was some kind of inclusion from fans.” He referenced a fighter who expressed discomfort competing “in front of billionaires,” noting the sentiment resonated with him.

But Strickland’s withdrawal reflects his evolving political stance. In August, he drew a line regarding MAGA’s ties to Israel and AIPAC-supported candidates, writing: “I can’t believe I’m saying this but I will vote for a Democrat over an aipac Republican candidate. Four years of a Democrat sounds easier than a politician owned by a foreign government.”

His position contrasts sharply with November 2024, when he posted a photo with Trump captioned “Absolute legend! Can’t wait to see the next four years.”

As a former middleweight champion and one of the country’s top male fighters, Strickland holds significant influence within the MMA community.

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Patriotism and Politics: Strickland’s Stand

In a move that has tongues wagging and keyboards clacking across the political and sporting landscapes, Sean Strickland, a fighter whose fists are as fierce as his political opinions, has taken a stand that many couldn’t have predicted. His refusal to attend a White House event, under the auspices of Donald Trump and in celebration of America’s 250th anniversary, isn’t just a footnote in sports news. It’s a blazing headline in the ongoing narrative of American politics, celebrity influence, and the shadowy implications of the “Epstein list.”

The Event in Question: A Celebration or a Controversy?

Donald Trump, a figure who polarizes opinion as easily as he breathes, has announced a UFC event that’s touted to be “one of the greatest and most historic sports events in history.” This event, hosted by Dana White, aims to mark America’s 250th anniversary with the kind of grandeur only Trump can envision. The White House’s promise of a spectacle featuring “the greatest champion fighters in the world” is overshadowed, however, by Strickland’s scathing refusal to attend due to the limited inclusion of the public and the distasteful association with the infamous “Epstein list.”

Strickland’s Bold Stance: A Reflection of Evolving Political Conscience

Strickland’s declination isn’t just about the lack of fan inclusion; it’s a manifest of his evolving political stance. Once a fervent supporter of Trump, Strickland’s recent actions and statements reflect a significant ideological shift. His discomfort with attending an event perceived to cater to the elite, coupled with his outspoken criticism of MAGA’s ties to Israel and AIPAC-supported candidates, highlights a growing disillusionment with the political entities and figures he once endorsed.

His statement, “I can’t believe I’m saying this but I will vote for a Democrat over an aipac Republican candidate,” is particularly telling. It underscores a broader, more profound disenchantment with the political landscape, where allegiance to foreign interests and the entanglements of wealth and power overshadow the democratic ideals of representation and public service.

The Implications: Beyond the Cage

Strickland’s stance is not just a personal or political declaration; it’s a microcosm of the larger dynamics at play within American society. His refusal to participate in an event that seemingly embodies exclusivity and elitism speaks volumes about the current state of American patriotism. What does it mean to celebrate America’s anniversary if the celebration itself is mired in controversy and exclusion?

Furthermore, Strickland’s shifting political allegiances reflect a broader trend of disenfranchisement and disillusionment among the electorate. The fighter’s journey from a Trump enthusiast to a critic underscores the complex, often fraught relationship between public figures and their political beliefs.

The Bottom Line

In essence, Sean Strickland’s decision to skip the White House UFC event is more than just a personal or political statement. It’s a glaring spotlight on the intricate dance of politics, public perception, and personal integrity. By choosing to stand aside, Strickland is not just distancing himself from “the Epstein list”; he’s questioning the very fabric of political allegiance and the nature of American patriotism.

As we dissect his actions and their implications, one thing becomes abundantly clear: In the ring of politics and public opinion, the fight is far from over.
