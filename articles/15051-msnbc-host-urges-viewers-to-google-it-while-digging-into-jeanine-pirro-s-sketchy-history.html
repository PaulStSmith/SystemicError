On Saturday morning's edition of MSNBC's "The Weekend," co-host Jonathan Capehart expressed both dismay and surprise that Donald Trump selected Fox News personality Jeanine Pirro to serve as Washington, D.C.'s interim U.S. attorney considering her history.

Speaking with his co-hosts, he claimed it doesn't take much effort to dig up Pirro's sketchy background when it comes to law enforcement and offered some tips to viewers.

"Talk about failing upward!" he exclaimed before continuing, "She's not qualified."

Our Analysis:

The Fox in the Henhouse: Trump's Latest Legal Farce

In a move that should shock no one but still manages to drop jaws, Donald Trump has once again proven that the depths of his cronyism know no bounds. The latest episode in this never-ending reality show of governance? The appointment of Fox News personality Jeanine Pirro as the interim U.S. attorney for Washington, D.C. Jonathan Capehart of MSNBC's "The Weekend" didn't mince words when he highlighted the absurdity and the blatant unfitness of Pirro for the role. And frankly, neither will we.

Failing Upward: The Jeanine Pirro Story

Capehart's exclamation, "Talk about failing upward!" couldn't be more apt. Pirro's career trajectory seems less about legal acumen and more about leveraging friendships and broadcasting bias. Qualifications? Apparently, a secondary concern when you're in the right circles. The idea that someone with Pirro's murky background in law enforcement could be considered for such a critical position is ludicrous. Yet, here we are, watching the bar for public service being buried underground.

A Sketchy Past Ignored

Pirro's history is not just a series of minor blips or easily dismissible mistakes. It's a treasure trove of controversies that would typically disqualify any sane person from holding public office, let alone a position of legal authority. Capehart's suggestion to "just Google 'Jeanine Pirro,' 'husband,' 'FBI,' 'Bernie Kerik,'" barely scratches the surface, but it's a good start. The scandal from 20 years ago, involving a plot to bug her husband's boat, reads like a bad crime novel that publishers would reject for being too far-fetched.

Cronyism Over Common Sense

The longstanding friendship between Trump and Pirro is the elephant in the room—or perhaps more accurately, the fox in the henhouse. Trump's pardon of Pirro's ex-husband, a convicted tax cheat, at the end of his first term, is the kind of favoritism that would make even the most corrupt of historical figures blush. It's a glaring example of how the lines between personal relationships and public duty have not just been blurred but obliterated.

The Bottom Line

Pirro's appointment is not just a failure of judgment by Donald Trump; it's an insult to the legal system and to the citizens of Washington, D.C. When individuals with questionable ethics and dubious qualifications are placed in roles of significant legal authority, it erodes public trust in our institutions. It's a stark reminder of the importance of integrity and competence in public service—a reminder that seems desperately needed yet tragically unheeded.

So, Capehart's dismay and surprise? Understandable. But perhaps the real surprise is that we continue to be surprised by these appointments. In the theater of the absurd that has come to characterize parts of American politics, the casting of Jeanine Pirro in this role is just another scene. It's up to the audience—aka the electorate—to demand a change in the script.
