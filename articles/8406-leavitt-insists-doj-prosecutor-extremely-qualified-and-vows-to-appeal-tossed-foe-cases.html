Leavitt insists DOJ prosecutor 'extremely qualified' and vows to appeal tossed foe cases

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 25, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Deep Dive into the White House’s Legal Quagmire

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt holds a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C. on Nov. 20, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said Monday that the Department of Justice will appeal a federal judge’s decision to dismiss cases against former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James.

Leavitt blamed U.S. District Judge Cameron McGowan Currie, who ruled that President Donald Trump unlawfully appointed his personal attorney, Lindsey Halligan, as interim U.S. attorney, without Senate confirmation.

“I know there was a judge who was clearly trying to shield Letitia James and James Comey from receiving accountability and that’s why they took this unprecedented action to throw away the indictments against these two individuals but the Department of Justice will be appealing very soon and it is our position that Lindsey Halligan is extremely qualified for this position, but more importantly, was legally appointed to it,” Leavitt said.

Halligan, whom the judge referred to in the opinion as “a former White House aide with no prior prosecutorial experience,” could potentially be disbarred.

White House spokeswoman Abigail Jackson said in a statement Monday afternoon, “The facts of the indictments against Comey and James have not changed and this will not be the final word on this matter.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the White House’s Legal Quagmire

In what reads like a chapter ripped straight from a political thriller, the White House, under the current administration, finds itself embroiled in a legal and ethical quagmire that not only challenges the boundaries of executive power but also raises serious questions about the politicization of the justice system. The latest development, as reported, involves the Department of Justice’s intent to appeal a federal judge’s decision to dismiss cases against former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James. The basis of the dismissal? An appointment deemed unlawful by U.S. District Judge Cameron McGowan Currie.

The Heart of the Matter: At the core, this controversy revolves around President Donald Trump’s appointment of his personal attorney, Lindsey Halligan, as interim U.S. attorney without the requisite Senate confirmation. This move, criticized widely, not only skirts the edges of legal propriety but also dances dangerously close to outright nepotism and cronyism. The White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt’s defense of this appointment only adds fuel to the fire of this burgeoning scandal.

The Judge’s Ruling: Judge Currie’s ruling shines a spotlight on a critical aspect of this case — the blatant disregard for established norms and procedures. Describing Halligan as “a former White House aide with no prior prosecutorial experience,” the judge’s words underscore a troubling trend of valuing loyalty over legality and competence. This is not merely a procedural hiccup; it’s a glaring red flag about the erosion of institutional integrity at the highest levels of government.

The Defense’s Stance: Leavitt’s assertion that the judge was “clearly trying to shield” Comey and James from accountability is a classic diversion tactic. It attempts to shift the focus from the illegitimacy of Halligan’s appointment to an alleged conspiracy to protect the defendants. This narrative, while potentially appealing to the administration’s base, lacks substantive legal merit and seems designed to obfuscate the real issues at hand.

The Implications: Beyond the immediate legal ramifications, this episode has far-reaching implications for the rule of law and democratic governance. The attempt to bypass Senate confirmation, coupled with the appointment of an individual conspicuously lacking the typical qualifications for such a critical role, represents a dangerous precedent. It suggests an administration willing to flout legal norms and procedures to advance its agenda and protect its allies.

In Conclusion: The White House’s decision to appeal Judge Currie’s ruling is not just a legal battle; it’s a litmus test for the resilience of the American judicial system. It challenges the notion of checks and balances that is foundational to American democracy. As this saga unfolds, it will be telling to see whether the courts will serve as a bulwark against overreach or if they will buckle under the weight of political pressure. The “facts of the indictments” may not have changed, as White House spokeswoman Abigail Jackson claims, but the context in which they are being pursued certainly has. This is not merely a legal skirmish; it’s a battle for the soul of the American justice system.
