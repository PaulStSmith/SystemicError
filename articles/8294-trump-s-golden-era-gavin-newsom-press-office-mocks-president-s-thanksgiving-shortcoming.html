'Trump's golden era': Gavin Newsom press office mocks president's Thanksgiving shortcoming

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

California governor Gavin Newsom’s press office has mocked the Trump administration in a scathing post to social media.

A screenshot of the Drudge Report front page was posted to X by the Governor Newsom Press Office, which took aim at Trump’s Thanksgiving policy blunders. Turkey prices were reported to have surged 24% and retail sales have dwindled as the cost-of-living crisis continues across the country.

Newsom’s team mockingly called it “Trump’s golden era” in their post. It reads, “Welcome to Trump’s golden era!” A photo of the Drudge Report headline was also shared. It reads, “A tariff Thanksgiving. Turkey price up 24%. Grim retail sales. Consumer confidence spirals.”

Analysis from The Times, which is the link Drudge Report had featured, found the cost of a traditional Thanksgiving meal would set back the average American family $107. This would be an increase of 9% total compared to 2024, and the increase also includes a 24% spike in the cost of a whole turkey.

Despite the reported price hike, the White House released a statement earlier this month claiming Thanksgiving was actually cheaper under the Trump administration than it was during Joe Biden’s presidency.

Their statement reads, “President Donald J. Trump promised to crush inflation and lower prices — and he’s delivering this Thanksgiving, with the classic holiday feast about 3% cheaper than last year.”

It comes after Trump used his Thanksgiving speech to rip into Illinois Governor JB Pritzker. He said, “The Mayor, who is a low IQ person... the mayor is incompetent and the governor is a big fat slob.”

“So Governor Pritzker, if you’re listening, let’s get your act together. Invite us in. I had a little bit of a Pritzker joke, I was going to talk about Pritzker and size, but when I talk about Pritzker, I get angry because he’s not letting us do the job.

“So I’m not going to tell my Pritzker joke. They have a very cute, little joke you know, some speech writer wrote some joke about his weight. But I would never want to talk about his weight. I don’t talk about people being fat. I refuse to talk about the fact that he’s a fat slob, I don’t mention it.”

Our Analysis:

The Political Theater of Blame and Mockery

In an era where political discourse often descends into the realm of the absurd, the recent exchange between California Governor Gavin Newsom’s press office and former President Donald Trump offers a quintessential snapshot of the current state of American politics. The Newsom team’s decision to take to social media to lampoon what they termed “Trump’s golden era,” referencing a surge in turkey prices and a bleak retail outlook, underscores the ongoing battle of narratives that defines our times.

“Welcome to Trump’s golden era!” This statement, dripping with irony, encapsulates the essence of political satire in the digital age. But beyond the surface-level mockery, lies a deeper commentary on the effects of political decisions on the everyday American. The post, anchored by a screenshot from the Drudge Report, highlights the 24% increase in turkey prices and the grim state of consumer confidence. This is juxtaposed against a backdrop of a cost-of-living crisis that continues to plague the country.

What stands out is the data cited from The Times, indicating that the cost of a traditional Thanksgiving meal has skyrocketed to $107, marking a 9% increase from the previous year. This figure, particularly the 24% spike in the cost of a whole turkey, paints a somber picture of economic strain. Yet, in a bizarre twist of reality and rhetoric, the White House’s statement claims a reduction in Thanksgiving meal costs by about 3% under Trump’s administration compared to Biden’s tenure. The juxtaposition of these conflicting narratives is not just confusing but highlights the absurdity of political claims detached from the lived experiences of citizens.

Trump’s response, a vitriolic attack on Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, further muddies the waters of constructive political discourse. His remarks, characterized by personal insults and an aborted attempt at a joke about Pritzker’s weight, exemplify the degradation of political speech to personal vendettas. Trump’s assertion, “I refuse to talk about the fact that he’s a fat slob, I don’t mention it,” is a paradoxical statement that accomplishes the exact opposite of its purported intent, showcasing a tactic of insinuation cloaked in denial.

This exchange between Newsom and Trump, rife with mockery and counter-mockery, serves as a microcosm of the broader political landscape, where substantive policy discussions are often overshadowed by personal attacks and sensational rhetoric. The real losers in this scenario are the American people, who are left to navigate the fallout of these political skirmishes amid rising living costs and economic uncertainty.

In dissecting this political theater, one must question the efficacy of such exchanges in addressing the pressing issues facing the nation. Does the mockery on social media contribute to constructive debate, or does it further entrench divisions and distract from the urgent need for viable solutions to economic challenges? The answer, it seems, is as complex as the political environment from which these episodes emerge.

The role of the press and political commentators in this context is crucial. It is incumbent upon us to cut through the noise, analyze the facts, and hold our political leaders accountable for their words and actions. As observers and participants in the democratic process, we must demand more than just entertainment from our political discourse; we must insist on integrity, truth, and a commitment to the common good.

In conclusion, while the exchange between Newsom’s press office and Trump may provide momentary amusement or outrage, it ultimately serves as a stark reminder of the need for a return to civility, substance, and sincerity in our political conversations. Only then can we hope to address the systemic issues that underpin the spectacle.
