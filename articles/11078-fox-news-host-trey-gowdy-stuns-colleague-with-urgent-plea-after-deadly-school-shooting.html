A Fox News host is blaming mass shootings on access to guns and young white males, after the latest school shooting that left two young children dead and 17 others injured during mass at a Catholic school. The gunman reportedly was in his early 20s and is now deceased.

“Minneapolis Police Chief Brian O’Hara said the shooter — armed with a rifle, shotgun and pistol — approached the side of the church and shot dozens of rounds through the windows toward the children sitting in the pews during Mass at the Annunciation Catholic School. Police believe the gunman then killed himself,” the Associated Press reported. “The children who died were 8 and 10, and 14 other kids were among the wounded, the [police] chief said. Dozens of youngsters were inside.”

Trey Gowdy, a Republican former U.S. Congressman from South Carolina and a former federal prosecutor, stunned his co-host while speaking live on Fox News (video below).

“Our system is reactive,” Gowdy lamented. “Something bad happens, we react to it. And what people are crying for now is, ‘How can we prevent this? How can we stop it?’ And the only way to stop it is to identify the shooter ahead of time or keep the weapons out of their hands.”

“And so we’re going to have to have a conversation of freedom versus protecting children. I mean, how many school shootings does it take before we’re going to have a conversation about keeping firearms out?”

“It’s always a young white male, almost always. I mean, did anyone this morning think, ‘I wonder if that was a female?’ Did any of y’all think that? I mean, there’s been one school shooting involving a female. One. Tennessee. But other than that, it is usually young white males.”

His co-host, off camera, protested.

“Don’t we have laws on the books for these types of situations?” she asked.

“Like what?” he pressed.

“Well, well murder is what happened, and maybe the why is that you had a 10-year-old and an 8-year-old praying in the pews. Were they targeted for their faith?” she asked.

Gowdy is not a liberal. On Tuesday, appearing on Fox News’ “Hannity,” he declared the left’s politics are “absolutely idiotic.”

Our Analysis:

A Stark Shift in the Gun Debate Narrative: The Unlikely Messenger

In a recent turn of events that could only be described as jaw-dropping, Trey Gowdy, a figure deeply entrenched in conservative politics and a former stalwart of the Republican Party, delivered a message on Fox News that many would have bet their bottom dollar would never cross such airwaves. The content of Gowdy's argument, as surprising as its platform, targeted two significant and sensitive topics in the American sociopolitical landscape: gun control and the racial and gender demographics of mass shooters.

The Unthinkable: A Conservative's Call for Gun Control?

Gowdy's remarks came in the wake of a devastating school shooting in Minneapolis that left two children dead and several others injured. The perpetrator, fitting the all-too-familiar profile of a young white male, unleashed horror upon innocent children attending Mass at the Annunciation Catholic School. It is within this context that Gowdy, a figure who has walked the corridors of power and possesses a deep understanding of the law as a former federal prosecutor, calls for a reevaluation of America's gun laws. His assertion that the nation's approach to preventing such tragedies is fundamentally "reactive" rather than proactive is a piercing critique of the status quo.

The Core of Gowdy's Argument:

Gowdy's argument is twofold. First, he highlights the disturbing regularity with which young white males are implicated in these heinous acts. This observation is not new, but coming from Gowdy, it carries a different weight. It challenges the often defensive posture of conservative media towards discussions on race, gender, and gun violence. Second, and perhaps more crucial, is his call for a national dialogue on the balance between freedom and the safeguarding of children. This is a significant pivot from the usual rhetoric that positions gun control measures as antithetical to freedom.

The Counterpoint: A Co-Host's Resistance

In a predictable contrast, Gowdy's co-host reacts not with introspection but with deflection, questioning whether laws already in place should have prevented this tragedy. This interaction is emblematic of a broader reluctance within conservative circles to confront the tangible implications of America's gun culture. The suggestion that the children might have been targeted for their faith, while speculative, points to a desperate search for any explanation other than the glaring issue of gun accessibility and the profiles of those most likely to commit mass shootings.

The Bigger Picture: A National Reckoning

What Gowdy's remarks signify is a potential crack in the previously impenetrable facade of conservative media's stance on gun control. It's a moment that demands attention not because it offers immediate solutions, but because it represents a significant deviation from the norm. It prompts the question: If figures like Gowdy are beginning to question the orthodoxy, what does this mean for the national conversation on gun violence?

Conclusion: A Moment of Truth

The article underlines a critical juncture in America's ongoing debate over gun control and societal safety. Gowdy’s unexpected stance, aired on a platform known for its conservative leanings, might just be the catalyst needed for a more honest and inclusive discussion on how to protect the most vulnerable among us—our children. It remains to be seen whether this marks a genuine shift in the dialogue or merely a fleeting moment of clarity. Nonetheless, it is a stark reminder that the cost of inaction is measured in human lives, and that perhaps, in the face of unspeakable tragedy, the lines that divide us could be reconsidered in the pursuit of a safer, more united society.
