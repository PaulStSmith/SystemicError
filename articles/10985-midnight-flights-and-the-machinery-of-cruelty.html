Midnight Flights and the Machinery of Cruelty

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Michael Popok’s live report should have been a political earthquake. At four o’clock in the morning, immigration rights advocates rushed into federal court in Washington, D.C. to stop the Trump administration from secretly deporting six hundred unaccompanied children. Kids between ten and seventeen, some already strapped into planes, were within minutes of being shipped out of the country with no hearings, no representation, and no rights. The only thing standing between them and disappearance was a handful of lawyers jolting a judge awake before dawn. That is how fragile the line is between legality and lawless cruelty in America under Trump.

Let’s not mince words: this wasn’t “policy.” It was a state-run child snatch. The administration wasn’t following asylum law, refugee law, or even the barest constitutional protections. It was operating on the principle that children can be disappeared at will, so long as it’s done fast enough and quietly enough. Planes at 3 a.m., court filings at 4:30 a.m., emergency orders before breakfast — this is the architecture of authoritarianism. The secrecy is not a side effect. It is the point.

The Anatomy of a Crime Against Humanity

Imagine it: six hundred kids corralled, processed, and loaded like cargo onto planes bound for Guatemala. No hearings. No case review. No chance to plead for asylum. Just the flick of a bureaucratic pen and the roar of jet engines. This is not a system of justice. This is an operation indistinguishable from trafficking — because what separates “deportation” from “disappearance” is oversight, and Trump’s DHS made sure there was none.

That’s the horror Popok’s report lays bare. Without that 4:30 a.m. court order, the children would already be gone, their lives dumped back into peril or worse. We would never know what truly happened on those flights. And that’s what gnaws at the edge of this story: the plausible deniability built into the system. “We sent them to Guatemala,” officials could say. But who’s watching the tarmac? Who’s tracking the manifests? Who ensures those planes don’t divert elsewhere — or that the children even arrive?

The Russian Shadow

Here’s where the nightmare sharpens. Donald Trump’s admiration for Vladimir Putin has never been subtle. He has praised him as “brilliant” and “a genius.” He has bent U.S. policy around his whims. And Russia, under Putin, is not just a geopolitical rival. It is one of the world’s most notorious producers and distributors of exploitative child material. That is not speculation — it’s documented in Interpol reports, cybersecurity seizures, and countless sting operations.

Now line up the dots. Trump builds a deportation machine that normalizes vanishing children without due process. Planes fly at night, destinations scribbled onto paperwork, oversight deliberately stripped away. At the same time, Trump cozies up to a regime whose criminal underworld profits off the global trade in children’s suffering. Does anyone really believe this system is airtight, incorruptible, immune from infiltration? The infrastructure itself invites corruption. It creates opportunities. It whispers: no one will ever know.

And that is the most damning truth. Even if every single child on those planes was in fact dumped into Guatemala, the system Trump engineered is tailor-made for darker uses. Once you allow children to be disappeared by government order, you have created the very conditions under which traffickers thrive. The distinction between deportation and disappearance is erased by the fog of secrecy.

Cruelty as Policy

We must be clear: cruelty was not an accident of Trump’s immigration policy. It was the point. Separating children from their parents at the border, locking toddlers in cages, trying to rush hundreds of kids onto planes before dawn — these were not “tough” decisions. They were sadistic displays of power. They sent a message: migrants are not people, they are objects. And once you reduce human beings to objects, any fate can be justified. Deportation, detention, disappearance — all become tools of control.

The midnight flights of children are the logical endpoint of this worldview. When you no longer see children as children, when you strip them of humanity, you can treat them as disposable inventory. The only thing surprising about this episode is how close it came to happening in the open, how narrowly it was stopped.

Why Abolition, Not Reform

This is why calls to “reform” ICE or “humanize” DHS ring hollow. You cannot reform a machine built for cruelty. You cannot polish the chains of a system designed to disappear children. The only moral response is abolition — to dismantle the structures that enable midnight flights, secret deportations, and the dehumanization of migrants. Anything less leaves the machinery intact, waiting for the next authoritarian to seize the controls.

Reform assumes the system is broken. But Trump showed us the truth: the system works exactly as designed. It is meant to terrify, to brutalize, to strip people of rights. Reform tinkers with the optics. Abolition cuts the wires.

The Plausible Nightmare

Speculation about Russian planes waiting on tarmacs, or children diverted into black markets, is not conspiracy. It is the logical extrapolation of a system that operates in darkness. When oversight is erased, when due process is discarded, when secrecy becomes the norm, the nightmare becomes not just possible but plausible. The point isn’t whether it happened on that particular night. The point is that the system made it possible, and would make it possible again.

That’s the true indictment of Trump’s immigration policies. They didn’t just brutalize children in the present. They built an infrastructure of cruelty that could be repurposed for even worse horrors in the future. Popok’s midnight dispatch was not only a record of what was nearly done. It was a glimpse into what remains possible if we fail to dismantle the machinery.

Conclusion: No More Midnight Flights

Six hundred children nearly vanished in the night. Six hundred lives almost extinguished from the American conscience by the roar of departing engines. Only a handful of advocates and a sleep-deprived federal judge stood in the way. That should terrify us. It should enrage us. And it should radicalize us to demand abolition, not half-measures.

The Trump administration built a system where deportation and disappearance blur into the same act. Where secrecy shields atrocity. Where cruelty is the message. And where the specter of even darker uses — trafficking, exploitation, complicity with regimes like Putin’s Russia — looms just beneath the surface.

If we do not dismantle that system, we invite the next authoritarian to finish what Trump started. There must never again be midnight flights of children. There must never again be planes loaded at 3 a.m. with no oversight, no hearings, no humanity. The machinery of cruelty must be torn down, root and branch, before it can be weaponized again.
