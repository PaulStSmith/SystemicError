'Intense backlash' sweeps conservatives off school boards nationwide

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Democrat Wins in School Board Races: A Reaction Against Culture Wars

Democrats invested heavily in sweeping Republican culture warriors off school boards across the country, and their efforts paid off.

Republicans lost school board seats in local races in Ohio, Pennsylvania and Texas after seizing onto educational culture war issues in the post-COVID era, as Democrats recruited candidates to run professional campaigns infused with money from the national party, reported Politico.

“Folks just want their school boards to be boring again,” said Lesley Guilmart, who was one of three Democrats to take back the majority in the Cypress-Fairbanks school board in the Houston, Texas, area. “They want normalcy. Once the board was taken over by a super partisan extremist majority, folks across the political spectrum were dismayed.”

Conservative groups like Moms for Liberty got candidates elected who opposed school closures and mask mandates during the pandemic and argued that public schools promoted progressive values, and Republicans used the energy around those battles to leverage wins in state and national elections.

Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis elevated Moms for Liberty and attempted to use his “war on woke” as a springboard for his presidential campaign, and Donald Trump’s anti-trans rights attacks on Kamala Harris proved to be one of his most effective ads during his 2024 re-election campaign, but those issues seem to be fading as voters continue worrying about rising costs.

“Covid went away, and the dissatisfaction with school districts abated,” said Neal McCluskey, director of the Cato Institute’s Center for Educational Freedom. It’s not totally gone, but it lessened. I think we’re more at a fatigue level.”

Democratic-aligned group Pipeline Fund, which is recruiting candidates for school board and state legislative races, are targeting states all over the map, and its founder and executive director, Denise Feriozzi, argued that success in those races will help change negative perceptions about the party.

“When you have a Democratic brand that is suffering, you can show people what it actually looks like to be a Democrat in Mobile, Alabama, and Anchorage, Alaska,” Feriozzi said.

Democrats notched wins in this month’s off-year elections in New Jersey, Virginia and elsewhere, and some believe voters prefer local politics in school board races than national issues, with GOP candidates in New Jersey’s Ocean City Board of Education race losing despite touting endorsements from Moms for Liberty and Turning Point USA.

“For many parents, those endorsements signaled a strong commitment to parental involvement and transparency in public education,” said Robin Shaffer, who was on the losing slate along with two incumbents, said in an email.

Shaffer said he and his fellow Republicans were “proud” of the endorsements but conceded they may have been a drag with some voters.

“For others, those associations carried national-level baggage and triggered intense backlash based on misconceptions about our actual views,” he said.

Our Analysis:

Democrat Wins in School Board Races: A Reaction Against Culture Wars

The recent Politico report on Democrats’ success in ousting Republican culture warriors from school board positions across the United States paints a vivid picture of the current political landscape in education. With significant victories in states like Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Texas, Democrats have effectively countered the Republicans’ post-COVID educational culture war strategies. This shift suggests a growing public desire for a return to normalcy in educational governance, away from the polarizing antics that have characterized recent years.

The Strategy Behind the Victories

Democrats’ approach, as detailed in the article, involved recruiting candidates to run professional campaigns bolstered by national party funds. This strategic infusion of resources and focus on professionalism appears to have resonated with voters tired of the drama and extremism that have plagued school boards. Lesley Guilmart’s quote, “Folks just want their school boards to be boring again,” encapsulates this sentiment perfectly. It’s a damning indictment of the chaos and partisanship that have dominated discussions around education, diverting attention from the core mission of schools: to educate.

The Culture War Backlash

The article also highlights the role of conservative groups like Moms for Liberty in elevating candidates who opposed school closures and mask mandates during the pandemic, while accusing public schools of promoting progressive values. However, the effectiveness of such tactics appears to be waning. The mention of Florida Governor Ron DeSantis and Donald Trump’s attempts to leverage these “culture war” issues for political gain, only to see them fade in importance, is particularly telling. It suggests that while these issues may have once offered a potent political weapon, their relevance is diminishing as voters prioritize more pressing concerns, like rising costs.

A Fatigue for the Fringe

Neal McCluskey’s commentary on COVID-related dissatisfaction and the current state of fatigue resonates deeply. It underscores a crucial point: voters are tired. They’re tired of the endless battles, the constant politicization of education, and the diversion from issues that directly impact the quality of education and their children’s futures. This fatigue is leading to a backlash against those who have sought to exploit educational institutions for political gain.

Democratic Brand Rehabilitation through Local Wins

The Pipeline Fund’s efforts to recruit candidates for school board and state legislative races across the country are an interesting development. Denise Feriozzi’s argument that these local wins can help improve the Democratic brand by showcasing effective governance is compelling. It’s a strategy that recognizes the power of local politics to influence national perceptions and underscores the importance of ground-level engagement in political rehabilitation efforts.

The Misconception of Endorsements

The case of GOP candidates in New Jersey’s Ocean City Board of Education race is particularly illustrative of the broader dynamics at play. Despite endorsements from Moms for Liberty and Turning Point USA, these candidates lost, suggesting that such backing might carry more liabilities than benefits in the current climate. Robin Shaffer’s admission that these endorsements may have “triggered intense backlash based on misconceptions about our actual views” speaks volumes. It hints at a growing disconnect between the national rhetoric of these groups and the local realities and desires of voters.

Conclusion: A Return to Substantive Education Policy

Ultimately, the victories of Democrats in these school board races signal a potential shift in the educational political landscape. Voters seem to be signaling a desire to move away from the divisive and often superficial battles that have dominated recent years, back towards a focus on substantive education policy and governance. This trend, if it continues, could lead to more pragmatic, less sensationalized school board meetings and policies, which would be a welcome development for students, parents, and educators alike.
