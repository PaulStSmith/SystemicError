'As a communicator, stop': Former Trump official blasts president’s Charlie Kirk address

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Rhetorical Missteps in Trump's Address on Charlie Kirk's Tragic Death

Screengrab / CNN

A former communications director for President Donald Trump took to CNN Thursday to blast the president’s Oval Office address on the killing of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, criticizing it for its “heated rhetoric” and labeling it as a missed opportunity to “unify the country.”

Kirk was shot and killed Wednesday while speaking at Utah Valley University in an event that has drawn international coverage and bi-partisan condemnation. Kirk had an intimate relationship with many officials in the Trump administration, including with the president himself, who shortly after the shooting delivered an address from the Oval Office.

“Those on the radical left have compared wonderful Americans like Charlie to Nazis and the world's worst mass murderers and criminals,” Trump said in his address. “This kind of rhetoric is directly responsible for the terrorism that we're seeing in our country today, and it must stop right now!”

Speaking on Trump’s address, Mike Dubke, who served as the White House communications director during Trump’s first term, said the president missed the mark.

“I watched the president's speech, and the first two-and-a-half minutes of that were, I thought, a unifying message,” Dubke said.

“Then, there was the turn towards the negativity, the heated rhetoric of Democrats, the reference to Republicans as being Nazis and all of that. As a communicator, stop after the two-and-a-half minutes because I think one of the powers of the Oval Office is, in times of strife, to try to unify the country, and I think that is still an important role for the president.”

As of Thursday morning, the culprit in Kirk’s shooting was still at large, with bi-partisan condemnation of the attack pouring in from across the political spectrum, including from former presidents Joe Biden and Barack Obama. A vigil for the right-wing influencer was held Wednesday night at the Idaho State Capitol that devolved into violence after a passerby shouted an expletive directed at Kirk.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Rhetorical Missteps in Trump's Address on Charlie Kirk's Tragic Death

In an era where political polarization seems to have shredded the very fabric of unity in the United States, the tragic killing of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk could have been a moment for collective mourning and a call for a ceasefire in the relentless culture wars. However, President Donald Trump's response, delivered from the sanctity of the Oval Office, instead fanned the flames of division, highlighting a missed opportunity to rise above the fray.

The Missed Opportunity for Unity

Mike Dubke, a former communications director for Trump, critiqued the president's address on CNN, pointing out a momentary glimpse of potential unity that was quickly overshadowed by what Dubke describes as "the turn towards the negativity." Initially, Trump's speech seemed to set a tone of reconciliation, but it sharply veered into heated rhetoric targeting the radical left, effectively blaming them for the climate of political violence. This pivot from unification to division starkly illustrates the president's inability or unwillingness to harness the power of the Oval Office to bridge the widening gap between Americans.

The Rhetoric of Division

Trump's speech laid blame at the feet of "those on the radical left," accusing them of equating "wonderful Americans like Charlie" with Nazis and mass murderers. This sweeping generalization not only exacerbates the us-versus-them mentality but also simplifies the complex landscape of political discourse in America. It's a dangerous simplification that ignores the multifaceted reasons behind the nation's divisiveness, reducing them to mere partisan bickering.

The Role of the President in Times of Crisis

Dubke rightly points out that one of the core roles of the presidency, especially in moments of national grief, is to unify the country. The Oval Office, with its storied history and symbolic power, serves as the ideal backdrop for messages of healing and unity. Yet, Trump's address serves as a stark reminder of a squandered opportunity to transcend political divides and offer solace to a nation in mourning.

Bipartisan Condemnation and the Echoes of Violence

In the wake of Kirk's death, figures across the political landscape, including former Presidents Joe Biden and Barack Obama, have come together to denounce the violence, showcasing a rare moment of bipartisan agreement. This collective voice of condemnation stands in contrast to the divisive tone of Trump's address, underscoring the possibility and necessity of unity in the face of tragedy. Furthermore, the vigil held for Kirk, which devolved into violence, is a grim testament to the charged atmosphere that currently pervades American society.

Conclusion

The tragedy of Charlie Kirk's death and the subsequent response by President Trump encapsulate the current state of American political discourse. Instead of using his platform to unify and heal, Trump opted to perpetuate division, thereby missing a critical opportunity to lead the nation towards reconciliation. This incident not only underscores the responsibilities of the presidency but also serves as a cautionary tale of the consequences of divisive rhetoric at the highest levels of government. In times of crisis, the American people deserve a leader who seeks to unite rather than divide, who offers solace instead of stoking the fires of partisanship.
