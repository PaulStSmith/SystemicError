When Trump’s Legal Fantasies Collide With Actual Law

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 24, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Comey and James Cases: Paper TigersTrump’s DOJ: A Vengeance Engine Wearing a DOJ BadgeA System That Still Pushes Back

The federal judiciary just handed Donald Trump a reality check so sharp it could cut steel. United States District Judge Cameron McGowan Currie didn’t merely dismiss the indictments against former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James — she exposed the entire operation as a legal farce held together by Trump’s usual cocktail of arrogance and contempt for process.

At the center of this implosion is Lindsey Halligan, Trump’s personal attorney, whom he attempted to shove into the role of interim U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia. Not through Senate confirmation. Not through the Vacancies Reform Act. Not even through the standard statutory method under 28 U.S.C. § 546. No, Trump simply installed her, as if federal prosecutors were interchangeable pieces in his personal vengeance machine.

Judge Currie’s ruling is a masterclass in understatement: “Ms. Halligan had no lawful authority to present the indictment.” Translation: this wasn’t a prosecutor; this was a political valet wearing a borrowed badge.

And once the court made that clear, the consequences were inevitable. An unlawful appointment means an unlawful prosecution. You can’t build a case on sand, and this was sand shoveled straight from Mar-a-Lago.

The Comey and James Cases: Paper Tigers

Comey and Letitia James were targeted not because evidence demanded it but because Trump’s ego demanded it. Comey refused to pledge personal loyalty; James had the audacity to enforce corporate fraud laws against the Trump Organization. In Trump-world, these are capital offenses.

But even a president with authoritarian fantasies must still obey the architecture of federal law. Trump didn’t. So the indictments evaporated the moment real legal scrutiny touched them.

Currie dismissed both cases without prejudice, which sounds generous until you realize it’s a formality. Nothing here can be resurrected unless a lawful prosecutor decides to refile — and no legitimate attorney with a functioning conscience or reputation would touch these embarrassments with industrial gloves.

Trump’s DOJ: A Vengeance Engine Wearing a DOJ Badge

This entire episode illustrates the same pathology that has marked Trump’s interactions with law enforcement from day one: a belief that institutions exist solely to be weaponized against his enemies. He attempted to convert the Justice Department into a privatized enforcer, staffed by loyalists whose only qualification was unblinking submission.

But courts are not MAGA rallies. Judges expect statutes, not sycophancy. They require jurisdiction, not performance art. When Trumpworld tried to smuggle a personal lawyer into the role of a federal prosecutor, the judiciary did what it’s supposed to do: it hit the brakes hard.

A System That Still Pushes Back

Trump’s supporters will cry “deep state,” as they always do when reality contradicts the leader’s fantasies. But the truth is simpler and far more damning: this operation collapsed because it was illegal, sloppy, and driven by political vengeance instead of law.

Judge Currie didn’t take a side. She enforced the rules. And the indictments fell apart because Trump and his loyalists chose politics over legality, spectacle over competence, and personal obsession over constitutional structure.

This ruling is a reminder that the machinery of justice still has guardrails. Trump keeps trying to rip them out, but the courts aren’t as easily bullied as his inner circle.

The Comey and James indictments didn’t fall because the targets were powerful. They fell because the prosecutions were fraudulent from the moment Trump handed his personal lawyer a role she was never legally empowered to hold.

Once again, Trump tried to bend the system to his will.

Once again, the system snapped back.
