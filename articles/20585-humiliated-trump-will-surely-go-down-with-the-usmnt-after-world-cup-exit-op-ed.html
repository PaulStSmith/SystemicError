Humiliated Trump 'will surely go down' with the USMNT after World Cup exit: op-ed

Based on an article by Daniel Hampton

Trump Pressed the Button, FIFA Blinked, and the U.S. Still Lost

The Setup

The source story is simple enough: Trump called FIFA president Gianni Infantino and asked for a review of a red-card suspension for U.S. striker Folarin Balogun; FIFA reversed the automatic ban; UEFA said the reversal crossed a red line; Belgium beat the United States 4-1 anyway, and all three host nations went out.

The Real Power Was Not on the Pitch

This is not a story about a president who merely had an opinion on a foul. It is a story about institutional power being exercised through personal pressure. Trump held the White House and used it to lean on FIFA. FIFA then made the decision that mattered: it changed the suspension. That is the line of accountability. Everything else is noise dressed up as sportsmanship.

Trump’s defense, that he “asked for a review” and never told FIFA what to do, is the usual laundering of influence. Leaders who understand their own leverage do not need to issue explicit orders. They know how to make the room understand what answer is expected.

FIFA’s Cowardice Is the Point

The embarrassing part is not that Trump asked. The embarrassing part is that FIFA complied. UEFA’s warning that the reversal “crossed a red line” is the rare honest line in this whole mess. It identifies what Trump’s defenders want to blur: this was not neutral procedure, and it was not harmless curiosity. It was a political intervention into a sporting decision, and the governing body blinked.

That matters because FIFA is not some innocent referee caught in a misunderstanding. It is an institution that routinely sells itself as above politics while making deeply political decisions when power walks in through the door.

The Story Tries to Make It About Trump’s Ego

The op-ed framing leans hard on Trump’s humiliation: he tied himself to the team, the team lost, and now he is supposedly stuck with the fallout. That is satisfying as a political punchline, but it is not the whole story. Trump did not “attach” himself to the team by accident. He used the team, and FIFA, to stage yet another demonstration that institutional boundaries bend when he calls.

The public lesson is not that he is unlucky. It is that he keeps testing how far he can push weak institutions before they admit who they answer to.

The Host-Nation Fantasy Was Always Disposable

The article notes that Trump had helped torch goodwill around the tournament and united fans abroad against the U.S. side. That is real, but it also misses the deeper pattern: this administration treats soft power as a prop and then acts shocked when the prop catches fire. Ties with allies, international reputation, and even a co-hosted World Cup become instruments for a domestic ego contest.

That is the governing logic here. Every institution becomes a stage. Every international relationship becomes leverage. Every setback becomes someone else’s problem.

The Pattern

This is what authoritarian-adjacent politics looks like in practice: not always a grand legal smash, but constant pressure on institutions that are supposed to stand apart from raw political force. A president makes the call, the governing body folds, the press turns it into a personality story, and the actual lesson gets buried under the spectacle.

The lesson is not that Trump cares too much about soccer. It is that he understands institutional pliability, and too many gatekeepers still help him prove it.
