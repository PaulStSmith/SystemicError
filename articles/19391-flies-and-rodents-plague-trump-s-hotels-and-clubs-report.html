Flies and rodents plague Trump's hotels and clubs: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 11, 20264 min read

Chicago, Illinois, USA: January 28, 2025 Trump Tower. (Photo credit: Mattyhenno / Shutterstock)

Health inspectors raised serious concerns after a recent visit to President Donald Trump’s Chicago hotel, according to a report published Tuesday.

A December inspection report at Trump International Hotel Chicago revealed that health officials found small flies in the bar and dish area of the rooftop restaurant, pooling wastewater on the main kitchen floor from prep sinks that were draining, and a dishwasher that wasn’t fully sanitizing guests’ dishes and utensils, The Daily Beast reported. They also uncovered debris in prep coolers and a cracked ice machine lid.

“Observed more than 10 small flies throughout bar area and 3 small flies in dish area. Instructed manager to service all areas affected by pests,” according to the report.

Health inspectors also saw workers handling food without gloves while they assembled sushi toppings and burger buns, The Beast reported. The report also stated that the employee bathroom did not have hand soap.

After the citations were issued on Dec. 17, the hotel management had until Dec. 23 to correct the problems. When they returned, both kitchen areas at the hotel had improved and met the inspectors’ standards; however, the broken ice machine lid was still not replaced.

This wasn’t the only Trump property to recently receive a citation for health violations. Trump National Golf Club Westchester was cited by local health officials for “critical” violations, according to recently released records.

Westchester County Department of Health officials inspected Trump’s New York club on Nov. 20 and observed insects, rodents and other health violations.

According to Tuesday’s report, the club was cited for “dirty surfaces,” food that was “uncovered, mislabeled, [and] stored on floor,” and “missing or inadequate sneeze guards.”

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Review of Trump’s Property Health Violations

In what reads like a poorly scripted episode of Kitchen Nightmares, President Donald Trump’s luxury establishments have been caught in the crosshairs of health inspectors, exposing a sordid tale of neglect and disregard for basic health standards. The reports from Trump International Hotel Chicago and Trump National Golf Club Westchester are not just embarrassing footnotes in the annals of hospitality; they are glaring indicators of systemic issues within the Trump brand’s management of luxury properties.

Trump International Hotel Chicago: A Case Study in Negligence

According to The Daily Beast, the December inspection of Trump’s Chicago hotel paints a picture of a property that is less a beacon of luxury and more a breeding ground for health violations. The discovery of small flies in the bar and dish area, pooling wastewater in the main kitchen, and a malfunctioning dishwasher that fails to sanitize dishes is not just appalling; it’s a public health nightmare. The presence of debris in prep coolers and a cracked ice machine lid only add insult to injury, underscoring a disturbing lack of attention to cleanliness and maintenance.

The report’s mention of workers handling food without gloves, particularly while assembling sushi toppings and burger buns, is a stark reminder of the basic principles of food safety being blatantly ignored. And let’s not overlook the absence of hand soap in the employee bathroom—a fundamental requirement in any establishment, let alone one bearing the Trump name.

Trump National Golf Club Westchester: Another Chapter of Shame

Moving from Chicago to New York, the situation at Trump National Golf Club Westchester does little to restore faith in the Trump brand’s commitment to health and safety. Described by health officials as having “critical” violations, the club’s issues range from the presence of insects and rodents to dirty surfaces and improperly stored food. The lack of sneeze guards, a basic yet crucial component in food service to prevent contamination, is particularly damning.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection of Leadership

These reports are not merely isolated incidents; they are symptomatic of a broader problem of leadership and accountability—or the lack thereof. When the properties of a former President of the United States, a man who once wielded immense power and influence, fail to meet the most basic health standards, questions must be asked about the culture and values being promoted within his businesses.

It’s telling that despite being given deadlines to rectify these egregious violations, compliance was met with minimal effort. The broken ice machine lid in the Chicago hotel, for example, remained unfixed even after inspectors returned. This lackadaisical response to serious health concerns reflects a disturbing disregard for public safety and well-being.

In Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The health violations at Trump’s properties are more than just regulatory missteps; they are a glaring testament to a systemic disregard for standards, safety, and decency. As patrons and the public, we must demand better, refusing to turn a blind eye to the shortcomings of those in positions of power and influence. It’s high time that the Trump brand, and others like it, are held to account, not just by health inspectors but by consumers and citizens who expect and deserve far better.
