Moral Infallibility and the Authoritarian Mind

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJanuary 27, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

One of the most persistent mistakes liberals, technocrats, and good‑faith analysts make when confronting authoritarian movements is assuming that the other side is arguing from evidence. They are not. What we are witnessing instead is a reversal of moral reasoning so complete that it deserves to be named plainly: conclusion‑first ethics.

The authoritarian does not ask, “What is the right thing to do?” and then act accordingly. They begin with a fixed axiom — “I am a virtuous, righteous person” — and then retrofit reality to protect that self‑image. Once virtue is assumed as an identity trait rather than an ongoing obligation, the moral system collapses into something both rigid and hollow.

This is not a bug. It is the operating system.

Virtue as an Axiom, Not a Question

In a functioning moral framework, virtue is provisional. It is something you attempt, fail at, reassess, and attempt again. It requires doubt. It requires the possibility of error.

Authoritarian moral logic eliminates that uncertainty at the start.

If I am good by definition, then:

  • my intentions are inherently pure,

  • my actions cannot be cruel — only “necessary,”

  • any harm I cause must be illusory, exaggerated, or deserved.

From this point forward, evidence becomes decorative. Facts are no longer inputs into judgment; they are obstacles to be cleared or tools to be selectively deployed. Reality is not evaluated — it is managed.

This is why contradictions do not bother them. Contradictions only exist if you believe truth is external to identity. Once morality is internalized as self‑concept, inconsistency ceases to be a problem.

Reverse Moral Engineering

Watch the process closely and the pattern becomes obvious:

  1. Conclusion: “I am righteous. My side represents order, civilization, and decency.”

  2. Selection: Facts that support this narrative are elevated; those that don’t are dismissed as propaganda.

  3. Reframing: Harmful outcomes are relabeled as side effects, necessities, or fabrications.

  4. Projection: Accusations are redirected outward — often toward those calling attention to the harm.

This is not reasoning. It is post‑hoc rationalization with a moral alibi.

The authoritarian is not lying to convince you. They are lying to preserve internal coherence. That distinction matters.

Cruelty as Maintenance, Not Violence

Once moral infallibility is established, cruelty must be laundered. The easiest way to do that is to redefine it.

Cruelty is no longer cruelty; it is:

  • “law enforcement,”

  • “border security,”

  • “restoring order,”

  • “hard choices forced upon us.”

Language does the work that conscience refuses to do.

The victims disappear into abstraction. Systems replace people. Outcomes are discussed without agents. Agency dissolves into inevitability.

This is how authoritarian movements normalize behavior that would otherwise provoke moral revulsion — not by denying the acts themselves, but by stripping them of ethical weight.

Victimhood as a Force Multiplier

Authoritarian moral logic becomes truly dangerous when it fuses with grievance.

If I am virtuous and oppressed, then any restraint placed on me becomes proof of tyranny. Any opposition becomes persecution. Any demand for accountability becomes an attack.

This is why trivial inconveniences are inflated into existential threats, while real, material suffering inflicted on others barely registers.

Victimhood is not experienced as a call for empathy. It is experienced as permission.

Permission to escalate.
Permission to retaliate.
Permission to abandon restraint.

Loyalty Replaces Ethics

At this stage, morality no longer concerns actions or outcomes. It concerns alignment.

  • If we do it, it is justified.

  • If they do it, it is evil.

  • If the same action occurs in both cases, context magically absolves one and condemns the other.

This is why authoritarian movements can denounce repression abroad while celebrating it at home. The contradiction is not accidental. It is structural.

Ethics collapses into tribal loyalty. The question is no longer “Is this wrong?” but “Who is doing it?”

Why Evidence Doesn’t Break the Spell

This is the part many observers still struggle to accept: no amount of evidence will fix this.

You cannot reason someone out of a position they did not reason themselves into. The belief that they are righteous is not a conclusion — it is a psychological foundation.

To abandon it would require:

  • admitting harm,

  • accepting moral fallibility,

  • acknowledging complicity.

For many, that cost is unbearable.

So the system hardens. The narrative tightens. Dissent becomes treason. Empathy becomes weakness. Reality becomes optional.

This Is Not New

History is unambiguous on this point.

The architects and foot soldiers of authoritarian systems did not see themselves as villains. They saw themselves as defenders of order, protectors of the nation, guardians against chaos.

The greatest atrocities of the modern era were not committed by people who thought they were evil — but by people who were certain they were right.

Moral certainty, when decoupled from accountability, is not virtue.

It is a weapon.

The Real Threat

The danger is not that these movements are immoral by their own standards.

The danger is that their standards are closed systems — immune to evidence, insulated from consequence, and allergic to self‑reflection.

When goodness becomes an identity rather than a practice, it no longer restrains behavior.

It licenses it.

And once a society accepts that some people are righteous by definition, it has already surrendered the ethical ground necessary to stop what comes next.
