GOP turn on Trump as they highlight issue president 'can't talk his way out of'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Republican Party’s Trump Conundrum: A Crisis Beyond ‘Happy Talk’

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers an address to the nation from the Diplomatic Reception Room of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 17, 2025. Doug Mills/Pool via REUTERS

The Republican Party is turning on Donald Trump once more after the president’s national address.

A GOP insider believes there is one issue Trump “can’t happy talk his way out of” as political analysts highlight the woeful cost of living crisis. The unnamed insider, speaking to CNN, is said to be advising on several midterm races and the cost of living crisis will likely be a hot topic for voters next year.

They said, “The president can’t happy talk his way out of what people feel. The White House staff around the president know what the message needs to be, and I wish that the president would listen.”

Trump has said numerous times the affordability crisis is a “hoax” but GOP insiders are urging the president to reconsider his position on the economy. A Republican Party member who served in the first Trump administration told Politico the “execution was abysmal” for the president’s comments in the national address.

They said, “It’s the right idea to talk about the economy more, but the execution was abysmal. He’s a very effective salesman when his heart is in it or when he’s on the attack. But the ‘I feel your pain’ speech — he just doesn’t have that club in his bag.”

A Trump adviser has since suggested it will “take some time to turn things around” with the economy, and that the White House are doing their best to keep the president focused.

They said, “They’re trying to keep him focused, and they’re trying to get back on message. It’s going to take some time to turn things around. … But we’ve got to get the advantage back on affordability issues.”

Trump was roundly criticised for his national address, with even the MAGA faithful considering the speech a waste of time. Other political commentators from across the political spectrum suggested Trump had lost the magic that made him president.

Ex-GOP strategist Rick Wilson suggested the speech was more of a “deep scream of outright panic” from the president than anything else.

He wrote, “His old, hypnotic MAGA mojo was gone, leaving only a desperate, ranting old man trying for one last time to inspire the old magic. It failed, utterly.”

In a video to go along with the Substack piece, Wilson added, “He can’t figure out how to pull himself out of the polling slump where he now has an approval rating of 38%, that is a screaming, flaming, white hot stinking disaster. In the space of a 20-minute scream-a-thon, every single one of the greatest hits of 2024, threw it against the wall. Guess what? None of it was convincing. Not a single bit of it was selling.”

Our Analysis:

The Republican Party’s Trump Conundrum: A Crisis Beyond ‘Happy Talk’

In a stunning yet unsurprising twist, the Republican Party finds itself at odds with Donald Trump, yet again. This time, the discord stems from Trump’s national address, which has sparked criticism from within his own ranks, highlighting a growing chasm between the former president’s perception and the harsh reality of the nation’s cost of living crisis. An unnamed GOP insider, candid enough to air grievances yet too cautious to come forward, has pinpointed the issue Trump “can’t happy talk his way out of,” according to a report by CNN. This sentiment underscores a fundamental disconnect not just between Trump and reality, but also within the party that once rallied unequivocally behind him.

The Illusion of the Affordability ‘Hoax’

Trump’s characterization of the affordability crisis as a “hoax” is not just dangerously out of touch; it’s a glaring testament to his inability—or unwillingness—to grasp the tangible struggles plaguing Americans. While GOP insiders urge a reevaluation of his stance, the question arises: can a leader who has repeatedly shown a penchant for denial over confrontation genuinely pivot to address the economic woes of the nation?

Execution or Evasion?

Critics from within Trump’s own administration, such as a Republican Party member quoted by Politico, have lambasted the “abysmal” execution of his national address. The critique doesn’t merely highlight a failed attempt at empathy but rather exposes a broader issue: Trump’s effectiveness, or lack thereof, when pulled away from his comfort zone of attacks and salesmanship into the realm of genuine connection and understanding. This inability to effectively communicate on critical economic issues reveals a leader disconnected not just from the public’s struggles but from the very essence of leadership itself.

A Desperate Attempt to Regain the ‘MAGA Mojo’

Rick Wilson’s scathing analysis of Trump’s address, describing it as a “deep scream of outright panic,” encapsulates the desperation seeping through the cracks of Trump’s facade. The attempt to rekindle the MAGA magic, as Wilson points out, fell flat, leaving behind a trail of disillusionment and highlighting a profound crisis of confidence. Trump’s plummeting approval ratings, underscored by Wilson, are not just numbers; they are a resounding rejection of his inability to address the pressing issues facing Americans.

The Path Forward: Affordability and Accountability

As Trump’s advisers scramble to refocus the narrative on the economy, the challenge remains monumental. The task is not just about turning around an economy; it’s about turning around a presidency marred by denial, disconnection, and disarray. The GOP’s internal strife over Trump’s handling of the cost of living crisis is more than a political skirmish; it’s a stark reminder of the consequences of leadership that prioritizes rhetoric over reality, division over unity, and personal vendettas over public service.

In conclusion, the Republican Party’s latest fallout with Trump over his national address and his dismissive stance on the affordability crisis is a telling episode in the ongoing saga of Trump’s tumultuous relationship with both his party and reality. As the GOP grapples with the ramifications of Trump’s rhetoric and its disconnect from the economic realities facing Americans, the need for a leadership that is grounded in empathy, understanding, and action has never been more apparent.
