Former Donald Trump attorney Alina Habba is all but certain to be headed for the door next week leaving behind portraits of herself on the walls and disgruntled staffers after her brief stint as a U.S. attorney comes to an end.

According to a report from the New York Times, her appointment as the U.S. attorney for the District of New Jersey on an interim basis began in "chaos" and is now drawing to a close, with employees looking forward to her departure while also considering leaving too.

Habba's appointment as a the U.S. attorney was derailed by both of New Jersey's Democratic Senators Cory Booker and Andy Kim, limiting her to a 120-day term in office.

According to the Times, "Her tenure has also shattered morale inside the U.S. attorney’s office and left many prosecutors looking for a way out, according to 16 close observers of the office," adding that she has infuriated career prosecutors by interjecting herself into their cases and keeping them out of the loop.

Case in point, the Times' Jonah E. Bromwich and Tracey Tully wrote Habba "granted a rare private meeting" with attorneys for Eliyahu Weinstein who was facing a 24-year sentence for investment fraud without inviting the attorneys handling the case.

The report notes, "The episode left members of her staff infuriated."

"Prosecutors have chafed at her availability to defense lawyers. She disbanded the office’s Civil Rights Division and killed the office’s longest-running prosecution just days before it was scheduled to go to trial.," the Times is reporting before adding, "Three framed pictures of herself now hang in a conference room named for a legendary New Jersey crime fighter, Frederick B. Lacey."

Habba's future in the office now lies with the state’s district court judges, who have the ability to extend her time in office which is scheduled to end on Tuesday.

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Alina Habba's Tenure: A Scathing Review

The saga of Alina Habba, former attorney for Donald Trump, in her role as the U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey, reads like a cautionary tale of how not to manage a vital federal office. Her brief stint, marred by controversy and chaos, is a textbook example of the dangers of political appointments devoid of merit or foresight. Let's dissect the cascade of missteps and the palpable relief at her imminent departure.

Chaos from the Start

Habba's tenure began in chaos and is ending in what can only be described as a relief for many. Described by the New York Times as a period that has “shattered morale” and sent prosecutors scurrying for the exits, it's evident that her leadership—or lack thereof—has been nothing short of disastrous. 16 close observers of the office have voiced their concerns, painting a picture of an environment where professionalism and protocol took a backseat to whimsy and caprice.

A Controversial Appointment

Habba's appointment, curtailed by Democratic Senators Cory Booker and Andy Kim to a mere 120 days, was contentious from the outset. The opposition from New Jersey's senators underscores a critical failure in the appointment process, questioning the suitability of Habba for the role. The limitation of her term speaks volumes about the lack of confidence in her capability to lead.

Misguided Priorities and Unprofessional Conduct

The report details how Habba infuriated career prosecutors by interjecting herself into cases and excluding those who were handling them. A glaring example of this was granting a rare private meeting with attorneys for Eliyahu Weinstein, a figure facing a substantial sentence for investment fraud, without the inclusion of the case's attorneys. Such actions not only undermine the integrity of the prosecutorial process but also betray a gross misunderstanding of the role's responsibilities.

Moreover, Habba's decision to disband the office’s Civil Rights Division and terminate the office's longest-running prosecution is not just questionable; it's a direct assault on the values and duties the office is supposed to uphold. These actions reflect a disregard for civil rights and a misalignment with the office's mission to pursue justice.

Vanity Over Virtue

Perhaps the most visually symbolic misstep is the hanging of three framed pictures of herself in a conference room named after Frederick B. Lacey, a legendary New Jersey crime fighter. This act of vanity is emblematic of her tenure: prioritizing self-image over the substantive work and legacy of the office she was meant to lead.

The Future of the Office

As her time in office draws to a close, with the possibility of an extension hanging by a thread, the damage wrought during her brief tenure will likely take time to repair. The next leader of the U.S. Attorney’s office for the District of New Jersey faces a daunting task: to restore morale, reestablish integrity, and rebuild what has been dismantled.

Conclusion

Alina Habba's tenure as the U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey is a stark reminder of the importance of appointing individuals based on merit, experience, and alignment with the office's foundational values. The relief at her departure is a testament to the turmoil her leadership has caused, underscoring the need for a return to professionalism, respect for the rule of law, and a commitment to justice above all.
