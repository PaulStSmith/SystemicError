'Gives up the game': Trump administration reportedly exposed flaw in tariff plan

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 2, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

The over-the-top reaction by the Donald Trump administration to a report that Amazon would show the increase in cost on goods affected by his tariffs effectively revealed that they know his trade war is grossly unpopular.

On Tuesday there was a back-and-forth squabble between the administration and Amazon before the company backed down, reportedly after Trump made a call to billionaire founder Jeff Bezos.

On MSNBC's "Morning Joe," the panel laughed at comments made by White House spokesperson Karoline Leavitt that the popular shopping website was engaging in a "hostile and political act by Amazon," which co-host Willie Geist jokingly characterized as being a "bunch of left-wing commies."

That led MSNBC regular Richard Haas to laugh and add, "Yes, it is capitalism, which I usually associate with the west. This was, as one Wall Street firm calls it, radical transparency and the White House probably could have pushed back and said, 'Well, they're listing the immediate costs, they're not talking about the long-term benefits.' But I thought it was really interesting."

Co-host Jonathan Lemire interjected, "We know that Bezos has really cozied up to him in the last handful of months; was there for the inauguration and... even after the first term, drew Trump's ire quite often because of his stewardship of the Washington Post that is no longer the case."

Turning to conservative guest David French, he noted, "Talk to us about that phenomenon but also just the fact that the White House got so upset about this. It gives up the game here. They know that these tariffs are going to hurt American consumers and be really unpopular and they don't want that in-your-face reminder of that."

"Yeah," French laughed. "They absolutely don't want that reminder. They want some sort of deniability with their base that price increases have anything to do with the tariffs and they want the base to think that price increases are going to be due to corporate greed."

Our Analysis:

The Thin-Skinned Administration: Trump's Tariff Tiff With Amazon

The article presents a vivid tableau of the Trump administration's theatrical response to Amazon's decision to highlight the increase in costs on goods directly impacted by Trump's tariffs. This episode, more than a mere squabble, underscores a fundamental truth about the Trump administration: their acute awareness of the trade war's unpopularity among the American public.

The Farce of Transparency

The administration's reaction to Amazon's move towards what one might call "radical transparency" is both comical and revealing. To label Amazon's decision as a "hostile and political act" is not only hyperbolic but also inadvertently comedic, particularly coming from a White House that has often been criticized for its own hostile and political acts. It's akin to a pot calling the kettle black, but the pot is also trying to start a trade war.

Karoline Leavitt's assertion, parroted without a hint of irony on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," that Amazon was acting like "a bunch of left-wing commies" for simply disclosing the direct impact of tariffs on prices, is a masterclass in Orwellian doublespeak. It's as if the administration believes in capitalism and free market principles only when they cloak the consequences of their own policies.

The Reality of Tariffs

Let's be clear: tariffs are taxes on imports that are typically paid by the company importing the goods, which then often passes these costs onto consumers. This is Economics 101. The Trump administration's attempt to obfuscate this fact, and their irritation with Amazon for pulling back the curtain, is nothing short of desperate.

David French's commentary hits the nail on the head. The Trump administration doesn't just fear the economic backlash of their trade policies; they fear the political repercussions. They want to maintain a facade of economic nationalism without having to confront the tangible consequences of such policies — higher prices for American consumers. This episode with Amazon is emblematic of the administration's broader approach to governance: deny, deflect, and demagogue.

The Trump-Bezos Saga

The article also touches on the evolving relationship between Trump and Jeff Bezos, which is a subplot worth noting. Initially, Trump's animosity towards Bezos was palpable, largely due to Bezos' ownership of the Washington Post, a publication that has been critical of Trump's presidency. However, the mention of Bezos "cozying up" to Trump in recent months, and the subsequent phone call that reportedly led Amazon to back down, paints a picture of political maneuvering and influence that should concern anyone who values democracy and the free market.

Conclusion

This episode is a microcosm of the broader issues plaguing the Trump administration: a disregard for the truth, a sensitivity to criticism, and a penchant for economic policies that sound good in a tweet but have deleterious effects on the very people they claim to champion. Amazon's attempt at transparency was not a political act; it was a rare moment of clarity in an era increasingly defined by obfuscation and bluster. The administration's over-the-top reaction is not just a sign of their awareness of the trade war's unpopularity; it's a testament to their fear of being held accountable for their actions.

In sum, the Trump administration’s handling of this situation is not just an overreaction; it's a strategic blunder that highlights their vulnerability on economic issues. The laughable attempt to cast Amazon as the villain in this scenario is a stark reminder of this administration's discomfort with the truth, especially when it reveals the flaws in their own policies.
