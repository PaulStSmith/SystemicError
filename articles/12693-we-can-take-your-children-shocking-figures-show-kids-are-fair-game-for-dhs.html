‘We can take your children’: Shocking figures show kids are 'fair game' for DHS

U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem holds a press conference, in Los Angeles, California, U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Aude Guerrucci

President Donald Trump's Department of Homeland Security is ripping all the guardrails off of the immigration system when it comes to children, The Atlantic reported in a deep analysis published Wednesday.

This follows the Biden administration's effort to put protections in place, following the family separation scandal that took place under the first Trump administration.

"Six months into Trump’s second term, children are once again fair game, according to dozens of lawyers, advocates, shelter operators, case managers, and others I spoke with in recent weeks," reported Stephanie McCrummen. "More systematically than in his first term, Trump’s administration is reaching into the federal immigration bureaucracy to roll back an array of protections for undocumented children, not only recent arrivals but also those who have only ever known life in this country. More and more, children are being picked up on family vacations, at traffic stops, and at worksites, and winding up in detention."

The numbers are staggering, according to the report.

"At least 150 children have been sent to a newly reopened Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention facility in Dilley, Texas, whose staff sometimes refer to them as 'inmates,' according to two lawyers who visited recently. Another 2,400 children are currently stranded in the [Office of Refugee Resettlement] shelter system, a situation becoming more distressing to families by the day."

Andrew Rankin, who practices immigration law in Memphis, had a blunt assessment: “The message is ‘We can take your children.’ The message is ‘We have the power.’ They want to scare the daylights out of people.”

The Trump administration has sought to put mass deportations into high gear, with the result that many people have faced little to no due process at all. The summary removal of Maryland father Kilmar Abrego Garcia to a Salvadoran megaprison where he reported being tortured and abused, despite court orders preventing his removal to that country, are an ongoing matter of litigation.

Our Analysis:

Systematic Erosion of Child Protections in Immigration: A Critical Analysis

The latest report from The Atlantic unveils a disturbing acceleration in the Trump administration's tactics concerning immigration, particularly with the handling of children—a demographic supposedly shielded by numerous legal and ethical safeguards. This marks a sinister regression into policies that not only mirror but, according to the report, exceed the cruelty of the administration's previous tenure. Let's dissect this with the precision of a scalpel, for the devil—and indeed the systemic error—is in the details.

"Ripping all the guardrails off of the immigration system when it comes to children"—this phrase alone should send chills down the spine of any reader with even a shred of conscience. Following the Biden administration's efforts to mend the scars left by the family separation scandal, Trump's DHS appears not just unrepentant but aggressively expansionist in its approach to undocumented children.

The Systematic Assault on Child Protections

Stephanie McCrummen's reporting highlights a systematic dismantlement of protections for undocumented children. The term 'systematic' is crucial here; it suggests not a series of unfortunate events but a deliberate, methodical strategy to terrorize the most vulnerable segment of the immigrant population. Children, as young as they are, are being targeted with increasing impunity—snatched during routine family activities, and thrust into a labyrinthine detention system that, for many, has no clear exit.

The Dilley Detention Facility: A Symptom of a Larger Malaise

The re-opening of the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facility in Dilley, Texas, where staff allegedly refer to child detainees as 'inmates', is emblematic of a broader dehumanization at play. To refer to children as such is not just a matter of semantics; it reflects a chilling indifference to the trauma and psychological damage inflicted by detention. That at least 150 children have been sent to this facility is a stark indicator of an administration that views cruelty as a deterrent.

The ORR Shelter System: A Holding Pattern of Despair

The plight of the 2,400 children stranded in the Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR) shelter system is a bureaucratic quagmire that families navigate with dwindling hope. The system, ostensibly designed for the welfare and eventual resettlement of refugees, has morphed into a purgatory where children are kept in limbo, exacerbating the distress of separated families.

The Message: Fear as a Tool of Governance

Andrew Rankin's blunt assessment encapsulates the administration's overarching message: "We can take your children." This is governance by fear, a tactic as old as it is effective. It is a clear signal that the administration is willing to leverage the well-being of children as collateral in its crusade against undocumented immigration. The summary removals and the case of Maryland father Kilmar Abrego Garcia—sent back to face torture despite court orders—underscore a brazen disregard for due process.

Conclusion: The Unmaking of America's Moral Fabric

The Trump administration's policies on immigration, particularly concerning children, are not just systemic errors; they are moral failures. The deliberate targeting of vulnerable children as a means to instill fear among undocumented communities is a tactic that undermines the very foundations of justice and human decency that the United States purports to stand for. As the administration pushes its mass deportation agenda into high gear, one must ask: at what cost? The erosion of America's moral standing seems a price too steep.

In this dark chapter of America's immigration saga, the true test will be whether the collective conscience of its people can rally to restore compassion and legality to a system now marked by its absence. The implications of these policies extend far beyond the immediate horror they inflict; they will define the legacy of a nation that once prided itself as a beacon of hope and freedom for the oppressed.
