'His bunkie is screaming': Epstein bombshell gives new details of supposed suicide attempt

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20255 min read

New details on Jeffrey Epstein’s supposed suicide attempt inside a Manhattan jail were revealed Monday after a batch of confidential documents were obtained and reported on by CBS News.

Epstein was arrested in July of 2019 on sex-trafficking charges, but would never stand trial after being found dead in his cell on Aug. 10, 2019 in what was ultimately ruled a suicide. His death came just weeks after another apparent suicide attempt in the early morning hours of July 23 in an incident Epstein initially alleged was an attempt on his life by his cellmate.

“[Epstein] is laying on the floor and his bunkie is screaming: 'I did nothing, I banged on my door to get him out of my cell,’” said one source close to the investigation, speaking with CBS News on the condition of anonymity.

Epstein’s cellmate at the time was Nicholas Tartaglione, a former law enforcement officer who was facing charges for four murders over a drug deal gone bad. Tartaglione has repeatedly denied that he tried to kill Epstein, but details in a corrections memo exclusively obtained by CBS News suggest that Epstein feared Tartaglione well before the July 23 incident.

“Epstein expressed concern about his cellmate the day before the incident, according to a corrections memo and a source who agreed to speak with CBS News on the condition they not be identified,” the report from CBS News reads.

“Epstein claimed to both the corrections officer and the source that he felt threatened by Tartaglione, a hulking retired cop-turned-drug-dealer, who was charged and later convicted for four murders.”

According to the memo, Epstein had feared Tartaglione long before making his concerns known to correctional officers, but had declined to voice them out of fear of retaliation, telling officers that “his bunkie told him that if he beat him up because of [Epstein’s child sex-trafficking] charges, the officers would not report it,” according to the memo.

Epstein told correctional officers that he had received violent threats from Tartaglione “for a week” leading up to the July 23 incident, and that he believed Tartaglione was trying “to extort” him.

The memo also detailed Tartaglione’s account of what happened that night.

“Jeff, what [are you] doing?” Tartaglione asked Epstein in the early morning hours of July 23, according to a separate memo.

Tartaglione told correctional officers that he, sleeping on a mattress on the floor beside Epstein, who had taken the bottom mattress in a bunk bed, felt something hit his legs. After turning on the light, Tartaglione told officers he observed Epstein sitting slumped on the floor “leaning to the side with his eyes opened” and with fabric around his neck, though unresponsive."

Epstein would go on to be placed on suicide watch the following day, on July 24, but taken off suicide watch on July 29, just five days before his death. In the days leading up to his death, the internal memos also reveal that Epstein was feeling optimistic for his future, telling correctional officers he was “too much of a coward” to kill himself, and that he “denied feeling hopeless” and was “reporting positive future plans and reasons to live for.”

Our Analysis:

The Enigma of Epstein's Demise: A Scathing Analysis

In a recent revelation by CBS News, the already murky waters surrounding Jeffrey Epstein's supposed suicide attempt and subsequent death have been further muddied by a batch of confidential documents. These documents shed light on the events leading up to the financier's death in a Manhattan jail in August 2019, a death that snuffed out the possibility of a trial for his sex-trafficking charges. Let's dissect the glaring inconsistencies and questionable actions that make this narrative not just suspicious but a damning indictment of the carceral system's failures.

The Prelude to a Controversy

Epstein's death was ruled a suicide, yet it occurred under circumstances so bizarre, they would be rejected by a Hollywood scriptwriter for being too far-fetched. This is a man who, mere weeks before his death, allegedly survived an attempt on his life by his cellmate, Nicholas Tartaglione, a former law enforcement officer turned accused murderer. This incident alone should have ringed alarm bells loud enough to warrant an unyielding watch over Epstein. Instead, what followed was a series of actions so negligent, they border on the criminally complicit.

The Cellmate: Tartaglione, a man of considerable physical prowess, charged with four murders, was Epstein's cellmate. This pairing raises the first red flag. Epstein, fearing for his life, expressed concerns about Tartaglione, concerns that were apparently disregarded until it was too late. Epstein's fear of retaliation for voicing his apprehensions paints a grim picture of the environment within the Manhattan jail, a place where the accused feel safer in silence than seeking protection.

A System Set to Fail

Suicide Watch, or Lack Thereof: Epstein was placed on suicide watch after the July 23 incident, only to be removed five days later. The rapid removal from suicide watch of an individual who had just allegedly attempted to take his own life—or was attacked, depending on the perspective—is not just a failure; it's a systemic malfunction. It's as if the jail authorities were playing a macabre game of Russian roulette with Epstein's life.

Optimism Before Death: Adding to the perplexity are memos revealing Epstein's optimism for the future, his claims of being "too much of a coward" to end his own life, and his apparent anticipation of positive future plans. For someone supposedly at the brink of suicide, this sudden shift to positivity and forward-looking plans is not just puzzling; it contradicts the very essence of a person resolved to die.

The Inescapable Conclusion

The negligence displayed in the lead-up to Epstein's death is not merely a series of unfortunate oversights; it's indicative of a justice system riddled with systemic errors and a blatant disregard for the safety and well-being of those within its walls. The confidential documents obtained by CBS News do not provide closure; they open a Pandora's box of questions regarding negligence, potential complicity, and the murky depths to which those with power can sink to protect their interests.

In the final analysis, Jeffrey Epstein's death illuminates the dark underbelly of a carceral system that fails not just the vulnerable and the victimized but seemingly also those with the means and the motive to reveal the sins of the powerful. This isn't just about Epstein; it's about every individual who's been failed by a system that's supposed to protect and serve. The story of Epstein's death is a glaring testament to the urgent need for systemic reform—a need that grows more evident and more pressing with each passing revelation.
