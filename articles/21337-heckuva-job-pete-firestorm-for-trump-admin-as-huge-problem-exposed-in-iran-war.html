'Heckuva job Pete!' Firestorm for Trump admin as 'huge problem' exposed in Iran war

The Presidency Burned Through the Arsenal

A war of choice, not a mystery

Reuters reports that the Army has burned through nearly all of its long-range precision surface-to-surface missiles after five months of war with Iran, including ATACMS and the newer Precision Strike Missiles. The immediate story is depletion and readiness. The actual story is political: the Trump regime chose to spend scarce strategic weapons in a war that, by the source’s own framing, has produced no meaningful gain.

Power sits at the top

This did not happen because the Pentagon got surprised by demand curves. It happened because the White House decided to use high-end munitions as if they were limitless. The president is the one insisting the U.S. has more weapons than anyone else. That line is not reassurance; it is cover. When a government treats precision strike capacity as a political prop, the people who pay are the services that have to keep fighting after the headline war is over.

Readiness as collateral damage

The article gestures at “concerns about military readiness,” but that phrasing softens the damage. These missiles matter for Ukraine and for any future conflict with China, yet they were consumed in a five-month campaign in Iran and then backfilled from other military stocks. That is not prudent inventory management. It is strategic cannibalization. The system protected the president’s war posture by borrowing against tomorrow’s wars.

The blame is being pushed downward

The ridicule in the source lands on Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and the administration’s “grand strategy,” but that is still too shallow if it stops at personnel snark. A secretary can be incompetent. Only a president and his war-making apparatus can authorize the spending of a finite arsenal on a conflict that, as one quoted analyst put it, yielded “nothing.” The article’s frame risks turning deliberate political damage into a competence story. It is not mainly competence. It is choice.

What this pattern reveals

The pattern is familiar: extract maximum force, ignore long-term capacity, declare victory in the absence of results, and leave the institutional cleanup to everyone else. Precision munitions are expensive, scarce, and strategic. Using them up for theater is what happens when a government confuses action with leverage. The cost is not just a hollowed-out stockpile. It is a further demonstration that this administration treats national power as something to burn for short-term bravado rather than preserve for actual defense.

The deeper indictment

The real scandal is not that the U.S. has fewer missiles than the president claims. It is that the country can still be pushed into a war that exhausts strategic assets while producing no gain, and then be told this is strength. That is not military seriousness. It is political vandalism dressed up as command.
