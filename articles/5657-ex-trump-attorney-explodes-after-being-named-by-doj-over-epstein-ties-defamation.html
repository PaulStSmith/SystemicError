Ex-Trump attorney explodes after being named by DOJ over Epstein ties: ‘Defamation!’

Alan Dershowitz, former attorney for both President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein, erupted Friday on Newsmax over what he said was a Justice Department document listing him as a “prominent” figure accused by Epstein’s victims.

“I want you to look at this paper that has been reproduced and sent by the Justice Department – it’s called prominent names, and it’s a list of prominent people who have been accused. Every single name of every accuser has been blacked out!” Dershowitz said, holding up the document.

“So, for example, let’s turn to me: it says ‘blank blank’ stated she gave him a massage on Epstein’s plane – parenthesis, ‘not a minor.’ I was never on an Epstein plane with a young woman, I never got a massage! This is total defamation, a total lie, but I can’t disprove it because they won’t give me her name!”

Dershowitz is among several prominent figures linked to Epstein who have faced allegations, including a claim by Virginia Giuffre that he raped her when she was 17, an allegation he has fiercely denied. Giuffre sued Dershowitz over the allegations, prompting Dershowitz to counter-sue before both parties settled in 2022. Giuffre would later die by apparent suicide.

An animated Dershowitz continued pleading his innocence, despite Newsmax host Bianca de la Garza trying – and failing – to interject. He also championed the recent news that Bill and Hillary Clinton had agreed to testify before the House Oversight Committee, and asked that he be considered to be called before the committee as well.

“I’m delighted that we’re gonna see public hearings; let them call me to the public hearings, I’ll tell them the truth about what happened!” Dershowitz continued. “I won’t claim any privileges, I won’t claim any inability to remember – I remember everything! And I know a great deal about it!”

No evidence exists to contradict Dershowitz’s claim that he had never flown on Epstein’s plane; however, his claim that he “never got a massage” stands at direct odds with remarks he made in 2019.

“I kept my underwear on during the massage,” Dershowitz said, admitting to having received a massage by an adult woman at Epstein’s New York mansion. “I don’t like massages particularly.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Dershowitz’s Dance with Defamation

In a recent spectacle on Newsmax, Alan Dershowitz—a man whose legal career has placed him in the orbit of some of the most controversial figures of our time, including President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein—delivered a performance rife with indignation and desperation. The subject? A Justice Department document that dared to list him as a “prominent” figure accused by Epstein’s victims, with all the accusers’ names conveniently redacted.

“Every single name of every accuser has been blacked out!” Dershowitz proclaimed, brandishing the document as if it were the very embodiment of his perceived martyrdom. He then launched into a tale of how this document falsely accused him of receiving a massage on Epstein’s plane—a claim he vehemently denies.

The Irony of Dershowitz’s Indignation

Let’s dissect the irony here. Dershowitz’s outrage hinges on his inability to disprove allegations due to the anonymity of the accusers detailed in the document. Yet, this is the same man who, in 2019, admitted to receiving a massage at Epstein’s mansion, albeit with the caveat that he “kept [his] underwear on.” This admission starkly contrasts his vehement denial on Newsmax and highlights a troubling pattern of selective memory and convenient narrative crafting.

The Rape Allegation and Its Aftermath

More disturbing is the rape allegation made by Virginia Giuffre when she was 17—a claim Dershowitz has fiercely denied. The lawsuit and countersuit that ensued between Giuffre and Dershowitz settled in 2022, only for Giuffre to later meet a tragic end by apparent suicide. This entire saga casts a long, dark shadow over Dershowitz’s vehement proclamations of innocence and his plea for public hearings to clear his name.

A Call for Transparency and Accountability

Dershowitz’s plea to be called before the House Oversight Committee to “tell them the truth about what happened” reeks of a desperate attempt to control the narrative under the guise of transparency. However, transparency is not selectively recalling events that align with one’s innocence while dismissing or contradicting previous admissions that suggest otherwise.

Conclusion: The Dershowitz Dilemma

In the grand scheme of things, Alan Dershowitz’s outburst on Newsmax is more than just a man vehemently denying allegations against him. It is a stark representation of the power dynamics at play, where prominent figures entangled in scandalous affairs attempt to manipulate public perception and legal outcomes in their favor.

The defamation Dershowitz cries foul over is not merely in the allegations themselves but in the inconsistency of his own narrative and the selective outrage displayed when the spotlight of accountability shines too brightly in his direction. It’s a classic case of protesting too much, perhaps, or at the very least, a demonstration of how the mighty can attempt to rewrite the narratives that threaten their legacy.

In the end, the court of public opinion is left to sift through the theatrics and the facts, a task made all the more daunting by the performers who master the art of obfuscation and deflection. But let’s not be fooled by the smoke and mirrors; the truth, as it often does, resides somewhere in the tangled web of what is said, what is unsaid, and what is conveniently forgotten.
