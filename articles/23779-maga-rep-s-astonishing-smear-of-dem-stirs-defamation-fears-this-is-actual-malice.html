MAGA rep's astonishing smear of Dem stirs defamation fears: 'This is actual malice'

The Smear Is the Strategy

A Lawmaker and a Television Network

The essential fact is not that Representative Anna Paulina Luna became “confused” about Abdul El-Sayed’s record or Hasan Piker’s rhetoric. It is that a federal officeholder used Fox News to attach two inflammatory falsehoods—Islamic rule and sexual violence—to political opponents.

Luna holds congressional power. Fox controls a national broadcast platform. El-Sayed, the candidate being targeted, and Piker, the activist linked to him, were cast as menacing outsiders. That hierarchy matters more than the ensuing social-media outrage.

Islamophobia as Electoral Machinery

Luna claimed El-Sayed had advocated for Sharia law. The evidence described in the reporting shows the opposite: he criticized a measure barring courts from considering Sharia because he viewed it as part of an effort to uproot Muslim Americans, and he has explicitly opposed incorporating religious law into American law.

Transforming that position into support for theocracy is not ordinary political distortion. It is an Islamophobic maneuver designed to make a Muslim candidate appear incompatible with the country he seeks to represent. The accusation does its work by treating Muslim identity itself as evidence of concealed allegiance.

Guilt by Proximity

The attack on Piker supplies the second half of the construction. Luna attributed to him a position the source says he did not advocate: the invasion and rape of American women. She then used his campaigning with El-Sayed to transfer that invented depravity onto the candidate.

This is guilt by association built from a fabricated premise. Piker’s notorious 2019 statement about September 11 and American foreign policy may be fair material for scrutiny. It does not substantiate Luna’s claim about mass sexual assault. Substituting one controversy for proof of an unrelated accusation is propaganda, not argument.

The Article Shrinks a Power Abuse Into a Lawsuit Story

The source correctly identifies both claims as false, but its framing drifts toward whether Luna might be sued. Quotations calling the remarks defamatory, insane, or actionable occupy space that should be devoted to the institutional mechanism: an elected official delivered incendiary disinformation through a friendly television program during a Senate campaign.

Defamation law may offer a remedy, but potential litigation is not the political meaning of the event. The injury is already broader than reputational damage. The smear tells voters that a Muslim candidate represents foreign religious conquest and that his allies threaten sexual violence. Even a successful lawsuit would not retract those associations from every audience that absorbed them.

Cruelty With a Constituency

Luna’s language fused Islamophobia, sexual panic, and partisan demonization into a single electoral message. Its purpose can reasonably be inferred from its construction: make cooperation with El-Sayed seem not merely undesirable but morally contaminating and physically dangerous.

That is how authoritarian political rhetoric narrows legitimate participation. Opponents are not presented as citizens with competing policies; they are recast as alien infiltrators and accomplices to barbarism. Once that premise is established, exclusion begins to look like self-defense.

The System Rewards the Lie

The larger pattern is an asymmetry of power disguised as controversy. A member of Congress and a major network can inject a false accusation into national politics in seconds. The targets must then spend their time disproving claims that never had evidence behind them, while coverage converts the abuse into a noisy exchange among partisan personalities.

That is not a malfunction in the politics of scapegoating. It is the operating model: powerful actors manufacture danger, weaker actors inherit the burden of rebuttal, and institutions describe the deliberate smear as another dispute. The lie travels as politics; the correction arrives as cleanup.
