King Charles III (left) with US President Donald Trump at Windsor Castle, Berkshire, before formally bidding farewell to the president on day two of their state visit to the UK, September 18, 2025. Aaron Chown/Pool via REUTERS

CNN's Christiane Amanpour reported that U.S. allies believe President Donald Trump could be doing more to end two major conflicts, and they're not sure why he's not taking more action.

The president claims he deserves a Nobel Peace Prize for ending six conflicts in his first six months back in office, although experts aren't sure what he's talking about, and Amanpour told "The Situation Room" that his inaction on conflicts in Ukraine and Gaza were once again on display during his state visit to Britain.

"The major existential problems in terms of foreign policy, as we keep saying, are first and foremost for Europe, the land war in Europe, that is the land war in Europe that is being waged by [Russian] President [Vladimir] Putin with no regard for what President Trump says or anybody else in the NATO alliance says," Amanpour said. "He just keeps escalating until he gets pushed back firmly, and I think it was really important that at the state banquet, the king, who doesn't get into overt politics but can speak, he basically said in his speech, 'Today as tyranny once again threatens Europe, we and our allies stand together in support of Ukraine to deter aggression and secure peace.'"

"President Trump was seated there, he nodded," she added. "But we still, as everybody is saying, have to see the president of the United States using the leverage that only the United States has, only the president of the president of the United States has to affect these wars in Ukraine and, of course, in the Middle East," she added. "The president of the United States is the only human being on Earth to have leverage with Prime Minister Netanyahu, and the fact that he's not using that leverage is being noticed by allies and analysts. They say that, you know, this is a president who could do it, it was alluded to in one question there, but so far hasn't exerted his influence."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Alleged Inaction in Global Conflicts: A Critical Analysis

The recent report by CNN's Christiane Amanpour brings to light a glaring contradiction between President Donald Trump's self-proclaimed peacemaking achievements and his apparent inaction on two major conflicts that continue to threaten global stability. Trump's claim of deserving a Nobel Peace Prize for purportedly ending six conflicts in his first six months back in office is a bold assertion that, upon closer examination, crumbles under the weight of reality and expert skepticism.

The Mirage of Peacemaking

Firstly, the ambiguity surrounding Trump's claim of ending six conflicts is startling. The lack of clarity and confirmation from independent experts raises questions about the veracity of these achievements. Such claims, without concrete evidence or acknowledgment from the global community, appear to be nothing more than political posturing. In the realm of international relations, words must be backed by verifiable actions, and in this case, Trump's assertions seem to lack substance.

The Silence on Ukraine and Gaza

Amanpour's report sheds light on Trump's puzzling silence regarding the ongoing conflicts in Ukraine and Gaza. These are not minor skirmishes but significant geopolitical crises that have severe implications for regional and global peace. The conflict in Ukraine, instigated by Russian aggression, poses a direct challenge to European security and the stability of the post-World War II international order. Similarly, the situation in Gaza remains a tinderbox for Middle Eastern peace, with humanitarian concerns mounting alongside unresolved political issues.

The Power of Presidential Leverage

The president of the United States is uniquely positioned to influence global affairs, wielding a combination of diplomatic, economic, and military tools unmatched by any other global leader. Trump's inaction, therefore, is not a matter of capability but seemingly of will. His failure to engage effectively with these crises, especially given his close relationship with leaders like Vladimir Putin and Benjamin Netanyahu, is a missed opportunity for diplomacy that could lead to meaningful progress towards peace.

The notion that Trump could leverage his relationships for peace is not far-fetched. However, his reluctance to do so—despite the apparent encouragement from international allies during state events, as highlighted in the king's speech during the state banquet in Britain—speaks volumes. It raises the question of whether Trump's foreign policy priorities align with genuine peacemaking or with the pursuit of personal accolades and political victories.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Missed Opportunities?

As Trump touts his peacemaking credentials, the reality on the ground tells a different story. The conflicts in Ukraine and Gaza continue unabated, with the human cost rising each day. The president's inaction and selective engagement on the global stage not only undermine his claims of being a peacemaker but also suggest a profound misunderstanding of the role of the United States in securing global peace.

The world does not need grandiose claims of peace prizes; it needs decisive action and genuine commitment to resolving conflicts. As the U.S. president, Trump has a unique opportunity to shape his legacy as a true peacemaker. However, based on the current trajectory, it seems more likely that his tenure will be remembered for missed opportunities and unfulfilled potential in the realm of international diplomacy.
