'Low odds': Elon Musk's vow to make Grok 'Odyssey' immediately fact-checked — by Grok

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Elon Musk Wants a Machine to Certify His Own Myth

The Setup

Elon Musk posted that Grok would generate a full-length Odyssey remake by the end of the year and insisted it would be “historically accurate.” The prompt was not a film plan so much as a billionaire’s latest attempt to dress ego up as cultural authority. The source of the claim, the platform, and the chatbot all sit inside Musk’s own orbit. That matters.

Power, Not Taste

This is not really about casting choices. Musk objected to Lupita Nyong’o and Elliot Page in the earlier AI clip by calling them “racist against the Greek people and their cultural heritage.” That sentence is ridiculous on its face, but the more important point is structural: a private owner is using his platform to turn personal grievance into pseudo-historical adjudication. He is not debating art. He is trying to define the terms of legitimacy.

The Bot That Blinked

The most revealing detail in the story is that Grok immediately undercut him. The chatbot called the odds of a full feature-length, historically accurate Odyssey movie “low” and described the technical gap in blunt terms. That is not democratic accountability. It is a machine briefly contradicting the man who owns the machine and the stage it speaks from. The contradiction is useful because it strips away the aura: for all the talk of future media, what remains is an expensive publicity loop.

Slop as Strategy

The clip that triggered Musk’s post already showed the genre. Stilted dialogue. Technicolor pastiche. Strikingly white “actors.” Users joked about it, defended it, compared it to old biblical epics, and treated the whole thing as a spectacle. That reaction is the point. AI output like this does not need to be good if it can be loud, shareable, and endlessly branded as innovation. Mediocrity is not a bug when the product is attention capture.

Who Gets Credited

The article’s real misdirection is its invitation to treat this as a quirky culture-war squabble over casting or fidelity to Homer. The actual decision-maker here is Musk, a billionaire using an owned platform, an owned chatbot, and a global audience to launder vanity into cultural commentary. Everyone else is reduced to reacting: users, critics, and even Grok itself. That is how concentrated power works. It converts everyone else into a comment section.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is familiar: private wealth, platform control, and technological hype combined into a machine for making personal impulses look like public significance. Musk is not reviving classical literature. He is trying to demonstrate that his tools can manufacture prestige on command. The democratic answer is not to pretend this is harmless eccentricity. It is to name it correctly: oligarchic self-mythology packaged as innovation, with the rest of us expected to applaud the demo.
