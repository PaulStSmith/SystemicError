'We all should be scared as hell': Red flag raised over Trump ally who terrifies even MAGA

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 23, 20254 min read

Supporters of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. president Donald Trump raise MAGA hats, on the day Trump returns for a rally at the site of the July assassination attempt against him, in Butler, Pennsylvania, U.S., October 5, 2024. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Right-wing provocateur Laura Loomer exerts a powerful influence over President Donald Trump, but a Democratic strategist warned that the MAGA enemies she has cultivated should signal just how extreme and dangerous she is to democracy.

The social media influencer has gotten the president to fire multiple federal employees for supposed disloyalty and readily agreed to comparisons of herself to disgraced senator Joseph McCarthy, who conducted the "Red Scare" purges of alleged communists in the 1950s, and Democratic strategist Antjuan Seawright told "CNN This Morning" that her associations were alarming.

"Well, I think it's scary because of the type of behavior and the people she cuddles and play footsie with politically, most of them are extremists," Seawright said. "She gets deep down into some of the dark, dangerous places and people – white supremacy, white nationalism, we've seen her flirt with those things. I think we have to separate, like trying to be an influence and influencing policy, influencing politics versus just doing what's bad and not long-term good."

"Attention is the most powerful drug in Washington, and I think she is overdosing on it right now," he added. "The question is, what happens when life after Trump kicks in? Because she's attached her brand to Trumpism and the extreme wing of the Republican Party, and I'm not sure how that survives."

Loomer's social media posts can direct waves of harassment at her targets or get them fired from their jobs, but the strategist took note that MAGA firebrands Tucker Carlson and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) had spoken out against her influence on the administration – and she has responded with accusations, threats and vulgar insults.

"When they think it's scary," Seawright said, "we all should be scared as hell."

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Influence of Laura Loomer: A Deep Dive

In the contemporary political landscape, the figure of Laura Loomer emerges as a testament to the power of social media influence over political discourse and, disturbingly, policy. Her sway over President Donald Trump and her seemingly unbridled capacity to get federal employees fired for perceived disloyalty draws a chilling parallel to the McCarthy era, renowned for its anti-communist purges. This comparison, rather than being shunned, is embraced by Loomer, a fact that in itself should raise red flags about the trajectory of American democracy.

The Danger of Extremism

Democratic strategist Antjuan Seawright's observations on CNN highlight a crucial concern: Loomer's comfort in mingling with the darkest corners of political extremism—from white supremacy to white nationalism. This isn't just a matter of political alignment; it's a dangerous dance with ideologies that undermine the very fabric of democracy. The distinction Seawright makes between influencing policy and politics for the better versus engaging in harmful, divisive actions is stark. Loomer's actions, as described, clearly fall into the latter category, leveraging attention not for productive discourse but for amplifying divisiveness.

The Addiction to Attention

Washington, D.C., has often been criticized for its obsession with the spotlight, but Loomer's case is a quintessential example of attention overdose. The strategist's metaphor underscores a significant issue: the relentless pursuit of visibility at the cost of political and social stability. Loomer's attachment to Trumpism and the extreme wing of the Republican Party poses a question worth pondering—what happens to such figures when the political tides turn? Their survival seems contingent not on the substance of their contributions to public policy or discourse but on their ability to stir controversy and remain in the public eye.

The Reaction of MAGA Firebrands

It's telling when figures like Tucker Carlson and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), themselves no strangers to controversy, express concern over Loomer's influence. The backlash from within her political sphere, met with accusations, threats, and insults from Loomer, is indicative of a toxic political environment where dialogue is replaced by defamation. Seawright's remark, "When they think it's scary, we all should be scared as hell," encapsulates the gravity of the situation. If those who thrive on political divisiveness find Loomer's tactics alarming, the broader public should indeed take notice.

Conclusion

Laura Loomer's influence on the Trump administration and her embrace of extreme, divisive tactics represent a significant threat to the principles of democracy. Her actions and associations with extremist ideologies are not just troubling; they are a clarion call to those committed to preserving democratic values to stand vigilant against the rise of such figures. The danger lies not only in the ideologies she flirts with but in the potent combination of social media influence and political power being wielded irresponsibly. As we navigate these turbulent political waters, the distinction between constructive political influence and destructive attention-seeking must be made clear, for the health of the democracy and the society it serves.
