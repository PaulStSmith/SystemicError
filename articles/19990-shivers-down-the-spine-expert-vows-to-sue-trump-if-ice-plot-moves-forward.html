'Shivers down the spine': Expert vows to sue Trump if ICE plot moves forward

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 10, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump points a finger during a meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu (not pictured) in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, U.S., April 7, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement’s presence at voting stations in the midterm elections would be illegal and could lead to the president being sued, an elections attorney has warned.

Marc Elias, in an appearance on Brian Tyler Cohen’s podcast, urged people to take note of Steve Bannon’s comments on ICE agents being present at ballot boxes across the country. Bannon said, “You can whine and cry and throw your toys out of the pram all you want, but we will never again allow an election to be stolen.”

The inference to ICE activity at the polls in November was denounced by Elias, who said, “Anyone who watches that clip and does not have shivers go down their spine, I don’t know what to tell them.

“We are facing an existential threat to democracy right now. Steve Bannon didn’t just say that there would be ICE in cities, what Steve Bannon said is that it would be ICE surrounding the polls. The images we saw in Minneapolis where they surrounded cars, where they surrounded peaceful protestors, where they surrounded US citizens, and we know in the tragic instances, what that meant for those individuals.”

Elias went on to describe Bannon as the “spiritual leader of MAGA” who “gives Donald Trump some of his worst ideas.” One such idea appears to be the ICE agents making their presence known on election day.

The Dem lawmaker added, “Is it legal? No. It would be totally illegal. But honestly, look at the images we have seen ICE do. They’re breaking all kinds of laws, they’re breaking laws every single day.

“What, though, is going to stop this is not some law in a book on a dusty shelf, but it’s going to stop because you and I are gonna call it out, everyone watching this is going to take this seriously.”

Elias also added that there would be a movement in the courts against ICE’s presence at polling booths. “When I say we’re going to sue, we sue, and we usually win,” he added. “Donald Trump and his cronies know that because after the 2020 election, I beat him and his team more than 60 times in court, and we’ll do everything we can do this time to make sure we have free and fair elections.”

Our Analysis:

ICE at the Polls: A Dance on the Edge of Democracy

In a recent episode of Brian Tyler Cohen’s podcast, Marc Elias, a seasoned elections attorney, dropped a bombshell that should make every American’s blood run cold. Elias highlighted comments made by Steve Bannon, the so-called “spiritual leader of MAGA,” advocating for U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents to be present at voting stations during the midterm elections. Bannon’s words, “You can whine and cry and throw your toys out of the pram all you want, but we will never again allow an election to be stolen,” reek of a dangerous authoritarianism that should have no place in American democracy.

The Legal Battlefield

Elias was unequivocal in his assessment: the deployment of ICE agents at polling stations would not only be unprecedented but blatantly illegal. Yet, his warning carries a deeper foreboding. It’s not just about the legality, which seems to be a mere footnote in the current political playbook of Bannon and his ilk, but about the chilling effect such a presence would undoubtedly have on voter turnout, particularly among immigrant communities.

Is it legal? No. Elias’s words are a stark reminder of the line being crossed. The imagery of ICE agents “surrounding cars, surrounding peaceful protestors, surrounding US citizens” evokes scenes more akin to authoritarian regimes than the United States. The reference to “tragic instances” underlines the lethal consequences such actions have had in the past. And yet, the suggestion that this force could be used to intimidate voters is met with a disturbing silence from too many in power.

The MAGA Playbook: Intimidation and Fear

Describing Bannon as a provider of “some of Donald Trump’s worst ideas” is no small statement. It speaks volumes about the level of extremism being entertained. The idea that ICE could be used as a tool to influence election outcomes by instilling fear is not only a gross misuse of power but a direct attack on the very foundations of democracy.

Elias’s call to action, emphasizing the power of public outcry and legal challenges, is crucial. His track record of defeating Trump and his allies in court over 60 times since the 2020 election offers a glimmer of hope. However, the fact that such measures are necessary is a damning indictment of the current state of American politics.

An Existential Threat to Democracy

What we’re witnessing is more than just a legal issue; it’s a moral crisis. The potential use of ICE to deter voters is a symptom of a larger disease plaguing American democracy: the weaponization of fear. Bannon’s comments, and the silence from his political allies, reflect a willingness to sacrifice democratic principles on the altar of political victory.

Elias’s warning should be a wake-up call. The fight for free and fair elections is not just in the courts but in the court of public opinion. It’s a fight that requires the vigilance and action of every citizen who believes in the fundamental right to vote without fear of intimidation or retaliation.

In a democracy, elections are the voice of the people, not an opportunity for the powerful to tighten their grip on power through fear and intimidation. The presence of ICE agents at polling stations would not just be a systemic error; it would be a systemic failure. It’s a scenario that every American, regardless of political affiliation, should stand against.
