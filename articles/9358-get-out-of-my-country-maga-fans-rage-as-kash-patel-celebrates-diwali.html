'Get out of my country!' MAGA fans rage as Kash Patel celebrates Diwali

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FBI Director Kash Patel attends the signing of an executive order by U.S. President Donald Trump on a deal that would divest TikTok’s U.S. operations from ByteDance from its Chinese owner ByteDance, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 25, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

MAGA fans expressed astonishment and disgust Monday night as FBI head Kash Patel publicly celebrated Diwali, the Hindu “Festival of Lights” celebrated each autumn by more than a billion people, including Hindus, Sikhs, Jains, and some Buddhists.

In a post on X, Patel wrote “Happy Diwali - celebrating the Festival of Lights around the world, as good triumphs over evil.”

Patel is the FBI’s first Indian-American to hold the position of director, of Gujarati Indian descent. His family hails from Bhadran village in Gujarat, India, and Patel was born in New York.

In posting his celebration of Diwali, Patel found himself under fire from MAGA fans, who melted down on social media.

Jeremy Carl, who described himself as being “on the front lines in a war against secularism,” shared Patel’s post and wrote, “Do you see the problem yet?”

X user John McGlone, who describes himself as an “unapologetic Christian American,” replied on X, “This is a Christian Nation. Check your foreigner stuff at the gate“

X user Johnny Blacksmith, who boasts he was “right wing before it was cool,” wrote in response, “Not compatible with American tradition.”

Marine Corps veteran Jim Eagle simply replied, “Shut the hell up,” with a gif of “Lost” actor Terry O’Quinn, who played the character “John Locke.”

Ben Garrett, pastor at Refuge Church in Ogden, Utah, shot back, “Go back.”

Podcaster Joel Webbon wrote, “Go back home and worship your sand demons. Get out of my country.”

Chris Nelson, a Florida Christian, replied, “What the hell is this? This is AMERICA. Not India.”

X user Scott Hambrick wrote back, “Deport.”

Our Analysis:

The Ignorance of MAGA Fans Towards Diversity: A Scathing Analysis

In a display that can only be described as both astonishingly ignorant and disturbingly predictable, MAGA fans have once again shown their true colors. This time, their ire was directed at FBI Director Kash Patel, a man who dared to celebrate his heritage by acknowledging Diwali, the Hindu “Festival of Lights.” This event, which is a beacon of hope, unity, and triumph of good over evil for over a billion people globally, somehow triggered a meltdown among a certain faction of MAGA supporters. Let’s dissect this display of bigotry and what it signifies about the current state of American discourse.

The Celebration of Diwali by Kash Patel: A Landmark Event Kash Patel, the first Indian-American director of the FBI, took to X (formerly Twitter) to extend Diwali greetings. Born in New York to a family of Gujarati Indian descent, Patel’s acknowledgment of his roots should be seen as a proud moment of diversity and representation at the highest levels of American government. Instead, it was met with vitriol and xenophobia from individuals who claim to stand for American values.

The Bigoted Backlash: A Showcase of Ignorance

The responses from MAGA fans ranged from the sadly predictable to the outright appalling. Jeremy Carl, self-described as being on the “front lines in a war against secularism,” questioned the celebration of Diwali as if the mere act of acknowledging a non-Christian festival is an affront to American secularism. This stance is not only ironic but blatantly hypocritical, given that secularism inherently supports the freedom of religious expression.

John McGlone, an “unapologetic Christian American,” and Johnny Blacksmith, who prides himself on being “right wing before it was cool,” both expressed sentiments that reek of xenophobia and a misguided sense of nationalism. McGlone’s assertion that America is a “Christian Nation” and Blacksmith’s claim that Diwali is “not compatible with American tradition” betray a fundamental misunderstanding of the United States as a melting pot of cultures and religions.

Perhaps most disturbing were the comments from Marine Corps veteran Jim Eagle, Pastor Ben Garrett, podcaster Joel Webbon, Florida Christian Chris Nelson, and X user Scott Hambrick. Their reactions ranged from telling Patel to “shut the hell up” and “go back” to the outright racist and dehumanizing command to “go back home and worship your sand demons.” These comments not only display an appalling level of intolerance but also a profound ignorance of the very foundation upon which America was built: freedom and diversity.

The Irony of American Tradition

The irony in declaring that Diwali—and by extension, Patel’s celebration of his heritage—is “not compatible with American tradition” is so thick you could cut it with a knife. America’s tradition, at its core, is a tapestry woven from diverse threads of culture, religion, and heritage. The very essence of American values champions the freedom to celebrate one’s heritage and beliefs.

To claim that America is a “Christian Nation” not only disregards the constitutional principle of separation between church and state but also ignores the rich diversity that defines the American populace. Such statements are not only factually incorrect but dangerous, as they seek to alienate and otherize American citizens and residents who contribute to the nation’s cultural richness and historical depth.

Conclusion: A Call for Tolerance and Understanding

The backlash against Kash Patel for celebrating Diwali is a stark reminder of the work that lies ahead in combating ignorance, bigotry, and xenophobia. It’s a call to all who believe in the true values of America to stand up against such narrow-mindedness and to champion the diversity that truly makes America great. In the end, the celebration of Diwali by an American official should not be a cause for division but a moment of unity, reflecting the triumph of good over evil, light over darkness, and knowledge over ignorance.
