Donald Trump was up late Wednesday night, posting on Truth Social, and attacking Comcast which is spinning off MSNBC from NBC, converting the cable network with a rebrand into My Source News Opinion World, or MS NOW for short.

Trump spent part of his evening touting his big win in Texas, where Republicans passed their redistricting bill, citing a poll by writing, "59% APPROVAL RATING FOR PRESIDENT TRUMP, MASSIVE LEAD OVER THE DEMOCRATS,” without citing what poll he was speaking of.

He then lashed out at California Governor Gavin Newsom, who has been a thorn in his side on social media, by writing, “Gavin Newscum is way down in the polls. He is viewed as the man who is destroying the once Great State of California. I will save California!!! President DJT,” again without what polls he is talking about.

One hour later, he attacked Comcast, a frequent target, with an all-caps attack.

“MSNBC IS DOING SO POORLY IN THE RATINGS THAT THEY ARE LOOKING TO CHANGE THEIR NAME IN ORDER TO GET AWAY FROM THE STENCH OF THEIR FAKE NEWS PRODUCT. SO MUCH FUN TO WATCH THEIR WEAK AND INEFFECTIVE OWNER, “CONCAST,” HEADED BY DOPEY BRIAN ROBERTS, HOPELESSLY AND AIMLESSLY FLAILING IN THE WIND IN AN ATTEMPT TO DISASSOCIATE ITSELF FROM THE GARBAGE THAT THEY CREATED! MSNBC IS A FAILURE BY ANY NAME!!!” he claimed.

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misdirection and Misinformation

Once again, we find ourselves in the peculiar world of Donald Trump's social media antics, this time on Truth Social. Trump's strategy, it seems, is as old as politics itself but with a modern twist—divert, distort, and attack. Let's dissect his recent tirades and the implications they carry.

The Art of Diversion

Trump's late-night spree kicks off with a boast about his "big win in Texas," referring to Republicans passing their redistricting bill. He claims a "59% APPROVAL RATING FOR PRESIDENT TRUMP, MASSIVE LEAD OVER THE DEMOCRATS,” yet conveniently forgets to mention the source of this poll. This tactic isn't new. Mentioning impressive numbers without context or source is a classic way to bolster one's image while leaving no trail to verify the claim. It's the illusion of popularity crafted from thin air.

Targeting Political Adversaries

Next, Trump sets his sights on California Governor Gavin Newsom, dubbing him "Gavin Newscum" and accusing him of destroying California. Again, Trump claims Newsom is "way down in the polls" without specifying which polls. This is a textbook example of creating a narrative with no foundation in reality. The strategy is simple: repeat a lie enough times, and it starts to wear the guise of truth. This approach not only aims to discredit Newsom but also positions Trump as the supposed savior of California, despite offering no plan or policy to support this grandiose claim.

The Comcast Conundrum

Trump's attack on Comcast and its decision to spin off MSNBC into My Source News Opinion World (MS NOW) is both bizarre and revealing. He revels in the network's supposed failure, attributing it to the "stench of their fake news product" and resorting to schoolyard insults like "CONCAST" and "DOPEY BRIAN ROBERTS." This segment epitomizes Trump's vendetta against media outlets that critique him. It's not about the quality of journalism or the truth but about discrediting any source of criticism. It's an attempt to erode trust in media, leaving only his narrative as the 'truth.'

Conclusion: A Distorted Reality

Trump's posts offer a window into a strategy that has become all too familiar. It's a world where facts are malleable, polls are mentioned without sources, and any critique is met with hostility and name-calling. The danger here isn't just in the misinformation, but in the cultivation of a reality where Trump is the perennial victor, unchallenged by facts or accountability.

His attacks on Comcast and Governor Newsom, coupled with unfounded boasts about approval ratings, serve not just as distractions but as efforts to reshape reality to his advantage. It's a potent reminder of the power of narrative and the importance of scrutinizing the sources of our information. In Trump's world, the truth is what he says it is—until we decide to look closer.
