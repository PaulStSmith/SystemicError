'The worst': Supreme Court Justice John Roberts shamed as bending to Trump to save himself

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The U.S. Supreme Court's decision to place Donald Trump beyond the reach of law looks worse with every passing week, according to a legal expert, and Chief Justice John Roberts may be remembered as the last person to hold his position.

The court's right-wing majority ruled in favor of the president, who was then cruising to the Republican presidential nomination last summer, in Trump v. United States, granting him broad immunity against the criminal charges he faced at the time for attempting to remain in power despite losing the 2020 election. Law professor Gene Nichol published a column for The State shaming the chief justice as a coward.

"The plan seems to be — if we grant the president everything he dreams of, maybe he’ll leave the high court unmolested," wrote Nichol, a law professor at the University of North Carolina. "The republic may fall but we’ll still have the cool robes and life tenure. John Roberts is becoming the Neville Chamberlain of post-democratic government in America."

After granting the president the power to break the law, the court has allowed him to illegally dismantle federal agencies and claw back congressionally approved funding without even bothering to explain their legal reasoning.

"The court seems to have invalidated, after nearly a century of ready and repeated judicial embrace, the notion of independent federal agencies," Nichol wrote. "In a brief, unsigned order, the justices just blocked a federal district court ruling preventing Trump from firing, contrary to statutory mandate, three of the five members of the Consumer Product Safety Commission. The landmark 1935 decision in Humphrey’s Executor v. U.S. was cast aside. No limitations on presidential discretion are now to be tolerated — no matter how much sense it may make to remove certain types of administrative decision-making from the sway of politics."

"All power to Trump, regardless of the claims of text and history," Nichol added, bitterly. "You demand it, Mr. President, we deliver, even if on the down low. Just spare us your wrath. We don’t even mind if you violate our decrees. Just lie when you do it. Easy enough."

Roberts' surrender to Trump made the court all but irrelevant, according to Nichols.

"It’s reasonable to wonder whether the rule of law can survive this," the professor wrote. "As John Roberts works to become our worst modern chief justice, will he also effectively become our last?"

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court's Dubious Legacy: A Surrender to Trumpism

In a move that reeks of political partisanship and a blatant disregard for the rule of law, the U.S. Supreme Court, under the guidance of Chief Justice John Roberts, has ostensibly granted former President Donald Trump a form of legal invincibility that stands in stark contrast to the principles upon which the United States was founded. The court's decision in Trump v. United States to offer Trump broad immunity against criminal charges related to his attempts to retain power post-2020 election loss not only undermines the democratic fabric of the nation but also signals a disturbing shift toward autocracy.

The Abdication of Judicial Responsibility

Gene Nichol's scathing critique of Chief Justice John Roberts labels him as the "Neville Chamberlain of post-democratic government in America," drawing a poignant parallel to the infamous policy of appeasement that ultimately led to the Second World War. Nichol's analogy is neither hyperbolic nor unwarranted. By capitulating to Trump's demands, Roberts and the court's right-wing majority have not only abdicated their judicial responsibility but have also compromised the integrity of the Supreme Court itself.

A Dangerous Precedent

The court's decision to invalidate the near-century-old precedent of independent federal agencies, as outlined in Nichol's column, is a glaring testament to the lengths to which the justices are willing to go to appease Trump. The unsigned order allowing Trump to fire members of the Consumer Product Safety Commission, despite statutory mandates to the contrary, is a flagrant assault on the separation of powers. It dismantles a fundamental principle of American governance: that no individual, not even the president, is above the law.

Roberts' Legacy: Complicity in the Erosion of Democracy

Nichol's assertion that the Supreme Court has become "all but irrelevant" under Roberts' leadership is a chilling indictment of the chief justice's failure to uphold the Constitution. By granting Trump unchecked power, the court has not only endangered the rule of law but has also set a dangerous precedent for future administrations. The notion that the court would rather maintain its "cool robes and life tenure" than protect the republic is a stark reflection of the moral and ethical decay that has beset the highest court in the land.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The implications of the Supreme Court's actions extend far beyond the tenure of Donald Trump. This decision has the potential to fundamentally alter the balance of power within the United States, paving the way for a future where the whims of a single individual can override the collective will of the people. It is a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy and the need for vigilance in its defense.

Nichol's critique, while harsh, is a necessary wake-up call. It is incumbent upon us, the citizens of the United States, to demand accountability from our leaders and to ensure that no individual, not even the president, can dismantle the democratic institutions that have stood the test of time. The Supreme Court's decision in Trump v. United States is not just a legal aberration; it is a systemic error that must be corrected if we are to preserve the principles of justice, equality, and democracy for future generations.
