Nobel-winning economist warns these 'drastic' cuts have nothing to do with money

Nobel prize-winning economist Paul Krugman tore into what he called President Donald Trump’s “war on science” Tuesday in a column, ripping into the administration's cuts on weather research.

“Trump’s cuts to scientific research aren’t about shrinking government and saving money,” Krugman wrote. “They’re about dealing with possibly inconvenient evidence by covering the nation’s ears and shouting ‘La, la, la, we can’t hear you.’”

Krugman’s column comes in the wake of the deadly flood in central Texas on July 4 that claimed the lives of at least 134 people, with many critics casting blame on the Trump administration’s proposed cuts to federal agencies that conduct weather research.

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, which oversees the National Weather Service, is facing a 40% budget cut, with a Trump budget proposal calling for around $2.3 billion less in spending for the upcoming fiscal year.

While Trump administration officials have defended the cuts as part of Trump’s efforts to reduce federal spending, some experts say the cuts to NOAA and other weather research efforts will, in the long run, increase spending.

"Hurricane response costs become greater when you have a poorer forecast,” said James Franklin, former chief of the National Hurricane Center, speaking with Krugman. “That’s a lot of cost savings that we seem willing to give up here. We’re going to turn off all that potential savings by saying we don’t care if the forecasts don’t continue to get better.”

Krugman also took aim at the timing of the proposed cuts, being proposed just weeks after the deadly Texas flood, where allegations remain of a muted, inadequate government response to the tragedy.

“The Trump administration is getting ready to effectively zero out the research that underlies improvements in weather forecasting,” he wrote.

“This includes shuttering the lab that sends teams of hurricane hunters into storms to collect data and drastically cutting a program that maintains river gauges to help predict floods. In this case Trump and company aren’t shrinking government, they’re basically dismantling it.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's War on Science: A Dangerous Game

Nobel prize-winning economist Paul Krugman has once again sliced through the facade of the Trump administration's policies, this time focusing his sharp critique on what he rightfully terms "Trump’s war on science." In the wake of the catastrophic flood in central Texas, which claimed at least 134 lives, Krugman's analysis is not just timely; it's a dire warning we cannot afford to ignore.

The Deafening Sound of Ignorance

Krugman doesn't mince words when he says, “Trump’s cuts to scientific research aren’t about shrinking government and saving money.” Let’s be clear: this is about an administration that chooses to stick its fingers in its ears, ignoring the cacophony of warnings from scientists and experts. The 40% budget cut to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) is not just a number on a page; it's a potential death sentence for those who rely on accurate weather forecasting to survive.

The False Economy of Cuts

The Trump administration's defense of these cuts as a measure to reduce federal spending is not just shortsighted; it's economically nonsensical. As James Franklin, former chief of the National Hurricane Center, articulates, the costs of hurricane response skyrocket with poorer forecasts. The $2.3 billion cut might look like savings today but will balloon into much greater costs tomorrow. The irony is palpable - in an attempt to save pennies now, we are throwing away dollars later.

The Timing: Insensitive and Inane

Krugman's critique of the timing of these proposed cuts is spot-on. To propose such drastic reductions in weather research immediately after a deadly flood is not just insensitive; it's a glaring testament to the administration's detachment from reality. The message is unmistakable: the suffering and loss of American lives are secondary to the agenda of dismantling governmental research capabilities.

Dismantling, Not Shrinking

Let’s not be fooled by the rhetoric of “shrinking government.” What we’re witnessing is a systematic dismantling of vital components of our nation's scientific infrastructure. To shutter the lab that sends hurricane hunters into storms or to cut the program that maintains river gauges is to blind ourselves deliberately to the forces of nature. It’s a move that doesn’t just shrink government; it shrinks our capacity to protect and prepare ourselves for the inevitable next disaster.

Conclusion

Krugman's column is more than a critique; it's a wake-up call. The Trump administration's war on science is not just a war on data or numbers; it's a war on the very principles of preparedness and prevention. It's a war with real casualties, as seen in Texas, and potentially more to come if these policies are not reversed. The question is, will we listen to the warnings of experts like Krugman, or will we continue down this perilous path, with our fingers in our ears, shouting “La, la, la, we can’t hear you” as the storm approaches? The answer to that question will define not just the legacy of an administration but the future safety and security of our nation.
