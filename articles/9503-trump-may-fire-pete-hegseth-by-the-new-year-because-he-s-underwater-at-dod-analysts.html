Trump may fire Pete Hegseth​ by the New Year because he's 'underwater' at DOD: analysts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth attend a cabinet meeting. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth may be the first member of Donald Trump’s cabinet to be fired from the job, and at least one analyst thinks that was part of the plan all along.

Hegseth has had a tumultuous year as Defense Secretary. In April, he was embroiled in a scandal where he leaked a classified strike to a journalist in a Signal group chat. He also held an awkward town hall with all of America’s generals, which attendees described as a “waste of time.” Meanwhile, he is also busy removing the television cameras from the Department of Justice, which some say will make it harder for Hegseth to appease the president through his favorite medium.

The Daily Beast’s David Gardner and Sarah Ewall-Wice discussed the prospects of Hegseth being removed from the administration on a new episode of “The Daily Beast Podcast.”

“We were told early on that Trump did not want a repeat of his first administration, where it was kind of chaos...It was a lot of unrest within the administration,” Gardner said. “We were told he was going to give his leading cabinet members a year, so there wasn’t this kind of constant departure thing.”

“So my guess is watch out for January because I don’t think he’s going to last much longer than that first year,” he continued.

Ewall-Wice argued that Trump’s rotating cabinet seemed to be pre-planned.

“If you say your cabinet members are not going to be long-term choices from the get-go, then you put them in this competition...to basically fight it out and prove who’s the most loyal to the president,” she said. “That has been something that the president has valued above all else, above how they handled the job, how they handled the policy.”

“Hegseth has maybe been underwater this entire time as a Defense Secretary, having little to no experience compared to other previous ones,” she continued. “He’s underwater in the job, but he’s not underwater, in the same sense, when it comes to his fight for the president.”

Our Analysis:

The Hegseth Debacle: A Symptom of Systemic Dysfunction

In what reads like a plotline pulled straight from a political thriller, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s tenure under President Donald Trump’s administration is teetering on the brink of termination. Yet, this impending dismissal, as analyzed on The Daily Beast Podcast, could very well have been the plan from the outset. This scenario, if true, is not merely a reflection of Hegseth’s competency—or lack thereof—but a damning indictment of the Trump administration’s approach to governance.

The Illusion of Stability

Hegseth’s appointment and potential firing is a testament to the faux stability Trump purportedly sought to instill in his second term. David Gardner’s comments that Trump intended to give his leading cabinet members a year to prove themselves is laughable at best. This claim suggests a deliberate ignorance or, worse, a calculated manipulation of the public’s expectation for governmental stability. The reality, as Sarah Ewall-Wice aptly pointed out, is that Trump’s cabinet has always been a gladiatorial arena where loyalty to the president overshadows competence and policy execution.

A Calculated Chaos

The Hegseth spectacle, marked by scandals and inefficacies, from leaking classified information to conducting futile town halls, is emblematic of the broader dysfunction within the Trump administration. The removal of television cameras from the Department of Justice, under Hegseth’s watch, is a particularly sinister move. It speaks to a broader strategy of obfuscation and control, prioritizing the appeasement of Trump’s ego over transparent governance.

Ewall-Wice’s analysis that cabinet members were never meant to be long-term choices but were instead placed in a competition of loyalty is a harrowing revelation. It underscores a governance philosophy where fealty trumps (no pun intended) capability. This philosophy is not just flawed; it is dangerous. Hegseth’s “underwater” performance, as Ewall-Wice puts it, is not merely a personal failure but a symptom of a system that values loyalty over expertise, spectacle over substance.

The Real Casualties

While much can be said about Hegseth’s faltering tenure, the true victims of this systemic error are the American people and the principles of democratic governance. When cabinet members are selected based on their willingness to engage in a loyalty contest rather than their ability to serve the public, the very foundations of effective governance are eroded.

The Hegseth saga, therefore, is not just a cautionary tale of one man’s inadequacy. It is a scathing critique of an administration that has systematically devalued expertise, transparency, and accountability in favor of loyalty, secrecy, and self-preservation. As January approaches, and with it the prospect of Hegseth’s departure, we are reminded that the issue at hand is far larger than the fate of one man. It is about the integrity of our institutions and the values we champion as a society.

In conclusion, the Hegseth debacle is a glaring reminder of the dangers posed by a governance model that prioritizes loyalty over competency. As the Trump administration continues to play this perilous game, one cannot help but wonder: at what cost?
