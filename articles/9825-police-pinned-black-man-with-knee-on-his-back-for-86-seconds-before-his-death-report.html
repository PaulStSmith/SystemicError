Police pinned Black man with knee on his back for 86 seconds before his death: report

By Paulo Santos

New, disturbing details have emerged about an incident that got a Kansas sheriff’s deputy charged with second-degree murder for the death of a Black man in custody, reported The Guardian on Friday — with the death bearing a striking resemblance to the murder of George Floyd in Minneapolis five years ago.

According to the report, “Richard Fatherly was charged last month with second-degree murder and an alternative count of involuntary manslaughter in Charles Adair’s 5 July death in the Wyandotte county detention center in Kansas City, Kansas. Adair had been arrested one day before his death on misdemeanor warrants for failure to appear on multiple traffic violations.”

According to civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who is representing Adair’s family, the newly revealed records show that “Charles Adair was handcuffed, lying on his stomach with a severely injured leg, and posed no threat when a deputy pressed a knee into his back, resulting in his death.”

Adair was held in that position for one minute and 26 seconds, immediately after being wheeled from the infirmary to his cell.

“At the time, Adair’s leg needed to be amputated and was so badly infected that he was taken straight to the hospital, a Kansas Bureau of Investigation agent wrote in the affidavit,” said the report. “Before Adair was cleared to return to the jail, he was diagnosed with a type of bone infection that sometimes develops in people with diabetes. A medical screening also found he was schizophrenic, the affidavit said.”

The report says Adair, who was handcuffed, threw himself out of his wheelchair and was incoherent after being put back in his cell, after which Fatherly pinned him to the ground with his knee as Adair screamed for help. “You’re done,” Fatherly told him, per the affidavit, as Adair stopped resisting and said, “Ok.” A medical report found the cause of death to be homicide from “mechanical asphyxia.”

Fatherly’s attorney has called the death an accident and denies his client was responsible.

The murder of George Floyd at the hands of former police officer Derek Chauvin, which was in very similar circumstances as this alleged incident, prompted furious protests all over the country and prompted at least 30 states and Washington, D.C. to pass policing reforms.

Our Analysis:

The Echoes of Injustice: A Familiar Tragedy in Kansas

The harrowing details emerging from Kansas City, Kansas, as reported by The Guardian, not only revive the anguish felt nationwide after the murder of George Floyd but also underscore a persistent, systemic issue within the United States’ law enforcement practices. The incident involving Richard Fatherly, a Kansas sheriff’s deputy charged with second-degree murder following the death of Charles Adair, a Black man in custody, is not merely a case of individual misconduct but a glaring example of the systemic rot that plagues our criminal justice system.

A Death That Mirrors George Floyd’s

The parallels between Charles Adair’s death and George Floyd’s murder are not just striking; they are deeply disturbing. Both Black men were rendered helpless and posed no threat when they met their deaths under the knee of law enforcement officers. Adair, like Floyd, was handcuffed, lying on his stomach, and in a vulnerable state due to a severely injured leg that needed amputation, and yet, he was subjected to a fatal restraint method that has been widely condemned and resulted in widespread protests and calls for policing reforms.

The Inhumanity of the Act

The details of Adair’s treatment while in custody paint a picture of utter disregard for human life and dignity. Handcuffed, with a leg so severely infected that immediate hospitalization was required, Adair was not treated with the urgency and care his condition warranted. Instead, after being deemed incoherent, he was pinned down with a knee to his back by Deputy Fatherly, as he screamed for help. The words reported to have been uttered by Fatherly, “You’re done,” to a man in such a dire state, reflect a chilling dehumanization that is as inexcusable as it is fatal.

The Denial of Responsibility

The attorney for Fatherly calling Adair’s death an “accident” is a familiar refrain in the litany of law enforcement-induced fatalities that have stained the United States. This denial does not just insult the intelligence of the public but also mocks the very concept of accountability. The medical report categorically found the cause of death to be homicide from “mechanical asphyxia.” To label such a death an accident is not only a gross distortion of facts but a blatant attempt to evade responsibility.

The Systemic Error

This incident, and the many others like it, signals a systemic failure that extends far beyond individual actors. It highlights a culture within law enforcement that is too often characterized by brutality, a lack of accountability, and a devaluation of Black lives. The fact that this can still happen after the global outcry following George Floyd’s murder and the subsequent policing reforms speaks volumes about the depth of systemic reform required to address these injustices.

In conclusion, the death of Charles Adair is a grim reminder of the work that remains to be done. It is a call to action for not only law enforcement agencies and the judicial system but for every individual who believes in justice and equality. The fight against systemic racism and police brutality is far from over, and Charles Adair’s death must not be in vain. The details of this case are not just disturbing; they are a damning indictment of a system that continues to fail the very people it is supposed to serve and protect.
