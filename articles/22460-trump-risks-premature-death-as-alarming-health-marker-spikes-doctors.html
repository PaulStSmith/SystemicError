Trump risks 'premature death' as alarming health marker spikes: doctors

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 24, 20262 min read

The Real Health Crisis Is the Man He Put in Charge

A Number, Not the Story

The source spends its energy on Trump’s weight, his junk food, and the familiar chatter around his body. That is the least important part of the story. The actual power question is not whether Trump is 238 pounds or 25 pounds heavier than last campaign season. It is why a president whose administration hands major health programs to Mehmet Oz is still being discussed as a personal health novelty instead of a political danger.

Power Sits With the Appointer

Trump holds institutional power because he is president. He decides who runs the machinery underneath him. The source notes that Oz, a former reality-show host described by experts as a quack grifter, now runs Medicare and Medicaid. That is not a side detail. That is the governance scandal. A television performer with a reputation for selling nonsense has been placed over programs that touch millions of people’s lives.

The Body as Distraction

The article’s framing leans into spectacle: the president’s weight, his diet, his exercise habits, his own remarks about dropping pounds. That is useful gossip, but it also misdirects attention away from the actual institutional damage. Trump’s body is not the public problem. The public problem is a political culture that treats obvious grift as personality, and deliberate delegation to frauds as mere eccentricity.

Cruelty as Routine

Trump’s history of mocking other people’s bodies matters here because it shows how shallow the current concern really is. He has used weight as a weapon against a beauty queen, a protester, Kim Kardashian, and Rosie O’Donnell. A man who made humiliation part of his political style now gets wrapped in concern when his own body becomes the subject. That is not moral complexity. It is the usual elite habit of laundering contempt into concern once the target is powerful enough.

Health Policy as Theater

The source includes doctors warning that excess body fat, especially around the middle, carries serious health risks. Fine. But the deeper issue is that the same political world that obsesses over Trump’s waistline has normalized handing health governance to a grifter. That is where the real consequence lives: not in the biometrics of one aging strongman, but in the corruption of public authority into branding, and of health administration into a stage prop.

The Pattern

This story is not about one man getting older or heavier. It is about a political system that constantly mistakes spectacle for substance and lets powerful people convert personal decay into institutional power. Trump’s weight is trivia. His appointments are not. When the state is run by men like him and Oz, the message is clear: the public is expected to watch the performance while the real decisions are made by people who should never have been trusted with them.
