'It's over': Victims' attorney insists Congress can seize Epstein birthday book

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 25, 20255 min read

Jeffrey Epstein's infamous birthday book is an "absolute fact," according to an attorney who represented more than 200 of the sex offender's victims.

Attorney Bradley Edwards told MSNBC's "The Last Word" the 2003 book, which was compiled on the occasion of the late financier's 50th birthday and revealed last week by the Wall Street Journal, that "multiple" victims of Epstein and his associate Ghislaine Maxwell knew about the birthday book, reported The Daily Beast.

“I do know that they were involved in the assembly of the book, several of them,” Edwards said. “They were told by Ghislaine to assist in putting the book together. Others have seen the book post-birthday and after it was assembled. So the existence of the book is an absolute fact.”

The Journal reported that Trump had contributed to the book by sketching a nude woman and writing a bawdy letter to his then-friend. Edwards told host Lawrence O'Donnell that Epstein's estate executors would undoubtedly hand the book over if subpoenaed by Congress.

“They would turn the book over immediately,” Edwards said. “Nobody would have to guess. You would immediately have the answers. You could flip to the page. Is there a letter? Is there not a letter? It’s over. The victims then get to move on. But that’s not what’s happening. Real people are being hurt here. The truth matters, transparency actually matters. Get this over with and get it behind us.”

The president insists the letter is "fake" and has sued the newspaper for libel, but the attorney said he knows for a fact that the Journal's reporting was accurate.

“It should probably be set in the Smithsonian as an artifact at this point in time," Edwards said. “It’s going to go down in history, so that the world can see this book and they’ll know who were Jeffrey Epstein’s best friends at the time. What letters did his family write to him? What other pictures were in there? Redact victim names, release the book and move on.”

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, who had been Trump's criminal defense lawyer before his return to the White House, will meet with Maxwell as soon as Thursday, which Edwards said was upsetting to victims — many of whom Epstein had abused when they were under 18. At least one was 14 at the time.

“The one thing that has really alarmed our clients is the information that now Ghislaine Maxwell is being visited in jail," Edwards said, "and that she is somehow being given some credibility and a platform on this particular topic. Does she have information that could potentially help? Yes. Will she be truthful about it? Who knows?“

Edwards said the victims he had represented were concerned that Trump and Maxwell had reached an understanding that would allow them to bury the matter.

“I have many clients who say, ‘President Trump and Ghislaine Maxwell were good friends back then, is he going to pardon her?’" Edwards said. "For him to do that, would be, he thought about you and he decided you do not matter. I don’t think that is on the table, maybe I’m putting too much trust in him.”

Our Analysis:

The Infamous Birthday Book: A Testament to Transparency or a Tool for Turmoil?

The recent revelation about Jeffrey Epstein's so-called "birthday book" has sent shockwaves through an already scandal-ridden narrative surrounding the late financier and convicted sex offender. According to attorney Bradley Edwards, who represented over 200 of Epstein's victims, the existence of this book is an "absolute fact," a claim that throws gasoline on the fire of this ongoing saga. Edwards's comments, made on MSNBC's "The Last Word," shed light not just on the book's existence but also on the involvement of Epstein's victims in its assembly, as directed by his associate, Ghislaine Maxwell.

The Alleged Contributors: From Victims to High-Profile Figures

The book, compiled for Epstein's 50th birthday in 2003 and unveiled by the Wall Street Journal, features contributions that range from the deeply disturbing to the bizarrely banal. Among these is a sketch by none other than Donald Trump, then a friend of Epstein, depicting a nude woman accompanied by a "bawdy letter." This contribution, if true, underscores the tangled web of high-profile associations that Epstein maintained. The fact that Trump has vehemently denied the letter's authenticity, going so far as to sue the newspaper for libel, adds another layer of intrigue and obfuscation to the story.

The Quest for Transparency and Truth

Edwards's call for transparency is a rallying cry for justice and closure for the victims. He articulates a simple yet profound solution: subpoena the book, verify its contents, and allow the victims to move on. This plea for straightforwardness is juxtaposed against a backdrop of legal and political maneuvering that seems to prioritize protecting the powerful over providing peace to the victims. The attorney's suggestion to place the book in the Smithsonian as a historical artifact is a poignant commentary on the societal importance of confronting and acknowledging the misdeeds of the influential.

The Unsettling Implications of Political Entanglements

The interaction between Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, Trump's former criminal defense lawyer, and Maxwell introduces a disconcerting layer of political complexity to the victims' quest for justice. The fear that Trump might pardon Maxwell, thus possibly negating the gravity of her alleged crimes, speaks volumes about the perceived intertwining of political power and judicial outcomes. Such concerns highlight the deep distrust in the fairness of the legal system when it comes to holding the elite accountable.

A Platform for the Perpetrators?

The mention of Maxwell being given a "platform" on this issue while in jail is particularly alarming. It suggests a scenario where those complicit in Epstein's crimes could manipulate the narrative, further victimizing those who have already suffered immensely. The speculation over Maxwell's potential to offer helpful information versus the likelihood of her being truthful encapsulates the dilemma at the heart of seeking justice in cases involving powerful individuals with extensive connections.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The saga of Jeffrey Epstein's birthday book is more than just a salacious detail in a scandalous story. It is a litmus test for the integrity of our legal and political systems. Will the pursuit of truth and transparency prevail, or will the interests of the powerful continue to overshadow the cries for justice from the victims? The answers to these questions will not only determine the outcome of this particular case but also set precedents for how society deals with similar situations in the future. The need for a clear, unequivocal stance against the exploitation and abuse perpetuated by the elite has never been more urgent.
