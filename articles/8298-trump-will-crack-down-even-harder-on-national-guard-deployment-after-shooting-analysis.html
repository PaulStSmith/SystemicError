Donald Trump is likely to crack down on National Guard deployments following a shooting in Washington D.C., a political commentator believes.

Writing in The Guardian, David Smith suggested the shooting of two National Guard troops near the White House would leave the president responding with tougher measures. Those measures, which may act as a response to the shooting which left two National Guard members in critically injured, will have an uncertain impact, Smith says.

He wrote, “But Trump, who had already decided the national guard deployment would continue to at least the end of February, is now likely to crack down even harder. He announced the deployment of an additional 500 national guard troops – though it is unclear what that will achieve.”

Smith would also suggest the “risk of escalation” which comes from the shooting is clear. Everytown for Gun Safety president John Feinblatt said the shooting showed that “no neighborhood is beyond the reach of this crisis.”

He said, “The fact that this can happen to members of the military just steps from the White House serves as a painful reminder that in a country awash with guns, no neighborhood is beyond the reach of this crisis.”

It was confirmed earlier today that a suspect had been arrested for the shooting of the two National Guard troops. Rahmanullah Lakanwal, 29, who has been taken into custody, was identified as an Afghan national who previously worked with the U.S. military.

Authorities have not released details on a possible motive for the attack. The two troops who were shot were at first reported as dead by West Virginia Gov. Patrick Morrissey, but this was later walked back, with current sources saying as of press time they are alive but in critical condition.

The US has since suspended immigration requests for Afghans as a result of the shooting. The US Citizenship and Immigration Services said the decision was made pending a review of “security and vetting protocols”.

Trump issued a statement calling the shooting an “act of terror” and that his administration would now work towards removing foreigners “from any country who doesn’t belong here”.

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Trump Response: A Critique of National Guard Deployment and Immigration Policy

In a recent article from The Guardian, the author David Smith delves into President Donald Trump’s expected crackdown on National Guard deployments following a tragic shooting near the White House. This response, while predictable, is indicative of a broader, more concerning trend in Trump’s governance: reactionary policies that often miss the mark on addressing the root causes of the issues at hand.

The Misguided Increase of National Guard Troops

Smith reports that Trump, in response to the shooting that critically injured two National Guard members, plans to deploy an additional 500 troops. The logic behind this move is, at best, flawed. The assertion that increasing military presence could act as a deterrent to such incidents is speculative and unsubstantiated. This approach is akin to putting a Band-Aid on a bullet wound; it superficially addresses the symptom while ignoring the underlying problem.

What’s more troubling is the lack of clarity on what this deployment aims to achieve. The article quotes Smith saying, “it is unclear what that will achieve,” highlighting the reactionary nature of this decision. It’s a classic Trump maneuver; a show of force that serves more as a political statement than a practical solution.

Escalation Risks and the Real Crisis at Hand

The article aptly points out the “risk of escalation” that comes with such a heavy-handed response. Everytown for Gun Safety president, John Feinblatt’s comments underscore the broader issue: America’s gun crisis. Feinblatt’s statement that “no neighborhood is beyond the reach of this crisis” is a poignant reminder of the pervasive nature of gun violence in the U.S. Yet, Trump’s administration seems more focused on flexing its military might than engaging in meaningful gun control discussions.

Questionable Immigration Suspensions

In a move that can only be described as knee-jerk, the U.S. has suspended immigration requests for Afghans in the wake of the shooting, as per the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. This decision, ostensibly for a review of “security and vetting protocols,” is a thinly-veiled overreaction that unjustly penalizes Afghan nationals seeking refuge.

The suspect, identified as an Afghan national who previously worked with the U.S. military, is being used as a pretext for broader, more restrictive immigration policies. Trump’s statement, calling the shooting an “act of terror” and his administration’s intent to remove foreigners “from any country who doesn’t belong here,” is a dangerous conflation of an isolated incident with broader immigration policy.

Conclusion: A Cycle of Misdirected Responses

The narrative spun by Trump in response to the shooting is a classic example of his administration’s propensity for misdirected responses to crises. Instead of addressing the root causes—America’s gun culture and the need for comprehensive gun control—Trump opts for a superficial show of force and punitive immigration policies.

This approach is not only ineffective but also harmful, diverting attention and resources from where they are most needed. It’s a cycle of systemic error that underlines much of Trump’s presidency: respond first, think later—if at all. The consequences of such governance are not just ineffective policies but a deeper entrenchment of the very issues they purport to solve.
