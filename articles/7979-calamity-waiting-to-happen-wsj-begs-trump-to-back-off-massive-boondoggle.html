'Calamity waiting to happen': WSJ begs Trump to back off massive 'boondoggle'

By Paulo Santos

The conservative Wall Street Journal editorial board urged President Donald Trump on Friday not to get involved in what they called a “boondoggle” of a liquefied natural gas project in eastern Africa.

The project is run by the French company TotalEnergies, and backed by the U.S. Export-Import Bank — but there are numerous red flags that have caused the British and Dutch governments to pull out, and, the board argued, the Trump administration should follow their lead.

“The project is a calamity waiting to happen,” wrote the board. “The region is prone to political instability, so much so that the Mozambique government last decade enlisted the Wagner Group, Russia’s mercenary outfit, to assist with security. TotalEnergies paused the project in 2021 after attacks by Islamic insurgents in the region. A human-rights group last month filed a criminal complaint with the French counterterrorism prosecutor’s office accusing TotalEnergies of being complicit with war crimes by Mozambique soldiers who were trying to put down the insurgency.”

TotalEnergies denies the allegations, said the board, and some have argued they were cooked up by “the climate lobby” to put down fossil fuels — but that doesn’t matter, the board argued, because the “project’s risks are incontestable” as violence erupts in Mozambique yet again.

“CEO Patrick Pouyanne is trying to undermine U.S. LNG growth and European sanctions on Russian gas,” argued the board, noting that Pouyanne has stated that if Trump is “pushing to abandon Russian LNG quicker, it’s because somewhere he knows that it will boost prices ... We must keep the diversity of our supplies.”

“He no doubt worries that Europe’s plan to wean itself off Russian gas will hurt his risky investments in a country where Mr. Putin can ruin a business on his political whim,” the board concluded. “Why would Mr. Trump finance another one of the Frenchman’s dubious bets?”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Wall Street Journal’s Editorial on Trump’s Involvement in the African LNG Project

In a rare alignment of conservative reasoning with environmental and geopolitical caution, the Wall Street Journal editorial board has issued a stark warning against former President Donald Trump’s potential entanglement in a liquefied natural gas (LNG) project in eastern Africa. Spearheaded by the French conglomerate TotalEnergies and buoyed by the U.S. Export-Import Bank, this venture has already seen the prudent withdrawal of British and Dutch investments due to a constellation of “red flags” that seem to flutter ominously in the project’s wake.

The Calamity Ahead

The editorial board minces no words in forecasting the project as a “calamity waiting to happen,” a statement that finds its footing in the unstable political terrain of Mozambique. The region’s volatility is underscored by the Mozambican government’s previous engagement with the Wagner Group, a Russian mercenary organization, for security purposes. This fact alone should raise alarm bells, given the Wagner Group’s notorious reputation and Russia’s geopolitical maneuverings.

TotalEnergies’ decision to pause the project in 2021, following attacks by Islamic insurgents, only adds layers to the already complex risk profile. Furthermore, the company’s entanglement in allegations of complicity with war crimes—accusations strong enough to prompt a criminal complaint in France—casts a long, dark shadow over the project’s ethical and operational integrity.

The Climate Lobby Strawman

The editorial’s dismissal of concerns as potentially fabricated by “the climate lobby” to undermine fossil fuels is a predictable, yet flimsy, attempt to redirect the conversation from the project’s inherent flaws to broader debates on energy sources. This maneuver fails to address the substantive issues at hand: the undeniable risks posed by political instability, security challenges, and ethical quandaries.

The Geopolitical Gambit

CEO Patrick Pouyanne’s comments, as highlighted by the board, reveal a tangled web of geopolitical and economic interests. The assertion that Trump’s push to abandon Russian LNG could inadvertently benefit TotalEnergies by inflating prices lays bare the intricate dance of energy politics and market dynamics. Pouyanne’s apprehension regarding Europe’s shift away from Russian gas underscores the precarious nature of investing in regions susceptible to the whims of political heavyweights like Putin.

Why Indeed?

The editorial concludes with a pointed question that should resonate with any observer wary of geopolitical entanglements and dubious investments: “Why would Mr. Trump finance another one of the Frenchman’s dubious bets?” This question is not merely rhetorical—it’s a clarion call for a reassessment of the United States’ involvement in foreign energy projects fraught with ethical, environmental, and political peril.

In essence, the Wall Street Journal editorial board presents a compelling case against American involvement in TotalEnergies’ LNG project in Mozambique. By dissecting the project’s multifaceted risks and the questionable motivations behind it, the board not only advises caution but also implicitly questions the wisdom of prioritizing fossil fuel investments in an era of increasing environmental and geopolitical instability.
