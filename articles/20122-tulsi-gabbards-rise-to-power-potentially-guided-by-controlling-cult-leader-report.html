Tulsi Gabbard’s rise to power potentially guided by ‘controlling cult’ leader: report

The Cult of Influence Was Never the Footnote

The Power Was Not the Title

The Washington Post’s reporting says Tulsi Gabbard’s political life may have been steered for years by Chris Butler, the leader of the Science of Identity Foundation, a group many former members describe as a cult. That matters because the formal job titles were always a distraction: the question is not who held office on paper, but who shaped the choices that officeholder made.

Guidance Is a Sanitized Word for Control

The memos described in the report are not harmless spiritual coaching. They allegedly told Gabbard what legislation to introduce, which policies to embrace, and how to perform on television. That is not vague mentorship. That is disciplined political management, packaged inside a private hierarchy that demanded secrecy and obedience.

The Real Decision-Maker

If the reporting holds, Butler did not merely influence a politician from the margins. He appears to have sat upstream of her public judgment for decades, narrowing the range of what she could say and do. That is the political reality the article’s language partly obscures by using “guided,” a word that softens what sounds much closer to command.

How Misdirection Works

The easier story is to treat this as personal eccentricity: an unusual relationship, a strange belief system, a politician with complicated loyalties. That framing is useful because it keeps attention on the visible figure and away from the structure behind her. But the source points to something more corrosive: deliberate political output shaped by a closed authority that answered to no electorate and disclosed nothing.

The Pattern Behind the Person

This is what institutional vulnerability looks like when private obedience networks enter public life. Politics becomes a delivery mechanism for a hidden chain of command, while the public is asked to debate the surface personality instead of the machinery steering it. The result is not confusion. It is the normalization of shadow governance, where power hides inside devotion and then moves through elected office wearing its victim’s name.
