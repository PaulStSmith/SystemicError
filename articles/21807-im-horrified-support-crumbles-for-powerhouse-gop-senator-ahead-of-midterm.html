'I’m horrified': Support crumbles for 'powerhouse' GOP Senator ahead of midterm

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

Susan Collins Is Not a Moderate. She Is a Delivery Mechanism.

The Story Beneath the Story

The Washington Post story says Maine Democrats and independents who once tolerated Susan Collins are done with her. That is the factual surface. The real story is simpler and uglier: a Republican senator built her brand on pleading innocence while repeatedly underwriting the conservative project that made Trump possible, and now the voters she relied on are noticing the bill.

Who Has Power

Donald Trump holds presidential power. Susan Collins holds Senate power. That is the institutional map, and it matters more than the consultant fiction about “mavericks” and “centrists.” Collins is not a powerless observer of Republican governance. She is part of the machinery that confirms judges, legitimizes nominees, and props up the federal right when it needs a respectable face.

Her vote for Brett Kavanaugh was not a footnote. It was a decision with consequences. So was her willingness to keep treating Republican rule as a problem of tone instead of content. The source shows voters finally connecting the performance to the outcome.

Collins’ Brand Was Always the Product

Collins built a political identity on being the Republican who could be trusted by Democrats and independents. That trust was never a virtue. It was a subsidy. Blue voters gave her cover, and she spent it on the conservative legal project, partisan normalization, and the institutional strengthening of the same movement that now runs on cruelty and grievance.

That is why the line from one voter about Kavanaugh being the “crack in the teapot” lands so hard. It was never just one vote. It was the moment the branding failed to conceal the operating system.

The Misdirection Works Until It Doesn’t

Trump’s claim that voters are mad at Republicans, not him, is self-serving nonsense, but it contains a grain of political reality: Republican voters often punish the party’s lesser-known enablers before they punish the main author of the damage. In Maine, that dynamic is breaking in reverse. Voters are no longer willing to treat Collins as an escape hatch from Trumpism.

The article’s framing still flattens responsibility by making this a story about whether Collins can hang on to her “usual cabal of blue voters.” That language almost politely disguises a harder truth. Those voters were asked to stabilize Republican power with their ballots, and Collins used that stability to remain useful to the right. The loss of patience is not volatility. It is belated political clarity.

The Decision Trail Is Visible

The source names several triggers: Collins’ vote for Kavanaugh, her support for RFK Jr., and the recent killing of a 26-year-old Colombian immigrant that sharpened disgust around the human cost of the political order Republicans defend. Each detail points in the same direction. The issue is not confusion or drift. It is a chain of decisions made by people with power, defended by institutions that prefer plausibility to accountability.

Collins now faces Troy Jackson, and the stakes are obvious because the Senate is a power center, not a book club. A four-seat shift can flip control. That is why the collapse of Collins’ bipartisan mystique matters. It is not about one senator’s feelings. It is about whether Democratic and independent voters will keep underwriting Republican authority in the name of moderation.

Maine Is a Warning to the Center

Maine is showing what happens when a Republican’s “independent” image finally collides with her record. Once the camouflage falls away, the bargain looks fraudulent: vote for the conservative, forgive the nominee, excuse the courts, absorb the damage, repeat. That arrangement survives only as long as voters accept being treated as props in someone else’s bipartisan theater.

The larger pattern is straightforward. Authoritarian politics does not rely only on its loudest figures. It also depends on respectable Republicans who launder the agenda through institutional calm. Collins is that kind of figure. The voters turning away from her are not overreacting. They are recognizing that a polished excuse for reactionary power is still reactionary power.
