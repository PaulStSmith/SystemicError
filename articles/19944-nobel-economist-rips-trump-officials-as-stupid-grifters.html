Nobel economist rips Trump officials as 'stupid grifters'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 12, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unveiling of Howard Lutnick: A Study in Amorality and Stupidity

President Donald Trump with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on February 10, 2025 (Official White House photo by Abe McNatt)

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is facing calls to resign following what Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-Maryland) describes as having “misrepresented the extent of” his “relationship with” the late billionaire financier and convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Lutnick, during a Senate Appropriations subcommittee hearing on Tuesday, February 10, acknowledged that he had lunch with Epstein even after he had been convicted of sex crimes.

Lutnick, however, testified, “I have nothing to hide. Absolutely nothing.”

In a scathing Substack column posted the following day, liberal economist Paul Krugman points to Lutnick as a glaring example of the type of people President Donald Trump’s second administration is attracting. And he stresses that Trump loyalists aren’t necessarily “evil masterminds,” but rather, “stupid, amoral grifters.”

“There’s a longstanding tradition in American politics of what Richard Hofstadter famously called the paranoid style — a way of thinking that sees conspiracies lurking everywhere,” Krugman explains. “MAGA World is particularly riddled with conspiracy thinking, from George Soros and Jewish space lasers, QAnon and the Great Replacement Theory, to Italian satellites hacking into voting machines to deliver the 2020 election to Joe Biden. But these are far-fetched fantasies. The truth is far more banal and shocking.”

Krugman continues, “There are people in positions of great power in the U.S. government engaged in evil conspiracies against everything that is good and decent. Their conspiracies are far more extensive and damaging than almost anyone imagined. But there are no evil masterminds behind this. Only amoral, stupid grifters like Howard Lutnick…. The only waves he has made are a result of his exceptional combination of stupidity and offensive tone-deafness.”

Lutnick, Krugman argues, “sits at the intersection of not one, but at least two ugly conspiracies” — one involving the cryptocurrency Tether and a “money laundering” case, the other involving Epstein.

“Lutnick has, in the past, vehemently denied having any association with Jeffrey Epstein, insisting that he severed all contact with the pedophile ringleader in 2005,” the economist laments. “But even the highly limited, extremely redacted release of the Epstein files — everything we’ve seen reeks of a major coverup — shows that he was flat-out lying. Not only did he stay in close contact with Epstein, the two men appear to have gone into business together…. In any previous administration, Lutnick’s naked conflicts of interest and his Epstein lies would have led to his immediate departure…. While MAGA World’s fantasy villains like George Soros are brilliant and subtle, MAGA’s real villains are uncouth and dim-witted. Yet they carry out their sinister schemes in broad daylight.”

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of Howard Lutnick: A Study in Amorality and Stupidity

In a recent exposé that should shock no one yet somehow still manages to, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick finds himself embroiled in a scandal that reads like a poorly written tragedy, teeming with the kind of characters you’d hope were fictional. Senator Chris Van Hollen (D-Maryland) has called for Lutnick’s resignation after it was revealed he “misrepresented the extent of” his relationship with the notorious Jeffrey Epstein. But let’s be clear: this is not just a simple case of poor judgment or a one-off lapse in moral compass. This is indicative of a much larger, more systemic issue that festers within the corridors of power.

Lutnick’s admission of having lunch with Epstein post-conviction is not merely an embarrassing faux pas; it’s a glaring spotlight on the kind of company the current administration finds palatable. And before the defenses come rolling in—no, this is not about giving someone a second chance. This is about a calculated decision to maintain ties with a convicted sex offender, a decision that speaks volumes about one’s character, or lack thereof.

Liberal economist Paul Krugman doesn’t mince words in his critique, painting Lutnick as a prime example of the caliber of individuals attracted to President Donald Trump’s second administration. Krugman’s depiction of Trump loyalists as “stupid, amoral grifters” is not just a scathing rebuke; it’s an alarm bell. The real horror, as Krugman aptly notes, is not in the far-fetched conspiracy theories that pervade MAGA World, but in the banal reality of who is actually wielding power.

The real conspiracies, as opposed to the ludicrous spectacles of “Jewish space lasers” and “Italian satellites,” are being orchestrated not by “evil masterminds” but by individuals like Lutnick, whose actions are defined by a blend of ineptitude and moral vacancy. It’s a sobering reminder that the true danger often lies not in the genius of evil, but in the banality of it.

Lutnick’s connections to both a cryptocurrency scandal and Jeffrey Epstein are not mere coincidences but symptomatic of a broader problem of accountability and transparency—or the lack thereof. His prior denials of any association with Epstein, now proven to be lies, underscore a troubling tendency to operate within shadows, engaging in activities that, under any other administration, would have spelled immediate political death.

Krugman’s comparison of MAGA’s real villains to its imagined ones is particularly poignant. While conspiracy theorists vilify figures like George Soros with attributions of brilliance and subtlety, the actual architects of America’s decline are neither: they are “uncouth and dim-witted,” yet frighteningly effective in their schemes, precisely because they operate in the open, shielded by a culture of impunity.

The case of Howard Lutnick is more than just a scandal; it’s a symptom of a diseased political system where amorality and stupidity are not disqualifiers but, seemingly, prerequisites. The fact that Lutnick’s egregious connections and actions have not led to his immediate ousting is a damning indictment of the current administration’s ethical compass—or, more accurately, its utter lack thereof.

In any sane world, Lutnick’s ties to Epstein alone, not to mention his involvement in other dubious activities, would be more than enough to demand his resignation. Yet, here we stand, in a world where such revelations are met with a shrug by those in power. If this does not serve as a wake-up call for the urgent need for systemic change, one shudders to think what will.
