A three-time Donald Trump voter’s declaration that the president’s current term “just absolutely sucks” sent former Trump FBI official Dan Bongino into a tailspin over the weekend.

It started when self-identified “American nationalist” Evan Kilgore took to X to ask his 187,000 followers, “Who else feels like the second Trump Administration just absolutely sucks??”

The MAGA influencer added, “I say this as a 3 time Trump voter.”

That led to a massive response from Bongino, who has been at war with some parts of MAGA since dropping out of the Trump FBI team.

“Did you ever NOTICE that the same people constantly talking about ‘noticing,’ never seem to notice all of the incredible things happening in the country right now? It’s as if they have an agenda, and that agenda is most definitely not your agenda. I don’t know the deformed hobbit in the quoted post below, except for seeing his occasional bulls--- on my timeline. But, you’ve all been seeing exactly this type of garbage on your timelines too. It’s not authentic, I promise you,” the podcaster ranted. “And once you NOTICE it, you’ll never un-notice it. No one is asking real movement people to blindly cheerlead for the administration, but facts do matter. Here are the facts for the astroturf doomers who claim everything ‘sucks,’ while doing jack s--- to fix anything themselves.”

Bongino then listed 16 purported facts about the Trump team’s accomplishments before adding, “And this is just the first year of the Trump 47 administration. So forgive me, but a big F#%* YOU to the astroturf doomer crowd. I’m tired of your bulls---, I see exactly what you claim to ‘notice’ and not, and so do a posse of others.”

Our Analysis:

A Breakdown of Bongino’s Meltdown and the MAGA Maelstrom

In a recent spectacle of intra-MAGA warfare, Dan Bongino, a former Trump FBI official turned podcaster, lashed out with vitriolic fervor at Evan Kilgore, a self-declared “American nationalist” and three-time Trump voter, over criticisms of the current Trump administration. The incident underscores the volatile, cannibalistic nature of political loyalty within the MAGA camp, revealing a deep-seated anxiety over the movement’s direction and narrative control.

The Trigger

Evan Kilgore’s critique of the “second Trump Administration” as being underwhelming, voiced on the social media platform X to his significant following, reveals a crack in the once seemingly impervious MAGA facade. Kilgore’s statement, “Who else feels like the second Trump Administration just absolutely sucks??” coupled with his self-identification as a “3 time Trump voter,” suggests not just a moment of disillusionment but a potential shift in the MAGA movement’s internal consensus.

Bongino’s Tirade: A Dissection

Bongino’s response to Kilgore’s critique is a textbook example of ad hominem attacks mixed with a desperate plea for unity under the guise of factual debate. His choice of words, referring to Kilgore disparagingly and denying the authenticity of his grievances, not only seeks to delegitimize criticism but also to paint any dissent as betrayal or manipulation by unseen forces.

The Irony of Accusation

Bongino accuses Kilgore and, by extension, any critics within the MAGA ranks of having “an agenda” that does not align with the movement’s goals. This accusation is rich with irony, considering Bongino’s own agenda of maintaining a semblance of unity and purpose within a movement increasingly characterized by infighting and disillusionment.

The “Facts” Fallacy

Bongino’s recourse to “facts” about the Trump team’s accomplishments is a classic diversion tactic. By listing purported achievements, he attempts to shift the conversation from subjective dissatisfaction to a debate over objective reality. However, the effectiveness of this strategy is questionable, as it fails to address the underlying causes of disillusionment within the MAGA ranks.

The Bigger Picture

This incident is emblematic of a larger trend within the MAGA movement and, more broadly, within American political discourse. The expectation of unwavering loyalty, the vilification of dissent, and the reduction of complex political realities to simplistic narratives of success or betrayal are symptomatic of a deeply polarized and increasingly intolerant political environment.

Conclusion

Dan Bongino’s response to Evan Kilgore’s criticism of the Trump administration reveals more than just a moment of intra-party conflict; it exposes the fragility of the MAGA movement’s ideological foundations and the lengths to which its adherents will go to silence internal criticism. While Bongino frames his tirade as a defense of the “real movement,” his actions highlight a movement in crisis, struggling to define itself in the face of evolving political realities and internal dissent.
