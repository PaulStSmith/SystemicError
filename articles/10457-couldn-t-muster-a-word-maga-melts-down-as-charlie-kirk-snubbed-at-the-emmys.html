MAGA is criticizing the Emmys for snubbing Charlie Kirk, a conservative podcast host and Turning Point USA founder, who was shot and killed last week during a speaking event at a Utah university.

Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) on Monday attacked Hollywood and reshared a Hollywood Reporter article, apparently upset over how Kirk was not mentioned during the award show acceptance speeches Sunday night, Politico reports.

“Hollywood claims they’re all about free speech,” Cruz wrote on social media. “And yet not even ONE of them could muster a word about @charliekirk11 being assassinated because of his speech.”

Cruz turned his attention to Hannah Einbinder, who won the Emmy for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series, and declared, “F--- ICE and free Palestine!”

The event mainly dodged politics and avoided Kirk's name, Politico reports.

And MAGA wasn't happy.

“The Emmys made ZERO reference to Charlie Kirk who was assassinated... This is the Hollywood left,” a user on X with more than 36,000 followers wrote.

Another conservative voice on X called Hollywood "evil" and "a joke," saying "it ignored a political voice with a major media presence who was gunned down just 5 days earlier."

"Charlie Kirk lost his life debating in the open for all to see and challenge. These clowns get paid millions to tell partisan “jokes” to their partisan audiences while losing money for their employers," a Salem Radio network affiliate posted on X, calling the show "insulting."

Cruz and other MAGA lawmakers have criticized people who have spoken out against Kirk's rhetoric, even calling for the firing of people who spoke out against right-wing extremism.

Our Analysis:

The Emmys, Charlie Kirk, and the MAGA Critique: A Scathing Analysis

In the latest episode of political theatre that underscores the chasm between Hollywood and the MAGA movement, the Emmys' alleged snub of Charlie Kirk has ignited a fresh wave of controversy. Kirk, a conservative podcast host and the founder of Turning Point USA, tragically lost his life to an assassin's bullet at a Utah university. Yet, his absence from Emmy acceptance speeches has become a peculiar battleground for those claiming the mantle of free speech advocacy, particularly within the conservative ranks.

The Hollywood vs. MAGA Showdown

Senator Ted Cruz's (R-TX) outrage over Hollywood's supposed silence on Kirk's assassination is as predictable as it is flawed. Cruz, leveraging a Hollywood Reporter article, took to social media to lambast Hollywood figures for their failure to acknowledge Kirk's death, asserting that it was a glaring omission motivated by political bias. "Hollywood claims they’re all about free speech,” Cruz wrote, “And yet not even ONE of them could muster a word about @charliekirk11 being assassinated because of his speech."

Let's dissect this for a moment. The expectation that the Emmys – an awards ceremony designed to celebrate achievements in television – should serve as a platform for political eulogies is not only misplaced but also reveals a profound misunderstanding of the event's purpose. It is a leap of logic to equate the absence of mention of Kirk with a wholesale rejection of free speech principles.

The Misdirected Anger

Cruz's targeting of Hannah Einbinder, who won for Outstanding Supporting Actress in a Comedy Series and used her acceptance speech to voice political statements, underscores a selective outrage that is as transparent as it is opportunistic. To juxtapose Einbinder’s political statements with the failure to mention Kirk is to conflate unrelated aspects of the discourse surrounding free speech and political engagement in the entertainment industry.

Moreover, the MAGA movement's characterization of Hollywood as "evil" and "a joke" for not referencing Kirk's tragic demise simplifies complex issues surrounding free speech, violence, and the role of entertainers. The assertion that Kirk "lost his life debating in the open for all to see and challenge" while entertainers are disparaged for their partisan performances misses the mark on several fronts. It neglects the fact that entertainers, too, often face significant backlash for their political stances, and it ignores the broader context of why Kirk was a controversial figure to begin with.

The Real Insult

To claim that the Emmys, or any awards show for that matter, has an obligation to address every political tragedy or figure is to misunderstand the nature of these events. The real insult here is not the absence of Kirk's mention at the Emmys, but rather the instrumentalization of his tragic death for political point-scoring. The focus, instead, should be on addressing the conditions that lead to such violence and fostering a more constructive dialogue around contentious issues.

Conclusion

The MAGA critique of the Emmys over the supposed snub of Charlie Kirk epitomizes the current political climate's divisiveness. It's a climate where tragedies are leveraged for ideological battles, where the expectation of universal homage across all platforms is both unrealistic and indicative of a deeper malaise. The conversation we should be having is far removed from who gets mentioned at an awards show; it should be about how to engage with each other's ideas without resorting to violence or vilification. But as long as the focus remains on scoring political points through outrage, that much-needed conversation remains on the back burner.
