'Art of the Deal' guy Trump suddenly can't make deals: NY Times

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 2, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Funding Fiasco: A Scathing Analysis

U.S. President Donald Trump makes a sports announcement at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 5, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

President Donald Trump posted on Truth Social Friday that he’s asking “the Court to clarify how we can legally fund SNAP as soon as possible,” after a federal judge ordered his administration to continue funding the program during the shutdown.

But Trump already knows how to shuffle money, says the New York Times Editorial Board. He’s already been funneling it wherever he wants for months, regardless of rules.

“President Trump has played fast and loose with federal law during the current government shutdown to fund the things he considers important,” the Board said. “He has found ways to pay military service members and F.B.I. agents. He has distributed tariff revenues to women with small children and arranged billions of dollars in financial support for Argentina. He has even ordered the Interior Department to keep federal lands open for hunting.”

But what Trump has refused to fund is as equally telling, wrote the editors.

“As the shutdown enters its second month, the president still will not agree to an extension of the federal tax credits that allow millions of Americans to afford health insurance,” the wrote. And up until Friday, the Trump administration declared it would stop distributing food stamps to more than 40 million lower-income families, declining to tap the program’s emergency reserve fund.

The government shutdown is causing Americans pain on many fronts, with more than a million federal workers going unpaid and the Small Business Administration not making loans. Additionally, regulators are not conducting many routine safety inspections of food processing plants.

Trump praises himself as the force behind the Art of the Deal, but the editorial board said he and his congressional allies “could end all of this by doing what they should have done months ago: making a deal.”

“Under current Senate rules, Republicans manifestly do not have enough votes to pass a funding bill on their own, and it is absurd that they continue to insist that Democrats should simply acquiesce. The hard work of governing in a democracy is hammering out a compromise.”

Instead, the editors said Trump has sought to heap pressure on Democrats to concede without compromise by suspending funding for projects in blue states, including the important Hudson River train tunnel between New Jersey and New York, and he has targeted mass transit in Chicago for cuts and rescissions.

His most recent ploy to raise the stakes by suspending the distribution of food stamps “is unconscionable,” said the editorial board, however passionately he claims in his Truth Social post that “I do NOT want Americans to go hungry.”

“Republicans say their party has become the party of the American working class. But many working families rely on the tax credits to afford health insurance. And many of those same families rely on food stamps to put enough food on the table,” wrote the editors. “Mr. Trump can serve their needs by demonstrating his skills as a negotiator. It’s time to make a deal.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Funding Fiasco: A Scathing Analysis

In a recent spectacle of political maneuvering, President Donald Trump has once again showcased his adeptness at navigating the murky waters of federal funding—albeit with a compass that seemingly points only towards his whims. According to a piece by the New York Times Editorial Board, Trump has been playing a high-stakes game of financial chess, reallocating funds with a brazen disregard for established rules and protocols. But as the government shutdown drags on, the consequences of his actions—or lack thereof—have become alarmingly apparent.

Trump’s Selective Funding: A Clear Agenda

The President’s approach to funding during the shutdown is as strategic as it is selective. On one hand, he has demonstrated a remarkable ability to funnel money towards sectors he deems critical, such as military service members, FBI agents, and even international financial support for Argentina. His decision to keep federal lands open for hunting further underscores his commitment to certain constituencies and their interests.

However, Trump’s fiscal generosity seems to halt abruptly at the doorstep of initiatives crucial to lower-income Americans. The refusal to extend federal tax credits for health insurance and the near cessation of food stamps distribution until a judge’s order highlights a glaring disregard for the welfare of millions. This selective funding paints a stark picture of a presidency prioritizing political leverage over the livelihood of the American populace.

The Art of the No-Deal

Trump’s self-proclaimed mastery of negotiation, famously dubbed the “Art of the Deal,” falls flat in the face of the ongoing government shutdown. The editorial rightly criticizes the administration and its congressional allies for their failure to broker a compromise with Democrats. The insistence on a unilateral approach to passing a funding bill, despite clear Senate arithmetic, reveals a disturbing preference for deadlock over democracy.

Moreover, Trump’s strategy to escalate the situation by withholding funding for projects in Democrat-leaning states and essential services like food stamps is not only unconscionable but strategically flawed. Such tactics not only deepen the divide but also amplify the suffering of countless Americans, many of whom are part of the working class the Republican Party claims to represent.

The Path Forward

The editorial board’s call for Trump to utilize his negotiation skills to end the shutdown and address the needs of all Americans is more than a plea for bipartisan cooperation; it’s a demand for a return to basic governance. The suspension of essential services like food stamps under the guise of political strategy is a stark reminder of the real-life implications of governmental inaction.

In a democracy, governance is a responsibility, not a bargaining chip. The editorial’s critique of Trump’s handling of the shutdown serves as a potent reminder of the need for compromise, compassion, and a genuine commitment to the welfare of the nation. Trump’s claim on Truth Social that he does not want Americans to go hungry rings hollow in the absence of actions to back it up. The time for artifice is over; it’s time for a deal rooted in the reality of Americans’ needs.

In conclusion, Trump’s handling of federal funding during the government shutdown is a testament to a presidency marred by selective priorities and a disturbing willingness to sacrifice the well-being of millions for political gain. The New York Times Editorial Board’s critique is not just a denouncement of Trump’s tactics but a clarion call for a course correction in the spirit of American values and democracy.
