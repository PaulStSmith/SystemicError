'Sad': Former FBI deputy director shows how departing Trump pick 'terrified' his employees

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino, next to. U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi, speaks at a press conference following the arrest in the D.C. pipe bomber investigation, at the Justice Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 4, 2025. REUTERS/Jessica Koscielniak

A former FBI leader trashed his successor Dan Bongino as he prepares to depart his post after less than a year on the job.

Andrew McCabe, who served in that role for nearly two years at the start of President Donald Trump’s first term, told “CNN This Morning” that Bongino’s complaints months ago about the tediousness of his job demonstrated how poorly suited he was for a leadership post in the federal law enforcement agency.

“This surprises absolutely no one, principally because of comments like that,” McCabe said, “and I would also add the absence of other comments that you would have expected all along from a deputy director, which is the incredible privilege of being able to work at the absolute pinnacle of the world’s premier law enforcement agency, working with the greatest employees on earth.”

“You know, I thought it was sad that in some of the comments I saw him release, or at least one of the comments he released last night about it on on social media, he never even referenced the employees,” McCabe added. “So I think it’s it’s the expected end of a of a period where that seat was filled by a guy who was unprepared for it and fundamentally unqualified for it. So I think at the end of the day, it’s probably a good thing for both Mr. Bongino and the FBI that he moves along.”

McCabe told host Audie Cornish that FBI Director Kash Patel was just as unsuited for his role.

“I think what we’ve seen now, there may be more inside the FBI that we’re not aware of, but what we’ve seen essentially is a reign of chaos where senior leadership has been expelled, run out of the building simply because they were there during a prior, during a prior term, which is now in disfavor,” McCabe said. “That loss of leadership, I think you’re seeing subtly in these major investigations that the FBI is engaged in very publicly, people I know for a fact, from talking to many FBI people, people are terrified by the fact that their their careers may be cut short simply because they worked on matters that are now disfavored.”

“Yeah, we’ve they’ve instituted polygraphs to find people who may be disloyal to the director,” he added. “These are things that haven’t happened in the FBI since J. Edgar Hoover, and they are ultimately bad for the organization in the country.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fallout: McCabe vs. Bongino and the FBI’s Leadership Crisis

The recent comments made by Andrew McCabe, a former FBI leader, about his successor Dan Bongino, and the broader implications for the FBI under the current administration paint a picture of institutional turmoil at one of the United States’ most critical law enforcement agencies. McCabe’s scathing critique of Bongino, aired on “CNN This Morning,” underscores not just a personal disappointment but hints at deeper systemic issues within the FBI.

McCabe’s Main Gripes:

  1. Bongino’s Alleged Unfitness for the Role: McCabe did not mince words when he described Bongino as “unprepared” and “fundamentally unqualified” for the leadership post he held. The crux of McCabe’s argument revolves around Bongino’s public complaints about the job’s tediousness, which, in McCabe’s view, betrays a lack of appreciation for the privilege of serving at the FBI’s helm.

  2. Lack of Respect for FBI Employees: A particularly poignant critique from McCabe is Bongino’s apparent disregard for the FBI’s workforce. The failure to acknowledge the employees, according to McCabe, is emblematic of Bongino’s unsuitability for his role, reflecting a broader disrespect for the institution itself.

  3. A Pattern of Chaos under Current Leadership: McCabe extends his criticism beyond Bongino, implicating FBI Director Kash Patel in fostering an environment of instability. He describes a “reign of chaos,” marked by the purging of senior leaders and a culture of fear among agents worried about their career longevity due to political witch-hunts reminiscent of the darkest days of J. Edgar Hoover.

The Implications Are Alarming:

McCabe’s allegations, if accurate, point to a deeply concerning trend within the FBI, where political loyalty is prized over professional competence and institutional integrity. The introduction of polygraph tests to root out those deemed disloyal to the director is a chilling development that should alarm not just FBI employees but all Americans who value the rule of law and the importance of an impartial, effective federal law enforcement agency.

A Cautionary Tale of Leadership Failure:

What this narrative underscores is the critical importance of leadership in safeguarding the integrity and effectiveness of vital institutions like the FBI. Leadership is not just about managing the day-to-day operations but about inspiring trust, upholding the agency’s core values, and protecting its people from the corrosive effects of political interference. The alleged failure of Bongino, and by extension, Patel, to fulfill these roles not only undermines the FBI but also erodes public trust in the institution’s ability to impartially enforce the law.

Conclusion:

The saga of McCabe’s critique of Bongino and the broader leadership issues within the FBI reflect a systemic error that goes beyond individual personalities. It’s a stark reminder of the dangers posed by placing individuals unprepared or unwilling to uphold the sanctity of their office at the helm of critical national institutions. The consequences of such missteps are far-reaching, affecting not just the morale of the agency’s dedicated employees but the very fabric of American democracy. The time for accountability and a reassessment of leadership values at the FBI is not just necessary; it’s urgent.
