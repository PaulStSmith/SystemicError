'Why now?' GOP lawmaker questions new probe into Epstein New Mexico complex

By Paulo Santos

A Republican state lawmaker is voicing concerns as New Mexico legislators push to investigate Jeffrey Epstein’s 8,000-acre compound that sits about 35 miles south of the state capital.

On Friday, Democratic state Rep. Andrea Romero officially introduced a proposal to establish a “truth commission” – at a cost of $2.5 million – to investigate activities at Epstein’s complex, known as “Zorro Ranch,” KOAT-TV, a local ABC affiliate, reported Sunday. But for one Republican state lawmaker, the timing of the probe was suspect.

“Why now?” said Republican state Rep. Stafani Lord on Friday, according to KOAT-TV. “Why not a long time ago? Every time I ride my motorcycle past [Zorro Ranch], I get sick to my stomach.”

The outlet also reported that Lord had raised her questions in the context of ongoing political tensions surrounding the matter, and made reference to President Donald Trump’s supposed pledge to release files related to Epstein.

Epstein, who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, had a long personal relationship with Trump, a relationship that has plagued his second stint in the White House as new revelations about their relationship continue to be unearthed.

Epstein purchased Zorro Ranch in 1993 from former New Mexico Gov. Bruce King, and is alleged to have sexually abused minors at the location alongside his longtime accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell. He hosted powerful figures at the compound throughout the years, including Britain’s Prince Andrew, former New Mexico Gov. Bill Richardson and celebrity Woody Allen.

Epstein had also reportedly planned to use Zorro Ranch to “seed the human race with his DNA” by impregnating women at the compound, the New York Times reported in 2019.

According to Romero, who represents Santa Fe, the proposed investigation would probe current and former state officials as to their knowledge of the activities at Zorro Ranch.

“This commission will specifically seek the truth about what officials knew, how crimes were unreported or reported, and how the state can ensure that this essentially never happens again,” Romero said. “There’s no complete record of what occurred.”

Zorro Ranch would besoldin 2023 to an undisclosed buyer, and its name changed to “Rancho de San Rafael.” The mysterious owner or owners have sincefiled a complaintto dispute the property tax burden they were issued after purchasing the property.

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Proposed Investigation of Epstein’s Zorro Ranch

In the latest chapter of the seemingly never-ending Jeffrey Epstein saga, New Mexico is stirring the pot with a proposal that’s both overdue and suspect, depending on who you ask. Democratic state Rep. Republican state Rep. Stafani Lord’s reaction, “Why now?”, isn’t just a throwaway line—it’s a cannon shot across the bow, hinting at political maneuvering and long-buried secrets. Lord’s visceral response to the ranch, a place that’s been a blight on New Mexico’s landscape for decades, highlights a broader question: Why has it taken so long for a formal investigation to materialize?

Epstein’s Ties to Trump: A Thorny Issue

The specter of Donald Trump looms large over this investigation. Epstein’s “long personal relationship” with Trump is a tangled web of political and social connections that many would prefer remain in the dark. The mention of Trump’s supposed pledge to release files related to Epstein adds another layer of intrigue (and skepticism) to the proceedings. It’s a convenient time for Democrats to push this investigation, with Trump’s shadow casting a pall over the Republican Party. Yet, one can’t help but question if the pursuit of justice is the sole motivator or if political point-scoring is at play.

The Alleged Horrors of Zorro Ranch

The allegations surrounding Zorro Ranch are the stuff of nightmares. Epstein’s purported plan to use the ranch to “seed the human race with his DNA” reads like a dystopian novel plot, yet it’s uncomfortably real. The compound’s guest list reads like a who’s who of the elite, raising unnerving questions about what exactly transpired within its secluded confines. The investigation, as proposed by Rep. Romero, aims to uncover the extent of state officials’ knowledge and complicity in Epstein’s activities. But one must ask: Is $2.5 million enough to unearth the truth, or is it merely a drop in the bucket given the scale and depth of Epstein’s connections?

A Mysterious Sale and Tax Disputes

The sale of Zorro Ranch to an undisclosed buyer adds another layer of mystery to an already convoluted tale. The subsequent dispute over property tax is a mundane footnote in the saga, yet it underscores the ongoing, tangible impacts of Epstein’s legacy on the state.

Conclusion: A Cynical View on a Necessary Endeavor

The proposed investigation into Zorro Ranch is a necessary step towards justice for Epstein’s victims and accountability for those who enabled or participated in his crimes. However, the timing and political undertones cannot be ignored. This probe must not devolve into a partisan witch hunt; it should strive to uncover the truth, no matter how politically inconvenient it may be.

In a story filled with power, privilege, and perversion, the pursuit of justice is labyrinthine and fraught with potential pitfalls. As New Mexico embarks on this investigation, one can only hope that clarity and accountability will emerge from the shadows of Zorro Ranch. But given the track record of those involved, skepticism remains a prudent stance.
