'Poetic justice': Leaked Trump strategy sends GOP into tailspin

Based on an article by Daniel Hampton

Paulo SantosAugust 28, 20263 min read

Trump’s Bad Polls Are Not a Messaging Problem

The Story Beneath the Leak

The reported leak to The Atlantic is useful because it exposes the self-protective fiction at the center of Trump’s circle: that voters are simply too dim to recognize his “accomplishments.” That claim is not analysis. It is damage control for a president whose numbers are collapsing and whose own party is trying to run away from him.

Power Sits With Trump

The relevant fact is not that Trump is misunderstood. It is that Trump is still setting the terms of Republican politics. His team is reportedly preparing a midterm strategy built around TV ads boasting about his record, which tells you everything about where the power lies and how little the party has learned. Vulnerable Republicans are not driving the agenda; they are bracing for impact from a president they enabled and still cannot disown cleanly.

That is the real institutional story. The GOP handed him the vehicle, accepted the damage, and now wants credit for pretending it was all a misunderstanding.

The Blame-Shifting Is Deliberate

The administration’s spin depends on a familiar trick: attribute political pain to voter ignorance instead of policy consequence. Trump’s allies point to stock market highs, a closed southern border, and rising manufacturing output. But even the source material flags the sleight of hand: manufacturing output is not the same thing as factory employment. That distinction matters because it reveals the same pattern that has defined Trumpism from the start: inflate a number, ignore the distribution of harm, and demand applause for the headline while workers absorb the fallout.

Mona Charen is right to reject the fantasy that this economy can be pinned on a virus or on Joe Biden. The tariffs, the Iran war, the price spikes: those are not rhetorical accidents. They are his record. He did this. He is still doing it. The problem is not that voters fail to understand Trump’s greatness. The problem is that his movement keeps trying to rename consequence as achievement.

The Party Enabled the Collapse

The source points to Republicans “sprinting” away from Trump with no clear destination. That is not leadership. It is panic after complicity. The party spent years building itself around a man it knew was corrosive, then acted surprised when the corrosion spread to every part of the structure.

Now the same politicians who made Trump possible want to behave as if they are innocent bystanders to the mess. They are not. They helped normalize the authoritarian premise that loyalty matters more than competence, that spectacle can replace governance, and that the public can be talked out of its own experience if the ad buy is large enough.

Polling Is Not the Mystery

Trump’s all-time-low polling is being treated by his allies as a communications puzzle. It is not. It is an accountability problem. Voters can see the result of his decisions in prices, trade policy, instability, and the widening gap between promise and reality. When a president’s defenders resort to “they just don’t know how great he is,” that is not evidence of strategic sophistication. It is evidence that the story they sold no longer survives contact with ordinary life.

The reported midterm plan to flood the airwaves with self-praise may very well backfire on the party that enabled him. If it does, the lesson will be simple: propaganda works only until people have to live inside the consequences.

The Larger Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in practice: concentrate power, flatten reality, reward the loyal, and blame the public when the results become impossible to hide. Trump’s allies are not confused. They are trapped in the lie they built. And the Republican Party, which chose this bargain, is now discovering that cowardice is not a governing strategy.

The deeper lesson is not about one bad poll cycle. It is about a political order that keeps trying to turn accountability into branding. That fails eventually. The damage, unfortunately, lands long before the spin does.
