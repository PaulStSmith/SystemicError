Snubbed: Pope gives JD Vance just 17 seconds — but has long meetings with other leaders

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 19, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:JD Vance's Vatican Snub: A Closer Look

U.S. Vice President JD Vance and his wife Usha Vance attend the inaugural Mass of Pope Leo XIV’s pontificate in St. Peter’s Square at the Vatican, Sunday, May 18, 2025. Jacquelyn Martin/Pool via REUTERS

JD Vance’s efforts to schmooze were rebuffed by Pope Leo XIV Sunday — as the pontiff shook him off to meet with other world leaders.

The vice president was in the Vatican to witness the pope’s inaugural mass. Afterward, he tried to speak to the leader of the Catholic church, who shook his hand but barely spoke in the 17-second encounter.

But the pope did meet behind closed doors with many other world leaders, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and Peruvian President Dina Ercilia Boluarte Zegarra.

The awkward greeting with Vance followed a sermon in which subtle digs were aimed at President Donald Trump's administration. Though he didn't name Trump, the pope talked about “hatred, violence, prejudice, the fear of difference, and an economic paradigm that exploits the Earth’s resources and marginalizes the poorest.”

It was widely considered to be a dig at Trump’s immigration policies.

The pope has been openly critical of Vance, and penned an op-ed in which he slammed the vice president for saying caring for others should come after your own family is looked after.

“JD Vance is wrong: Jesus doesn’t ask us to rank our love for others,” he wrote.

Our Analysis:

JD Vance's Vatican Snub: A Closer Look

In a world teeming with political subtext and religious undertones, the recent interaction—or lack thereof—between Vice President JD Vance and Pope Leo XIV is a tableau rich with implications. Let's dissect this episode, not just for its awkward surface tension, but for the deeper currents it reveals.

The Snub Heard Around the World

JD Vance, known for his controversial stances and alignment with the Trump administration, found himself on the receiving end of a cold shoulder from none other than Pope Leo XIV. Despite Vance's efforts to engage, their encounter was brief and, frankly, dismissive. The pontiff's decision to engage more meaningfully with other world leaders, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and Peruvian President Dina Ercilia Boluarte Zegarra, speaks volumes. This wasn't just a missed handshake; it was a calculated diplomatic move.

The Sermon's Sharp Edges

Pope Leo XIV’s sermon, while not naming names, left little to the imagination. His condemnation of "hatred, violence, prejudice, the fear of difference, and an economic paradigm that exploits the Earth’s resources and marginalizes the poorest" is a thinly veiled critique of the Trump administration's policies—policies that Vance has publicly supported. The pope's words were not just spiritual guidance but a political broadside aimed at the heart of Trumpian philosophy.

The Pope's Pen as a Sword

Further complicating this narrative is Pope Leo XIV's direct criticism of Vance in an op-ed. The pope's rebuke of Vance's view that caring for one's family should precede care for others is not just a theological dispute; it's a public denouncement of the vice president's ethical framework. In the pope's view, Vance's stance is antithetical to the teachings of Jesus, a bold statement that underscores the deep ideological divide between Vance and the Vatican.

The Unspoken Implications

This incident is not merely about personal slights or bruised egos; it's a manifestation of the larger conflict between progressive religious leadership and conservative political movements. Pope Leo XIV's actions and words are a clear signal that the Vatican is not afraid to oppose policies and ideologies it views as contrary to Christian teachings and human decency.

Conclusion

JD Vance's rebuff at the Vatican is a microcosm of the broader ideological battle raging across the globe. It's not just about who the pope shakes hands with; it's about what those handshakes represent. In this case, Pope Leo XIV's refusal to engage deeply with Vance is a powerful statement against the politics of division and exclusion.

The Vatican, through its leader, has shown that it will not stand idly by as policies and perspectives that undermine human dignity and the sanctity of the environment prevail. This episode is a stark reminder that in the arena of global leadership, moral and ethical stands are as significant, if not more so, than diplomatic niceties.
