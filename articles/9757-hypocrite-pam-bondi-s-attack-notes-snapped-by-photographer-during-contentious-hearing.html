'Hypocrite!' Pam Bondi's attack notes snapped by photographer during contentious hearing

Attorney General Pam Bondi drew reactions from those watching her appearance before the Senate Judiciary Committee on Tuesday, when, instead of answering specific questions from Democrats, she turned to attack them personally.

Bondi attacked Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) over a campaign talking point from more than 15 years ago about his service as a U.S. Marine. She claimed Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL) took money from someone who knew sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

When it came to Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI), Reuters photographer Jonathan Ernst noticed that Bondi flipped open a file that provided her with the lines to use in attacking the Senators.

At the top of a folder was a tweet from Whitehouse from July in which he called for ethics investigations into members of the U.S. Supreme Court who may have violated rules.

The second note reads “Whitehouse” in bold letters, then fed Bondi the line, “You claim to be a social justice warrior, but you’re a member of the Senate,” and then cuts off in the photo. The next line reads, “You rail against dark money but you work with dark money groups.” The third bullet point was underlined in blue pen, reading, “You talk about corruption but you push for legislation that would ....,” and cuts off. The fourth bullet point read simply, “You’re a total hypocrite.”

She didn’t appear to use the attacks, however.

In a handwritten note below the lines, one talking point that she did attempt to deploy.

“If asked admit personnel or firings — I’ll tell you a personnel issue that I’m [missing word] agents are on the streets keeping America [missing words] a paycheck b/c you voted to shut [missing words].”

Several times, Bondi ignored questions about personnel matters by saying outright she refused to answer the question or by attacking the Democrat, saying they voted to shut down the government.

Finally, a note that she used several times, that was also handwritten, read, “Epstein— Did you take $ from Reid Hoffman???”

Hoffman appeared to know Epstein and gave money to Democratic officials, thereby connecting a Democratic member to Epstein through a few degrees of separation. Democrats have specifically asked for information about President Donald Trump’s direct relationship with Epstein and demanded that they turn over the entire investigation files.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by Attorney General Pam Bondi

In what can only be described as a circus of political evasion, Attorney General Pam Bondi’s recent performance before the Senate Judiciary Committee has provided a stark illustration of how far the art of deflection has evolved within the political arena. The tactics deployed by Bondi, rather than addressing the pressing issues at hand, turned the session into a personal attack on her interrogators. This maneuvering not only undermines the democratic process but also highlights a worrying trend in political discourse where accountability is dodged with impunity.

Attacking the Messenger Instead of the Message

Bondi’s strategy was clear from the outset: if you can’t disarm the question, disarm the questioner. Her attacks on Senators such as Richard Blumenthal and Dick Durbin were not just below the belt; they were irrelevant detours from the questions posed. Bringing up Blumenthal’s decades-old campaign missteps and attempting to smear Durbin with guilt-by-association tactics concerning Jeffrey Epstein are tactics that scream desperation and diversion. These are not the actions of an official confident in their position or moral standing but of someone using smoke and mirrors to evade scrutiny.

The use of prepared attacks, as evidenced by the notes captured by Reuters photographer Jonathan Ernst, is particularly telling. It suggests a premeditated plan to deflect and distract rather than engage with the substance of the committee’s inquiries. When Bondi flips open her file to reveal a tweet from Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse as ammunition, it’s clear that her priority lies not in transparency or accountability but in political theatre.

The Hypocrisy of Accusing Others of Hypocrisy

The notes intended to attack Whitehouse — accusing him of being a “total hypocrite” — are the pinnacle of irony. Bondi accuses the senator of railing against dark money while working with dark money groups, yet by sidestepping legitimate questions and attacking the integrity of the committee members, she embodies the very hypocrisy she seeks to expose. This tactic, while perhaps effective in a superficial sense, erodes public trust in our institutions and the officials who serve them.

Refusing to Answer: A Dangerous Precedent

Perhaps the most concerning aspect of Bondi’s testimony was her outright refusal to answer questions on personnel matters, coupled with her repeated attempts to redirect the conversation to unrelated issues. This not only stonewalls the committee’s efforts to perform its oversight function but sets a dangerous precedent for future administrations. If officials can simply refuse to answer difficult questions or resort to attacking the opposition, it paves the way for a government lacking in transparency and accountability.

Conclusion: The Need for Accountability

Bondi’s performance should not be viewed in isolation but rather as a symptom of a broader problem. The tactics of evasion, personal attacks, and playing to the gallery have become all too common. This not only disrespects the democratic process but also insults the intelligence of the public. As constituents, we must demand better from our officials. The health of our democracy depends on it.

In the end, the spectacle provided by Attorney General Pam Bondi serves as a stark reminder of the work that needs to be done to ensure our leaders are held to the highest standards of transparency and accountability. Let’s not allow the political discourse to be dragged further into the mire of evasion and personal attacks. The stakes are simply too high.
