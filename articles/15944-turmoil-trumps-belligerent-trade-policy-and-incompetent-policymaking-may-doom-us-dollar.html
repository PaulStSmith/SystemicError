'Turmoil': Trump’s 'belligerent trade policy' and 'incompetent policymaking' may doom US dollar

By Paulo Santos

Although the U.S. dollar declined in value against the euro and the British pound under former President George W. Bush during the 2000s, it later rebounded. According to XE's currency converter, 1 U.S. dollar was worth €0.87 and £0.75as of Wednesday afternoon, April 16.

The U.S. dollar, even with fluctuations, has been the world's main reserve currency since the end of World War 2. But according to The Economist, the U.S. dollar may be experiencing major vulnerability thanks to the economic chaos being created by President Donald Trump's steep new tariffs.

In an article published on April 16, The Economist explains, "Stocks down, yields down, dollar up. A reliable relationship between America's listed companies, government bonds and the value of the currency has held, in moments of panic, for most of modern financial history. Until now."

READ MORE: 'Economic downturn': Financial experts outline ways to survive Trump’s 'fiscal hell'

The Economist continues, "The turmoil in financial markets over the past month, driven by an extraordinary rise in American tariffs, has been unsettlingly different. During the stock market slumps of 2008 and 2020, for instance, the dollar rose. When investors are fearful, they normally rush to the safety of American debt, bidding up the greenback in the process. This time round, investors are eschewing Treasuries."

U.S. Treasury bonds, historically, have been considered safe havens for investors — even during recessions.

But The Economist observes, "Yields on American ten-year government bonds, which rise when prices fall, have increased from 4.2 percent to as high as 4.5 percent over the past month. Meanwhile, the dollar has fallen by over 9 percent against a basket of other currencies since its peak in mid-January. The breakdown of the once-solid relationship reflects the impulsiveness of the current American government. President Donald Trump's belligerent trade policy, his administration's incompetent policymaking and some of his advisers' suspicion about the dollar's global role have shaken foreign investors."

According to The Economist, investors "once rolled their eyes at the idea of the dollar being dethroned" — but now, must "take the notion more seriously."

"Overseas investors do not lack for reasons to park their money elsewhere," The Economist notes. "Yet those looking to limit exposure to Mr. Trump's whims face a challenge: the dollar has no obvious successor."

Our Analysis:

The Dollar Dilemma: Trump's Tariffs and the Tumultuous Tide of Trade

The value of the U.S. dollar, a beacon of economic stability and the world's main reserve currency since the twilight of World War 2, is now seemingly at the mercy of President Donald Trump's capricious trade policies. Through the lens of an article by The Economist, highlighted on April 16, we're led into the chaos unfurling within the financial markets, a direct repercussion of Trump's steep new tariffs. Here's a dive into the economic turmoil Trump's administration is spearheading, with a scathing critique of the policies undermining the dollar's dominance.

The Bush Era vs. The Trump Turmoil

The journey of the U.S. dollar, from its decline in the 2000s under George W. Bush to its position on April 16, where 1 U.S. dollar was worth €0.87 and £0.75, showcases a resilient rebound. However, the current presidency has introduced a volatile variable in the form of Trump’s trade tariffs, throwing a wrench into the once predictable machinery of financial markets.

An Unsettling Shift

The Economist spells it out: traditionally, the relationship between America's stocks, government bonds, and the dollar's value was almost sacred - until now. The introduction of "an extraordinary rise in American tariffs" has disrupted this harmony, creating a scenario where, contrary to past crises (2008 and 2020), investors are steering clear of Treasuries, historically the go-to safe haven. The result? A significant drop in the dollar's value and a rise in yields on American ten-year government bonds from 4.2 percent to as high as 4.5 percent over the past month.

The Crumbling Pillars of Trust

The article shines a glaring spotlight on the impulsiveness and incompetence of the current American government, led by Trump's belligerent trade policy. This erratic governance has not only shaken foreign investors' confidence but also sparked a critical reassessment of the dollar's unassailable position.

The Search for Alternatives

Investors, once dismissive of the dollar being dethroned, are now faced with a dire reality. The challenge? The dollar has no obvious successor. This conundrum is exacerbated by the pressing need to limit exposure to the whims of an unpredictable administration, pushing investors into a corner with few viable exits.

Conclusion: The Trump-Induced Fiscal Hell

The narrative woven by The Economist doesn’t just hint at an economic downturn; it paints a vivid picture of a financial landscape marred by the whims of a single administration. The dollar, a symbol of economic might and stability, is now navigating through troubled waters, with Trump's tariffs serving as the storm disrupting its course. The implications of this are far-reaching, not just for the U.S. but for the global economy at large. It's a stark reminder that the actions of a nation's leaders can have profound and lasting impacts on its economic standing on the world stage.

So, as the prospect of a Trump-triggered recession looms larger, the question remains: How will the U.S., and indeed the world, navigate through this fiscal hell? The answer, while unclear, is certainly worth pondering — with the hope that the ship can be steered back to calmer waters before it's too late.
