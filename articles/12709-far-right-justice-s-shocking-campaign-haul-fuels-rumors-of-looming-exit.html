Far-right justice's shocking campaign haul fuels rumors of looming exit

A notorious far-right state Supreme Court justice raised $0 in her latest fundraising haul — adding fuel to the rumors she may be planning to retire, reported Associated Press Wisconsin statehouse reporter Scott Bauer.

"Conservative Wisconsin Supreme Court Justice Rebecca Bradley, who is up for reelection in April, raised no money for a reelection bid through the end of June, according to a report her campaign filed with the state today," wrote Bauer. At the same time, he noted, one of her key liberal rivals, Wisconsin Court of Appeals Judge Chris Taylor, raised $583,000.

"The last time Bradley reported raising any money at all was in 2022 when she reported collecting $700. This year's Wisconsin Supreme Court race broke national records for spending on judicial races, with more than $100 million spent by the candidates and others," wrote Bauer. "Bradley has not officially said whether she is running again, although she told WisPolitics in April that she planned to run again. This new filing showing no fundraising or spending will only fuel speculation that Bradley may not be running again."

In response to the news, Taylor's campaign stated, "Judge Taylor is breaking fundraising records and receiving support from every corner of the state ... Rebecca Bradley is apparently sitting back and hoping far-right billionaires and special interests flood the state with campaign cash."

This comes a month after conservative Milwaukee talk radio host Mark Belling sounded the alarm that Bradley could be on the brink of retiring.

Bradley, one of the court's most outspoken defenders of right-wing causes, has been known to bitterly attack fellow judges she disagrees with, calling this year's judicial election, which saw Judge Susan Crawford hold the seat for liberals, "disgusting."

Liberals and Democratic interests managed to elect a majority on the officially nonpartisan state Supreme Court, long dominated by right-wing interests, in 2023. Since then, the court has issued rulings unwinding GOP legislative gerrymanders and protecting abortion rights.

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Justice Rebecca Bradley's $0 Fundraising Effort

Once again, the political theater offers us a spectacle that would be amusing if it weren't so deeply symptomatic of the rot within. The latest act comes from Wisconsin, where Conservative Wisconsin Supreme Court Justice Rebecca Bradley has raised precisely $0 for her reelection bid through the end of June. This revelation, as reported by Scott Bauer of the Associated Press, isn't just a trivial tidbit for the local gossip columns. It's a glaring neon sign pointing to the increasingly blurred lines between judicial independence and political maneuvering.

The Stark Contrast in Fundraising

While Bradley's campaign coffers gather dust, her "key liberal rival," Wisconsin Court of Appeals Judge Chris Taylor, has reportedly raked in a whopping $583,000. This stark discrepancy isn't just a tale of differing fundraising strategies or the ebb and flow of political support. It's a stark illustration of the political battleground that judicial races have become. The last time Bradley reported raising any money at all was a meager $700 in 2022, a sum that wouldn't even cover the catering at a modest campaign event.

The Implications of a $0 Haul

Bradley's fundraising drought adds considerable weight to the rumors of her potential retirement. If true, this would mark a significant shift in the Wisconsin Supreme Court's dynamics, which recently saw liberals and Democratic interests gain a majority. This shift has led to landmark rulings unwinding GOP legislative gerrymanders and protecting abortion rights, showcasing the court's critical role in shaping the state's legal landscape.

The Role of Big Money and Special Interests

Taylor's campaign's response to Bradley's $0 haul is particularly telling. The accusation that Bradley is "apparently sitting back and hoping far-right billionaires and special interests flood the state with campaign cash" is a stark reminder of the outsized influence of money in judicial races. This scenario is hardly unique to Wisconsin but is emblematic of a broader, systemic issue where judicial impartiality is under siege by the highest bidder.

Bradley's Contentious Tenure

Bradley, described as one of the court's "most outspoken defenders of right-wing causes," has not shied away from controversy, engaging in bitter attacks against fellow judges and lambasting the judicial election process itself as "disgusting." Such behavior hardly seems befitting of a judge's robe, yet it underscores the increasingly partisan nature of what are purportedly nonpartisan roles.

Conclusion: A Troubling Forecast

The situation surrounding Justice Rebecca Bradley's reelection campaign—or lack thereof—serves as a microcosm of the larger, systemic issues plaguing the American judicial system. The intertwining of judicial roles with partisan politics, the alarming influence of big money, and the erosion of public trust in the judiciary's impartiality are symptoms of a democracy in distress.

As the Wisconsin Supreme Court tilts on the brink of a potentially significant transformation, one thing is clear: the stakes are higher than ever. The outcome of this judicial race, and others like it across the nation, will have profound implications not just for the residents of Wisconsin, but for the very fabric of American democracy. The time has come for a rigorous examination and reevaluation of the processes that allow for such glaring systemic vulnerabilities. Without it, we risk descending further into a quagmire where justice is not blind, but blinded by the glare of gold.
