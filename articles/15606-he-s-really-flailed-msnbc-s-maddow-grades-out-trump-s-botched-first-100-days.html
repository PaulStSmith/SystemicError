'He's really flailed': MSNBC's Maddow grades out Trump's 'botched' first 100 days

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20254 min read

Making a rare appearance on Saturday as a guest on MSNBC host Katie Phang's last show, Rachel Maddow graded out Donald Trump's first 100 days and the best she could say about the second-term president that he has "flailed."

Jumping right into it, Maddow, after saying she would miss Phang hosting her own show, began, "Let me just say, the 100 days, there has never been a president who has botched the first 100 days of his presidency more than Donald Trump has. You don't have to take that as a subjective view. It's the view shown in scientific polling of the American public. The public is deeply, deeply, deeply against what Trump is doing. He's -- the YouGov polling that came out this week, I think he is 19 points underwater!"

Pointing out that she believes Trump is "more ambitious" than during his first term before losing to President Joe Biden in 2020, she added, "I do think we're in the middle of an attempted authoritarian overthrow of American democracy. I mean, I think everybody who predicted that was right, but I don't think he's any better at it."

Maddow also noted expectations for Trump the second go-around were high but that he has stumbled as he's been mired himself in pushing his policies.

"He was going to be better at doing what he wanted to do, and that's what we should brace ourselves for. And I actually don't think that's the way it's worked out," she observed.

'"I think the other thing I was talking about sort of what the pundit class got wrong heading into Trump's second term," she elaborated. "I think what we –– there was, again, a kind of pundit, common wisdom or kind of observer common wisdom that the American people weren't going to resist, you know, weren't going to stand up, that there was there was no sign of the kind of mass resistance to what trump is doing. That's turning out not to be the case at all, it's just different this time."

Our Analysis:

Maddow's Brutal Assessment of Trump's Second Term: A Dive into the Depths of Political Disarray

In what could only be described as a scathing critique, Rachel Maddow, a notable figure in political commentary, offered a grim view of Donald Trump’s performance during the first 100 days of his second term. Her analysis, delivered on Katie Phang's final MSNBC show, underscores a presidency in turmoil, marked by widespread public disapproval and ineffectual governance. Maddow's insights serve as a clarion call to those who might have underestimated the chaotic trajectory of Trump's return to the Oval Office.

The Public's Verdict: A Resounding Rejection

Maddow's reference to the YouGov polling data is a damning indictment of Trump's presidency. A 19-point deficit in public approval is not merely a stumble; it's a catastrophic plunge into the abyss of political unpopularity. This isn't about partisan bickering or ideological differences; it's a quantifiable measure of a leader's failure to resonate with or even adequately address the concerns of the American populace. When Maddow points to this "scientific polling," she's not just offering an opinion; she's presenting hard evidence of a presidency in crisis.

The Illusion of Ambition Versus the Reality of Incompetence

It is particularly striking that Maddow contrasts Trump's purported ambition with his actual performance. The expectation—or perhaps the fear—was that Trump would return with a refined strategy, a clearer vision, or at the very least, a better understanding of the levers of power. Instead, what we've witnessed is a series of blunders that suggest not just a failure to learn from past mistakes but an alarming regression. Maddow's assertion that we are witnessing "an attempted authoritarian overthrow of American democracy" is not made lightly. It's a conclusion drawn from observing a pattern of behavior that is antithetical to democratic principles and practices.

The Pundit Class and the Power of Public Resistance

One of the most intriguing aspects of Maddow's analysis is her critique of the pundit class and their failure to anticipate the resilience of the American public. There was a prevailing narrative that suggested a passive acceptance of Trump’s governance, a narrative that has been thoroughly debunked by the events of Trump's first 100 days. The "mass resistance" Maddow highlights is a testament to the vibrancy of American democracy and the willingness of its citizens to stand up against what they perceive as an existential threat to their democratic institutions.

Conclusion: A Presidency Adrift

Maddow's commentary is more than just a critique of Trump's second term; it's a sobering examination of the current state of American politics. Her analysis, grounded in fact and bolstered by polling data, paints a picture of a presidency that is not only failing to meet the expectations of its citizens but is actively undermining the very foundations of American democracy. The message is clear: this is not business as usual. This is a wake-up call for anyone concerned about the health and future of democratic governance in the United States.

As we reflect on Maddow's words, it's essential to recognize the role of public engagement and resistance in shaping the trajectory of this presidency. The days ahead will undoubtedly be challenging, but if Maddow's analysis has shown us anything, it's that the spirit of democracy is alive and well—and it's fighting back with vigor.
