“When Civility Burns: The JoAnna St. Germain Fallout and the Boiling Point of American Rage”

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 4, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Secret Service is aware of comments made by a Maine teacher Getty Images/AP
© Getty Images/AP

There’s a point in every slow-motion collapse when someone, somewhere, finally says the thing everyone’s been too afraid to say. That moment just happened in Waterville, Maine. And her name is JoAnna St. Germain.

In a blistering Facebook post now burned into the national psyche, St. Germain—an English teacher—called on the Secret Service to “take out every single person who supports Trump’s illegal, immoral, unconstitutional acts.” The backlash was immediate. The outrage predictable. The headlines wrote themselves.

But let’s not get distracted by the pyrotechnics. Let’s talk about the fire underneath.

St. Germain didn’t simply call for blood in a vacuum. Her words were born from years of watching a twice-impeached demagogue dismantle the American experiment in real time—with barely a slap on the wrist. She watched the Department of Education get gutted, LGBTQ+ children be targeted like political piñatas, and the promise of “checks and balances” melt into a soggy myth.

And now, with Trump somehow clawing his way back into office for a second term, flirting openly with a third, St. Germain snapped. Not because she’s unstable, but because the country is.

Let’s be clear: her language was reckless. Dangerous, even. You don’t call for the extrajudicial elimination of political opponents, no matter how monstrous they may be. But let’s not pretend this is a one-sided moral crisis. Where was this same collective hand-wringing when Trump praised dictators, threatened journalists, or told violent extremists they were “very fine people”?

What St. Germain did was publicly voice a desperation that many Americans have internalized silently. She didn’t radicalize herself; the system radicalized her by refusing to hold a criminal president accountable. Her moral compass may be spinning wildly, but she is reacting to a nation where fascist cosplay is becoming federal policy.

And the political right? Suddenly very concerned about teacher conduct. The same movement that’s tried to rewrite slavery in textbooks and ban history they find uncomfortable now wants to talk about the “appropriateness” of what’s taught in classrooms. Give us a break.

The truth is, we’ve entered an era where one side wages ideological warfare and the other is expected to respond with “civility.” The St. Germain story isn’t about a rogue teacher. It’s about what happens when you keep telling people the house isn’t on fire while the flames lick the windows. Eventually, someone stops whispering “Fire!” and starts screaming “Burn it all down.”

That’s not a call to violence. It’s a warning about what happens when institutions pretend that democracy is unbreakable—until it shatters.

The teacher said the quiet part out loud. Maybe it’s time the rest of us start listening—not to her words, but to what created them.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
