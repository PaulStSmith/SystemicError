Fox News caught avoiding camera angles that would expose Trump's tiny crowds: analyst

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he arrives to deliver remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, U.S. December 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

It’s no coincidence that President Donald Trump chose Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, for a MAGA rally on Tuesday. Pennsylvania, as Democratic strategist James Carville famously noted back in the late 1980s, is a complex and volatile swing state that can go either Democrat or Republican. And Trump has a history of both winning and losing in the Keystone State, which he carried in 2016 and 2024 but lost to Democrat Joe Biden in 2020.

The Mount Pocono event marked Trump’s return to MAGA rallies after putting them on the back burner during his second presidency. But Salon’s Sophia Tesfaye, in a biting article published on Wednesday, attacks the Mount Pocono gathering as a “flop.”

“Donald Trump’s midterm reboot was supposed to be the triumphant return of a political heavyweight,” Tesfaye explains. “After Democrats saw impressive gains in off-year elections across the country in November, White House advisers promised the president would return to the campaign trail to storm the 2026 midterms with the same ‘fire and dominance’ he claimed to wield in 2024 — infamous weave and all. But if his Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania rally is any preview of what the GOP should expect, Trump’s promise should be read as a threat. Far from a comeback, his return rally was a flop.”

Trump aggressively defended his economic policy during the rally, which, Tesfaye emphasizes, suffered from both poor attendance and bad messaging.

“Trump’s team clearly hoped the blue-collar community in one of the country’s most important swing states would give him a friendly launchpad,” the Salon journalist writes. “While I expected a crowd of a few thousand with the nostalgic sound of MAGA chants echoing off metal bleachers, I tuned into Fox News Tuesday evening to find the president in a conference center ballroom inside a local casino that appeared to hold, generously, 200 people. And even that small crowd seemed hesitant, almost resigned, as Trump ranted for nearly an hour.”

Tesfaye adds, “Fox News, of course, dutifully avoided any wide shots. But the truth was clear on screen: The MAGA magic had vanished.”

According to Tesfaye, Trump’s “gaslighting” on the economy during his Pennsylvania rally did nothing to persuade swing voters.

“The Trump of 2026 is not the Trump of 2024,” Tesfaye observes. “The president is clearly tired, angry, confused and incapable of adjusting to a country in economic crisis. Even Fox News can’t spin this.”

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Mirage: Trump’s Mount Pocono Rally Unpacked

In an era where political theatrics often overshadow substance, the recent spectacle at Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, serves as a testament to the diminishing allure of what was once considered an invincible political force. President Donald Trump’s decision to stage a rally in this critical swing state was, on its face, a strategic maneuver. However, the outcome, as dissected by Sophia Tesfaye in her Salon article, paints a picture of a campaign that’s lost its luster, drawing a stark contrast between the past triumphs and the current tribulations of the Trump MAGA movement.

Strategic Missteps and the Illusion of Dominance

Trump’s choice of Pennsylvania for his rally was no mere coincidence. With its notorious reputation as a swing state capable of tipping the scales in favor of either major political party, the logic behind targeting this demographic landscape is sound. However, the execution and the resultant atmosphere at the Mount Pocono event leave much to be desired. Trump, who has had a rollercoaster relationship with Pennsylvania’s electorate — winning in 2016 and 2024 but facing defeat in 2020 — seemingly failed to recapture the magic that once swayed the Keystone State.

The portrayal of the rally as a “flop” by Tesfaye is a scathing indictment of the Trump team’s inability to mobilize its base or to inject new vigor into its campaign strategy. The anticipated “triumphant return” fizzled into an underwhelming gathering that struggled to fill a casino ballroom. This imagery is emblematic of a broader issue within the MAGA campaign: the erosion of its once formidable grassroots appeal. Tesfaye’s account of the tepid attendance and the lack of enthusiasm among the attendees underscores a palpable sense of disillusionment that has crept into the MAGA ranks.

The Messaging Quagmire

The core of Trump’s address — a staunch defense of his economic policies — did little to resonate with those beyond his base. The critique of poor attendance and “bad messaging” at the rally highlights a fundamental disconnect between Trump’s rhetoric and the prevailing concerns of swing voters. In an era marked by economic uncertainties, the electorate seeks solutions, not gaslighting. Tesfaye astutely points out that the Trump on display in 2026 is a shadow of his former self: “tired, angry, confused and incapable of adjusting to a country in economic crisis.” This characterization not only speaks to the personal decline of Trump but also to the broader inadequacies of his political machinery to adapt to the changing contours of American society.

Fox News and the Vanishing MAGA Magic

The role of Fox News in this narrative is particularly telling. As Tesfaye notes, the network’s deliberate avoidance of wide shots at the rally is indicative of a concerted effort to mask the dwindling support for Trump. This manipulation of coverage speaks volumes about the challenges faced by the MAGA movement in maintaining its facade of invincibility. The “MAGA magic,” once a potent force in American politics, appears to have dissipated, leaving behind a trail of disillusioned supporters and a campaign struggling to find its footing.

Conclusion: The MAGA Conundrum

Tesfaye’s analysis of the Mount Pocono rally is a microcosm of the larger troubles plaguing the Trump campaign. The event, intended as a showcase of strength, instead revealed the vulnerabilities of a movement grappling with the realities of its waning influence. The MAGA campaign, once synonymous with political dominance, now faces an existential crisis as it attempts to navigate an increasingly skeptical and fragmented electorate.

The Trump team’s misadventure in Pennsylvania is a stark reminder that political momentum is fleeting, and without a coherent message and genuine engagement with the concerns of voters, even the most formidable campaigns can quickly unravel. As the MAGA movement contemplates its next moves, the lessons from Mount Pocono will undoubtedly loom large. Will they adapt and evolve, or continue on a path of decline? Only time will tell, but as Tesfaye’s critique suggests, the magic — if it ever truly existed — is gone.
