'Nobody's ever done anything like this': New 'wildly illegal' Trump vote ploy sparks fear

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump Administration's Election Meddling: A Dangerous Precedent

Donald Trump (photo via Reuters)

A new initiative being driven by Donald Trump’s administration to meddle in elections is causing concern, according to a report from the New York Times.

Not content with twisting the arms of the Republican Party at the state level to redistrict in an effort to hold on to a slim GOP majority in the House, the White House is now compiling a first-ever national voter roll by demanding information from over 30 states — most of which have been resisting.

That, in turn, has “elicited serious concerns among voting rights experts” who believe the current administration may use the data to cast more doubt on future elections because the efforts are being led by allies of the president who still maintain the 2020 election was stolen.

According to the Times’ Devlin Barrett and Nick Corasaniti, “The initiative has proceeded along two tracks, one at the Justice Department’s civil rights division and another at its criminal division, seeking data about individual voters across the country, including names and addresses, in a move that experts say may violate the law.”

That led election expert Justin Leavitt of Loyola Marymount University’s law school to warn, “Nobody has ever done anything like this.”

The report notes that administration officials plan to share the information with the Department of Homeland Security which, during the current administration, has been hellbent on deporting immigrants.

That has election officials leery about motive.

In a letter to the Justice Department, Justin R. Erickson, general counsel for Minnesota’s secretary of state, wrote, “Equally concerning is the possibility that the D.O.J. will use the data inappropriately and the fact that the D.O.J. does not appear to have complied with the necessary legal requirements to obtain or use data on several million people.”

Leavitt, agreed, adding, “It’s wading in, without authorization and against the law, with an overly heavy federal hand to take over a function that states are actually doing just fine. It’s wildly illegal, deeply troubling, and nobody asked for this.”

Our Analysis:

Trump Administration's Election Meddling: A Dangerous Precedent

The Trump administration's latest move to compile a national voter roll by aggressively soliciting personal information from over 30 states is not just a step too far; it's a brazen leap into uncharted and perilous waters. This initiative, as reported by the New York Times, signals a dire escalation in efforts to undermine the integrity of our electoral system, disguised as a crusade for "election security."

The Real Agenda: Undermining Democratic Processes

At its core, this initiative is not about securing elections or ensuring the sanctity of the vote. It's about control. By amassing a national database of voter information, the Trump administration is laying the groundwork for unprecedented surveillance and potential manipulation of voter rolls. The involvement of figures who continue to propagate the baseless claim that the 2020 election was stolen only adds fuel to the fire of skepticism regarding their true intentions.

Legal and Ethical Quagmires

The methods of data collection, as outlined in the report, raise significant legal and ethical questions. The Justice Department, under the guise of its civil and criminal divisions, is seeking personal details of voters across the nation — a move that experts like Justin Leavitt of Loyola Marymount University’s law school have flagged as potentially illegal. This action not only oversteps federal boundaries by intruding into a domain traditionally managed by states but also sets a dangerous precedent for federal overreach into personal privacy.

The Shadow of Deportation

Equally alarming is the administration's plan to share this sensitive data with the Department of Homeland Security, an agency that has notoriously prioritized deportations under Trump's tenure. This raises the specter of voter intimidation and suppression, particularly among immigrant communities and communities of color, further eroding trust in an already beleaguered electoral system.

State Resistance: A Beacon of Hope

The resistance from over 30 states to this initiative is a testament to the resilience of our democratic institutions against authoritarian tendencies. State officials, like Minnesota’s secretary of state general counsel Justin R. Erickson, have voiced their concerns loud and clear, highlighting the potential misuse of data and the legal shortcuts taken by the Department of Justice.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

The Trump administration's attempt to meddle in the electoral process through a national voter roll is not just concerning; it's a glaring red flag for the health of our democracy. It underscores the need for vigorous oversight, staunch resistance from states, and public awareness to safeguard the cornerstones of our democratic process. As citizens, it is our duty to remain vigilant and question the motives behind such initiatives that threaten to erode the very fabric of our democratic society.
