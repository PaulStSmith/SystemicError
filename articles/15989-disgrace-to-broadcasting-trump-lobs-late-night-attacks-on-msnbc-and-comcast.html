Fox News again clobbered its competitors in the ratings for the first quarter of 2025, with the conservative network boasting it had posted the highest-rated quarter with weekday viewers in cable news history, and the president gloated as if he had something to do with that success.

"Wow! Fake News MSNBC has REALLY BAD RATINGS, in fact, not much better than the broken broadcasting disaster known as CNN," Trump posted Tuesday at 11:16 p.m. on Truth Social. "Comcast, which also has the ailing network known as NBC, is trying to stay away from lawsuits by disassociating NBC from MSNBC, but it won’t work. Comcast, the owner of both, and it’s Chairman, Brian Roberts, are a disgrace to the integrity of Broadcasting!!!"

Telecommunications giant Comcast will spin off some of its cable networks as traditional TV audiences dwindle, and MSNBC, E!, CNBC, USA, Oxygen, SYFY and the Golf Channel will become a new, publicly traded company.

Fox News averaged 1.92 million total day viewers in the first quarter of this year, which was up 48 percent, while MSNBC posted 593,000, down 27 percent, and CNN averaged 428,000, down 8 percent.

MSNBC has said that its weekday primetime viewership shot up by 96 percent since Trump's inauguration, and CNN says its primetime viewership increased by 7 percent in March when compared to the previous month.

Our Analysis:

The Ratings Game: Trump's Late-Night Barrage

In the eerie glow of late-night social media musings, former President Donald Trump took to Truth Social to unleash a tirade against MSNBC and CNN, two networks he has long branded as the epitome of "Fake News." His comments come in the wake of Fox News's announcement that it had crushed its competitors in the ratings for the first quarter of 2025, a victory Trump seemed to bask in as if he were the puppeteer behind the network's success. But let's dissect this spectacle with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

The Ratings Reality Check

Firstly, the numbers are stark. Fox News's triumph in the ratings war isn't just a win; it's a landslide, boasting 1.92 million total day viewers, a staggering 48 percent increase. Meanwhile, MSNBC's viewership dwindled by 27 percent to 593,000, and CNN's audience shrank by 8 percent to 428,000. These figures are not just statistics; they're a testament to the shifting landscape of cable news viewership and, perhaps, the broader political sentiment of the nation.

Trump's Victory Lap: A Hollow Echo

Trump's gloating tweet—"Wow! Fake News MSNBC has REALLY BAD RATINGS..."—is a classic example of his penchant for reveling in the perceived failures of his adversaries. Yet, this celebration overlooks a crucial detail: the role of a president, former or otherwise, is not to be a cheerleader for a cable news network's ratings success, especially when that success is aligned so closely with one's political agenda. It raises the question—where should a leader's priorities lie?

The Dynamics of Disdain

Trump's attack extends beyond the networks themselves, targeting Comcast and its Chairman, Brian Roberts, as "a disgrace to the integrity of Broadcasting." This move is quintessentially Trumpian, blurring the lines between personal vendettas and public discourse. The irony here is palpable; Trump's own assaults on journalistic integrity have been a hallmark of his political career, making his critique of Comcast's "disgrace" an exercise in sheer hypocrisy.

A Shift in the Media Landscape

The article also notes Comcast's decision to spin off several of its cable networks into a new, publicly traded company. This move, a response to the dwindling traditional TV audience, underscores a seismic shift in how we consume media. The rise of digital platforms and the decline of cable news viewership are realities that all networks must grapple with, irrespective of their political leanings or ratings victories.

MSNBC and CNN: A Silver Lining?

Despite the ratings downturn, both MSNBC and CNN have seen some upticks—MSNBC's weekday primetime viewership surged by 96 percent since Trump's inauguration, and CNN saw a 7 percent increase in March primetime viewership compared to February. These numbers suggest that while the battle for eyeballs is fierce, the war is far from over. They hint at a resilience and potential for rebound, driven perhaps by the very controversies that Trump so loves to stoke.

Final Thoughts

In the grand chessboard of cable news and political influence, Trump remains a formidable player, using his platform to sway public opinion and settle personal scores. However, the focus on ratings as a barometer of truth or quality is a misguided venture. The real measure of a news network's integrity lies not in its viewership numbers but in the accuracy, fairness, and depth of its reporting. In the end, the incessant fixation on ratings and partisan attacks only serves to distract from the more pressing issues facing our society. It's time to turn down the noise and demand more from our leaders and our media.
