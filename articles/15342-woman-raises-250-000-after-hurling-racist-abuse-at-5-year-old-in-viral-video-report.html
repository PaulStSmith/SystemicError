Woman raises $250,000 after hurling racist abuse at 5-year-old in viral video: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A woman caught using racial slurs against a kindergarten-age boy in Minnesota has raised a quarter of a million dollars after video of the incident went viral, according to reports.

Cell phone video spread widely on social media Wednesday showing a man confronting a white woman at a playground at Soldiers Field Memorial Park for allegedly calling the boy the N-word, and she then repeats the slur to the man filming the video and accuses the child of stealing from her, reported the Star Tribune.

“If he acts like one then he’s going to be called one,” the woman says in the video.

Social media commentators say the child is a Black boy who is around 5 years old and has autism, but the newspaper has not independently confirmed those details.

The woman was identified on social media as Shiloh Hendrix, of Rochester, and a woman using that name set up a page on a Christian fundraising service saying the video had put her family into a “very dire situation" and justified her actions, saying the boy had stolen something from her 18-month-old’s diaper bag and claiming that she “called the kid out for what he was.”

“I fear that we must relocate,” Hendrix wrote. “I have two small children who do not deserve this. We have been threatened to the extreme by people online. Anything will help! We cannot, and will not live in fear!”

The fundraiser had a goal set for $250,000, and she appears to have collected that much by Friday morning.

One of the anonymous donors listed their name as "Dylan Roof," which is the name of a man who killed nine Black worshipers at a historic Black church in South Carolina in 2015.

“Now she’s fundraising on this, and that is just abhorrent and totally unacceptable,” said Rochester NAACP president Wale Elegbede. “She needs to face legal consequences for all her actions. This is a disgrace.”

Our Analysis:

The Monetization of Hate: A Case Study

In a world where the boundary between infamy and fame becomes increasingly blurred, the case of a woman in Minnesota exploiting racial hatred for profit is a stark illustration of a deeply troubling trend. This woman, identified as Shiloh Hendrix, has managed to raise a quarter of a million dollars after being caught on video using racial slurs against a kindergarten-age boy. This incident, reported by the Star Tribune, not only highlights the virulent racism that still pervades sections of American society but also underlines the perverse incentives created by online fundraising platforms.

The Incident at Soldiers Field Memorial Park

The video, which spread like wildfire across social media, showcases a confrontation between Hendrix and a man who calls her out for allegedly using the N-word against a small child. Hendrix's response, as captured in the video, is to double down on her racism and attempt to justify it by accusing the child of theft. Her words, “If he acts like one then he’s going to be called one,” encapsulate the dehumanization and bigotry that are all too prevalent in incidents of racial abuse.

Exploiting Racism for Profit

What is perhaps more shocking than the act of racism itself is Hendrix's subsequent move to capitalize on her notoriety. Setting up a fundraiser on a Christian fundraising service, Hendrix paints herself as the victim of a "very dire situation," claiming that her family has been threatened and that they are forced to live in fear. This narrative conveniently glosses over the initial act of racism that prompted the backlash, framing Hendrix as a mother simply protecting her children, rather than as someone who publicly demeaned a child based on race.

The fundraiser's success, reaching its $250,000 goal, is a damning indictment of the current state of our society. It suggests that there is a substantial audience willing to financially support individuals who engage in, or are accused of, racist behavior. Even more disturbing is the donation made under the name "Dylan Roof," a convicted murderer known for his racially motivated attack on a Black church. This not only highlights the extent to which some elements of society are prepared to endorse racism but also the grim reality of hate being monetized.

Legal and Moral Accountability

The response from Wale Elegbede, president of the Rochester NAACP, calling for Hendrix to face legal consequences, underscores the need for accountability. It is not enough to decry such actions on social media or through public statements. There must be tangible repercussions for those who engage in hate speech and then seek to profit from it.

Conclusion

The incident at Soldiers Field Memorial Park and its aftermath should serve as a wake-up call. It's a vivid illustration of how racism can be not just a moral failing but also a lucrative venture for some. This perverse reality raises serious questions about the platforms that enable such fundraising and the societal values that allow individuals to profit from spreading hate. As we move forward, it is imperative that we confront these challenges head-on, ensuring that there are consequences for hate speech and that we do not provide a financial incentive for bigotry.
