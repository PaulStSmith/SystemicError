'Utterly lawless': NYT report on new FBI directive stuns nation

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20262 min read

A Directive That Turns Oversight Into Theater

What Changed

The reported move was simple and ugly: federal agents were told not to investigate confrontations involving immigration agents, even as the Trump administration faced mounting criticism over killings by federal officers. The immediate context matters: ICE agents fatally shot Lorenzo Salgado Araujo in Houston and Joan Sebastian Guerrero in Biddeford, and protests were already spreading from North Carolina to Oregon, Utah, Arizona, Texas, and Maine.

Who Holds Power

This is not a story about confusion. It is a story about power protecting itself. The Justice Department and Homeland Security can deny the policy change all they want, but the reporting points to an institutional decision to narrow scrutiny of immigration agents while shifting control over investigations closer to the agencies implicated in the conduct. That is not oversight. That is self-policing with a badge on top.

The Fox In The Henhouse

The source gets the basic dynamic right when it notes that ICE Homeland Security Investigations would lead the investigations while lacking jurisdiction to investigate civil rights violations. That is the core scandal. When the people whose mission is enforcement are also the people deciding whether enforcement crossed the line, the outcome is pre-decided. The structure is built to convert force into administrative routine and accountability into a procedural inconvenience.

The Framing Problem

The weakest part of the public reaction is how quickly this kind of story gets flattened into a bureaucratic dispute. It is not “law enforcement scrutiny” that is being lightly adjusted. It is the public’s only thin safeguard against federal abuse being pushed farther out of reach. And the familiar language about “assault on federal officer cases” does the usual work: it centers the discomfort of the armed state, not the deaths, fear, and intimidation imposed on immigrants and protesters.

Cruelty With Plausible Deniability

The reporting also shows how authoritarian systems launder brutality through routine. One Maine protester held a sign saying “a man was lynched here.” That is not an overreaction pulled from nowhere; it is a political diagnosis of how state violence gets normalized and then defended as order. Meanwhile, the administration offers denials, the FBI director posts a FIFA photo, and the machinery keeps moving. The message is that accountability is optional, but branding is not.

The Bigger Pattern

This is what institutional cowardice looks like when it is dressed up as governance: abuse is escalated, oversight is narrowed, and the public is told to trust the same agencies implicated in the harm. ICE cannot be dignified into reform by press releases, training modules, or internal review. It is an enforcement apparatus built to brutalize vulnerable people and then hide behind jurisdictional jargon. The political lesson is plain: when the state authorizes cruelty and then inspects itself, impunity is not a bug. It is the design.
