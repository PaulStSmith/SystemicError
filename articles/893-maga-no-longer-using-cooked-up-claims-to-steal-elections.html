MAGA no longer using 'cooked-up' claims to steal elections

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

Fraud as a Reflex, Not an Argument

The Setup

Andrew Egger’s point is not that Republicans have uncovered election crimes. It is that they have found something more useful: a habit of accusation that can be deployed whenever the result is inconvenient. In the cases he cites, the pattern is the same. Trump declares fraud first, allies echo him, and evidence is optional.

That matters because this is not confusion. It is a power tactic built around a simple premise: if you control enough loyalists, you do not need to prove the election was stolen. You only need to make enough people repeat that it was.

Who Has the Power

The institutional power here sits with Donald Trump and the Republican coalition that has trained itself to treat his claims as command. The source makes that plain. When Trump called Virginia’s redistricting referendum a “rigged election,” some Republicans hesitated. In Los Angeles, by contrast, they jumped in without even pretending to verify anything.

That distinction is the story. Trump is not an outside critic floating theories. He is the force that sets the terms of legitimacy for his party. When he says a result is fraudulent, the party machinery does not investigate; it adjusts.

The Los Angeles Tell

The Los Angeles mayoral fight is the clearest example in the source because it shows how thin the accusation really is. Nithya Raman moved ahead of Spencer Pratt as ballots were counted, and Republicans treated that ordinary electoral pattern as suspect. There was no cited evidence of malfeasance. The “crime” was that late-counted ballots favored the wrong candidate.

That is not a fraud claim. It is a demand that counting stop when the margin becomes politically unpleasant.

The article’s own description exposes the laundering effort. Mail-in ballots arriving at or near the deadline were more pro-Raman than earlier votes. That is called voter behavior. Republicans converted it into insinuation because the result did not flatter them.

Blame Management

This is where the misdirection does its work. The source shows blame being shifted away from the people making the accusation and toward voters, election administrators, and the process itself. “Fraud” becomes a cloud word: it sounds grave, but it identifies no actor, no act, no evidence.

That is deliberate political cowardice. It lets powerful figures avoid the burden of proof while still poisoning the legitimacy of the outcome. It also gives supporters a clean emotional script: do not ask what happened, ask why the result feels wrong.

George F. Will’s observation in the source sharpens the point. Trump’s fraud belief is not a response to evidence; it is bound up with his belief that losing is impossible for him. That certainty spreads downward. Once a leader’s ego becomes the party’s epistemology, facts stop governing the movement. Loyalty does.

The Midterm Playbook

Egger’s warning about 2026 is worth taking seriously because it describes an easily reusable structure. If Democrats do well, Trump can call the outcome fraudulent. If Republicans cannot prove it, that hardly matters. The purpose is not legal success; it is narrative control.

The playbook works because a large portion of the GOP base is already conditioned to accept Trump’s version of reality over observable facts. The source says it directly: the base will go along with him if he claims a major election was stolen. The mechanics are low effort and high payoff. No evidence required. Just outrage, repetition, and enough institutional cowardice to let the lie harden.

The Larger Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics cheapen democracy without formally abolishing it. They do not always need to block voting outright. They can corrode trust in counting, normalize suspicion of ordinary results, and train followers to treat any unfavorable outcome as illegitimate. The target is not just an election. It is the public’s ability to believe that votes mean anything when the wrong people win.

The real scandal in the source is not ballot counting in Los Angeles. It is the ease with which a major party can turn normal democratic variance into a fraud narrative, then call that strategy principled opposition. That is not a misunderstanding of elections. It is the weaponization of grievance by people who know exactly where the power sits.
