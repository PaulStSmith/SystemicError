Pete Hegseth accused of 'shouting the quiet part out loud' with one remark during new rant

U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth holds a briefing amid the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran, at the Pentagon in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 2, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth launched a blistering attack on the press Friday during a Pentagon briefing, attacking multiple outlets for their coverage of the ongoing Iran war – though one remark in particular left onlookers stunned for being unusually candid.

Hegseth, a former Fox News contributor, took aim at several outlets during the briefing for news headlines that he argued painted a distorted picture of the Trump administration’s ongoing military operations against Iran.

“Some in this crew – in the press – just can’t stop! Allow me to make a few suggestions,” Hegseth said during the briefing.

“For example: ‘Mideast war intensifies’ splashing on the screen the last couple of days, alongside civilian or energy targets that Iran has hit, because that’s what they do. What should the banner read instead? How about ‘Iran increasingly desperate?’ Because they are! They know it, and so do you!”

Hegseth then pivoted to single out CNN, specifically for its reporting on the supposed panic within the Trump administration over the global fallout from Iran’s closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a major shipping route through which a fifth of the world’s oil trade passes.

“More fake news from CNN, reports that the Trump administration ‘underestimated’ the Iran war’s impact on the Strait of Hormuz. Patently ridiculous, of course!” Hegseth said. “It’s a fundamentally unserious report. The sooner David Ellison takes over that network, the better.”

Hegseth’s reference to Ellison – a staunch ally to President Donald Trump and billionaire who runs Paramount Skydance, which is poised to acquire CNN in a proposed merger – was particularly telling, several critics noted.

“Really shouting the quiet part out loud that Trump’s billionaire allies are buying up news publishers and controlling social media and AI platforms to push Trump’s lies and propaganda,” wrote Melanie D’Arrigo, executive director for the Campaign for New York Health, in a social media post Friday on X to her nearly 85,000 followers.

“An administration, openly acknowledging they are looking forward to more favorable coverage from CNN’s future owner: Trump linked oligarch, David Ellison,” wrote X user “The Maine Wonk,” a prominent political commentator and self-described “radical centrist” to their nearly 115,000 followers. “Unnerving and embarrassing.”

Veteran CNN reporter Barbara Starr also took aim at Hegseth over his remarks, reminding the Defense Secretary of both his ‘legal and moral obligation’ to defend a free press and that CNN war correspondents have died in their efforts to cover global conflicts.

“Listen up Mr. Defense Secretary,” Starr wrote in a social media post on X in response to Hegseth’s remarks. “CNN has had personnel in combat zones for decades. CNN has had [personnel] killed and wounded and all with lives changed forever. You have a legal and moral obligation to defend the free press, even the ones you don’t personally like.”

Following the press conference, a CNN spokesperson told Raw Story: “we stand by our reporting.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s Attack on the Press

In a recent display of contentious interaction between government officials and the media, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s remarks during a Pentagon briefing have sparked a significant amount of discussion and criticism. His pointed criticism of the press, specifically targeting CNN and the broader media’s coverage of the Iran war, underlines a growing tension between the current administration and the fourth estate. This analysis seeks to dissect the situation, focusing on the distribution of institutional power, the decision-making process, and the framing of the narrative.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Institutional Power: The Department of Defense, under the leadership of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, holds significant institutional power in this context. The Department is responsible for military operations, including those against Iran. The administration, as represented by Hegseth, wields the authority to conduct these operations and to communicate about them to the public.

Decision-Making: Hegseth’s decision to publicly criticize the press for their portrayal of the war with Iran reveals a deliberate choice to confront media narratives that the administration considers unfavorable. His remarks were not spontaneous reactions but calculated statements made during a Pentagon briefing, a setting that underscores the gravity and official capacity of his criticisms.

The Article’s Framing

Misdirection of Responsibility: The framing of Hegseth’s attack suggests a tension between the government’s portrayal of military operations and the media’s coverage of the same. While Hegseth positions himself and the administration as defenders of a more ‘accurate’ narrative against an allegedly biased press, it is crucial to question whether the criticism is directed at the appropriate actors. The press, exercising its role in a democratic society, reports on government actions and their consequences. Criticizing the media for coverage that the administration deems negative does not address the underlying actions or policies leading to the reported outcomes.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating: Hegseth’s criticisms imply a false equivalence between media reporting and government action, suggesting that unfavorable coverage is equivalent to misunderstanding or misrepresenting the administration’s objectives. This narrative overlooks the essential function of the press to scrutinize and report on government actions independently. By attacking the media, specifically CNN, and looking forward to more favorable coverage from a future owner allied with the administration, Hegseth’s remarks dangerously lean towards advocating for a less critical, more compliant press, which is antithetical to the principles of a free and independent media.

Criticism: Misplaced or Justified?

Melanie D’Arrigo and “The Maine Wonk” raise valid concerns about the implications of Hegseth’s remarks for press freedom and the integrity of democratic discourse. They highlight the troubling prospect of media ownership influencing news coverage to favor particular political narratives, a significant issue that extends beyond the immediate context of Hegseth’s comments.

Barbara Starr’s Response: Starr’s rebuttal underscores the vital role of war correspondents and the press at large in maintaining governmental accountability and informing the public. Her reminder of the legal and moral obligations to defend a free press serves as a poignant counterpoint to Hegseth’s criticisms, emphasizing the sacrifices made by journalists to cover conflicts and the importance of their work.

Conclusion

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s attack on the press, particularly in the context of the Iran war coverage, reflects a broader struggle over narrative control between the government and the media. While the administration has legitimate interests in promoting its perspectives, undermining the press and advocating for media compliance with political narratives threatens the foundational principles of press freedom and democratic accountability. The criticisms lodged by Hegseth, while reflecting frustration with media portrayals, misdirect responsibility by attacking the messengers rather than engaging constructively with the substance of the coverage.
