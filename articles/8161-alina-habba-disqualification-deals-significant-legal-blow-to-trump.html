Alina Habba disqualification deals significant legal blow to Trump

Alina Habba attends her swearing-in ceremony as interim U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey, in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 28, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

An appeals court on Monday upheld a lower court ruling disqualifying Alina Habba, President Donald Trump’s interim U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey, NBC News reports.

Trump appointed Habba, who has represented Trump in several high-profile cases, including the E. Jean Carroll defamation case, in March. After her interim term expired in July 2025, the administration attempted to use legal maneuvers to keep her in the role, leading to significant legal controversy.

In a 32-page ruling, the 3rd Circuit Court of Appeals found that Habba’s appointment violates the Federal Vacancies Reform Act, NBC News explains.

“It is apparent that the current administration has been frustrated by some of the legal and political barriers to getting its appointees in place,” wrote Judge Michael Fisher.

Journalist and former BuzzFeed News legal editor Chris Geidner posted the news on Bluesky, saying, “NEWS: A unanimous Third Circuit panel rules that Alina Habba is not U.S. Attorney, cannot be Acting U.S. Attorney, and cannot have the powers delegated to her.”

Retired Random House copy chief and author Benjamin Dreyer replied to Geidner, saying, “Well, that’s a funsy ruling!”

Journalist Bill Grueskin wondered on Bluesky, “Do we know what this means for any indictments that she may have signed, or other cases done under her authority?”

Tennis titan Martina Navratilova mused on X, “The irony. A lawyer illegally appointed to be the Law Czar in NJ… go figure.”

CNN anchor Jim Sciutto writes on X that this ruling is “dealing a legal blow to the Trump administration that could have far-reaching consequences on other appointments across the country.”

J-L Cauvin, comedian and Democratic candidate for Congress in New Jersey’s 11th congressional district posted on X, “Alina Habba is illegally serving as NJ’s U.S. Attorney. Trump’s fondness of barely (il)legal continues.”

University of Michigan Law professor Barb McQuade posted on X that “Third circuit finds appointment of interim US Attorney Alina Habba is invalid. Courts have consistently struck down Trump’s efforts to evade Senate confirmation process.”

Our Analysis:

Legal Contortions and Political Ambitions: The Alina Habba Saga

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of legal and political acrobatics, the Trump administration’s attempt to install Alina Habba, a key legal warrior for Trump, as the interim U.S. Attorney for the District of New Jersey, has crashed and burned. On Monday, the appeals court delivered a ruling that reads like a narrative of defiance against the Federal Vacancies Reform Act. The 32-page ruling by the 3rd Circuit Court of Appeals didn’t just uphold a lower court’s decision; it served as a stark reminder of the Trump administration’s relentless pursuit of power, often skirting the edges of legality.

The Unconventional Path of Alina Habba

Habba, known for representing Trump in several high-profile cases, including the infamous E. Jean Carroll defamation case, was appointed in March, with her interim term set to expire in July 2025. However, the Trump administration’s attempt to extend her tenure through what can only be perceived as legal gymnastics, has sparked significant controversy. It’s a glaring testament to the administration’s strategy: if the standard process doesn’t suit your needs, bend the rules until they break.

Judicial Rebuff and Legal Implications

Judge Michael Fisher’s commentary in the ruling does more than just lay down the law; it underscores a palpable frustration with the administration’s tactics. The unanimous decision by the Third Circuit panel not only strips Habba of her title but also raises a cloud of uncertainty over the legal actions taken under her supposedly authoritative mantle.

The reactions from various corners — from Chris Geidner’s straightforward announcement to Martina Navratilova’s ironic musing and Jim Sciutto’s forewarning of broader implications — encapsulate a broader narrative. This isn’t just about a failed appointment; it’s a reflection of a systemic attempt to undermine the very foundations of the U.S. legal and political appointment processes.

A Pattern of Controversy

What’s particularly disturbing here is not just the attempted sidestepping of the Senate confirmation process, as highlighted by Barb McQuade, but the brazenness with which these maneuvers are executed. The Trump administration’s fondness for operating within the gray areas of legality, as quipped by J-L Cauvin, is not just a matter of barely (il)legal actions but a dangerous precedent that threatens the integrity of the American judicial and political systems.

The Fallout

Bill Grueskin raises an essential question: what happens to the indictments and cases overseen by Habba? This ruling doesn’t just invalidate an appointment; it potentially unravels a slew of legal actions taken during her tenure. The implications are vast and unsettling, with the legitimacy of numerous legal proceedings now under a cloud of doubt.

Conclusion: A System Under Siege

The Alina Habba saga is a stark embodiment of the Trump administration’s cavalier attitude towards legal norms and structures. It’s a narrative that goes beyond one appointment, touching on the broader issues of governance, accountability, and the rule of law. As this chapter unfolds, one thing is clear: the administration’s systemic error in judgment has exposed a disturbing willingness to flout the very system it’s supposed to uphold. The courts have spoken, but the consequences of this saga will reverberate far beyond the halls of justice.
