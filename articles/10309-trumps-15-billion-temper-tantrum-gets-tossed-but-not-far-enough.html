Trump’s $15 Billion Temper Tantrum Gets Tossed – But Not Far Enough

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A federal judge in Florida just tossed out Donald Trump’s $15 billion defamation lawsuit against The New York Times and Penguin Random House. Judge Steven D. Merryday, a George H.W. Bush appointee, dismissed the bloated 85-page screed for being “improper and impermissible,” noting that it took Trump’s legal team 80 pages of sycophantic rambling before they even got around to alleging defamation. In the judge’s own words: “A complaint is not a public forum for vituperation and invective. Not a protected platform to rage against an adversary.”

Merryday gave Trump’s lawyers 28 days to refile, with strict instructions to keep it under 40 pages. In other words, they’ve been told to come back with something resembling a legal argument instead of a campaign rally flyer. Trump’s legal team, of course, spun this humiliating rebuke as a procedural hiccup, vowing to press on against the so-called “Fake News.”

Let’s be clear: this case never should have survived four days on the docket. It’s not merely “improper” or “digressive.” It’s frivolous. The judiciary exists to adjudicate disputes, not to babysit Trump’s ego or provide him with yet another stage to perform his tired persecution routine. Every time Trump weaponizes the courts to settle personal grudges, he not only wastes judicial resources but also erodes the already fragile trust in our legal system.

This SubStack’s position is simple: Judge Merryday should have dismissed the case with prejudice and sanctioned both Trump and his attorneys. Enough of this endless cycle of performative litigation. If ordinary citizens filed lawsuits this hollow, they’d be laughed out of court and slapped with fines. Trump and his legal hacks deserve no special treatment. In fact, they deserve harsher penalties for abusing the court system to pump out propaganda under the guise of legal filings.

By declining to end this charade outright, the court leaves the door open for Trump’s circus to waste another month’s worth of judicial time and taxpayer money. It’s a disgrace that the man who has spent his entire life dodging accountability is once again allowed to turn the legal process into his personal PR machine. If there were any justice in Florida’s Middle District, Trump’s lawyers would be writing a check to the court clerk right now, not drafting their next whine-filled novel.

This is not accountability. This is indulgence. The judiciary must stop enabling Trump’s theater of grievance and start imposing consequences.
