'Bonkers' new numbers on Trump's job market leave CNN expert aghast: 'Blew my mind'

CNN’s Harry Enten was gobsmacked by “bonkers” findings in new polls on President Donald Trump and the job market.

The White House and Trump administration officials started out the week tempering public expectations for the coming jobs report — expected to be released Wednesday — and Enten told “CNN News Central” the public has seriously soured on the president’s handling of the economy.

“Donald Trump took a beautiful swan and turned it into an ugly duckling, as far as the American people are concerned,” Enten said, looking at poll results.

“Because these numbers ... [are] absolutely crazy in how the American people have totally turned on Donald Trump. Take a look here: Trump’s net approval rating on jobs and employment in January 2025, look at that, overall plus-nine points. Hey, that’s pretty good. But down he goes – minus-13 points overall.”

“But you think that’s nuts, take a look at the independents,” he added. “They go from plus-seven way, way, way down, off the screen, to minus-30 points. That’s a 37-point drop in the net approval for Donald Trump among independents on the issue of jobs and employment.”

That change in public opinion in the past year has been staggering, he said.

“This next chart is actually the one that that really blew my mind,” Enten said. “It’s about people’s views of the job market in general over a similar period of time. Yeah, okay, if you think these numbers are crazy, this next one these are bonkers, okay. What are we talking about here? Americans rate the job market as bad or good, February 2025, equal shares say fair or say bad or good, 42-42. Look at this now, over just a year’s time. Now the clear majority, 52 percent, up like a rocket, rated it bad compared to just 33 percent, down to the basement, who rated as good.”

“So what we’ve seen is Americans rating the job market, it was an even split a year ago, and now the clear majority, by a 19-point margin rated as bad rather than good,” Enten added, “and that goes a long way to explain why Donald Trump’s numbers on jobs and unemployment have fallen to the floor.”

Our Analysis:

A Dive into the Depths of Discontent: Trump’s Plummeting Popularity

CNN’s Harry Enten’s recent exposition on the dramatic nosedive in public approval of Donald Trump’s handling of the economy is not just an analysis; it’s a siren call to the Trump administration. The term “bonkers” doesn’t begin to cover the seismic shift in public opinion revealed by these polls. Let’s dissect these findings with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

The Stunning Reversal

In an almost Shakespearian tragedy (or comedy, depending on your perspective), Donald Trump’s approval rating on jobs and employment has spiraled from a commendable plus-nine points in January 2025 to a dismal minus-13 points. This isn’t merely a dip; it’s a nosedive. But wait, the plot thickens when we turn our gaze to the independents. Their approval plummeted from plus-seven to a staggering minus-30 points. This isn’t just a drop; it’s a freefall. A 37-point drop? It’s as if Trump’s administration decided to take a swan dive off the Empire State Building without a parachute.

The Independent Exodus

The independents’ exodus is particularly noteworthy. These are not your die-hard party loyalists who stick to their guns come hell or high water. These are the swing votes, the undecideds, the thinkers. And they are fleeing Trump’s camp like it’s on fire. A 37-point drop signifies more than discontent; it’s a damning indictment of Trump’s economic stewardship.

The Job Market Judgment

Enten’s revelation about Americans’ perception of the job market further cements the administration’s economic epitaph. In a year, the view of the job market has shifted from a balanced 42-42 split between good and bad, to a decisive 52 percent bad versus 33 percent good. This isn’t just a shift; it’s a tectonic movement. A 19-point margin clearly articulates the public’s verdict on the job market under Trump’s watch.

The Bigger Picture

What does this all mean? Simply put, Donald Trump has taken the economy from what was perceived as a relatively stable condition to a state of disarray and dissatisfaction. The numbers don’t lie. The public’s confidence in Trump’s economic leadership has not just eroded; it has collapsed. This is a clear signal that the administration’s policies and approaches are not resonating with the American populace, particularly the critical independent voters.

The Final Word

Harry Enten’s analysis, while shocking, is a necessary wake-up call. It’s not just about the numbers; it’s about what they represent. The dramatic shifts in public opinion are symptomatic of deeper economic malaises and a growing distrust in Trump’s ability to steer the American economy to safer shores.

In the grand scheme of things, these polls are more than mere statistics. They are a loud, resounding message to the Trump administration. Whether this message will be heeded or ignored remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: if current trends continue, the administration will find itself in an untenable position come the next election cycle. The American people are watching, and they are not impressed.
