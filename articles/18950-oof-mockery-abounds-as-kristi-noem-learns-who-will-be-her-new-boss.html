'Oof': Mockery abounds as Kristi Noem learns who will be her new boss

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 25, 20264 min read

Former Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem may have been fired from that role following President Donald Trump’s rage over her massive taxpayer expenditure on a commercial promoting herself, but she wasn’t cast out of the Trump administration entirely — and her new assignment was met with widespread mockery as it was widely seen as a move to humiliate her.

Trump initially announced her firing as a new appointment as Special Envoy to the Shield of the Americas — a small, made-up role that was a clear downgrade to a Cabinet-level office. And on Tuesday, CBS News’ Olivia Gazis reported that “In her capacity as Special Envoy to Shield of the Americas Kristi Noem will report directly to Deputy Secretary of State Chris Landau, per a State Department official.”

This new development that her boss will be a deputy triggered a fresh wave of ridicule.

“Oof,” wrote Politico diplomatic correspondent Felicia Schwartz.

“In March Madness terms, this is basically like getting kicked off the starting five and wounding up as the unlucky student who ensures the team mascot isn’t being hassled by drunk frat boys,” writer Charlotte Clymer posted.

“What about her emotional support Lewandowski?” wrote veteran and political commentator Andrew Donaldson, a reference to Corey Lewandowski, the on-again, off-again Trump adviser who reportedly ran vast swathes of the department for Noem and has long been rumored to be in an extramarital affair with her, which they both deny.

“Assistant Regional Manager to the Deputy Secretary of State,” wrote Atlantic Council senior fellow Daniel Tannebaum — referencing the running joke on the workplace sitcom “The Office,” in which iconic salesman Dwight Schrute frequently gets drunk on power from a meaningless assistant title that he keeps saying incorrectly to make it sound more important.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Kristi Noem’s New Role

In the political theater, roles and titles often serve as both rewards and reprimands, a dynamic starkly illustrated in the recent reshuffling of former Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem within the Trump administration. The transition from a Cabinet position to the title of Special Envoy to the Shield of the Americas, a role that has been described as “small” and “made-up,” is not just a demotion but a narrative device ripe for analysis. The mockery that ensued, as reported, deserves a closer examination through the lens of institutional power, decision-making authority, and the framing of the narrative.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

The institutional power in this scenario is held by President Donald Trump and the administration he leads. The power to appoint, dismiss, and reassign roles within the administration lies squarely with the President and his senior advisors. The Secretary of Homeland Security is a Cabinet-level position, indicating significant influence and responsibility. The move to a specially created role, reporting not directly to the President or Secretary of State but to a deputy, signifies a substantial reduction in both power and prestige.

Decision-Making Authority

The decision to reassign Noem from a Cabinet-level position to Special Envoy was made by President Trump, possibly with input from his closest advisors. This decision reflects the President’s authority to shape his administration according to his priorities, perceptions, and, as the article hints, personal grievances. The choice of role and the subsequent reporting structure—having Noem report to Deputy Secretary of State Chris Landau—further underscores the decision’s intent to reduce her influence within the administration.

Framing Misdirects Responsibility

The article’s framing, focusing on mockery and ridicule from various commentators, shifts the narrative away from a critical analysis of institutional dynamics and decision-making processes. Instead of examining the implications of such a reshuffle within the administration’s power structure, it centers on the personal humiliation of Noem and the schadenfreude of political observers. This approach obscures the more significant issue of how political power is wielded and the potential impact on governance.

Moreover, the references to Corey Lewandowski and the insinuation of an extramarital affair serve to distract from the core issues of governance, power, and accountability. These comments, while perhaps indicative of the informal power structures and personal dynamics within the administration, do not contribute to an understanding of the decision-making process or its implications for policy and administration.

Conclusion

The real story behind Kristi Noem’s demotion is not found in the mockery or the sensationalist aspects of her personal life but in the dynamics of power, loyalty, and control within the Trump administration. The President’s decision to reassign Noem to a less influential role reflects both the prerogatives and the pitfalls of executive power. The narrative, as presented, misses an opportunity to critique the substantive issues at play, opting instead for a focus on interpersonal drama and public ridicule. This approach does not serve the public interest, nor does it provide a meaningful critique of the administration’s use of power. In situations like these, it’s crucial to look beyond the surface-level spectacle and examine the underlying structures and decisions that shape our political landscape.
