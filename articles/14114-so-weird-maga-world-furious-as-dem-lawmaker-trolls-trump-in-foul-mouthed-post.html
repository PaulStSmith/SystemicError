Rep. Eric Swalwell (D-CA) made a not-so-subtle dig at Donald Trump's new nickname given to him by Wall Street investors upset over the market volatility caused by the president's on again-off again tariffs.

The derogatory term " TACO" — which stands for "Trump Always Chickens Out" — is reportedly used by Wall Street bankers who have started betting against the president after noticing the market manipulation that has resulted from Trump imposing, then backing off his tariffs.

In a video posted to his social media, an apparent staffer approaches Swalwell and asks, "Hey, Congressman...What the f--- is up with Trump always chickening out on tariffs?"

Instead of answering, Swalwell tucks into a Taco Bell taco conveniently laid out on his desk.

MAGA reacted harshly to Swalwell's attempt at troll humor.

RedWavePress called the post "bizarre," while The Blaze called it "very odd" and asked, "What was the point of this???"

Conservative outlet @amuse wrote to their 640,000 followers, "MEN? Eric Swalwell is kicking off the Democrat's $20 million effort to attract young men to the party. What do you say guys? Want to share a taco with Eric?"

"Eric Swalwell shared a video using profanity while eating a taco. Is this the type of messaging likely to regain support for the Democratic Party?" wrote influencer Ian Miles Cheong.

TownHall conservative news echoed the same sentiment: "Eric Swalwell has posted a video featuring the f-word while eating a taco. Do you think this is the kind of messaging that will win back support for the Democrat party?"

The account of @cveridis, which identifies as Libertarian, posted, "Swalwell is a California elected representative. I called him a tool years ago. He has not improved. That he tries to connect himself with our honored taco is a slap in the face. Cringeworthy trolling!"

Our Analysis:

The Swalwell Saga: Tacos, Tariffs, and Trolling

In a political landscape increasingly dominated by performative antics, Rep. Eric Swalwell (D-CA) has thrown his hat into the ring with a stunt that can only be described as a half-baked attempt at humor, thinly veiling a critique of Donald Trump's erratic trade policies. The term "TACO" (Trump Always Chickens Out), coined by Wall Street investors frustrated with the market volatility Trump's tariff threats have caused, serves as the foundation for Swalwell's theatrical dig—a video of him nonchalantly eating a Taco Bell taco in response to a question about Trump's flip-flopping on tariffs.

The Joke's on Who?

The intent behind Swalwell's video is clear: to ridicule Trump's inconsistent stance on tariffs and to resonate with a younger audience through a blend of pop culture reference and social media savviness. However, the execution leaves much to be desired. Instead of a sharp, poignant critique, what we have is a scene that feels more like a rejected Saturday Night Live sketch than a serious political statement.

Bold in its attempt but lacking in substance, Swalwell's taco-eating performance is emblematic of a broader issue within Democratic Party messaging—the struggle to connect with voters through means that are both relatable and respectful of their intelligence. The criticism from conservative outlets, while predictably hyperbolic, underscores a valid point: is this the type of messaging likely to regain support for the Democratic Party, or does it risk trivializing the serious implications of Trump's trade policies?

Conservative Outrage: Predictable yet Telling

The reaction from conservative media—ranging from calling the post "bizarre" and "very odd" to lambasting Swalwell for using profanity—while expected, also highlights a hypocrisy within their ranks. The same outlets that dismiss Swalwell's taco stunt as juvenile are often the first to defend Trump's far more egregious behavior on the grounds of him being "politically incorrect" or "telling it like it is."

Furthermore, the conservative focus on Swalwell's attempt to appeal to young men through such antics, while mocking it as desperate, misses a crucial point. The GOP has its own history of trying (and often failing) to engage younger voters through attempts at humor and pop culture references. The difference here, however, is not just in the method but in the underlying message. Swalwell, for all his faults in delivery, is attempting to highlight a serious issue—the impact of erratic policy decisions on the economy.

A Missed Opportunity for Meaningful Dialogue

The real tragedy of Swalwell's taco episode is not in its failure to entertain or even its potential to alienate. It's in the missed opportunity to foster meaningful dialogue about the consequences of Trump's tariff policies. Behind the layers of attempted humor and social media virality lies a critical issue affecting millions of Americans—job security, the stability of the economy, and the global standing of the United States.

In an era where the lines between politics and entertainment are increasingly blurred, the need for direct, honest communication has never been more critical. Swalwell's stunt, while perhaps well-intentioned, ultimately serves as a reminder of the challenges Democrats face in crafting a message that is both engaging and substantive.

Conclusion: Beyond the Taco

As we move closer to another election cycle, the Democratic Party would do well to reassess its approach to voter engagement. It's not enough to simply mock or parody the opposition; there must be a concerted effort to present a coherent, compelling alternative that addresses the real concerns of the electorate.

The "TACO" incident, while a minor blip in the grand scheme of things, is a stark illustration of the pitfalls of prioritizing style over substance. In the end, voters are looking for more than just a quick laugh—they're looking for leaders who can navigate the complex issues facing our nation with seriousness and sincerity.
