MAGA lawmaker accused of lying on sworn statement as GOP Senate primary gets ugly

The campaign of Sen. John Cornyn (R-TX) is accusing one of his primary rivals, Wesley Hunt, of committing voter fraud in 2016 by lying to election officials so he could take a provisional ballot he was ineligible for.

Hunt, along with Attorney General Ken Paxton, is locked in a close contest with Cornyn for the nomination. Most polls show Paxton at least slightly ahead of Cornyn, and Hunt placing third, with both candidates keen to reduce Hunt’s numbers in the hope his voters will go to them.

The allegation was laid out on X Friday by Matt Mackowiak, a longtime Austin-based Republican strategist now running communications for Cornyn’s campaign.

“In a bombshell development today in the U.S. Senate primary in Texas, little known 2nd term Congressman Wesley Hunt attempted to show he voted in the 2016 general election, but his provisional ballot was not counted because he was not a registered voter,” wrote Mackowiak. “Far more significant is the revelation that he claimed to the Election Judge and in a sworn affidavit that he was discharged in Oct. 2016 (one month before), but his official congressional biography, his campaign biography, and his military discharge document all show he was discharged four years earlier in 2012.”

According to the allegation, Hunt falsely told an elections judge that he had only just been discharged, as an explanation for why his voter registration wasn’t in the system, so that he could take a provisional ballot.

“Wesley Hunt has now unwittingly proved he committed voter fraud by lying in a sworn statement to an Election Judge both verbally and on a sworn document,” Mackowiak continued. “His military discharge form, and his official biography prove he was discharged in 2012, not in 2016 as he claimed in an attempt to illegally vote. Corrupt Ken Paxton should investigate Wesley Hunt for voter fraud and Wesley should admit he lied in a sworn document.”

The Senate primary has grown more and more fiery in recent months. Cornyn has also launched attacks on Paxton over accusations of marital infidelity, as well as alleged illegal use of his public office to boost his campaign.

Our Analysis:

The Sizzling Drama of Texas Politics: Unpacking the Accusations Against Wesley Hunt

In a political landscape already bristling with tension, the latest accusations lobbed in the Texas Senate primary are akin to throwing gasoline on a bonfire. Senator John Cornyn’s campaign has thrown a proverbial grenade into the mix, accusing Wesley Hunt, a relatively lesser-known figure in this high-stakes triad, of voter fraud during the 2016 general election. This allegation not only stirs the pot but effectively blasts the lid off it, revealing the depths to which political campaigns will dive in their quest for victory.

The Allegation Unfolded

Matt Mackowiak, a strategist turned mouthpiece for the Cornyn campaign, unveiled the charge with a flair that would make any drama enthusiast proud. According to Mackowiak, Hunt’s attempt to prove his civic participation in 2016 backfired spectacularly, exposing him instead as a fraudster who lied about his discharge date from the military to finagle a provisional ballot he had no right to. The crux of the matter here is the discrepancy in dates: Hunt’s official records show he was discharged in 2012, not October 2016 as he claimed.

A Closer Look at the Accusation

This isn’t merely a matter of misremembered dates or bureaucratic snafus. If the allegations hold water, Hunt’s actions represent a deliberate attempt to circumvent electoral integrity. Such actions, especially coming from someone in Hunt’s position, strike at the very heart of democratic processes. Yet, one cannot help but ponder the timing and the messenger of these allegations. With Cornyn and Paxton neck and neck, and Hunt trailing as a distant third, the unveiling of this ‘bombshell’ reeks of strategic political maneuvering more than an altruistic concern for electoral integrity.

Cornyn vs. Paxton vs. Hunt: A Political Ménage à Trois

The dynamics within this primary contest are nothing short of a Shakespearean drama, with accusations of marital infidelity and illegal campaign tactics already muddying the waters. Into this volatile mix, the voter fraud accusation adds another layer of intrigue and desperation. It’s clear that both Cornyn and Paxton perceive Hunt not just as a rival but as a potential spoiler who could dilute their chances of clinching the nomination.

The Irony of Calling for an Investigation

The call for an investigation by none other than “Corrupt Ken Paxton” — as Mackowiak delightfully dubs him — is rich with irony. Paxton, whose tenure has been marred by his own share of legal and ethical controversies, investigating Hunt for voter fraud is akin to the pot calling the kettle black. This scenario underscores the convoluted mess that often characterizes political contests, where the lines between right and wrong are blurred by ambition and the hunger for power.

The Bigger Picture

Beyond the immediate drama and partisan squabbles, this episode reflects a troubling trend in American politics where allegations of electoral malfeasance are increasingly weaponized. While ensuring the integrity of elections is paramount, one must question the motives behind such accusations and the impact they have on public trust in the electoral process.

In the end, this sordid affair is more than just a story of alleged voter fraud; it’s a mirror reflecting the dark underbelly of political campaigns where truth is often the first casualty. As this saga unfolds, the voters of Texas—and indeed, the nation—must sift through the theatrics to find the truth, a task made all the more challenging in an era of rampant disinformation and political polarization.
