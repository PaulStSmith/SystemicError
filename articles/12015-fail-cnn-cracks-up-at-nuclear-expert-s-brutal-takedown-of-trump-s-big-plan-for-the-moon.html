'Fail': CNN cracks up at nuclear expert's brutal takedown of Trump's big plan for the moon

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Nuclear policy analyst Joe Cirincione with CNN’s Boris Sanchez and Brianna Keilar (CNN screenshot)

CNN's Brianna Keilar introduced nuclear policy analyst Joe Cirincione to discuss the Trump administration's big plans to put a nuclear reactor on the moon.

She mentioned Cirincione's book, "Nuclear Nightmares: Securing the world before it is too late," before segueing into the subject at hand.

"Speaking of nuclear nightmares, last we spoke to you, it was about nuclear wasps, radioactive wasps," Keilar said. "Now we're talking about a nuclear reactor on the moon, which raises some safety concerns."

"Well, if the purpose of this announcement was to distract attention from the Epstein files, it briefly succeeded," Cirincione quipped as Keilar and co-host Boris Sanchez cracked up. "Sean Duffy was trending on social media for the last 24 hours right behind Ghislaine Maxwell."

He continued, "But if it's to propose a serious plan for the human colonization of Mars, I think it fails the test."

Cirincione said that a small, modular, launchable nuclear reactor has been in the cards for a while, but Duffy's announcement "actually resets the timeline that NASA set just five years ago of being able to have a nuclear reactor ready to launch by 2026," Cirincione said.

"Now, he's proposing 2030 for a bigger reactor, about 10 times the size of the one that was originally proposed several years ago. I just don't see the technology available. I don't think we're going to be able to make this technological leap, but we are certainly going to spend billions of dollars trying to do so, probably at the at the expense of other vital NASA missions."

Cirincione said that such a mission would "require a launch vehicle that doesn't yet exist, require a reactor that doesn't yet exist, and pose some serious safety hazards themselves, like the risk of failure of the system, the explosion of the system, or another launch failure as they try to put this in orbit and then onto the moon. I don't think the risks are worth it," Crincione said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Lunar Nuclear Ambitions: A Folly Beyond Measure

In what reads like a science fiction subplot torn straight from the pages of an overzealous space opera, the Trump administration's plan to plant a nuclear reactor on the moon has surfaced, stirring a pot of controversy and skepticism. CNN's Brianna Keilar's conversation with nuclear policy analyst Joe Cirincione sheds light on this absurdity with a precision that cuts deeper than the vacuum of space.

The Distraction Dance

Cirincione's remark that this announcement might serve as a momentary diversion from the Epstein debacle is both humorous and painfully indicative of the administration's modus operandi. The attempt to eclipse scandals with grandiose schemes is not novel, but the audacity to propose lunar nuclear reactors as a viable distraction is both laughable and worrying. It's a testament to the lengths at which current governance will stretch to shift the narrative, a tactic as transparent as it is desperate.

A Technological Leap Too Far

Cirincione's analysis is unforgiving and rooted in reality. The proposal not only disregards the established timeline set by NASA but also blissfully ignores the technological and financial hurdles that accompany such a fantastical venture. The mention of a reactor "10 times the size" of what was initially proposed reeks of hubris and a fundamental disconnect from practicality and feasibility. This isn't just moving the goalposts; it's launching them into a lunar orbit without a sliver of a plan on how to reach them.

A Misallocation of Cosmic Proportions

The financial implications of this moonshot are staggering. Billions of dollars potentially funneled into a project with questionable returns, all while other vital NASA missions could suffer from neglect. This isn't just an issue of fiscal irresponsibility; it's a damning critique of priorities. In an era where space exploration should be about advancing human knowledge and solving terrestrial problems, such as climate change or resource scarcity, diverting a colossal amount of resources to a dubious nuclear project on the moon is, frankly, ludicrous.

Safety Last?

The safety concerns cannot be overstated. The risks of launching and operating a nuclear reactor in a lunar environment are profound, not just for the personnel involved but for the broader implications of a potential disaster beyond Earth. The administration's cavalier attitude towards these risks underscores a troubling disregard for safety, scientific rigor, and environmental considerations.

Conclusion: Ground Control to Major Tom

In sum, the Trump administration's lunar nuclear reactor plan is a folly of cosmic proportions. It's a clear example of governance by distraction, a penchant for technological overreach, and a misallocation of resources that could otherwise address more pressing issues. The moon, with its silent beauty and scientific promise, deserves better than to be the backdrop for such a misguided escapade.

As the dust settles on this discussion, one can only hope that reason, practicality, and a recommitment to Earth's pressing needs will prevail. But until then, this plan remains a stark reminder of the current administration's failure to ground its policies in the realities of science, safety, and global stewardship.
