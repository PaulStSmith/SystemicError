'Very hard to imagine': Trump's bizarre proclamation doesn't pass muster with legal expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 29, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Quixotic Crusade Against Biden’s Autopen Use

President Donald Trump looks on aboard Air Force One during travel to Palm Beach, Florida, from Joint Base Andrews, Maryland on Nov. 25, 2025. REUTERS/Anna Rose Layden

A legal expert on Friday said it’s unlikely that President Donald Trump will have the legal ability to carry out his bizarre move after he claimed that all laws and pardons under former President Joe Biden signed by autopenwere null and threatened perjury against him if he claims otherwise.

No requirement in the Constitution has said that the president needs to physically sign documents, Berit Berger, a former federal prosecutor, told CNN on Friday afternoon.

“It’s absolutely appropriate for a president to have directed a subordinate to sign for him or to use a document,” Berger said.

It’s unclear what Trump’s legal mechanism is for undoing all the documents Biden signed with an autopen, she added. It’s also difficult to know which exact documents were, in fact, signed by autopen, or which ones Trump has referred to.

Trump has threatened to charge Biden with perjury if he claims he did not use it for a law or pardon. But Berger argued that might not be the actual case, considering the precedent, and that the likelihood that the Department of Justice or Trump could create against him for using the autopen is slim.

“This would also seem very unlikely, again... a Supreme Court decision that granted President Trump wide immunity for acts that were done in his official capacity. Actually, President Biden would be able to rely on that same language and saying that anything that he did in his official capacity, and one could certainly argue that signing pardons or documents, whether he used an autopen or not, was actually in his official capacity. That same immunity would apply to him as well. I’m not exactly sure what perjury President Trump would be referring to but at least, the use of the autopen, given the fact that it has been used by so many presidents in the past, it’s been done so widely, it would be very hard for me to imagine a situation where this would form the basis of any potential criminal liability.”

Trump has not named any exact orders or documents. The autopen has been used among multiple administrations — not just Biden’s administration.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Quixotic Crusade Against Biden’s Autopen Use

In an absurd twist of political theater, former President Donald Trump has made an outlandish claim that all laws and pardons signed by autopen under President Joe Biden’s administration are null. Moreover, he theatrically threatened perjury charges against Biden if he denies using an autopen. This bizarre move not only lacks legal standing but also flies in the face of established presidential norms and practices.

The Autopen: A Non-Issue Turned Political Spectacle

The use of an autopen for signing documents is neither novel nor controversial. It’s a practice steeped in practicality, embraced by multiple administrations, both Republican and Democrat. The assertion by Trump that Biden’s use of an autopen somehow invalidates his presidential acts is not just baseless but laughably misguided. Berit Berger, a seasoned legal expert and former federal prosecutor, underscores this point, affirming that no constitutional requirement mandates a president to physically sign every document. The president’s authorization, not the method of signing, is what imbues a document with legal force.

A Legal Wild Goose Chase

Trump’s threat to charge Biden with perjury over the use of an autopen is equally far-fetched. Berger rightly points out the improbability of such charges sticking, given the Supreme Court’s stance on presidential immunity for actions taken in official capacity. The legal gymnastics required to turn the use of an autopen into a perjurious act would be Olympic in difficulty—and ultimately fruitless. It’s a testament to the lengths to which Trump is willing to go to undermine his successor, leveraging any means, no matter how implausible, to cast doubt and aspersions on Biden’s presidency.

A History Ignored

The historical precedent for autopen use further undermines Trump’s already shaky ground. Presidents have long utilized this tool for the sake of efficiency and practicality, a necessity in the fast-paced arena of national governance. Trump’s selective amnesia regarding this fact is either a calculated attempt to stir controversy where there is none or a profound misunderstanding of presidential norms—likely a bit of both.

The Real Issue at Hand

What we’re witnessing is not a genuine legal concern but a political sideshow, a distraction from substantive issues facing the nation. Trump’s focus on the autopen is a misdirection, a bid to keep his base engaged and angry, feeding off manufactured outrage. It’s a tactic that disregards the intelligence of the American public and disrespects the office of the presidency.

In the grand scheme, Trump’s latest tirade against Biden is unlikely to gain any legal traction. It serves only as a reminder of his relentless pursuit of the spotlight, his penchant for controversy, and his disregard for the norms and practices that underpin the American political system. The autopen saga is, at its core, a non-issue inflated into a pseudo-scandal, emblematic of Trump’s style of politics—one rooted in spectacle over substance, and division over unity.
