MAGA lawmaker's fury over Charlie Kirk questions gets reporter suspended: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Suspension of A.G. Gancarski: A Case Study in Political Sensitivities and Media Ethics

FILE PHOTO: Charlie Kirk, Turning Point USA founder, puts on a MAGA hat during the AmericaFest 2024 conference sponsored by conservative group Turning Point in Phoenix, Arizona, U.S. December 19, 2024. REUTERS/Cheney Orr/File Photo

A Florida reporter who specializes in political coverage has been suspended because a Republican lawmaker, best known for his inflammatory anti-Muslim rhetoric, objected to questions about the shooting of far-right conservative Charlie Kirk.

According to a report from Mediaite’s’ Sarah Rumpf, Florida Politics founder and publisher Peter Schorsch put reporter A.G. Gancarski in no-man's land after Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL) flipped out on X because the reporter texted him questions about a bill he had sponsored when he was in the state senate that would have allowed open carry on Florida college campuses.

That bill failed to be approved.

Gancarski asked Fine, “If Charlie Kirk getting shot affects your position on campus carry?” before adding, “If gun control had been in play could the tragedy have been avoided?”

Fine replied in the text chain, “I learned that Charlie Kirk was shot 23 minutes ago. I am repulsed that you would even think to ask a political question when all anyone should be doing is praying for his survival. Never contact me again.”

Not content with that, Fine posted a screenshot of the exchange on X where he wrote, “You don’t hate the media enough.”

That drew the attention of publisher Schorsch, who noted on X, he has “immediately suspended” his employee.

He then added, “I cannot be the person I am, a critic of extremism on both the left and right if those who work with me lack the basic empathy in horrific situations. Perhaps other outlets can go immediately to the political ramifications of a tragedy like the shooting of a cultural leader, but that is not what I am about.”

For its part, Mediaite reported that Gancarski responded in a tweet that later disappeared after his X account “went dark” at his publisher's recommendation, where he wrote it was a “valid question” and insisted, “I stand by the question. Tragedy is ultimately what tests policy positions.”

Our Analysis:

The Suspension of A.G. Gancarski: A Case Study in Political Sensitivities and Media Ethics

In an episode that slices through the already taut tensions between politicians and the press, A.G. Gancarski, a reporter for Florida Politics, finds himself cast into professional purgatory. The catalyst? A series of questions directed at Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL), a figure whose controversy seems only matched by his legislative fervor, particularly regarding his stance on gun laws. This scenario unfolds against a backdrop painted with the broad strokes of political allegiance, journalistic inquiry, and the ever-present specter of tragedy, in this case, the shooting of far-right conservative Charlie Kirk.

Gancarski's Query: A Flashpoint for Controversy

Gancarski's approach, while arguably abrupt, hinged on a legitimate line of inquiry: Does a public figure's stance on gun control shift in the wake of violence? It's a question that probes the heart of policy-making—whether personal experience trumps ideological commitment. Yet, the timing and phrasing of the question, posed shortly after Kirk's shooting, were met with ire and disgust from Fine, who accused the reporter of insensitivity.

Rep. Randy Fine's Reaction: A Study in Deflection

Fine's response, an impassioned plea for prayers over politics, carries the unmistakable undertone of an attempt to sidestep a difficult conversation. By framing Gancarski's inquiry as morally repugnant, Fine not only absolves himself from answering but also shifts the narrative towards the reporter's alleged lack of empathy. The subsequent decision to amplify this exchange on social media, accompanied by a caption that could be read as an incitement against the media, escalates the situation further, blurring the lines between personal affront and professional critique.

Peter Schorsch's Decision: A Misguided Attempt at Moderation?

In what appears to be a knee-jerk reaction to Fine's outrage, Peter Schorsch, founder and publisher of Florida Politics, suspended Gancarski. Schorsch's justification, that his platform cannot partake in discussing the political ramifications of a tragedy like Kirk's shooting due to a commitment to critiquing extremism "on both the left and right," is puzzling. It suggests a misunderstanding of the role of media in scrutinizing the political implications of public events, especially those involving public figures and their legislative preferences.

The Core Issue: Journalism or Sensationalism?

At the heart of this debacle lies a critical question: Should journalists refrain from probing into the political implications of tragic events? Gancarski's stance, as briefly articulated in a now-vanished tweet, argues that tragedy indeed tests policy positions. This perspective, though uncomfortable for some, is essential in a democratic society that relies on a free press to question, critique, and hold power to account.

In Conclusion: A Misfire in the Battle for Empathy and Ethics

The suspension of A.G. Gancarski is not a victory for empathy, as Schorsch might believe, but rather a stifling of essential dialogue. By succumbing to the pressure exerted by Rep. Fine's emotional rebuttal, Florida Politics has inadvertently endorsed a dangerous precedent: that politicians can avoid scrutiny by invoking moral high ground, especially in the immediate aftermath of tragedy. This incident underscores the fragile balance between sensitivity and the imperative journalistic duty to question policy and its proponents, irrespective of the timing. In the end, the real casualty may well be the public's right to expect a robust, unflinching examination of the policies and positions held by those in power.
