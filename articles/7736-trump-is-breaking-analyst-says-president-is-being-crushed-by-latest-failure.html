Trump is 'breaking': Analyst says president is being crushed by latest 'failure'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump participates in a roundtable discussion with business leaders at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 10, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump’s efforts to criminally prosecute his political adversaries have finally “collapsed,” at least according to journalist Jason Easley, who argued the latest court rebuke to the Trump administration to be the final nail in the coffin for the president’s “revenge campaign.”

“The Trump revenge campaign has fallen apart,” Easley wrote in an analysis published Friday on PoliticusUSA, a liberal news website.

“His demand to [Attorney General] Pam Bondi that she indict people on his enemies list has so far resulted in zero convictions. Before Trump returned to office, many Americans were concerned that he would be able to abuse his power to crush criticism with political prosecutions successfully… Trump hasn’t been able to break the United States. Instead, it seems the U.S. is breaking Trump.”

Trump’s Justice Department has targeted a number of the president’s political adversaries, including FBI Director James Comey, New York Attorney General Letitia James, former National Security Adviser John Bolton, and Georgia District Attorney Fani Willis, all of whom Trump has publicly smeared.

Courts have repeatedly rebuked the Trump administration, however, throwing out both Comey’s and James’ indictments, sparking outrage among the president’s most loyal supporters. And this week, the courts rebuked Trump yet again after throwing out a second attempt by the DOJ to indict James, who previously brought civil charges against Trump for allegedly inflating the values of his company’s assets.

“Donald Trump is going to keep trying to get his revenge on people whom he has deemed his enemies, but just like all of Trump’s other efforts to establish an undemocratic regime, it has failed,” Easley wrote.

“The United States still has more battles to wage to protect itself from its own president, but what Trump thought he could waltz into office and do has flopped, and America is better off because of it.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Justice: Trump’s Failed Vendetta

In a recent analysis by Jason Easley for PoliticusUSA, a narrative unfolds that could be mistaken for a plot from a dystopian novel, yet it is the reality under the Trump administration. The attempt by President Donald Trump to criminally prosecute his political adversaries has been described as a “revenge campaign,” a term that chillingly evokes authoritarian regimes rather than a democracy. The collapse of this campaign, as Easley puts it, is not just a failure on Trump’s part but a testament to the resilience of the American judicial system.

Trump’s Vendetta Against Democracy

It’s not just the audacity but the brazenness of Trump’s actions that stand out. Demanding Attorney General Pam Bondi to indict individuals on his so-called “enemies list” reveals a stark departure from democratic norms. It’s a tactic straight out of the authoritarian playbook, aiming to weaponize the justice system against political opponents. The targets? High-profile figures like FBI Director James Comey, New York Attorney General Letitia James, former National Security Adviser John Bolton, and Georgia District Attorney Fani Willis. The pattern is clear: anyone who dares to challenge or criticize Trump finds themselves in the crosshairs.

The Courts’ Rebuke: A Beacon of Hope

The judicial branch, however, has shown remarkable integrity and resilience. The dismissal of indictments against Comey and James, followed by a second failed attempt against James, underscores a critical point: the courts are not willing to be complicit in Trump’s vendetta. This judicial resistance is a beacon of hope, a reminder that the system, although under strain, is capable of standing up to blatant misuse of power.

The Bigger Picture: Democracy’s Resilience

Easley’s analysis goes beyond the immediate failures of Trump’s attempts at political prosecution. It highlights a broader narrative of resistance and resilience. The idea that Trump hasn’t been able to break the United States is powerful. It suggests that despite the unprecedented challenges posed by his administration, the foundational principles of democracy and justice remain intact.

The Ongoing Battle

Yet, the article ends on a note of caution. The battle to protect the U.S. from its own president is ongoing. Trump’s consistent efforts to establish what Easley terms an “undemocratic regime” are a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic institutions. Each attempt to misuse the justice system for political revenge, even if unsuccessful, chips away at the public’s trust in these institutions.

Conclusion

The collapse of Trump’s revenge campaign is a victory for democracy, but it’s also a warning. It illustrates the lengths to which Trump is willing to go to silence his critics and the importance of a robust and independent judiciary in safeguarding democratic norms. As America moves forward, it’s crucial to remember the lessons learned from this episode: vigilance and resistance are key to preserving democracy. The U.S. may have withstood Trump’s attempts at subversion this time, but the battle for the soul of the nation is far from over.
