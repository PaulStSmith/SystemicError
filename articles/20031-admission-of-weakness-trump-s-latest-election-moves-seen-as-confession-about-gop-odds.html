The Guardian reported on Sunday about Trump's efforts to impact the midterms on behalf of the GOP.

"Donald Trump has made clear that he is willing to bring the full weight of the White House to bear to prevent his Republican party losing control of the US Congress in the midterm elections next year, orchestrating a more direct and legally dubious intervention than any of his predecessors," according to the outlet. "The US president’s multipronged approach includes redrawing congressional district maps, seeking to purge voter rolls, taking aim at mail-in voting and voting machines, and ordering the justice department to investigate Democrats’ prime fundraising tool."

But, according to the reporting, this wouldn't be happening if Trump didn't sense a problem.

"Yet while Trump’s gambit is a flex of executive power, it could also be seen as an admission of potential weakness. The incumbent president’s party typically loses seats in midterm elections. In 2018, Democrats won enough to take back the House, stymieing Trump’s agenda and leading to his impeachment," the article states, noting that "only 37% of voters approve of the way Trump is handling his job as president, according to a Quinnipiac University national poll released on Wednesday, while 55% disapprove."

The report also quotes Wendy Schiller, a political science professor at Brown University in Providence, Rhode Island, who said, "Trump and the Republicans would not be trying to stack the deck if they didn’t think they were going to lose the hand. They are looking at poll numbers and they know midterms are bad to incumbent presidents over the last 60 years and it’s a very slim margin in the House."

“In order for Trump to sustain the loyalty of the House – he’s already gotten everything he pretty much wants – he needs them to think he’s on their side so he’s going to go out and be very public about rigging the voting system to keep them in power," Schiller then added. "Will that be enough to overcome general unhappiness at the moment that the voters seem to have with the economy, inflation, even Trump’s border policies? It’s not enough to keep the Republicans in line. You have to get independent voters to vote for you again and that’s at risk for the Republicans right now.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Midterm Election Maneuvers: Desperation or Strategy?

In a revelation that should surprise exactly no one, Donald Trump's latest political escapades involve a series of moves that many are calling attempts to "rig" the upcoming midterm elections. According to The Guardian, Trump is pulling out all the stops to ensure the GOP retains control of Congress, employing tactics that range from the questionable to the downright legally dubious. Let's dissect the desperation and strategy behind these efforts.

The Multi-Pronged Assault on Fair Elections

Trump's strategy, as outlined by The Guardian, is a comprehensive attack on the integrity of the electoral process. Redrawing congressional district maps, purging voter rolls, targeting mail-in voting and voting machines, and ordering the Justice Department to investigate the Democrats' primary fundraising tool are not just actions of a confident incumbent. They reek of desperation.

Redrawing Congressional District Maps & Purging Voter Rolls: These are old tricks in a new guise, gerrymandering and voter suppression updated for the Trump era. It's a blatant attempt to skew the playing field in favor of the GOP.

Attacking Mail-In Voting and Voting Machines: In the midst of a global pandemic, efforts to undermine mail-in voting are not just anti-democratic; they're potentially endangering voters' health. This move is particularly insidious, given its potential to disenfranchise the most vulnerable.

Weaponizing the Justice Department: Perhaps the most troubling of all Trump's strategies is the commandeering of the Justice Department to launch investigations into Democratic fundraising. This is a gross misuse of power, turning a theoretically impartial body into a tool for political gain.

Admission of Weakness?

Despite the authoritarian overtones of these actions, they could also be interpreted as signs of Trump's vulnerability. The incumbent president's party typically loses seats in midterm elections, and with Trump's approval ratings languishing at 37% according to a Quinnipiac University poll, the desperation is palpable. Wendy Schiller's analysis hits the nail on the head: Trump and the Republicans wouldn't be attempting to "stack the deck" if they weren't acutely aware of their precarious position.

The Bigger Picture

Schiller further argues that Trump's efforts are not just about retaining GOP control but also about maintaining his grip on the party by projecting loyalty. However, this strategy may be insufficient to sway independent voters, whose support will be crucial for a Republican victory.

Conclusion: Desperation, Strategy, or Both?

Trump's midterm election strategy is a dangerous gambit that undermines the democratic process. It's a clear indication of his perceived vulnerability and an attempt to manipulate the system in his favor. However, it's also a calculated move designed to solidify his base and ensure GOP loyalty. The real question is, will it work, or will it backfire, further alienating independent voters and sealing the fate of the Republican Party in the midterms? Only time will tell, but one thing is certain: democracy should not be the casualty of political ambition.
