‘Pill for bald guys’: Analyst jokes Trump meltdown signals new money-making scheme

President Donald Trump received a brutal parade of mockery in The Guardian on Friday, over his recent temper tantrum about how he looked on the cover of Time Magazine.

Trump took particular offense at how thin and insubstantial his hair looked in the photo, raging, “They ‘disappeared’ my hair, and then had something floating on top of my head that looked like a floating crown, but an extremely small one. Really weird! I never liked taking pictures from underneath angles, but this is a super bad picture, and deserves to be called out. What are they doing, and why?”

“Donald Trump’s latest tirade is, at its core, about his own battle with the ravages of time. Literally and figuratively,” wrote Dave Schilling. “Once again, Trump has come face to face with what he actually looks like, which would terrify and enrage just about anyone. When he’s not obsessing over the afterlife, Trump spends an inordinate amount of time kvetching about his appearance. First, it was a painting that made him look like a time-traveling Tudor monarch who just got kicked out of a Sizzler buffet line. Now, he’s directed his ire at Time magazine, which put him on the cover in tribute to his efforts to broker peace in Gaza“ — even though the article was one of his most complimentary writeups in mainstream media in a long time.

In Time’s photo, Schilling wrote, “Trump’s signature metallic yellow wave of hair looks thin and wispy, like someone dumped a handful of dry angel hair pasta on his head as a college prank.”

Trump has been rather notorious throughout his public career, even long before he became president, for flaunting his peculiar hairstyle, so his fury over being confronted with this unflattering image makes plenty of sense, wrote Schilling: “I recognize that the natural first reaction to realizing you’re losing your hair is to be angry about it. Surely, it must be someone else’s fault. It’s the angles or the lighting. The reality is what it is, though, and it’s better to live in the world than to imagine it as something else. The man is nearly 80.”

Indeed, wrote Schilling, Trump ought to make the most of it and do what he has done with every other facet of his personality and public office: cash in.

“Spin this negative into a positive and let Trump – who has already successfully sold his fans hats, steaks, fake college educations, wine and an imploding economy – endorse a pill for bald guys. From pain comes profit,” wrote Schilling. “What’s more American than that?”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Vanity Fair: A Symptom of a Deeper Malady

In an era where the absurd has become the norm, the spectacle of a former President, Donald Trump, throwing what can only be described as a petulant fit over his portrayal in a magazine cover is both ludicrous and alarmingly telling. The Guardian’s recent piece, capturing Trump’s rage over his depiction in Time Magazine, serves as a perfect microcosm of the man’s unyielding narcissism and his bizarre priorities at a time when the world faces crises of monumental proportions.

The Art of the Tantrum

Trump’s indignation at the supposed slight against his hair by Time Magazine is not just a trivial display of vanity; it is a vivid illustration of his perpetual obsession with image over substance. His complaint, “They ‘disappeared’ my hair, and then had something floating on top of my head that looked like a floating crown, but an extremely small one,” borders on the theatrically absurd. It’s a line that might be amusing if it weren’t so depressingly indicative of the man’s priorities.

The Mirror of Truth

Dave Schilling, in his analysis, hits the nail squarely on the head: Trump’s tirade is less about the specific photo and more about an ongoing battle with his own image. The former President has long been notorious for his peculiar hairstyle—a symbol, perhaps, of his broader refusal to engage with reality. Schilling’s commentary, “Once again, Trump has come face to face with what he actually looks like, which would terrify and enrage just about anyone,” is not just a jab at Trump’s vanity; it’s a scathing critique of his entire persona.

The Business of Being Trump

The suggestion that Trump should capitalize on this latest indignity by endorsing a product for baldness is a masterstroke of irony. It encapsulates the essence of Trump’s America: the transformation of every conceivable aspect of life, no matter how sacred or personal, into a commodity. “Spin this negative into a positive and let Trump – who has already successfully sold his fans hats, steaks, fake college educations, wine, and an imploding economy – endorse a pill for bald guys,” Schilling writes. This is not just satire; it’s a bleak commentary on the commodification of the American Presidency under Trump.

The Larger Picture

What’s most American, then, if not the relentless pursuit of profit, even at the cost of dignity? Trump’s saga with Time Magazine might seem like a frivolous distraction. However, it’s emblematic of a larger, more systemic problem: the cult of personality over policy, image over integrity, and profit over people. In this light, Trump’s tantrum is not just a moment of personal vanity but a symptom of the broader malaise afflicting American politics.

In conclusion, the Guardian’s piece on Trump’s latest meltdown is more than a mere recounting of a former President’s vanity; it’s a pointed critique of the man and the system that enabled him. It forces us to confront not just the absurdity of Trump’s preoccupations but the values and priorities of the society that continues to be captivated by his spectacle. The real question isn’t why Trump is obsessed with his image; it’s why we, as a society, continue to indulge in these distractions at the expense of addressing the systemic errors that plague our political landscape.
