The father-in-law of Donald Trump's lesser-known daughter is finding out the hard way that his appointment as an adviser to the president is less important than he apparently believed it would be.

According to a report from Politico, businessman Massad Boulos, father of Michael Boulos who married Tiffany Trump in 2022, was taken into the fold and given a job as senior adviser on the Middle East and Africa policy during the 2024 presidential transition and things have not panned out as expected.

As Politico's Felicia Schwartz and Robbie Gramer are reporting, Boulos "has been given formal titles without clear responsibilities and kept out of key discussions, or been brought into negotiations after much of the work has already been completed."

The report notes that his tenure got off to a rocky start when the Trump administration basically pulled the plug on his first major endeavor in April where he was denied the use of a government plane as he set out for a trip to Africa.

According to the report, Boulos showed up at Joint Base Andrews expecting to fly out with a small State Department delegation and was left hanging until he was contacted by White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and told the government "wouldn’t pay for the U.S. military to fly him to central Africa." and that "He’d have to trim down his multi-country trip by cutting a stop in Angola, and fly commercial the next day instead."

The report notes that the incident is indicative of the tenuous relationship he has with the White House where some insiders have chafed at his assumption that he is a player in the administration.

According to the Politico report, "Reports that Boulos misrepresented his wealth and the size of his business holdings before entering government also didn’t sit well with some, one of the people said."

Addressing his less-than-impressive status within the second Trump administration, one insider suggested, "The job was more symbolic, but he didn’t know that. Everyone knew it but him.”

The report notes that Boulos declined to comment, but added that White House spokesperson Karoline Leavitt stated he is doing an "exceptional job" and cited a deal he worked out between Congolese and Rwandan foreign ministers.

A Closer Look at the Boulos Debacle: Politics, Perception, and Hollow Titles

The saga of Massad Boulos, father-in-law to the lesser spotlighted Tiffany Trump, unfolds like a cautionary tale of political hubris, nepotism, and the illusion of influence within the labyrinthine corridors of the Trump administration. Let's dissect the sordid details of Boulos' experience, as reported by Politico, and expose the layers of irony and dysfunction that seem to pervade this administration.

The Illusion of Influence

Boulos' appointment as a senior adviser on Middle East and Africa policy was, by all accounts, a move that smacked of nepotism—a term that seems all too familiar in the context of the Trump White House. Yet, what makes Boulos' case particularly juicy is the stark disconnect between title and authority. The report outlines a man granted "formal titles without clear responsibilities," a token figure kept on the periphery of real power. This is emblematic of a broader issue within the administration: the dilution of expertise and experience in favor of superficial appointments.

The Rocky Start and Symbolic Gestures

The anecdote of Boulos being denied a government plane for his ambitious Africa trip is more than just an embarrassing setback; it's a metaphor for his entire tenure. Denied the symbols of power and influence, Boulos was relegated to a commercial flight, a tangible manifestation of his diminished standing. This incident is not merely about logistical hiccups but highlights a systemic disregard for clear roles and responsibilities within the administration.

The Question of Competence and Integrity

More alarming than the logistical snafus are the whispers of Boulos misrepresenting his wealth and business acumen. This raises significant questions about the vetting process and the criteria for appointments within the administration. It's one thing to appoint allies and family members to positions of power, but it's another to do so without due diligence. This lax approach to governance not only undermines the credibility of the administration but also jeopardizes the integrity of its policies and initiatives.

The Verdict from Within

Perhaps the most damning indictment comes from within the administration itself, with insiders suggesting that Boulos' job was "more symbolic." This revelation is a stark reminder of the Trump administration's penchant for optics over substance, where titles are doled out as tokens of favor, not as indicators of responsibility or expertise.

The Official Spin

In a predictable twist, White House spokesperson Karoline Leavitt lauds Boulos for doing an "exceptional job," citing a singular deal as evidence. This attempt at spin is as transparent as it is laughable. One successful negotiation does not a competent adviser make, especially when weighed against the backdrop of misrepresentation and ineffectiveness.

Conclusion

Massad Boulos' story is a microcosm of the broader dysfunctions within the Trump White House: nepotism, superficiality, and a profound disconnect between titles and tangible influence. As the political circus continues, one can't help but wonder about the real cost of these symbolic gestures and hollow appointments. The Boulos debacle is not just a personal failure but a systemic one, shedding light on the flawed foundations upon which this administration is built.
