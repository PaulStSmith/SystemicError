The late Princess Diana is at the center of a new revelation about the relationship between President Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein, the disgraced financier who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges. The revelation sparked fury from the White House.

According to Trump biographer Michael Wolf, Trump and Epstein allegedly competed to see which of the two could sleep with Diana first — with the contest continuing until the Princess of Wales died in the extensively covered 1997 vehicle crash in Paris, France.

“They had a competition, Trump and Epstein,” Wolff told The Daily Beast, speaking on the publication’s podcast Inside Trump’s Head.

“Of who would be the one, the first one to sleep with Princess Diana... they just understood, what could you get from these people? Both Trump and Epstein. What can you get from somebody, is the question you would always ask about anybody.”

The Daily Beast’s report triggered an angry response from the White House, with Trump's Communications Director Steven Cheung lashing out at Wolff, who personally interviewed Epstein in 2017 and claims to possess more than 100 hours of recorded conversations with the late sex offender.

“Michael Wolff is a lying sack of s--t and has been proven to be a fraud,” Cheung told The Daily Beast. “He routinely fabricates stories originating from his sick and warped imagination, only possible because he has a severe and debilitating case of Trump derangement syndrome that has rotted his peanut-sized brain.”

The revelation, however, would be consistent with past comments made by Trump over the years. English journalist Selina Scott reported in 2015 that Trump said he wanted Diana to become his “trophy wife” following her 1996 divorce from then-Prince Charles, sending her flowers following the separation.

Trump would go on to tell radio personality Howard Stern, on air in 1997, that he believed he “could have” slept with Diana, and would repeat the claims again to Stern in 2000, saying he would have slept with the Princess of Wales “without hesitation.”

Trump’s ties with Epstein have been under increased scrutiny since thebombshell reportfrom The Wall Street Journal in July that revealed new details about their relationship, suggesting it was more intimate than previously known. The Justice Department is also sitting on thousands of files related to Epstein that it refuses to release — files that Trump wastold he was named inby Attorney General Pam Bondi.

Our Analysis:

The Deplorable Objectification of Princess Diana: Trump, Epstein, and the Depths of Disrespect

In a revelation that plumbs new depths of depravity, we've been served a story that manages to intertwine the late Princess Diana's memory with the sordid saga of Jeffrey Epstein and Donald Trump. According to Michael Wolff, a biographer with a penchant for stirring the pot, these two men allegedly treated the pursuit of Diana as a game—a contest to see who could bed her first. This isn't just tabloid fodder; it's a grim reflection of how power and privilege are wielded to dehumanize and objectify.

The Contest of the Condemnable

Wolff's allegations suggest a deplorable level of disrespect towards Diana, reducing her legacy to a notch on the bedpost in a macabre competition. That this "contest" reportedly persisted until her tragic death in 1997 only adds a layer of grotesque callousness to the tale. It's a narrative that reeks of entitlement and exploitation, symptomatic of the darkest corners of wealth and influence.

A White House in Fury

The White House's response to these claims was, predictably, a vehement denial. Trump's Communications Director, Steven Cheung, retorted with an ad hominem attack on Wolff, labeling him a "lying sack of s--t" and attributing his allegations to "Trump derangement syndrome." Yet, this defense is as weak as it is coarse. Cheung's rebuttal does nothing to confront the substance of Wolff's claims, opting instead for character assassination.

A Pattern of Objectification

Trump's history of comments about Diana adds a layer of credibility to Wolff's claims. His past boasts about potentially making Diana his "trophy wife" and his distasteful speculation on Howard Stern's radio show about sleeping with her paint a portrait of a man for whom women are conquests rather than equals. These are not isolated incidents but part of a broader pattern of behavior that has been widely documented and criticized.

The Unanswered Questions

The revelation also shines a light on the still-opaque relationship between Trump and Epstein. With the Justice Department sitting on thousands of files related to Epstein, of which Trump is reportedly named, one must ask: what more is there to uncover? The reluctance to release these files only feeds speculation and concern about the extent of their association and the activities it entailed.

Conclusion

This latest episode in the Trump-Epstein saga is more than a salacious scandal; it's a stark reminder of the toxic mix of power, entitlement, and misogyny that still permeates the upper echelons of society. It underscores the urgent need for transparency and accountability, not just in the corridors of power but in how we remember and respect figures like Princess Diana. She deserves far better than to be posthumously dragged into the mire of men who seem incapable of viewing women as anything but objects to be won.
