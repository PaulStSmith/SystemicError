GOP judge uncorks damning warning for Todd Blanche as DOJ suffers another big court loss

Blanche Is Losing in Court Because This Was Never a Real Legal Theory

The case was the point

Todd Blanche just absorbed his 20th straight loss in Donald Trump’s campaign to pry voter data out of states, and a Republican-appointed judge dismissed this one with prejudice. That is the factual center of the story. Everything else is the apparatus around a bad-faith operation that keeps trying to turn election administration into a federal fishing expedition.

A demand with no legitimate basis

Judge Philip Brimmer’s ruling matters because it exposes the emptiness of the Justice Department’s posture. The December demand letter to Colorado offered no real basis or purpose for the request, only the recycled language of a supposed follow-up. That is not law enforcement. It is bureaucratic intimidation dressed up as procedure.

The court also said what the DOJ wanted plainly collided with the Civil Rights Act of 1960. Colorado’s voter list is not some outsider record waiting to be collected by federal enforcers. It is a state-created list, maintained by the state because federal law requires states to keep updating their rolls. Blanche’s theory was so sloppy that, taken seriously, it would criminalize the very maintenance federal law demands.

The misdirection is the mechanism

This is the familiar authoritarian move: seize on elections, imply hidden fraud, demand sensitive data, and then act as if the refusal proves guilt. The target is not just Colorado. The target is the idea that states have any independent authority over their own election systems when a hostile White House wants to bully them.

That is why the framing matters. This was not a principled dispute over election integrity. It was an attempt to launder suspicion into power. The reporting makes clear that the department had no coherent legal basis, and that the courts keep treating the case accordingly. The confusion is not accidental. It is the strategy.

Republicans keep enabling the machine

The most revealing detail is political, not judicial. Blanche’s nomination advanced the same day the Colorado ruling emerged, after he had to rescind a proposed $1.8 billion “anti-weaponization fund” to win over two Republican holdouts. That fund, NPR reported, could have compensated Trump allies, including Jan. 6 rioters. So while Blanche loses in court, Senate Republicans still shepherd him toward permanent authority after extracting tactical concessions over money that would have rewarded political loyalists and insurrectionists.

That is not a party struggling with principle. It is a governing coalition learning how much corruption it can normalize before a few members balk at the optics.

Even Republican judges are rejecting the stunt

Twelve of the twenty rulings against the Justice Department came from judges appointed by Republican presidents. Eight were appointed by Trump himself. That should destroy any fantasy that this is a partisan mismatch between conservative judges and Democratic resistance. The legal theory is collapsing because it is indefensible, even in venues often friendly to Republican power.

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold called the attack what it is: an assault on elections and democracy. That is accurate. The state is defending its voter records from unlawful federal interference while Blanche’s team keeps acting as if repeated losses are just temporary inconvenience.

The broader pattern

This story is about how authoritarian politics works when it is forced to wear institutional clothes. It uses the Justice Department to pressure states, uses the Senate to launder unfit nominees, and uses bogus election paranoia to justify data collection that should never have been demanded in the first place. The point is not legal success. The point is to normalize federal aggression, keep the base fed on grievance, and train the public to accept official harassment as ordinary governance.

The lesson is simple: when power is deployed to invent a problem, the court losses are not a setback to the project. They are evidence of how far the project has already gone.
