Tradwife aesthetic collapses as Trumpism curdles into 'unmitigated disaster': columnist

When the Costume Starts Sweating

The Label Was the Product

Nara Smith, the model-influencer who built a massive following by performing a pristine, hyper-domestic fantasy, now wants to distance herself from the “tradwife” label. Jessica Grose reads that retreat as evidence that the retro-MAGA aesthetic is losing heat. That’s the surface story. The deeper story is that the label was never the point. It was the monetizable wrapper around a reactionary fantasy, and once the political weather changed, the branding got embarrassed.

Power Was Never in the Kitchen

The real power here did not sit with the influencer staging gum and Skittles in an immaculate kitchen. It sat with the platforms that rewarded the performance, the audience that amplified it, and the political movement that made regressive gender scripting feel culturally profitable. Smith could court conservative attention, joke about rage-baiting, and later claim egalitarian normalcy because the system around her made contradiction lucrative. That is not confusion. That is the business model.

The Pivot Is Not Innocence

Her disavowal of the tradwife tag is best understood as audience management, not moral clarity. When conservative femininity was cashing clicks, the pose was convenient. When the second Trump administration turned its own gender mythology into an obvious liability, the costume became harder to defend. People like Smith do not merely reflect culture; they surf it, sell it, and step away from it when it starts smelling like defeat.

MAGA Femininity Curdled

The source gets one thing right: the broader aesthetic has gone stale. Young people are souring on the right, right-wing influencers are losing audience, and even the manosphere’s old mascots no longer carry the same menace or glamour. That matters because authoritarian politics depends on style as much as policy. It needs its own romantic vocabulary, its own sexual politics, its own domestic theater. Once that theater starts reading as desperate, the spell breaks a little. Not because the movement has become less harmful, but because it has become more legible.

Cringe Is Not Accountability

There is a temptation to treat this as a culture-war trend report, as if the only thing that happened is that the tradwife aesthetic stopped being cool. That is too small. The underlying damage was never just aesthetic. It was the normalization of narrow, cartoonish gender hierarchy as a public ideal. The fact that it now looks tacky does not erase the years it spent laundering reactionary politics through polished, feminine content.

The Real Lesson

This story is about how authoritarian politics metastasizes through influencer culture: first as aspiration, then as monetized performance, then as a liability people pretend they never endorsed. The pattern is familiar. Reactionary movements rarely present themselves as raw power at first; they arrive as lifestyle, irony, or personal choice. By the time the audience notices the coercion inside the performance, the grift has already paid out.
