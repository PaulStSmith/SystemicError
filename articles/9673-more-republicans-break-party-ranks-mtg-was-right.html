More Republicans break party ranks: 'MTG was right'

WASHINGTON — In the unusual world of Congress during a shutdown, far-right firebrand Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) has emerged as an unlikely ally of Democrats, seeking to save millions of Americans from spiraling health care costs. And she’s not the only Republican making such an admission.

The government shutdown comes as Republicans call for a “clean” continuing resolution that funds the government as it stands. However, at the end of 2025, the subsidies for the Affordable Care Act, or ACA, will expire.

Democrats want a deal to continue the subsidies, but according to Punchbowl News founder Jake Sherman, “Republicans do not want to extend these Obamacare premium tax credits at all, period.”

Democrats may agree with Greene that subsidies help fund costly healthcare premiums, but they aren’t welcoming her with open arms quite yet, with Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) making a dig at her online, writing, “Even a broken clock is right twice a day.”

“Nothing she does surprises me,” Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL) told Raw Story on Thursday. “For a change, she’s using common sense.”

Some Republicans, too, agree with Greene and the Democrats on extending the subsidies.

When speaking to Senate Republicans who remain on Capitol Hill during the shutdown, Raw Story found more strange bedfellows generated by increasing voter support for tax breaks on healthcare costs.

Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) said that the Affordable Care Act is “important to a lot of us, not just to Democrats.” She agreed that the subsidies should be extended, though she would like to see some reform. She didn’t specify what.

“But the sooner we can get an appropriations bill through, the better off we’re going to be,” she said. “There are many discussions going on, and I have been in very close contact with Sen. Jeanne Shaheen (D-NH), who is very constructive and is trying to find a path forward.

Collins refused to answer about being in a coalition with Greene.

Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) told Raw Story that he became the second Republican speaker of the North Carolina state House since the Civil War because he “was convinced” that former President Barack Obama “was going to make a bad healthcare decision.”

Now, he appears to have evolved.

“We will be making a bad healthcare decision if we don’t help — all we’re really trying to do is reduce the waste and abuse,” he told Raw Story, noting that it should be a tax cut that nixes high-income wage earners. “I do think there should be skin in the game for people that have means.”

Ultimately, he confessed that “MTG is right” and noted that only “a handful of members” want to see the subsidies expire. That isn’t what reporters are hearing on the House side, however.

One of those who opposes the subsidies is Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI), who told Raw Story that “Obamacare totally failed” and Republicans want to “fix it,” but “the way to fix it is not to throw more money at it.” He went on to call the subsidies “a massive fraud” that “won’t fix the problem.”

The Kaiser Family Foundation reported that in 2025, “18.7 million (77%) of the total 24.3 million ACA Marketplace enrollees live in states President Trump won in the 2024 election.”

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Political Alignments and Healthcare Debates

In the latest twist from the circus that is Congress during a government shutdown, we find ourselves in a narrative so bizarre it could only belong to the present era. The far-right, often controversial Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) has, in what can only be described as a plot twist, positioned herself alongside Democrats in the quest to save millions from the gaping maw of spiraling healthcare costs. This isn’t just a simple case of strange bedfellows; it’s a stark illustration of the unpredictable and often hypocritical theater of American politics.

The Unlikely Crusader

Marjorie Taylor Greene, a name synonymous with far-right rhetoric and controversy, suddenly champions an issue close to the heart of Democratic policy: the extension of subsidies for the Affordable Care Act (ACA), also known disdainfully by many in her party as “Obamacare.” The irony is as rich as the healthcare executives profiting from America’s labyrinthine healthcare system. Greene, along with a select few Republicans, has acknowledged the importance of these subsidies in mitigating healthcare costs. Yet, the Democratic response, as exemplified by Sen. Cory Booker’s (D-NJ) remark, “Even a broken clock is right twice a day,” underscores the deep-seated skepticism towards Greene’s pivot. Sen. Dick Durbin’s (D-IL) backhanded compliment about Greene using “common sense” for a change further highlights the incredulity of her allies.

The Republican Rift

The article paints a picture of a Republican Party at odds with itself over healthcare—a subject that has long been a battlefield of American politics. On one side, there are Republicans like Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME) and Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), who express a desire to extend ACA subsidies, albeit with calls for unspecified reforms or reductions in “waste and abuse.” Their rhetoric suggests a recognition of the ACA’s importance to their constituents, a far cry from the party’s previous attempts to dismantle it entirely.

On the other end of the spectrum, we have figures like Sen. Ron Johnson (R-WI), who remains steadfast in his opposition, labeling the ACA a “massive fraud” and dismissing the idea of further subsidies. This internal discord is a microcosm of the larger ideological battles within the party, reflecting a division between pragmatic governance and ideological purity tests.

A Healthcare System in Disarray

The article touches on a critical point: the looming expiration of ACA subsidies by the end of 2025 and the potential impact on millions of Americans. With the Kaiser Family Foundation reporting that a significant majority of ACA Marketplace enrollees live in states won by Trump in the 2024 election, the political implications are clear. The Republican opposition to extending these subsidies not only threatens the health and financial stability of millions but also risks alienating a substantial portion of their own base.

Conclusion: The Theater of the Absurd

What we witness here is more than just political maneuvering; it’s a glaring spotlight on the absurdities and contradictions of American healthcare politics. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s sudden advocacy for ACA subsidies, while surprising, serves as a reminder of the unpredictable and often contradictory nature of political alignments. The reluctance of Democrats to fully embrace Greene’s support speaks volumes about the deep divisions and the mistrust that permeates our political system.

In the end, the debate over ACA subsidies is a stark reminder of the stakes involved in healthcare policy—a matter of life, death, and financial ruin for millions. As politicians jockey for position and leverage, the American public waits anxiously in the wings, hoping for a resolution that seems increasingly mired in political theater rather than grounded in a genuine concern for their well-being. The saga of the ACA subsidies is yet another act in the ongoing drama of American politics, where ideology often trumps common sense and the health of the nation hangs in the balance.
