'That's not the point!' Ex-Trump official hammered for saying Kimmel can just do a podcast

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Thin Veil of Free Speech: Unmasking Power Dynamics

Mike Dubke and Meghan Hays/CNN

A former White House official shut down a conservative's attempt to defend President Donald Trump forcing ABC to drop late night talk show host Jimmy Kimmel because he didn't like his criticism.

ABC announced it would pre-empt the comedian's program indefinitely after Federal Communications Commission Chairman Brendan Carr suggested the agency would revoke licenses for stations owned by parent company Disney if it didn't punish Kimmel for his coverage of Charlie Kirk's assassination.

"This is not about free speech, it's about power," said Meghan Hays, a former communications staffer to President Joe Biden. "Whether or not the Disney could have fired Jimmy Kimmel, absolutely everyone can, you know, for what he's wearing. Absolutely. That's not a free speech issue. But what we are silencing people for critiquing the president, and they are using the power of these [corporate] mergers to be able to do that, and that is what is not right here, and I think what's interesting is why there aren't more people speaking up about the government using the power to silence media. We are running dangerously close to state-run media."

" CNN This Morning" host Audie Cornish pointed out that the media landscape was much wider than it once was, and former Trump communications director Mike Dubke agreed, suggesting that Kimmel might start a podcast instead.

"Can I can I defend you for one second?" Dubke said to Cornish. "What I'm trying to say and what I took your question to mean is, there are so many other avenues for entertainers, for news to go out into the public. Now, with the internet and all these other places, the media doesn't exist as it did before."

"But that's not the point," Hays interjected.

"When there were three stations and they did control the narrative," Dubke continued. "Now the narrative is is there's 1,500 different avenues one can take, and, you know, Jimmy Kimmel can find find another way. He can. I bet you he's going to podcast."

"This isn't about Jimmy Kimmel," Hays rebutted. "This is about the government using their power in the merger here to control, to control organizations and control corporations."

Dubke argued that TV networks made business decisions by pulling talk show hosts who Trump has been targeting on social media for months.

"There were 40 million reasons for [Stephen] Colbert to go off the air," Dubke said. "There are, there were Democratic administrations, along with Republican administrations, going back decades that have done this, as I said, in private, and now it's out in the open and we're going, 'Oh, my.'"

Our Analysis:

The Thin Veil of Free Speech: Unmasking Power Dynamics

In an egregious display of power over principle, the saga of President Donald Trump forcing ABC to drop Jimmy Kimmel for his critique not only unveils the fragility of free speech in the face of authority but also highlights a dangerous precedent for media censorship. Let's dissect the layers of hypocrisy and control veiled as concern for public discourse.

Meghan Hays, a voice of reason amidst a cacophony of power plays, hits the nail squarely on the head. "This is not about free speech, it's about power," she asserts, cutting through the thinly veiled attempts at justifying the indefensible. The scenario she describes is not one of a company making a business decision but a clear and present danger of government overreach, where criticism of the president becomes grounds for punitive action against media outlets.

The Illusion of Choice in Media Diversity

The argument presented by Mike Dubke, suggesting that the modern media landscape's diversity somehow compensates for the loss of a platform like Kimmel's, is a red herring of the highest order. Yes, the internet has democratized content creation to an extent, but to suggest that this mitigates the government's coercive silencing of a critical voice is to completely miss the forest for the trees. The issue at hand is not about whether Kimmel can find an alternative platform but about the chilling effect government intimidation has on free expression.

The False Equivalence of Business Decisions

Dubke's attempt to rationalize the suppression of critical media voices as mere "business decisions" is a grotesque oversimplification. When these decisions are influenced—or outright dictated—by threats of regulatory retaliation from the government, we're not in the realm of business. We're knee-deep in the murky waters of censorship. The comparison to past administrations' private actions does little to comfort; rather, it underscores a systemic problem of manipulation and control across the political spectrum.

A Dangerous Precedent for State-Run Media

What we're witnessing is a slide towards state-run media, a scenario where the government exerts direct or indirect control over what can be said, criticized, or broadcasted. Hays' concern about the lack of uproar over this trend is as alarming as it is valid. The silence from many quarters speaks volumes about the normalization of such power dynamics, where the sanctity of free speech is trampled under the guise of regulatory compliance or business strategy.

Conclusion: A Call to Arms

The situation is not just about Jimmy Kimmel, a late-night talk show, or even the specific critiques that led to this juncture. It's a glaring symptom of a much deeper malaise—a systemic error where power, unchecked and unbalanced, threatens the foundational principles of free expression and democracy. The defense mounted by Meghan Hays against the conservative narrative is not merely commendable; it's a clarion call for vigilance and resistance against the encroaching shadows of authoritarian control over the media. Let this be a wake-up call: The battle for free speech is far from over, and it's one we cannot afford to lose.
