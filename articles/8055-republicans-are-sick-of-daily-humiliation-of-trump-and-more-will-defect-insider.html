Republicans are sick of 'daily humiliation' of Trump — and more will defect: insider

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20255 min read

Republican Party insiders have a precise date in mind for when more party members, sick of “daily humiliation,” will finally break with President Donald Trump, according to Politico’s politics bureau chief Jonathan Martin.

Martin made an appearance on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe” on Thursday, where he was pressed about the recent high-profile instances of Republicans in Washington abandoning the party or breaking with Trump on key issues. The well-connected bureau chief claimed to have spoken with a GOP senator who had a very specific time in mind for when many more Republicans might break ranks with the president, and it all has to do with next year’s all-important midterm elections.

“Quite frankly, I talked to a former GOP senator,” Martin said. “[They] said two words to me: filing deadlines.”

“Why do filing deadlines matter?” he continued. “Because what the senator was talking about was the filing deadlines for primaries next year. Which is to say, when that clears, when that passes, when these lawmakers know who is or is not running against them in primaries next year, then you’ll see even more freedom, even more independence.”

Trump’s most common response to members of his own party defying his will has been to suggest that somebody run against them in their next primary race, and offer to endorse someone who does so. Despite Trump’s waning approval rating overall, his endorsements are still seen as extremely valuable in Republican primary races for things like the House and Senate. Before Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene announced her early resignation from the House, Trump had branded her a “traitor” for breaking with his stances and suggested somebody primary her.

Once the filing deadlines have passed, members of Congress will be able to gauge how much of a threat their challengers are and use that information to determine how much they can defy Trump. There would also not then be the possibility that Trump could back a new candidate against them if there is not already a serious challenger, at least not for 2026.

“These guys care about their seats and about their reelections,” Martin explained. “If they see that they don’t have a primary challenger by a date certain next year, 2026, they can start saying what they actually think about what [conservative commentator] George Will calls the ‘moral slum’ of this administration.”

Primary filing deadlines vary by state. In some places, like Arkansas and Illinois, the deadlines already passed last month. In others, like Delaware and Louisiana, the deadlines are as far off as July.

Martin also touched on the frustrations Republicans in Washington are feeling about the laundry list of scandals coming out of the Trump administration, which many feel obligated to defend or downplay for the time being, the most notable currently being Pete Hegseth’s boat strike debacle.

“I think it’s one more rock on the back of the members of Congress that they’re carrying up the hill,” Martin said. “The hill is Mount Trump, and the hill is having to burden this daily humiliation.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of a Breakpoint: GOP’s Filing Deadlines Fantasy

In a recent spectacle of political theatre, Politico’s politics bureau chief Jonathan Martin, during an appearance on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” unveiled what seems to be the latest episode in the ongoing saga of the Republican Party’s turbulent relationship with President Donald Trump. Citing a conversation with a former GOP senator, Martin pinpointed the magic moment when Republican Party insiders believe members will finally muster the courage to defy Trump: the passing of primary filing deadlines for the midterm elections. This narrative, while tantalizing for those yearning for a GOP revolt against Trump’s iron grip, is a masterclass in the art of political evasion and self-preservation.

The Myth of Post-Deadline Valor

According to Martin, the logic goes something like this: once the filing deadlines for primaries pass, GOP members, assured that no Trump-endorsed challenger can emerge from the shadows, will suddenly find their voices—and their spines—to criticize the president openly. This theory rests on the assumption that the primary threat of being “primaried” out of a job is the sole chain binding these politicians’ tongues. However, this overlooks the deeper, more insidious loyalty to Trumpism that pervades the party, not merely as a strategy for reelection but as an ideological cornerstone.

Trump’s Endorsement: A Golden Ticket?

It’s no secret that Trump’s endorsement is considered a golden ticket in Republican primaries, a fact that has kept many a GOP lawmaker in line. Yet, the idea that the absence of a primary challenger will liberate these individuals to speak out against the “moral slum” of the Trump administration, as conservative commentator George Will puts it, is optimistic at best and naïve at worst. Trump’s influence extends beyond the primaries; it permeates the party’s base, shaping the very fabric of its identity. The fear of losing his endorsement, or worse, earning his ire, doesn’t vanish with the passing of a filing deadline.

The Realpolitik of Self-Preservation

What Martin’s revelation truly underscores is the extent to which political survival trumps (pun intended) principle within the GOP ranks. The notion that these lawmakers are merely biding their time, waiting for a safe moment to express their true feelings, is indicative of a broader moral bankruptcy. It’s not just about avoiding a primary challenge; it’s about the fear of alienating a base that remains loyal to Trump. The real issue at hand is not when GOP members will break with Trump, but whether they have the ethical backbone to do so at all.

The Daily Humiliation

The so-called “daily humiliation” that GOP members endure, as they navigate the scandal-ridden terrain of the Trump administration, is a self-inflicted wound. Their unwillingness to condemn actions that they would have found indefensible under any other administration speaks volumes about their complicity. The latest in this parade of indignities, Pete Hegseth’s boat strike debacle, is but one more testament to the moral compromises the party continues to make.

Conclusion: A Mirage of Rebellion

In essence, the idea that a significant number of Republicans will defy Trump after the primary filing deadlines is a mirage. It’s a convenient narrative that allows GOP members to feign a future courage they lack in the present. The real test of their resolve won’t come when the threat of a primary challenge disappears; it comes every day they choose party over principle, silence over condemnation. The GOP’s purported day of reckoning with Trump is not tied to a calendar event; it’s a matter of moral choice—a choice many have already made.
