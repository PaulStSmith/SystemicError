Wall Street dreads economic catastrophe as Trump guardrails erode: 'What is he doing?'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 20, 20265 min read

Donald Trump looks on at the White House in Washington, D.C. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

Wall Street is sounding the alarm as Trump’s Iran war threatens to crater an already fragile economy, with financial analysts warning the protective guardrails shielding the U.S. from economic catastrophe are rapidly eroding.

Just three weeks into the conflict, the damage is already mounting. Oil prices have exploded past $100 a barrel with no relief in sight, inflation is climbing, hiring has stalled, wage growth is collapsing, and mortgage rates are surging as market anxiety deepens. The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady Wednesday, but the underlying economic picture is darkening by the day, Politico reported.

“The guardrails that protected the U.S. economy from President Donald Trump’s policy jolts are wearing thin,” according to the report.

Gregory Daco, EY-Parthenon’s chief economist, warned of systemic vulnerability. “The U.S. is now confronting inherent fragilities,” he said. “The typical buffers that would prevent any type of external shock — like an oil price shock — from disproportionately affecting the economy are smaller than usual.”

“Downside risks are rising, and this is an extremely fluid situation,” Daco added.

The financial sector is rapidly losing confidence in the administration’s economic stewardship, Politico reported. A Bank of America survey of global fund managers released Tuesday found inflation expectations surging, with 28 percent now expecting Democrats to retake both houses of Congress in the midterms — up from just 20 percent a month ago.

Bob Elliott, CEO and CIO of investment firm Unlimited Funds, expressed the shift in sentiment bluntly. “Until this war happened, everyone thought we were going to have a pretty good growth year,” he told Poltico. “Now it’s pretty clear that growth is going to be soft.”

Even Republican insiders are panicking. “The thing that underlines every strong economy is consistency and progress, and things that promote confidence, and I just don’t see any of those attributes being displayed on a disciplined, routine basis by the White House,” said Chuck Coughlin, a veteran Republican strategist in Arizona. “Most of the country is looking at the president, going: ‘What is he doing?’”

Goldman Sachs has now pegged the odds of a U.S. recession within the next year at 25 percent. Other major banks are warning that inflation and growth risks look far more acute than they did just weeks ago, before oil prices started soaring.

The longer Iran keeps the Strait of Hormuz disrupted, the worse the economic fallout becomes. Release of global oil reserves, sanctions relief, and political risk insurance for tankers cannot fully offset the cascading damage to global supply chains and GDP.

Andrew Hollenhorst, chief U.S. economist at Citi, summed up the deteriorating picture before Wednesday’s Fed meeting: “Things look a little bit weaker than before. Once an oil shock is added to the equation, it’s a really unpleasant combination of data and events.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Economic Turbulence

The recent Politico article paints a dire picture of an economy teetering on the brink, ostensibly due to President Trump’s decision to engage in a conflict with Iran. This narrative, however, demands a closer examination to separate the wheat from the chaff – to discern the actual drivers of economic instability from convenient political narratives.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

First and foremost, it’s critical to identify who holds the reins of institutional power in this scenario. The President of the United States, as commander-in-chief, certainly wields significant influence over foreign policy and military actions. The decision to engage militarily with Iran falls squarely on President Trump’s shoulders. This action has undeniable economic implications, particularly in the realms of oil prices and global market stability.

However, the framing that attributes the entirety of the economic fallout to this single decision overlooks the complexity of economic systems and the multitude of actors who play pivotal roles. It’s essential to consider the roles of other institutions and actors, including the Federal Reserve, global oil markets, and international geopolitical dynamics.

Economic Outcomes and Responsibility

The direct economic outcomes mentioned – such as surging oil prices, inflation, stalling hiring, and collapsing wage growth – are indeed tied to the conflict. Yet, to place the entire burden of a fragile economy on this incident alone is a misdirection. The U.S. economy’s fragility, as noted by EY-Parthenon’s chief economist Gregory Daco, points to systemic vulnerabilities and a lack of robust buffers against external shocks.

The Federal Reserve’s decision to hold interest rates steady, for instance, is a critical piece of this puzzle. While the conflict contributes to market anxiety, the Fed’s policies and responses play a crucial role in shaping economic outcomes. This illustrates the shared responsibility among various decision-makers, not just the executive branch.

Misdirected Responsibility and Scapegoating

The article’s focus on Trump’s actions, while not entirely misplaced, risks simplifying a complex situation into a convenient scapegoat narrative. Economic stewardship is a shared responsibility, encompassing a wide range of domestic and international policy decisions. The financial sector’s loss of confidence, as reported, reflects broader uncertainties and is not solely the aftermath of the Iran conflict.

Republican strategist Chuck Coughlin’s remarks on the lack of consistency and progress further underscore the importance of measured and disciplined policy-making, beyond military decisions. The sentiment shift among financial analysts and the increased recession odds from Goldman Sachs highlight a broader context of economic anxiety that predates the current administration.

Conclusion

While President Trump’s decision to engage with Iran undoubtedly plays a significant role in the current economic turbulence, attributing the entirety of the economic woes to this conflict alone is an oversimplification. The narrative overlooks the multifaceted nature of economic systems and the shared responsibility among various institutional actors.

A nuanced analysis reveals a confluence of factors contributing to the fragile economic state, including systemic vulnerabilities, policy decisions across the board, and external geopolitical tensions. To navigate these turbulent waters, a broad and informed perspective is essential, one that recognizes the complexity of global economics beyond the confines of partisan politics.
