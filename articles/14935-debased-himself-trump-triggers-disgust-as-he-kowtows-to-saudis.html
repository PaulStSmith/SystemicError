'Debased himself': Trump triggers disgust as he kowtows to Saudis

U.S. President Donald Trump and Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed Bin Salman walk as he arrives at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, May 13, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump saluted each Saudi Arabian general who greeted him during his visit to Riyadh Tuesday, causing critics to melt down over the spectacle.

Standing next to Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Trump was seen saluting and shaking hands as the long line of generals waited to greet the American president.

Journalist and former UN diplomat Olga Nesterova pointed out, "Trump goes ahead and salutes every single Saudi official at the Royal Court in Riyadh."

The X account @JoeyFotoFr displayed the following post: "As an American citizen, my president salutes no one in the world. If I'd accept an exception it would be a salute to the flag, covering the body of a fallen American soldier. Saluting these guys is national humiliation by the traitor Trump, a pig POTUS."

Republicans Against Trump wrote, "Trump salutes a Saudi general. Disgusting, disgraceful. So much for 'America First," and asked, "Why does Trump keep saluting generals from autocratic regimes?"

@JoJoFromJerz, with nearly a million followers on X, wrote, "Hey MAGA — should I 'cry harder' because the sitting so-called President debased himself and our entire country for all the world to see…"

The account of Artie Vandelay, with 10,000 followers, referenced the murder of Saudi dissident and Washington Post columnist Jamal Ahmad Khashoggi, who was killed in 2018 in an operation that U.S. intelligence determined was approved by the Saudi Crown Prince.

"While standing next to the guy who had a resident American journalist beheaded and dismembered for writing things he didn’t like. All seems on point," the account posted.

Travis Matthew, with 61,000 followers wrote, "No other American President has ever saluted a foreign general. Until the draft dodger entered office."

@sensiblemiddle echoed that sentiment, writing, "5X draft dodger Trump mocks our own soldiers calling them 'suckers' and 'losers' but honors Saudi soldiers with a full salute.

According to Newsweek, "There has been a discussion in recent years about the proper etiquette for presidents saluting the military, particularly those from other nations. A returned salute by Trump to a North Korean general during his first term sparked criticism, with some saying he should not have shown respect to a hostile nation. Others said it was courteous to return the gesture."

Our Analysis:

The Salute Heard Round the World: Trump's Riyadh Greetings

In a spectacle that has sent ripples of controversy across the political spectrum, President Donald Trump's recent actions in Riyadh have ignited a firestorm of criticism. During his visit, Trump was seen offering a military salute to each Saudi Arabian general that greeted him, a move that has sparked a fierce debate over protocol, respect, and the implications of such gestures by a sitting U.S. President.

The Incident

Standing alongside Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Trump's series of salutes to a long line of generals has been met with a mixture of shock and indignation. Critics, such as journalist and former UN diplomat Olga Nesterova, have pointed out the seemingly unorthodox nature of Trump's actions, suggesting that they represent a break from traditional diplomatic etiquette. The response on social media platforms like X (formerly Twitter) further amplifies this sentiment, with users expressing everything from disdain to outright anger.

The Criticism

A particularly scathing critique came from the X account @JoeyFotoFr, which lambasted Trump for what they perceive as a national humiliation. The notion that a U.S. President should not salute foreign military personnel, especially those from countries with questionable human rights records, underpins much of the criticism. This sentiment was echoed by the group Republicans Against Trump, who labeled the salute as "disgusting" and "disgraceful," questioning Trump's allegiance to his 'America First' philosophy.

Moreover, the reference to the murder of Jamal Khashoggi by an account underscores a crucial point of contention. It highlights a perceived inconsistency in Trump's actions – saluting the military leaders of a nation implicated in the brutal murder of a resident American journalist.

The Context

It's essential to note that the debate over a U.S. President saluting foreign military personnel is not new. Trump's previous salute to a North Korean general had already sparked discussions about the appropriateness of such gestures. These actions raise questions about the balance between diplomatic courtesy and the symbolic weight of a salute, especially coming from the Commander-in-Chief of the American armed forces.

Analysis

The controversy surrounding Trump's salutes in Riyadh is not merely about the act of saluting; it's about what that gesture represents in the broader context of U.S.-Saudi relations, American foreign policy, and the values the United States purports to stand for. At the heart of the issue is a fundamental question: What message does it send when a U.S. President offers military honors to the leaders of a regime with a contentious human rights record?

Critics argue that such actions undermine the principles of human rights and democracy that the U.S. ostensibly champions, sending mixed signals to both allies and adversaries on the global stage. Furthermore, it raises concerns about the implications for America's moral standing and leadership.

In stark contrast to the reverence shown to Saudi generals, Trump's reported derogatory comments about American soldiers being "suckers" and "losers" add a layer of irony and hypocrisy to the situation. These actions, juxtaposed with his controversial remarks, paint a picture of a President whose actions and words are at odds with each other, and more importantly, with the values and traditions of the office he holds.

Conclusion

The incident in Riyadh is not just a fleeting moment of diplomatic protocol; it's a reflection of the complex interplay between politics, power, and principle. As the debate rages on, one thing is clear: the implications of Trump's salutes extend far beyond the confines of traditional etiquette, touching on deeper questions about respect, allegiance, and the values that define American leadership on the world stage.
