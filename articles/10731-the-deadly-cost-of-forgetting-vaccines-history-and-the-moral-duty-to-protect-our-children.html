The Deadly Cost of Forgetting: Vaccines, History, and the Moral Duty to Protect Our Children

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 8, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The human story is littered with plagues. For most of history, parents buried children not because of accidents or old age, but because of diseases we now consider preventable. Smallpox, polio, rubella, measles, diphtheria—names that once meant terror now read like footnotes in a history book. That miracle did not happen by chance. It happened because humanity, for once, chose collective responsibility over reckless individualism. It happened because of vaccines.

And yet, in 2025, in an era where scientific knowledge and medical technology are more advanced than ever, we are witnessing an obscene regression: diseases once eradicated are clawing their way back. Not because the science failed. Not because the medicine stopped working. But because people chose arrogance over evidence, conspiracy over history, and neglect over responsibility.

A Brief Reminder From History

The concept of vaccination is not modern. In the late 18th century, Edward Jenner pioneered the smallpox vaccine by using cowpox to induce immunity. But even before that, in the American Revolution, George Washington, the commander of the Continental Army, ordered variolation—an early, cruder form of inoculation using live smallpox virus—for his soldiers. It was a dangerous procedure compared to today’s standards, but it saved lives. He understood a fundamental truth: unchecked disease would kill more soldiers than musket balls ever could.

That lesson was not lost on the generations that followed. The 20th century brought mass immunization campaigns that slashed childhood mortality, extended life expectancy, and gave parents the revolutionary privilege of watching children grow up without the constant specter of infectious disease. In 1980, smallpox—the most brutal killer in human history—was officially eradicated. Polio, once a scourge that left children in iron lungs, was nearly stamped out worldwide thanks to widespread vaccination. Each victory was won not by chance, but by the coordinated decision of societies to vaccinate.

The Modern Betrayal

What makes the current wave of vaccine skepticism so grotesque is not only its disregard for evidence, but its betrayal of the very progress our ancestors fought and died for. We know vaccines work. We know they are overwhelmingly safe. We know that refusing them opens the door to outbreaks. These are not debates; they are settled facts.

And yet, we now see parents choosing to “roll the dice” with their children’s lives. They tell themselves that the odds of severe illness are small, that their children “probably won’t get sick,” or that they’ll be safe because “other people vaccinate.” It is magical thinking at its most destructive.

This is not skepticism. It is negligence. Worse—it is abuse. Because the cost of that negligence is not borne by the parent’s ego; it is borne by the child’s body.

The Real Math

The argument often tossed out is, “The chance of getting seriously ill is small.” Yes, smaller than the certainty of suffering in the pre-vaccine era. But here’s the math: before the chickenpox vaccine, more than 10,000 children in the U.S. were hospitalized annually, and around 100–150 died each year. Since the vaccine’s introduction, those numbers have collapsed. Similar stories repeat across measles, rubella, and polio.

Vaccines don’t just reduce risk—they transform it. They turn a world where child funerals were common into a world where such tragedies are vanishingly rare. To sneer at that transformation because “nothing is 100% safe” is to misunderstand basic risk management. Everything in medicine carries risk. But the risk of vaccination is infinitesimal compared to the risk of disease. The scale is not even close.

The Community Responsibility

Here is the other brutal truth: vaccination is not just about your child. It’s about everyone. Immunization protects newborns who are too young for vaccines, cancer patients whose immune systems are suppressed, and the elderly whose defenses are weakened. It creates a wall of protection—herd immunity—that shields the vulnerable.

When a parent refuses vaccination, they are not simply making a “personal choice.” They are lowering the walls and inviting disease back in. They are gambling with the lives of other people’s children, the sick, the fragile, the immunocompromised. That is not autonomy. That is reckless endangerment.

The False Heroism of Refusal

Anti-vaccine rhetoric often cloaks itself in the language of courage: “I’m not afraid. I’m questioning the system. I’m thinking for myself.” But refusing vaccines is not bravery. It is cowardice masquerading as defiance. It is ignorance sold as independence. True courage is protecting your child, even if it means enduring a needle prick, even if it means acknowledging that generations of scientists and doctors know more than your gut instinct.

Refusal is not a stand for freedom. It is a surrender to disease.

A Plea to Parents

Parents: you love your children. You would throw yourself in front of a car to save them. You would work two jobs to keep them fed. You would fight anyone who threatened them. Why, then, would you hand them back to the claws of diseases we already defeated? Why would you risk polio’s paralysis, rubella’s congenital defects, measles’ brain damage, HPV’s cancers—all for the hollow satisfaction of “going against the grain”?

Vaccines are not just science—they are acts of love, distilled into medicine. To vaccinate is to declare: “My child’s life matters, and so does the life of every child they will ever meet.” To withhold that protection is not just negligence—it is abuse by omission.

The Cost of Forgetting

The gravest danger we face today is not the virus itself but the rot of amnesia. We forget the rows of iron lungs. We forget the cemeteries filled with small coffins. We forget the terror parents once felt at the word “outbreak.” Forgetting leads to complacency, and complacency leads to the return of plagues. Already, we see outbreaks of measles and polio in communities where vaccination rates have plummeted. These are not random accidents. They are predictable consequences of choices made by adults who valued ideology over evidence.

If history teaches anything, it is that disease does not forgive arrogance. The virus does not care about your suspicions. The bacterium does not care about your feelings. They only care about survival, and they will exploit every crack we leave in our defenses.

Conclusion: The Line We Must Not Cross

Vaccination is not optional. It is not a lifestyle choice. It is the bedrock of modern public health. To turn away from it is to betray centuries of progress and condemn children—our own and others’—to needless suffering.

We are at a crossroads: one path leads forward, to a future where we continue to reduce disease, extend life, and protect the vulnerable. The other path leads backward, to iron lungs, mass graves, and the unbearable grief of parents who could have prevented it but didn’t.

There is no middle ground. Not vaccinating your child is not skepticism, not independence, not freedom. It is child abuse. And the price will be measured not in arguments on social media but in the lives of children who deserved better.
