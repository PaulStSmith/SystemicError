'We went and reconfigured the numbers': Leavitt admits manipulating DC crime stats

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Manipulation: The "Reconfiguration" of Crime Statistics

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt holds a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 19, 2025. REUTERS/Al Drago

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt admitted Tuesday that crime statistics in Washington, D.C. had recently been "reconfigured" to support President Donald Trump's claims.

During a White House briefing, a reporter asked Leavitt to clarify what Trump meant when he vowed to rid Washington of "slums."

"First, when it comes to what you said, and the president said, the slums of Washington, D.C., he's referring to the most dangerous communities, neighborhoods, and streets in the city where unfortunately violence has ravaged these communities and taken the lives of, again, far too many law-abiding D.C. residents," the press secretary explained.

"There has been a surge, despite what many of you have been falsely reporting, and I believe it was The Washington Post who put out a map claiming it to be fact-based when it was just based on, I don't know, accounts that they've heard on the street, not actual statistics and data," she continued. "So we went and reconfigured the numbers, and as I said, half, nearly half of all the non-illegal alien-related arrests have occurred in wards seven and eight."

On Tuesday, The Washington Post reported that the Department of Justice had opened an investigation into Washington, D.C. police for allegedly manipulating crime data.

"D.C. gave Fake Crime numbers in order to create a false illusion of safety," Trump claimed in a post on Monday night.

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Manipulation: The "Reconfiguration" of Crime Statistics

In an audacious admission that seems ripped straight from the pages of a dystopian novel, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt has confirmed that the administration has been creatively interpreting crime statistics in Washington, D.C. This revelation wasn't stumbled upon through an investigative journalist's dogged pursuit but was instead blithely announced during a White House briefing. The aim? To prop up President Donald Trump's narrative and justify his controversial pledge to cleanse the nation's capital of its so-called "slums."

"Reconfigured" – let's pause on that term for a moment. In any other context, such an admission of manipulating data to fit a political agenda would be met with scandal. However, in the current climate, it's delivered with a casualness that is as chilling as it is telling.

Leavitt's defense of Trump's derogatory remarks about "slums" in D.C. was cloaked in a veneer of concern for the residents of the city's most dangerous areas. Yet, the underlying message was unmistakable: the administration believes that reshaping the data to fit a narrative is a perfectly acceptable strategy. The press secretary went as far as to accuse The Washington Post of reporting on "accounts they've heard on the street" rather than "actual statistics and data," only to then boast about the administration’s own data manipulation efforts. The irony would be delicious if it weren't so dire.

The claim that "nearly half of all the non-illegal alien-related arrests have occurred in wards seven and eight" is a manipulative statistic designed to stoke fear and justify heavy-handed policies. It's a classic case of using numbers out of context to paint a picture that aligns with the administration's goals, sidestepping the complexities and realities of crime and community challenges in those areas.

Furthermore, the timing of these revelations couldn't be more suspect. With the Department of Justice opening an investigation into the alleged manipulation of crime data by Washington, D.C. police, Trump's preemptive strike against "Fake Crime numbers" reeks of a calculated attempt to discredit any findings that may undermine his stance.

Let's be clear: this isn't just about crime statistics. This is about the weaponization of data to support a political narrative, irrespective of the truth. It's about the erosion of trust in public institutions and the very principles of transparency and accountability that underpin a functioning democracy.

As readers, citizens, and critical thinkers, we must ask ourselves: what are the implications of a government that resorts to "reconfiguring" facts to serve its purposes? How do we navigate a landscape where truth is no longer a shared baseline but a malleable commodity?

The answers to these questions will define not just the future of political discourse in the United States, but the very fabric of democracy itself. In the meantime, we remain vigilant, skeptical, and unyielding in our demand for truth. In an era of "alternative facts" and manipulated narratives, these principles are our only bulwark against the creeping tide of authoritarianism.
