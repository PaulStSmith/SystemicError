'Disgraceful': Ex-prosecutor blasts Trump's new Fox News pick as 'affront to the office'

CNN legal analyst Elliot Williams didn’t hold back Thursday night as he delivered a scathing assessment of President Donald Trump’s pick of Fox News host Jeanine Pirro as interim U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia.

Williams, himself a former federal prosecutor, slammed past comments Pirro made while amplifying false claims about the 2020 election on her Fox News show. He also reminded viewers that Pirro was cited in Dominion’s defamation lawsuit against the network.

“They were at best twisting the truth and, at worst, outright lies,” Williams told CNN’s Kaitlan Collins on Thursday. “This is the biggest U.S. Attorney’s Office in the country,” he said, which employs "hundreds of people."

He added: “How do you build faith among the people who work in the office when your record as a leader is disgraceful and, quite frankly, an affront to the office?"

ALSO READ: Trump is a troll – but his 'ugly' pope selfie demands a damning response

Pirro, a former county judge and assistant district attorney, held her last law enforcement job in 2005 and has spent the last two decades in right-wing media, Williams said. While she once held elected office and has prosecutorial credentials, the CNN analyst noted: “That’s it.”

“Since then, she’s demonstrated a willingness to step out for the president of the United States in a role that really ought to be independent of the White House,” he said. “So it’s sort of more of the same.”

But there was a bright side to Pirro’s elevation to the high-profile Department of Justice role.

“The pick before her was so terrible, she seems quite reasonable,” Williams concluded about the collapse of Trump’s failed nomination of Ed Martin.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of Pirro's Appointment: The Merging of Media and Justice

In a recent CNN segment that could only be described as a firestorm of criticism, legal analyst Elliot Williams tore into President Donald Trump's appointment of Fox News host Jeanine Pirro as the interim U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia. This decision is not merely controversial; it is a grotesque blurring of the lines between partisan media and the supposedly impartial judicial system.

Twisting Truths and Outright Lies

Williams, leveraging his experience as a former federal prosecutor, highlighted Pirro's problematic past, especially her role in amplifying baseless claims about the 2020 election on her Fox News show. Cited in Dominion’s defamation lawsuit against Fox News, Pirro's conduct, according to Williams, ranged from "twisting the truth" to "outright lies." This conduct is not only unbecoming of a media personality but utterly disqualifying for a role as critical as the U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia.

The Office's Integrity at Stake

Williams underscored the magnitude of the position, noting that it is the largest U.S. Attorney's Office in the country, staffed by hundreds. The question then arises: How can someone whose leadership has been marred by disgrace and is an affront to judicial standards possibly inspire faith among the people in this office?

From Prosecutorial Credentials to Partisan Commentary

Pirro, who last served in a law enforcement capacity in 2005, has since entrenched herself in the right-wing media landscape. Despite her previous roles as a county judge and assistant district attorney, her recent history paints a picture of someone far removed from the impartiality required of her new position. Williams aptly points out that her willingness to serve the president's interests over those of justice is a glaring red flag.

A Lesser of Two Evils?

In a damning conclusion, Williams suggests that Pirro's selection might only seem palatable when compared to Trump's prior failed nomination of Ed Martin. This comparison does not serve as an endorsement of Pirro’s suitability but rather highlights the dire state of affairs where such nominations are considered.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

The appointment of Jeanine Pirro as the interim U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia is a stark illustration of the systemic errors plaguing the current administration. It reflects a disturbing trend of prioritizing loyalty over integrity, partisan affiliation over professionalism, and personal gain over public service. This decision should not merely be critiqued; it demands a resolute and unequivocal condemnation.

In the face of such appointments, one must question the direction in which the justice system is headed and the long-term implications for the rule of law. Williams's scathing assessment serves not just as a critique of a single appointment but as a clarion call for vigilance and accountability in the face of political machinations that threaten the very foundations of justice.
