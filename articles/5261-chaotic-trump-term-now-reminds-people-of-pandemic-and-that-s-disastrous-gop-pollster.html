Chaotic Trump term now 'reminds people of pandemic' — and that's disastrous: GOP pollster

As President Donald Trump approaches his 400th day back in the White House next week, one Republican pollster is issuing a bleak assessment of his performance so far — one he argued had been “pretty chaotic” and runs a real risk of handing Democrats a decisive victory in the upcoming midterm elections.

“There’s a sense that this is a pretty chaotic administration,” said Whit Ayres, a GOP pollster and political consultant with 40 years of experience, speaking with Politico Monday.

“And it seems to remind people of the pandemic period in the first term. Joe Biden’s fundamental message in 2020 was to restore normalcy, and that seemed to be persuasive to enough people to get him elected.”

Indeed, many have described Trump’s second stint in the White House as “chaotic,” with its unpredictability only increasing in recent months.

For instance, the Trump administration has increasingly pivoted from its previous positions. Trump has backtracked on his bid to acquire Greenland, the administration announced plans to end its immigration enforcement operations in Minnesota, and Trump’s recent threat towards Canada over a bridge came and went with no follow-through.

That chaos, Ayres argued, had largely been responsible for bringing Trump’s approval ratings to “his lowest point in the second term.” As of Friday, a new AP-NORC poll found that Trump’s approval rating sits at 36%, a figure that Ayres warned could spell doom for the Republican Party this fall.

“Historically, when [a president is polling] above 50%, the party loses seats but not that many,” Ayres told Politico. “When the president’s job approval is below, the average loss of seats is 32.”

Trump’s performance among key voter blocs has also plummeted. According to a newYouGov/Economist poll, the president’s approval among voters aged 18 to 29 fell by a staggering51 pointssince he took office last year. He’s also lost substantial ground with other key voter blocs, includingnon-college-educated votersandindependents.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Chaotic Tenure: A Recipe for Republican Downfall?

As President Donald Trump marches toward his 400th day in his second term, the political atmosphere resembles less a structured administration and more a scene from a poorly directed political thriller. Whit Ayres, a seasoned GOP pollster, has sounded the alarm, and it’s not the jingle of victory bells. His assessment of Trump’s performance is nothing short of a harsh reality check for the Republican Party.

The Chaos Unpacked

Labeling Trump’s administration as “pretty chaotic” is like calling a hurricane a slight breeze. It’s an understatement. The erratic policy shifts—the comedic retreat from acquiring Greenland, the abrupt halt of immigration enforcement in Minnesota, and the blustering but toothless threats towards Canada over a bridge—are not just policy pivots; they’re symptomatic of a deeper malaise. This isn’t governance; it’s governance by chaos.

Trump’s approval rating, languishing at a dismal 36%, should be a wake-up call, but it seems more like a lullaby to which the GOP is blissfully snoring. Ayres’ analysis isn’t just a warning; it’s a glaring neon sign of impending doom. A president’s approval rating is not just a number—it’s a harbinger. And with Trump, it’s forecasting a bleak mid-term election for the Republicans.

The Voter Backlash

The plummeting approval ratings among key voter blocs are not just statistics. They are voices. Voices of the young, the educated, and the independent voters who are increasingly disenchanted with what they see. A staggering 51-point drop among voters aged 18 to 29 isn’t a dip; it’s a nosedive.

What does this tell us? That Trump’s second term is not just failing to recapture the magic of his first term; it’s actively alienating swathes of the electorate. The loss of support among non-college-educated voters and independents is particularly telling. These were the very groups that buoyed Trump to his first-term victory. Now, they’re drifting away, disillusioned and disappointed.

The Bigger Picture

Ayres’ bleak assessment and the dire numbers from AP-NORC and YouGov/Economist polls paint a picture that’s hard to ignore. The Republican Party, under Trump’s chaotic leadership, is steering toward an electoral iceberg. The upcoming mid-term elections are not just another political milestone; they’re a referendum on Trump’s governance (or the lack thereof).

This isn’t just about Trump or the GOP. It’s about the fabric of American democracy. A chaotic administration erodes trust, destabilizes the political landscape, and sows discord among its citizens. When the leader of the free world is at his “lowest point”, it’s not just a number. It’s a crisis.

In Conclusion

Trump’s presidency, particularly this second term, is a case study in how not to govern. The erratic policies, the dwindling support, the ominous poll numbers—all point to a systemic failure. The Republican Party needs to wake up and smell the chaos. This isn’t just a chaotic administration; it’s a clarion call for change. As the mid-term elections loom, one can only hope that this call does not go unheeded. For the sake of the party and the country, let’s hope someone is listening.
