Lawyer calls DOJ out on major Epstein contradiction that hasn’t ‘gotten enough attention’

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Department of Justice’s Staggering Incompetence: Unearthing a Million Documents

Politico Senior Writer and attorney Ankush Khardori appears on CNN, Dec. 27, 2025. (Screengrab / CNN)

Ankush Khardori, lawyer and senior writer for Politico, called out the Justice Department Saturday for a major contradiction regarding Jeffrey Epstein, and one that he said hadn’t “gotten enough attention.”

While the DOJ is now releasing its Epstein files in a phased rollout – which it’s compelled to by law – last summer, the agency attempted to shut down further investigations into the disgraced financier, claiming to have reviewed all related files, according to a DOJ memo that leaked in June.

And then, on Christmas Eve, the DOJ announced that it had uncovered “a million more documents” related to Epstein, and would therefore need more time to properly redact and review the files before releasing them.

Appearing on CNN Saturday, Khardori was asked how it was possible for the DOJ to “just find a million new documents.”

“This is an excellent question,” Khardori said. “It’s particularly a good question, I think, for a reason I don’t think has gotten enough attention, because the Trump Justice Department claimed that they reviewed all the documents before they issued a joint memo saying that there was nothing further to investigate, that they’ve run over the prior investigation.”

The new documents, the DOJ claimed, were delivered to the agency from the Southern District of New York, the same district where Epstein-associate Ghislaine Maxwell was prosecuted and convicted of sex trafficking. As to how the DOJ could overlook such a large trove of documents, Khardori said that only one reasonable explanation existed: “incompetence.”

“In terms of how they find a million documents, I’ve never seen this happen before, [and] I can tell you as someone who represented corporations once in response to Justice Department criminal investigations and had to facilitate the production of documents,” Khardori said.

“If a corporate defendant waited until the last day to respond to a subpoena, said that they were doing rolling productions, and then told the government a week later that they had a million documents they had missed, the Justice Department would go ballistic. This is a total professionally incompetent endeavor [that] reflects very poorly on the [Pam] Bondi and [Todd] Blanche Justice Department.”

Our Analysis:

The Department of Justice’s Staggering Incompetence: Unearthing a Million Documents

In what can only be described as a jaw-dropping display of bureaucratic bungling, the Justice Department has somehow managed to unearth “a million more documents” related to Jeffrey Epstein, months after confidently asserting they had reviewed all pertinent files. Ankush Khardori, a lawyer and senior writer for Politico, shone a spotlight on this astounding contradiction, a saga that reeks of either gross incompetence or a more sinister reluctance to dig deeper into the Epstein scandal.

Last summer’s premature closure of further investigations into Epstein by the DOJ, under the guise of having scoured all existing files, now appears laughably misguided, or worse, intentionally misleading. The leaked DOJ memo from June stands as a testament to either the department’s ineptitude or its potential complicity in burying uncomfortable truths.

Khardori’s appearance on CNN brought the issue into sharp relief. When asked how the DOJ could “just find a million new documents,” he rightly pointed out the absurdity of the situation. The notion that such a vast cache of documents could simply have been overlooked strains credulity. It begs the question: What is really going on within the corridors of the Justice Department?

The revelation that these documents originated from the Southern District of New York, the very jurisdiction that prosecuted Epstein associate Ghislaine Maxwell, adds another layer of intrigue. It suggests a level of disorganization, at best, and at worst, a deliberate attempt to obfuscate the true breadth of Epstein’s network and the extent of his crimes.

Khardori’s comparison to how the Justice Department would react if a corporate defendant were to suddenly “discover” a million documents it had previously overlooked illustrates the hypocrisy and double standards at play. In the corporate world, such a scenario would lead to severe repercussions. Yet, when the Justice Department itself is the party guilty of this “total professionally incompetent endeavor,” the response seems to be little more than a collective shrug.

This debacle reflects poorly on the leadership of the DOJ under Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche, highlighting either a monumental failure of oversight or a deliberate obfuscation. The implications of this incompetence (or potentially willful ignorance) are far-reaching, undermining public trust in the Justice Department and the integrity of the investigations into Epstein’s sordid affairs.

It’s time for a rigorous accounting of how this could have happened and why. The victims of Epstein’s crimes and the public at large deserve answers, not more bureaucratic blunders. The DOJ must be held to account for this latest fiasco, ensuring such a glaring oversight is never repeated. The stench of incompetence (or worse) emanating from this situation is a stark reminder of the need for transparency, accountability, and reform within the highest echelons of our justice system.
