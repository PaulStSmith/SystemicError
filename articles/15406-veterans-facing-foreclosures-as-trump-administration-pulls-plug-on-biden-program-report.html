Veterans facing foreclosures as Trump administration pulls plug on Biden program: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 2, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Reuters)

At a time when veterans with VA loans are struggling to keep their homes out of foreclosure, the Veterans Administration (VA) under Donald Trump's stewardship has decided to pull the plug on a program built specifically to keep them housed.

According to a report from NPR, the program known as VA Servicing Purchase (VASP) is on the chopping block with the approval of Republicans who feel President Joe Biden "unilaterally" forced it upon the country.

As NPR's Chris Arnold, Quil Lawrence wrote, VASP helps veterans who have fallen behind on their home payments by placing them in a program that lumps the delinquent amount into a separate "low-interest rate loan that the VA then owns outright."

Without that support, there is a risk that the nearly 90,000 VA loans that are in trouble, with a third already in foreclosure, will come due and force vets from their homes if they can't find alternative loans to fill the gap.

The NPR report notes, "Mortgage industry groups, housing advocates and veterans organizations have been warning the VA that shutting down VASP without replacing it with something else first would result in large numbers of vets losing their homes, many of whom are in this financial peril because of the VA's own mistakes."

According to Mike Calhoun, president of the nonprofit Center for Responsible Lending, "With the expiration of VASP, tens of thousands of Veterans and their families are now at significant risk of losing their homes," before adding, the "VA should extend VASP until this program is up and running."

In a letter to Congress sent last month, the non-profit National Consumer Law Center warned, "Without VASP or immediate access to alternative policy tools that provide relief … many of these homeowners will be forced to sell their homes and move or face foreclosure."

Our Analysis:

The VA's Disastrous Decision: A Closer Look at the VASP Program Cancellation

In what can only be described as a stunning disregard for the welfare of veterans, the Trump administration, with a nod from Republican allies, has decided to axe the Veterans Administration Servicing Purchase (VASP) program. This decision, as reported by NPR, is not just a policy shift; it's a direct blow to the lives of thousands of veterans already struggling to keep their homes.

The Essence of VASP

VASP isn't just another government program. It's a lifeline for veterans who've hit hard times. By converting delinquent payments into a separate low-interest rate loan owned by the VA, it offered a tangible solution for those at risk of losing their homes. The simplicity and effectiveness of this mechanism cannot be overstated – it's a targeted approach to prevent foreclosure and homelessness among veterans.

The Political Play

The axing of VASP has been framed by Republicans as a pushback against what they perceive as unilateral action by President Joe Biden. However, this narrative crumbles under scrutiny. The decision to pull the plug on a program that directly benefits veterans in dire financial straits seems less about policy principles and more about political gamesmanship. It's a chilling reminder of how the welfare of those who served can be sidelined for political points.

The Fallout

The NPR report lays bare the dire consequences of this decision. With nearly 90,000 VA loans in jeopardy – a third of which are already in foreclosure – the cancellation of VASP is not just a policy reversal; it's a potential crisis in the making. The warnings from mortgage industry groups, housing advocates, and veterans organizations speak volumes. They unanimously agree that shutting down VASP without a ready alternative will lead to a significant uptick in homelessness among veterans.

Mike Calhoun of the Center for Responsible Lending puts it starkly: tens of thousands of veterans are now at significant risk of losing their homes. This isn't hyperbole; it's a foreseeable outcome of a poorly considered decision. The call to extend VASP until a viable replacement is devised seems like common sense – yet, it appears to fall on deaf ears.

The Harsh Reality

The VA's own mistakes are cited as a contributing factor to the financial peril many veterans face. This adds an additional layer of irony – and tragedy – to the decision to end VASP. It's not just the removal of support; it's the exacerbation of a problem the VA helped create.

Conclusion

The decision to axe VASP is a glaring example of systemic failure. It reflects a broader issue where the needs of veterans are sidelined for political expediency. The potential fallout – increased homelessness and despair among those who served – is a price too high for any political victory.

The call for action is clear: The VA must reconsider its stance on VASP. Beyond that, there's a dire need for policies that genuinely address the needs of veterans, rather than using them as pawns in political maneuvering. The veterans deserve better. Our nation owes them that much.
