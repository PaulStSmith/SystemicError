The conservative New York Post reported in a "midnight scoop" that Trump had scrapped $5 billion in foreign aid in a "rare" action called a "pocket rescission." According to Post reporter Steven Nelson, it is a "legally debated maneuver that hasn't been used in 48 years."

"President Trump is moving to cancel nearly $5 billion in congressionally approved foreign aid and peacekeeping spending in a rare 'pocket rescission,' The Post has learned — making use of a legally debated maneuver that hasn’t been done in 48 years," Nelson reported. "Trump on Thursday night notified Congress of his request to cancel the funds, which had been tied up in a court case until earlier in the day."

The report notes, "A pocket rescission is a request that’s presented to Congress so late in the fiscal year — which ends Sept. 30 — that it’s made regardless of whether Congress acts."

But reporters were quick to sound an alarm.

Punchbowl News founder Jake Sherman flagged the news over night going into Friday, saying, "This would be the first step toward a shutdown next month."

Co-founder John Bresnahan agreed, writing, "This could very well lead to a shutdown."

One X user, District of Aluminum, added, "Vainglorious Russ Vought has decided to jeopardize a government shutdown with a $5 Billion 'Pocket Rescission' of foreign aid. He should have worked with Congress through appropriations. The villainy lies in his certainty that he alone knows the soul of America."

Our Analysis:

Trump's "Pocket Rescission": A Precarious Play

In what can only be described as a midnight maneuver, Donald Trump has executed a legally ambiguous action that teeters on the brink of fiscal irresponsibility and constitutional overreach. According to the conservative-leaning New York Post, Trump has scrapped a whopping $5 billion in foreign aid through a "pocket rescission." This move, as the Post’s Steven Nelson reports, marks the first use of such a tactic in nearly half a century—a period that has, for better or worse, respected certain fiscal norms and procedures.

A Rare and Legally Debated Maneuver

A "pocket rescission," for those unfamiliar with the term (and understandably so, given its 48-year hiatus from political action), is a request submitted to Congress so belatedly in the fiscal year (ending September 30) that it effectively bypasses the need for congressional action. This maneuver relies on a legally debated interpretation of presidential power, which should raise eyebrows regardless of one's political orientation. The fact that this hasn't been done in decades speaks volumes about the controversy and potential constitutional implications surrounding such an act.

The Implications of Trump's Action

The implications are clear and concerning. First, there's the immediate threat of a government shutdown—a scenario that Punchbowl News’ founders Jake Sherman and John Bresnahan have flagged as a real possibility. Such an outcome would not only disrupt government services but also signal a troubling disregard for the fiscal and operational stability of the nation's institutions.

Second, and perhaps more insidious, is the precedent this sets. Trump's decision to go rogue with a pocket rescission, bypassing the established appropriations process, strikes at the heart of Congressional authority over the purse—a foundational principle of the United States' constitutional framework. It's a bold assertion of executive power that, if left unchallenged, could erode the checks and balances that underpin American democracy.

The Villainy of Certainty

The critique by one X user, District of Aluminum, encapsulates the broader issue at hand: Trump, perhaps emboldened by former officials like Russ Vought, is acting under the dangerous assumption that he alone can dictate the fiscal and foreign policy direction of the United States. This "certainty" that one man knows the "soul of America" better than the collective wisdom of its elected representatives is not just hubris; it's a direct challenge to the democratic processes that govern appropriations and foreign aid.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Gamble

Trump's pocket rescission is more than a rare and legally debated action. It's a gamble—a gamble with the stability of government operations, with the integrity of the appropriations process, and with the principles of democratic governance itself. As this situation unfolds, one must question whether the potential short-term gains of $5 billion in rescinded foreign aid are worth the long-term costs to America's democratic institutions and fiscal stability.

In this precarious moment, the responsibility falls on Congress and the judiciary to respond decisively. To allow this maneuver to pass without challenge would set a dangerous precedent, emboldening further executive overreach and undermining the delicate balance of power that defines American government. The time for scrutiny, debate, and action is now—before this rare maneuver becomes a regular playbook for bypassing the legislative branch.
