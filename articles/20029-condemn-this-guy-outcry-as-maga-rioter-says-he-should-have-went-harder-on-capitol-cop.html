Former Capitol Police Sgt. Aquilino Gonell, who resigned while citing trauma from being attacked by Trump supporters during the January 6 Capitol riots, sounded the alarm over the weekend about one of the MAGA rioters who is being honored by the military.

"She was a traitor then, is a traitor now and she will always be in the future," Gonell said of rioter Ashli Babbitt.

Pardoned Jan. 6 rioter Ryan Nichols Sr. chimed in with, "So are you."

"We should have went harder on you that day," Nichols added.

Gonell responded to Nichols, saying, "This is coming from a guy claimed was innocent and asked the judge for leniency."

"This is the type of person Trump pardoned. He proudly asserts that they should have injured a US Capitol Police Officer worse than they did on January 6th," said lawyer Mark S. Zaid, who noted that Gonell "had to medically retire."

"But MAGA supports law enforcement? Seriously? Then show me by condemning this guy," Zaid added.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of MAGA's "Support" for Law Enforcement

In a world where political rhetoric often diverges wildly from reality, the recent exchange between former Capitol Police Sgt. Aquilino Gonell and pardoned Jan. 6 rioter Ryan Nichols Sr. serves as a stark reminder of the chasm between proclaimed values and actual behavior within the MAGA movement. Gonell, a victim of the violent insurrection at the U.S. Capitol, has rightly called out Ashli Babbitt, a participant in the riot, as a traitor. Nichols' response, a vile affirmation that the violence of that day should have been more severe, pulls back the curtain on a disturbing facet of MAGA ideology.

The Irony of MAGA's Stance on Law Enforcement

It's a narrative as old as the movement itself: MAGA claims to stand for the sanctity of law and order. Yet, when confronted with the reality of their actions - the brutal assault on law enforcement officers during the Capitol riots - their true colors bleed through. Nichols' reprehensible statement, “We should have went harder on you that day,” is not just an attack on Gonell; it's an affront to the very notion of law and order.

It's worth pondering, how does a movement that so vocally champions the police justify or even celebrate the violent actions of January 6th? How do they reconcile their cries of "back the blue" with the encouragement, or at the very least, the dismissive indifference towards the physical harm inflicted upon those tasked with protecting our Capitol? The hypocrisy is as blatant as it is appalling.

The Role of Presidential Pardons in Perpetuating Violence

The pardoning of individuals like Nichols by former President Trump is not merely a legal formality; it's an endorsement of their actions and a signal to like-minded individuals that violence is not just acceptable, but commendable. Mark S. Zaid, in highlighting Gonell's forced medical retirement due to injuries sustained during the riot, underscores the severity of the situation. Trump's pardons serve as a chilling reminder of the lengths to which he will go to protect and embolden those who commit acts of violence in his name.

The Call for Accountability

Zaid's challenge to the MAGA movement to condemn individuals like Nichols is more than a call for accountability; it's a test of the movement's commitment to the principles of law and order they so frequently tout. The silence or outright support from MAGA ranks in response to such challenges speaks volumes. It reveals a movement deeply entangled with extremism, willing to forsake the foundational values of democracy and civil society for political gain.

Conclusion: The True Face of MAGA's "Support" for Law Enforcement

As the dust settles, the bitter truth becomes clear: MAGA's proclaimed support for law enforcement is, at best, a convenient facade, discarded as soon as it becomes politically expedient. The movement's tolerance, if not endorsement, of violence against police officers exposes a fundamental hypocrisy at its core. It begs the question: How can any movement claim to support law and order while cheering on those who assault the very individuals sworn to uphold it?

The actions and attitudes of individuals like Nichols, and the pardons that protect them, are not anomalies but rather symptomatic of a broader, more disturbing trend within MAGA. The refusal to condemn, much less acknowledge, the inherent contradictions in their stance on law enforcement is a damning indictment of their proclaimed values. In the end, the MAGA movement's support for law enforcement is nothing more than a hollow echo, drowned out by the cheers for those who would do harm to those in uniform.
