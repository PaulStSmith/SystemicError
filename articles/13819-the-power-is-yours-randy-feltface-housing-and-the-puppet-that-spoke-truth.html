"The Power is Yours": Randy Feltface, Housing, and the Puppet That Spoke Truth

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 10, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

At first glance, Randy Feltface might look like a fuzzy purple puppet doing stand-up for laughs. But beneath the googly eyes and manic energy lies something sharper—something scalpel-precise. In just over a minute of crowd work in a Portland comedy show, Randy peels back the glossy veneer of property management and exposes the raw nerve of America's housing crisis.

"What do you do, Kai?"

With that deceptively innocent question, the unraveling begins.

Punching Up, Not Down

Randy picks his target well: Kai, a property manager in Portland. Not a landlord. Not a politician. Just a middle cog in the machine. But it’s that choice that makes the critique hit harder. By going after someone who isn’t the obvious villain, Randy highlights the banality of complicity.

Kai isn’t the system—he’s just in it. And like so many, he probably tells himself he’s just doing his job. But when Randy mocks the gap between aspiration and occupation—“You wish you worked for a real estate agency”—and then delivers the gut-punch:

"When did your dreams die, Kai?"

—we’re no longer watching comedy. We’re watching a reckoning.

Jokes with Teeth

There’s nothing accidental about the flow. The moment Randy finds out there are lots of vacancies in Portland and plenty of unhoused people, the setup becomes a systemic indictment.

"You should lower the rental prices. Do you reckon?"

It’s a rhetorical question with a bite. He’s pointing out the insanity of a housing market where abundance doesn’t translate into accessibility. Supply is high, demand is desperate, and yet prices remain out of reach. Why? Because housing isn’t treated as shelter—it’s treated as an asset.

And then, like a velvet-covered guillotine, Randy says:

"There is clearly a right answer and a wrong answer."

No laugh track. Just silence—or maybe nervous chuckles from an audience realizing they’re implicated too.

The Puppet as Prophet

Comedy allows uncomfortable truths to slip past defenses. Puppets amplify that effect. We expect truth from journalists, maybe philosophers, but not from something sewn together and operated with a hand. That subversion is what makes Randy Feltface so dangerous—and so effective.

He disarms with absurdity, then slashes with reality. The laughter he generates isn’t just amusement—it’s a pressure release for an audience staring down the barrel of systemic cruelty they usually avoid.

Portland as Microcosm

Randy couldn’t have picked a better city than Portland. It’s a progressive haven with a deeply broken housing market. The contradiction is delicious for satire. The audience likely agrees on the need for reform. And yet, many of them—like Kai—might be part of the inertia that keeps the system grinding.

So when Randy makes Kai choose between profit and ethics, he’s not just teasing an audience member—he’s indicting a city, a class, and a worldview.

Beyond the Joke

What makes this segment resonate isn’t just its clarity—it’s the urgency. This wasn’t a skit with a tidy resolution. Randy leaves the moral dilemma hanging, unanswered. Because that’s the real punchline: the power is yours.

It’s easy to laugh at a puppet berating a property manager. Harder to admit that we all, in some way, prop up the machinery. Whether through jobs, silence, or rationalizations, we’re part of a culture that lets buildings sit empty while people sleep on sidewalks.

So yes, it’s comedy. But it’s also a sermon. A confrontation. A demand for agency.

And as Randy reminds us—

"No judgement, but there is clearly a right answer and a wrong answer."

That line lingers longer than any laugh.

Because deep down, we already know which one we’re choosing.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
