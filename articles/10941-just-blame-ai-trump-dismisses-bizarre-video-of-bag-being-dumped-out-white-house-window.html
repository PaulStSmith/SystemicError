'Just blame AI': Trump dismisses bizarre video of bag being dumped out White House window

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump gestures during a cabinet meeting at the White House. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump dismissed the circulation of a viral video that appeared to show a dark bag being thrown out of an upstairs window at the White House — saying it didn't happen and it was probably faked with artificial intelligence.

"There's a video that is circulating online now of the White House, where a window is opened to the residence upstairs, and somebody is throwing a big bag out the window," said Fox News' Peter Doocy. "Have you seen this?"

"No," said Trump. "That's probably AI-generated. Actually, you can't open the windows, you know why? They're all heavily armored and bulletproof."

"So that's a fake video?" Doocy pressed him.

"Well it's got to be, because I know every window up there," said Trump. "The last place I'd be doing it is that, because there's cameras all over the place, right, including yours. No, but every window — I've never seen a window that's, in fact my wife was complaining about it the other day, she said, love to have a little fresh air come in. But you can't. They're bulletproof. And number one, they're sealed, and number two, each window weighs about 600 pounds. You'd have to be pretty strong to open them up."

Doocy then walked over to Trump and showed him the video on his phone. "It looks like it's on the 15th Street side, on the right hand?" he said.

"Yeah, those windows are sealed," said Trump. "They're all sealed, you can't open them." He went on to say, "One of the problems we have with AI, it's both good and bad. If something happens really bad, just blame AI ... But those windows are heavily sealed."

Our Analysis:

Trump's AI Distraction: A Masterclass in Deflection

In an era where the absurd has become mundane, the latest installment from the Trump saga feels like a scene ripped straight from a political satire, yet here we are, dissecting it with all seriousness. The former President, Donald Trump, faced with a viral video depicting a rather clandestine toss of a dark bag out of a White House window, resorts to a defense as bizarre as the accusation: blaming artificial intelligence for fabricating the footage.

"That's probably AI-generated," Trump declares, in response to Fox News' Peter Doocy's query about the video. This assertion, wild as it may seem, plays into a broader narrative Trump has often leaned on: deflect, deny, and dissemble. It’s a playbook page we've seen before, but with a modern twist—blame the robots.

Firstly, let's address the "heavily armored and bulletproof" windows defense. While it’s true that the White House, a fortress of democracy, is equipped with such security measures, Trump’s assertion serves a dual purpose: it not only attempts to debunk the video's authenticity but also subtly reminds the public of his high regard for security—albeit in a context that's bizarrely off-topic.

Then there’s the claim that "you'd have to be pretty strong to open them up," implying the implausibility of such an act. This argument might hold water if not for the fact that the essence of the accusation isn't about the feasibility of opening windows but rather the act captured in the video, true or not. The focus on the windows' weight and the impossibility of their being opened is a classic misdirection—a sleight of hand to divert attention from the substance of the accusations.

Trump's comments reflect a deeper issue at play: the weaponization of technological skepticism. By invoking the specter of AI as a bogeyman capable of generating convincing falsehoods, Trump taps into legitimate anxieties about deepfakes and misinformation. Yet, in doing so, he muddies the waters further, contributing to a climate where truth is not just contested but rendered malleable.

Let's not gloss over the casual, almost flippant suggestion that "If something happens really bad, just blame AI." This remark, though likely intended as a throwaway line, encapsulates a dangerous precedent. It hints at a future where accountability can be sidestepped with a shrug and a blame on technology. In Trump’s world, AI is not just a tool or a threat; it’s a convenient scapegoat for any and all inconveniences, real or imagined.

In his final act of deflection, Trump reiterates the impossibility of the scenario due to the windows being "heavily sealed." Again, missing the forest for the trees, or perhaps intentionally ignoring it. The focus on the physical impossibilities ignores the larger, more pertinent questions about the nature of the video and what it purportedly depicts.

In Conclusion

Donald Trump's responses to the viral video scandal are a microcosm of his broader approach to controversy: deflect, distract, and distort. By blaming artificial intelligence, he not only sidesteps the issue at hand but also plays into growing fears about the technology's potential for misuse. In doing so, Trump crafts a narrative where reality is negotiable, and truth is what he decrees it to be. It's a dangerous game, one that underestimates the intelligence of the public and overestimates the plausibility of his diversions.

In the end, the saga of the mysterious dark bag and the AI-generated controversy is yet another episode in the ongoing reality show that is Trump's political career. It's a reminder that in Trump's world, the truth is not just out there—it's whatever he says it is, regardless of the facts. And that, perhaps, is the most troubling takeaway of all.
