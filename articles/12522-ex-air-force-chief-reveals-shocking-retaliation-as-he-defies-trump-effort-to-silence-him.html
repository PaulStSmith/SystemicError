Frank Kendall wrote in the New York Times that he’d been told by organizations and law firms that they want nothing to do with him — because he dared criticize the actions of the current president.

And he warned of the dire state of democracy in a country where that could happen.

“Since I left the government in January, I have been told by several organizations that they either couldn’t openly employ me, hold my security clearances or otherwise be associated with someone visibly criticizing the administration,” the former Secretary of the Air Force wrote.

“In one instance, I was told that a nonprofit that had asked me to serve as a distinguished fellow withdrew that offer because its senior leadership felt I had become too partisan. One corporate chief executive told me I had become toxic for writing and speaking about the administration’s abuses of power.

“I expected some of these responses, but it’s disappointing to experience, nonetheless. I have lost count of how many of my fellow national security professionals have told me they are grateful that I have spoken out, but in the same breath say they are afraid to do the same.”

Kendall lamented the number of former government officials who have had their security clearances ripped from them and have been threatened with retribution just for vocally standing against the president.

“This climate of menace and apprehension extends to companies’ willingness to employ or associate with those who criticize Mr. Trump or his administration. I am one of those people,” he wrote.

Kendall wrote that he had vehemently opposed George W. Bush’ s presidency.

“We didn’t worry about being arbitrarily arrested or investigated, having any government funding for our organizations cut off, or being personally and viciously attacked on social media and in the press,” he wrote.

“I cannot make that statement today.”

Our Analysis:

A Critical Analysis: The Silencing of Dissent

In a recent op-ed for the New York Times, Frank Kendall, former Secretary of the Air Force under President Joe Biden, has shone a harrowing spotlight on a dire issue plaguing American democracy: the systematic blackballing of dissenting voices. His experiences, as outlined, reveal a chilling atmosphere where criticism of the ruling government, particularly the administration of Donald Trump, results in professional ostracization and punitive measures. This article paints a stark portrait of the current state of freedom of speech and the consequences faced by those daring to speak out.

The Paradox of Partisanship

Kendall's narrative starkly illustrates the paradox of partisanship that has seeped into the fabric of American society and governance. He recounts being labeled as toxic and partisan for merely exercising his First Amendment rights—rights that are supposed to be foundational to American democracy. The irony is palpable when considering Kendall's own admission of opposing George W. Bush's presidency, yet not fearing the repercussions he faces today. This shift towards intolerance and punishment of political dissent is a red flag signaling a move away from democratic norms and towards authoritarian practices.

The Professional Cost of Political Dissent

The consequences Kendall details extend far beyond the personal. They signify a broader, more disturbing trend of stifling critical voices in the national security realm. When seasoned professionals are deterred from speaking out due to fear of losing security clearances or employment opportunities, the nation's security posture is weakened. The "climate of menace and apprehension" that Kendall describes is not just an attack on individuals but on the very principles of open debate and critical evaluation that safeguard democratic governance and accountability.

The Silence of the Security Community

Perhaps the most damning indictment in Kendall's piece is the silence of his peers. The acknowledgment that many in the national security community are grateful for his outspokenness yet too fearful to join him speaks volumes about the toxic atmosphere that has been cultivated. This silence, borne out of fear of retribution, is a stark reminder of the power dynamics at play and the chilling effect they have on free speech and critical discourse.

A Call to Action

Kendall's op-ed should serve as a clarion call for those concerned with the health of American democracy. The systematic silencing and punishment of dissenting voices—particularly those with the expertise and experience of Kendall—is a dangerous trend that must be confronted and reversed. It requires a collective effort from all sectors of society, including the media, academia, and the legal community, to ensure that the principles of free speech and democratic debate are upheld.

In conclusion, Frank Kendall's experiences and the broader implications of his narrative underscore a troubling erosion of democratic norms in the United States. The silencing of dissent, particularly from those within the national security establishment, poses a grave threat not just to individual careers but to the very fabric of American democracy. It is imperative that this trend be recognized, challenged, and reversed to preserve the principles upon which the nation was founded.
