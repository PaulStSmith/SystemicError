The Supreme Court just told us Trump is going to lose this case: foremost expert

Predicting the Unpredictable: Trump’s Inevitable Defeat and the Supreme Court’s Selective Silence

A Predictable Pattern of Power

The Supreme Court’s recent decision to deny a stay in the Trump v. Miot and Mullin v. Doe case, concerning the revocation of Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for Haitian and Syrian immigrants, breaks from its recent pattern of accommodating the Trump administration’s legal requests. Historically, the Court has frequently granted stays that align with Trump’s directives, which raises critical questions about the power dynamics and biases within the highest court. Linda Greenhouse, a seasoned Supreme Court reporter, frames this anomaly as an indication of Trump’s inevitable loss, suggesting that the justices themselves recognize the untenability of the administration’s position.

The Misuse of Executive Power

Central to this case is the Trump administration’s attempt to redefine the consultation process required by law when altering the TPS. The administration’s approach, as noted in the lower court’s decision, involved minimal communication—a single email from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to the State Department—deemed a “brazen violation” of the required procedural standards. This act not only exemplifies the misuse of executive power but also highlights a disturbing trend of governance by decree, sidestepping established legal protocols and the agencies designed to enforce them.

Judicial Inconsistencies and Unexplained Decisions

The Supreme Court’s silence on why it denied the stay, diverging from its usual practice of siding with the administration, is particularly telling. Such unexplained decisions contribute to the opacity of a court that is supposed to epitomize transparency and reasoned judgment. The lack of dissent noted in the decision further obscures the motivations and alignments within the Court, leaving the public and legal experts to speculate about the undercurrents influencing these judicial outcomes.

The Role of Legal Scholarship and Media Framing

Greenhouse’s commentary, while speculative, is grounded in decades of legal observation and highlights the significant role that experienced legal journalism plays in interpreting the Court’s opaque maneuvers. However, the media’s approach to covering such legal battles often misses deeper systemic issues of judicial bias and the erosion of procedural norms. Instead of merely predicting outcomes based on past decisions, there is a critical need for media to scrutinize the consistency of judicial principles applied in each case.

The Bigger Picture: Procedure as a Shield

This case is not just about the immediate legal standings of hundreds of thousands of immigrants but also reflects broader issues of administrative accountability and the integrity of legal procedures. The procedural failings called out in this case serve as a microcosm for a larger pattern of administrative overreach and a lack of accountability that has characterized much of Trump’s approach to governance.

Conclusion: A System in Need of Oversight

The Supreme Court’s decision in Trump v. Miot and Mullin v. Doe may well predict an impending legal defeat for Trump. However, the greater takeaway should be the need for consistent judicial oversight and the protection of procedural integrity against political whims. As the Court continues to shape the landscape of American law and life, its decisions—and the processes by which they are made—must be scrutinized with rigor and without presumption. This case offers a crucial reminder of the ongoing battle for transparency and accountability at the highest levels of power.
