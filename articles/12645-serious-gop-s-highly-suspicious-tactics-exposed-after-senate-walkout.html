‘Serious:’ GOP's 'highly suspicious' tactics exposed after Senate​ walkout

A Democratic lawmaker who participated in a walkout Thursday during a confirmation hearing for a Trump-nominated judge called Republican leadership’s conduct during the meeting “a serious, unprecedented” breaking of committee rules.

“This was a serious, unprecedented rule-breaking to jam this guy through,” said Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse (D-RI), speaking on CNN Thursday.

Several Democratic lawmakers walked out of the Senate Judiciary Committee earlier Thursday during a confirmation hearing for Emil Bove, principal associate deputy attorney general at the Justice Department, who President Donald Trump nominated for the Third Circuit in the US Court of Appeals.

Bove, who a whistleblower complaint claimed openly urged DOJ staff to defy court orders related to halting deportation flights of migrants, has had his confirmation opposed by Democrats, who attempted to use committee rules in their favor Thursday by objecting to Bove’s confirmation.

Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) attempted to initiate the objection to Bove’s confirmation, as is his right under committee rules, only to be interrupted by the committee chair, Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA), who ignored Booker’s request and carried on with Bove’s confirmation.

“They could have tabled Sen. Booker, they could have simply voted him down, but instead of doing that, they just called the vote while he was talking,” Whitehouse said.

“So you have the clerk of the court starting to call the vote, interrupting a speaking United States senator in violation of an existing rule, and that was like 'okay, we've put up with enough of this,' so we got up to get out to take away the quorum.”

And while the walkout didn’t stop Bove’s confirmation from moving forward, Whitehouse slammed the Republican-led process as being “highly suspicious” given the circumstances surrounding the Trump pick for a lifetime federal court position.

“It's highly suspicious because it's happening side by side with this effort to have Trump judges bottle up the hearing in court, the contempt hearing in court that would reveal Bove's role in that incident where they were planning to disobey court orders and tell courts, 'F you,’” Whitehouse said.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Bove Confirmation Circus

In what can only be described as a brazen display of partisan power play, Republican leadership has once again trampled over established committee rules and basic decency to force through the confirmation of Emil Bove, a Trump-nominated judge, to the Third Circuit in the US Court of Appeals. This is not merely a procedural faux pas; it is a glaring example of the lengths to which the GOP will go to stack the judiciary with ideologically aligned judges, regardless of the norms they bulldoze in the process.

Unprecedented Rule-Breaking

Senator Sheldon Whitehouse's (D-RI) assertion that we witnessed “a serious, unprecedented rule-breaking” is no hyperbolic statement. The GOP's actions during the confirmation hearing were not just a deviation from norms; they were a flagrant violation of them. When Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) attempted to exercise his rightful objection to Bove’s confirmation—a prerogative granted to him under committee rules—he was blatantly interrupted by Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA), the committee chair, who decided that the rules were more what you'd call "guidelines" than actual rules.

Let's be clear here: This isn't about being sore losers. This is about maintaining a semblance of procedure and fairness in a system that's rapidly being corroded by partisan interests. The Republican leadership's decision to not only ignore Booker’s objection but to proceed with calling the vote while he was still speaking is both disrespectful and undemocratic.

Suspicious Circumstances

The context surrounding Bove’s nomination only adds fuel to the fire. Bove, accused via a whistleblower complaint of urging Department of Justice staff to defy court orders concerning the deportation of migrants, is hardly the paragon of judicial impartiality and adherence to the rule of law that one would hope for in a lifetime appointment to the federal courts. Whitehouse's remarks on the "highly suspicious" nature of the GOP-led confirmation process are spot on. It reeks of desperation and manipulation, aiming to secure as many Trump-appointed judges in the judiciary as possible, irrespective of their qualifications or ethical standings.

The fact that this confirmation is occurring "side by side with this effort to have Trump judges bottle up the hearing in court" is deeply troubling. It suggests a concerted effort not just to reshape the judiciary in Trump's image but to actively interfere with the judicial process to protect allies and potentially obstruct justice.

The Walkout: A Symbol of Protest

While the Democrats' walkout may not have stopped Bove's confirmation from moving forward, it served as a powerful symbol of protest against the Republican's steamrolling tactics. It was a statement that they would not be complicit in the undermining of committee rules and the erosion of democratic processes.

Conclusion

What we are witnessing is not politics as usual. It is a concerted effort by the GOP to install judges who are ideologically aligned with them, at the expense of the integrity of the American judicial system. The manner in which Emil Bove's confirmation was handled should alarm anyone who cares about the balance of power and the rule of law in the United States. It is a stark reminder of the systemic error that pervades our current political and judicial appointment processes—a flaw that needs urgent correcting.
