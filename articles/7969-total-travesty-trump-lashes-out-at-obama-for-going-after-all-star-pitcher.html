‘Total travesty!’ Trump lashes out at Obama for ‘going after’ All-Star pitcher

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 7, 20254 min read

President Donald Trump launched into a lengthy tirade Saturday in defense of professional baseball player Roger Clemens, who he argued has unfairly been denied proper recognition due to unproven “rumors and innuendo,” fueled in part by the Obama administration.

“The Baseball Hall of Fame Committee is voting on admitting new Members TOMORROW, and these highly respected owners, executives, writers, and, most importantly, Hall of Famers, should do the right thing by finally putting Roger Clemens, known as “The Rocket,” in the Hall!” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

“Roger Clemens is the only pitcher who has won 300 games to not have the honor of being enshrined in the Hall of Fame, which is a total travesty!”

Clemens has faced accusations of using performance enhancers since the late 1990s, starting with testimony from his former trainer who alleged he had injected Clemens with steroids and human growth hormone, or HGH. While Clemens has repeatedly denied the claims, he would go on to be indicted in 2010 over charges of obstruction and perjury after testifying before a House committee related to the allegations, though would later be found not guilty.

Trump seized on the timing of Clemens indictment to lay blame for him not being recognized in the Hall of Fame directly at Obama’s feet.

“The only reason he is not is because of rumors and innuendo, which were not proven,” Trump wrote.

“He never tested positive and, when the Obama DOJ went after him in a criminal case claiming that he did take steroids, Roger, who has always denied taking any drugs, was FULLY ACQUITTED OF ALL CHARGES.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tirade: A Critical Dissection

In a recent spectacle of misplaced priorities, President Donald Trump has taken to his social media platform, Truth Social, to launch a lengthy tirade in defense of professional baseball player Roger Clemens. Trump’s argument hinges on the belief that Clemens has been unjustly excluded from the Baseball Hall of Fame due to “rumors and innuendo,” a situation he attributes, quite bizarrely, to the Obama administration.

The Misdirection Play

Trump’s statement, “The Baseball Hall of Fame Committee is voting on admitting new Members TOMORROW...,” serves as a classic example of his penchant for dramatizing and personalizing issues, drawing attention to what he perceives as an injustice towards Clemens. He praises Clemens as “The Rocket,” extolling his achievements and lamenting his exclusion from the Hall of Fame as a “total travesty.”

However, this bombastic support conveniently glosses over a significant aspect of Clemens’ career: the accusations of using performance enhancers. These aren’t mere whispers in the wind but allegations grounded in testimony from Clemens’ former trainer, who claimed to have injected Clemens with steroids and human growth hormone (HGH). Clemens’ subsequent indictment in 2010 over charges of obstruction and perjury, although he was found not guilty, further complicates the narrative.

The Blame Game

In a bewildering leap of logic, Trump attempts to pin the blame for Clemens’ Hall of Fame exclusion squarely on the shoulders of the Obama administration. He asserts, without substantiation, that the only barrier to Clemens’ recognition is “rumors and innuendo” that were supposedly amplified by Obama’s Department of Justice.

This claim not only ignores the complex and multifaceted process by which athletes are elected to the Hall of Fame but also attempts to politicize what is fundamentally a sports and ethics issue. The Hall of Fame’s voting process, conducted by writers and members, is influenced by a variety of factors, including a player’s integrity, sportsmanship, and character. Trump’s reduction of Clemens’ exclusion to a political vendetta is not only simplistic but misleading.

The Unproven and the Unprovable

Trump’s statement, “He never tested positive and, when the Obama DOJ went after him...Roger...was FULLY ACQUITTED OF ALL CHARGES,” employs a common rhetorical strategy: conflating legal acquittal with factual innocence. While it’s true Clemens was acquitted, this does not equate to an unequivocal vindication in the court of public opinion or in the specific context of the Hall of Fame’s moral and ethical standards.

The emphasis on Clemens never having tested positive for performance enhancers sidesteps the broader issue of integrity within sports. Performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs) have been a shadow over baseball, and the Hall of Fame has historically taken a firm stance against admitting players with credible accusations against them, regardless of legal outcomes.

Conclusion: A Swing and a Miss

Trump’s defense of Roger Clemens is emblematic of his broader approach to controversy and conflict: deflect, distract, and blame. By implicating the Obama administration in Clemens’ Hall of Fame exclusion, Trump seeks to politicize an issue that is, at its core, about sportsmanship and integrity.

In this tirade, Trump misses the larger point: the Hall of Fame, and by extension the sport of baseball, values certain ideals that transcend individual achievement. Clemens’ case is complex, involving both remarkable talent and serious allegations. To reduce this nuanced issue to a matter of political persecution is not only inaccurate but a disservice to the discourse on ethics in sports.

In sum, Trump’s latest foray into sports commentary is a stark reminder of his tendency to view every issue through a lens of personal loyalty and political vendetta, rather than principle or fact. It’s a narrative that, while consistent with his brand, does little to advance a meaningful conversation about integrity, both in sports and beyond.
