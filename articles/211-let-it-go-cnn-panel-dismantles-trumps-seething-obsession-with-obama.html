'Let it go': CNN panel dismantles Trump’s seething obsession with Obama

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosJune 24, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Story, BrieflyPower Is Not the Problem HereMisdirection as StrategyRacism Is Not a Side NoteThe Real Comparison Trump Cannot EscapeSystemic Error

Trump’s Obama Fixation Is Not a Personality Quirk. It Is a Political Cover Story.

The Story, Briefly

MS NOW’s Katy Tur and Peter Baker used Trump’s fixation on Barack Obama as the lens for a broader criticism: Trump keeps measuring himself against a more popular predecessor, blames Obama for his own failures, and cannot stop turning everything into a contest he is losing. The reporting also notes Trump reposting a racist video about Obama and Michelle Obama, then deleting it after Republican senators objected.

Power Is Not the Problem Here

The central fact is simple: Trump holds the institutional power. Obama does not. Yet the public conversation keeps drifting toward Obama’s popularity, Obama’s legacy, and Trump’s emotional inability to accept the comparison. That framing flatters Trump by treating him like a man trapped in rivalry rather than the president who controls the state.

This is the oldest dodge in modern political media: convert an abuse of power into a psychological puzzle. A president who can order policy, shape agencies, and inflame public life is not merely “obsessed.” He is using a former president as a permanent scapegoat for his own failures.

Misdirection as Strategy

Trump’s habit of blaming Obama for the Russia investigation, the Iran deal, and “all manner of things” is not confusion. It is a deliberate laundering of responsibility. The lie works because it shifts attention from the current administration’s choices to a fantasy enemy who can no longer answer back in office.

That is why the “why can’t he let it go?” frame is so weak. Letting it go would require accountability. Trump does the opposite: he recycles Obama as a political screen whenever he needs to hide his own record. The target changes, but the function stays the same.

Racism Is Not a Side Note

The racist repost about Obama and Michelle Obama was not a random lapse of taste. It was part of the same machinery: dehumanize the Black predecessor, poison the comparison, and turn political grievance into racial contempt. The fact that it was removed only after Republican senators publicly objected tells you where the guardrails are supposed to come from and how often they fail.

Even then, the restraint came from political embarrassment, not principle. The system did not reject the post because it was grotesque. It reacted because the grotesque became costly.

The Real Comparison Trump Cannot Escape

The useful comparison is not Trump versus Obama as personalities. It is Trump’s claims versus outcomes. On Iran, the source notes a clear contrast with the Obama agreement and suggests Trump cannot produce a better result. That is the real political problem: he ran on superiority and governs by substitution, outrage, and symbolic demolition.

This is why the metaphors around the reflecting pool, the ballroom, and the self-congratulating spectacle matter. They point to priorities, and those priorities are visibly detached from governing. Trump’s project is not competence. It is dominance, performance, and the refusal to be measured by facts.

Systemic Error

This story is not about one man’s obsession with another man. It is about a political system that keeps translating abuse into personality, and power into drama. That translation is convenient for everyone who benefits from looking away from the actual decision-maker.

When a president blames a predecessor for his own record, the lie is not just rhetorical. It is administrative. It teaches the public to expect spectacle where there should be accountability, and grievance where there should be responsibility. That is how institutional cowardice protects itself: by calling deliberate misdirection a personal hang-up.
