A Trump administration order to keep an aging and unneeded Michigan coal-fired power plant open and online reportedly is costing taxpayers about $615,000 per day, or $113 million to date. Closing the plant would save taxpayers about $640 million by 2040.

“The Trump administration in May ordered utility giant Consumers Energy to keep the 63-year-old JH Campbell coal plant in western Michigan, about 100 miles north-east of Chicago, online just as it was being retired,” according to The Guardian.

“The costs of unnecessarily running this jalopy coal plant just continue to mount,” Michael Lenoff, an attorney with Earthjustice, which is suing over the order, told The Guardian.v

President Donald Trump signed a national energy emergency order on his first day in office this year, rolling back regulations.

In court documents, Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel said that the administration’s latest order is “arbitrary and illegal.”

Consumers Energy, the operator of the plant, did not ask the Trump administration for the order to keep the coal plant running, and the Trump administration did not consult local regulators, a spokesperson for the Michigan public service commission (MPSC), told the Guardian in May.

“The unnecessary recent order … will increase the cost of power for homes and businesses in Michigan and across the midwest,” the chair of the MPSC, Dan Scripps, said in a statement at the time.

Reporting on what it called Trump’s “pro-fossil fuel agenda,” The Guardian in January quoted the president:

“We have something that no other manufacturing nation will ever have, the largest amount of oil and gas of any country on Earth, and we are going to use it – let me use it,” Trump said in his inaugural address. “We will be a rich nation again, and it is that liquid gold under our feet that will help to do it.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Coal Plant Fiasco: A Costly Misadventure

In a move that reeks of both environmental and economic disregard, a Trump administration order has shackled taxpayers with a daily bill of $615,000 to keep a dilapidated Michigan coal-fired power plant running—a plant that, by all accounts, should have been retired. This decision, as reported by The Guardian, has so far cost a staggering $113 million, with projections suggesting it could save taxpayers around $640 million by 2040 if the plant were closed. The rationale—or lack thereof—behind this decision encapsulates a broader narrative of the administration’s pro-fossil fuel agenda, which prioritizes outdated energy sources over both fiscal prudence and ecological sustainability.

The Economics of Obsolescence

The JH Campbell coal plant in western Michigan, a 63-year-old facility, stands as a monument to a bygone era of energy production. Yet, in a move that defies economic logic, the Trump administration in May unilaterally decided to keep this “jalopy” of a plant operational. This decision is not just a financial drain, costing taxpayers an exorbitant sum daily, but it also flies in the face of the inevitable shift towards more sustainable and cost-effective energy sources.

Michael Lenoff, an attorney with Earthjustice, aptly highlighted the absurdity of the situation, stating, “The costs of unnecessarily running this jalopy coal plant just continue to mount.” This statement underscores the financial hemorrhage caused by adhering to outdated energy policies, which serve neither the public interest nor the environmental imperative of our times.

Arbitrary and Illegal?

Michigan Attorney General Dana Nessel has called the administration’s order “arbitrary and illegal,” a characterization that points to a potentially deeper malaise within the decision-making process. The unilateral nature of this order, issued without consultation with local regulators or the plant’s operator, Consumers Energy, suggests a disregard for procedural norms and local impacts. It’s a stark reminder of the administration’s penchant for top-down directives that sideline expert opinions and relevant stakeholders.

Ignoring the Cost to Consumers

The Michigan Public Service Commission (MPSC), through its spokesperson and chair Dan Scripps, has voiced concern over the increased cost of power for homes and businesses in Michigan and across the Midwest as a consequence of this order. This highlights a critical aspect often overlooked in the broader national discourse—the immediate financial impact on consumers. It’s a scenario where political decisions, detached from economic realities and environmental considerations, directly burden the populace.

Trump’s Pro-Fossil Fuel Agenda: A Dubious Legacy

Trump’s inaugural address promise, to leverage the United States’ oil and gas reserves as a path to prosperity, underscores a fundamental miscalculation. It is a vision that glaringly ignores the global shift towards renewable energy sources and the pressing reality of climate change. His assertion, “We will be a rich nation again, and it is that liquid gold under our feet that will help to do it,” not only misreads the economic landscape but also underestimates the environmental and health costs associated with fossil fuel dependency.

Conclusion

The decision to keep the JH Campbell coal plant operational is emblematic of an administration that prioritizes an outdated and environmentally detrimental energy agenda over economic rationality and environmental stewardship. It’s a policy choice that imposes a financial burden on taxpayers, disregards legal and procedural norms, and contributes to the broader ecological crisis facing our planet. The ongoing costs of this decision serve as a stark reminder of the need for energy policies that align with economic realities and environmental imperatives, rather than clinging to the relics of a fossil fuel past.
