'We got played again': MAGA turns on House Republicans over 'fake' Epstein files release

The Republican-led House Oversight Committee was promptly ridiculed Tuesday night into Wednesday morning for its release of 33,000 files on Jeffrey Epstein; the vast majority of which, critics have noted, were either redacted or already public.

“Literally 97% of the ‘Epstein files’ just released by the House Oversight Committee were already public,” wrote Evan Kilgore, formally an ambassador for the far-right nationalist group Turning Point USA, in a social media post on X Tuesday to his more than 130,000 followers. “We got played again.”

The Trump administration has faced growing scrutiny in recent months over its handling of its investigation into Epstein, the disgraced financier who died in 2019 awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, and is alleged to have run a blackmail operation targeting powerful individuals. Amid growing pressure, the House Oversight Committee released a trove of Epstein files Tuesday evening, but its release has only prompted increased scrutiny.

“Oh my god, Republicans just fake released the Epstein files again,” wrote Kyle Kulinski, host of the Kyle Kulinski Show, in a social media post on X to his more than 500,000 followers.

Democratic lawmakers joined in on the pile on as well, with Rep. Robert Garcia, the ranking member of the House Oversight Committee, labeling the file drop as a dud, and urging Americans not to consider the matter settled.

“The 33,000 pages of Epstein documents [House Oversight Chair and Republican Rep.] James Comer has decided to ‘release’ were already mostly public information,” Garcia said, speaking with the Washington Post in a report published Wednesday.

“To the American people – don’t let this fool you. After careful review, Oversight Democrats have found that 97% of the documents received from the Department of Justice were already public. There is no mention of any client list or anything that improves transparency or justice for victims.”

The Trump administration faced similar criticism back in February, when it released what it called “Phase 1” of the Epstein files to a select-few far-right influencers. Like the House Oversight Committee’s recent file drop, those too were largely already public, and in some cases, more redacted than past releases of the same documents.

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) has joined with Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) to circumvent the House Oversight Committee by pursuing a discharge petition, a legislative tool that would force a vote on releasing all files related to Epstein currently held by the Justice Department.

Our Analysis:

The Great Epstein File Farce: A Masterclass in Political Smoke and Mirrors

The recent debacle surrounding the so-called release of the "Epstein Files" by the Republican-led House Oversight Committee is not just a failure; it's a glaring testament to the art of political obfuscation and public manipulation. In what can only be described as a farcical attempt to appear transparent, the committee has managed to insult the intelligence of the American public with a dump of 33,000 files, of which a staggering 97% were already public knowledge. This isn't just a drop in the bucket; it's a drop that's already evaporated.

The Illusion of Action

Let's cut through the fog. The proclaimed release, lauded by some corners as a step towards accountability for the disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein, was nothing more than a theatrical display of redundancy. Critics, including those from within the ranks of far-right nationalism, have openly mocked the effort, pointing out the sheer pointlessness of repackaging public information and labeling it as a revelation. Evan Kilgore's frustration, shared with his substantial following, encapsulates the sentiment perfectly: "We got played again." And indeed, the American public did get played—served a reheated meal and told it was a feast.

A Pattern of Pretense

This isn't the first time the Trump administration and its allies have engaged in this masquerade. Recall the February fiasco, dubbed "Phase 1" of the Epstein file releases, which similarly resulted in a dissemination of largely already-public, and in some instances, more heavily redacted documents than their predecessors. One must ask, to what end? The pattern here is unmistakable: a deliberate strategy to feign progress while obfuscating the truth.

Democratic Outcry and Bipartisan Efforts

Representative Robert Garcia's dismissal of the file drop as a "dud" and his plea for Americans to see through this charade is a beacon of rationality in this orchestrated chaos. The Oversight Democrats' findings—that a mere 3% of the documents provided any new information—underscores the manipulation at play. It's a sobering reminder that in the quest for transparency and justice for Epstein's victims, political gamesmanship has taken center stage over genuine pursuit of truth.

The bipartisan effort led by Representatives Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Ro Khanna (D-CA) to employ a discharge petition in order to bypass the committee's bottleneck offers a glimmer of hope. This legislative maneuver, should it succeed, could compel the release of all Epstein-related files held by the Justice Department, potentially uncovering the unvarnished truth that has been so meticulously shielded from public view.

Conclusion: The Quest for Transparency

In the grand theater of political posturing, the release of the "Epstein Files" by the House Oversight Committee stands out as a particularly egregious act of deception. It's a stark reminder that in the corridors of power, the appearance of action often supersedes substantive progress. The American people deserve better. They deserve transparency, accountability, and a commitment to justice untainted by political calculus.

As this saga unfolds, one thing remains clear: the battle for truth in the Epstein case is far from over. It's a battle against not just the individuals who committed or facilitated heinous acts but against a system that perpetuates opacity and evasion. The quest for transparency continues, and it's a quest that demands our collective vigilance, outrage, and action.
