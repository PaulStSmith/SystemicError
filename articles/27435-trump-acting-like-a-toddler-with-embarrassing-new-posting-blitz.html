Trump acting 'like a toddler' with embarrassing new posting 'blitz'

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosNovember 24, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A summit inflated into a world orderRecognition as presidential currencyPutin withheld the validation

Donald Trump marked a reported trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin by publishing 26 images that cast the three presidents as a new “Big Three” entitled to shape the world’s future. The “toddler” comparison came from veteran Washington reporter Simon Marks, who described Trump’s Saturday posting spree as the performance of a child returning from a party eager to display his new friends. The mockery is memorable; the underlying politics are more consequential. An American president was presenting personal acceptance by two authoritarian leaders as both a diplomatic achievement and a source of his own legitimacy.

A summit inflated into a world order

According to Marks’s analysis for The i Paper, which AlterNet highlighted, Trump filled his Truth Social account with photographs and AI-generated scenes from meetings held on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China. The captions repeatedly placed Trump, Xi and Putin together as coequal global figures.

One post compared their meeting with the 1945 Yalta summit, pairing images of Trump, Xi and Putin with Winston Churchill, Joseph Stalin and Franklin D. Roosevelt beneath the caption “The Great Powers 1945, 2026.” Another AI-generated image showed the three contemporary presidents striding together past their national flags, though the synthetic display reportedly included errors in those flags.

The analogy did more than flatter Trump. Yalta involved sweeping decisions by Allied leaders as the Second World War approached its end. Marks reported that the four-point Shenzhen declaration was far narrower. Trump nevertheless promoted the meeting as the beginning of a “G3,” enlarging a limited diplomatic document into the supposed birth certificate of a new great-power directorate.

That substitution—imagery for substance, personal stature for policy—is the central maneuver. The posts offered no need to demonstrate comparable results if Trump could manufacture comparable iconography.

Recognition as presidential currency

Several posts stressed that Xi regarded Trump as an equal, while others attacked Barack Obama’s conduct around foreign leaders as supposedly submissive. The contrast revealed the metric Trump wanted his audience to use: diplomacy judged not by what the United States secured or surrendered, but by whether another powerful man appeared to grant Trump sufficient status.

The Putin material made that politics of recognition even more explicit. Trump published an image labeling the Russian president “My Friend,” which AlterNet reported drew widespread online ridicule as needy and embarrassing.

Marks argued that no other modern American president had treated relations with a foreign leader as a purely personal bond rather than one conducted for the country the president represents. Whatever the historical sweep of that claim, it identifies the institutional distortion visible in the posts themselves. Trump did not advertise a public benefit from friendship with Putin. He advertised the friendship.

That is not merely undignified presentation. When the president depicts diplomacy as a private relationship among dominant personalities, national interests become secondary to his standing within their circle. Xi and Putin lead states with their own strategic objectives. Trump’s feed recast them as characters in a story about Trump being accepted.

Putin withheld the validation

Even the proposed “Group of Three” remained more aspiration than established arrangement in the reporting. When BBC correspondent Steve Rosenberg asked Putin on Friday whether the G3 represented a new mechanism for addressing global problems, Putin replied: “Not yet.”

The answer stopped short of endorsing Trump’s framing while leaving it tantalizingly unresolved. Marks suggested that those two words would encourage Trump to believe the possibility remained open.

That exchange exposes the imbalance beneath the pageantry. Trump devoted a 26-post promotional barrage to declaring the arrival of an exclusive global club; Putin retained the power to decide whether the club existed at all. The American president supplied the ad campaign, the AI mythology and the professions of friendship. His purported friend supplied only “not yet.”
