'They're gonna confine him': Legal expert flags evidence Supreme Court will rein Trump in

The U.S. Supreme Court has in some instances failed to issue negative orders against Donald Trump, but that could happen very soon, a legal expert said on Sunday.

Legal expert Norm Eisen, who worked in the White House as special counsel and special assistant to the president for ethics and government reform, is also known for being a thorn in the president's side.

On Sunday, he appeared on MSNBC, where he highlighted some evidence that the Supreme Court is ready to rein Trump in.

"The Supreme Court is clearly concerned also, if you look at the trajectory of their cases rejecting Donald Trump again and again on issues like immigration," Eisen said. "He said he would be a dictator on day one. The essence of dictatorship is this kind of defying the constitution and laws, so it's not only on the 14th amendment, but across the board."

Eisen went on to describe a recent court victory he had against Trump.

"We were in court with a big coalition on Friday. He's reorganizing the entire federal government unconstitutionally, and that federal court agreed. We won on Friday. We got a [Temporary Restraining Order]," he added. "Trump is running amok. The Supreme Court are not just robots calculating the meaning of the words in the constitution. They're human beings. They're political actors. They see this run amok administration, this unlawful administration."

He concluded, "So that coloration also tends to lead me to believe they're going to confine him."

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court's Trump Dilemma: A Closer Look

The article reports on a rather bold assertion by legal expert Norm Eisen regarding the U.S. Supreme Court's disposition towards former President Donald Trump. Eisen, known for his critical stance against Trump, suggests that the Supreme Court is on the verge of issuing orders against Trump, a move that, if true, could signify a seismic shift in the judicial treatment of the former president's actions. But how much water does Eisen's argument hold, and what can we decipher from the Supreme Court's past behavior?

The Claims of Norm Eisen: Unpacking the Predictions

Eisen, a notable figure for his role in ethics and government reform during the Obama administration, makes a critical analysis based on the Supreme Court's trajectory in handling cases related to Trump, especially on immigration. He argues that the Court is ready to "rein Trump in," citing their repeated rejection of Trump's propositions as evidence of their concern over his defiance against constitutional norms.

However, there's a glaring issue with Eisen's argument. The Supreme Court, though it has rebuffed Trump on several occasions, operates with a level of unpredictability, especially given its current conservative lean. To suggest that the Court is gearing up for a more aggressive stance against Trump based on past decisions is a stretch, at best speculative, and at worst, wishful thinking.

Analyzing the Supreme Court's Stance

The Supreme Court's decisions indeed reflect a complex relationship with the Trump administration's policies, particularly on immigration. Yet, interpreting these decisions as a clear indicator of impending negative orders specifically against Trump simplifies the nuanced and multifaceted nature of Supreme Court rulings. The Court's decisions are often grounded in legal reasoning that transcends the simplistic binary of for or against a particular political figure.

Eisen's portrayal of the Court as "not just robots calculating the meaning of the words in the constitution" but as "human beings" and "political actors" is a poignant reminder of the inherent subjectivity within the judiciary. However, it's essential to tread carefully when attributing motives or future actions to the Court based on this subjectivity.

The Bigger Picture: The Role of the Judiciary

Eisen's comments reflect a broader concern with the Trump administration's approach to governance, especially the alleged reorganization of the federal government in an unconstitutional manner. His victory in obtaining a Temporary Restraining Order against such reorganization efforts underscores the critical role of the judiciary in checking executive overreach.

Yet, Eisen's optimism regarding the Supreme Court's future actions against Trump seems to overlook the Court's inherent conservatism and its potential reluctance to engage in what could be perceived as political battles. The judiciary's independence is its cornerstone, and while it has mechanisms to check on the executive, predicting its moves, especially in high-stakes political contexts, is fraught with uncertainty.

Conclusion

While Eisen's analysis and his intimate understanding of legal battles against Trump offer an intriguing perspective, it's essential to approach his predictions with caution. The Supreme Court's decisions are complex and grounded in legal principles that do not always align neatly with political expectations or desires.

In the grand chessboard of U.S. politics and governance, the Supreme Court plays a game that is meticulously calculated and deeply rooted in the constitution. Its moves are not easily predicted, and while it may indeed issue negative orders against Trump in the future, such outcomes are far from guaranteed based on past actions alone. In short, the Supreme Court's next move remains, as always, shrouded in judicial mystery.
