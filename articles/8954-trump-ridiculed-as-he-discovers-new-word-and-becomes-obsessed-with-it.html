MSNBC’s Steve Benen pointed out the president’s sudden obsession with “affordability” — a word that marks a curious shift in his rhetorical strategy, particularly on his social media platform, Truth Social.

Until this week, “He literally never wrote a tweet that included the word ‘affordability,’” Benen wrote.

But since the GOP was routed in elections Tuesday — with everyday costs being a central issue — he seems to have become obsessed, Benen wrote.

On Election Day 2025, Trump boldly proclaimed, “If affordability is your issue, VOTE REPUBLICAN! Energy costs, as an example, are plummeting.” Despite his claims, energy costs are actually rising, not falling, Benen said.

Following Democratic election victories, Trump doubled down, declaring, “Affordability is our goal.” He then launched into a passionate rant: “2025 Thanksgiving dinner under Trump is 25% lower than 2024 Thanksgiving dinner under Biden, according to Walmart. My cost are lower than the Democrats on everything, especially oil and gas! So the Democrats ‘affordability’ issue is DEAD! STOP LYING!!!”

In reality, Walmart simply reduced costs by decreasing the number of items and replacing brand-name products with cheaper alternatives - not through any White House intervention.

During a Fox News interview with Bret Baier, Trump made even more audacious claims. He asserted that he had brought energy costs “way down” and that grocery costs were also “way down” — both statements being demonstrably false.

Perhaps most tellingly, Trump claimed, “You know, they have this new word called ‘affordability’ and [Republicans] don’t talk about it enough. The Democrats did.”

The article argues that Trump’s newfound focus on “affordability” is more rhetorical than substantive. Republicans can repeatedly use the word, but it won’t change the fact that the party has failed to effectively address cost-of-living challenges, Benen wrote

Democrats’ electoral success on Tuesday, Benen argues, came not from merely mentioning affordability, but by highlighting the GOP’s substantial failures on the issue and offering genuine alternatives.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s “Discovery” of Affordability: A Desperate Rhetorical Pivot

In what can only be described as a comical twist of fate, Donald Trump, the former President known for his bombastic and often factually challenged statements, has purportedly stumbled upon a “new word” — affordability. This revelation, as reported by MSNBC’s Steve Benen, is not just a testament to Trump’s disconnect with the common man’s struggles but also highlights a desperate pivot in his rhetorical strategy post a significant GOP electoral defeat.

Let’s dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned cynic.

The Sudden Obsession

Until the GOP faced a rout in the recent elections, Trump’s narrative seldom, if ever, touched upon the real issues of affordability and cost of living. His sudden proclamation on Election Day 2025, urging voters to choose Republican for lower energy costs, is at best a laughable attempt to retrofit his platform to address everyday concerns. “If affordability is your issue, VOTE REPUBLICAN!” he declared, despite rising energy costs contradicting his baseless claims.

This pivot isn’t just superficial; it’s fundamentally misleading. Trump’s assertion that energy and grocery costs are “way down” flies in the face of reality, where both continue to burden the American populace. The claim that a Thanksgiving dinner in 2025 under his aegis would be 25% cheaper is not only unsubstantiated but outright deceptive, given Walmart’s cost-cutting measures that had nothing to do with his policies.

The Rhetorical vs. The Real

Trump’s newfound fixation with “affordability” and his audacious claim of its novelty is a clear indication of his and the GOP’s detachment from the core issues facing Americans. The rhetorical shift is nothing more than a smokescreen, an attempt to rebrand without addressing the substantive failures of his administration and the Republican Party in tackling the cost-of-living crisis.

“Affordability is our goal,” he asserted, yet his administration’s track record speaks volumes of a different reality — one where affordability for the average American seemed to be far from the priority list. This rhetorical pivot is a feeble attempt to reclaim a narrative lost to the realities of everyday Americans struggling to make ends meet.

Democratic Response and Electoral Success

The Democrats’ electoral success, as Benen points out, wasn’t a mere consequence of highlighting the GOP’s failures on affordability. It was their ability to offer genuine alternatives and solutions to the pressing cost-of-living issues that resonated with the voters. In contrast, the GOP, under Trump’s lead, seems to have resorted to a strategy of verbal gymnastics — using the word “affordability” without a substantive plan to back it up.

Conclusion

Trump’s attempt to co-opt the term “affordability” post a significant electoral loss is a stark reminder of his and the GOP’s disconnect with the American people’s needs and struggles. It’s a desperate strategy, one that hopes to mask the absence of real policies and solutions with hollow rhetoric. As we dissect these claims and unravel the truth behind the bombast, it’s clear that Trump’s discovery of “affordability” is nothing more than a rhetorical ploy, bereft of substance and disconnected from the real challenges facing Americans today.

In the end, the GOP’s and Trump’s sudden focus on affordability is not just a failure of policy but a failure of understanding, empathy, and genuine engagement with the electorate’s needs. The lesson here is clear: You can change the narrative, but without changing the substance, it’s all just empty words.
