When Tuesday night, November 4 arrived, Democratic strategists were on pins and needles as they awaited returns in 2025’s off-year elections. And there was a lot of good news for Democrats, from double-digit victories in gubernatorial races in Virginia and New Jersey to landslide wins in three Pennsylvania Supreme Court retention votes.

Those elections were widely viewed as a referendum on Donald Trump’s second presidency, giving Democrats some optimism about the 2026 midterms.

Now, Democratic and GOP strategists are paying close attention to a U.S. House special election in Tennessee that will be held this Tuesday, December 2. With former Rep. Mark Green having retired from Congress, the election finds Democratic State Rep. Aftyn Behn up against Republican Matt Van Epps.

Reporting for USA Today in late November, reporters Joey Garrison and Zac Anderson explain, “Fresh off Democrats’ domination in the off-year elections, a congressional race in Tennessee has become an unlikely test for whether a national blue wave is building that could produce a seismic shakeup in next year’s midterm election.”

Behn and Van Epps are competing in a very GOP-leaning congressional district. Yet Behn is surprisingly competitive in polls.

“Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District was never supposed to be competitive,” Garrison and Anderson report. “It’s the creation of Tennessee’s Republican-controlled legislature, which drew its boundaries in 2022 by splitting Democrat-stronghold Nashville into three districts, each dominated by conservative rural counties and different Middle Tennessee suburbs. One was District 7, which includes parts of Nashville’s Davidson County and 13 other countries.”

The USA Today reporters add, “Trump won Tennessee’s Congressional District 7 by 22 percentage points over Kamala Harris in the 2024 election. But both Democrats and Republicans are bracing for a close election next week. A poll from Emerson College Polling/The Hill found Van Epps leading by only 2 percentage points, 48 percent to 46 percent, within the survey’s margin of error. Even if Behn doesn’t win the race — something that would have been unthinkable a few weeks ago — a defeat by single-digits could signal major trouble for Republicans in the 2026 midterms.”

Our Analysis:

The Tennessee Bellwether: A Prelude to Political Upheaval?

In an era where political narratives can pivot on the head of a pin, the upcoming special election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District is shaping up to be less of a mere vote count and more of a seismic predictor for the 2026 midterms. The recent USA Today report by Joey Garrison and Zac Anderson provides a compelling overview, yet beneath their analysis lies a deeper story of political strategy, voter sentiment, and the ever-looming shadow of Donald Trump’s presidency.

The Democrats’ Surprising Surge

The Democratic Party, buoyed by significant victories in what many considered bellwether elections across Virginia, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania, is now eyeing Tennessee with a mix of hope and incredulity. Aftyn Behn, a Democratic State Rep., stands as a testament to the party’s newfound vigor, challenging Republican Matt Van Epps in a district so gerrymandered, it could have served as a case study in political engineering.

The real story here isn’t just the election; it’s the fact that this election is competitive at all. Tennessee’s 7th, as Garrison and Anderson note, was designed to be a Republican stronghold, a district where Democrats were meant to tread water, not make waves. Yet here we are, with Behn polling within the margin of error against Van Epps. This doesn’t just hint at a possible blue wave; it screams of a political landscape in flux, where voter disillusionment with the status quo is becoming increasingly palpable.

Gerrymandering: A Double-Edged Sword

The creation of Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District is a perfect example of gerrymandering’s double-edged sword. In their zeal to dilute Democratic influence in Nashville, Tennessee’s Republican-controlled legislature may have inadvertently created a battleground district. This should serve as a cautionary tale: manipulate the political map too much, and you might just lose your way.

The fact that Trump’s 22 percentage point victory in 2024 over Kamala Harris is now a mere footnote ahead of this special election speaks volumes. Garrison and Anderson capture the essence of this shift, but fail to drive home the irony: a district carved out for easy Republican victories is now a nail-biter.

The Implications Are Clear

Even if Behn loses, a close race in such a district is a damning indictment of the GOP’s current strategy and perhaps its overall direction. The article hints at this but tends to bury the lede under the specifics of polling data and candidate profiles. The broader narrative is about more than just an election; it’s about a potential realignment of American political dynamics.

As we approach this critical juncture, the message to both Democrats and Republicans should be clear: underestimate the voter’s capacity for change at your peril. This Tennessee election is not just a local affair; it’s a microcosm of national sentiment, a referendum on gerrymandering, and a litmus test for the 2026 midterms.

The USA Today report provides a solid overview, but perhaps misses an opportunity to fully explore the undercurrents of dissatisfaction and desire for change that have made this election so competitive. As political analysts, we must look beyond the surface to understand the profound shifts occurring within the American electorate. Tennessee’s 7th may just be the canary in the coal mine.
