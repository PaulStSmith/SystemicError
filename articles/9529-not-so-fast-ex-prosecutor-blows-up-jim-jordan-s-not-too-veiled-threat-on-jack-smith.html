'Not so fast': Ex-prosecutor blows up Jim Jordan's 'not-too-veiled' threat on Jack Smith

U.S. Rep. Jim Jordan (R-OH) speaks during a House Oversight Committee hearing with U.S. governors about state policies regarding undocumented migrants, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

U.S. Rep. Jim Jordan (R-OH) recently sent a “not-too-veiled” threat to ex-Special Counsel Jack Smith, and one former federal prosecutor has a message for him: “Not so fast.”

Ex-prosecutor Joyce Vance wrote on Substack about Jordan and other Republicans, who are accusing Smith of operating the prosecution of Trump in an inappropriate manner. Specifically, GOP lawmakers have protested against reports that Smith had sought their call information in connection with an investigation into Jan. 6.

“ABC reported today that the House Judiciary Committee wants to have former special counsel Jack Smith testify—behind closed doors—about investigating the Mar-a-Lago, January 6, and Donald Trump. Jim Jordan, the Ohio Republican who chairs the Committee, wants an interview by October 28. He is calling for Smith to turn over documents and communications too,” Vance wrote. “Why now? Last week, there was reporting (very unsurprising to anyone who has ever investigated a federal case) that Smith’s probe obtained phone records regarding a number of Republican lawmakers as part of the January 6 case investigation. Jordan wrote to Smith, ‘As the Committee continues its oversight, your testimony is necessary to understand the full extent to which the Biden-Harris Justice Department weaponized federal law enforcement.’”

But Vance had a message for Jordan and others: “Not so fast.”

“Not so fast, though. Obtaining phone records means getting call information—that can mean which phone number called which other phone number, when, and possibly, how long the call lasted. It’s easy to understand why prosecutors would want that information in virtually any case they’re investigating,” according to Vance. “Here, given reports that Trump had numerous calls leading up to and on January 6 (for instance, one with brand new Alabama Senator Tommy Tuberville), it would be surprising if they hadn’t done so.”

Vance went on to say that Jordan’s allegation that this is the weaponization of the DOJ “should fall on deaf ears.”

“Jack Smith was investigating one of the most serious situations our country has ever faced—an effort to interfere with the smooth transfer of power between two American administrations, with involvement by the outgoing president who had lost the election—using routine investigative techniques,” the ex-prosecutor wrote. “Jordan and other Republicans should be able to differentiate between that and wiretaps, since these are statutory creatures and Congress sets the requirements for when they can be used.”

She added, “Jordan admonished Smith that he was ‘ultimately responsible for the prosecutorial misconduct and constitutional abuses of your office,’ a comment that is a not-too-veiled threat in the era of revenge prosecutions.”

Our Analysis:

The Theatre of the Absurd: Jim Jordan’s Misguided Crusade

In a recent maneuver that reeks of political theatrics more akin to a McCarthy-era witch hunt than a legitimate inquiry, U.S. Representative Jim Jordan (R-OH) has launched a not-too-veiled threat against former Special Counsel Jack Smith. This act is not just a spectacle; it’s a masterclass in misdirection and misinformation, aimed at undermining the integrity of the legal process and, by extension, the foundations of democracy itself.

Joyce Vance, a former federal prosecutor and a voice of reason in these turbulent times, penned a sharp rebuttal on Substack. Vance’s analysis cuts through the political noise with the precision of Occam’s razor, laying bare the absurdity of Jordan’s accusations and the Republican chorus backing him.

The Allegation: A Cloak of Righteous Indignation

At the heart of Jordan’s tirade is the accusation that Jack Smith’s investigation into the events surrounding January 6 and the Mar-a-Lago documents case represents a weaponization of the Department of Justice (DOJ) against Donald Trump. Specifically, Jordan’s ire was drawn by reports that Smith had sought call information from GOP lawmakers as part of his investigation—a standard practice in virtually any federal investigation, yet portrayed as an egregious overreach.

The Reality: Standard Investigative Procedure

Vance’s response to Jordan’s bombastic claims is both simple and devastating: obtaining phone records is Investigative Procedures 101. This is not the shadowy overreach of a politicized DOJ but the basic due diligence of any serious investigation, especially one concerning potential interference with the United States’ democratic processes.

The notion that investigating calls made and received by key figures around January 6 constitutes a weaponization of justice is not just flawed; it’s fundamentally dishonest. Vance highlights the necessity of such investigative steps in the context of attempting to understand the full scope of the events leading to and occurring on January 6. This was, after all, an unprecedented assault on the Capitol, with implications that continue to reverberate through the halls of power.

The Weaponization Claim: A Hollow Argument

Vance aptly notes that Jordan’s attempt to frame the investigation’s routine aspects as nefarious acts should fall on deaf ears. The differentiation between the statutory requirements for obtaining call records and more intrusive measures like wiretaps is a crucial one, yet it’s a distinction that Jordan and his allies seem either willfully ignorant of or deliberately obfuscating.

A Not-Too-Veiled Threat: The Echoes of Authoritarianism

Jordan’s admonition that Smith is responsible for prosecutorial misconduct and constitutional abuses is, as Vance points out, a thinly veiled threat in an age increasingly characterized by revenge politics and prosecutions. This is not just an attack on Smith but a broader attempt to intimidate and undermine the legal professionals and institutions holding the line for justice and accountability.

Conclusion: The Need for Discernment

What Joyce Vance lays bare is the essential truth that Jim Jordan’s campaign against Jack Smith is not about justice, oversight, or the protection of civil liberties. It’s a calculated act of political theatre designed to discredit the DOJ, dilute the gravity of January 6, and deflect scrutiny from the real issues at hand.

In an age where misinformation can spread unchecked, Vance’s voice is a beacon of clarity. It reminds us of the importance of discernment, the need to question the narratives being sold to us, and the imperative to defend the institutions and processes that underpin American democracy. In the face of such brazen attempts to rewrite the narrative of January 6 and to undermine the legal pursuit of truth and accountability, we must remain vigilant, informed, and, above all, critical of those who would trade our democratic principles for political gain.
