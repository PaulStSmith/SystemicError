After midnight Monday — and seemingly out of nowhere — Donald Trump lashed out at the New York Times with a claim the venerable paper should be sued by people who chose to stay out of the stock market since 2016 because of the newspaper — and a former columnist's influence.

The president began by focusing his ire on Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman, apparently unaware that the columnist left the Times several months ago. Krugman wrote at the time on The Contrarian, "If you check out my Substack, you will see that I have by no means run out of energy or topics to write about. But from my perspective, the nature of my relationship with the Times had degenerated to a point where I couldn’t stay."

Regardless of the widely publicized departure, Trump took to Truth Social in the early hours of Monday to write, "Paul Krugman of the New York Times has been predicting Doom and Gloom ever since my great election success in 2016. In other words, he has been wrong for YEARS, as ALL markets have been hitting new HIGHS, and are now higher than ever before."

He then added, "People stayed out of the 'BEST MARKET IN HISTORY' because of this Trump Deranged BUM. Sue them!"

Our Analysis:

Trump's Midnight Meltdown: A Farcical Attack on Facts and Figures

In an episode that reads like a script from a political satire, former President Donald Trump has once again unleashed his infamous wrath on Twitter's doppelganger, Truth Social, targeting both the New York Times and Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman. The spectacle unfolded after midnight on Monday, a time perhaps more suited to contemplation rather than capricious attacks on the press. Yet, in what appears to be a blend of misinformation and misplaced blame, Trump's tirade offers a perfect case study in the art of deflecting responsibility and distorting reality.

The Misdirected Ire

Trump, in his characteristic disregard for up-to-date information, lambasted Paul Krugman, seemingly oblivious to the fact that Krugman had parted ways with the New York Times months before. Krugman's departure, which was anything but clandestine, highlighted a divergence in the nature of his relationship with the Times, pushing him towards the autonomy of Substack. This critical detail, however, did not deter Trump from his diatribe, a testament to either his refusal or inability to engage with current realities.

The Krugman Conundrum

Krugman, a figure who has long been a thorn in the side of Trump and his administration, is lambasted for his "Doom and Gloom" predictions post-2016. Trump's portrayal of Krugman as a harbinger of economic apocalypse is as laughable as it is inaccurate. The markets, which are influenced by a plethora of factors far beyond the musings of a single economist, indeed experienced growth. However, to attribute market fluctuations solely to Krugman's commentary is to misunderstand the complexity of global economies. Moreover, Trump's assertion that people stayed out of the "BEST MARKET IN HISTORY" due to Krugman's influence is a gross exaggeration, if not an outright fabrication.

The Call to Legal Arms

Perhaps the most bewildering aspect of Trump's tirade is his call for legal action against the New York Times and by extension, Krugman. "Sue them!" he declares, for what he perceives as their role in deterring potential investors from the stock market. This suggestion is as frivolous as it is reflective of Trump's penchant for litigious threats against his critics. The notion that the Times, or any publication, could be legally accountable for individual investment decisions based on editorial content is both ludicrous and a dangerous precedent for freedom of the press.

Conclusion: The Farce Continues

In sum, Trump's latest outburst is a microcosm of his tenure in the political spotlight: rife with inaccuracies, personal vendettas, and a profound detachment from the principles of responsible leadership. His attack on Krugman and the New York Times is not merely an isolated incident of late-night venting; it is indicative of a broader disdain for critical journalism and informed critique.

As we dissect Trump's words, we're reminded of the importance of fact-checking, the value of dissenting voices in economics and politics, and the enduring need to challenge falsehoods emanating from positions of power. In a world ever more polarized, the role of the critic is not just necessary; it is vital to the health of our discourse and democracy.
