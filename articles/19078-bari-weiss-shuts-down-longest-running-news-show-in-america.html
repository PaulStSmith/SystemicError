Bari Weiss shuts down longest-running news show in America

By Paulo Santos

CBS News is shutting down its flagship radio network – once home to legendary newsman Edward R. Murrow – as part of its ongoing downsizing operation since the network was taken over by a billionaire ally of President Donald Trump.

Editor-in-chief and known MAGA ally Bari Weiss and president Tom Cibrowski announced Friday morning the network would be laying off dozens of employees, about 6 percent of its workforce, and shutting down CBS Radio, which provides news programming to about 700 affiliated stations around the country. The changes are effective May 22, reported the Hollywood Reporter.

“While this was a necessary decision, it was not an easy one,” Weiss and Cibrowski wrote to CBS Radio staff. “A shift in radio station programming strategies, coupled with challenging economic realities, has made it impossible to continue the service. We are sharing this announcement now to fulfill our commitments to our radio partners and affiliates, which require advance notice of the service’s conclusion.”

The radio network launched in 1928 and its World News Roundup is the longest-running news broadcast in the U.S. It hit the chopping block in the second round of cuts since Trump ally David Ellison took control of parent company last summer, and these latest layoffs reflect the decision making of Weiss, whom he personally installed to lead that division despite having no background in TV news.

“It’s no secret that the news business is changing radically, and that we need to change along with it.” Weiss and Cibrowski wrote to employees. “New audiences are burgeoning in new places, and we are pressing forward with ambitious plans to grow and invest so that we can be there for them. That means some parts of our newsroom must get smaller to make room for the things we must build to remain competitive.”

“But these are very hard choices and today is a difficult day,” they added.

Some of the network’s biggest stars, including Anderson Cooper, have left CBS News since Weiss took charge in protest of the MAGA-friendly direction she’s leading the network, especially its flagship “60 Minutes” program and CBS “Evening News,” which has seen its viewership fall below 4 million.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Shutdown of CBS Radio

The recent announcement that CBS News is shuttering its historic radio network, a decision made under the leadership of editor-in-chief Bari Weiss and president Tom Cibrowski, has ignited a flurry of commentary and speculation. With the backdrop of ownership changes and notable affiliations with political figures, the situation demands a nuanced analysis that goes beyond surface-level attributions of cause and effect.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Firstly, it’s crucial to identify where the institutional power lies in this scenario. The closure of CBS Radio is a direct consequence of decisions made by the top brass at CBS News, specifically Weiss and Cibrowski. However, the influence of David Ellison, a billionaire ally of President Donald Trump and the figure responsible for Weiss’s appointment, cannot be overlooked. Ellison’s acquisition of the parent company and subsequent leadership changes set the stage for the network’s current direction and policies.

Decision-makers: Bari Weiss and Tom Cibrowski, acting under the broader influence of David Ellison.

Framing and Responsibility

The article frames the shutdown as a response to “a shift in radio station programming strategies” and “challenging economic realities.” While economic pressures on traditional media are well-documented, the emphasis on these factors can mislead readers about the depth of the strategic considerations involved. The narrative also somewhat glosses over the impact of Weiss’s editorial direction, which aligns with a “MAGA-friendly” approach, a significant pivot that has alienated staff and potentially contributed to declining viewership and loss of talent such as Anderson Cooper.

Misdirection of Responsibility: The framing suggests external forces and market dynamics as the primary causes, underplaying the role of leadership decisions in shaping the network’s fortunes.

Criticism and Context

Criticism directed at CBS News for downsizing operations by eliminating its radio network must consider the broader context of media evolution and economic viability. However, it is equally important to scrutinize the strategic decisions made by those in power, particularly when such decisions align with political affiliations that influence editorial direction and potentially alienate portions of the audience.

The layoffs and shutdown, while attributed to economic and strategic necessities, are inseparable from the larger transformations under Weiss’s leadership. The mention of efforts to “grow and invest” to reach “new audiences” is telling—it implies a deliberate shift in target demographics and content focus, likely influenced by the political leanings of CBS News’s upper management.

Criticism Validity: While economic justifications for downsizing are not without merit, the specific outcomes in this case appear closely tied to the broader strategic realignment under Weiss and Ellison’s influence. Thus, criticism of the decision to shut down CBS Radio should account for both economic factors and the subjective strategic choices made by those in control.

Conclusion

The closure of CBS Radio is more than a story about economic hardship in traditional media; it’s a tale of how shifts in leadership and political affiliations at the top can dramatically alter the course of longstanding institutions. While it’s necessary to acknowledge the real challenges facing broadcast media, it’s equally crucial to question the motives and decisions of those who steer these media giants. In this instance, the responsibility for the network’s current trajectory lies squarely with its leadership, guided by an agenda that may not align with the legacy or the interests of its traditional audience.
