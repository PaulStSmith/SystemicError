Living in a fantasy: Trump’s biggest liability isn't his lies

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Fantasy Economy Runs on Public Denial and Private Gain

The Source Story, Briefly

The reporting behind this piece says Trump is pretending away the affordability crisis, pinning gas-price relief on Venezuelan oil, and preparing to spend part of a $400 million campaign fund on ads about his own record. It also notes a new U.S.-linked company got rights to 17 Venezuelan oil fields for 100 years, while the country’s oil infrastructure remains badly degraded and expensive to repair.

Who Actually Holds Power

This is not a story about a confused president drifting through events. It is a story about a president who still controls federal leverage, campaign money, and the official megaphone, and who is using all three to sell a fiction. Trump is the decision-maker. His advisors, who cushion his ego instead of enforcing reality, are part of the mechanism. So is the unnamed private operator tied to a 100-year oil arrangement that the public does not understand and was not asked to approve.

That matters because the article’s real subject is not delusion in the abstract. It is power exercised without accountability, then dressed up as optimism.

The Misdirection Is the Point

Calling the affordability crisis a “Democrat hoax” is not a mistake of rhetoric. It is a blame-shift. Trump is trying to convert the consequences of his own policies into a partisan hallucination manufactured by his opponents. That is not how people who are merely mistaken talk. It is how politicians evade responsibility when the facts are bad and the polling is worse.

The same trick shows up in the oil story. He talks as if Venezuelan crude can be snapped into place like a campaign prop and used to cure gas prices by election day. But the source material says the oil is tar-like, the infrastructure is weak, and the payoff would take years, not weeks. Trump is selling immediate salvation from a process that cannot possibly produce it.

The Deal Smells Like a Racket

The reporting should not be sanitized into some clever energy strategy. A U.S. government-linked arrangement giving a private operator rights to 17 oil fields for 100 years deserves suspicion, not deference. The article itself flags the obvious question: who is that operator, and what is the relationship to Trump?

Trump says taxpayers will not pay for the infrastructure rebuild. The source article reminds us he said the same thing about his ballroom, and taxpayers are now on the hook for more than $600 million. So the issue is not simply whether the oil works. The issue is whether the public is once again being promised a free lunch while private hands secure the long-term spoils.

Midterm Messaging as Self-Worship

A president with weak approval and widespread anger over affordability would normally change course. Trump is doing the opposite. According to the source, he wants to make himself the centerpiece of the GOP’s midterm message and redirect part of a $400 million war chest toward ads about his own record.

That is not strategy. It is vanity funded by institutional power. He is treating the party as a relay system for his personal mythology, even as the underlying conditions he claims to be fixing remain unresolved. The point is not to solve the problem. The point is to narrate the problem as already solved.

What This Reveals

The larger pattern is familiar: authoritarian-style politics replaces responsibility with performance, then asks supporters to treat performance as governance. Trump’s delusion is dangerous not because it is eccentric, but because it is operational. It becomes a method for laundering failure, legitimizing opaque deals, and keeping loyalists invested in a reality that serves him and costs everyone else.

The story is not that Trump is out of touch. It is that he is using the machinery of the state and the party to make truth optional, while private actors and public funds absorb the consequences.
