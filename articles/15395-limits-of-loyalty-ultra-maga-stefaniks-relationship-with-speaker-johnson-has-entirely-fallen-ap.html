'Limits of loyalty': 'Ultra MAGA' Stefanik’s relationship with Speaker Johnson has 'entirely fallen apart'

Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-NY) has reportedly been feuding with Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) since returning to Capitol Hill after she did not secure the crucial job she believed was already hers.

Just days before the Senate planned to confirm Stefanik as the next United States ambassador to the United Nations, President Donald Trump reportedly called her and asked her to withdraw.

According to aNew York Times report published Wednesday, Stefanik had already sent off her longtime chief of staff to start a new job at the State Department when she received that call. The New York Republican, who describes herself as an "ultra MAGA" supporter, had even completed a “farewell tour” of her district by that time. She had checked out schools for her son in New York City and was ready to move into "the $15 million Manhattan penthouse that comes with what is considered a fairly cushy job," per the Times.

Instead, Stefanik had to come back to Capitol Hill. She holds Johnson responsible for her predicament and is no longer hiding her disagreement with the speaker.

On Tuesday, she publicly denied his statement after the speaker said he was “having conversations” with her about the New York governor's race.

“This is not true,” she wrote on the social platform X, adding: “I have had no conversations with the Speaker regarding the [g]overnor’s race.”

Following this, Johnson corrected himself and said: “Elise is one of my closest friends."

“We haven’t specifically talked about her running for governor. She’s coming in to visit with me and it’s all good," he told reporters at the Capitol on Tuesday.

But according to the Times' report, their relationship has "collapsed."

"After Ms. Stefanik’s nomination was pulled, the speaker promised her a position back at the leadership table — in the last Congress, she served as conference chair, the No. 4 Republican — and said publicly that she would also return to the Intelligence Committee. That would require removing a Republican from the panel, to keep the number of Democrats and Republicans even," the report said.

"Ms. Stefanik’s plight seemed to crystallize in one succinct cautionary tale [on] the limits of loyalty in the MAGA universe. Even one of the president’s most stalwart defenders, an effective ally since his first impeachment trial, ultimately did not get what she had long been promised," it added.

Since her return, her relationship with Johnson has entirely fallen apart, leading to a conflict between two Trump loyalists. These feud between two House leaders is likely to escalate as the speaker attempts to advance the president’s domestic policy agenda.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Melodrama: Stefanik vs. Johnson

In the latest episode of the Republican soap opera that could very well be titled "The Limits of Loyalty: A MAGA Tale," we find Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-NY) in a feud that reads more like a Shakespearean tragedy than a political disagreement. The New York Times brings us a story of ambition thwarted, promises broken, and alliances torn asunder in the corridors of power. But let's peel back the layers of this political drama and see what's really at play.

The Plot Thickens: Stefanik, an "ultra MAGA" supporter, was on the brink of becoming the next United States ambassador to the United Nations, a position that comes with not just international prestige but also a $15 million Manhattan penthouse. One can almost picture Stefanik bidding adieu to her constituents, her bags packed for New York City, only for the rug to be pulled from under her feet by none other than President Donald Trump, the MAGA kingpin himself.

The Twist: Trump's call asking Stefanik to withdraw her nomination reads less like a strategic political maneuver and more like a scene from a reality TV show where the protagonist is blindsided. But why? The article stops short of providing a clear motive, leaving a gaping hole in the narrative. Was it a momentary whim of Trump's, or part of a larger, Machiavellian scheme? The omission is glaring and begs for speculation.

The Feud: Upon her return to Capitol Hill, Stefanik found herself at loggerheads with Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA). The dispute, marked by public denials and awkward retractions, is not just a personal vendetta but a manifestation of the deeper ideological rifts within the Republican Party. Stefanik's quick pivot from potential UN ambassador to a disillusioned House member is a stark reminder of the volatile nature of political fortune, especially within the MAGA fold.

The Fallout: Johnson's offer to reinstate Stefanik into a leadership position, necessitating the removal of another Republican from the Intelligence Committee, is a band-aid solution to a hemorrhaging wound. It's a move that reeks of desperation, an attempt to quell the unrest but one that may very well exacerbate the internal divisions.

The Moral of the Story: The Times' report frames Stefanik's ordeal as a "cautionary tale on the limits of loyalty in the MAGA universe." Indeed, Stefanik's saga is emblematic of a larger, systemic issue within the Republican Party, where loyalty to Trump is both the currency of power and the kiss of death. It's a world where promises are as malleable as Play-Doh, and where one's standing can change with the direction of the wind.

But let's not lose sight of the forest for the trees. This feud between Stefanik and Johnson, while ripe for media speculation and public intrigue, is but a symptom of the underlying malaise that afflicts the GOP. It's a party at war with itself, struggling to reconcile the traditional conservative values with the populist, often mercurial, whims of Trumpism.

In the grand scheme of things, Stefanik's thwarted UN ambitions and her subsequent squabble with Johnson are mere distractions from the substantive issues facing the country. As this MAGA melodrama unfolds, one can't help but wonder: Is this what political leadership has come to? A never-ending cycle of feuds, broken promises, and loyalty tests?

The Final Act: As the Republican Party continues to grapple with its identity crisis, the Stefanik-Johnson feud is likely to be remembered not for its unique drama but as yet another chapter in the ongoing saga of a party at a crossroads. The real question is, what comes next? Will the GOP find its way out of the wilderness, or will it continue to be consumed by its own systemic errors? Only time will tell, but one thing is certain: the path forward is fraught with more twists and turns than the most convoluted of political thrillers.
