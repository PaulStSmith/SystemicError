A doctor who served as Vice President Dick Cheney’s cardiologist has fumed at RFK Jr.’s recent advice to the American people.

The Secretary of Health and Human Services shared a video of himself and Kid Rock backing two messages to the public. The video, which was posted to Kennedy’s X account, is captioned, “I’ve teamed up with Kid Rock to deliver two simple messages to the American people: GET ACTIVE + EAT REAL FOOD.“

Jonathan Reiner, the former George W. Bush administration cardiologist, has since criticized this messaging and pointed out the major flaws RFK Jr. is currently overseeing in office.

He wrote, “There’s no one leading the CDC. We have the worst measles outbreak in 30 years. Americans don’t trust information coming from HHS. 20 million people don’t have health insurance.”

Reiner has been open with his criticism for RFK Jr. in the past, with the long-serving cardiologist aghast at the Secretary of Health’s advice and apparent lack of credibility for the position in Donald Trump’s administration.

On February 15, Reiner responded to RFK Jr’s claim that Americans should not eat foods that come in packaging. The Secretary of Health said, “If it comes from a package, you should be suspicious. If it comes from the ground, if it comes from the water, if it comes from the air, it’s probably the right thing to eat.”

Reiner replied with an image of bags of salads on a store shelf, adding, “Don’t be suspicious just because food is sold in a package. Read the label.”

Further criticism from Reiner came when RFK Jr. admitted he is not afraid of germs because he “used to snort cocaine off of toilet seats.”

The admission came during an appearance from RFK Jr. on Theo Von’s podcast. The clip prompted Reiner to write, “Not an ideal resume item for an official who directs US health policy.”

Our Analysis:

The Charade of Unqualified Leadership: RFK Jr. and the Erosion of Public Health Messaging

In a recent display that could only be described as a tragicomedy of leadership, RFK Jr., the current Secretary of Health and Human Services, has partnered with none other than Kid Rock to deliver health advice to the American populace. The advice, simplistic at its core, “GET ACTIVE + EAT REAL FOOD,” might seem innocuous or even beneficial at a glance. However, the devil, as always, is in the details—or in this case, the lack thereof, and the source it’s coming from.

Dr. Jonathan Reiner, a cardiologist with a history of serving under the George W. Bush administration, has stepped forward as a voice of reason amidst this farce. His criticisms of RFK Jr. are not only warranted but highlight a broader issue of credibility and competence within the current administration’s approach to health and science.

The Missteps of RFK Jr.

RFK Jr.’s advice and actions have been a series of unforced errors that would be comedic if their implications weren’t so dire. His recommendation against eating foods from packaging, countered by Reiner’s rational suggestion to simply “Read the label,” exemplifies a fundamental misunderstanding of modern nutrition and accessibility issues. Not everyone has the privilege to access or afford fresh, unpackaged foods, making RFK Jr.’s advice not only impractical but out of touch.

Moreover, RFK Jr.’s admission of his past drug use, specifically mentioning snorting cocaine off toilet seats, is a bewildering attempt at relatability that falls flat. It does nothing but undermine the seriousness with which the position of Secretary of Health and Human Services should be approached. Dr. Reiner’s response, highlighting this as “Not an ideal resume item for an official who directs US health policy,” is a masterclass in understatement.

The Real Issues at Hand

The spectacle of RFK Jr.’s partnership with Kid Rock and the subsequent backlash from health professionals like Dr. Reiner distract from more pressing issues. The absence of leadership at the CDC, the worst measles outbreak in 30 years, and a pervasive distrust in the HHS are all critical challenges that require immediate and competent attention. Not to mention, the fact that 20 million Americans are without health insurance is a glaring indictment of the current state of healthcare accessibility in the country.

The Bottom Line

The role of the Secretary of Health and Human Services is critical, especially in times of health crises. It demands a figure who can navigate the complexities of public health with expertise, credibility, and sensitivity. RFK Jr.’s recent actions and statements, as highlighted by Dr. Reiner, do not instill confidence in his ability to fulfill these requirements.

As the American public grapples with health misinformation, access to care, and a pandemic that has highlighted the fractures within our healthcare system, it’s clear that what we need is not more celebrity-endorsed simplicity. We need informed, evidence-based leadership that can address the nuanced realities of health and healthcare in the 21st century.

Dr. Reiner’s criticisms are not just a callout of RFK Jr.’s inadequacies but a sobering reminder of the importance of qualifications, experience, and credibility in public health leadership. In the end, the health of the nation is far too important to be left in the hands of those who see it as a stage for theatrics rather than a serious responsibility deserving of respect and competence.
