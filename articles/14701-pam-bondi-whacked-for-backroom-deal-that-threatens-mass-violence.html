Pam Bondi whacked for 'backroom deal' that threatens 'mass violence'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 19, 20255 min read

The U.S. Department of Justice on Friday announced that it settled litigation centering on "forced-reset triggers," devices that allow semiautomatic rifles to fire faster, and which gun violence prevention groups warn effectively turn semiautomatic rifles into machine guns.

Some gun reform groups said on Friday that the move effectively legalizes machine guns.

The settlement allows the sale of forced-reset triggers. Under the terms of the settlement, a manufacturer of the device, Rare Breed Triggers, will be allowed to sell the devices but will not be allowed to design them for use in a pistol, according to the Department of Justice (DOJ). The company also agreed to enforce its patent to ward off infringement.

Under former President Joe Biden, some of the devices were classified by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives in 2022 as machine guns, which are illegal under federal law.

In 2023, the DOJ sued Rare Breed Triggers, leading to a ruling that blocked the company from selling the devices. Separately, the National Association for Gun Rights sued over the DOJ's classification of the devices as machine guns. Last year, a federal judge ruled in favor of the National Association for Gun Rights and struck down the ban.

According to the Friday announcement from the DOJ, the settlement resolves both of those cases, which were on appeal.

Brady, a gun violence prevention group, condemned the settlement and said in a statement on Friday that it "benefits the gun industry, circumvents gun laws, and paves the way for mass violence."

"The Trump Administration's secret settlement with the gun lobby to permit the sale of Forced Reset Triggers will turn already deadly firearms into weapons of mass destruction," said Kris Brown, the president of Brady. "This dangerous backroom deal is not only an astonishing abuse of power, but undermines decades of sensible government gun safety policy and puts whole communities at immediate serious risk."

"The Trump administration has just effectively legalized machine guns. Lives will be lost because of his actions," said Vanessa Gonzalez, vice president of government and political affairs at Giffords, another gun violence prevention group.

"This move puts our communities in danger. Machine guns have no place on our streets," wrote the group Everytown for Gun Safety on Friday.

The settlement aligns with an executive order signed by U.S. President Donald Trump in February, which directed the attorney general to look at orders, guidance, and other actions by entities in the executive branch to "assess any ongoing infringements" of rights under the Second Amendment.

"This Department of Justice believes that the Second Amendment is not a second-class right," said Attorney General Pam Bondi in a statement on Friday

Our Analysis:

The DOJ's Settlement on Forced-Reset Triggers: A Closer Look

In what can only be described as a bewildering capitulation to the gun lobby, the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) announced a settlement last Friday that has effectively thrust the debate over gun control back into the dark ages. The settlement revolves around "forced-reset triggers," a device that, for all intents and purposes, morphs semi-automatic rifles into machines of rapid fire destruction. Gun violence prevention groups are understandably up in arms, claiming that this settlement legalizes machine guns by a backdoor, despite the sugar-coated legalese presented by the DOJ.

The Settlement's Details: A Devil's Playground

At the heart of this settlement is Rare Breed Triggers, a manufacturer now allowed to sell these controversial devices, albeit with a laughably specific caveat: they must not design them for use in a pistol. This stipulation is a fig leaf at best, offering a thin veil of regulation over a gaping wound in gun safety legislation. Furthermore, Rare Breed Triggers is tasked with enforcing its patent to prevent infringement, a move that smacks more of commercial interest protection than public safety.

A Step Backward Under the Guise of Second Amendment Rights

This settlement aligns disturbingly well with an executive order signed by President Donald Trump, which essentially tasked the attorney general with scouring for any perceived "infringements" on Second Amendment rights. Attorney General Pam Bondi's statement, asserting that the Second Amendment is not a "second-class right," rings hollow when the rights to life, safety, and peace of entire communities are trampled underfoot.

The Response from Gun Violence Prevention Groups

Organizations dedicated to preventing gun violence have rightly condemned this settlement. Brady's statement, highlighting the deal as a "dangerous backroom deal," underscores the nefarious nature of this agreement, pointing out its potential to transform "already deadly firearms into weapons of mass destruction." Similarly, Giffords and Everytown for Gun Safety have voiced their concerns, stressing the increased risk to community safety and the absurdity of making machine guns more accessible.

The Real Winners and Losers

Let's not mince words: the gun industry is the clear winner here. This settlement is less about protecting constitutional rights and more about lining the pockets of gun manufacturers and lobbyists. The losers? Every American who now faces increased danger from more lethal firearms being sold under the guise of legality.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

This settlement is not just a legal blip; it's a clarion call to all those concerned with gun safety and community well-being. It's a stark reminder of the power of the gun lobby and the need for continued vigilance and activism. The DOJ's decision to settle may have been intended to close a chapter, but for those fighting for sensible gun control, it has only turned a new, more daunting page.

In the final analysis, the DOJ's settlement is a betrayal of public trust and safety. It's a move that prioritizes the interests of the few over the welfare of the many. And it's a decision that we must not only criticize but actively work to counter through legislative efforts, public activism, and relentless advocacy for sensible gun control measures.
