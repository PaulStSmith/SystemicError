Marco Rubio exposed for ‘secret talks’ amid Trump admin’s starvation campaign

Secretary of State Marco Rubio was revealed by Axios Wednesday to be engaged in “secret talks” with the grandson of ex-Cuban President Raúl Castro, bypassing official communication channels as part of the Trump administration’s brutal campaign to starve the Caribbean nation of resources in the pursuit of regime change.

“Our position – the U.S. government’s position – is the regime has to go,” one senior Trump administration official told Axios in its report Wednesday, speaking on the condition of anonymity. “But what exactly that looks like is up to [President Donald Trump] and he has yet to decide. Rubio is still in talks with the grandson.”

While Cuba has faced crippling sanctions and an embargo imposed on it by the United States since the late 1950s, an executive order Trump signed last month imposed even harsher penalties on nations supplying the Caribbean nation with oil, setting off a chain reaction that’s shuttered hospitals and starved people of food.

Trump openly cheered his administration’s use of starvation as a negotiating tactic on Tuesday, calling Cuba a “failed nation” that should “absolutely make a deal.” He also refused to rule out an outright attack on Cuba similar to the United States’ attack on Venezuela last month.

And on Wednesday, sources revealed to Axios that Rubio is apparently in regular contact with Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez Castro, the grandson of Raúl Castro, and the great-nephew of Fidel Castro, talks that have reportedly been “surprisingly” friendly.

“I wouldn’t call these ‘negotiations’ as much as ‘discussions’ about the future,” the senior Trump administration official told Axios.

Another source who was “familiar with the talks” told Axios that Rubio is “looking for the next Delcy in Cuba,” referring to acting Venezuelan President Delcy Rodríguez, who’s led Venezuela since the Trump administration abducted Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro last month.

“There’s no political diatribes about the past. It’s about the future,” the source told Axios. “[Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez Castro] could be straight out of Hialeah, [Florida]. This could be a conversation between regular guys on the streets of Miami.”

The United States has sought to topple Cuban’s government since the late 1950s after revolutionaries – led by the Castros and Argentine revolutionary Che Guevara – ousted the U.S.-backed dictatorFulgencio Batista, whose leadership, critics say, maintained the Caribbean nation as a “virtual slave state” to the benefit of U.S. companies.

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Ethics of Secret Diplomacy and Starvation Tactics

In a recent revelation by Axios, a bewildering scene of clandestine diplomacy unfolds, featuring Secretary of State Marco Rubio engaged in “secret talks” with none other than the grandson of ex-Cuban President Raúl Castro. This maneuver, buried deep within the Trump administration’s controversial and arguably brutal strategy aimed at Cuba, starkly illuminates the lengths to which the U.S. will go in its quest for regime change in the Caribbean nation.

The Stark Reality of Sanctions

First, let’s address the elephant in the room: the U.S. sanctions and embargo against Cuba. Initiated in the late 1950s, these sanctions have morphed into a stranglehold, tightened even further by President Trump’s executive order targeting nations that supply Cuba with oil. The consequences? Hospitals shuttered and a populace starved of food. This draconian approach to foreign policy, cheered on by Trump as a “negotiating tactic,” is not only inhumane but fundamentally flawed. The idea that starvation and suffering will lead to a beneficial deal for the people of Cuba is as misguided as it is morally reprehensible.

The Role of Marco Rubio

Enter Marco Rubio, the Secretary of State, who has been revealed to conduct “surprisingly friendly” discussions with Raúl Guillermo Rodríguez Castro. The Axios report suggests these talks are not aimed at rehashing the past but are rather focused on the future. However, the clandestine nature of these discussions raises significant questions about transparency and the true intentions behind them. Is Rubio seeking a figure akin to Delcy Rodríguez of Venezuela, aiming to install a compliable leader in Cuba as part of the U.S.’s broader strategy for regime change in the region?

The Misguided Celebration of Suffering

Trump’s open celebration of using starvation as a tool is not only alarming but a stark deviation from basic human decency. Referring to Cuba as a “failed nation” and suggesting that it should “absolutely make a deal” underlines a disturbing willingness to leverage human suffering for political gain. This stance, coupled with the refusal to rule out military attacks—similar to the assault on Venezuela—highlights a dangerous and aggressive foreign policy approach that risks escalating conflicts and perpetuating suffering.

The Historical Context

The U.S.’s obsession with toppling Cuba’s government traces back to the late 1950s, following the revolution that ousted the U.S.-backed dictator Fulgencio Batista. It’s essential to recognize that this historical context is not just a backdrop but a continuous thread influencing the current administration’s policies and actions. The portrayal of Batista’s regime as a “virtual slave state” beneficial to U.S. companies sheds light on the complex interplay of economic interests and political power that continues to shape U.S.-Cuba relations.

Conclusion

The Axios report on Rubio’s secret talks with Castro’s grandson and the Trump administration’s broader strategy towards Cuba reveal a troubling picture of U.S. foreign policy marked by secrecy, suffering, and the pursuit of regime change. This approach, characterized by sanctions that punish the Cuban people and covert negotiations that bypass standard diplomatic channels, is both ethically questionable and strategically dubious. It’s high time for a reassessment of these tactics, with a move towards transparent diplomacy and policies that prioritize human rights and stability over geopolitical maneuvering.
