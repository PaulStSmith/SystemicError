'China sees an opening': Expert says Trump's miscalculation creates a 'problem' for U.S.

While talking trade on CNN, the editorial director of the Economic Innovation Group and host and co-creator of the New Bazaar podcast, Cardiff Garcia, said President Donald Trump has given China an opening to our allies.

“So what's happening now is that there's just a standoff between the two countries?" Host Audie Cornish asked.

“I think each side is waiting for the other one to blink,” Garcia said. “And so that the de-escalation can start, right? Because in the meantime, it's going to hurt both economies. The thing to know, though, is that China has been preparing for exactly this moment since at least the first Trump term, developing a bunch of tariffs then on China. That's essentially when the trade wars really escalated. It was continued, in fairness, under Joe Biden, and now it's gone to a level that I think people just hadn't anticipated. China's been getting ready for this. It's been developing a lot of legal tools, economic tactics that it can use to harm specific American industries in response.”

Garcia later added, “So [China is] betting that they can ride it out longer than the U.S. can, and so in the meantime, there's a standoff, and yeah, people in both places are going to get hurt.”

Cornish then said, “Also, it's interesting because unlike a smaller country, a Vietnam, right, or Italy or whatever, like people being like, okay, yeah, let's talk, let's talk. We heard that quote right about ‘drinking poison’ China is talking about the U.S. as a bully that they have to stand up to, which is not like out of the realm of how other countries are seeing [the tariffs]. I think that's like an added element to the negotiation here or lack thereof."

“Well, previously part of the American strategy was precisely to develop deeper relationships with traditional U.S. allies and then essentially confront China that way to try to get it to impose changes on the way that it trades with other places,” Garcia said.

“The problem now is that China sees an opening, and now it's China that's going to traditional U.S. allies and saying, hey, we're a big trading partner of yours, too, and you can see that the U.S. is not reliable. It has imposed tariffs on you guys as well. So now let's us get together and confront the U.S. instead. So, how far will that go? I don't know right. It really depends on what happens in the next few months, but that is the strategy that they're deploying.”

Our Analysis:

The Geopolitical Chess Game: Trump, Biden, and China's Strategic Maneuvering

In a recent discussion on CNN, Cardiff Garcia, the editorial director of the Economic Innovation Group, shed light on the intricate and high-stakes trade tensions between the United States and China. Through Garcia's insights, a narrative unfolds, revealing a geopolitical chess game where patience, strategy, and the ability to endure economic discomfort are paramount. However, beneath the surface of this analysis lies a more profound implication: the shifting dynamics of international alliances and the potential reconfiguration of global economic power structures.

Trump's Opening Gambit: A Double-Edged Sword

The crux of Garcia's argument centers on President Donald Trump's aggressive trade policies towards China. Described as giving "China an opening to our allies," Trump's actions, intended to curtail China's economic ascendancy and address trade imbalances, inadvertently set the stage for China to court traditional U.S. allies. This maneuver is not merely a reaction but a part of a long-term strategy by China, which, according to Garcia, has been "preparing for exactly this moment since at least the first Trump term."

The Biden Continuation: An Underestimated Escalation

While it's convenient to lay the blame solely at Trump's feet, Garcia makes it clear that the trade war's escalation continued under Joe Biden's administration. This continuity underscores a fundamental miscalculation: the assumption that escalating trade tensions would coerce China into submission. Instead, what we've observed is China's strategic patience and its readiness to deploy "legal tools, economic tactics" aimed at fracturing the economic bulwark the U.S. has attempted to consolidate among its allies.

China's Countermove: Exploiting U.S. Reliability

Garcia highlights a critical shift in the narrative — China positioning itself as a reliable alternative to the U.S. for global trade partnerships. By approaching traditional U.S. allies, who have also felt the sting of American tariffs, China is not just seeking to dilute U.S. influence but is also challenging the very foundation of post-World War II economic order. The irony is palpable; the United States, once the architect of global trade networks and alliances, is now perceived as a disruptor, if not a direct threat, to the stability of these systems.

The Standoff: A Test of Endurance and Strategy

The current standoff between the U.S. and China, as Garcia articulates, is more than just a waiting game. It's a test of strategic endurance and the ability to bear economic pain for a potentially greater geopolitical payoff. The notion that China is "betting that they can ride it out longer than the U.S. can" speaks volumes about the changing dynamics of global power. It's a daring bet, predicated on the belief that economic resilience and strategic alliances can outlast the fluctuations of American political and economic policies.

A Reflection on Strategy and Missteps

What we're witnessing, through the lens of Garcia's analysis, is a pivotal moment in global trade and politics. The U.S.'s approach to China, marked by a blend of aggression and inconsistency, has not yielded the desired results. Instead, it has opened a Pandora's box of geopolitical realignments and economic recalibrations. The lesson here is clear: in the realm of international relations, actions driven by short-term gains or domestic political considerations can have long-lasting implications that reshape global alliances and economic landscapes.

In conclusion, Garcia's commentary not only provides a snapshot of the current U.S.-China trade tensions but also serves as a cautionary tale about the complexities of geopolitical strategy. The unfolding narrative is a stark reminder that in the game of international politics, there are no simple moves, and the consequences of miscalculation can extend far beyond the tenure of any single administration.
