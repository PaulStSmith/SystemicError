‘Eviscerate due process’

According to the Council on Criminal Justice, here are some key cuts and reductions reported in May of this year:

  • In April, Trump terminated 373 grants from the DOJ’s Office of Justice Programs (OJP) amounting to more than $300 million after $500 million was restored by a judicial decision.

  • These terminated grants provided federal support for violence reduction, policing and prosecution, victims’ services, juvenile justice and child protection, substance use and mental health treatment, corrections and reentry, justice systems enhancements, research and evaluation, and other state- and local-level public safety functions.

  • The cuts affected grantees from mostly not-for-profit organizations across 37 red and blue states alike as well as in urban, suburban, and rural areas.

  • About 40% of those cuts involved references to diversity, equity, race, gender and so on, while the deepest cuts were to organizations that provided training and technical assistance to OJP grantees.

With respect to D.C., we learned from several news outlets including Fox News that three days after White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt announced the formation of the Making D.C.     Our Analysis:

    The Unabashed Chronicle of a Despot: Trump's Second Reign

    In the world of political analysis, rarely does one encounter a piece as unflinching and damning as the one penned by Gregg Barak, emeritus professor of criminology and criminal justice. His portrayal of Donald J. Trump's administration post-2024 election victory is less a critique and more a scathing indictment of a regime that seems to revel in its disdain for democratic norms and the rule of law. Let's dissect this article's core claims with the precision of a scalpel, understanding the implications of a second Trump term through the lens of criminological expertise.

    Donald J. Trump: A Career Criminal at the Helm

    Barak doesn't mince words, labeling Trump as a "career criminal," a moniker that would be shocking if it weren't so meticulously backed by evidence. The depiction of Trump as the "Teflon Don" or the "Houdini of White-collar Crime" isn't just clever wordplay; it's an assertion grounded in the former president's historical and continuous evasion of accountability. It's a bold claim, yet Barak supports it by pointing to a pattern of behavior that spans decades, culminating in an administration that allegedly operates more like an organized crime family than a traditional presidential leadership.

    Dictatorial Lawlessness and Amplified Corruption

    The article claims that Trump's second term has seen an escalation in "dictatorial lawlessness" and corruption. This isn't just about the misuse of presidential powers; it's about the erosion of democratic safeguards and the blatant disregard for the principles of justice and equality. The deployment of the National Guard in Los Angeles and D.C., as described by Barak, serves as a prime example of this authoritarian overreach, supposedly aimed more at demonstrating power and instilling fear than at addressing any genuine security concerns.

    An Attack on the Public Commons

    Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Barak's analysis is the assertion that the Trump administration has actively sought to divide the nation and erode public rights across the board. From civil rights to environmental rights, the article paints a picture of a concerted effort to dismantle the very foundations of American democracy. The cuts to the DOJ's Office of Justice Programs, as highlighted by Barak, are not just policy decisions; they are portrayed as direct attacks on the mechanisms that uphold justice and public safety.

    The Allegations of Corruption and Lawlessness

    Barak's article is replete with allegations of corruption and lawlessness, ranging from undermining oversight and conflict of interest to outright suppression of dissent. These claims are presented not just as isolated incidents but as part of a systematic attempt to consolidate power and enrich the president and his allies. The staggering figure of $3.4 billion, purportedly earned by Trump since his 2017 inauguration, is presented as evidence of profiteering at the expense of the nation.

    The Verdict

    In analyzing Gregg Barak's article, one can't help but be struck by the depth of concern for the state of American democracy. The piece is not just a critique of a president or an administration; it's a dire warning about the potential death knell of democratic principles under the weight of authoritarianism and corruption.

    While some may argue that Barak's tone borders on the hyperbolic, the evidence he presents paints a disturbing picture of a presidency untethered from accountability and a nation at the mercy of its leader's whims. It's a compelling, if unsettling, analysis that demands not just attention but action from all who hold dear the values of justice, equality, and democracy.

    In the end, Barak's article serves as a clarion call to recognize, resist, and rectify the systemic erosion of democratic norms and the rule of law. Whether one agrees with his assessment or not, the implications of ignoring such warnings could be far-reaching, not just for the United States but for the global order at large.
