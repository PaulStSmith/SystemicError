Fiona Hill, who served as Trump’s senior director for European and Russian affairs during his first term, gave a grim outlook on Trump’s ongoing efforts to end the war in Ukraine, telling Politico Monday that Trump had “completely ceded narrative control to (Russian President Vladimir) Putin.”

The White House was quick to fire back at Hill, labeling her as “deranged” and mocking her credibility.

“(Hill is the) go-to Trump deranged ‘expert’ on Russia,” said White House spokesperson Anna Kelly, speaking with Politico. “President Trump has done more to deliver peace in seven months than Joe Biden did in four years, and he looks forward to discussing a potential peace agreement with President Zelenskyy and European leaders tomorrow.”

Trump is expected to meet today with Zelenskyy in Washington, D.C., just days after meeting with Putin in Alaska on Friday in a meeting that has left some European leaders fearful that “Trump’s affinities with Russia” and “his admiration for Putin” may bode poorly for Ukraine.

Geopolitical experts have also raised alarms that Trump may accept a less-than-ideal agreement to bring about a quick end to the war, one motivated, in part, by the president’s longstanding aspiration of being awarded a Nobel Peace Prize. Hill considers herself among those experts, and expanded on her fears Monday to Politico.

“It’s clear that Putin intends to take as much territory by force and kill as many Ukrainian forces and civilians as he can so that Ukraine is in an even weaker position when the time eventually comes to sit down,” Hill said.

“Then Putin might decide to ‘bank’ his territorial gains, and bog the Europeans and U.S. down in peacemaking/keeping operations in support of … Ukraine, while he applies other forms of pressure to undermine Zelenskyy and Ukraine. Imagine the chaos he can sow in an enforced Ukrainian election as part of a peace deal. He will diminish Ukraine one way or another.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Dubious Path to "Peace" in Ukraine: A Critical Analysis

The recent uproar surrounding President Donald Trump's upcoming meeting with Ukrainian President Volodmyr Zelenskyy has once again thrown the spotlight on the intricate and often murky waters of international diplomacy. However, the White House's explosive reaction to criticism, particularly towards Fiona Hill, a former Trump administration official, reveals much more than just a simple disagreement over foreign policy strategies.

Fiona Hill's Warning: Hill's commentary, as reported by Politico, paints a bleak picture of Trump's approach to ending the war in Ukraine. Her assertion that Trump has "completely ceded narrative control to (Russian President Vladimir) Putin" is not just a throwaway critique. It's a damning indictment of Trump's foreign policy posture, suggesting a dangerous deference to Putin that could have severe implications for Ukraine and broader European security. The White House's retort, branding Hill as "deranged," is not only a grossly unprofessional personal attack but also a transparent attempt to deflect from the substance of her concerns.

The White House's Response: The statement by White House spokesperson Anna Kelly, as quoted in the article, is a masterpiece of deflection and distortion. Kelly's claim that "President Trump has done more to deliver peace in seven months than Joe Biden did in four years" is a bold assertion, yet it conspicuously lacks any grounding in the complex realities of international diplomacy or the specific dynamics of the Ukraine conflict. Such statements are designed to muddy the waters and shift the focus away from legitimate critiques of Trump's foreign policy.

Trump's Meetings with Putin and Zelenskyy: The article highlights the apprehension surrounding Trump's meetings with both Putin and Zelenskyy. The fear that Trump's "affinities with Russia" and "his admiration for Putin" could undermine Ukraine's position is not unfounded. Trump's previous encounters with Putin have raised eyebrows, with critics often accusing Trump of being overly conciliatory to the Russian leader. The potential for a "less-than-ideal agreement" to end the war, driven by Trump's personal ambitions for a Nobel Peace Prize, should raise alarm bells for anyone concerned with a just and lasting resolution to the conflict.

The Bigger Picture: Hill's expanded fears, as reported, underscore a critical point: the danger of Russia using force and intimidation to weaken Ukraine before any peace negotiations. Her concerns about Putin "banking" his territorial gains and the subsequent chaos he could sow in Ukraine are not just hypothetical scenarios. They reflect a strategic pattern of behavior by Russia in its dealings with neighbors and in its broader geopolitical strategy.

Conclusion

The White House's attack on Fiona Hill and its attempts to paint Trump's foreign policy in a positive light do not hold up under scrutiny. The real issue at hand is the potential consequences of Trump's approach to the Ukraine conflict and his interactions with Putin. The concerns raised by geopolitical experts, including Hill, are based on a sober assessment of the situation and deserve serious consideration, not derision or dismissal.

In navigating these turbulent geopolitical waters, it's crucial that policy decisions are grounded in a realistic understanding of the actors involved and the potential outcomes of their actions. The pursuit of peace in Ukraine, and indeed anywhere, should not be driven by personal accolades or political posturing but by a commitment to justice, sovereignty, and the well-being of all peoples involved.
