Shaniqua McClendon, vice president of political strategy for Crooked Media, and Ashley Pratte Oates, senior vice president at The Herald Group, joined MSNBC's Alex Witt on "Alex Witt Reports" on Sunday to discuss the impact of Trump's moves against Mamdani.

Recently, media reports indicate that Trump has offered a job in his administration to Eric Adams, the incumbent Mayor of New York City, to help clear the field for Adams to face off against Andrew Cuomo, the city's former mayor. Trump has also reportedly been helping Cuomo behind the scenes.

Oates described the situation as a "lose-lose" scenario for Cuomo.

"This might start to show that Trump's influence isn't as strong as he thinks it is," Oates said. "Cuomo's team saw this as a disadvantage because he would be associated as Trump's pick. This was a lose-lose situation and the only clear winner was going to be Mamdani."

McClendon argued that Trump's efforts to meddle in the New York City mayoral race show how afraid he is of Mamdani.

"Donald Trump is afraid of what Mamdani will be able to do to him as mayor," McClendon said. "If Donald Trump knows anything, he knows optics and media presence. Him going back and forth with Mamdani is not going to be the same as going back and forth with Chuck Schumer or Hakeem Jeffries. [Mamdani] is going to bring it to him."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Fear of Mamdani: A Closer Look

In the latest chapter of political theater, President Donald Trump's maneuvers against New York City mayoral candidate Zohran Mamdani have been laid bare, revealing a tangled web of fear, strategy, and potential self-sabotage. Shaniqua McClendon and Ashley Pratte Oates, two astute analysts, provided their insights on MSNBC, shedding light on the desperation seeping out of Trump's strategies.

The Trump Maneuver: Too Clever by Half?

Trump's political machinations have always been as subtle as a sledgehammer, but his latest ploy—offering a job to incumbent Mayor Eric Adams to supposedly clear the path for Andrew Cuomo—reeks of desperation. Trump's meddling is a classic chess move, but in a game where the pieces don't move according to his will. This isn't 4D chess; it's a toddler playing with checkers on a chessboard.

Ashley Pratte Oates aptly describes the situation as a "lose-lose" scenario for Cuomo. Here's the kicker: If Trump's influence was a currency, it would be rapidly depreciating. The fact that Cuomo's team sees an association with Trump as a disadvantage speaks volumes. It's not just a sign of the times; it's a flashing neon billboard declaring Trump's waning influence.

Mamdani: The Real Winner?

The fear of Zohran Mamdani articulated by Shaniqua McClendon is particularly telling. Trump, a man who has built an empire on bravado and media manipulation, is purportedly quaking in his boots at the prospect of going toe-to-toe with Mamdani. Why? Because Mamdani isn't just another politician for Trump to bulldoze with tweets and tirades. Mamdani represents a new breed of political fighter, one who, according to McClendon, "is going to bring it to him."

This isn't just about policy or politics. It's about presence. Trump's entire modus operandi relies on dominating the media landscape, but in Mamdani, he potentially faces an opponent who can not only match but surpass him in this arena. The notion of Trump fearing Mamdani isn't just plausible; it's a testament to Mamdani's perceived power.

The Bottom Line

Trump's actions reveal a man scrambling to maintain relevance in a political landscape that's shifting beneath his feet. Attempting to play kingmaker in the New York City mayoral race, Trump has inadvertently highlighted his own vulnerabilities and the strength of his opponents. In trying to undermine Zohran Mamdani, Trump has unwittingly amplified his significance.

This is more than just a political skirmish; it's a harbinger of the battles to come. If Mamdani's candidacy is strong enough to send Trump into a strategic tailspin, what does that say about the future of politics in New York City—and beyond? One thing is clear: Trump's fear of Mamdani is not just about the mayoral race. It's about the changing tide of American politics, and Trump is finding himself adrift.
