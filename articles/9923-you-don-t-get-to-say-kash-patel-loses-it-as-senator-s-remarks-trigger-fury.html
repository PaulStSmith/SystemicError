'You don't get to say!' Kash Patel loses it as senator's remarks trigger fury

Sen. Cory Booker (D-N.J.) and FBI Director Kash Patel (Photo: Screen capture)

Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) triggered FBI Director Kash Patel into a rage during the Senate Judiciary Committee hearing on Tuesday when he pointed out that key agents working on important task forces involving terrorism and even child trafficking have been diverted to work instead on immigration matters.

Booker said that Patel has made the U.S. “less safe” — which sent Patel into a fury.

“Donald Trump promised to make us all safer. You have pushed out senior FBI agents with decades of knowledge and experience. In fact, this is the first time in FBI history that neither the director nor the deputy director have any experience with the FBI. You’ve shifted the agency’s priorities primarily to pursuing Donald Trump’s immigration enforcement agenda,” said Booker.

“According to ICE data, the Cato Institute, which is a conservative think tank, found that 20% of all agents have been diverted from their work to do assist ICE immigration enforcement,” Booker continued.

He then questioned whether FBI agents who investigate crimes against children have been assigned to immigration enforcement.

Patel employed a tactic during the hearing, objecting to the “premise” of the question whenever he chose not to answer, which Booker charged.

“You don’t get to say that America is not safer under this leadership!” Patel yelled as the hearing chairman, Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-IA) slammed his gavel. “America is safer because of the work of the FBI!”

The two men yelled over each other for several minutes until Grassley silenced them, with a telling off for Booker.

Booker ultimately told Patel, “I don’t think you’re long for your job.”

“You are an embarrassment to the division of this country!” Patel said, telling Booker, “Your time is over.”

“Sir, you don’t tell me my time is over! The people of New Jersey tell me when my time is — you can’t lecture me!” Booker yelled.

“I’m not going anywhere!” Patel yelled back.

“I am not afraid of you!” Booker maintained.

Our Analysis:

The Booker-Patel Showdown: A Disturbing Reflection of Priorities and Professionalism

In an electrifying exchange that could only be described as a political theater at its most raw, Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) and FBI Director Kash Patel turned a routine Senate Judiciary Committee hearing into a battleground, exposing deep fissures in the nation’s law enforcement priorities and the volatile nature of political discourse. This confrontation, far from being a mere squabble, lays bare the concerning redirection of FBI resources and a troubling departure from experienced leadership under Patel’s tenure.

A Dangerous Pivot: Terrorism and Child Trafficking vs. Immigration

Booker’s allegations that the FBI has diverted significant resources from critical areas such as terrorism and child trafficking to immigration enforcement is not just alarming—it’s a damning indictment of misplaced priorities. By Booker’s account, backed by data from the conservative Cato Institute, a staggering 20% of all agents have been reassigned to assist ICE in immigration matters. This startling figure suggests a profound shift in focus from the FBI’s traditional core missions, ostensibly to align with Donald Trump’s immigration enforcement agenda.

Patel’s reaction to Booker’s critique was not just a display of fury but a manifestation of the defensiveness that often accompanies flawed policy decisions. Patel’s attempt to sidestep Booker’s queries by objecting to the “premise” of the questions rather than addressing the substance is a tactic as old as politics itself—yet its use here does little to reassure the public about the agency’s direction or priorities.

Leadership and Experience in Question

Booker’s point that “this is the first time in FBI history that neither the director nor the deputy director have any experience with the FBI” is a red flag that cannot be ignored. The implications of having the top echelons of the FBI devoid of seasoned agency experience are profound, potentially undermining the Bureau’s effectiveness and eroding institutional knowledge.

A Clash of Visions and Values

The heated exchange between Booker and Patel transcended the typical political banter to reveal a deeper clash of visions and values. Patel’s assertion that “America is safer because of the work of the FBI,” while theoretically true, misses the point Booker was making: Is America truly safer when resources are diverted from combating terrorism and protecting children to enforcing immigration policies?

Professionalism Under Fire

The decorum—or lack thereof—displayed during the hearing, especially as Patel told Booker, “Your time is over,” and Booker’s retort, “Sir, you don’t tell me my time is over! The people of New Jersey tell me when my time is — you can’t lecture me!” speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse. Such exchanges, while they might make for compelling television, do little to inspire confidence in the professionalism or the gravity with which these issues are being addressed.

Conclusion: The Real Losers

In the end, the real losers of this political spectacle are the American people, particularly those who rely on the FBI to prioritize their safety over political agendas. The Booker-Patel showdown, while illuminating, leaves us with more questions than answers about the direction of the FBI and the safety of the nation. As the dust settles, one thing is clear: a reassessment of priorities is desperately needed, with a focus on restoring the FBI’s commitment to its core missions. Anything less is a disservice to the American public and the dedicated agents of the FBI.
