'That's going to be a problem': Jim Jordan menaces Jack Smith before secretive hearing

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 18, 20254 min read

Former special counsel Jack Smith is scheduled to testify in a closed-door session before the House Judiciary Committee on Wednesday. Committee Chair Jim Jordan (R-OH) signaled his antagonistic approach when speaking with reporters ahead of the hearing.

Smith had requested a public hearing, which Jordan declined. The closed-door session will focus on Smith’s investigations into Donald Trump, which were discontinued following the president’s re-election.

According to Politico, Smith faces significant constraints regarding what he can disclose to the committee. Reporters Hailey Fuchs and Kyle Cheney note that these limitations could provoke confrontations with Republicans seeking to place Smith in legal jeopardy. “Smith must navigate Byzantine secrecy laws and rules that limit what he can disclose to lawmakers. All the while, Republicans are looking to trip him up and incriminate him, to portray him as a tool of a weaponized Justice Department — an allegation they’ve brandished amid recent revelations that Smith obtained phone records of at least eight GOP senators as part of his probe into Trump’s efforts to subvert the 2020 election results.”

Jordan indicated he intends to pressure Smith regardless of legal restrictions. He told reporters, “What they did all along, everything was wrong … a lot of things that were just not normal course of investigation or prosecution. If he comes in and doesn’t answer questions, that’s going to be a problem.”

As a former DOJ prosecutor, Smith is limited in what he can reveal. While the department issued a waiver, uncertainty remains about its scope.

Additional complications arise from a Florida federal judge’s 11-month prohibition on releasing details from Smith’s classified documents investigation report. Trump has urged the judge to maintain this restriction indefinitely, further constraining what Smith can legally discuss about that probe.

House Judiciary Committee Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) countered Jordan’s approach, stating, “They are trying to get him on the fast road to one of their ridiculous prosecutions.”

Our Analysis:

The Theatre of Political Antagonism: A Closer Look at the Jack Smith Testimony

In a move that reeks of political theatre more than genuine oversight, Former Special Counsel Jack Smith is set to testify in a closed-door session before the House Judiciary Committee. The orchestration of this event, particularly by Committee Chair Jim Jordan (R-OH), reveals a disturbing glimpse into the increasingly antagonistic and theatrical nature of American politics.

The Closed-Door Decision: A Strategy of Obfuscation?

Jack Smith’s request for a public hearing was outright rejected by Jordan, opting instead for a closed-door session. This decision raises immediate red flags about transparency and accountability. Public hearings allow for a direct channel of information to the electorate, ensuring that the proceedings are not only witnessed in real time but are also subject to public scrutiny. By moving this spectacle behind closed doors, Jordan has effectively muzzled the possibility of an unfiltered narrative, ensuring that whatever emerges from this session will have passed through a partisan filter.

Legal Labyrinth and Republican Strategy

The limitations imposed on Smith’s disclosure abilities are not just procedural hurdles; they are a minefield designed to entrap. The Politico reporters, Hailey Fuchs and Kyle Cheney, aptly describe this scenario as Smith needing to “navigate Byzantine secrecy laws and rules” while facing Republicans eager to “trip him up and incriminate him.” This is a classic example of political machinations prioritizing the discrediting of opposition over the pursuit of truth. The aim here is clear: to paint Smith, and by extension the Justice Department, as partisan tools. This narrative is dangerously misleading and undermines the integrity of judicial processes.

Jordan’s Unfounded Antagonism

Jim Jordan’s comments to reporters ahead of the hearing are telling. His determination to pressure Smith, despite legal constraints, is indicative of a troubling disregard for the rule of law and procedural fairness. Jordan’s assertion that “everything was wrong” with Smith’s investigations into Donald Trump not only preempts the hearing’s findings but also signals a prejudiced approach that is unbecoming of his position.

The Trump Factor

The involvement of a Florida federal judge, at Trump’s behest, to restrict the release of details from Smith’s classified documents investigation adds another layer of complexity to this already convoluted affair. Trump’s influence looms large over this proceeding, with his efforts to indefinitely maintain these restrictions serving as a stark reminder of his ongoing impact on the political landscape. This maneuvering to shield himself from scrutiny further entangles the legal and political narratives, complicating Smith’s ability to testify freely.

A Democratic Counter

Rep. Jamie Raskin’s (D-MD) counter to Jordan’s approach sheds light on the Democrats’ perception of this hearing. Describing the Republicans’ efforts as a path to “one of their ridiculous prosecutions” underscores the deeply partisan nature of this inquiry. Raskin’s remark encapsulates the frustration and polarization that have come to define much of the contemporary political discourse in the United States.

Conclusion: Theatrics Over Truth

The Jack Smith testimony before the House Judiciary Committee, marred by restrictions, legal entanglements, and a palpable partisan divide, is emblematic of a broader issue within American politics. When political theatrics are prioritized over the pursuit of truth, the only casualty is public trust. As this spectacle unfolds behind closed doors, one must wonder: who benefits from turning the gears of justice into a partisan battleground? The answer, unfortunately, seems to be no one—least of all the American public.
