CNN anchor Brianna Keilar was interviewing Rep. Bryan Steil (R-WI) on Thursday when the conversation turned testy over cost-of-living prices, particularly groceries.

Steil tried to put blame on President Joe Biden and his administration.

“When we think about groceries, one of the biggest drivers is the cost of diesel fuel. It’s a tractor in the field during the harvest,” Steil said. “It’s a truck bringing those goods to the grocery store. The energy policy that we have put in place with the Trump administration is beginning to reduce those prices and a massive shift from where we were during the Biden administration to reduce those prices.”

Keilar pushed back.

“Where is it beginning to reduce those prices?” she asked.

“I think you see oil prices coming down dramatically,” Steil said.

“No you said groceries, you said it’s beginning to reduce those prices — groceries,” she said.

“It will flow through the process with those energy prices coming down for our farmers. I don’t offer that we are where we need to be, at the end result. I offer one of the largest drivers inside grocery prices is diesel costs,” he said.

Keilar interjected again, pressing on the issue further.

“How long will that take? Because groceries have been up every month since the start of Trump’s presidency,” Keilar asked.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Political Masterclass

In a recent fiery exchange on CNN, Rep. Bryan Steil (R-WI) and CNN anchor Brianna Keilar delved into the murky waters of cost-of-living increases and the specter of a government shutdown. The conversation, predictably, veered into a blame game with Steil pointing fingers at President Joe Biden and his administration for the soaring prices of groceries. But let’s dissect this encounter with the precision of a scalpel cutting through political rhetoric.

Steil’s Strategy: Blame and Deflect

Steil’s argument hinges on the cost of diesel fuel being a significant driver behind the rising grocery prices. He nostalgically references the energy policies of the Trump administration as the fiscal messiah that “is beginning to reduce those prices,” conveniently glossing over the current state of affairs. This is a classic move in the political playbook: credit your side for any perceived positive and blame the opposition for every negative.

The Energy Policy Mirage

Steil’s claim about the Trump administration’s energy policies reducing grocery prices is a masterclass in deflection. It’s a bold strategy, attempting to bridge the gap between diesel prices and grocery bills with a policy that has been out of action since the Biden administration took over. The assertion that these policies are now beginning to reduce prices is not just optimistic; it’s borderline fantastical without hard evidence to back it up.

Keilar’s Counterattack: Seeking Substance

Brianna Keilar, armed with facts and a healthy dose of skepticism, challenges Steil’s vague assertions. “Where is it beginning to reduce those prices?” she asks, cutting through the fluff. Steil’s response, pointing to dropping oil prices, does little to solidify his argument, especially when Keilar reminds him the discussion was about groceries, not general energy costs.

The Elusive Timeline

Perhaps the most telling moment of this exchange is when Keilar presses Steil on the timeline for these supposed benefits to materialize in the grocery aisle. Steil’s vague assurance that it will “flow through the process” is emblematic of a broader trend in political discourse: promising the moon with no plan on how to get there.

Conclusion: The Blame Game Continues

This exchange between Steil and Keilar is a microcosm of the larger political landscape, where responsibility is often a hot potato, and tangible solutions are lost in a sea of rhetoric. Steil’s attempt to pin rising grocery prices solely on the Biden administration, while harking back to the supposed golden era of Trump’s energy policies, serves more as a distraction than a solution.

In the end, the American public is left craving a substantive discussion on how to tackle the cost-of-living crisis, rather than witnessing another round of the blame game. Keilar’s persistence highlights the need for accountability and specificity in political discourse—a need that is all too often left unmet.
