Trump putting MAGA 'puppet' in charge of probe into Reflecting Pool fiasco

Trump Breaks the Pool, Then Tries to Own the Investigation

The Setup

Donald Trump personally oversaw a $16 million renovation of the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. The result, according to the reporting, was a green, slimy mess with a split lining. Trump blamed vandalism. His own handpicked D.C. prosecutor could not make that charge stick, and the blame shifted to the Interior Department, which had been in charge of the work.

Who Actually Holds Power

The answer is not hard: Trump does. He controlled the project, chose the officials around it, and now wants to control the review of the damage. That is the real story. Not the pool. Not the landscaping failure. The power move is the attempt to convert institutional oversight into a loyalty exercise.

The nomination of Dennis Kirk, a Project 2025 author and reliable right-wing operative, to run Interior’s Inspector General office is not a neutral staffing decision. It is an effort to place a partisan enforcer inside the office that is supposed to investigate the administration’s conduct. Call that what it is: preemptive cover.

The Blame Game Is the Point

Trump’s reflex here is familiar. First, break something publicly. Then deny responsibility. Then blame vandals, or bureaucrats, or whoever is weakest in the chain. The reporting shows the sequence clearly: the president oversaw the project, the damage became visible, the vandalism claim collapsed, and the administration pivoted toward finding a more compliant watchdog.

That is not confusion. It is misdirection by design. The goal is to make an executive failure look like an unsolved mystery, and then ensure the “investigation” lands in friendly hands.

Project 2025 Was the Clue

Kirk is not some random technocrat. He helped write the Project 2025 playbook for seizing control of the federal workforce. That matters because the administration is not merely filling a post. It is importing the architecture of takeover into the machinery of oversight itself.

Rep. Jared Huffman is right to call Kirk a fake watchdog. A watchdog is not a watchdog when it owes its position to the person it is supposed to scrutinize. It is a muzzle with a badge.

The Watchdog Was Already Beaten Down

The Interior Department’s inspector general, Mark Lee Greenblatt, was fired and left vacant for 18 months, while at least five congressional investigation requests sat unresolved. That detail matters more than the pool scandal because it shows the broader method: weaken oversight first, then install a loyalist, then declare the system “reformed.”

This is how accountability is hollowed out in plain sight. Not with a grand announcement, but with vacancies, purges, and nominees chosen for obedience.

The Larger Pattern

The Reflecting Pool fiasco is not a decorative mishap. It is a miniature version of authoritarian governance: the leader makes the mess, lies about the cause, punishes independent scrutiny, and tries to staff the cleanup crew with people loyal to him personally.

Congress cannot pretend this is a normal oversight dispute. When the White House seeks to populate the inspector general’s office with Project 2025 personnel after firing the existing watchdog, the message is unmistakable. The point is not to fix corruption. The point is to control who gets to name it.
