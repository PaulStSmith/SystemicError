'Never in a million years': CNN host shocked by data on Trump's 'political loser' policy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The U.S. Supreme Court will hear arguments on president Donald Trump's executive order ending birthright citizenship, but Americans are broadly opposed to that policy – and CNN's John Berman was shocked by its unpopularity.

Three district courts have already paused his order to end birthright citizenship for the children of people who are in the U.S. illegally, and CNN's Harry Enten showed polling data on the issue that found Americans increasingly support that right which is enshrined in the Constitution's 14th Amendment.

"A lot of legal scholars will agree this is a fringy idea, and I think the American public agrees it is a fringy idea," Enten said. "Take a look here: Ending birthright citizenship, and keep in mind this polling specifically asked about for children born to immigrants illegally here. Look at this: Just 41 percent support, 56 percent oppose. You rarely get 56 percent of the country to agree on anything, but on this one, they do agree that ending birthright citizenship, even for children who are here, to parents who are born, who are here illegally – they oppose that idea."

"Keep in mind, I've looked at every single poll on the subject," Enten added. "Different question wording, [but] in every single one, the majority oppose. Oftentimes the opposed the opposition number is actually higher than the 56 [percent]. If anything, this is the low level, at least at this point in time."

That opposition is actually growing over time, Enten said.

"This 41 percent who support that idea, currently, only about four in 10 Americans," Enten said. "Take a look here: This was a stat that I think surprised Mr. Berman, he was surprised by this one. Look, he's shaking his head off on the side, support ending birthright citizenship, we get 41 percent. Now, look where it was in 2010 during the Tea Party craze, right, 47 percent. How about in the early 90s? It was a majority who supported. The number has been going down, down, down on this particular subject, which I think is quite surprising to a lot of folks, because there's obviously the anti-immigration fervor in this country seems higher. But on this particular question, support ending birthright citizenship, in fact, we're near record lows on this number at least over the last 30 years."

Berman pointed out that support for deportations was rising, and Enten agreed the public opposition to ending birthright citizenship was clearly moving in the opposite direction and had been for decades.

"Never in a million years, this is never what I would have predicted on this, which is why it's so important to see what the polls are telling us here," Berman said.

Enten was also surprised by another finding that showed 51 percent of Democrats were motivated to vote on that issue, while only 31 percent of Democrats were motivated to vote on birthright citizenship.

"This is a political battle they're not winning," Enten said. "Democrats are winning this political battle, and if I were advising the Donald Trump White House, I'd say stick to other issues on immigration, not this one, because this one, as I said, is a political loser."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Supreme Court Hearing on Ending Birthright Citizenship

The Supreme Court's decision to hear arguments on President Donald Trump's executive order to end birthright citizenship has ignited a firestorm of public opinion, with CNN's analysis revealing a surprising consensus among Americans. This analysis aims to dissect the layers of this contentious issue, grounding our commentary in the stark realities the polling data presents.

Public Opposition: A Defiant Stance Against Trump's Executive Order

CNN's John Berman expressed astonishment at the overwhelming opposition to the policy aimed at ending birthright citizenship for children born in the U.S. to undocumented immigrants. According to polling data presented by CNN's Harry Enten, a significant 56 percent of Americans oppose the repeal of birthright citizenship, a right enshrined in the Constitution's 14th Amendment. This figure starkly contrasts with the 41 percent who support the policy change.

It's critical to emphasize the constitutional grounding of birthright citizenship. The 14th Amendment, a cornerstone of American civil rights, unequivocally grants citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the United States. Trump's executive order not only challenges this foundational principle but does so in the face of significant public opposition.

Enten's analysis reveals a surprising trend: opposition to ending birthright citizenship has grown over time, even amidst periods of heightened anti-immigration sentiment. This suggests a profound shift in public opinion, moving away from restrictive immigration policies towards a more inclusive understanding of citizenship and rights.

The Political Landscape: A Losing Battle for Trump

The data presents a clear message: the American public does not support the revocation of birthright citizenship, even for children of undocumented immigrants. This stance is not a fleeting sentiment but a robust conviction that has only strengthened over the years. The decline in support for ending birthright citizenship, from a majority in the early '90s to a record low today, underscores a significant transformation in societal attitudes towards immigration and citizenship.

Moreover, the political implications of this issue are unmistakable. Enten's observation that 51 percent of Democrats are motivated to vote on this issue, compared to a lesser 31 percent motivated by other immigration issues, signals a potent electoral dynamic. The Trump administration, in pushing for the end of birthright citizenship, is not only confronting a constitutional and moral quagmire but is also venturing into a political minefield.

Conclusion: A Fringe Idea at Odds with American Values

The Supreme Court's consideration of this executive order highlights a critical juncture in the American legal and political landscape. The public's opposition to ending birthright citizenship is not just a rebuke of a policy proposal; it is a reaffirmation of core American values embedded in the Constitution.

The narrative that emerges from CNN's analysis is clear: The attempt to end birthright citizenship is a fringe idea, at odds with both the legal foundations of the United States and the prevailing sentiments of its people. As the nation watches the Supreme Court's deliberations, the court of public opinion has rendered its verdict. In this battle over the essence of American identity and the rights of its people, inclusivity and constitutional fidelity have prevailed over exclusion and political expediency.
