MAGA fans demand Trump address rising costs as 'the biggest thing'

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, U.S. December 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

The top priorities of MAGA adherents attending President Donald Trump’s Pennsylvania rally were roughly in tune with those of the rest of American voters: Inflation.

Conservative media company Right Side Broadcasting Network, known for their live stream coverage of Trump rallies and America First events, sent host Matthew Alvarez to Trump’s Pennsylvania rally at Mount Airy Casino Resort on Tuesday to interview Trump fans waiting in line and inside the venue before Trump took the stage.

What they caught on tape was not exactly the issue Trump focused on.

“I would like to see [Trump] talk more about grocery prices because he’s the guy that can do it. And he’s done it,” said one woman Alvarez interviewed. “I would like them to say to the Democrats, you talk about affordability. Okay. What’s your policy that’s different than mine that’s going to help the American people? And if you have it, why don’t we work together in the Congress now and fix it? … So I’d like to see the golden age come, stock market go up, crypto go up, prices come down, wages go up, manufacturing comes back.”

Another fan, while demanding Trump “get rid of immigrant freeloaders,” also asked Trump to “lower fuel prices.”

“And that’s it?” asked Alvarez.

“That’s the biggest thing,” the fan confirmed.

But Trump didn’t really address his tactics for lowering U.S. inflation, be it fuel or groceries. Instead, he resorted to scolding voters who were unwilling to do with less.

“You can give up certain products, you can give up pencils because under the China policy, you know, every child can get 37 pencils. They only need one or two,” Trump told fans at the Pennsylvania stop.

This prompted one CNN anchor to call Trump “an out of touch, literally gold-gilded president who outright refuses to recognize the economic reality for the vast majority of Americans.”

“He’s putting 37 gold things on literally one [Oval Office] wall while he’s asking families to tighten their belts at the holidays to live without more than two pencils or two dolls in the name of an economic policy that everyone knows is making inflation worse,” said MS NOW host Nicole Wallace.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Dissonance at Trump’s Pennsylvania Rally

In a recent gathering that could be mistaken for a dissonant echo chamber rather than a political rally, attendees of former President Donald Trump’s event in Pennsylvania voiced concerns that resonate with a larger segment of the American electorate: inflation. However, as reported by the conservative media company Right Side Broadcasting Network, the priorities voiced by MAGA adherents and the substance of Trump’s address diverged significantly, revealing a chasm between voter concerns and Trump’s rhetoric.

The MAGA Priorities: Inflation and Beyond

Attendees expressed a desire to hear Trump’s plan for tackling inflation — a pressing issue affecting Americans across the political spectrum. One supporter highlighted a wish for Trump to focus on reducing grocery prices, indicating a belief in his capability to influence such economic indicators. This sentiment underscores a broader yearning for solutions to everyday economic strains faced by Americans, from the price of groceries to fuel costs.

Yet, another supporter’s demand to “get rid of immigrant freeloaders” as a solution to lower fuel prices injects a xenophobic undertone into the economic discourse, diverting attention from systemic issues and policy-based solutions to inflation. This diversion exemplifies a concerning trend where complex economic challenges are oversimplified into polarizing and divisive narratives.

Trump’s Response: A Masterclass in Deflection

Rather than addressing these economic concerns with substantive policy proposals or acknowledging the hardships inflation imposes on American families, Trump’s response was a masterclass in deflection. His suggestion that Americans “give up certain products” and his metaphorical allusion to children’s pencils under a “China policy” not only trivializes the impacts of inflation but also deflects responsibility away from his administration’s policies and towards a scapegoat.

Trump’s remarks prompted CNN anchor Nicole Wallace to brand him as an “out of touch, literally gold-gilded president,” a characterization that finds its proof in Trump’s apparent disconnect from the economic realities faced by the majority of Americans. This critique is bolstered by Trump’s lavish aesthetic choices, juxtaposed with his call for economic austerity among American families.

Conclusion: The Grand Dissonance

The Pennsylvania rally encapsulates a broader issue within the MAGA movement and Trump’s political machinations: a grand dissonance between the articulated needs of his supporters and his own agenda. While MAGA adherents voice legitimate economic concerns, Trump’s responses remain mired in deflection, scapegoating, and a disconnection from the realities of those he claims to represent.

This dissonance is not merely rhetorical; it has tangible impacts on the ability of American politics to address critical issues such as inflation. As long as political leaders prioritize divisive narratives over substantive policy discussions, the economic well-being of Americans will remain hostage to political theater.

In the end, the rally serves as a stark reminder of the systemic error in our political discourse: the failure to bridge the gap between the concerns of the electorate and the priorities of their leaders. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must demand more than deflection and divisiveness from those who seek to lead.
