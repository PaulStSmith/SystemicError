‘Major step’: Ex-official warns Trump just moved closer to dark goal ‘he’s always wanted’

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump walks at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 1, 2025. REUTERS/Jessica Koscielniak

A top former Trump White House national security official is warning that the president’s federalization of the Washington, D.C. Metropolitan Police and deployment of the National Guard and other federal agents to the nation’s capital is a “major step” toward martial law.

Olivia Troye is a Republican who worked at the National Counterterrorism Center, the United States Department of Energy Office of Intelligence and Counterintelligence, and the Department of Homeland Security Office of Intelligence and Analysis, as well as serving as Vice President Mike Pence’s Homeland Security and Counterterrorism Advisor.

During a nearly 90-minute news conference on Monday, Trump threatened to also deploy to D.C. other U.S. Armed Forces and threatened that other major Democratic-run cities he sees as crime-ridden could be next.

Crime in Washington, D.C. is at a 30-year low, with double-digit declines last year and this year, and is not in the top 10 of violent or dangerous U.S. cities.

“That Trump presser wasn’t about security or safety,” Troye wrote on Monday. “It was a major step towards martial law, something he’s always wanted.”

She also posed the following questions related to the President’s announcement:

“In 2020, FBI & other federal law enforcement warned against misusing them in DC—they’re fine with it now?”

“Why deploy the National Guard to DC today, but not on Jan 6, the most violent moment in years for the city?”

Brian Schwalb, the Attorney General for the District of Columbia, wrote: “The Administration’s actions are unprecedented, unnecessary, and unlawful. There is no crime emergency in the District of Columbia. Violent crime in DC reached historic 30-year lows last year, and is down another 26% so far this year. We are considering all of our options and will do what is necessary to protect the rights and safety of District residents.”

Others have raised the specter of martial law and authoritarianism.

“Trump watched the insurrection from the White House and thought it was fine, but now he wants to put DC under martial law,” noted Naveed Shah, political director of the progressive veterans group Common Defense.

“Make no mistake: federalizing the police and deploying the National Guard to DC isn’t about a non-existent crime wave. It’s an authoritarian move to crush dissent and strip Americans of their right to self-govern. This is a chilling precedent for every city in America,” wrote attorney Andrew Weinstein, a Visiting Fellow for the Syracuse University Institute for Democracy, Journalism, and Citizenship.

In response to a video clip of Trump threatening to duplicate these efforts in New York City, Baltimore, and Oakland, Minnesota Democratic Governor Tim Walz wrote: “The road to authoritarianism is littered with people telling you you’re overreacting.”

Political scientist and political risk researcher Ian Bremmer noted that President Trump is “gradually normalizing use of the national guard in liberal cities. had he done this in his first term, people would be outraged and take to the streets. now not so much. paves the way for using the national guard at a more strategically important time. say, during elections.”

“The president teasing martial [law] for multiple American cities is a dangerous and unlawful power grab,” warned Constitutional Law Professor Anthony Michael Kreis. “The Anglo-American constitutional tradition has rejected this kind of arbitrary rule as far back as the Petition of Right in 1628. This is the stuff of a wannabe tinpot Stuart king.”

Our Analysis:

The Road to Martial Law: Analyzing Trump's Latest Power Play

In what can only be described as a chilling advancement towards autocracy, former President Donald Trump has once again stirred the pot of authoritarianism with his recent threats to federalize the Washington, D.C. Metropolitan Police and deploy the National Guard and other federal agents across the nation's capital. This move, as critiqued by a plethora of voices including Olivia Troye, a Republican and former Trump White House national security official, signifies a "major step" towards martial law, a concept seemingly coveted by Trump.

A Cloak of Security or an Iron Fist?

Despite crime in Washington, D.C. being at a 30-year low, Trump's nearly 90-minute tirade was less about public safety and more a blatant display of power. It's pivotal to underscore the misrepresentation of facts to justify this authoritarian push. The narrative of a crime-ridden capital is starkly contradicted by statistics showing significant declines in violent crimes. This begs the question, why the sudden need to militarize the city's police force?

A Double Standard of Deployment

Troye's poignant inquiries highlight a glaring inconsistency in Trump's approach to law enforcement deployment. The absence of the National Guard on January 6, during one of the city's most tumultuous periods, versus the current push for militarization under the guise of non-existent crime waves, reveals a disturbingly selective application of federal power.

Voices of Reason Amidst the Authoritarian Crescendo

Critics from various fields have been quick to denounce Trump's actions as a dangerous leap towards authoritarianism. Brian Schwalb, the Attorney General for the District of Columbia, and others have emphasized the lack of a crime emergency that would necessitate such extreme measures. This collective outcry underlines a fundamental threat to American democracy and the right to self-governance, painting a grim picture of a nation under the shadow of military rule.

The Bigger Picture: A Strategy of Fear and Control

What is particularly alarming is the potential for this strategy to be replicated in other Democratic-run cities, as hinted by Trump. This isn't merely about public safety; it's a calculated move to suppress dissent and centralize control, setting a dangerous precedent for the erosion of civil liberties across the country. Political figures and scholars, including Minnesota Democratic Governor Tim Walz and political scientist Ian Bremmer, have recognized this trajectory towards authoritarianism, urging the public to acknowledge and resist this gradual normalization of military intervention in civil affairs.

Moreover, the mockery from Democratic Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, while humorous, underscores a serious issue at hand: the blatant overreach of executive power. The reference to the Epstein files, though seemingly off-topic, serves as a stark reminder of the numerous scandals and controversies that have plagued Trump's tenure, further questioning the legitimacy and motives behind such drastic measures.

Conclusion: A Crossroads for Democracy

The discourse surrounding Trump's recent actions is not merely about the federalization of police or the deployment of the National Guard; it's a reflection of a deeper, systemic struggle between democratic principles and authoritarian impulses. As a nation, the response to these developments will not only determine the immediate future of Washington, D.C. but also set a precedent for the handling of dissent, the role of military and federal forces in civilian life, and the very essence of American democracy.

In the face of such a blatant power grab, it is crucial for citizens, lawmakers, and leaders to stand in defense of democratic values and resist the encroachment of authoritarianism. The path forward should be grounded in a commitment to transparency, accountability, and the unwavering protection of civil liberties.
