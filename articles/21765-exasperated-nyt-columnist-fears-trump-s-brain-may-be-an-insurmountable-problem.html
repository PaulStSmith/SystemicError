Exasperated NYT columnist fears Trump's brain may be an 'insurmountable' problem

Trump Is Building the Lie Before the Election He Wants to Break

The Real Story

A New York Times exchange between Frank Bruni and Bret Stephens centered on Trump’s foreign-policy chaos, his corruption, and his effort to poison trust in the vote before the midterms. That is the surface. The deeper story is simpler and uglier: Trump is using the machinery of the presidency to preemptively delegitimize electoral defeat, while institutions that should stop him are still being narrated as if they are merely “concerned.”

Power, Not Confusion

The decisive power here sits with Trump and the officials he is able to fire, neutralize, intimidate, or order around. Bruni’s examples matter because they are not abstract warnings. He points to Trump removing or sidelining election overseers, sending federal investigators into Georgia, and issuing executive orders aimed at voter rolls and mail voting. That is not noise. That is an effort to shape the rules, the referees, and the outcome before ballots are even counted.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The usual elite framing treats this kind of behavior as a symptom of instability, as if the problem were Trump’s temperament rather than the deliberate political project underneath it. Stephens calling the Iran problem “workable” but Trump’s brain “insurmountable” is the kind of language that personalizes a regime-level threat into a psychological inconvenience. The issue is not whether Trump is erratic. The issue is that he and his movement know exactly what they want: an electoral system Americans cannot fully trust unless their side wins.

Corruption With a Flag on It

The reported $2 million “development fee” to a Trump holding company fits the pattern. So do the executive actions, the election interference posture, and the institutional sabotage. This is not incidental graft around the edges of public life. It is a governing style in which private enrichment, state power, and partisan coercion reinforce one another. The point is not merely to win. It is to make the office itself an engine for impunity.

ICE Is Not Broken

Stephens also raised the recent ICE killings during vehicle stops in Texas and Maine, including the death of Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero in Biddeford, Maine. The article notes that advocates dispute the state account and want an independent investigation. That is not enough. ICE is not a neutral agency that occasionally needs better oversight. It is an armed deportation apparatus that normalizes violence against immigrants and then hides behind process language when people die. The problem is structural. ICE should be abolished.

The Pattern

This story is not about one speech, one column exchange, or one especially chaotic presidency. It is about an authoritarian method that combines electoral sabotage, state violence, corruption, and blame-shifting into one political routine. Democrats are not the mirror image of that project; they are the institutional barrier against it, flawed and often timid as they are. The democratic task is not to pretend this is ordinary partisanship. It is to name the machinery for what it is and stop treating deliberate democratic destruction as a series of unfortunate misunderstandings.
