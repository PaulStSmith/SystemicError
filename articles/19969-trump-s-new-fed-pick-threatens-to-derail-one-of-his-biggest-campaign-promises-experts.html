Trump's new Fed pick threatens to derail one of his biggest campaign promises: experts

Donald Trump’s selection to replace Jerome Powell as the chair of the Federal Reserve has a history that leads many observers to believe the president is not going to like what comes next after his Senate confirmation, according to the Washington Post.

Trump has prioritized lowering interest rates throughout his criticism of Powell’s tenure. However, Trump also campaigned on making housing more affordable — goals that may conflict with nominee Kevin Warsh’s policy priorities.

According to Post reporter Andrew Ackerman, Warsh believes the Federal Reserve’s bond portfolio is unsustainably large and should be reduced. Trimming the $6.6 trillion portfolio of Treasury bonds and mortgage-backed securities could push mortgage rates higher, potentially undermining Trump’s affordability objectives.

The tension reflects a fundamental policy conflict. Warsh has consistently criticized the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet as “bloated and distortionary,” arguing that an oversized central bank portfolio artificially suppresses long-term interest rates and inflates asset prices.

Bill English, former director of the Federal Reserve’s division of monetary affairs, noted the contradiction: “If all he does is move to a smaller Fed balance sheet, it’s hard to see how that would be consistent with lower mortgage rates, and that creates some tension with the president.”

Trump recently stated, “We can drop interest rates to a level, and that’s one thing we do want to do. That’s natural. That’s good for everybody.”

This contrasts sharply with Warsh’s stated philosophy: “Each time the Fed jumps into action, the more it expands its size and scope, encroaching further on other macroeconomic domains. More debt is accumulated… more capital is misallocated… more institutional lines are crossed… risks of future shocks are magnified.”

Jon Hilsenrath, visiting scholar at Duke University’s economics department, suggested the fundamental tension will constrain Warsh’s ability to satisfy Trump. “Almost everywhere you look, Warsh is kind of hamstrung, including on the balance sheet.”

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Trump’s Fed Chair Nominee: A Critical Analysis

In an intriguing twist of political and economic strategy, Donald Trump’s selection of Kevin Warsh to replace Jerome Powell as the chair of the Federal Reserve is a decision that seemingly contradicts the president’s own policy priorities. This article delves into the inherent conflicts between Trump’s aspirations for lower interest rates and affordable housing, and Warsh’s policy stance on reducing the Federal Reserve’s bond portfolio. Let’s dissect the layers of this paradox with the precision of a scalpel.

The Conflict at the Core

Trump’s vocal criticism of Powell’s tenure centered around the high interest rates, with a clear inclination towards making borrowing cheaper for Americans. On the surface, this is a populist move, aligning with Trump’s campaign promises of economic prosperity for the average citizen. Enter Kevin Warsh, a nominee whose policy priorities seem at odds with Trump’s advertised goals. Warsh, as reported by the Washington Post, views the Federal Reserve’s $6.6 trillion bond portfolio as “unsustainably large” and advocates for its reduction.

The Irony of Trump’s Choice

It’s almost poetic, the irony of Trump nominating Warsh, a figure whose policy actions could directly undermine Trump’s own affordability objectives. Reducing the Federal Reserve’s bond portfolio, as Warsh suggests, would likely lead to higher mortgage rates, countering Trump’s affordability goals. This scenario is a classic example of political contradiction, where the left hand seems unaware of what the right hand is doing—or worse, doesn’t care.

Warsh’s criticism of the Federal Reserve’s balance sheet as “bloated and distortionary” is not without merit, citing that an oversized portfolio suppresses long-term interest rates and inflates asset prices. However, this stance directly conflicts with Trump’s simplistic view that lower interest rates are universally “good for everybody.”

The Experts Weigh In

Bill English, a former director at the Federal Reserve, and Jon Hilsenrath, a visiting scholar, both highlight the tension between Warsh’s policy stance and Trump’s objectives. English points out the obvious contradiction in wanting a smaller Fed balance sheet while also desiring lower mortgage rates. Hilsenrath goes further, suggesting that Warsh’s position on the balance sheet will constrain his ability to fulfill Trump’s promises, essentially hamstrung by the fundamental tension between their policy perspectives.

The Misalignment of Economic Philosophies

Warsh’s philosophy, as quoted, speaks volumes about the broader economic implications of constant Fed intervention: “Each time the Fed jumps into action...more debt is accumulated… more capital is misallocated… more institutional lines are crossed… risks of future shocks are magnified.” This perspective, which warns against the dangers of an overly interventionist Fed, starkly contrasts with Trump’s more narrow focus on interest rates and housing affordability.

Conclusion: A Complicated Chess Game

Trump’s nomination of Warsh as the Fed Chair is a complex maneuver, fraught with inherent contradictions and potential for policy conflict. While Warsh’s concerns about the Federal Reserve’s outsized role in the economy are well-founded, his policy priorities could inadvertently sabotage Trump’s own economic goals. This scenario underscores the intricate dance between political objectives and economic realities, where the pursuit of populist promises can clash with the principles of sound monetary policy. It’s a systemic error, a misstep in the complicated chess game of governance, where strategy and ideology collide with the practicalities of economic stewardship.
