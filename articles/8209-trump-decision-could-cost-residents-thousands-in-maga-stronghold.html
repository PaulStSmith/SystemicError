Trump decision could cost residents thousands in MAGA stronghold

President Donald Trump made a decision that’s hurting residents in a stronghold of support that helped propel him to re-election.

The president denied disaster aid Oct. 22 to two electric utilities in rural northern Michigan – where his support has historically run strong since entering politics – that would shift billions of dollars in costs from federal taxpayers to working-class customers who now potentially face thousands of dollars in rate hikes to pay for repairs to the power grid following a three-day ice storm in March, reported Politico.

“It could be tens of millions of dollars left on the backs of the members,” said Allan Berg, the CEO of Presque Isle Electric & Gas, known as PIE&G, in northeastern Michigan.

Great Lakes Energy, in northwestern Michigan, warned on its website that “all storm-related costs not reimbursed by state or federal disaster aid will be paid for by the cooperative’s entire membership.”

Politico’s E&E News obtained documents showing the Trump administration documented $90 million in damage to utility infrastructure, which is about five times the federal threshold to qualify for disaster aid, but the Federal Emergency Management Agency informed Democratic Gov. Gretchen Whitmer that assistance was “not warranted.”

That decision “could make the co-op actually go broke if something isn’t done to make them whole again,” said Pete Rose, a retired PIE&G foreman.

The utilities are nonprofit electric cooperatives serving rural areas and owned by customers, and Whitmer notified Trump in August that customers faced at least $4,500 per household in surcharges and rate increases without federal aid.

Trump has denied at least nine gubernatorial disaster requests since April despite FEMA documenting damage that met the federal threshold for aid, and he justified his decision in Michigan using fine print in a way that had never been done before.

“We can’t find a similar disaster where Category F is denied,” said Rep. Jack Bergman (R-MI), who represents the area and has asked Trump to reconsider. “This is something nobody asked for. Our members did not want this ice storm. FEMA is a federal program designed to ensure when large natural disasters occur, they can come and make the playing field level.”

The Republican-led state House overwhelmingly approved $100 million in recovery funds in March, but the Democratic-controlled state Senate still has not taken action on the package, showing how disaster response if responsibility was shifted from the federal government to states.

“You look at northern Michigan, it’s a Republican area,” said state Rep. Parker Fairbairn, a Republican who sponsored the bill. “If this would have happened in Detroit or Grand Rapids, I think they would have seen funding from the state already in big numbers.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Denial of Disaster Aid: A Political Storm Hits Michigan

In an act that can only be described as a stunning betrayal, President Donald Trump has made a decision that has sent shockwaves through rural northern Michigan—a stronghold of support that once helped to secure his re-election. By denying disaster aid to two electric utilities in the region, Trump has not only turned his back on his supporters but has also highlighted a troubling trend of prioritizing political strategy over the wellbeing of American citizens.

The Heart of the Matter

The core of this issue lies in Trump’s refusal on October 22 to provide federal disaster aid to Presque Isle Electric & Gas (PIE&G) and Great Lakes Energy. This decision comes in the aftermath of a devastating three-day ice storm in March, which left the utilities with a whopping $90 million in damages—far exceeding the federal threshold for disaster aid. The denial of aid means that the financial burden of repairs now falls squarely on the shoulders of working-class customers, potentially leading to thousands of dollars in rate hikes per household.

A Calculated Betrayal

What makes this move particularly egregious is the blatant disregard for the well-being of a community that has historically supported Trump. Allan Berg, CEO of PIE&G, succinctly put it, stating that this could leave “tens of millions of dollars left on the backs of the members.” The decision not only threatens the financial stability of these residents but also risks pushing the cooperatives to the brink of bankruptcy.

Politicizing Disaster Response

Trump’s justification for this denial, citing fine print in a manner unprecedented in disaster relief history, is a clear indication of a politicized approach to what should be non-partisan support. This is underscored by the fact that Trump has denied at least nine gubernatorial disaster requests since April, despite FEMA’s documentation of qualifying damage.

Representative Jack Bergman (R-MI), who represents the affected area, expressed confusion and frustration over the denial, emphasizing that FEMA is designed to “make the playing field level” in the face of natural disasters. This sentiment reveals a growing concern over the politicization of disaster response under Trump’s administration.

State vs. Federal: A Divisive Strategy

The Republican-led state House’s approval of $100 million in recovery funds contrasts sharply with the Democratic-controlled state Senate’s inaction, highlighting a divisive political landscape. State Rep. Parker Fairbairn’s comments suggest a belief in a partisan bias in disaster funding, implying that areas with stronger Democratic leanings, such as Detroit or Grand Rapids, would have received state funding more readily.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

Trump’s decision to deny disaster aid to Michigan’s rural electric utilities is a stark reminder of the systemic failures that plague our political system. It raises serious questions about the criteria used to allocate federal aid and the implications of politicizing disaster response. As residents of northern Michigan brace for the financial impact of this decision, one can’t help but wonder about the long-term consequences of prioritizing political strategies over the needs of American citizens.

In an era where bipartisan support should be the cornerstone of disaster response, this incident serves as a cautionary tale of the dangers of allowing political allegiances to dictate the distribution of aid. It’s a systemic error that undermines the very principles of fairness and equality that should guide our nation’s actions, especially in times of crisis.
