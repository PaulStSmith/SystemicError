As reported by the Washington Post on Thursday, new data from corporate outplacement firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas found that employers in October announced 153,000 job cuts, which marked the highest number of layoffs in that month since October 2003.

Total announced job cuts in 2025 have now reached 1.1 million, a number that the Post describes as a “recession-like” level comparable to the steep job cuts announced in the wake of the dotcom bust of the early 2000s, the global financial crisis of 2008, and the onset of the Covid-19 pandemic in 2020.

John Challenger, the CEO of Challenger, Gray & Christmas, told the Post that the huge number of October layoffs showed the economy was entering “new territory.”

“We haven’t seen mega-layoffs of the size that are being discussed now—48,000 from UPS, potentially 30,000 from Amazon—since 2020 and before that, since the recession of 2009,” he explained. “When you see companies making cuts of this size, it does signal a real shift in direction.”

CNBC noted that the Challenger report found that the tech sector is

currently being hardest hit by the layoffs, and it said that the adoption of artificial intelligence was a significant driver of job cuts.

“Some industries are correcting after the hiring boom of the pandemic, but this comes as AI adoption, softening consumer and corporate spending, and rising costs drive belt-tightening and hiring freezes,” the report said. “Those laid off now are finding it harder to quickly secure new roles, which could further loosen the labor market.”

With the backing of Big Tech investors, President Donald Trump has pushed to prevent states from regulating AI, over the objections of labor groups and progressive lawmakers. Last month, Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) warned that without strong regulation, tech billionaires’ investments in AI will likely “increase their wealth and power exponentially” while wiping out “tens of millions” of jobs.

According to Bloomberg, however, AI adoption is just one factor in companies’ decision to enact mass layoffs, as some firms have also cited the need to protect their profit margins from the impacts of President Donald Trump’s tariffs, which have raised prices for a wide variety of products and materials.

Democratic lawmakers were quick to seize on the news of mass layoffs as evidence that Trump is sending the US economy into a ditch.

“Trump put billionaires in charge of everything,” remarked Rep. Greg Casar (D-Texas) in a social media post. “It’s a disaster.”

“Trump inherited the fastest growing economy in the [Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development], fastest reduction in inflation, record job creation,” said Rep. Sean Casten (D-IL). “Dumb tariffs, racist immigration policies, attacks on the rule of law and termination of congressionally mandated programs did this.”

Sen.Chris Murphy(D-CT), meanwhile, simplywrotethat “Trump’s economy suuuuucks.”

Our Analysis:

Economic Downward Spiral: A Closer Look at the US Labor Market Debacle

In a recent report that sends shivers down the spine of the American workforce, the Washington Post unveils a grim picture of the US labor market, now seemingly in freefall. The report, heavily citing data from corporate outplacement firm Challenger, Gray & Christmas, highlights a harrowing October with 153,000 job cuts—a figure that screams “crisis” louder than a town crier on the eve of a plague. This is not merely a bad month; it’s the worst October we’ve seen since 2003, catapulting 2025’s total announced job cuts to a staggering 1.1 million. The term “recession-like” does not begin to cover the severity of this situation; we’re in the throes of a labor market catastrophe that echoes the darkest days of the early 2000s, the 2008 global financial crisis, and the onset of the Covid-19 pandemic.

John Challenger, CEO of Challenger, Gray & Christmas, frames the situation as “new territory” for the economy, pointing to the gargantuan layoffs from giants like UPS and Amazon as harbingers of a significant shift. Yet, what’s truly new here? The pattern of corporate giants slashing jobs in the face of economic downturns is as old as capitalism itself. The real news is the speed and scale at which these layoffs are happening, coupled with the tech sector bearing the brunt of the impact.

The report sheds light on a crucial factor driving this wave of layoffs: the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI). The tech industry, once the darling of American economic growth, is now seeing a correction that’s been exacerbated by AI, softening consumer and corporate spending, and rising costs. This is not merely a correction; it’s an upheaval that’s leaving many workers stranded without immediate prospects, thereby loosening the labor market even further.

But let’s not gloss over the political dimensions of this crisis. President Donald Trump is painted as a key antagonist in this narrative, with his administration’s push against regulating AI—despite the protests of labor groups and progressive lawmakers—serving as a case in point. Senator Bernie Sanders‘s warnings about AI enriching tech billionaires at the expense of “tens of millions” of jobs seem more prophetic than ever. Yet, the issue is multifaceted; Trump’s tariffs have also been implicated in the layoffs, indicating a broader economic mismanagement that Democratic lawmakers are keen to highlight.

Representatives Greg Casar and Sean Casten, along with Senator Chris Murphy, offer scathing critiques of Trump’s policies, linking them directly to the economic downturn. Casar’s remark, “Trump put billionaires in charge of everything. It’s a disaster,” cuts to the heart of the matter, encapsulating the perceived prioritization of the ultra-wealthy over the average American worker. Casten’s and Murphy’s comments further underscore the sentiment that Trump’s economic policies have been catastrophic.

The Bigger Picture

What we’re witnessing is not just a series of unfortunate events but a systemic failure exacerbated by flawed policies and a dangerous underestimation of technology’s impact on the labor market. The layoffs are symptomatic of a deeper malaise—a concoction of unchecked technological advancement, policy missteps, and corporate greed.

The tragedy here is not just the loss of jobs but the apparent disregard for the human cost of these economic shifts. As AI continues to evolve and political leaders navigate the treacherous waters of economic policy, the question remains: Who is looking out for the American worker?

In times like these, the role of critical, incisive journalism cannot be overstated. It’s not enough to report the numbers; we must question the systems that produce them. As we dissect the causes and implications of this labor market implosion, let’s not forget the faces behind the figures—the workers whose lives are upended by decisions made in boardrooms and government offices far removed from their own realities.

In the end, the US labor market’s downward spiral is a stark reminder of the need for compassionate, intelligent, and forward-thinking policies that prioritize people over profits. The current situation may be grim, but it also presents an opportunity for substantive change. The question is, will we seize it, or will we continue to spiral?
