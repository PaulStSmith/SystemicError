'Godless and gutless': Republicans called out as fiery Dems go on the offensive

U.S. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (R-LA) attends a press conference, more than a month into the longest U.S. government shutdown in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 10, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

WASHINGTON — As the government shutdown neared its end on Wednesday, one prominent Democratic member of Congress had choice words for Republican leaders, whom she called “godless” and “gutless.”

Rep. Ayanna Pressley (D-MA), a leading U.S. House progressive, told Raw Story in an exclusive interview that she was shocked by her Republican colleagues and what they had put Americans through in the more than 40 days since the government closed.

“I’m just disappointed because we’re in this position at all,” Pressley said. “Our Republican colleagues, I think they have proven that they are godless, they are gutless, and they are complicit in wholesale harm.

“So we’re here because of their incompetence and their indifference, and I resent that we’re in the position at all. They don’t know how to govern.”

Senate Democrats prompted the shutdown, holding up a government funding measure in an attempt to force Republicans to work to avoid spiraling health-care costs for millions of Americans thanks to the lapsing of Affordable Care Act subsidies.

Democrats held out for more than a month, even as Republican threats mounted, prominently including attempts to fire federal workers and to suspend Supplemental Nutritional Assistance Payments (SNAP), popularly known as food stamps, a vital resource for millions of low-income Americans.

On Sunday, eight senators – seven Democrats and Sen. Angus King (I-ME), who caucuses with the party — climbed down, citing such threats as good reason to give up the fight.

Pressley said many Republicans were “so-called Christians,” given their willingness to enact such punitive measures for political gain.

“This is what they do? We deny people? They’re medically deprived and they go hungry? It’s unconscionable, and the worst part: 100 percent preventable,” Pressley said.

“So I just resent that we were put in the position at all. But for House Democrats, I’m proud that we stayed united and never lost the plot, which is [to support] the people.”

That might have been a veiled shot at Democrats in the Senate, amid plenty of party calls for Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) to step down, having failed to keep his caucus united.

Among House Democrats who spoke to Raw Story on Wednesday, Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA) was not calling for Schumer to go — instead focusing fire on Republicans under Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), who kept the House closed for eight weeks.

“I think Chuck is not the martinet or the dictator of the Senate Democrats,” Beyer said.

“And you get, you know, good people like Sen. Tim Kaine (D-VA) and Angus King and Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL), who were more responsive to the 42 million without SNAP benefits and the 8 million federal employees without paychecks. So that’s not his fault.

“You know, [Schumer’s] done a very good job, holding over together for seven weeks because of the shutdown.”

Raw Story asked: “What do you make of Speaker Johnson?”

Beyer replied, “I would have to say that for me, he’s the most disappointing member of the US Congress.

“First of all, because in his daily press conferences, he just lied again and again and again. He also used the most extreme language, you know, this ‘Marxist’ and ‘communist’ and all this crazy stuff.

“I mean, I’m not big into name-calling, especially things that are sort of disconnected from reality. Also, I thought there was a pettiness, a meanness, about the way he managed this place, locking all the doors, not lifting the barriers. Poor staff had to wait in line 30 and 45 minutes to get in in the morning because they would lock all the doors … that’s worrying about a leader.”

On Wednesday, Johnson finally swore in Rep. Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ), a new congresswoman due to become the 218th and deciding signature on a discharge petition to force a vote on releasing the Jeffrey Epstein files, related to the late financier and sex offender and his links to powerful men, including President Donald Trump.

“That was petty, it was small,” Beyer said, adding that Republicans had been “on a seven-week paid vacation” while Grijalva had been “coming in for free.

“All of our folks have come in, but [Johnson] missed the fact that the Senate stayed in. And so there was the opportunity to do a lot of other work that needs to be done in committee after committee, and that didn’t get done.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Analyzing the Latest Government Shutdown Farce

The recent government shutdown saga, as reported by Raw Story, paints a vivid picture of the political theater that has come to define American governance. Representative Ayanna Pressley’s scathing remarks towards her Republican colleagues, describing them as “godless” and “gutless,” underscore the deep-seated frustrations boiling over in the corridors of power. Yet, beneath the surface of this heated rhetoric lies a more complex narrative of political brinkmanship, ideological warfare, and the collateral damage inflicted upon the American people.

The Blame Game: A Bipartisan Bloodsport

The article meticulously outlines the sequence of events leading to the shutdown, initiated by Senate Democrats in their quest to address the looming crisis of healthcare affordability. This move, while noble in its intentions, set the stage for a dangerous game of chicken with Republicans, who seemed all too willing to escalate the situation to catastrophic levels. The threats to fire federal workers and suspend SNAP benefits represent a new low in political strategy, leveraging the basic needs of millions as bargaining chips.

However, let’s not be mistaken: the Democrats’ hands are not entirely clean in this ordeal. The shutdown, prompted by their actions, reveals a troubling willingness to gamble with the lives of those they vow to protect. While their cause—preventing a health care crisis—was just, the methodology raises questions about the efficacy and ethics of such political maneuvers.

“Godless and Gutless” - A Reflection of Our Times?

Pressley’s condemnation of Republicans as “godless” and “gutless” is a poignant reflection of the moral vacuum that seems to have engulfed Capitol Hill. Her critique, particularly targeting the so-called Christian values of her opponents, strikes at the heart of a glaring contradiction: a professing adherence to Christian principles while simultaneously endorsing policies that jeopardize the well-being of the vulnerable.

Yet, this moral outrage, while justified, only highlights the broader systemic failures of our political institutions. It’s a vivid illustration of how political discourse has descended into a cesspool of name-calling and character assassination, diverting attention from the substantive issues at hand.

The Unseen Victims

The real tragedy of this political spectacle is the plight of the American people, particularly the low-income families reliant on SNAP benefits and the federal workers caught in the crossfire. Their suffering, a direct consequence of political deadlock, exposes a disheartening reality: the interests of the average citizen are often secondary to the political ambitions and ideological battles of their elected representatives.

A Call for Leadership

In dissecting the comments of Rep. Don Beyer and the actions of Speaker Mike Johnson, one cannot help but lament the absence of genuine leadership. Johnson’s tactics, characterized by Beyer as petty and mean-spirited, and his willingness to engage in misinformation, are indicative of a broader leadership crisis. The locking of doors and the obstruction of legislative processes not only inconvenience staff but also symbolize a deeper dysfunction within the halls of Congress.

Conclusion: A Systemic Malaise

The government shutdown, with its theatrics, blame-shifting, and real human costs, is a stark reminder of the systemic malaise afflicting American politics. It’s a narrative of lost moral compasses, abandoned responsibilities, and the erosion of democratic norms. As Pressley and her colleagues navigate these turbulent waters, one can only hope for a renaissance of principled leadership and a recommitment to the service of the American people.

But hope, as history has shown, is a scarce commodity in the transactional marketplace of Capitol Hill. Until our representatives can transcend partisan pettiness and genuinely prioritize the welfare of their constituents, the cycle of dysfunction is doomed to repeat itself, with the American people invariably bearing the brunt.
