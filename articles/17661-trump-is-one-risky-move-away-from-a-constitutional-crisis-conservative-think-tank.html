Exploiting Loopholes: Trump’s Strategic Use of Acting Cabinet Secretaries

The Power to Appoint, The Choice to Defer

In a recent maneuver characteristic of his administration, President Donald Trump has opted to retain Todd Blanche and Keith Sonderling as acting heads of the Department of Justice and the Department of Labor, respectively. This decision skirts close to constitutional boundaries, leveraging the peculiarities of the Federal Vacancies Reform Act (FVRA) and other specific statutes that exempt these positions from the usual constraints on acting appointments. The choice to use these “acting” appointments allows Trump prolonged control without Senate confirmation, effectively bypassing a key check on executive power.

Institutional Manipulation

The heart of the issue lies in Trump’s exploitation of legislative oversight and gaps within the FVRA. The Act typically restricts the duration and selection of acting officials to ensure temporary leadership does not become a backdoor method for avoiding Senate scrutiny. However, for the Attorney General and Secretary of Labor, specific statutes provide exceptions, allowing these acting officials to serve indefinitely. This isn’t just a loophole; it’s a deliberate manipulation of statutory frameworks designed to maintain a semblance of balance between the branches of government.

Misdirection and Consequences

The focus on the legality of these appointments, as discussed by Thomas A. Berry of the Cato Institute, somewhat misdirects from the broader issue of why these loopholes exist and are exploited. The discussion often becomes mired in legal technicalities, diverting attention from the substantive impact of having potentially unqualified, indefinitely acting leaders in critical government roles. This tactic dilutes the Senate’s constitutional role in advising and consenting to key appointments and centralizes power within the executive, undermining democratic governance.

A Pattern of Authoritarian Control

Trump’s reliance on acting appointments is part of a larger pattern of authoritarian behavior. By avoiding the confirmation process, Trump not only evades public and legislative scrutiny but also places loyalists in key positions without the usual vetting processes that confirmations entail. This strategy erodes institutional norms and concentrates power, a tactic often employed by leaders with authoritarian tendencies.

Systemic Insight: The Dangers of Executive Overreach

Trump’s strategic use of acting officials reveals a critical vulnerability in the American system of checks and balances. It underscores the potential for executive overreach when legislative mechanisms fail to adapt to new political realities or are left intentionally vague. The enduring consequence of such actions is a weakened legislative branch and a disproportionately empowered executive, leading to a shift in the balance of power that can have long-lasting impacts on governance. This situation calls for a reevaluation of existing laws governing appointments to ensure they are robust enough to withstand exploitation by any administration seeking to extend its influence unduly.
