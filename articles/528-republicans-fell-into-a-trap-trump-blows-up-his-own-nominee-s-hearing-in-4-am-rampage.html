'Republicans fell into a trap': Trump blows up his own nominee's hearing in 4 AM rampage

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The SetupWho Actually Holds PowerThe Blame GameThe Real ContemptWhat the Article MissesThe Larger Pattern
Trump Treats National Security Like a Poker Chip

The Setup

Donald Trump says he is canceling a Senate hearing for his DNI nominee, Jay Clayton, because Republicans “fell into a trap” over FISA and a separate U.S. attorney confirmation fight. The immediate story is about appointments and surveillance law. The real story is that Trump is openly turning both into leverage, then pretending the resulting mess was someone else’s manipulation.

Who Actually Holds Power

The power here is not with the Senate Intelligence Committee chairman, Tom Cotton, even if Senate procedure would normally place the hearing in his hands. Trump is the one announcing the cancellation at 3:54 a.m. on Truth Social, dictating the terms, and insisting the hearing will not go forward until his preferred prosecutor replacement is approved. That is executive power being used to override institutional process in public.

The Blame Game

Trump frames Democrats as the betrayers: they allegedly agreed to a deal, then “broke” it by threatening to vote against FISA. That framing is convenient, but it hides the more important fact that Republicans chose to bundle unrelated priorities into a bargaining scheme in the first place. The article presents this as a legislative trap. More accurately, it reads like self-inflicted hostage-taking, followed by a search for a scapegoat.

The Real Contempt

The source notes that Bill Pulte had already drawn bipartisan criticism as unqualified to serve as DNI, and yet he was still in position as acting director. That detail matters. The issue is not just partisan gamesmanship; it is the casual degradation of national security appointments into loyalty tests and placeholders. If the figure being installed is widely viewed as lacking minimal qualifications, then the machinery here is not merely clumsy. It is contemptuous.

What the Article Misses

The weakest part of the reporting frame is the assumption that this is mainly a dispute over timing or legislative strategy. It is bigger than that. Trump is not reacting to institutional boundaries; he is advertising that he can ignore them when they interfere with his dealmaking. The Senate is treated as a venue for ratifying his preferences, not as a branch with its own authority.

The Larger Pattern

This is the governing model: convert public institutions into bargaining chips, create the crisis yourself, then accuse opponents of manufacturing disorder. Surveillance policy, prosecution appointments, and intelligence leadership become pieces on a board controlled by the White House through pressure and spectacle. That is not confusion. It is a method.
