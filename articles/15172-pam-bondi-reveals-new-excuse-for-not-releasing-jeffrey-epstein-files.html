Pam Bondi reveals new excuse for not releasing Jeffrey Epstein files

Attorney General Pam Bondi offered another excuse this week for the delay in releasing files related to wealthy sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein.

Over two months after Bondi initially claimed she was releasing the Epstein documents, the attorney general was asked about the delay on Wednesday.

"The FBI, they're reviewing, there are tens of thousands of videos of Epstein with children or child porn, and there are hundreds of victims, and no one victim will ever get released," she told reporters at the White House.

"It's just the volume, and that's what they're going through right now," Bondi added. "The FBI is diligently going through that."

The attorney general also dismissed claims made by Rep. James Comer (R-KY) that some files had been destroyed.

"I haven't seen that statement, but I'll call him later and find out," Bondi said.

The failure to release the Epstein files has been a sore point with President Donald Trump's MAGA base.

Our Analysis:

The Evasive Maneuvers of Attorney General Pam Bondi

Pam Bondi's latest excuse for the sluggish pace in releasing the Jeffrey Epstein files is both a revelation and a testament to bureaucratic dilly-dallying at its finest. Two months have passed since Bondi boldly claimed she would unveil documents related to the notorious sex trafficker. Yet, here we stand, no closer to the truth, with only more excuses to chew on.

Let's dissect the Attorney General's reasoning: an overwhelming volume of evidence, comprising tens of thousands of videos of Epstein engaging in criminal activities with minors and a staggering number of victims. While the magnitude of these files is undoubtedly vast, Bondi's justification raises more questions than it answers. Why now? Why is the volume of evidence suddenly an insurmountable obstacle, two months after her initial promise?

Bondi's dismissal of Rep. James Comer's claims that some files had been destroyed is equally troubling. Her response, "I haven't seen that statement, but I'll call him later and find out," is a masterclass in evasiveness. It is not the response of someone diligently pursuing the truth but that of someone caught unprepared and looking to deflect.

Furthermore, Bondi's remarks were made at the White House, adding a layer of political gravity to her statements. It's hard not to view her comments through a political lens, given the setting and the personages involved. The delay in releasing the Epstein files is not just a procedural hiccup; it's a sore point with President Donald Trump's MAGA base, indicating the potential for political fallout.

The FBI's role in this saga is also worth scrutinizing. Bondi assures us that the FBI is "diligently" sifting through the evidence. However, given the high-profile nature of this case and its implications, the public's patience for bureaucratic diligence is wearing thin. The victims and the public deserve transparency and swift justice, not endless delays shrouded in vague assurances.

In conclusion, Attorney General Pam Bondi's explanations for the delay in releasing the Epstein files are insufficient and unconvincing. They serve as a stark reminder of the challenges in holding powerful figures accountable and the ease with which bureaucratic inertia can be used as a shield against transparency. The victims of Jeffrey Epstein's crimes, and indeed the public at large, deserve better than this.
