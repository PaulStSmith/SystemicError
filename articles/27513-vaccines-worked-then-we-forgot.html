Vaccines Worked Then We Forgot

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20268 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Vaccination made deadly diseases disappear from everyday life. Kennedy turned that success against the protection that produced it. Accountability must extend beyond resignation.

The empty polio ward should have settled the argument.

Children who never needed an iron lung. Parents who never buried a child killed by measles. Adults who lived without smallpox scars. Vaccination helped make those ordinary lives possible. Its success also created a political vulnerability: the people it protected often cannot see what they escaped.

Nobody interviews the parents outside the hospital their child never entered. There is no photograph of the funeral that never happened.

The injection remains visible. The catastrophe it prevented disappears.

Vaccine opponents exploit that imbalance. They point to a world made safer by vaccination and insist that vaccination was unnecessary. They inherit protection built through generations of scientific work, then undermine the institutions and habits that sustain it.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. brought that agenda into the federal government.

His departure from office would stop him exercising its powers. It would not answer what responsibility he bears for how he used them.

Before the American measles vaccination program began in 1963, an estimated three million to four million people contracted the disease annually. Among reported cases, approximately 400 to 500 people died each year, 48,000 were hospitalized, and 1,000 developed encephalitis.

That was the ordinary burden of a disease now dismissed as an ordinary childhood illness. The word “ordinary” concealed the hospitalizations, brain injuries, and funerals.

Polio’s retreat was equally concrete. The United States reported more than 21,000 paralytic cases in 1952. After vaccination began, the number fell to 2,525 in 1960 and 61 in 1965.

Those figures represent people spared paralysis. The classmates who could keep walking. The children who could breathe without mechanical assistance. The families who did not spend their lives organizing care around a preventable injury.

A WHO-led modeling study estimated that immunization against 14 diseases saved 154 million lives between 1974 and 2024, including 101 million infants. Measles vaccination accounted for approximately 94 million lives saved.

These are scientific estimates of deaths prevented, rather than individually counted people whose alternate lives can be observed. The scale is nevertheless extraordinary: vaccination changed who survived childhood.

Precision matters. Smallpox is the only human disease eradicated worldwide. Polio has been pushed toward eradication. Measles, mumps, and whooping cough have not been globally eradicated, nor are all three on the verge of it.

Their diminished presence in many people’s lives reflects protection that must be maintained. A disease suppressed by vaccination does not become harmless when people forget why they vaccinate.

The empty ward is evidence that prevention worked. It cannot justify dismantling prevention.

Kennedy’s public conduct supplies ample grounds for scrutiny without jokes about his reported parasite infection or attacks on his history of addiction. Neither establishes culpability. His statements and decisions are the relevant evidence.

On March 11, 2025, during an active measles outbreak, Kennedy told Sean Hannity that the measles vaccine “does cause deaths every year.” FactCheck.org found no evidence supporting that annual-death claim and documented other misleading assertions in the interview.

Vaccines can have adverse effects. Serious complications must be investigated, disclosed, and placed in their proper statistical context. Inflating those risks while minimizing the disease corrupts the informed consent Kennedy claims to defend.

The protective alternative was already established. Two doses of MMR are approximately 97% effective against measles. Some people cannot safely receive this live vaccine; others are too young for routine vaccination. Their vulnerability makes protection in the surrounding community especially important.

Kennedy has also endorsed MMR. That belongs in the record.

In April 2025, he publicly identified vaccination as the most effective means of preventing measles transmission. Later that same day, however, he promoted claims that doctors had healed hundreds of children using treatments lacking evidence for treating measles. FactCheck.org’s subsequent timeline documented supportive statements appearing alongside misleading claims that could discourage vaccination.

A health secretary cannot expect one accurate sentence to erase the consequences of the next misleading one.

Then came the exercise of institutional power.

On June 9, 2025, HHS announced that Kennedy had removed all 17 sitting members of the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices, the panel advising CDC on vaccine use. The department described the purge as a way to restore public trust. Its announcement also complained that existing appointments would have prevented the administration from choosing a majority until 2028.

HHS itself made political control part of the explanation for removing the experts.

On August 5, 2025, HHS announced a wind-down of BARDA’s mRNA vaccine development investments, affecting 22 projects worth nearly $500 million through cancellations and reductions.

Those projects involved a different vaccine technology from MMR. Their cancellation is not evidence that Kennedy caused measles deaths. It demonstrates the wider reach of his agenda: vaccine skepticism was being translated into decisions about public research and preparedness.

The consequences of inadequate measles protection were already visible. In April 2025, Texas announced a second death in its outbreak: an unvaccinated child with no reported underlying conditions.

By November 24, 2026, CDC had recorded 6,039 confirmed U.S. measles cases that year, compared with 2,289 in 2025 and 285 in 2024. Those surveillance figures remain preliminary.

On November 17, federal health authorities acknowledged that the United States had lost its measles elimination status. The country had first achieved that status in 2000. Losing it marked the return of sustained domestic transmission, rather than the appearance of an occasional imported infection.

Kennedy did not create every condition behind the resurgence. Vaccination coverage was falling before he took office. CDC reports kindergarten MMR coverage declining from 95.2% in 2019–2020 to 92.4% in 2025–2026. National averages also conceal communities with much larger gaps.

That history prevents an honest account from assigning the entire outbreak to him. It also establishes the vulnerability he inherited. His responsibility was to strengthen protection against a known threat. His record includes misleading statements about that protection and the wholesale removal of the experts guiding it.

There was ample warning of what collapsing vaccination coverage could produce.

Samoa’s 2019 measles epidemic involved 5,707 cases and 83 deaths. Coverage had fallen sharply before the outbreak. WHO and UNICEF documented the catastrophe and the emergency response.

Kennedy visited Samoa before the epidemic. Associated Press later reported that newly obtained emails undermined his account of the trip; Samoan officials said the visit strengthened anti-vaccine activists’ credibility. Those findings do not establish that he personally caused Samoa’s deaths. They place his activity within a documented history of vaccine distrust and lethal consequences.

The accusation against Kennedy concerns foreseeable harm. A public official need not desire deaths for his conduct to warrant investigation into responsibility for them. The relevant questions include what he knew, which warnings he received, what he chose to do, and what harm those choices caused.

Declaring a homicide offense proved would run ahead of the evidence. Treating resignation as sufficient would abandon the effort to establish it.

Congressional oversight and independent investigation should preserve and examine the records: scientific briefings, internal warnings, communications about public guidance, decisions affecting vaccination programs, and any interference with disease surveillance or reporting.

Investigators must distinguish existing failures from damage Kennedy added. Prosecutors must assess applicable law, jurisdiction, causation, and the evidence of his knowledge and conduct. Where that evidence supports criminal charges, they should pursue them.

Prison must remain a possible consequence if a prosecutable crime is established. Resignation must not become an escape hatch.

Responsibility also extends to the people who entrusted him with power. Trump appointed him. The senators who confirmed him authorized his leadership of the nation’s health department. Their decisions deserve scrutiny alongside his.

Vaccination gave millions of people the privilege of growing up without firsthand knowledge of these diseases. Anti-vaccine politics exploits that privilege while endangering the next generation.

Children should not have to acquire their grandparents’ diseases to teach adults their grandparents’ lessons.

The empty polio ward was an achievement. Officials who undermine the protection that emptied it owe the public far more than a resignation letter.
