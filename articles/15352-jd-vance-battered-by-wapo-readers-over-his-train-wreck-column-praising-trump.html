JD Vance battered by WaPo readers over his 'train wreck' column praising Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Vice President JD Vance is reflected on a mirror, on the day President Donald Trump and British Prime Minister Keir Starmer meet at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 27, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Readers of the Washington Post ripped apart an op-ed written by Vice President J.D. Vance Friday morning, which praised President Donald Trump’s first 100 days.

Vance believes, “In his first term, President Trump also began the critical process of reindustrializing the American economy and rebalancing relations with our trading partners. That includes some like China, which have taken advantage of our workers for decades."

"He was elected again to finish the job, and that’s exactly what we’re doing."

To which one user replied, “I don’t believe you can 're-industrialize' by crippling the economy. Or by being out of step with the majority American workers who chooses not to work in factories or coal mines,” wrote one user.

Multiple people had thoughts about Vance’s comments on the tariffs and trade deals. “Our trade negotiations with India are hardly alone. In the weeks since Liberation Day, the Trump administration has heard from 130 different trading partners seeking negotiations, with nearly 20 already having sent written proposals,” the Vice President wrote.

“The only thing Trump has created is chaos. Working class people bear the brunt of this chaos with higher prices and taxes (tariffs),” wrote Jack Forsithe.

Sargar had several questions for the Vice President, “Yes, Trump tanked Biden's good economy; how's that working out? Alienated the world against America; how's that working out? Making China stronger and more successful; how's that working out?”

Vance claimed of Trump, “He is fighting for common-sense values, including freedom of expression and freedom of religion.”

However TOMGBY believes it not freedom Trump is fighting for, “So we are revisiting the nationalist agenda of the 1930’s with an economy based on recreating the market crash of 1929."

"Everything Trump is accomplishing is by trampling on the Constitution, the Congress, the Courts and the rights of individuals! And you praise that!? Many things that the Biden administration accomplished were to benefit people! Almost all of Trump’s actions benefit few if any beyond soothing his childish ego," they added.

Another person summed up the whole column very simply. “This is not an opinion column, it's a minion column. Minion: submissive follower or underling, often implying someone who is servile and unimportant. See also, JD Vance,” they wrote.

Our Analysis:

Vance's Ode to Trump: A Masterclass in Misdirection

In an op-ed that could only be described as a literary genuflection before the altar of Donald Trump, Vice President J.D. Vance extols the first 100 days of Trump's presidency with a fervor that oscillates between blind adulation and glaring oversight. The Washington Post readers, in their wisdom, were quick to dissect Vance's arguments, revealing a narrative so deeply flawed it borders on the fantastical.

Reindustrialization or Regression?

Vance's claim that Trump initiated the "critical process of reindustrializing the American economy" warrants scrutiny. The notion that a return to coal mines and factory floors is the panacea to America's economic woes is not just antiquated; it's dangerously myopic. It neglects the global shift towards renewable energy and technology-driven industries, sectors that promise sustainable job creation far removed from the precarious positions in industries Trump romanticizes. One reader's rebuttal encapsulates this perfectly, highlighting the dissonance between Vance's vision and the aspirations of the majority of American workers.

The Tariff Trap

The op-ed's comments on tariffs and trade negotiations further expose the chasm between Vance's rhetoric and economic reality. While Vance paints a picture of a world clamoring for trade deals with the U.S., the reality, as pointed out by reader Jack Forsithe, is a landscape of chaos where the working class bears the brunt of Trump's erratic trade policies through higher prices and tariffs. This isn't economic strategy; it's economic sabotage.

Values or Vandalism?

Perhaps most egregious is Vance's assertion that Trump is a crusader for "common-sense values," including freedom of expression and freedom of religion. This claim flies in the face of a presidency marked by attempts to stifle dissent, undermine the press, and enact policies that have been criticized as discriminatory under the guise of religious freedom. The reader TOMGBY's comparison of Trump's agenda to the nationalist fervor of the 1930s is a chilling reminder of the dangers of conflating demagoguery with defense of values.

A Minion's Tale

The concluding critique, branding Vance's column as a "minion column," is a fitting epitaph for an op-ed that serves not as a piece of analysis but as a testament to Vance's subservience to Trump's narrative. It's a stark illustration of the intellectual acrobatics some are willing to perform to remain in the good graces of power, sacrificing integrity at the altar of political expediency.

In sum, Vance's op-ed is less a triumphant recounting of Trump's first 100 days and more a vivid display of the cognitive gymnastics necessary to ignore the myriad ways in which the Trump presidency has been marked by regression, both economically and socially. The Washington Post readers' critiques serve not only as a refutation of Vance's claims but as a clarion call for a more nuanced and fact-based discourse on the legacy of Trump's policies.
