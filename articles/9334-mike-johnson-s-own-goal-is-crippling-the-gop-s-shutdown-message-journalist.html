Mike Johnson's 'own goal' is crippling the GOP's shutdown message: journalist

The Republican Party’s message to voters that the government shutdown, with no end in sight, is the fault of Democrats is being muddled by House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) whose refusal to seat a newly elected Democrat is sucking up all the oxygen in the room, according to a journalist.

During an MSNBC “Morning Joe” panel discussion on the budget impasse, Politico’s Jonathan Martin claimed Johnson is handcuffing his own party’s attacks on Democrats by not swearing in Arizona’s Adelita Grijalva, who was elected to fill her late father’s seat in the House.

After it was pointed out that Arizona’s attorney general is now suing the speaker for dragging his feet, Martin noted that Johnson’s fears about the possible release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, with Grijalva being the deciding vote, is complicating getting the government back to work.

“I mean, this is about, it’s about, at least in part, the Epstein files,” he pointed out. “They [Republicans] know that she’s the decisive vote here — would be able to release that.”

“Moreover, I mean, this is also just reflective, it seems to me, of an own goal,” he suggested. “It’s a reminder he’s keeping the House out. And that’s something, at a moment of the shutdown, that I think a lot of that’s contributing, I think, to why the Republicans are faring so poorly in the polls.”

Our Analysis:

The Republican Paradox: Self-Sabotage and the Blame Game

The current narrative unfurling in the halls of power in Washington offers a textbook example of political paradox, one where the Republican Party, ostensibly masters of messaging and manipulation, finds itself ensnared in its own web of contradictions. At the center of this convoluted saga is House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), whose actions—or lack thereof—are not just undermining his party’s attempts to shift blame onto Democrats for the government shutdown but are emblematic of a broader crisis of leadership and accountability.

The Unseated Democrat: A Symptom of a Larger Disease

Let’s dissect the situation surrounding the refusal to seat Adelita Grijalva, a newly elected Democrat poised to fill her late father’s seat. This isn’t merely an administrative hiccup or a procedural delay; it’s a clear-cut manifestation of partisan pettiness impeding governance. Johnson’s reluctance, as reported, is not just about a seat—it’s about the potential release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, with Grijalva holding the decisive vote. The implications here are staggering: are we to understand that political machinations and the fear of transparency are now valid reasons to override electoral outcomes?

The Epstein Excuse: A Convenient Distraction?

The invocation of the Jeffrey Epstein files by Johnson as a point of concern is a red herring of the highest order. It’s a smoke screen designed to distract from the real issue at hand: the undermining of democratic principles. The idea that the release of these files is a legitimate basis to stall the functioning of government is preposterous. It suggests that the Republican leadership is more concerned with potential embarrassments or revelations than with ensuring the government is operational and serving its people.

Polling and Public Perception: The Unraveling of a Strategy

The Republican strategy, as it were, appears to be backfiring spectacularly. Jonathan Martin of Politico highlights how Johnson’s maneuvers are contributing to the GOP’s nosedive in public opinion polls. This isn’t rocket science; it’s a simple equation. When you blatantly disregard democratic norms, use scare tactics over transparency, and halt government operations, the public notices. The GOP’s attempt to pin the shutdown on Democrats is not just failing—it’s imploding, with each passing day serving as a reminder of who is truly keeping the government shuttered.

The Own Goal: A Metaphor for Misguided Leadership

Martin’s characterization of the situation as an “own goal” is apt. In attempting to navigate the political landscape with a mix of fearmongering and obstruction, Johnson has scored against his own team. This incident is a microcosm of a larger, systemic problem within the GOP leadership: a tendency to engage in short-term, high-stakes gambits without considering the long-term damage to the institution of democracy itself.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and a Return to Principles

The saga of Mike Johnson and the unseated Adelita Grijalva is not just a political skirmish—it’s a glaring symptom of a diseased political system where partisan interests are routinely placed above the country’s. It’s high time for Republican leaders to introspect: Is the pursuit of power worth the erosion of democratic norms? The answer should be a resounding no. It’s imperative for the health of the American democratic system that leaders, irrespective of their party, prioritize governance over gamesmanship and transparency over tactical obstructions.

In a functioning democracy, elected officials are seated, not sidelined because of potential political inconvenience. The GOP’s current trajectory is unsustainable and, frankly, unacceptable. The American people deserve better—they deserve a government that works for them, not one that is paralyzed by its own leaders’ fear of accountability.
