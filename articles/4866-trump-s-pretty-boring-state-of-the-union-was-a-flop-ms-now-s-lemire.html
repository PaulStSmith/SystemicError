Donald Trump’s record-long State of the Union address got about as low of marks as possible from MS NOW’s Jonathan Lemire who claimed he couldn’t see it changing one person’s mind about the president’s disastrous second term -- according to recent polls.

Worse still, he added, the entire speech was a boring drag.

With the entire “Morning Joe” panel pouncing on Trump’s “serial lying,” Lemire claimed he heard nothing that he didn’t hear from the president’s rambling, almost daily, Oval Office appearances.

After observing, “Last night was President Trump’s biggest audience he will see all year, a national audience. Millions of people watched and he came into last night reeling,” he added, “I can’t imagine he changed a single mind last night. I can’t imagine he won over a single voter last night and that’s bad for the Republicans who will be on the ballot this November who have to defend his policies.”

“And I was also just struck, frankly, it’s pretty boring,” he reported. “Yes, it had a couple nice moments of patriotism; he saluted the men’s [Olympic] hockey team. He singled out a few Americans who deserve to be recognized for their heroism at home or abroad. But it felt like a campaign speech. It felt like more of the same.”

“Like, yes, he was loud. Yes, he, you know, was animated. I don’t know, though that it’s going to change the trajectory of his presidency one bit,” he predicted.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s SOTU: A Masterclass in Monotony and Missed Opportunities

Donald Trump’s latest State of the Union address appears to be a record-setter, not in innovation or inspiration, but in inducing widespread yawns and eye rolls, if MS NOW’s Jonathan Lemire’s scathing review is anything to go by. In a political landscape where every speech can be a pivotal moment, Trump seemingly squandered what Lemire describes as his “biggest audience he will see all year.” Let’s dissect this debacle and its implications for the political circus that is Washington.

The Unchanging Trump Show

At the heart of Lemire’s critique is an accusation that Trump’s speech was a rerun of his “rambling, almost daily, Oval Office appearances.” This isn’t just a missed opportunity; it’s a glaring testament to Trump’s apparent inability or unwillingness to adapt, evolve, or innovate. In an era where change is the only constant, Trump’s steadfast adherence to his usual rhetoric is not just boring—it’s borderline negligent. It begs the question: if you have the nation’s undivided attention and choose to say nothing new, do you really have anything to say at all?

A Symphony of Snore

Describing the speech as a “boring drag,” Lemire touches on an issue that’s perhaps even more damaging than the recycled content: the presentation. It’s one thing to stick to your guns, content-wise, but to fail to deliver it in a way that engages, especially during a State of the Union address, is a clear misstep. Trump’s speech, punctuated by the occasional nod to patriotism and heroism, couldn’t shake off its inherent dreariness, akin to a tepid rerun of a show you’ve seen too many times.

No Minds Changed

One of Lemire’s most critical assessments is the claim that Trump likely “didn’t change a single mind” or “win over a single voter.” In a period characterized by deep political divisions and widespread uncertainty, the ability to sway the undecided or even soften critics is invaluable. Trump’s failure in this department isn’t just a personal loss; it’s a missed opportunity for the Republican Party, which, as Lemire points out, will soon have to defend his policies at the ballot box.

The Verdict: A Missed Opportunity

The overarching theme of Lemire’s review and, by extension, Trump’s State of the Union address, is one of missed opportunities. In a moment designed for unity, inspiration, and direction, the president opted for redundancy over reinvention, monotony over motivation. For an administration that prides itself on defying expectations, this was a moment remarkably in line with them—predictably uninspired and uninspiring.

In conclusion, Lemire’s critique paints a picture of a presidency stuck in a loop, incapable of breaking free from its own shadow. The State of the Union address, a golden opportunity to reset, reassure, and realign, was reduced to little more than background noise—a forgettable blip in the relentless news cycle. For a president who thrives on the spectacle, this may be the most damning indictment of all: not that he said too much, but rather, that he had nothing new to say at all.
