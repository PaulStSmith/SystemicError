'Start arresting!' Bannon calls for locking up officials 'stirring the pot'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Dallas, TX - August 6, 2022: Former White House’s chief strategist for Donald Trump Administration Steve Bannon reports from CPAC Texas 2022 conference at Hilton Anatole (Shutterstock)

MAGA pontificator Steve Bannon claimed that it's time to ignore claims of "authoritarianism" and start locking up government officials who are stoking "civil unrest" in the United States, according to an article in The Spectator.

Bannon told editor Freddy Gray that the left was "calling for [unrest] nationwide," and that "we’re in for another of summer of riots."

“They just kicked it off,” Bannon said of the unrest in Los Angeles.

“We need to start arresting government officials, including the Mayor of Los Angeles, Karen Bass, who’s stirring this pot up," Bannon said, even if it means suspending habeas corpus.

“If Gavin Newsom is saying, ‘Hey, come on, arrest me.’ Hey, well, if he gets in the way of federal officials trying to sort this mess out, he should be arrested,” Bannon said.

Gray wrote, "Bannon is on something of a roll at the moment, having just, in his words 'taken out' Elon Musk from the Trump administration."

Bannon has long been at odds with Musk, whom he calls a "dangerous narcissist” and an "illegal alien" who's controlled by the Chinese Communist Party and needs to be deported back to South Africa.

Further, Bannon claimed that the Trump administration will be using "its legal powers to bust up the monopolistic power of Big Tech."

“We’re going to break up Facebook," Bannon said. "We’re going to break up Google. We’re going to break up Amazon. We’re going to break… I think hopefully we get to eventually break up Walmart. You’ve got too much concentration of private power. It’s obvious it’s anti-populist. It’s anti-economic nationalist."

Bannon continued, "The tech bros at their core are soft. It’s like the elites in England. It’s like the elites know England went from a country that ran the world. You know why? Because it generated people that had character values and were tough. You’re not tough anymore. You’re a bunch of f------ p------. Just like the tech bros. They lived a soft life. They’ve never had to make hard decisions. They’ve never had any kind of real challenges in their life, hard challenges. And that’s where they’re soft. And the soft will never beat the hard.”

Our Analysis:

Bannon's Authoritarian Blueprint: A Dive Into the Abyss of Hypocrisy

In a move that reeks of irony so potent it could only emanate from the depths of the MAGA movement's most fervent echo chambers, Steve Bannon, the self-proclaimed crusader against the "elites," has now openly endorsed the kind of authoritarian measures that would make even the most hardened autocrats blush. According to a recent article in The Spectator, Bannon has laid out a vision so dystopian, it warrants a thorough dissection.

The Call to Arrest Government Officials

Bannon's assertion that it's time to "start locking up government officials" for allegedly stoking civil unrest is not only a gross misinterpretation of the situation but a dangerous precedent. His target, among others, is Karen Bass, the Mayor of Los Angeles, whom he accuses of "stirring this pot up." This is nothing short of a call for political repression, wrapped in the thin veneer of restoring order. Bannon's willingness to suspend habeas corpus—a fundamental protection against arbitrary detention—is a testament to his disregard for the rule of law, a cornerstone of democratic governance.

The Demonization of Opposition

Bannon's rhetoric further spirals into the abyss as he takes aim at California Governor Gavin Newsom, suggesting that Newsom's hypothetical challenge to be arrested if he impedes "federal officials trying to sort this mess out" should be taken up. This line of thinking is not only dangerous but indicative of a broader strategy to demonize and delegitimize political opposition. It's a classic authoritarian tactic: portray your political adversaries as not just wrong, but as existential threats to the nation that must be dealt with by any means necessary.

The War on Big Tech: A Populist Masquerade

Bannon's declaration of war against Big Tech is a masterclass in populist posturing. By vowing to "break up Facebook," "Google," "Amazon," and even "Walmart," Bannon is attempting to don the mantle of an anti-monopoly crusader. Yet, this stance is deeply incongruent with the broader MAGA movement's embrace of unchecked capitalism and its benefits to the elite. The irony is palpable when considering Bannon's own elite status and history of benefiting from the very system he now criticizes. This is not a genuine call for economic populism; it's a calculated move to exploit genuine grievances for political gain.

A Descent into Personal Attacks

Bannon's tirade against Elon Musk as a "dangerous narcissist" and an "illegal alien" controlled by the Chinese Communist Party is a descent into the kind of baseless, personal attacks that have become all too common in political discourse. It's a distraction, a way to shift focus from substantive policy discussions to sensationalist and unfounded claims. This tactic not only undermines the credibility of the accuser but poisons the well of public debate.

The Cult of Toughness

Bannon's lamentation of the "soft" tech bros and elites, contrasted with a nostalgic yearning for a past when people were "tough" and had "character values," is a thinly-veiled appeal to toxic masculinity. It's a misguided belief that strength and toughness are the ultimate virtues, ignoring the complexities of leadership and governance. This rhetoric is not just divisive; it's dangerous, promoting a vision of society where might makes right and empathy is a weakness.

In conclusion, Bannon's comments to The Spectator are a stark reminder of the authoritarian impulses lurking within certain factions of the MAGA movement. This vision of America—one where political opponents are jailed without due process, where dissent is crushed under the guise of law and order, and where might is right—is not only fundamentally un-American; it's a recipe for tyranny. As we dissect Bannon's words, let us not forget the lessons of history: democracy is fragile, and the cost of silence in the face of authoritarianism is far too high.
