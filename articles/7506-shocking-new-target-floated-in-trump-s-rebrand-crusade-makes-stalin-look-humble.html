Shocking new target floated in Trump's rebrand crusade: 'Makes Stalin look humble'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media while on his way to board Marine One to depart for Joint Base Andrews, from the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 22, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump’s handpicked board of trustees voted on Thursday to add Trump’s name to the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, while allegedly muting the objections of Democratic members of the board and likely in violation of federal law that named the Kennedy Center by statute.

Democratic lawmakers reacted to the news with disgust and dismissal, telling Raw Story the rename will not stick and will be reversed swiftly when they retake power in Washington.

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) had one of the most forceful reactions, speculating MAGA allies will eventually try to rebrand the White House.

“Well, I mean, he appointed all the sycophants to the board,” she said. “So I mean, they’re going to name the White House — they’re going to try to name the White House after him before we’re done with this, and then we’re going to take the White House back and we’re going to fix it all. Enjoy having two years of that.”

Asked for comment, Rep. Brendan Boyle (D-PA) mockingly claimed that the Longworth House Office Building will have his name added to it because he says so.

Boyle added, “Everything’s about him, the plaques that he actually put up, plaques with his, with his nonsense” — a reference to a series of juvenile plaques installed below various former presidents’ pictures written by Trump himself.

Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA) took a similar position to Ocasio-Cortez.

“An infinite ego. You know, I mean, I just, it makes Stalin look humble. The board that he handpicked, it’s embarrassing and, and it won’t last very long.”

Our Analysis:

The Trumpian Revision of History: A Critical Analysis

In a move that’s as audacious as it is absurd, President Donald Trump’s handpicked board of trustees at the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts has voted to etch the Trump name onto this venerable institution. This decision, allegedly steamrolling over Democratic objections and potentially flouting federal law, sparks a critical examination of the Trump administration’s relentless pursuit to immortalize its legacy by any means necessary.

The Kennedy Center: Named by statute, the Kennedy Center stands as a beacon of American culture and artistic achievement. The attempt to rename it in honor of Trump not only challenges legal boundaries but also raises profound ethical questions about the politicization of national cultural institutions. The law isn’t a mere suggestion, yet this administration seems to treat it as an inconvenient obstacle in its quest for adulation.

Democratic Response: The reaction from Democratic lawmakers ranges from disgust to mockery, highlighting a bipartisan concern over Trump’s overt self-aggrandizement. Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s comments encapsulate the broader anxiety that Trump’s influence, if left unchecked, could extend even to the sacrosanct White House. This hyperbolic vision, while seemingly outlandish, underscores the perceived extremity of Trump’s ego-driven politics.

Rep. Brendan Boyle’s sarcastic retort about renaming the Longworth House Office Building after himself serves as a biting critique of Trump’s narcissism. It’s a poignant reminder that public service and public recognition are two vastly different concepts—a line blurred incessantly by Trump’s actions.

Ego vs. Humility: The comparison of Trump to Stalin by Rep. Don Beyer, in terms of ego, is particularly scathing. While hyperbolic, it emphasizes the severity with which some view Trump’s attempts to immortalize his presidency. The real danger lies not in the renaming of buildings but in the precedent it sets for distorting democratic values and historical truth in service of one man’s legacy.

The Board’s Role: The complicity of Trump’s “handpicked” board in this renaming saga cannot be overlooked. It’s a glaring example of how cronyism can undermine institutional integrity and independence. The board’s decision, likely influenced by allegiance rather than artistic or cultural considerations, reflects a broader trend of governance under Trump—where loyalty is prized over legality and ethics.

Conclusion: The proposed renaming of the Kennedy Center is more than a mere act of vanity; it’s a symbol of a deeper, systemic issue within the Trump administration. It’s an attempt to rewrite history, to claim cultural victories unearned, and to place personal legacy above national heritage. The swift backlash from Democratic lawmakers not only provides hope for a reversal of this decision but also serves as a rallying cry for the preservation of America’s cultural institutions against the tide of political egoism.

In the broader narrative of Trump’s presidency, this episode might be just a footnote. Yet, it’s emblematic of a tenure marked by self-interest, disregard for tradition, and a relentless desire to impose a personal brand on the fabric of American society. The battle over the Kennedy Center’s name is a microcosm of a larger struggle for the soul of America’s public spaces and historical narrative. It’s a struggle that requires vigilance, lest we find ourselves living in a landscape littered with monuments not to collective achievement, but to individual hubris.
