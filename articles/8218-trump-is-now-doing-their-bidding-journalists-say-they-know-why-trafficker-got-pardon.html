President Donald Trump has been hammered over his recent pardon of a convicted Honduran drug trafficker, with the president being pressed for an explanation as his administration escalates military pressure on Venezuela over alleged narcotics trafficking. But on Sunday, two journalists said they’ve uncovered the real reason behind the move.

On Friday, Trump said he plans to pardon Juan Orlando Hernández, former president of Honduras and convicted drug trafficker who, according to court testimony, planned to “stuff the drugs right up the noses of the gringos.”

The move comes amid Trump declaring Venezuela’s air space to be closed, ongoing strikes on suspected Venezuelan drug-carrying sea vessels, and the deployment of an aircraft carrier strike group to Venezuela’s coast – all with the purported aim of combating drug trafficking.

While Republican lawmakers have struggled to reconcile Trump’s war on drugs with his pardoning of a major drug trafficker, journalist Pedro Gonzalez said recently that he believes he’s figured out the reason.

“There’s a 90% chance Trump wants to pardon Juan Orlando Hernández, the former president of Honduras, who was convicted for his role in a major drug trafficking enterprise, because he is connected to people like Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen and the rest of the tech bro morons,” wrote Chronicles Magazine correspondent Gonzalez in a recent social media post on X.

Thiel and Andreessen – two billionaire tech industry giants that hold major influence over the Trump administration – are both strong backers of Honduran Zones for Employment and Economic Development, or ZEDEs, a proposal for a type of autonomous city or area with its own administrative and political system operating on libertarian principles.

Hernández was a supporter and promoter ZEDEs, which Gonzalez – tying the two topics together – suspects is the primary reason for Trump’s unprecedented and poorly timed pardon of the convicted drug trafficker.

“Let’s be very clear: the narco dictator Trump is pardoning was beloved by the crypto world for creating lawless, sovereign zones for tech utopias organized around crypto,” wrote journalist Ryan Grim in a social media post on X. “The current [government] moved to shut them down. The crypto class fought back and Trump is now doing their bidding.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Pardoning of a Convicted Drug Trafficker: A Closer Look

In a move that can only be described as both bewildering and deeply troubling, President Donald Trump has decided to pardon Juan Orlando Hernández, the former president of Honduras and a convicted drug trafficker. This decision comes at a time when Trump’s administration is purportedly ramping up military efforts to combat drug trafficking in Venezuela. The contradiction here is as stark as it is absurd.

The Surface-Level Hypocrisy

On the surface, the hypocrisy of escalating military actions against Venezuela for alleged narcotics trafficking while pardoning a notorious drug trafficker is glaring. It’s akin to setting fire to a house under the guise of pest control, only to rescue the very pests you vowed to exterminate. The Republican lawmakers, who are often too quick to champion Trump’s every move, find themselves in a quagmire, struggling to weave a coherent narrative around this debacle.

The Underlying Motives

Journalists Pedro Gonzalez and Ryan Grim have peeled back the layers on this sordid onion, revealing a stench that wafts from the intersection of politics, tech utopias, and libertarian fantasies. The connection between Hernández and tech billionaires like Peter Thiel and Marc Andreessen, who are staunch supporters of Honduran Zones for Employment and Economic Development (ZEDEs), is telling.

ZEDEs, for the uninitiated, are proposed autonomous cities with their own rules, detached from the legal and social frameworks of their host countries. Hernández was a promoter of these libertarian playgrounds, which should raise eyebrows not just for their potential to harbor unregulated drug markets but for their embodiment of lawless capitalism.

Gonzalez’s and Grim’s analyses suggest that Trump’s pardon is less about any misguided sense of justice and more about appeasing the tech elite who dream of a world where their wealth and influence can literally buy them sovereignty. The irony here is as rich as it is infuriating — a convicted drug trafficker pardoned because he champions the kind of deregulated havens that could facilitate the very crimes for which he was imprisoned.

The Crypto Connection

Adding another layer of intrigue (and perhaps, inevitability) to this saga is the crypto world’s lament over the current government’s move to shut down these ZEDEs. Hernández was their champion, and by extension, so is Trump, doing the bidding of the “crypto class” under the guise of anti-drug warfare. This isn’t just about drugs; it’s about the battle for a future where tech moguls can operate with impunity, governed only by their whims and the algorithms that enrich them.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

This pardon is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger, systemic error where wealth, influence, and utopian tech fantasies can sway the highest decisions of power, even at the cost of contradicting publicly stated goals. Trump’s actions here are not just poorly timed; they are a stark illustration of the dangers of allowing a nexus of tech billionaires and libertarian ideologies to influence governance to the point where convicted drug traffickers are pardoned, not for any noble reason, but to further the interests of the few.

The implications are as dangerous as they are clear: In the world Trump and his ilk are trying to build, money and influence trump justice, coherence, and the common good.
