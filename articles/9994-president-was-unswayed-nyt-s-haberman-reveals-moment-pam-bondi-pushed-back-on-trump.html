'President was unswayed': NYT's Haberman reveals moment Pam Bondi 'pushed back' on Trump

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump looks on as Attorney General Pam Bondi speaks. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Attorney General Pam Bondi stood up to Donald Trump in the course of a recent DOJ shake up, according to new reporting.

Bondi has publicly supported the president’s moves, but a Saturday article from Maggie Haberman and others at the New York Times shows the behind-the-scenes push back.

It started with James Comey, whom Trump had sought to prosecute. According to the report, things turned heated with “the resignation of Erik S. Siebert, the U.S. attorney who believed the evidence against Mr. Comey was insufficient.”

“By mid-September, Mr. Trump was determined to rid himself of Mr. Siebert, a 15-year veteran of the office. Ms. Bondi and Mr. Blanche, who had worked closely with Mr. Siebert on immigration, drug and gang cases, pushed back,” according to the Times. “The president was unswayed. On Friday, Sept. 19, he told reporters he wanted Mr. Siebert to leave. The prosecutor, who had hoped to find another job in the department, knew that time was up and resigned, according to officials in his office who requested anonymity to avoid retribution.”

The report continues:

“The next morning, shellshocked staff members in the U.S. attorney’s office awakened to find an email in their inboxes from Maggie Cleary, a veteran state prosecutor, saying she was their new boss.”

Bondi and Blanche kept pushing back with Trump’s new appointment, as well, the report states.

“Mr. Trump announced that Ms. Halligan, a former member of his criminal defense team who had previously specialized in insurance law, would run the office. That her first order of business was indicting Mr. Comey was obvious,” the article notes. “Ms. Bondi and Mr. Blanche had not been given much of a head’s up, or veto power, over the pick. Both quietly questioned Ms. Halligan’s credentials to run such an important U.S. attorney’s office, according to a person with knowledge of her thinking.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Shadows of Power: The Bondi-Trump Confrontation

In a recent revelation by Maggie Haberman and her team at the New York Times, a complex narrative of loyalty, defiance, and the tumultuous dynamics within the Department of Justice has been unveiled, centering around Attorney General Pam Bondi and President Donald Trump. This tale not only exposes the fissures within the Trump administration but also brings to light the precarious balance of power and principle.

The Comey Conundrum

At the heart of this drama is the figure of James Comey, whose potential prosecution became a pivot for controversy. Trump’s desire to prosecute Comey, a move fraught with political peril, sets the stage for a crisis within the DOJ. Enter Erik S. Siebert, the U.S. attorney with a backbone, who found the evidence against Comey insufficient for prosecution. His resignation, catalyzed by Trump’s dissatisfaction, marks a significant turning point, illustrating the dangerous territory of allowing political vendettas to dictate the course of justice.

Bondi’s Rebellion

Amidst this chaos, Pam Bondi emerges not merely as a character in Trump’s narrative but as a formidable actor in her own right. Despite her public alignment with Trump’s policies, Bondi’s behind-the-scenes resistance to the president’s attempts to manipulate the DOJ showcases a rare instance of backbone in Trump’s often-unquestioning cabinet.

The appointment of Maggie Cleary and later, Ms. Halligan, a former member of Trump’s criminal defense team with a background in insurance law, as heads of the U.S. attorney’s office, reveals a disturbing trend of favoritism and cronyism. The selection of Halligan, in particular, whose first order of business was indicting Comey, signals a blatant misuse of power for personal vendettas.

The Unanswered Questions

Where does this leave us? With an administration embroiled in its own internal conflicts, the real casualty here is the integrity of the Justice Department. Bondi and her colleague Mr. Blanche’s pushback against Trump’s maneuvers may be commendable, but it also raises questions about the extent of their opposition and its ultimate effectiveness. Their inability to stop Halligan’s appointment, coupled with their lack of “head’s up” or “veto power,” underscores a harrowing reality: the erosion of institutional norms and the consolidation of power by those loyal to Trump, rather than to the Constitution or the rule of law.

Conclusion: The Price of Political Warfare

This narrative is more than a mere political skirmish; it’s a symptom of a broader disease affecting the American political system. The Bondi-Trump confrontation, while significant, is merely a skirmish in the ongoing battle for the soul of the Justice Department—a battle that continues to be fought in the shadows, with the American public left to decipher the truth from the fragments left behind.

In the end, the New York Times report is not just a story about internal disputes; it’s a cautionary tale of what happens when power is unchecked, and loyalty to individuals supersedes commitment to democratic principles and the rule of law. The question remains: Will we heed this warning, or will it be lost in the cacophony of the next news cycle?
