The stark difference between 2020 and 2026

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Coup Is Not a Bug. It Is the Plan.

Donald Trump’s latest election rhetoric is not random bluster. The source piece describes a rehearsed script: declare victory was “rigged,” deploy ICE and the National Guard, seize ballot boxes, criminalize protest, and wrap the whole operation in emergency language. The point is not persuasion. It is pretext. This is what authoritarian politics looks like when it stops pretending to be embarrassed.

Power Is the Story

The central fact is institutional power. Trump is not imagining violence from the margins; he is planning to use the machinery of the state. The article is right to focus on the replacement of earlier restraints with loyalists like Blanche, Miller, and Homan. The whole architecture depends on staffing: who signs, who obeys, who delays, who looks away. That is where democratic collapse happens. Not in a dramatic speech alone, but in the offices and command chains that make the speech operational.

The relevant question is not whether Trump sounds extreme. He does. The question is whether the people around him are positioned to turn that extremism into state action. The source says they are.

Misdirection Is the Method

The framing here is not confusion. It is a deliberate laundering of intent. Trump, according to the source, would blame Democrats, foreigners, Antifa, and “communist radical domestic terrorists” for the very instability his own orders would create. That is classic authoritarian inversion: provoke the disorder, then use the disorder as justification for more power.

The article is also clear on motive. This is about avoiding investigation, avoiding conviction, and preserving personal impunity. That matters because it strips away the comforting lie that this is about election integrity or public safety. It is about one man’s legal survival and the faction built around him. The citizens are not the client. His freedom is.

The Real Enablers Are Ordinary

The strongest part of the source is that it refuses the lazy myth that history is made only by singular villains. Trump may initiate the chain, but the chain only works if ordinary officials comply: a National Guard captain, a county clerk, a local cop, a poll worker. That is the actual danger. Not obedience in the abstract, but obedience with a badge, a uniform, or a stamp.

This is where the article lands its sharpest political point. Authoritarian outcomes are assembled by small acts of compliance under pressure, each one rationalized as temporary, procedural, or above one person’s pay grade. That is how democratic institutions die while their personnel still insist they are just doing their jobs.

ICE Has No Democratic Role

If ICE is in the story, the democratic answer is abolition, not reform theater. ICE is built for coercion, family separation, fear, and political intimidation. A federal force that can be turned toward ballot suppression and domestic terror is not a neutral administrative tool with bad messaging. It is an instrument of state violence.

The source’s warning about masked ICE agents near ballot boxes should not be read as a narrow election-security concern. It is evidence that the agency itself is structurally incompatible with democratic legitimacy. You do not train your way out of that. You do not oversight your way out of that. You dismantle it.

Local Officials Are the Front Line

The article is right to push attention downward, because that is where resistance becomes real. County election officials, sheriffs, state legislators, governors, attorneys general, local lawyers, neighbors who know each other’s names: these are the actors who can either absorb a command or refuse it. National drama is secondary to whether local institutions have already decided what they will do when pressure arrives.

That is also why performative outrage is useless. Democratic survival will not be rescued by cable commentary or late-breaking speeches. It depends on whether local officials have the backbone, legal preparation, and public backing to say no when federal power comes dressed as emergency.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian projects do not need mass agreement, only enough compliance in the right places. Trump understands this. He is not merely threatening to break norms; he is mapping the weak points where norm-breakers, careerists, and cowards can be induced to help him do it.

That is the system Americans are actually living in: not a contest between equal parties, but a fight between institutions that can still be used for democratic accountability and a movement that treats those same institutions as obstacles to be seized. The work, then, is not to marvel at the danger. It is to name the people who will be asked to comply, and to make it harder for them to pretend they had no choice.
