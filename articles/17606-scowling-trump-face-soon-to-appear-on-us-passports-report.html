'Scowling Trump' face soon to appear on US passports: report

Branding Over Borders: Trump’s Passport Play

A Portrait of Power

The proposed redesign of the U.S. passport to include a portrait of President Donald Trump marks more than a superficial alteration; it signifies a deeper, more troubling shift toward personalizing what should be neutral, national symbols. Trump’s image, reportedly to be superimposed over the Declaration of Independence inside the passport, symbolizes a conflation of individual leadership and state identity that is unprecedented in modern U.S. history.

Institutional Decisions and Responsibilities

The decision to redesign the passport with Trump’s image originates from the State Department, under the guise of celebrating the 250th anniversary of American independence. This move, requiring approval and likely prompted by Trump himself, illustrates a direct use of executive influence over national symbols. The State Department’s compliance reveals a troubling acquiescence to personal vanity over public service integrity. Additionally, this redesign aligns with similar efforts across other federal departments, including the Treasury and the National Park Service, pointing to a coordinated effort to engrave Trump’s image on widely circulated national items.

Misdirection and Unprecedented Moves

Labeling this redesign as a commemorative act for the 250th anniversary of independence is a clear misdirection. Historical norms have kept such personal aggrandizements at bay, as noted by experts like Edward Kolla. The insertion of a current president’s image on crucial documents like passports (and potentially currency) disrupts democratic norms and risks setting a precedent where state symbolism is subject to the whims of its current leader, much like authoritarian regimes.

Systemic Pattern of Personalization

This passport redesign is not an isolated incident but part of a broader strategy to personalize governmental and national symbols under Trump’s brand. From renaming federal institutions after himself to introducing high-cost “Trump Card” visas, these actions collectively represent a shift toward an autocratic branding of state apparatuses. This strategy not only undermines the democratic separation between individual and state but also dilutes the symbolic meaning of national identity, reducing it to a mere extension of presidential ego.

Broader Political Insight

The move to redesign U.S. passports with a sitting president’s image encapsulates a larger, more disturbing trend toward the personalization of government under Trump’s administration. It exemplifies how the lines between state and individual can blur, pulling the U.S. toward a governance style that mirrors authoritarian regimes where the leader’s image is synonymous with the state. This strategy, if unchecked, threatens the foundational democratic principle that institutions belong to the people, not to any transient leader. The passport, a symbol of a citizen’s rights and identity, should not be co-opted as a tool for personal legacy building.
