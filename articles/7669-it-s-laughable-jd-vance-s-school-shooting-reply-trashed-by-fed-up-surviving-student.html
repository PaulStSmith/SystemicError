JD Vance on Saturday made a comment about the Brown University school shooting, and a surviving student called it “laughable.”

Vance this weekend responded to the shooting, which reports say led to at least two deaths, in part by soliciting prayers.

“Terrible news out of Rhode Island this evening. We’re all monitoring the situation and the FBI stands ready to do anything to help,” Vance wrote on social media before adding, “We’re all thinking of and praying for the victims tonight.”

Sophomore Zoe Weissman, who also survived the Parkland shooting, was asked on MS NOW about the vice president’s comment.

The host asked her, “Is that enough from leadership in this country? What more do you think students want to hear?”

Weissman replied, “It’s honestly laughable.”

“This administration was the same one that was in power when the shooting in Parkland happened, and they said the same exact thing. They sent their thoughts and prayers and told us that they were thinking of us. Clearly they weren’t,” she said. “If they actually cared about us and they were actually praying for us, and they actually wanted us to do well, they would do something to end the gun violence problem in this country. And I think a lot of my fellow students can agree with that, that we’re fed up with the current administration and congress is inaction.”

Our Analysis:

JD Vance’s “Thoughts and Prayers” Met With Scorn by Surviving Student

In the wake of the tragic school shooting at Brown University, which resulted in at least two deaths, JD Vance’s response has sparked controversy and criticism. Vance, in a rather formulaic and now all-too-familiar political response, took to social media to express his condolences and offer prayers. His message, “Terrible news out of Rhode Island this evening. We’re all monitoring the situation and the FBI stands ready to do anything to help. We’re all thinking of and praying for the victims tonight,” was met with a scathing rebuttal from Zoe Weissman, a sophomore at Brown University who also survived the Parkland shooting.

The Echo Chamber of “Thoughts and Prayers”

Vance’s statement, while perhaps well-intentioned, falls into the abyss of repetitive and hollow political rhetoric that surfaces after every mass shooting in America. The phrase “thoughts and prayers” has become a symbol of inaction and indifference from those in positions of power, a mere placeholder till the news cycle moves on. It’s a phrase that, to many victims and their families, rings empty and insincere, a sentiment echoed by Weissman’s reaction.

Zoe Weissman’s Piercing Critique

Weissman’s response to Vance’s statement was unfiltered and poignant. She labeled the vice president’s comment as “laughable,” a stark indictment of the administration’s failure to address the root causes of gun violence in America. Her criticism does not merely stem from a place of emotional reaction but from a place of lived experience and a desperate plea for change. “This administration was the same one that was in power when the shooting in Parkland happened, and they said the same exact thing. They sent their thoughts and prayers and told us that they were thinking of us. Clearly they weren’t,” Weissman stated.

Her words underscore a fundamental disconnect between the political class and the citizens they are supposed to protect and serve. The repetition of history in the form of continuous mass shootings and the corresponding inaction to address gun violence showcases a systemic failure and a lack of genuine political will.

A Call to Action Ignored

Weissman’s critique extends beyond Vance and touches on a broader legislative paralysis. Her assertion that if leaders truly cared, they would “do something to end the gun violence problem in this country,” is a sentiment shared by many Americans who are exhausted by the cyclical nature of this issue. It’s a damning indictment of the current administration and Congress’s inaction and a call for substantive policy reform that goes unheeded time and again.

Conclusion: The Hollow Echoes of Political Platitudes

The interaction between Vance’s “thoughts and prayers” and Weissman’s rebuttal encapsulates the frustration and anger of a generation that has grown up in the shadow of gun violence. It highlights the growing chasm between those in power and those who bear the brunt of their inaction. As Weissman aptly puts it, the continuous cycle of violence, met with nothing but platitudes, has left many “fed up” with the status quo.

The tragedy at Brown University, like those before it, calls for more than thoughts and prayers; it demands action, accountability, and a willingness to confront the complex issues at the heart of America’s gun violence epidemic. Until then, statements like Vance’s will continue to be met with scorn and derision from those who live with the consequences of political inaction.
