Why Trump’s $10 billion IRS lawsuit is even more 'crazy' than it appears

President Donald Trump is suing the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for $10 billion in response to the leaking of his tax returns in 2020. The leak, reported by the New York Times, showed that he paid only $750 in federal income taxes in 2016 and 2017 and, before that, paid no federal income taxes at all in ten out of 15 years.

The New Republic’s Timothy Noah takes a close look at the lawsuit in an article published on February 2, laying out some reasons why it is even “more outrageous” than what is being reported.

“Last week,” Noah explains, “Donald Trump filed suit against the Internal Revenue Service, demanding $10 billion in compensation for the unauthorized disclosure of his taxes in September 2020….. This is a story that, the more you dig into the details, the more outrageous it becomes. News coverage has actually failed to capture fully how very stupid this lawsuit is. I have now reviewed the relevant documents, and can attest that, even for Trump, this lawsuit is an outlier. It’s bats—— crazy.”

Noah notes that then-IRS employee Charles Littlejohn “downloaded Trump’s tax information in October 2018 and gave it to The New York Times in May 2019.” Littlejohn, who pled guilty, is now serving a five-year sentence in federal prison.

“Trump’s $10 billion, if he got it, would be the third-biggest civil judgment in United States history,” Noah observes. “It would be the biggest civil judgment ever awarded to a plaintiff in a case where the defendant didn’t kill at least one person…. The Trump Administration has requested $15 billion to fund the IRS this fiscal year. So yes, Trump wants to help himself to two-thirds of the IRS’ annual budget. So, wow, the whole thing is pretty nuts.”

Our Analysis:

The Absurdity of Trump’s $10 Billion Lawsuit Against the IRS

In a move that could only emerge from the convoluted world of Donald Trump’s legal endeavors, the former president has launched a $10 billion lawsuit against the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). This legal action isn’t just a headline-grabber; it’s an audacious, almost farcical attempt to seek reparation for the 2020 leak of his tax returns, which, as reported by the New York Times, painted a picture of Trump’s financial dealings that many found dubious at best.

The Core of the Outrage

Timothy Noah of The New Republic dives into the heart of this lawsuit, unearthing layers of absurdity that almost defy belief. Trump, whose tax returns revealed he paid a mere $750 in federal income taxes in 2016 and 2017, and none at all in ten out of the fifteen years prior, is now claiming damages to the tune of $10 billion. Noah, with a tone that oscillates between disbelief and indignation, labels the lawsuit as “bats--- crazy,” a sentiment that seems mild considering the facts at hand.

The Details: Beyond Belief

The lawsuit’s foundation lies in the actions of Charles Littlejohn, a then-IRS employee who leaked Trump’s tax information to The New York Times. Littlejohn, who has since pleaded guilty and is serving a five-year sentence, becomes a side note in the broader narrative of Trump’s legal audacity.

What stands stark against the backdrop of this legal drama is the amount Trump is demanding: $10 billion. To put this into perspective, as Noah does with scathing clarity, this sum would represent the third-biggest civil judgment in United States history. It’s a figure that is not only staggering but also ludicrous when one considers that it demands two-thirds of the IRS’ annual budget. The irony? This is the same IRS that Trump’s administration requested $15 billion for in the fiscal year of the lawsuit.

The Unspoken Implications

This lawsuit, preposterous in its scope and demands, underscores a troubling reality of America’s current political and legal landscape. It’s not just about the money; it’s about what Trump’s actions signify for the norms of political accountability and legal recourse.

The audacity to claim such an astronomical sum from a public institution, for the leak of information that arguably should be public knowledge, speaks volumes about Trump’s disregard for the principles of transparency and public accountability. It’s a bold move that seeks to weaponize the legal system against the mechanisms of government transparency, setting a dangerous precedent for future interactions between public figures and the institutions designed to hold them accountable.

A Reflection of Our Times

In essence, Trump’s $10 billion lawsuit against the IRS is more than just a legal anomaly; it’s a reflection of the surreal and often troubling times we inhabit. It challenges us to consider the extent to which the legal system can be manipulated for personal vendettas and how the lines between public interest and personal grievance have become increasingly blurred.

The lawsuit, as Noah rightly points out, is deserving of every ounce of scrutiny and criticism it receives. It’s a stark reminder of the need for vigilance in an era where the outrageous can become the norm, and the lines between legitimate legal grievances and audacious personal crusades can all too easily become indistinct.

In a world where accountability seems to be on the wane, this lawsuit doesn’t just stand out for its financial audacity; it serves as a dire warning of the perils of a society where such actions can be contemplated, let alone executed. It’s a story that, while almost laughably outrageous, carries undercurrents of a deeply concerning shift in the socio-political landscape — one where might often seems to outweigh right, and accountability is a currency in dangerously short supply.
