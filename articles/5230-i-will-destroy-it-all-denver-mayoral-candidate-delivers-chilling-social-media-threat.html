'I will destroy it all': Denver mayoral candidate delivers chilling social media threat

An independent mayoral candidate in Denver, Colorado, posted multiple expletive-filled outbursts on Monday, threatening a prominent local journalist.

Robert Treta, a local construction businessman who has backed a platform of building tiny homes across the city to improve affordability, previously ran in a crowded field in 2023 and lost that race — but not before sitting for an interview with 9News’ Kyle Clark.

Evidently, Treta is still seething about the result of that interview, because he posted aggressive attacks on Clark and 9News to X, one year out from another potential bid for office in 2027.

“I will destroy it ALL. In Colorado, in Denver and in ALL surrounding areas,” wrote Treta. “Kyle, you listen to me @KyleClark get out of Denver and Colorado before I force you to. @9NEWS hey 9 news. I don’t f--- around. I hope you know this. Do your homework on me.”

The startling vow forced the journalist to respond.

“Hi Robert. I wish you the best of luck in your run for Denver Mayor, but language like this is unnecessary,” Clark replied.

Treta, however, wasn’t done.

“I don’t give 3 s---s from Sunday about what you think,” Treta posted. “I want you all out. And I will force you all out. You and your whole b------- propaganda progressive organization. I told you Kyle and 9news over and over and over. Get that 9news person off the f------ board of ‘caring for Denver’. You failed to do that. What have I been saying to you? I don’t like when you don’t do things that I command you to do, I get upset. You did not follow my instructions. Now you will suffer the consequences.”

“I don’t like to do this Kyle. I don’t,” he continued. “You seem like a good father. You do. You should go elsewhere like joe Nachio did. Go live another life away from here. You are not going to disqualify me from another election. @PatriotMarkCook @Joeoltmann I can tell you right now. That ain’t happening. And don’t f------ play stupid. You’re best off blocking me for the 20th time. You are. So much more to say here. Really there is. Tell me again how that FEF sanctioned debate was negotiated with Denver and 9news. @DenverElections @9NEWS I know. I know. You won’t engage me on that either.”Thanks to Raw Story

Our Analysis:

The Unhinged Rhetoric of Robert Treta: A Dangerous Precedent for Political Discourse

In a startling and frankly disturbing series of events that could only unfold in today’s tumultuous political climate, Robert Treta, a Denver mayoral hopeful known more for his construction business than any political acumen, has catapulted himself into the headlines for all the wrong reasons. His expletive-laden tirades against 9News’ Kyle Clark not only serve as a shocking example of the degradation of political discourse but also raise alarming questions about the suitability of certain individuals for public office.

Treta’s Unholy War on Journalism

Treta’s aggressive assaults on Clark and by extension, on 9News, reek of a deeper, more systemic issue plaguing our political landscape: the vilification of the press. “I will destroy it ALL. In Colorado, in Denver and in ALL surrounding areas,” Treta proclaims, in a manner more befitting a tyrant in the making than a mayoral candidate. His threats to “force” Clark out of Denver and to dismantle what he perceives as a “progressive organization” betray not only a profound misunderstanding of freedom of the press but a dangerous authoritarian streak.

A Misguided Crusade Against ‘Propaganda’

Treta’s fixation on removing a journalist from the board of ‘caring for Denver’ underlines a disturbing trend where politicians attempt to dictate the operations of independent organizations, smacking of censorship and control rather than genuine concern for community welfare. His repeated insistence that 9News follow his “commands” is absurdly autocratic, displaying an ego untempered by the realities of public service or the legal and ethical frameworks that govern our society.

The Folly of Treta’s Approach

Clark’s measured response to Treta’s barrage—”I wish you the best of luck in your run for Denver Mayor, but language like this is unnecessary”—highlights the chasm between professional decorum and the gutter politics championed by Treta. It’s a sad day when a journalist must remind a political candidate of the basic tenets of respect and civility.

Treta’s subsequent messages, laced with threats and a bizarre reference to forcing Clark to live “another life away from here,” not only cross the line into personal harassment but also flirt dangerously with incitement. His inability to engage in constructive criticism or articulate a coherent policy critique without resorting to vulgarity and intimidation is a red flag that Denver’s electorate cannot afford to ignore.

What This Means for Denver

As Denver looks to its future, the specter of a Treta administration—marked by coercion, threats, and an adversarial stance against the press—poses a grave threat to the democratic principles that underpin our society. This episode is not merely a case of a political candidate losing his temper; it’s a glaring warning signal of the potential erosion of civil liberties and a free press under leaders who wield threats instead of arguments.

Treta’s tirade against Clark and 9News is emblematic of a broader, more insidious trend in American politics where those seeking office not only challenge the credibility of the media but also threaten those who hold them to account. This is not about one man’s vendetta against a journalist; it’s a reflection of a deeper, more systemic error that, if left unchecked, threatens to undermine the very foundations of our democratic discourse.

In sum, Treta’s conduct is not just unbecoming of a mayoral candidate; it’s a stark reminder of the vigilance required to protect our public sphere from descending into a cesspool of threats, lies, and authoritarian impulses. Denver, and indeed all of America, deserves better.
