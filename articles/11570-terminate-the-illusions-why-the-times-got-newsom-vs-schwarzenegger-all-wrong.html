Terminate the Illusions: Why the Times Got Newsom vs. Schwarzenegger All Wrong

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The False EquivalenceSchwarzenegger’s “Virtue” TrapNewsom’s Move Is Defensive, Not CynicalWhat’s Really at StakeThe Hard Truth

The New York Times’ latest write-up on Gavin Newsom’s redistricting ballot measure versus Arnold Schwarzenegger’s “terminate gerrymandering” crusade is a masterclass in false equivalence. It frames the story as if two honorable men, each with their own virtues, are nobly squaring off over principles. In reality, one is trying to stop Donald Trump’s Republican Party from permanently rigging the House of Representatives, and the other is offering watermelon juice and platitudes while democracy gets strangled in Texas.

The False Equivalence

The Times insists on portraying Greg Abbott’s Texas gerrymander and Newsom’s proposed California counter as two sides of the same coin. Both, we’re told, are equally partisan distortions of democracy. That’s nonsense. Abbott’s plan is a deliberate, authoritarian assault: permanently disenfranchising minority voters to guarantee GOP control no matter how people actually vote. Newsom’s measure, by contrast, is explicitly temporary—a three-cycle pause to counteract Abbott’s power grab and keep the House competitive until the next census. Calling these things equivalent is malpractice, not journalism.

Schwarzenegger’s “Virtue” Trap

Arnold Schwarzenegger is cast as the guardian of post-partisan purity. His independent redistricting reforms from the 2000s were historic and important. But refusing to adapt when the rules of the game have been shredded isn’t virtue—it’s suicide. His refusal to see asymmetry lets Republicans lock in minority rule while California plays fair.

Schwarzenegger may believe he’s saving democracy from corruption. What he’s actually doing is demanding Democrats bring a salad fork to a knife fight. Worse, the Times lets him hide behind folksy color commentary: T-shirts with slogans, breakfast plates of walnuts and raisins, a splash of watermelon juice. This is political theater, not serious analysis of a five-alarm constitutional crisis.

Newsom’s Move Is Defensive, Not Cynical

Let’s be clear: Newsom is not proposing a partisan land grab for the sake of it. He’s sounding the emergency alarm. The GOP has abandoned democracy as a governing principle. They have openly embraced rigging maps, suppressing voters, and sabotaging elections as a path to permanent power. Newsom’s “pause” on California’s independent system is about survival. Democracy cannot endure if only one side agrees to play fair.

The Times buries this lede. They quote Newsom’s adviser calling it a “five-alarm fire” for democracy but then treat it as just another partisan spat. That’s not contextual reporting—it’s stenography with garnish.

What’s Really at Stake

  • Abbott’s gerrymander is an authoritarian power grab.

  • Newsom’s measure is a democratic countermeasure.

  • Schwarzenegger’s absolutism is well-meaning but suicidal.

The Times should be telling readers that California’s choice in November is not between “principle and cynicism.” It’s between unilateral disarmament in the face of fascism, or deploying an ugly but necessary tool to prevent a permanent Trumpist stranglehold on Congress.

The Hard Truth

No one celebrates gerrymandering. It is a blight on democracy. But in this moment, refusing to counter GOP map-rigging isn’t noble—it’s surrender. Newsom’s move is not about chasing raw power; it’s about ensuring that voters in 2026, 2028, and 2030 still have meaningful elections at all.

Schwarzenegger’s “terminate gerrymandering” crusade might look good on a T-shirt. But if his position prevails while Republicans gerrymander Texas into a fortress, the only thing being terminated will be the idea of free and fair congressional elections in the United States.

And the Times? They chose to serve us watermelon juice when the house is on fire.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
