Trump DOJ sues 6 blue states for access to voter rolls

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi attends a House Appropriations Justice Subcommittee hearing on U.S. President Donald Trump’s budget request for the Department of Justice, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 23, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

President Donald Trump’s Justice Department has announced it will take six blue states to court demanding access to voter registration lists.

“Every state has a responsibility to ensure that voter registration records are accurate, accessible, and secure — states that don’t fulfill that obligation will see this Department of Justice in court,” Attorney General Pamela Bondi said in a statement, according to Reuters.

California, New York, Michigan, Minnesota, New Hampshire, and Pennsylvania are all being targeted by the DOJ’s Civil Rights Division.

The court filing claims that the Justice Department has a legal right to access the voter registration lists to conduct oversight.

“The Civil Rights Division has, in the last several months, sent requests for voter registration-related information to at least 24 states, including requesting a complete list of all registered voters from at least 22 states,” said Reuters.

The lawsuit against these six states adds to the lawsuit announced last week against Maine and Oregon, which also alleged the states refused to turn over copies of all voter registration lists.

The demands for the lists follow a September report stating that the Justice Department is collaborating with the Department of Homeland Security to transfer voter registration data for use in immigration investigations.

President Donald Trump has spent years alleging unverified claims that millions of undocumented immigrants are voting illegally in the United States.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the DOJ’s Voter Registration Demands

The recent announcement by President Donald Trump’s Justice Department, led by Attorney General Pamela Bondi, marks a controversial escalation in the administration’s ongoing crusade against what it perceives as voter fraud. The decision to take six blue states to court to demand access to voter registration lists is not just an unprecedented move; it’s a blatant political maneuver disguised under the veneer of ensuring “accurate, accessible, and secure” voter records.

The Targeted States: A Political Pattern?

The states in the crosshairs—California, New York, Michigan, Minnesota, New Hampshire, and Pennsylvania—are predominantly blue states, which raises immediate red flags about the motivations behind these legal challenges. The inclusion of these states, particularly those that serve as Democratic strongholds or battleground states, suggests a strategic selection rather than a random or comprehensive approach to ensuring voter registration integrity.

Legal Right or Legal Might?

The Justice Department’s assertion of a legal right to access voter registration lists for oversight is a contentious point. While oversight of elections is a legitimate function of the federal government, the demand for complete lists of all registered voters from states under the guise of this oversight crosses into potentially invasive territory. It’s a fine line between ensuring the integrity of voter rolls and engaging in a fishing expedition that could intimidate voters or serve as a precursor to unwarranted voter purges.

A Cloak for Immigration Investigations?

The revelation that the Justice Department is collaborating with the Department of Homeland Security to potentially use voter registration data for immigration investigations is especially troubling. This move not only diverts from the purported aim of preventing voter fraud but also weaponizes voter data against immigrants. President Trump’s long-standing, yet unverified, claims of millions of undocumented immigrants voting illegally seem to be the driving force behind this initiative—a claim that has been consistently debunked by multiple studies and investigations.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

This aggressive push by the Trump administration to access state voter registration lists under the auspices of combating voter fraud sets a dangerous precedent. It threatens to politicize voter registration data, intimidate voters, and potentially suppress voter turnout in key states. The lack of evidence supporting widespread voter fraud, coupled with the targeted approach against predominantly blue states, suggests that these actions are more about political gain than securing the integrity of elections.

In a democracy, the integrity of the electoral process is paramount, but so is the protection of individual privacy and the assurance that voter suppression tactics will not be tolerated. The Justice Department’s latest move is a deeply concerning development that should be scrutinized and challenged at every turn.
