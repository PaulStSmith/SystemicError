Trump's masterclass in losing was little more than a photo op

Based on an article by Terry H. Schwadron

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump left the much-advertised summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin without a reported agreement on war, trade or any other immediate dispute. The hour-long meeting, including translation and prepared remarks, produced photographs and a statement of principles broad enough to cost all three leaders almost nothing. Its clearest result was not diplomatic progress but political theater: Trump publicly welcomed Putin as “a friend,” Xi delivered a warning over Taiwan, and three powerful men affirmed a world order built around sovereignty, fossil fuels and insulation from outside accountability.

A summit short on settlements

According to the English-language readout published by China’s state news agency Xinhua, the three leaders reiterated that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.” They also opposed interference by international institutions including the United Nations and the International Criminal Court and endorsed increased global oil production and supply “for the benefit of all nations.”

Those declarations did not resolve any of the conflicts that made the meeting consequential. AlterNet reported no breakthrough concerning Russia’s war in Ukraine, tensions over Taiwan or the economic disputes between Washington and Beijing. Even a separate trade agreement with Thailand was not ready to be signed.

Calling the gathering “unprecedented” supplied grandeur that its substance did not. The spectacle established that Trump, Xi and Putin could occupy the same round table. It did not establish that Trump had persuaded either counterpart to concede anything.

Putin received the recognition Trump withheld from Ukraine

Trump’s most consequential contribution was symbolic, but symbolism is not trivial when a head of state is trying to preserve his international standing. By greeting Putin as “a friend,” Trump offered public warmth to the Russian president while Russia continued its assault on Ukraine amid allegations of war crimes and attacks on civilians.

The meeting allowed Putin to appear alongside the presidents of the United States and China as an equal arbiter of global affairs. Critics in the United States and Ukraine immediately condemned that treatment, according to AlterNet. Whatever Trump intended, the visible benefit flowed to Putin: renewed prestige without a reported concession on Ukraine.

That imbalance exposes the weakness behind the pageantry. Trump possessed the institutional power to condition American recognition on results. Instead, he supplied recognition first and emerged without a reported result.

Xi used the room to set a boundary

Xi, meanwhile, used the meeting to press his government’s position on Taiwan, warning Trump against what Beijing considers “provocative” action to defend the island. The reported exchange presented no comparable American gain.

AlterNet also noted that a CNN live report from China went dark when the subject of Chinese spying for advances in artificial-intelligence chips arose. The interruption alone does not establish why the feed failed, but the episode fit the meeting’s larger asymmetry: Beijing could advance its preferred message while unwelcome subjects disappeared from view.

Trump’s smaller gestures elsewhere at the regional economic forum only sharpened the contrast. He reportedly joked to the Philippine president that he might have received a golden baton like the one associated with Gen. Douglas MacArthur, while the Daily Beast published an image that appeared to show Trump falling asleep during a meeting with other leaders. Neither episode would matter much beside a diplomatic achievement. In the absence of one, they became emblems of a trip organized around performance.

The agreement was in what remained untouched

The summit’s common ground was revealing precisely because it demanded so little. A nuclear-war statement repeated a familiar principle. The call for more oil served the material interests of major powers and producers. Hostility toward international institutions offered each leader a version of the same protection: fewer external constraints on state power.

That is not a peace settlement or a governing program for international stability. It is an accommodation among leaders who prefer accountability to stop at their borders and power to speak for itself.

Trump did not merely fail to dominate the meeting. He helped stage a display in which Putin gained legitimacy, Xi issued a warning and no reported conflict moved closer to resolution. The photographs recorded access to power. They also recorded who used it—and who came home with nothing concrete to show for it.
