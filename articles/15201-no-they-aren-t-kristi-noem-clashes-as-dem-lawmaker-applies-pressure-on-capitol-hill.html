'No they aren't!' Kristi Noem clashes as Dem lawmaker applies pressure on Capitol Hill

Secretary Kristi Noem clashed with a Democratic House member over whether U.S. citizens have been illegally deported by the Department of Homeland Security.

Noem and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent testified before the House Appropriations Committee Tuesday on budget-related matters.

Rep. Lauren Underwood (D-IL) took the opportunity to ask Noem about the Constitutional right to due process, which judges have ruled is being flouted by the Trump administration as they deport undocumented migrants.

"Do you believe that the Constitution grants everyone in our country the right to due process, including non-citizens?" Underwood asked.

"The administration has the authority to —" Noem began, before Underwood interrupted her.

"Ma'am, I'm looking for a yes or no...yes or no — reclaiming my time, Mr. chairman!" Underwood declared as Noem continued to talk over her.

Underwood began again, "Secretary Noem, I'm just looking for a yes or no answer here. Do you believe the Constitution guarantees due process to everyone in America?"

"Due process is exactly what this Congress —"

"Yes, or no? OK ma'am, I'll take that as a no," Underwood said as Noem continued to talk. "Excuse me, ma'am, I'm trying to ascertain your understanding of the law as it applies to to your department, and you, as its leader, should be able to give us a yes or no answer. Because judge after judge has ruled that the law is not being followed. Do you believe that the U.S. government has the authority to deport American citizens?"

"No, and we are not deporting U.S. citizens." Noem answered.

"OK, great, I'm so happy to hear that you believe that the law does not give you that authority, because the federal government has no authority under U.S. laws to deport any American citizen...I know everyone viewing this hearing today knows that several American citizens have been deported to date."

"No, they haven't. That is not true." Noem countered.

"Secretary Noem, that was not a question...That oath that we both swore before taking office was to protect and defend the Constitution of the United States. Not the president, not a political party — the Constitution," Underwood concluded.

Last week, DHS deported three children along with their undocumented migrant mothers. The children, including one with cancer, were U.S. citizens.

Analysis: The Clash of Constitutional Interpretation and Political Allegiance

In a recent spectacle of political theatre, Secretary Kristi Noem and Representative Lauren Underwood engaged in a heated exchange that highlights not just a clash of ideologies, but a fundamental questioning of the U.S. government's adherence to constitutional rights. This incident, stemming from a House Appropriations Committee hearing on budget matters, swiftly transcended its initial scope to touch upon the core of American democratic values: the right to due process.

The Right to Due Process: A Constitutional Guarantee?

At the heart of this confrontation lies the question of due process - a principle enshrined in the Constitution, guaranteeing fair treatment through the normal judicial system. Rep. Underwood's questioning aimed to shed light on the Trump administration's deportation practices, particularly concerning undocumented migrants and, shockingly, U.S. citizens.

Secretary Noem's evasive responses to straightforward questions regarding the Constitution's applicability to non-citizens and American citizens alike reveal a troubling ambivalence or, worse, a deliberate obfuscation of the administration's understanding and respect for constitutional rights.

The Deportation of U.S. Citizens: A Blatant Disregard for Legal and Moral Standards

The article notes that, despite Noem's denials, there have been instances where American citizens were deported - a fact that undermines the legality and morality of the Department of Homeland Security's actions under her leadership. The deportation of three U.S. citizen children, including one battling cancer, is not just a bureaucratic oversight; it is a gross violation of constitutional rights and a stark illustration of the administration's failure to protect its most vulnerable citizens.

Political Allegiance vs. Constitutional Oath

Rep. Underwood's closing remarks underscore a pivotal concern: the allegiance of government officials. Should their loyalty lie with the president, their political party, or the Constitution? Underwood's insistence on the constitutional oath highlights a critical expectation of government officials - to prioritize the Constitution above all else, even when it contradicts the administration's agenda.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Need of Correction

This exchange between Noem and Underwood is emblematic of a larger, systemic issue within the current administration: a disregard for constitutional values in favor of political expediency. It's not just about the legality of deporting non-citizens or even citizens; it's about the fundamental right to due process and the government's duty to protect and uphold the Constitution.

The administration's actions, as questioned by Rep. Underwood, not only challenge the integrity of the U.S. legal framework but also erode the trust of its citizens. When government officials, tasked with defending the Constitution, fail to do so, it is not just a failure of individual duty but a systemic error that demands immediate correction.

In conclusion, the clash witnessed during the House Appropriations Committee hearing is a microcosm of the broader political and constitutional dilemmas facing the United States. The imperative to adhere to constitutional guarantees of due process, irrespective of citizenship status, is a non-negotiable tenet of American democracy. The administration's failure to recognize this principle not only undermines the country's legal foundations but also its moral authority.
