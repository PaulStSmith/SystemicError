He’s 'a coward': Legal expert demolishes Alito in damning take-down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 1, 20263 min read

The Alito Deception: Disguising an Assault on Voting Rights

Dismantling Rights Under the Guise of Legality

Justice Samuel Alito’s majority opinion in Louisiana v. Callais, as dissected by Professor Richard Hasen, serves as a stark illumination of how the U.S. Supreme Court can exercise its immense power to erode civil liberties while outwardly maintaining a facade of adherence to legal precedents. Hasen accuses Alito of cowardice, suggesting that he intentionally obscured his true intentions through legal minutiae to weaken the Voting Rights Act, particularly Section 2, which since 1965 has been a cornerstone in protecting minority voters from discriminatory practices.

Institutional Power and Responsibility

The Supreme Court holds significant interpretative power over the Constitution and its amendments. By authoring an opinion that effectively nullifies Section 2 without outright repealing it, Alito not only exercises this power but does so in a manner that sidesteps direct accountability. This decision does not occur in a vacuum; it follows a pattern established in the 2013 Shelby County decision, where the Court similarly dismantled key protections under the Voting Rights Act. The pattern here is clear: a gradual but deliberate weakening of voter protections, executed through judicial interpretations rather than legislative changes, which shields the actors from immediate political repercussions.

Misdirection and Concealment

Alito’s strategy, as Hasen points out, involves a profound level of misdirection. By claiming no radical change while effectively gutting the law, Alito not only misleads the public but also potentially diminishes the energy around opposition movements. This approach suggests a tactical decision to minimize backlash while achieving the desired conservative outcome. It’s a manipulation of public perception, indicating a troubling trend where justices may prioritize personal or ideological beliefs over transparent judicial reasoning.

Consequences of Judicial Actions

Since the Brnovich ruling, no successful suits under Section 2 have been brought forward, demonstrating the chilling effect Alito’s opinions have had on efforts to challenge discriminatory laws. This consequence directly impacts minority voters, particularly in states like Louisiana, where there is significant overlap between racial minorities and Democratic voters. Alito’s opinions provide multiple pathways for states to suppress votes without facing legal repercussions, effectively disenfranchising large swaths of the electorate.

Broader Patterns of Erosion

This scenario reflects a broader, more disturbing trend of judicial activism from the bench, where Supreme Court justices use their rulings to reshape the political landscape subtly but profoundly. It underscores a shift towards more opaque, consequence-heavy decisions that strategically undermine civil rights while avoiding the political fallout that might come from more straightforward, honest adjudication.

Conclusion: The Illusion of Judicial Neutrality

Justice Alito’s actions in Louisiana v. Callais and similar cases reveal a deliberate, systematic approach to dismantling voter protections under the guise of legal necessity and procedural adherence. This not only challenges the integrity of the judicial system but also raises critical questions about the role of the Supreme Court in a democratic society. As these decisions continue to shape the political field, the facade of neutrality and objectivity the Court is supposed to maintain becomes increasingly tenuous. The real systemic error here is not just in the decision itself but in the broader acceptance of such judicial maneuvering as a normal and unassailable part of our legal landscape.
