Insider trading accusations fly over suspicious Iran war oil trades

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 8, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Market Manipulation Masquerading as War: Unpacking the Iran Conflict’s Financial Underbelly

Market Manipulation Masquerading as War: Unpacking the Iran Conflict’s Financial Underbelly

Dubious Timing and Dubious Profits

At the heart of recent revelations is a stark and troubling sequence of events suggesting blatant market manipulation linked directly to the Trump administration’s announcements concerning the Iran war. According to The Kobeissi Letter, an enormous volume of crude oil shorts were placed suspiciously close to Axios’s report on a potential end to the Iran conflict. This isn’t random trading; it’s a calculated, forewarned gamble, likely based on insider knowledge, reaping a massive $125 million as oil prices plunged then surged within hours.

Institutional Power and Responsibility

The real power in this scenario rests with those who had prior knowledge of the impending news about the Iran war—specifically, members of the Trump administration. By controlling the flow of information, they effectively controlled the market. The decision to possibly leak or time the release of sensitive geopolitical news for financial gain points to a grievous exploitation of power. Those with the ability to manipulate such information hold the reins, not the market itself.

Misdirected Blame and Scapegoating

The subsequent outcry from various public figures, from historians to financial experts, highlights the broader issue of blame misdirection. While some commentators have decried the corruption and called for accountability, others, like former Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, have spun this into a narrative of elite conspiracy that distracts from the immediate issue: potential illegal insider trading fostered by or within the highest echelons of government.

A Pattern of Manipulative Governance

This incident is emblematic of a larger pattern of behavior observed during the Trump administration: the use of governmental power for personal or political gain. Such actions not only undermine the integrity of the administration but also jeopardize national and global economic stability. The manipulation of oil markets through geopolitical maneuvers shows a callous disregard for the wide-reaching consequences, affecting everything from national economies to individual livelihoods.

Cruelty and Corruption: More Than Just Market Movements

The financial maneuvers surrounding the Iran war reveal a dual layer of cruelty and corruption. Beyond the immediate financial implications, these actions play with lives—both the lives of soldiers on the ground and those economically impacted by such market manipulations. This isn’t merely about profits; it’s about a fundamental disrespect for the sanctity of life and the principles of democratic governance.

Systemic Insight: Governance or Grift?

This scenario compels us to question whether we are witnessing governance in action or grift wrapped in the guise of political maneuvering. When geopolitical decisions that should aim to secure peace and stability are used instead to secure personal financial gains, the very foundations of democratic governance are at risk. This isn’t just about the Iran war or oil prices; it’s about the integrity of our political system and the urgent need for robust mechanisms to prevent such abuses of power. The systemic error here is clear: a governance model that allows, if not encourages, the exploitation of power for personal gain, with global consequences.
