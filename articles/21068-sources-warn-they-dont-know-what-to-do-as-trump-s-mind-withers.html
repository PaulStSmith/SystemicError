Sources warn they 'don’t know what to do' as Trump's mind withers

Trump’s Decline Is Not the Story. The War Machine Is.

The Column Mistakes Symptoms for Power

Daily Mail columnist Andrew Neil says people around Donald Trump believe he is deteriorating, that his aides do not know what to do, and that he is escalating toward something “massive” against Iran. The column also reports a secret U.S. attempt to restart a peace process, 14 straight nights of renewed U.S. attacks, talk of a ground incursion, even whispers about tactical nuclear weapons. That is the actual record the piece leaves behind: not confusion, but a government openly floating catastrophic force.

Who Actually Holds the Levers

The institutional power here sits with the president, the White House, and the national-security circle briefing journalists in private while acting in public. Trump may be aging, angry, and erratic, but he still occupies the office that can order strikes and widen war. The anonymous “close confidant” and “influential figures close to Trump” in the column do not reduce that power; they launder it. They turn governance into rumor, and war planning into personality gossip.

Age Talk Is a Convenient Detour

The article wants the reader to stare at Trump’s “mental stability” and “revenge mode.” That framing is useful because it shifts attention away from the real issue: deliberate escalation by a state apparatus that already launched repeated attacks and is now testing the rhetoric for something worse. Trump’s age may be relevant to his judgment. It is not the central political fact. The central fact is that a president can still drag an entire country toward another Middle East catastrophe while the surrounding elite narrates it as a medical mood swing.

The Silence Around Tactical Nuclear Weapons

The most revealing detail is not Trump’s profanity or bluster. It is the reported conversation in which tactical nuclear weapons were treated as a way to “send a message.” That is not strategic seriousness. It is imperial rot: the casual normalization of world-ending force as a communications tool. The silence Neil describes after he objected says everything. In these circles, the problem is rarely the proposed violence itself. The problem is the embarrassment of saying it out loud.

The Framing Is Weak on Purpose

This kind of coverage personalizes a structural emergency. It invites readers to worry about whether Trump is too old, too unstable, too impulsive, as if the U.S. war state would become benign with a more polished executive. It would not. The same institutions that brief columns, escalate strikes, and game out invasions are the institutions that make this possible. A different face would still be presiding over the same machinery of secrecy, coercion, and empire.

The Pattern Behind the Panic

The larger pattern is older than Trump: American power routinely disguises escalation as uncertainty, and disasters as indecision. Anonymous aides, backchannel peace probes, “massive attack” talk, and nuclear theater all serve one function. They keep the public focused on the personality at the top while the state normalizes the next violent step. The issue is not merely that Trump is deteriorating. It is that a failing president is still being used to advance a system that treats war as management and catastrophe as leverage.
