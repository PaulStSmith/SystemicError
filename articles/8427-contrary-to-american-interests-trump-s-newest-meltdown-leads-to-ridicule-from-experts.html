Donald Trump on Sunday uncorked a massive attack on certain allies amid a brewing new scandal, leading to condemnation from experts and political observers alike.

Trump took to Truth Social during what some have called “one of the biggest foreign policy scandals in history“ involving disputed reports that Marco Rubio disseminated Russia-written documents to Ukrainian officials and passed them off as a U.S. peace deal.

In his weekend blow up, Trump blamed former President Joe Biden for the war and said in all caps, “I INHERITED A WAR THAT SHOULD HAVE NEVER HAPPENED, A WAR THAT IS A LOSER FOR EVERYONE, ESPECIALLY THE MILLIONS OF PEOPLE THAT HAVE SO NEEDLESSLY DIED. UKRAINE ‘LEADERSHIP’ HAS EXPRESSED ZERO GRATITUDE FOR OUR EFFORTS, AND EUROPE CONTINUES TO BUY OIL FROM RUSSIA.”

The responses came quickly.

MSNBC journalist Sam Stein reported on Sunday:

“Trump with a lengthy statement that says not too much but accuses Ukraine leader of not expressing gratitude for the help the US given which is just factually not true (Zelensky has gone out of his way to express gratitude after the Oval blow up).”

CNN journalist Jim Sciutto wrote, “Three European countries account for the bulk of those Russian oil purchases: Hungary, Slovakia and Turkey. Trump himself [g]ave Hungary a one-year exemption to buy Russian oil earlier this month.”

Ex-federal prosecutor Joyce Vance added, “This war is not about Donald Trump. It’s about Russian aggression, which he now seeks to reward, contrary to Ukrainian, European, and American interests.”

Rep. Don Bacon, a Republican congressman, chimed in with, “The President continues to ignore that it is Russia who invaded Ukraine & bombing cities every night. Putin ordered this invasion. Why the moral confusion? The war started before Trump’s first term. I’ve heard President Zelenskyy thank America many times for our military aid.”

Michael MacKay, who says he has a Ph.D. from the London School of Economics and Political Science, also weighed in:

“Fascist Russia is at war with Western civilization and invaded Ukraine in 2014. The Russian Federation is the aggressor and the sole reason for the state of war. Trump is a Russian asset and traitor to the United States.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Latest Tirade: Fact vs. Fiction

In a recent outburst that can only be described as peak Trump, the former President took to Truth Social to unload a barrage of accusations amidst what is being dubbed “one of the biggest foreign policy scandals in history.” His targets? A curious blend of former allies, European nations, and the current U.S. administration. But let’s dissect the rhetoric, the responses, and the reality buried under the bombast.

Trump’s Claims: A War of Words

Donald Trump’s tirade centers on a highly disputed scenario involving Senator Marco Rubio, alleged Russian-written documents, and Ukraine—a concoction ripe for political theatre. Trump, with his characteristic flair for dramatics, doesn’t just stop there. He redirects the blame for the ongoing war squarely onto former President Joe Biden’s shoulders, asserting in all caps that he “INHERITED A WAR THAT SHOULD HAVE NEVER HAPPENED,” further accusing Ukraine of ingratitude and Europe of energy complicity with Russia.

But how do these claims hold up under scrutiny? Not well.

The Facts: Unpacking the Outrage

  • Ukraine’s Alleged Ingratitude: Trump’s assertion that Ukraine has shown “ZERO GRATITUDE” is, to put it mildly, factually bankrupt. President Zelensky has, on numerous occasions, expressed his thanks to the U.S. for its support, directly contradicting Trump’s claims. This gratitude has been public and unequivocal, making Trump’s accusation not just misleading but demonstrably false.

  • Europe’s Oil Purchases: Trump points fingers at Europe for continuing to buy oil from Russia, conveniently omitting his own administration’s actions—such as granting Hungary a one-year exemption to purchase Russian oil. This selective memory suggests a narrative crafted more for political expediency than factual accuracy.

  • Misplaced Blame and Historical Revisionism: Perhaps most egregiously, Trump’s tirade attempts to rewrite the history of Russian aggression. The claim that he “inherited a war” glosses over the complex geopolitical dynamics that predate his administration. It’s a simplification that serves only to absolve him of responsibility while ignoring the broader context of Russian actions in Ukraine since 2014.

Responses: Voices of Reason

The chorus of responses to Trump’s statements provides a necessary reality check. From MSNBC’s Sam Stein to ex-federal prosecutor Joyce Vance, the consensus is clear: Trump’s narrative is not only flawed but dangerous. It misrepresents the situation in Ukraine, distorts the U.S.’s role and contributions, and, perhaps most alarmingly, plays into Russian propaganda by muddying the waters of accountability.

Republican congressman Don Bacon and Michael MacKay, with his scholarly credentials, both emphasize a crucial point: Russia is the aggressor here, a fact that Trump’s rhetoric dangerously obfuscates. By painting the war as a product of Biden’s administration or Ukrainian ingratitude, Trump diverts attention from the real issue—Russian aggression—and, wittingly or not, undermines the unity and resolve of those opposing it.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

Trump’s latest outburst is more than just a political sideshow; it’s a disservice to the truth and a distortion of the complex realities facing Ukraine, Europe, and the U.S. It’s critical to see through the smoke and mirrors of such rhetoric, understanding that the issues at hand demand nuanced, informed, and fact-based discussions—not inflammatory and misleading broadsides aimed at settling personal scores or rewriting history.

In the end, the real loser in Trump’s narrative isn’t any single nation or leader—it’s the truth itself, sacrificed on the altar of political expediency. And in a time of global crisis, such sacrifices are something we can ill afford.
