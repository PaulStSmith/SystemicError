Trump can't let go of an 11-year grudge — or his shoe lifts

Based on an article by Tara Dublin

Paulo SantosAugust 31, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Was Never a Meme. The Institutions Made Him Real.

Trump blocking an activist on Twitter is a trivial detail. The real story is what that detail exposes: a political press that mistook contempt for theater, a Republican Party that chose capture over responsibility, and a set of institutions that kept treating deliberate authoritarian behavior as quirky personality. The source’s personal grievance is useful because it points to the larger crime: years of people with power refusing to act like they understood the danger in front of them.

The First Failure Was Not Trump’s

Trump did not rise because he was subtle. He rose because institutions misread him on purpose. The source points to the moment he kicked Univision’s Jorge Ramos out of a press conference in Dubuque in August 2015. That was not a sideshow. It was a public test of whether press access mattered more than press integrity.

The answer, from the media, was yes. They stayed. They covered. They normalized the performance. Once the gatekeepers decide that open hostility is just “brashness,” they are not being neutral. They are laundering aggression into spectacle.

The Republican Party Chose This

The source is right about the central fact that matters: Trump did not conquer the GOP from outside it. The party let him compromise it, one excuse at a time, until cowardice became discipline. That is not confusion. That is a political choice made by people who knew exactly what he was and accepted it anyway.

The result is not just a degraded party. It is a captured one. A party that once claimed to stand for law, order, and institutions now treats open criminality, grievance politics, and creeping authoritarianism as a brand strategy. The source’s contempt for the “entire GOP” is not excess. It is the minimum honest response to a decade of surrender.

The Media Kept Calling It Charisma

The press loves a strongman so long as he makes good copy. That is the disease. Trump was treated as an entertaining aberration until his behavior became too obvious to ignore, and even then the language softened the damage. “Brash.” “Brazen.” “Unprecedented.” These words function like anesthetic. They describe the performance while dodging the consequence.

What the source gets right is that the press was not merely fooled. It was complicit through appetite. It wanted the ratings, the access, the novelty. It preferred the drama of a destabilizing man to the boring obligation of saying plainly that he was dangerous and unfit. That is how authoritarian politics wins in a media ecosystem built to reward interruption over accountability.

Power Reveals Itself in Who Gets Protected

The source mentions Trump later ordering his DOJ to unblock people he had blocked on social media, which is almost comically revealing. Even petty digital behavior became an exercise in state power. That is the pattern: he turns every institutional tool into a personal grudge machine.

And the surrounding world adjusted. The source points to the Epstein Files cover-up, to Trump’s refusal to pay E. Jean Carroll until he had to, and to Republicans staying silent while the machinery of impunity kept running. This is what institutional weakness looks like in practice: not one dramatic collapse, but a series of small accommodations that teach the powerful they can get away with anything.

The Point Is Not the Block List

The source is funniest when it is most contemptuous, but the real argument is not about social media vindication. It is about the cost of refusing to confront obvious corruption early, loudly, and collectively. Trump’s career was not built on one election or one scandal. It was built on repeated permissions granted by people who should have known better.

That is the broader political pattern. Authoritarian politics does not require one grand coup. It advances through media normalization, partisan cowardice, and institutional officials who decide that preserving their own comfort matters more than stopping the damage. The harm is never accidental. The misdirection is.
