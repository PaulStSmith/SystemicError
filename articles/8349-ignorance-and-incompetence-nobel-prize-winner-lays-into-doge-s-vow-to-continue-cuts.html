'Ignorance and incompetence': Nobel Prize winner lays into DOGE's vow to continue cuts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 26, 20255 min read

A Nobel Prize winner has denounced the DOGE section of Trump’s administration as the “damage” from their internal cuts “persists.”

Economist Paul Krugman believes the Department of Government Efficiency, initially headed up by Tesla CEO Elon Musk, has been more damaging than anything for the country and the president’s plans for his second term.

It was reported earlier this week that DOGE was dead, with lead staff members reshuffled into different departments, but the official DOGE account on X confirmed the department is still working on government cuts.

In post in response to a Reuter’s report Sunday claiming the agency was dead, DOGE responded, “President Trump was given a mandate by the American people to modernize the federal government and reduce waste, fraud and abuse. Just last week, DOGE terminated 78 wasteful contracts and saved taxpayers $335M. We’ll be back in a few days with our regularly scheduled Friday update.”

But Krugman believes the ongoing cuts made by the department are more damaging and have “wreaked havoc” throughout the government. He wrote, “Arguably DOGE’s biggest ‘achievement’ was shutting down the U.S. Agency for International Development. And the dismantling of USAID has left a legacy of death.

“Back at home, DOGE wreaked havoc on the U.S. government through a combination of arrogance, ignorance and sheer incompetence. Musk is gone, at least for now, from the Trump administration. So are many of the young acolytes he parachuted into temporary positions of power in government agencies, although some have managed to worm their way into longer-term positions.”

Other political commentators believe DOGE has been a complete failure for the government as none of the changes made by Musk and his team stuck when implemented. The Lincoln Project founder Rick Wilson suggested DOGE has “created more costs than it saved.”

He said, “I was told that DOGE was going to create trillions and trillions of dollars in cuts from wasteful government spending and as it turns out it was a net negative. It created more costs than it saved.”

Speaking to Reuters, Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor confirmed the department no longer exists.

He said, “That doesn’t exist. There is no target around reductions.” Kupor would go on to say it’s no longer a “centralized entity” in the administration.

DOGE did not provide any details of what they had cut, when they had cut it, or how much they had effectively saved but did claim to have cut government expenditure down by tens of billions of dollars.

Our Analysis:

A Dysfunctional Department: The DOGE Debacle

The story of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) under the Trump administration, spearheaded initially by none other than the Tesla magnate Elon Musk, reads like a political farce, but with real consequences that are anything but laughable. Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman’s denunciation of DOGE’s impact as “damage” that “persists” is a damning indictment of an initiative that was, ostensibly, aimed at streamlining and modernizing federal government operations.

The Irony of “Efficiency”

It’s almost poetic in its irony that a department named for government efficiency has been anything but. The fact that it was led by Elon Musk, a figure with no prior governmental experience, only to be declared “dead” before an official account on X (formerly Twitter) insisted it was still operational, is emblematic of the chaotic and counterproductive nature of its existence.

Krugman’s Critique

Krugman’s critique cuts deep, highlighting the profound implications of DOGE’s operations. The closure of the U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID) stands out as a particularly egregious example of the department’s misguided priorities. The dismantling of an agency critical to global humanitarian efforts for the sake of “efficiency” underscores a fundamental misunderstanding of the value such organizations provide, both in terms of moral leadership and in fostering global stability.

The Musk Factor

Elon Musk’s departure from the administration and the subsequent fate of his “young acolytes” further underscores the transient and superficial approach DOGE took towards government reform. The notion that inexperienced appointees could parachute into complex bureaucratic systems and enact meaningful change is, as evidenced, a recipe for disruption and dysfunction.

Financial Follies

The assertion from DOGE’s official account that it had terminated wasteful contracts and saved taxpayers money is immediately undercut by the lack of transparency and detail regarding these purported savings. This, coupled with Rick Wilson’s assertion that DOGE has been a “net negative” financially, paints a picture of a department that not only failed to achieve its goals but potentially exacerbated the very issues it was created to address.

The Dissolution of DOGE

The confirmation from Scott Kupor, Director of the Office of Personnel Management, that DOGE no longer exists as a “centralized entity” within the administration is the final nail in the coffin for the department’s troubled existence. The absence of a clear target for reductions or a coherent strategy only further illustrates the hollowness of DOGE’s claims of efficiency and savings.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Hubris

The DOGE saga is a cautionary tale of hubris, inefficiency, and the perils of entrusting government reform to individuals and ideologies that prioritize optics over substance. The damage inflicted by DOGE, as Krugman articulates, is a sobering reminder of the real-world consequences of political vanity projects. It’s a narrative that demands not only scrutiny but a reevaluation of how governmental efficiency and reform are approached in the future.
