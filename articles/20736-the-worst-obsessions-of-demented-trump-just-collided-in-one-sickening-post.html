The worst obsessions of demented Trump just collided in one sickening post

Based on an article by John Casey

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Uses State Power to Turn Identity Into a Weapon

A Presidency Built on Humiliation

The source story centers on a familiar Trump move: he takes a public institution, strips it of dignity, and uses it to stage his personal grievance. The immediate object is a doctored image of Barack and Michelle Obama, but the real subject is power: who gets treated as legitimate, and who gets marked as foreign, dirty, or disposable.

This is not random internet nastiness. It is political messaging from the office of the presidency, delivered through Trump’s own platform, and aimed at reinforcing a hierarchy of race, religion, and belonging. The insult is the point.

The Decision Was His

Trump made the choice to repost the image, just as he made the choice to keep relitigating the Obamas, to keep using “Hussein” as a sneer, and to keep feeding his base material that turns Blackness and Islam into a threat signal. The article is right to treat this as a pattern, not an isolated lapse. Trump is not confused about what these symbols do. He uses them because they work on the people he wants activated.

The power structure is straightforward. Trump has the institutional authority of the presidency, the media ecosystem of the right, and a loyal audience trained to read contempt as strength. The Obamas do not have that machinery behind them in this context. They are the target, not the drivers, of the story.

Racism With a Foreign Patron

The hypocrisy is not incidental. Trump’s own plane, now a $400 million Boeing 747 gifted by Qatar and repainted in his preferred colors, exists because a Gulf monarchy gave him a flying palace. He is perfectly happy to receive luxury from a Muslim, Arabic-speaking state when the transaction flatters him. What he cannot tolerate is Arabic script or Muslim identity appearing near the people he wants to degrade.

That tells you exactly how his politics works. Muslim identity is acceptable when it is monetized, gifted upward, or folded into Trump’s self-image. It becomes dangerous only when it is attached to people he wants to stigmatize, whether that means the Obamas or schoolchildren in hijabs. The issue is not religion. The issue is domination.

Children as Political Targets

The reposted video of Black Muslim kindergarten girls in hijabs is even uglier because it shows the machinery of dehumanization at ground level. These are children. Trump did not merely “comment” on a school photo; he amplified a frame that invites his followers to see Muslim girls as an intrusion, a problem to be banished, a visible marker of contamination.

That is where the article is strongest: it refuses the comforting lie that this is just trolling. It is not. It is a directed attempt to make ordinary visibility look like a threat. And because the message comes from Trump, it lends permission to the ugliest impulses in his movement. The replies calling for deportation or banning a garment worn by children are not a side effect. They are the ecosystem doing what it was fed to do.

Misdirection as Method

The surface story is about a plane, or the Obamas, or a kindergarten photo. That is the misdirection. The underlying story is about legitimacy: who is allowed to occupy public space without being smeared, and who must constantly defend their presence.

Trump’s politics depend on replacing policy with degradation. He does not need a coherent program when he can make identity itself into a battlefield. He turns a former first family into a racialized punching bag, then uses the same machinery to train his audience against Muslim children. The harm is not accidental. It is the mechanism.

The article correctly identifies the contradiction Trump “apparently doesn’t see, or doesn’t care about.” He denounces what he happily profits from. He borrows prestige from a Muslim monarchy and then weaponizes Muslim imagery against domestic enemies. That is not inconsistency. It is the old authoritarian habit of treating outsiders as useful when they serve power and disgusting when they stand in the way of it.

The Pattern Is the Message

This story is bigger than Trump’s personal vulgarity. It shows how modern authoritarian politics works in practice: symbolic pollution, racial scapegoating, and institutional cowardice wrapped inside spectacle. The office remains powerful even when it is used for degradation. The platform remains powerful even when it is used for propaganda. And the target class remains clear: people whose dignity can be stripped to mobilize someone else’s resentment.

The lesson is not that Trump is unusually rude. It is that he uses the presidency to define belonging by insult, and to teach his movement that cruelty is a civic style. That is how democratic culture gets corroded from the top down: not by one outrageous post, but by the steady normalization of who may be mocked, who may be marked, and who may be treated as less than fully entitled to belong.
