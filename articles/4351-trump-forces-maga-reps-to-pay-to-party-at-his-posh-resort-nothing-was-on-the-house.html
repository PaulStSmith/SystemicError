Trump forces MAGA reps to pay to party at his posh resort: 'Nothing was on the House'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 12, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Irony: Trump’s MAGA Cash Cow

President Donald Trump gestures from the stage next to U.S. Representative Steve Scalise (R-LA) at Trump National Doral Miami in Miami, Florida on March 9, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump was lining his pockets and simultaneously making MAGA lawmakers pay up for their stays at his Trump National Doral Golf Club in Miami this week, according to reports Wednesday.

The four-star luxury resort has rooms starting at $600+ per night, and “nothing was on the House” during the Republican policy retreat this week in Florida, according to The Swamp, The Daily Beast’s Substack.

“America is at war and casualties are mounting, gas prices and the cost of living are skyrocketing, and federal workers forgotten in a government shutdown are wondering when they’ll get their next paycheck,” The Swamp reported. “So what better for GOP lawmakers to do than to head for the Miami sunshine for the annual House Republican policy retreat?”

The resort also apparently has a 125-foot water slide, $420 spa treatments and a $31 burger on the menu.

“Super suckup Speaker Mike Johnson gushed it was an ‘amazing’ venue and insisted attendees among the House’s 218 Republicans had ‘so much’ to celebrate while they were chatting by the pool,” according to The Swamp.

Republicans were reportedly using the venue to discuss talking points and consider how they could develop a legislative agenda to appeal to more voters ahead of the midterm elections this fall, according to The New York Times.

Johnson also admitted there was work for Republicans ahead, as concerns over the GOP’s performance grow as the party tries to maintain a sense of optimism despite a potentially grim outlook in the elections.

“We’ve got a little hiccup with some of the Hispanic and Latino voters for certain, because some of the immigration enforcement was viewed to be overzealous,” Johnson said in an onstage interview.

“And, you know, everybody can describe it differently. But here’s the good news: We’re in a course correction mode right now,” Johnson added. “We’re going to have a new Secretary of Homeland Security.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Irony: Trump’s MAGA Cash Cow

In a move that could only be described as brazenly on-brand for former President Donald Trump, reports have surfaced that he was literally cashing in on MAGA lawmakers during their stay at his Trump National Doral Golf Club in Miami. This four-star luxury resort, where rooms start at a jaw-dropping $600+ per night, played host to the Republican policy retreat amidst a backdrop of national crises. This isn’t just a story about luxurious accommodations; it’s a glaring emblem of the GOP’s disconnect with the American people.

The Art of the Upsell in Times of Crisis

The juxtaposition couldn’t be more stark or more telling. As The Swamp, via The Daily Beast’s Substack, points out, “America is at war and casualties are mounting, gas prices and the cost of living are skyrocketing, and federal workers forgotten in a government shutdown are wondering when they’ll get their next paycheck.” And yet, the GOP’s solution? Retreat to the sunny climes of Miami to bask in luxury that’s so out of touch, it might as well be on another planet.

Let’s not gloss over the amenities mentioned: a 125-foot water slide, $420 spa treatments, and a $31 burger. These aren’t just random luxury items; they’re symbols of a party that seems to have forgotten the plight of the very people they’re elected to serve. And as for Speaker Mike Johnson’s comments on the retreat being an ‘amazing’ venue with ‘so much’ to celebrate? It’s as if the Republican leadership is living in a bubble, impervious to the struggles of everyday Americans.

Talking Points Over People

The Republicans reportedly used this opulent setting not just as a backdrop for relaxation but also to strategize on how to expand their voter base ahead of the midterm elections. Yet, Speaker Johnson’s admission that the party has a “hiccup” with Hispanic and Latino voters due to perceived overzealous immigration enforcement is an understatement that borders on the comical. Describing systemic issues affecting millions as a “hiccup” demonstrates a disconcerting level of trivialization of genuine concerns.

Johnson’s optimistic note on “course correction” and the appointment of a new Secretary of Homeland Security feels like too little, too late. It’s akin to rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic; superficial changes that fail to address the deeper, systemic issues at play.

A Tale of Two Realities

This retreat at Trump’s Doral Golf Club is emblematic of a broader issue within the GOP: a disconnect between their lived reality and that of the voters they purport to represent. In times of national hardship, the optics of indulging in luxury while many Americans struggle to make ends meet is not just bad politics—it’s a stark display of indifference.

In essence, the GOP’s sunny retreat in Miami is more than a simple gathering; it’s a microcosm of the systemic issues plaguing the party. It’s a reminder that in times of crisis, true leaders are found not by the poolsides of luxury resorts, but in the trenches with those they serve. As the GOP looks towards the midterm elections, one can’t help but wonder: will they recognize the need for genuine, systemic change, or will they continue to bask in the disconnected opulence of Trump’s Doral? Only time will tell, but if history is any indication, optimism seems like a tall order.
