'No, no, no, no, no': GOP lawmaker shut down over LA protest violence blame

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 9, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Nicole Malliotakis, Xochitl Hinojosa (CNN screenshot)

Rep. Nicole Malliotakis (R-NY) got a lesson on how the government works on CNN on Sunday when a former DOJ official explained to her that the escalation of violence in Los Angeles can solely be linked back to Donald Trump's firing spree since taking office.

During the panel segment on CNN's "State of the Union," the New York Republican claimed that the president had no choice but to send the National Guard to Los Angeles to quell the protests of ICE immigrant sweeps despite Califonia lawmakers saying there is no need.

With the pro-immigrant, anti-ICE protests growing, Malliotakis asserted, , "You see an ICE agents attacked, you've seen their cars put on fire, stones thrown at them –– this is unacceptable and you're also talking about enforcement of our immigration laws."

As she stated people who have been taken into custody have been, "... gang members, these were drug traffickers, human smugglers, people who committed robbery, child abuse, sex, offense, the worst of the worst," she was interrupted with, "Are you sure that they were?" to which she responded, "Well that's what the reporting says."

After adding, "The fact that, look, we would not have this situation in Los Angeles if it were not that they had this sanctuary status that refuses cooperation with ICE agents. It makes it more dangerous for everyone, the agents and the community when the ICE agents have to go in there and find these criminals instead of the city and state turning them over," former DOJ Public Affairs Director Xochitl Hinojosa took exception.

"Well, it was also children and mothers, and there's video of violence against these ––are mothers and small children and the videos are terrible," she told the GOP lawmaker. "What I'll say is it is a president's responsibility to de-escalate a situation anytime there is violence in our country. Then they should work with local law enforcement to do that."

"What happened here is sending in the National Guard only escalates it," she continued. "Hegseth saying that he wants to send in the Marines that are equipped for war only escalates it. You know, arresting the president of SEIU and going in and conducting raids only escalates. The interesting part about this is the Department of Justice would normally go in through the Community Relations Service; they did this with George Floyd. They've done this with a whole bunch of other instances where they go in and they work with state and local law enforcement to de-escalate what is happening."

"The problem here is that Trump essentially fired everyone in the Community Relations Service, he did, I worked there" she continued as the GOP lawmaker objected. No, no, no, no, no! The federal government's role is to help state and. local law enforcement, to deescalate. He essentially got rid of all of those employees because he does not believe in civil rights, and instead he's escalating it by sending in the National Guard."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of CNN Panel Segment on Escalation of Violence in Los Angeles

In a recent panel segment on CNN's "State of the Union," Rep. Nicole Malliotakis (R-NY) found herself in a whirlpool of controversy and correction. She attributed the escalation of violence in Los Angeles to the necessity of deploying the National Guard to quell protests against ICE immigrant sweeps. Her justification? A narrative that conveniently overlooks broader systemic issues and fixates on sensationalized accounts of violence against ICE agents. "This is unacceptable," she declares, painting a picture that frames the response to ICE actions purely as lawlessness, without diving into the deeper waters of why these protests are happening in the first place.

Malliotakis's rhetoric is a classic example of misdirection. By focusing solely on the violence and categorizing detainees in a monolithic group of "the worst of the worst," she sidesteps the complex and nuanced reality of immigration enforcement and its impacts on communities. The challenge to her narrative comes from a former DOJ official, Xochitl Hinojosa, who brings to light a more nuanced understanding of the situation. Hinojosa points out the presence of children and mothers among those affected by violence, hinting at a more indiscriminate impact of enforcement actions than Malliotakis's account suggests.

The conversation then shifts to a critical analysis of the Trump administration's handling of the situation, with Hinojosa criticizing the decision to send in the National Guard and suggesting that it only served to escalate tensions. She points out that, historically, the Department of Justice would employ a more nuanced approach through the Community Relations Service, focusing on de-escalation and cooperation with local law enforcement. "Trump essentially fired everyone in the Community Relations Service," Hinojosa asserts, framing this as a deliberate undermining of civil rights and a preference for militarized responses over community-oriented solutions.

The Heart of the Matter

The essence of this discussion touches on fundamental questions about the role of federal authority in local law enforcement and the strategies employed to address civil unrest. Malliotakis's stance represents a broader trend within certain political circles that favor hardline, enforcement-first approaches to immigration and public protest. This perspective is not only myopic but dangerously simplistic, ignoring the root causes of unrest and the documented benefits of community policing and engagement.

Hinojosa's critique unveils a troubling reality of the Trump administration's approach to civil rights and conflict resolution. The dismantling of the Community Relations Service is emblematic of a disinterest in pursuing justice and peace through dialogue and understanding. Instead, the priority appears to have been on showcasing strength and authority, even at the cost of escalating tensions and sidelining the civil rights of communities.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Approach

This CNN panel segment is a microcosm of the larger systemic errors plaguing American politics and governance. Malliotakis's arguments, while perhaps appealing to a certain base, fail to address the complexities of immigration, protest, and law enforcement interaction. The response from Hinojosa, grounded in her experience and a broader perspective on justice, highlights the deficiencies and dangers of the approach advocated by Malliotakis and, by extension, the Trump administration.

The lesson here is clear: Escalation is not a solution. The path to safety and security lies not in militarization and enforcement but in understanding, cooperation, and respect for civil rights. The events in Los Angeles, as discussed in this CNN segment, should serve as a cautionary tale, not just about the specific policies of one administration, but about the underlying attitudes and approaches that led to those policies. Let it be a reminder that in seeking solutions to our most pressing issues, we must ensure that our methods do not exacerbate the very problems we aim to solve.
