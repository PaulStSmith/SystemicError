Schumer and Jeffries told to step down over failure to fight ‘war-crazed’ Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 12, 20266 min read

A coalition of peace groups on Wednesday launched a new national campaign calling for the top Democrats in Congress—Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries—to resign from their leadership roles, citing their failure to sufficiently fight back “against a war-crazed Trump administration.”

The coalition, which includes Peace Action and RootsAction, launched a petition declaring that it is “time for congressional Democrats to replace Schumer and Jeffries with leaders who are willing and able to challenge the runaway militarism that has dragged our country into launching yet another insanely destructive war,” this time against Iran.

“Sen. Chuck Schumer and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries have not acted to prevent war on Venezuela or the current war on Iran,” the petition reads. “They worked to delay a vote on Iran until after the war had started, while failing to clearly oppose it before or after the launch of the war. Schumer and Jeffries have shown that they cannot be trusted to prevent more wars, more threats of wars, or the transfer of another half a trillion dollars a year into the war machine.”

Kevin Martin, president of Peace Action—the largest grassroots peace network in the US—said in a statement that he doubts “at this point whether many people look to Chuck Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries for ‘leadership’ in Congress, but we would settle for them getting with the program and representing their base, and the majority of Americans, who want them to stand strongly against Trump’s illegal wars and domestic terror campaigns against the American people.”

“They need to speak out loudly and clearly, and get their caucuses in line, to oppose the upcoming $50 billion or more for Trump’s illegal war of aggression on Iran, and to cut off US weapons to Israel,” said Martin. “Failing to do so will only increase calls for them to step down or be replaced by colleagues who understand where the American people are on these and other critical issues.”

Since the start of the illegal US-Israeli assault on Iran, Schumer and Jeffries have focused largely on procedural objections to the war, the Trump administration’s incompetence, and the president’s failure to clearly articulate his objectives, rather than explicitly opposing the military onslaught.

In an appearance on NBC‘s “Meet the Press” on Sunday, Jeffries declined to say whether he would oppose the Trump administration’s expected push for $50 billion in new funding for the unauthorized war on Iran.

“We’ll cross that bridge when we get to it,” Jeffries said, chiding the administration for failing to “make its case as to the rationale or justification for this war of choice in the Middle East.”

Sarah Lazare and Adam Johnson wrote for The Nation last week that “it’s not enough to check the box, to do the bare minimum, to reinforce every argument for war only to balk at the process and ask whether there’s a ‘plan’ for after the myriad war crimes have already been committed.”

“The only way to read this half-hearted response from the Democratic Party leadership,” they argued, “is de facto support.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Democratic Leadership’s Response to Militarism: A Scathing Review

In a move that can only be described as too little, too late, a coalition of peace groups has rightfully called for the resignation of top Democrats in Congress, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. Their crime? A glaring failure to effectively counter what has been termed as “a war-crazed Trump administration.” This scenario paints a dismal picture of a leadership in disarray, one that has seemingly forgotten the art of opposition, let alone the principle of it.

The Coalition’s Grievances

At the heart of the coalition’s fury, represented by organizations like Peace Action and RootsAction, lies a petition that boldly declares the necessity for Schumer and Jeffries to step aside. Why? Because they have spectacularly failed to prevent, or even mitigate, the onslaught of “runaway militarism” dragging the United States into another devastating conflict, this time against Iran. The indictment doesn’t stop there. Schumer and Jeffries are accused of not just passive inaction but of undermining efforts to oppose the war on Iran by delaying crucial votes until after the war had commenced. This is not just failure; this is complicity dressed as incompetence.

The Leadership’s Lackluster Response

What we’ve seen from Schumer and Jeffries is a masterclass in how not to lead. Rather than taking a firm stand against the Trump administration’s illegal wars and domestic terror campaigns, they have chosen the path of least resistance. They’ve focused on procedural quibbles and the administration’s incompetence—essentially missing the forest for the trees. Their opposition, if it can even be called that, is so tepid that it barely registers on the scale of resistance. This isn’t leadership; it’s a carefully choreographed capitulation.

Kevin Martin, president of Peace Action, hits the nail on the head when he expresses doubt that many see Schumer and Jeffries as sources of “leadership” in Congress. The expectation isn’t for them to perform miracles but to at least represent their base and the majority of Americans who oppose Trump’s militaristic endeavors. Yet, they falter even at this low bar, signaling a disconnect so profound it’s almost farcical.

The Real Cost of Inaction

The consequences of Schumer and Jeffries’ inaction and their apparent acquiescence are not just political but profoundly moral. By focusing on procedural objections and failing to oppose the military onslaught explicitly, they’ve essentially provided de facto support for an unauthorized war. This isn’t just a failure; it’s a betrayal—a betrayal of the values they purport to represent and a betrayal of the constituents who rely on them to be their voice in Congress.

A Call for Change

The call for Schumer and Jeffries to resign isn’t just about holding them accountable for their failures. It’s about recognizing the urgent need for leadership that understands and represents the will of the people. It’s about finding leaders who are willing to challenge the status quo, who understand the costs of war, and who are prepared to fight for peace and justice, not just with words but with actions.

In their half-hearted response to the Trump administration’s militarism, Schumer and Jeffries have not only failed their party; they have failed their country. And in doing so, they’ve made one thing abundantly clear: the Democratic leadership needs a drastic overhaul if it hopes to represent the values and interests of the American people genuinely.

The coalition’s campaign for their resignation is not just warranted; it’s necessary. For the sake of the party and the country, let’s hope it serves as a wake-up call.
