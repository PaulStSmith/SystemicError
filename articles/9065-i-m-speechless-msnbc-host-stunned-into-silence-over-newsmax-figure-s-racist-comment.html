'I'm speechless': MSNBC host stunned into silence over Newsmax figure's 'racist' comment

By Paulo Santos

On MSNBC, host Jonathan Capehart was stunned into silence early Sunday morning as he reacted to a right-wing Newsmax figure’s “racist” comment.

Capehart was addressing the issue of Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits, which are in danger as the federal government shutdown looms over the nation, when he said, “Let’s stop talking about brother man for a minute and let’s talk about, still on SNAP, what a host [on] Newsmax had to say.”

Stumbling over his words and pausing throughout his sentences, Capehart added, “And you’ll understand why I’m speechless after you listen to what he said about people on SNAP benefits and what they’re using their benefits for.”

The video played of the Newsmax host saying, “This is a program that has exploded over the last 20 years, and we are just dumping 100 billion a year into a program that we all know is being so woefully corrupted and exploited, and people are selling their benefits. People are using them to get their nails done, to get their weaves and their hair. I mean, this is a really ugly program.”

Capehart then cuts in with, “’Get their weaves done, get their hair, really ugly program.’ What he just said there is really ugly.”

Another panelist cut in to say it “was really racist,” and “was all dog whistle.”

Our Analysis:

The Rhetoric of Division: A Deep Dive into a Newsmax Commentary

In an era where the media’s influence is both omnipresent and profound, the comments made by a Newsmax host regarding the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits have ignited a fiery discourse on the intersection of race, welfare, and political ideology. Jonathan Capehart’s reaction on MSNBC serves as a mirror reflecting the broader societal schism these comments exacerbate. Let’s dissect the layers of rhetoric, the underpinnings of prejudice, and the stark reality of SNAP benefits that have been so cavalierly caricatured.

The “Racist” Commentary

The Newsmax host’s comments, suggesting SNAP benefits are “being so woefully corrupted and exploited,” and that beneficiaries are using funds for frivolous purposes like “getting their nails done, to get their weaves and their hair,” are not just inaccurate, they’re insidiously racist. This isn’t merely an opinion; it’s a deliberate narrative strategy that targets vulnerable populations by using coded language that is all too familiar in the dialogue surrounding welfare programs in the United States.

Dog Whistles and Stereotypes

Calling these comments a “dog whistle” is apt, but perhaps too generous. This isn’t the subtle tuning of a whistle; it’s a bullhorn amplifying a long-debunked stereotype that minorities, particularly Black individuals, misuse governmental assistance for superficial extravagance. This narrative conveniently ignores the reality that the vast majority of SNAP beneficiaries are in dire need of assistance to afford basic necessities, like food, in a country where economic inequality is rampant.

The Misrepresentation of SNAP

The assertion that SNAP is “a really ugly program” that has “exploded over the last 20 years” and involves annual expenditure of “100 billion a year” demands a factual dissection. First, describing SNAP as “ugly” is a grotesque mischaracterization of a program that is a lifeline for millions of Americans, including children, the elderly, and the disabled. The growth of the program reflects not an inherent flaw, but rather the increasing economic disparities and the number of Americans who live in food-insecure households. The implication that the program is rife with corruption and exploitation is not only misleading but a dangerous falsehood that undermines the legitimacy and necessity of the assistance.

Economic Ignorance or Malice?

One must question whether the comments made by the Newsmax host stem from a place of economic ignorance or deliberate malice. The SNAP program, with strict eligibility requirements and oversight, has one of the lowest fraud rates among federal programs. The narrative of widespread corruption is a myth, propagated to stigmatize those who rely on such assistance, painting them as unworthy or lazy.

The Bigger Picture

The outrage expressed by Jonathan Capehart and the subsequent labeling of the comments as racist and as dog whistles is more than justified; it’s necessary. In highlighting these comments, Capehart and others draw attention to a broader, ongoing issue of racial and economic discrimination that is perpetuated by certain media narratives. This isn’t just about debunking falsehoods on a news show; it’s about challenging a harmful ideology that seeks to divide and diminish the struggles of the less fortunate.

Conclusion

The comments made by the Newsmax host and the reaction they’ve garnered are emblematic of a deeper societal ailment—a willingness by some to dehumanize and discriminate against the marginalized under the guise of fiscal conservatism or the critique of government programs. As we dissect these narratives, we must remain vigilant in our efforts to challenge and debunk them, for in their lies and distortions, we find the seeds of division and inequality that threaten the fabric of our society.
