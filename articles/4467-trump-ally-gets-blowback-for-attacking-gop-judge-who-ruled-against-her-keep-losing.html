Trump ally gets blowback for attacking GOP judge who ruled against her: 'Keep losing'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 9, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Kari Lake (Shutterstock)

Donald Trump appointee Kari Lake vowed to fight a ruling by an “activist” judge, but an insider was quick to point out that the judge was appointed by Ronald Reagan.

Lake’s actions in office were ruled “void” on Saturday after a conservative judge found that she was “illegally elevated.” She was put in charge of the United States Agency for Global Media (USAGM) by Trump, and soon went down a path of trying to systematically destroy her own agency.

Then, as part of a lawsuit brought against Lake, a judge has ruled that she was improperly put in charge.

David Folkenflik reported that Lake told NPR that “she will appeal Judge Lamberth’s ruling, invoking what she says is President Trump’s mandate to cut waste in spending.”

“An activist judge is trying to stand in the way of those efforts at USAGM,” she said in a statement.

But ex-GOP lawmaker Barbara Comstock was quick to point out how ridiculous that notion is.

“Judge Royce Lamberth, a Texan, former JAG, former US Attorney and appointed to the court by President Reagan is a well known conservative, you ignoramus,” Comstock wrote. “Keep Losing girl.”

Skye Perryman, President and CEO of Democracy Forward, co-counsel for the plaintiffs in the suit, also weighed in:

“Today is a win in the fight against autocracy. The court confirmed what we have long known: Kari Lake took over not to lead U.S. Agency for Global Media, but to dismantle it. Not only are Lake’s actions contrary to the mandate of Congress, but the president’s installation of her at the agency without Senate confirmation also violated the Constitution. Voice of America has played a critical role since World War II to combat misinformation and disinformation, often promulgated by the world’s autocrats. This decision is a powerful affirmation of the rule of law and comes as threats to democracy loom not only around the world, but here at home. The court’s action protects federal media professionals across the Voice of America and affirms the power of independent journalism.”

Perryman further added that, “We are honored to have represented workers, unions and journalists in resisting this administration’s power grab.”

Our Analysis:

Systematic Destruction vs. Judicial Intervention: The Lake Saga

In the swirling vortex of political upheaval that seems to perpetually define the Trump era, Kari Lake’s venture into the realm of the United States Agency for Global Media (USAGM) stands out as a particularly brazen attempt to undermine the very fabric of an institution. Appointed by Donald Trump, Lake’s mission appeared less about stewardship and more about demolition. Yet, in an ironic twist of fate, the hammer of justice swung by none other than a judge appointed by Ronald Reagan—a figure often revered in conservative circles—has declared her actions “void” and her appointment “illegal.”

The Illusion of Activism vs. The Reality of Conservatism

Lake’s defense, as reported, reeks of desperation and misinformation. Her attempt to paint Judge Royce Lamberth’s decision as the work of an “activist” judge is not just misleading; it’s laughably inaccurate. Lamberth, with his deep conservative roots and a career appointed by Reagan, is about as far from an “activist” judge as one can imagine. This label, often thrown around by those facing judicial rebuke, does little more than betray Lake’s lack of a substantive defense. Barbara Comstock’s scathing retort—”you ignoramus”—though harsh, aptly captures the absurdity of Lake’s claim.

The Bigger Picture: Autocracy vs. Democracy

The implications of Lake’s actions—and the subsequent legal ruling—extend far beyond personal political ambitions or intra-party squabbles. Skye Perryman’s statement encapsulates the gravity of the situation. The fight against Lake’s appointment is emblematic of a broader battle for the soul of American democracy. The USAGM, and particularly the Voice of America, has been a bulwark against misinformation and a beacon of free press globally. Lake’s attempt to dismantle this institution was not just a policy disagreement; it was an assault on the principles of independent journalism and the rule of law.

Perryman’s assertion that Lake’s takeover was not about leadership but destruction is a chilling reminder of the autocratic impulses lurking within the Trump administration. The court’s decision serves as a powerful countermeasure, reaffirming the importance of checks and balances, and the role of an independent judiciary in safeguarding democracy.

A Win for Democracy and Independent Journalism

The ruling against Lake is more than a legal rebuke; it’s a victory for the principles that define a democratic society. As threats to democracy and the free press continue to loom large, both domestically and internationally, this decision stands as a testament to the resilience of democratic institutions and the enduring power of the rule of law. Perryman’s pride in representing the workers, unions, and journalists opposing this “power grab” underscores the collective effort required to defend democratic norms.

In sum, Lake’s saga is a cautionary tale of what happens when political figures attempt to subvert the institutions they are meant to serve. It is also a reminder of the judiciary’s critical role in maintaining the balance of power and protecting the foundations of democracy. As this chapter closes, one can only hope it serves as a deterrent to future attempts at democratic erosion. But in an era where such attempts seem increasingly frequent, vigilance remains the watchword.
