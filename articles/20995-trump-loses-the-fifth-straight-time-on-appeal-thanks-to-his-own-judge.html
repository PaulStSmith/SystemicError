Trump loses the fifth straight time on appeal — thanks to his own judge

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

ICE’s Detention Order Hit a Wall Because It Was Never Lawful Power

The 9th Circuit just joined a widening judicial revolt against Trump’s mass detention scheme. The court said ICE and the administration had misread a 1996 immigration statute into something far larger and harsher than Congress actually wrote, and it matters immediately in California, Arizona, Nevada, and Washington, where thousands of people are detained.

The Power Is the Detention State

This is not a technical dispute about paperwork. The real issue is who gets to lock people up, on what theory, and for how long. Trump pushed a mandatory detention policy that tried to convert immigration enforcement into automatic confinement. That is executive power at its rawest: not deciding cases, but pre-emptively denying liberty and daring the courts to catch up.

The court’s ruling exposes the same pattern every time this administration reaches for control. Start with a blunt policy, dress it up as statutory interpretation, and hope the machinery of detention outruns legal review. That is not governance. It is coercion with legal stationery.

What the Court Actually Rejected

The 9th Circuit did not just nibble at the edges. It rejected the claim that Congress, back in 1996, quietly created a sweeping rule mandating detention for millions of people already living in the United States. The majority said that supposed transformation would have been too large to have gone unnoticed for three decades.

That is the core fact here: the administration was not faithfully enforcing a clear law. It was trying to retroactively manufacture one. Even by the standards of immigration punishment, that is a brazen move.

Bond Hearings Are the Bare Minimum

The practical consequence is straightforward. People who have lived in the United States for years and built families can now be detained only after a bond hearing, where an immigration judge has to decide whether they can safely remain in their communities while their cases proceed.

That is not leniency. It is the minimum legal check on state power. The fact that this had to be litigated across more than 10,000 cases tells you everything about how far ICE and the administration were willing to push. When a detention system needs this much judicial correction, the problem is not a few missteps. The system itself is the problem.

Misdirection Is the Point

The official language tries to make this sound like a dispute over “immigration law” or “detention policy.” That framing is too soft. What happened here is deliberate overreach. The administration claimed Congress had silently authorized mass detention, then acted as if the courts were the ones distorting reality by objecting.

That is a familiar authoritarian trick: present a power grab as administrative routine, then treat any resistance as legal confusion. It is especially obscene in the immigration context, where the state already relies on detention to isolate people from counsel, family, and community pressure. ICE is not a reformable agency that occasionally needs guidance. It is an enforcement arm built to criminalize vulnerability. Abolish ICE.

The Pattern Is Bigger Than One Circuit

The 9th Circuit decision is the fifth straight appellate loss for Trump on this policy, and the broader record is worse for the administration than any spin can hide. Politico’s numbers show 460 federal judges ruling against the detention scheme in more than 10,000 cases, versus 54 judges in about 1,100 cases siding with it. That is not a close constitutional debate. That is institutional rejection.

The deeper pattern is simple: executive cruelty keeps trying to outrun legal limits, and the federal courts keep having to drag it back. The problem is not that the law is ambiguous. The problem is that Trump’s immigration project depends on treating human confinement as a default administrative setting. When the state needs that much forced detention to function, the legal fight is only a symptom. The disease is the system itself.
