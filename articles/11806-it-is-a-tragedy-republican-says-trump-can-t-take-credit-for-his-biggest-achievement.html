'It is a tragedy': Republican says Trump 'can't take credit' for his biggest achievement

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20254 min read

Donald Trump had a huge achievement, but he "can't take credit for it," according to a former George W. Bush employee.

MSNBC host Elise Jordan on Saturday gave "credit where credit's due," saying, "I give the Trump administration first term huge credit for slicing and dicing regulations and getting that vaccine to the American people and to the world."

"It was, in my opinion, the best, top achievement of the Trump administration. The vaccine gave us our lives back. He can't take credit for it," she added. "It's so entrenched now in partisan warfare that MRNA funding is now slashed from the American budget."

Journalist Brandy Zadrozny agreed, saying, "Yeah, it really is a tragedy."

"You saw even last week Trump had a press conference and he said Warp Speed was really good, and it was it was a miracle of modern science," she said, noting that Trump "can't even take credit for it because the more populist part of his MAGA base was usurped by this anti-vaccine sentiment."

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Trump's Vaccine Achievement: A Closer Look

In a recent commentary that might raise more eyebrows than it soothes, MSNBC host Elise Jordan and journalist Brandy Zadrozny have ventured into the complex legacy of the Trump administration's role in the rapid development and distribution of the COVID-19 vaccine. Their analysis, while seemingly straightforward, dances around a minefield of contradictions and political intrigue that warrants a deeper dive.

Trump's Unclaimed Trophy?

Jordan's assertion that the Trump administration deserves "huge credit for slicing and dicing regulations and getting that vaccine to the American people and to the world" is a statement that, on its surface, many might reluctantly nod in agreement with. The operation known as Warp Speed indeed played a pivotal role in the unprecedentedly swift development of multiple COVID-19 vaccines. However, the accolade comes with an asterisk so large it's practically its own paragraph.

The Irony of Anti-Vax Sentiments

The heart of the irony lies in Jordan's observation that Trump "can't take credit for it." This isn't merely because of an oversight or modesty but rather a bizarre twist of political fate. Trump's base, which once rallied behind every word he said, has become so entrenched in anti-vaccine sentiment that acknowledging one of his administration's genuine achievements would be tantamount to political heresy in their eyes.

This scenario is a stark demonstration of the complex relationship between political leadership and public health policy. When the very crowd you've mobilized turns against a life-saving scientific achievement because it doesn't align with their prevailing narrative, it's a clear sign of a leadership failure. The failure here isn't in the operational success of Warp Speed but in fostering an environment where science is secondary to political allegiance.

The Tragedy of Slashed MRNA Funding

The discussion then shifts to an even more concerning development: the slashing of MRNA funding from the American budget. Here lies a grotesque irony; the technological marvel that brought us swiftly out of the darkest days of the pandemic is now seeing its financial lifelines cut. This decision is not just a step back; it's a leap into a chasm of wilful ignorance. The advancements in MRNA technology hold promise not just for future pandemics but for a range of diseases, including cancer. To cut its funding is to deny future generations the fruits of this scientific breakthrough.

Populism vs. Science: The Battle Continues

Zadrozny's remarks underscore a critical issue: the battle between populism and science. Trump's acknowledgment of Warp Speed as a "miracle of modern science" is drowned out by the louder, more populist voices within his base. This scenario is a microcosm of a larger, more disturbing trend where scientific achievements are scrutinized through the lens of political ideology rather than evaluated on their own merits.

Conclusion

The praise for Trump's administration in accelerating the vaccine's development is a nuanced subject, fraught with contradictions. It's a reminder that in the realm of public health, political victories are fleeting, and the real winners should be the people. The tragedy is not just in the inability of a leader to claim a legitimate victory but in the broader implications for science and public health policy when partisan politics take precedence over empirical evidence and human well-being. This analysis isn't just about giving credit where it's due; it's a clarion call to realign our priorities towards a future where science and health are shielded from the capricious winds of political fortune.
