'What's going on here?' MSNBC host stunned by White House court response

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Stark Reflection on Trump's Defiance of Judicial Authority

Donald Trump (Photo by Nathan Howard for Reuters)

The refusal of Donald Trump's administration to accept that the courts have ruled that it must bring back to the U.S. Kilmar Abrego García from a maximum security El Salvador prison where he was mistakenly sent left the panel on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" grasping for reasons to explain their refusal to accept the loss, have him returned and put out the firestorm.

Citing a brutal appeals court ruling that Trump and his Department of Justice are brazenly flouting rulings from a unanimous Supreme Court on down, co-host Joe Scarbrough was incredulous the president and his advisers have failed to read the room when even conservative Trump allies have also taken the side of the Maryland father of three.

After pointing out that Judge J. Harvie Wilkinson III wrote the case “should be shocking not only to judges, but to the intuitive sense of liberty that Americans far removed from courthouses still hold dear,” Scarborough piled on.

"It's pretty unambiguous," he exclaimed. "I mean in 2019 a judge ruled he [Abrego Garcia] couldn't be taken out of the country. The Trump administration then obviously agreed –– they didn't appeal it. The Trump administration this time said we made a mistake and, again, it's just like the Signalgate thing : know when to say, 'Hey, we made a mistake, we're going to bring you back and move on.'"

Noting that some in the White House are trying to "paint this as left wingers" who are furious about the deportation without due process, he added, "I mean, this isn't MSNBC and CNN, the New York Times editorial. No, it's not, it's Joe Rogan, right? Joe Rogan is going out there and saying, what's going on here, man?"

He then stated, "I don't understand this."

Our Analysis:

A Stark Reflection on Trump's Defiance of Judicial Authority

In a recent debacle that can only be described as a flagrant disregard for the rule of law, the Trump administration's refusal to repatriate Kilmar Abrego García, despite a unanimous Supreme Court ruling, stands as a shocking testament to its willingness to flout judicial authority. This situation, as discussed on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," underscores not just a political crisis but a fundamental threat to the principles of liberty and justice.

The Stubbornness of Power

The Trump administration's obstinance in the face of a clear legal mandate is nothing short of astounding. The courts, including the Supreme Court, have spoken: Kilmar Abrego García should not have been deported. Yet, Trump and his Department of Justice act as if they are above the law, a stance that is as dangerous as it is indefensible.

Judicial Rebuke

Judge J. Harvie Wilkinson III's words that this case "should be shocking not only to judges, but to the intuitive sense of liberty that Americans far removed from courthouses still hold dear," resonate deeply. It's a damning indictment of the Trump administration's actions, pointing to a broader issue of how liberty is perceived and protected in the United States. When a unanimous Supreme Court decision is not enough to provoke action, what is?

The Unlikely Critics

It's particularly telling when the chorus of criticism isn't just coming from the usual suspects. When figures like Joe Rogan, who cannot be pigeonholed into the mainstream media narrative often criticized by conservative circles, are raising alarms, it's indicative of a broader consensus of concern. This isn't a left or right issue; it's a matter of basic human rights and due process.

A Failure to "Read the Room"

The Trump administration's failure to "read the room," as co-host Joe Scarborough puts it, is a gross miscalculation. Even conservative allies, who have stood by Trump through various controversies, find themselves at odds with the administration on this issue. This isn't about partisan politics; it's about acknowledging and correcting a grave mistake.

The Signalgate Comparison

Scarborough's comparison to the "Signalgate thing" is apt—both situations highlight a stubborn refusal to admit wrongdoing, even when it's clear to all observers. The ability to admit a mistake, correct it, and move on seems to be a foreign concept to the Trump administration, a stance that only serves to exacerbate crises rather than quell them.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The refusal to bring Kilmar Abrego García back to the U.S., against the backdrop of a unanimous Supreme Court ruling, is a clear display of the Trump administration's disdain for judicial oversight and due process. This incident should not only shock the conscience of the judiciary and the American public but also serve as a rallying cry for all who believe in the principles of justice and the rule of law. It's time for the administration to acknowledge its mistake, comply with the court's ruling, and restore a measure of faith in the government's commitment to justice. Anything less is a betrayal of American values.
