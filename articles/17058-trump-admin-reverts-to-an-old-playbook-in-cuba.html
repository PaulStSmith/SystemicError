Trump admin reverts to an old playbook in Cuba

A Relentless Grasp: The U.S.’s Enduring Quest for Control Over Cuba

The Power Play

In tracing the contours of U.S. policy toward Cuba, one cannot overlook the stark dynamics of power at play. President Donald Trump’s recent intensification of this approach, including threats and sanctions coupled with military maneuvers suggestive of an impending invasion, is but the latest chapter in a long narrative of U.S. dominion. These actions are not isolated incidents but are deeply rooted in a historical context of U.S. attempts to shape Cuba in its own image, from economic exploitation to political interventions.

Historical Context as a Tool of Coercion

The U.S. obsession with controlling Cuba stretches back well over a century. From the Monroe Doctrine to Theodore Roosevelt’s stark expressions of frustration over Cuba’s resistance to U.S. dictates, the pattern is clear. The U.S. has historically viewed Cuba not as a sovereign nation but as a backyard garden to be pruned and landscaped at will. Such a perspective reveals a fundamental disrespect for Cuba’s independence, illustrating a broader American foreign policy approach that often dismisses the sovereignty of smaller nations when inconvenient to U.S. interests.

The Misdirection of Cultural Supremacy

The narrative often peddled, of bringing civilization or democracy to Cuba, has served as a veneer for more insidious intentions. This misdirection shifts focus from the exploitation and control motives to a supposed benevolent intervention. The historical U.S. view, as echoed by figures like Leonard Wood, who doubted Cubans’ capacity for self-governance, blatantly ignores the agency of the Cuban people and their capability to manage their affairs. Moreover, it insidiously suggests that any assertion of independence from Cuba is a sign of ingratitude or hostility rather than a legitimate exercise of sovereign will.

From Occupation to “Americanization”

The pattern of overreach continued with the U.S. military occupation that morphed into a cultural and economic “Americanization” of the island. The imposition of U.S.-style systems and the deep penetration of American business interests into every sector of Cuba’s economy were not merely capitalist ventures but a concerted effort to reshape Cuban identity and governance. This overwhelming influence led to a backlash, epitomized by Fidel Castro’s revolution and his subsequent efforts to “Cubanize” Cuba, an endeavor to reclaim Cuban culture and political autonomy.

The Illusion of Shift and the Reality of Stagnation

The brief thaw in relations under President Barack Obama, marked by resumed diplomatic ties and less aggressive rhetoric, appeared to be a shift toward mutual respect and engagement. However, Trump’s aggressive rollback to traditional hardline policies underscores a disheartening trend: short periods of conciliation followed by prolonged stretches of aggressive dominance. This oscillation between engagement and hostility reveals a deeper inability or unwillingness of U.S. policy frameworks to genuinely accept and deal with an independent Cuban state on equal terms.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Sovereignty and Respect

The U.S. treatment of Cuba is a microcosm of a larger foreign policy approach that too often sees might as right. A genuine reevaluation of this policy, one that respects Cuba’s sovereignty and right to self-determination, is overdue. Recognizing and correcting this historical pattern of overreach may not only lead to better relations with Cuba but could also serve as a model for U.S. interactions with other nations. It’s time for the U.S. to align its actions with its principles, treating other nations not as pawns on a geopolitical chessboard but as sovereign equals.
