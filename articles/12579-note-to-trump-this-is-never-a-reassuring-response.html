Note to Trump: This is never a reassuring response

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 20, 20257 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Ask yourself a simple question.

If someone wrote treacherous, slanderous lies claiming that you had engaged in some of the most disgusting conduct imaginable years ago — and you had not done anything of the kind — how would you respond?

Keep that answer to yourself, but I’m going to take a wild guess at the word choice you wouldn’t have selected:

”THEY GOT NOTHING!”

But it was with those words that the president of the United States greeted the nation on his tragically misnomered “Truth Social” media account with this really, really not great attempt at deflection:

“If there was a “smoking gun” on Epstein, why didn’t the Dems, who controlled the “files” for four years, and had Garland and Comey in charge, use it? BECAUSE THEY HAD NOTHING!!!”

To be fair, Trump did not use the full sentence, “they had nothing on me.” But “they had nothing” lands something akin to “they didn’t even find the drugs” when lambasting authorities for an unwarranted search of one’s home.

For me, it immediately had me humming lyrics from the great George Michael classic, Careless Whisper: “I’m never gonna dance again / Guilty feet have got no rhythm…”

Michael died tragically just 26 days before Trump first took office in 2017. But he seems to have written the soundtrack of Trump's life.

The most recent fuse to ignite intemperate Trump rage was a Wall Street Journal bombshell reporting that it had obtained a photo album that included a letter sent by Trump in 2003 to convicted child predator Jeffrey Epstein on the occasion of the latter’s 50th birthday.

From the Journal:

“The letter bearing Trump’s name, which was reviewed by the Journal, is bawdy — like others in the album. It contains several lines of typewritten text framed by the outline of a naked woman, which appears to be hand-drawn with a heavy marker. A pair of small arcs denotes the woman’s breasts, and the future president’s signature is a squiggly “Donald” below her waist, mimicking pubic hair.

The letter concludes: “Happy Birthday — and may every day be another wonderful secret.”

Trump’s angry response was duly noted by the newspaper:

“Trump denied writing the letter or drawing the picture. “This is not me. This is a fake thing. It’s a fake Wall Street Journal story,” he said.

“I never wrote a picture in my life. I don’t draw pictures of women,” he said. “It’s not my language. It’s not my words.”

He told the Journal he was preparing to file a lawsuit if it published an article. “I’m gonna sue The Wall Street Journal just like I sued everyone else,” he said.

Trump reiterated those threats Friday morning. And a willing line of sycophants, led by Vice President J.D. Vance, joined in condemnation of the Journal for claiming to have possession of this alleged birthday card when of course it could not possibly exist.

I’m going to venture a guess that if the Wall Street Journal published this story under direct threat of a Trump lawsuit, the odds of the newspaper having invented the existence of the birthday card — and not having forensically verified its authenticity — pretty much approximate zero.

The Journal didn’t accuse Trump of criminal wrongdoing. It simply added fuel to the fire of Trump’s undeniable friendship with Epstein — right around the time Epstein is alleged to have committed his most heinous crimes.

It does not follow that there is evidence of any criminal guilt whatsoever on Trump’s part. Even if he had cavorted with Epstein and some women — which video evidence seems to suggest is not an unimaginable prospect — it is an unreasonable and potentially libelous inference to suggest he engaged in any conduct involving underage women.

My guess is Trump did not. But that’s not one of his problems here.

Those problems include Trump’s own documented history of stoking — for much political gain — wild conspiracy theories about Democrats covering up for Epstein, being complicit in Epstein’s crimes and otherwise having adopted pedophilia as a key element of their party’s philosophy.

As of this morning, however, Trump has pivoted rather sharply to attacking Democrats for doing nothing with the Epstein files because “they had nothing” in them. Well, it can’t be both, can it?

But what looms even more ominously for Trump is the truth that he had more than a passing friendship with Epstein and that his efforts to deny or reinvent those — and those of his flock — just continue to make things much, much worse.

The Journal’s reporting of the bawdy birthday card in 2003, if accurate, is quite consistent with Trump’s public affection for Epstein, as I posted here, in which he most certainly had this to say: “He’s a lot of fun to be with… It is even said that he likes beautiful women as much as I do, and many of them are on the younger side.”

Trump knows the story isn’t over. He knows this isn’t about what has been said, but about what might be coming—through testimony, depositions, and receipts that he can’t control or deny. He knows the Epstein case remains toxic.

More from Michael’s Careless Whisper:

“There’s no comfort in the truth / Pain is all you’ll find.”

Trump’s tolerance for that remains to be seen.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump's "THEY GOT NOTHING" Gambit

In a world where the truth often stretches thinner than a politician's promise, former President Donald Trump's reaction to explosive accusations linking him to Jeffrey Epstein is as predictable as it is troubling. His response, encapsulated in the defiant cry of "THEY GOT NOTHING!" on the ironically named "Truth Social" platform, serves as a masterclass in deflection that deserves nothing short of a scathing analysis.

The Dubious Denial Dance

Trump's strategy, if we can grace it with such a term, is akin to a suspect who, when accused of possession, retorts with "they didn’t even find the drugs." This doesn’t so much absolve the accused as it does raise eyebrows about their understanding of innocence. Trump’s attempt to distance himself from the damning allegations by suggesting a lack of a "smoking gun" is as feeble as it is transparent.

The Bawdy Birthday Card Bombshell

At the heart of this latest controversy is a birthday card to Epstein, reported by The Wall Street Journal, adorned with a naked woman outline and a suggestive message, allegedly from Trump's hand. Trump's vehement denial and threats of lawsuits against the WSJ are as predictable as sunrise, yet they smack of desperation rather than innocence. After all, if the WSJ, under direct lawsuit threats, chose to publish, one must ponder the strength of their evidence.

Trump and Epstein: A Friendship Under Fire

It's not the card, per se, that stokes the flames of controversy but the undeniable, documented camaraderie between Trump and Epstein. Trump has previously been quoted speaking of Epstein in glowing terms, noting their shared appreciation for "beautiful women...on the younger side." These words, now echoing ominously against the backdrop of Epstein's heinous crimes, cast a long shadow over Trump's vehement denials.

The Hypocrisy of Conspiracy Theories

Adding a layer of irony thick enough to choke on, Trump has historically peddled conspiracy theories linking Democrats to Epstein's crimes, painting them as enablers or participants in pedophilia. Yet, when the spotlight swivels to illuminate Trump's own connections to Epstein, his tune changes to a desperate refrain of "they had nothing." This flip-flop not only undermines his previous assertions but highlights a staggering level of hypocrisy.

The Inescapable Truth

Trump's attempt to deflect and deny may momentarily distract, but it cannot erase the looming specter of further revelations. The truth, as George Michael hauntingly sings in Careless Whisper, offers no comfort, only pain. Trump's association with Epstein, underscored by the WSJ's reporting, suggests a saga far from concluded, with potential for much more to surface.

Conclusion: A Dance with Denial

Trump's response to the Epstein association allegations is a textbook example of deflection and denial. However, the effectiveness of such tactics is waning in the face of mounting evidence and the relentless pursuit of truth by investigative journalism. As the story unfolds, one thing becomes clear: denial might be a river in Trump's world, but it's quickly drying up under the harsh glare of scrutiny.
