'Nonsense numbers': Washington Post delivers math lesson to smash Trump claims

As President Donald Trump’s tariffs begin to kick in around the world, The Washington Post analyzed two of his claims: “we were losing $2 billion a day [under President Joe Biden], and “now we’re making $3 billion a day.”

In an analysis, Glenn Kessler wrote, “both the Treasury Department and the data released by Customs and Border Protection show Trump is way off the mark.”

Kessler broke down the February tariffs imposed on Canada and Mexico, which are 25%. It additionally calculated the March (20% tariffs) and April (145% tariffs) on China.

The monthly Treasury report detailed the tariffs added up to $8.16 billion for the month of March, or $263 million a day.

“Biden collected $76 billion in customs duties in fiscal 2024, or almost $210 million a day,” the outlet noted.

While the April numbers have not yet been released, Kessler wrote, “We will be generous and assume all of the collections were customs duties. On April 15, the day after Trump spoke, customs and excise taxes were $250 million. On April 18, this past Friday, the government collected $323 million. That’s an increase, perhaps reflecting some impact from his tariff hike, but it’s still almost one-tenth of Trump’s $3 billion claim.”

Kessler gave Trump “Four Pinocchios” and said, “Trump is peddling two nonsense numbers. First, tariffs are already bringing in $3 billion a year. He’s off by a factor of 10. Second, that Biden was losing $2 billion a year. That reflects a misunderstanding of trade — though with profound consequences.

The outlet also underscored that, “Trump is wrong to suggest this money is being collected by countries." Additionally, Kessler wrote, "economists agree that tariffs — essentially a tax on domestic consumption — are paid by importers, such as U.S. companies, which in turn pass on most or all of the costs to consumers or producers who may use imported materials in their products.” Meaning, “Americans will face higher prices for goods.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tales: A Factual Dissection

In a recent analysis by The Washington Post, President Donald Trump's grandiose claims about the financial windfalls from his imposed tariffs have been thoroughly debunked. Trump's assertions, namely that the United States was "losing $2 billion a day" under President Joe Biden and that currently "we’re making $3 billion a day," not only stretch the bounds of credulity but shatter them entirely. Glenn Kessler of The Washington Post takes on the herculean task of dissecting these claims, revealing them for the fabrications they are.

The Reality Check

Trump's Arithmetic Misfires

First and foremost, the assertion that the U.S. was hemorrhaging $2 billion daily under Biden's tenure is not just incorrect; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of how trade deficits work. Trade deficits do not equate to direct financial losses. Furthermore, Trump's boast of a $3 billion daily earning from tariffs is wildly inaccurate. According to the Treasury Department and data from Customs and Border Protection, the numbers don't even come close. For instance, in March, the tariffs amounted to $8.16 billion or $263 million a day, a far cry from Trump's $3 billion figure.

The Tariff Bluster

Kessler meticulously breaks down the tariffs imposed on Canada, Mexico, and China, showing that the numbers Trump touts are fantastical at best. For example, Biden's collection of customs duties in fiscal 2024 amounted to almost $210 million a day, not even in the same ballpark as Trump's claims. The notion that April's figures could somehow vindicate Trump's $3 billion claim is laughable, especially when the actual numbers from mid-April show collections ranging from $250 million to $323 million.

The Four Pinocchio Award

Kessler's analysis earns Trump "Four Pinocchios," a testament to the fallacious nature of his claims. Trump's misunderstanding—or deliberate misrepresentation—of how tariffs work and their impact on the economy is telling. Tariffs are not a foreign country paying the U.S.; they are taxes on domestic consumption. These costs are borne by U.S. companies and, ultimately, American consumers through higher prices on goods.

The Misguided Tariff Strategy

Trump's tariff strategy, aside from being based on erroneous financial claims, reflects a misunderstanding of global trade dynamics. Economists universally agree that tariffs are a tax on domestic consumers. The notion that these tariffs would be a cash cow for the U.S. treasury is misleading. Instead, they contribute to higher costs for American consumers and businesses, potentially harming the very economy Trump claims to bolster.

Conclusion: A Tale of Misinformation

Trump's claims about the profitability of his tariff policies are not just wildly inaccurate; they're dangerously misleading. They paint a picture of a financial boon when, in reality, the tariffs represent a tax on American consumers and businesses. This analysis by The Washington Post not only debunks Trump's financial fantasies but also highlights the importance of grounding economic policy discussions in fact, not fiction. The real story here is not about billions made or lost but about the profound consequences of misunderstanding—and misrepresenting—economic principles.
