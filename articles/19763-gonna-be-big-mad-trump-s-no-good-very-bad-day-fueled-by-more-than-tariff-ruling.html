'Gonna be big mad': Trump's no good, very bad day fueled by more than tariff ruling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 22, 20264 min read

President Donald Trump’s Friday got off to a rough start as three critical components of his economic agenda collapsed.

A major economic indicator, gross domestic product (GDP), slowed significantly, dropping to just 1.4% annually as inflation rose. And the U.S. Supreme Court struck down the legal basis for the president’s sweeping global tariffs, ruling that his use of that authority was unlawful.

“U.S. growth slowed more than expected near the end of 2025 as the government shutdown impacted spending and investment, while a key inflation metric showed high prices are still a factor for the economy, according to data released Friday,” CNBC reported.

GDP was expected to come in at 2.5%, experts predicted.

For 2025, the president delivered GDP far softer than his predecessor, President Joe Biden, did in 2024.

“For the full year in 2025, the U.S. economy grew at a 2.2% pace, down from the 2.8% increase in 2024,” CNBC noted.

Trump and his administration had been aiming for 3% to 4% growth.

“Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, in December, “said that it’s been a ‘very strong’ holiday season for the economy and predicted that the U.S. would end the year at 3% real GDP,” CNBC reported at the time.

Meanwhile, inflation “held firm in December, according to a gauge most closely watched by Fed officials that increased 3% from a year ago,” CNBC added, meaning that overall prices are three percent higher than they were one year ago.

But for Trump, perhaps the most devastating news came from the Supreme Court, where Chief Justice John Roberts, writing for a 6–3 majority, said Trump had acted outside his authority by unilaterally imposing global tariffs without congressional approval.

Critics were quick to weigh in.

“The ‘Trump economy’ is a story of lost jobs, and higher prices, caused by greed, corruption and incompetence,” noted professor and investor Adam Cochran.

“It’s hard to imagine a ruling that cuts more deeply to the heart of Trump’s identity in public life,” Politico’s Kyle Cheney wrote, “he has linked his presidency to the ability to use tariffs as a deal-making cudgel and bend other global powers to his will.”

Aaron Fritschner, Deputy Chief of Staff for U.S. Rep. Don Beyer (D-VA) wrote simply, “He’s gonna be big mad.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Collapse of Trump’s Economic Agenda

The article provides a comprehensive view of the significant setbacks President Donald Trump faced, marking a tumultuous period for his economic policies. Here, we dissect the claims and the real implications behind these developments.

GDP Growth Takes a Plunge

The slowdown of the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth to a meager 1.4% annually is the first blow to Trump’s economic agenda. This figure is not just a number; it’s a stark indicator of economic stagnation, falling significantly short of the expected 2.5%. The optimism of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who predicted a 3% real GDP by the end of the year, now seems like a distant dream. This shortfall is not merely a deviation from projections but a reflection of deeper economic malaise.

Inflation: The Unrelenting Adversary

Inflation remaining stubbornly high at 3% is another critical blow to the administration’s economic strategies. This isn’t just about numbers ticking up; it’s about the decreasing purchasing power of the average American, the strain on household budgets, and the overall economic discomfort felt by many. When the administration fails to curb inflation, it fails the people it vows to protect.

Supreme Court’s Rebuke

Perhaps the most dramatic setback for Trump was the Supreme Court’s ruling against his use of unilateral global tariffs. This decision not only undermines a significant component of his economic policy but also strikes at the heart of his public persona as a deal-maker able to wield tariffs as a weapon. The Supreme Court’s decision, led by Chief Justice John Roberts, is a clear rebuke of Trump’s overreach, emphasizing the necessity of congressional approval in such matters.

Critics’ Chorus

The voices of critics like Adam Cochran and Kyle Cheney echo the broader consequences of Trump’s policies - lost jobs and higher prices. These aren’t merely oppositional stances but are grounded in the reality of the administration’s economic outcomes. Their critiques underscore the disconnect between Trump’s promises and the tangible effects of his policies on the economy and by extension, on the lives of the American people.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Economic Disarray

The article paints a grim picture of Trump’s economic legacy - one marred by unfulfilled promises, legal setbacks, and a disconnect from the real-world impacts of his policies. This is not just about a series of unfortunate events but a systemic failure to steer the economy towards sustainable growth and stability. The administration’s inability to achieve projected GDP growth, control inflation, and legally justify its economic strategies represents a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard for the economic wellbeing of the nation.

In essence, the unfolding scenario is a testament to the administration’s flawed economic vision and execution. As the repercussions of these setbacks continue to unfurl, the ‘Trump economy’ risks being remembered not for its ambition, but for its profound disconnection from the economic realities and needs of the American populace.
