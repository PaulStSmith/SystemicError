'Awful': Outrage as legal US resident fined for not having 'papers' proving residency

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A federal agent aims at protesters at an ICE facility in Illinois. REUTERS/Jim Vondruska

Two immigrants were sitting in Rogers Park, Chicago, when Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents arrived and demanded their papers. What unfolded was detailed by the Chicago Tribune: Rueben Antonio Cruz, a 60-year-old legal U.S. resident, was ordered to stand up, shoved into an ICE vehicle, and interrogated as agents drove around the neighborhood.

Federal law requires legal residents and green card holders to carry their documentation at all times. While some states have “show me your papers” laws that allow local police to demand proof of residency, Illinois does not.

Cruz explained that his papers were at home. ICE agents pressed him for more information, asking about his parents and birthplace. Cruz responded that his parents had died.

Ultimately, agents verified his legal status in their database but still issued him a $130 fine for not having his documentation at the time.

Cruz’s companion, who is homeless and did not have any legal status or documents, was taken away by federal agents.

“America has never been a place where people need to ‘show one’s papers.’ Ticketing a lawful permanent resident — and forcing them to appear in court and pay a fine for not carrying their papers — is unnecessary and cruel,” said Ed Yohnka, communications director for the ACLU of Illinois.

Yohnka added, “It does not make our communities stronger or safer. It is simply part of the Trump Administration’s attempt to make life uncomfortable for all immigrants. It is just awful.”

Before Trump’s administration, the federal requirement for carrying documents was rarely enforced, but under his new quota of 3,000 daily arrests, enforcement has intensified.

Since early September, the Department of Homeland Security reports arresting and intending to deport 800 migrants in Chicago — far below the White House’s daily target. The administration has tried to discredit reports of quotas, but it remains unclear if such metrics are still in effect.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Reality of “Show Me Your Papers” in Chicago

In a disturbing echo of a darker past, two immigrants found themselves at the sharp end of America’s immigration enforcement in Rogers Park, Chicago. The incident, as reported by the Chicago Tribune, not only sheds light on the aggressive tactics employed by Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents but also underscores a troubling shift in the enforcement of federal documentation laws under the Trump administration.

Rueben Antonio Cruz, a 60-year-old legal U.S. resident, experienced firsthand the invasive and demeaning nature of these tactics. Despite his lawful status, Cruz was forcibly detained, shoved into an ICE vehicle, and subjected to an interrogation that seemed ripped from a bygone era of authoritarian governance. This is not merely an overzealous application of federal law—it’s a clear manifestation of a policy of intimidation and harassment.

Federal law indeed mandates that legal residents and green card holders carry their documentation at all times. However, the application of this law has traditionally been lenient, recognizing the impracticality and invasive nature of demanding papers on the spot. Illinois, understanding the potential for abuse, has wisely refrained from enacting “show me your papers” laws that empower local police to demand proof of legal residency. This discretion is rooted in a respect for individual rights and a recognition of the contributions immigrants make to the fabric of American society.

Yet, what happened to Cruz and his companion—a homeless individual without legal status—highlights a stark departure from these values. The fact that agents verified Cruz’s legal status but still fined him $130 for not carrying his papers at the moment of the encounter is both petty and punitive. It’s a tactic designed not to enforce law but to instill fear.

The commentary from Ed Yohnka, communications director for the ACLU of Illinois, nails the broader implications of this incident. The enforcement of such documentation requirements, especially in a state that has rejected the “show me your papers” approach, does nothing to enhance community safety or strength. Instead, it’s a clear-cut case of the Trump administration’s broader strategy to make life unbearable for immigrants, legal or otherwise. It’s punitive, it’s cruel, and it’s fundamentally un-American.

The intensification of document enforcement, with ICE agents roaming neighborhoods to interrogate and detain, marks a significant escalation in the administration’s immigration crackdown. The supposed quota of 3,000 daily arrests, while officially discredited by the administration, points to an unhealthy obsession with targeting immigrants rather than addressing the complex issues surrounding immigration in a humane and effective manner.

The arrest and intended deportation of 800 migrants in Chicago, as reported by the Department of Homeland Security, underscores a bleak reality. It’s a number that falls short of the White House’s alleged daily target but still represents hundreds of lives disrupted by aggressive enforcement policies.

In sum, the incident in Rogers Park is not an isolated event but a symptom of a broader, more disturbing trend. It’s a clear signal that the principles of dignity, respect, and fairness, which should underpin our approach to immigration, are being eroded. As we reflect on this unsettling development, it’s crucial to remember that the strength of our communities lies in our diversity and our commitment to upholding the rights of all individuals, regardless of their origin. The path forward should be one of compassion, respect, and understanding—not fear, fines, and forced deportations.
