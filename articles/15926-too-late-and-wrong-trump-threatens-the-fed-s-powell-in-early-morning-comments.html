'Too late and wrong': Trump threatens the Fed's Powell in early morning comments

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump was up early on Thursday morning, launching a fresh attack on Fed chair Jerome Powell for ignoring his demand to lower interest rates while threatening him with termination.

In apparent reaction to reports that the European Central Bank (ECB) is poised to cut interest rates for the seventh time in a year today, the president –– reeling from criticism about his handling of the economy –– tried to shift the blame to Powell.

Coming a day after Powell instead warned about the tariff war Trump began, and stated, "Without price stability, we cannot achieve the long periods of strong labor market conditions that benefit all Americans,” hinting at no rate reduction on the horizon, Trump flipped out on Truth Social.

Early Thursday, he wrote, "The ECB is expected to cut interest rates for the 7th time, and yet, 'Too Late' Jerome Powell of the Fed, who is always TOO LATE AND WRONG, yesterday issued a report which was another, and typical, complete 'mess!'"

He then insisted that, "Oil prices are down, groceries (even eggs!) are down, and the USA is getting RICH ON TARIFFS. Too Late should have lowered Interest Rates, like the ECB, long ago, but he should certainly lower them now."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Rhetoric: A Closer Look

Donald Trump's recent tirade against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell pulls back the curtain on a concerning blend of misinformation, scapegoating, and misunderstanding of economic policy. Trump's early morning outburst, as reported, focuses on his dissatisfaction with Powell's refusal to bend the knee to his demands for lower interest rates, juxtaposed with the European Central Bank's (ECB) contrasting approach.

Misguided Economic Policy Critique

Trump's criticism of Powell for not lowering interest rates in tandem with the ECB's actions reveals a stark disregard for the independence of the Federal Reserve and a fundamental misunderstanding of its role in the U.S. economy. Powell's warning about the implications of Trump's tariff war is a sober reminder of the broader economic impacts of such policies. Trump's response, however, is to shift the blame onto Powell, with the claim that "Without price stability, we cannot achieve the long periods of strong labor market conditions that benefit all Americans" being twisted into an opportunity for political grandstanding rather than a moment for serious economic reflection.

The Blame Game

Trump's attempt to divert attention from his own economic policies to Powell is a classic move in political scapegoating. His assertion that the U.S. is "getting RICH ON TARIFFS" is a gross oversimplification and misrepresentation of the complex economic impacts of tariffs, which often result in increased prices for consumers and strain on various sectors of the U.S. economy. The reference to lower oil prices and groceries, including eggs, as an argument for rate cuts overlooks the multifaceted factors that influence these prices, including global supply and demand dynamics.

The Misuse of Power

Trump's statement that "Powell’s termination cannot come fast enough!" is particularly alarming. It underscores a dangerous inclination towards undermining the Federal Reserve's independence, a cornerstone of its ability to manage the U.S. monetary policy free from political pressure. This kind of rhetoric not only threatens the institutional integrity of the Federal Reserve but also risks the credibility of the U.S. economic policy on the global stage.

Reality Check

It's essential to recognize the broader context of Trump's attacks on Powell. This is not just about interest rates; it's part of a larger pattern of behavior where Trump seeks to control or undermine any institution or individual that does not align with his immediate interests or viewpoints. The ECB's decision to cut interest rates is based on its own economic context and should not be directly compared to the Federal Reserve's policy decisions without acknowledging the different economic conditions and challenges each entity faces.

In conclusion, Trump's latest outburst against Jerome Powell is more than just a political squabble; it's a manifestation of a troubling approach to economic policy and governance. By prioritizing personal grievances and misunderstandings of economic principles over informed policy decisions, Trump not only jeopardizes the economic stability of the United States but also undermines the institutions designed to protect it. The need for a rational, informed, and independent approach to economic policy has never been clearer.
