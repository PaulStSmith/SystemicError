GOP election deniers 'quietly admit' Trump actually lost in 2020

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Lie Was Never the Point

Power Before Principle

CNN asked five Republican gubernatorial nominees who won in 2020, and the answer changed the moment general elections entered the frame. Andy Biggs, Stacy Garrity, Tom Tiffany, John James, and Rick Jackson all have records of lying about the election or helping try to overturn it. Now some of them are suddenly willing to say Biden “won” or was “elected,” without explanation.

That is the story. Not growth. Not reflection. Not truth. Power changed the incentive structure, and the lie became less useful.

The Election Was a Tool

Election denial was never a sincere theory of the world. It was a political technology: keep the base inflamed, keep Trump central, keep reality negotiable for as long as it served the movement. Once the same candidates needed broader support, the magic words changed.

That tells you who had agency. These Republicans were not dragged along by confusion or misinformation. They helped build the machinery, used it, and now are trying to step around it without paying the cost of having done so.

Misdirection, Straight Up

The article’s real value is that it exposes how much Republican rhetoric depends on euphemism. “Questions” about election security. “Certified” winner. Careful phrasing designed to preserve the grievance without saying the thing too plainly.

That is not moderation. It is damage control. The lie is still there; it is just being laundered for a different audience. The goal is not truth. The goal is to keep the coalition intact long enough to win the next race.

Trump’s Fraying Control

Brian Beutler’s point about the Trump project matters: its claim to mastery only works while everyone acts as if it is inevitable. Once Republican voters start ignoring Trump-backed candidates in primaries, and once Trump-backed nominees start backpedaling on election denial, the performance loses its force.

That is why the cracks matter. Not because the movement has become harmless, but because its internal discipline is weaker than its propaganda pretends. The people running this operation know exactly what they are doing. When the incentives shift, they reveal it.

The Collapse of the Costume

The failed censorship push against Jimmy Kimmel and the success of Bad Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime show point to something these operatives keep trying to deny: they do not dominate American culture, and they do not own the national story.

That matters politically because authoritarian projects survive on the fantasy of total control. When they cannot command the narrative, they fall back on grievance, spectacle, and selective truth-telling. The costume only works while everyone pretends the emperor is still wearing it.

The Pattern

This is the broader lesson: reactionary politics in the U.S. often treats lying as infrastructure. It is not a glitch in the system. It is how the system is built to function when accountability is weak and ambition is strong.

The moment truth becomes electorally inconvenient, the lie is dropped, repackaged, or denied retroactively. The people who built the falsehood do not become principled when they soften their language. They become more tactical. And that is the more dangerous truth hiding in plain sight.
