Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard is at the storm of what a former GOP strategist has called a worse moment for Donald Trump’s administration than the Yemen leak.

Highly sensitive military information had been leaked inadvertently in March 2025 when a group chat including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Vice President JD Vance also featured The Atlantic’s editor-in-chief, Jeffrey Goldberg. It marked an embarrassing moment for the administration, but there are fears of even bigger new leak problems involving Gabbard, according to political analyst Rick Wilson.

The National Security Agency flagged a phone call between two foreign intelligence members, with highly sensitive communication — reportedly involving somebody close to Trump — brought to Gabbard’s attention.

The director, rather than allowing the NSA’s investigation to continue, took a paper copy to Susie Wiles, the president’s chief of staff, according to The Guardian.

This move could be damaging to the administration, according to Wilson, who appeared on political commentator Molly Jong-Fast’s podcast to discuss the problem Gabbard has caused.

Wilson said, “She is in some deep, deep, deep, deep s--t. The intelligence community people who are still around give a damn about their job. They give a damn about the country and the security and she has put us in danger.

“This is at a level that is so above and beyond because of both the nature of the target, whoever this foreign intelligence person was, and the collection system from the NSA that got the information. This is something incredibly sensitive, crown-jewel-level stuff.

“This is a blazing red flag about Gabbard’s inability and lack of temperament to do this job.”

Fast Politics host Jong-Fast then asked if this situation is “worse or better” than the Yemen strike leak from Hegseth last year.

“This is wildly worse,” Wilson replied. “That foreign intelligence person almost certainly will become aware that they are targeting them in a certain way, they will communicate differently.

“The other part of this is what it implicates in the Trump administration. We have seen in what we know of the complaint so far is that the person they were talking to is ‘close to Trump,’ this does not mean it is a government official.

“Given Donald Trump’s propensity for using outside actors, Steve Whitkoff, Jared Kushner, who are not government officials, to conduct diplomacy and foreign policy, I’m deeply concerned that somebody inside the Trump government... gets on the phone and calls another person and says ‘did you hear about this thing that we’re doing?’”

Our Analysis:

Gabbard’s Gambit: A Security Fiasco

In what can only be described as a staggering lapse in judgment, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard finds herself at the epicenter of a controversy that makes the Yemen leak look like a minor faux pas. Rick Wilson, a former GOP strategist turned political analyst, has minced no words in highlighting the gravity of the situation, dubbing it a potentially catastrophic moment for Trump’s administration. But is it just about Gabbard’s alleged mishandling, or does this incident unravel a deeper, more systemic issue within the administration?

The Leak: A Symptom of a Larger Malaise

The details, as reported, are damning. A phone call, flagged by the National Security Agency for containing “highly sensitive communication” and involving an individual “close to Trump,” was not just intercepted but mishandled in a manner that can only be described as breathtakingly irresponsible. Gabbard’s decision to hand over a paper copy of this sensitive information to Susie Wiles, the president’s chief of staff, instead of allowing the NSA to continue its investigation, is the stuff of security nightmares.

Wilson’s commentary on the matter is both scathing and terrifyingly accurate. The intelligence in question was not just any run-of-the-mill classified information but was described as “crown-jewel-level stuff.” This isn’t just about the mishandling of sensitive information; it’s a glaring testament to Gabbard’s “inability and lack of temperament” for her role.

The Broader Implications

Wilson’s analysis, however, points to a more sinister undercurrent within the Trump administration. The involvement of someone “close to Trump” — potentially an outside actor not officially part of the government — is a recurring theme that raises red flags about the administration’s operational integrity. Donald Trump’s history of using individuals like Steve Whitkoff or Jared Kushner for diplomatic endeavors, as Wilson notes, complicates the situation further, blurring the lines between official governmental diplomacy and personal dealings.

This incident isn’t just “wildly worse” than previous leaks because of the immediate security implications; it’s indicative of a systemic problem of governance and accountability. The fact that sensitive national security information could be so cavalierly handled points to a troubling disregard for the protocols and principles that are supposed to safeguard the nation’s secrets.

A Crisis of Confidence

At its core, this debacle is a crisis of confidence. Confidence in Gabbard’s capabilities as the Director of National Intelligence is now severely undermined, casting a long shadow over her tenure. But more importantly, it’s a crisis of confidence in the administration’s ability to manage its affairs with the necessary level of security and discretion.

Wilson’s stark warning about the potential for foreign intelligence to alter their communication strategies in light of this leak underscores the broader implications for national security. Once the genie is out of the bottle, it’s not just about managing the fallout but dealing with the long-term strategic disadvantages that such leaks pose.

Conclusion: A System in Disarray

The Gabbard episode is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a broader malaise affecting the Trump administration. It’s a malaise characterized by a cavalier attitude towards sensitive information, a penchant for involving non-governmental actors in official capacities, and a glaring lack of accountability at the highest levels.

In the final analysis, this isn’t just about Gabbard’s failure; it’s about an administration that seems perpetually unable to grasp the sanctity of the responsibilities it bears. As the details continue to unfold, one thing is clear: this incident will remain a stain on Trump’s presidency, serving as a stark reminder of the perils of negligence and hubris in the handling of national security.
