Trump risks 'biblical disaster' with new reaction to 'panic situation': oil industry giant

A global finance insider warned the Trump administration against making risky moves to bring down oil prices in response to Iran’s threats to the global supply chain.

Terry Duffy, chief executive of CME Group, which operates the exchange where U.S. oil futures are traded, told other industry insiders this week at a conference that the administration risked a “biblical disaster” if officials attempted intervene in the derivatives markets during President Donald Trump’s war with Iran, reported Financial Times.

“Markets do not like it when governments intervene in pricing,” Duffy told the conference in Boca Raton, Florida. “Such a move would risk a ‘biblical disaster’ if investors lost confidence in markets to set the price of critical commodities,” FT added.

The U.S. Treasury Department is reportedly considering various measures to bring down spiking oil prices, including intervention in the futures market, and the administration announced the release of millions of barrels of oil from the strategic reserve to hold off a price shock.

“The administration could pursue other options to shelter U.S. consumers, such as temporarily suspending federal taxes on gasoline, relaxing environmental rules on fuel or temporarily banning U.S. oil exports,” FT reported.

A series of large, unexplained trades in recent days set off rampant speculation among investors that the Treasury Department was selling off crude futures, but a person familiar with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the agency had not intervened in oil markets.

“The idea of the U.S. Treasury selling front-month crude futures” was getting “more attention than usual,” wrote analysts for Rapidan Energy Group. “Given the current panic situation we cannot completely rule it out.”

The Treasury Department declined to comment on the speculation, but spokesman for the Department of Energy said it had not been involved in oil derivatives trading or advising other government agencies on that course of action.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of U.S. Administration’s Potential Intervention in Oil Prices

Institutional Power and Decision Makers

The article discusses the Trump administration’s consideration of various measures to manage spiking oil prices, highlighting the role of the U.S. Treasury Department and the Department of Energy. These bodies hold significant institutional power in this context, capable of influencing national economic and energy policies. The decision to intervene in the oil market, directly or indirectly, falls squarely within the purview of these government entities, particularly under the guidance of the Treasury Secretary and energy department officials. It’s important to note that while the CME Group CEO, Terry Duffy, offers a critical perspective, he does not possess institutional power over U.S. policy decisions; his role is advisory at best.

Decision Makers and Their Actions

The decision maker in this scenario, potentially leading to market intervention, would be the Trump administration, specifically through actions initiated by the Treasury Department under Scott Bessent’s advisement. The administration’s announcement to release millions of barrels of oil from the strategic reserve directly impacts market conditions. However, the speculative actions, such as selling off crude futures, remain unconfirmed by the department, with a spokesperson declining to comment.

Framing and Responsibility

The article’s framing suggests a potential overreach or misguided intervention by the Trump administration in an attempt to counteract oil price spikes attributed to geopolitical tensions with Iran. By focusing on the administration’s considered responses to these spikes, the article might shift responsibility toward government actors while underemphasizing the complex global dynamics that contribute to oil price fluctuations, including market speculations, investor responses, and international geopolitical events.

Moreover, the framing around Terry Duffy’s warning against government intervention in pricing highlights a critical industry perspective, cautioning against potential market destabilization. This serves to underscore the risks associated with government actions in free markets, though it might overstate the administration’s direct influence on market prices in the absence of confirmed interventions.

Conclusion

The Trump administration, particularly through the Treasury Department and the Department of Energy, holds the institutional power and decision-making capability in this situation. While the administration’s considerations and actions regarding oil prices are central to the narrative, the article’s focus on these potential interventions may misdirect responsibility somewhat, by not fully accounting for the broader market and international factors at play. Furthermore, speculation about unconfirmed government actions in the futures market introduces uncertainty that could mislead readers about the extent of the administration’s direct involvement.

Criticism of potential government intervention in oil pricing is valid, given the risks of market destabilization highlighted by industry experts. However, it’s crucial to maintain a balanced perspective that recognizes the multifaceted causes behind oil price fluctuations, avoiding the trap of attributing excessive control or blame to a single actor within a complex international landscape.
