Trump allies go down in flames in attempted takeover of the Washington DC Bar

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 10, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump's allies made a dash to try and take over the Bar Association for Washington, D.C. — but the effort failed badly.

In March, NBC News reported that two Trump allies were running for the D.C Bar's leadership. Bradley Bondi, Attorney General Pam Bondi's brother, and Alicia Long, who was a deputy to brief interim U.S. Attorney Ed Martin, tried to seize control.

After a vote, Bondi couldn't cross 10% support among the membership.

According to the Bar Association website, Diane A. Seltzer received 34,982 votes to Bondi's 3,490 votes. Long did significantly better than Bondi with over 8,800 votes. She still lost, however, to Amanda C. Molina, who had 26,380 votes.

As NPR reported in May, the race garnered a lot of attention, resulting in the highest vote among its membership ever. The next largest election was in 1990 and saw less than one-third of the participation.

"My priority is making sure that the rule of law is upheld, that we feel that we are safe to do our jobs and that we can go forward every day representing the clients we choose," Seltzer said at the time of the NPR interview.

As reporter Carrie Johnson explained, "The Bar president mostly plays an administrative role and has no say in attorney discipline. Seltzer's running against prominent securities attorney Brad Bondi. Bondi has represented billionaire Elon Musk and the Trump Media & Technology Group."

"BIG WIN for the D.C. Bar & democracy President-Elect Diane Seltzer triumphed with a staggering 90% of the vote. Given what one Bondi has done as AG, we did not need to take a risk on another heading the DC Bar," commented Norm Eisen, a former ethics czar for Barack Obama's administration.

"If they were going to make me vote for the first time in D.C. Bar elections, at least the person I voted for won convincingly. I will now resume not caring about the D.C. Bar one iota," said D.C. lawyer Sean Marotta.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Trump Allies' Failed Bid for D.C. Bar Leadership

In a striking display of political ambition clashing with professional integrity, the recent attempt by President Donald Trump's allies to commandeer the leadership of the Bar Association for Washington, D.C., ended not with a bang, but a whimper. This episode stands as a testament to the legal community's resilience against political encroachment, but also raises questions about the future of such institutions in the face of increasingly bold political maneuvers.

The Failed Coup

Bradley Bondi and Alicia Long, both closely allied with Trump, made a bold move to infiltrate the ranks of the D.C. Bar's leadership. The sheer audacity of this attempt cannot be overstated. Bondi, with familial ties to Attorney General Pam Bondi, and Long, a deputy to brief interim U.S. Attorney Ed Martin, sought to leverage their political connections in a domain that prides itself on impartiality and the rule of law. Their failure was not just evident but emphatic, with Bondi unable to garner even 10% of the membership's support.

Diane A. Seltzer and Amanda C. Molina, the victors in this electoral skirmish, did not just win; they triumphed with a level of support that underscores the legal community's commitment to professional, rather than political, leadership. Seltzer's victory, in particular, receiving 34,982 votes to Bondi's 3,490, is a resounding endorsement of her vision for the D.C. Bar.

A Record-Breaking Election

The interest this election stirred within the D.C. Bar's membership is worthy of note. Garnering the highest vote among its membership ever, it eclipsed the previous record set in 1990 by a significant margin. This heightened engagement signals a legal community increasingly aware of and resistant to political influence in its governance.

Seltzer's priorities, as stated in her NPR interview, resonate as a clear manifesto for maintaining the independence and integrity of the legal profession. Her commitment to upholding the rule of law and ensuring lawyers feel safe to represent their chosen clients without fear of political reprisal is a stark contrast to the political motivations perceived to be behind Bondi and Long's candidacies.

The Role of the Bar President

The significance of this election extends beyond the numbers. As NPR's Carrie Johnson pointed out, the Bar president's role is primarily administrative, with no say in attorney discipline. This fact makes the political undertones of Bondi's and Long's bids all the more curious and concerning. Were they seeking a platform, however symbolically, to advance a political agenda? Or was it an attempt to gain leverage within an influential legal community?

Reaction and Reflection

The reactions from figures like Norm Eisen, a former ethics czar for Barack Obama's administration, encapsulate the broader legal community's relief and satisfaction with the election's outcome. Eisen's comment highlights a collective apprehension about the potential implications of a Bondi presidency for the D.C. Bar, given the controversial record of his sister, Pam Bondi, as AG.

On the flip side, D.C. lawyer Sean Marotta's tongue-in-cheek statement about resuming his indifference towards the D.C. Bar post-election underscores a potentially troubling apathy within the legal community. It's a reminder that vigilance and engagement are crucial in safeguarding the independence of legal institutions from political influence.

Conclusion: A Victory for Professionalism over Politics

The failed bid by Trump's allies to take over the D.C. Bar leadership is a stark reminder of the ongoing challenges facing professional organizations in an era of heightened political polarization. The overwhelming support for Seltzer and Molina signals a legal community firmly rooted in the principles of professionalism, integrity, and independence. However, this episode should not lead to complacency but rather serve as a call to arms for continued vigilance against the encroachment of politics into spaces that are foundational to democracy and the rule of law.
