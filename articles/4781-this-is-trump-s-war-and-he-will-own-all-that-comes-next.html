The United States is now at war with Iran.

A single person — Donald J. Trump — has released the dogs of war on one of the most dangerous countries in the world, and done it without the consent of Congress or our allies, or even a clear explanation to the American people.

Just four days after delivering a State of the Union address in which he spoke of ending eight wars — spending just three minutes discussing Iran and a preference for “diplomacy.”

Anyone who has doubted Trump’s intention to replace American democracy with a dictatorship should now be fully disabused.

I share your despair, sadness, and fear. Even if our president was a wise and judicious man, surrounded by thoughtful advisers with impeccable integrity and wisdom, this would be a highly dangerous move.

Trump is facing the consequences of his decision in his first term to abandon the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action negotiated with Iran by Barack Obama and backed by France, Germany, the United Kingdom, Russia, and China.

Trump walked away from that treaty because it was Obama’s — and he hates Obama because Obama negotiated safeguards against Iran enriching uranium to weapons grade. Obama also got Obamacare through Congress, addressed climate change and nuclear proliferation, and was rewarded the Nobel Peace Prize.

Obama was a winner. Trump is a loser. Trump cannot stomach this.

But why should America and thousands if not millions of innocent people pay the price of Trump’s egomaniacal stupidity?

Trump claimed in June to have disarmed Iran. He claimed again in his State of the Union last Tuesday to have “obliterated” the Iranian nuclear weapons program (an assertion rejected by the International Atomic Energy Agency).

Since then, Iran has taken steps to dig out the nuclear facilities hit during those strikes and it has resumed work at some sites long known to American spy agencies.

But those same spy agencies say there’s no evidence that Iran has made active efforts to resume enriching uranium or trying to build a mechanism to detonate a bomb.

Iran’s stockpiles of enriched uranium remain buried after June’s strikes, making it nearly impossible for Iran to build a bomb “within days,” as Trump and his lapdogs claim.

Trump says he wants “regime change.” But unlike Venezuela, the Islamic Republic has nearly a million men under arms. Any attempt to overthrow that regime will require American troops on the ground, and almost surely inflict mass casualties on Americans and on Iranians.

Besides, Trump won a second term promising “no regime change” and in 2024 he campaigned as “the first president in decades who started no new wars.”

He hasn’t prepared the American people for this. In his State of the Union he bragged again about having ended eight wars. He spent just three minutes discussing Iran and his preference for “diplomacy.”

He said Iran has refused to foreswear any nuclear weapons ambitions. Yet just hours before his address, Iran’s foreign minister reaffirmed on X that his country would “under no circumstances ever develop a nuclear weapon.”

Trump noted the Iran regime’s killing of thousands of protesters, but this hardly justifies a war that may cause the deaths of thousands more innocent civilians. (On Saturday morning, Iran’s Red Crescent said more than 60 children were killed in the strike on Shajarah Tayyebeh school in the southern town of Minab (a toll that has since been raised to 85.)

Make no mistake. The costs of this war — mayhem and deaths in the Middle East, higher oil prices (as Iran closes the Straight of Hormuz), increased risk of terrorism in Europe and the United States — could be catastrophic.

Yet Americans don’t support this war. They haven’t been told why we’re waging it. Trump’s MAGA base doesn’t want him to engage in regime change. Congress hasn’t approved this war.

Trump is going to war for himself and his boundless, malicious ego.

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Iran Escalation: A Dangerous Game of Ego

In a world already teetering on the brink of geopolitical chaos, the United States’ unilateral declaration of war on Iran, as orchestrated by Donald J. Trump, marks a precipitous descent into what could very well be an abyss of unprecedented global turmoil. The article penned by Robert Reich, an emeritus professor of public policy at Berkeley and former secretary of labor, lays bare the recklessness of Trump’s actions, actions driven not by strategic foresight but by a dangerous mix of ego and disdain for his predecessor.

The Unsanctioned March to War

Trump’s decision to engage in full-scale hostilities with Iran, without the consent of Congress, allies, or the American populace, is a stark reminder of the perils inherent in the concentration of military decision-making power in the hands of a single individual. His flagrant disregard for democratic norms and international diplomacy is not just troubling; it’s a clarion call for urgent and comprehensive reform in how the U.S. decides matters of war and peace.

A Legacy of Antipathy

The roots of this escalation can be traced back to Trump’s withdrawal from the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), a move motivated more by personal vendettas against former President Barack Obama than any grounded policy rationale. This withdrawal not only undermined years of diplomatic efforts but also set the stage for the current crisis. It’s a classic case of cutting off one’s nose to spite the face, with grave implications not just for the U.S. but for global security at large.

The Mirage of Victory

Trump’s claims of having “obliterated” the Iranian nuclear weapons program stand in stark contrast to assessments from the International Atomic Energy Agency and U.S. intelligence. It’s a dangerous game of smoke and mirrors, where rhetoric is disastrously disconnected from reality. The assertion that Iran is on the brink of developing a nuclear weapon is not just misleading; it’s a fabrication designed to justify war.

A War Without Justification

The justifications for war offered by Trump are as flimsy as they are hypocritical. Citing the Iranian regime’s internal repression while planning a military campaign that will inevitably lead to civilian casualties is the height of cynicism. Moreover, Trump’s promise of no new wars and his sudden pivot to military aggression against Iran exposes a startling disregard for consistency, truth, and the lives of both Americans and Iranians.

The Human and Economic Toll

The potential consequences of this war — from regional instability and the devastation of civilian lives to skyrocketing oil prices and heightened global terrorism risks — cannot be overstated. Yet, the American public, whose sons and daughters will bear the brunt of this conflict, has been left in the dark, their voices sidelined in a decision that will reverberate for generations.

The Egotistical Imperative

At the heart of this debacle is Trump’s ego, a force so potent that it seemingly eclipses the collective will of the American people, the wisdom of diplomatic counsel, and the lessons of history. His war on Iran is not a strategic maneuver but a manifestation of personal vendettas and a desperate grasp for a legacy — a legacy that, if this war proceeds, will be marked by bloodshed and sorrow.

Conclusion: A Call to Reason

As Reich’s analysis vividly illustrates, the United States stands on a precipice, pushed there by a leader whose actions are guided not by the national interest but by personal animosity and a relentless pursuit of self-aggrandizement. This is not just a systemic error; it’s a systemic failure, one that demands immediate rectification lest the consequences become irrevocable. The war with Iran, based on the whims of an egotistical leader, is a stark reminder of the need for a return to principled, transparent, and democratic foreign policy decision-making.
