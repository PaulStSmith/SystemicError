'Out of control whining': GOP reprimanded for double standard over Trump bill

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA). (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

NBC News reporter Ryan Nobles noted that House Republicans once spent considerable time complaining that they were unable to read legislation from Democrats and the administration of former President Joe Biden. Now that they've been caught not reading President Donald Trump's budget bill before voting for it — and it's causing them problems.

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) made news on Tuesday when she confessed in an X post that she had no idea that the House budget bill had a provision in it "that strips states of the right to make laws or regulate AI for 10 years."

"I am adamantly OPPOSED to this and it is a violation of state rights, and I would have voted NO if I had known this was in there," Greene wrote.

"We have no idea what AI will be capable of in the next 10 years, and giving it free rein and tying states' hands is potentially dangerous," she continued. "This needs to be stripped out in the Senate."

She wasn't the only one who voted to approve a bill without knowing what was in it.

Rep. Mike Flood (R-NE) also confessed he didn't know about a provision that would block the federal courts from holding Trump and his administration in contempt of court.

"I am not going to hide the truth. This provision was unknown to me when I voted for that bill, and when I found out that provision was in the bill, I immediately reached out to my Senate counterparts and told them of my concern," Flood told a town hall meeting in his district.

The audience booed.

Nobles said that this had been a problem when Republicans felt they couldn't read Democrats' proposed bills, but it doesn't appear to be one for the GOP today.

"What's even more remarkable about that, Katie, is that it was House Republicans, in particular, that whined about that — just out of control whining about it during the Biden administration. You recall the two big pieces of legislation that President Biden pushed through under reconciliation, the CARES Act and the Inflation Reduction Act. Those were all massive bills that had a lot of reading attached to them, and they were passed relatively quickly once they got agreement on some of the finer points.

"And Republicans argued over and over again that there wasn't enough time to read these bills, let alone process them," recalled Nobles.

Republicans then argued that the bills should go through "regular order" and the various committees for markup.

"So, here they are in a very similar situation, a thousand page bill that has all these different elements attached to it, many of which have nothing to do with each other, but yet are all packed into this one package because Donald Trump wants to get every single agenda item done in one fell swoop, because he doesn't trust the idea of breaking them apart."

He noted that it was something Republicans claimed they weren't going to do anymore.

"And yet we're still doing it in this version of Congress," he closed.

Our Analysis:

Hypocrisy Unveiled: The GOP's Selective Reading Crisis

In a stunning display of political irony, House Republicans find themselves embroiled in a self-inflicted quagmire, one that reeks of hypocrisy and a glaring disregard for the principles they once vociferously championed. NBC News reporter Ryan Nobles sheds light on a situation that can only be described as a comedic tragedy for the GOP, detailing how their prior complaints about unread Democratic legislation have come full circle with their recent blunder regarding President Donald Trump's budget bill.

The Unread Provisions: An Ode to Irony

Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) and Rep. Mike Flood (R-NE) have unwittingly become the poster children for this latest GOP debacle. Greene's admission of ignorance regarding a critical provision in the House budget bill that would strip states of the authority to regulate artificial intelligence for a decade is both shocking and revelatory. Her claim of being "adamantly OPPOSED" to a provision she hadn't known existed until after casting her vote is a stark testament to the dysfunction within the House GOP.

Similarly, Rep. Flood's confession about his unawareness of a provision aimed at protecting Trump and his administration from federal court contempt charges not only adds to the farce but also highlights a disturbing willingness to overlook significant legislative details. The audience's reaction at Flood's town hall meeting – a collective boo – serves as a palpable expression of public disdain for such negligence.

The GOP's Selective Amnesia

What makes this scenario particularly galling is the GOP's historical penchant for decrying the rapid passage of significant Democratic bills, notably during the Biden administration. The CARES Act and the Inflation Reduction Act, both substantial pieces of legislation, were criticized by Republicans for allegedly not providing enough time for thorough review, despite their own rush to pass similarly hefty bills under the Trump administration.

The hypocrisy doesn't end there. The GOP's previous insistence on "regular order" and comprehensive committee markups now seems like a distant memory, a discarded principle in the rush to fulfill Trump's legislative wishlist in one fell swoop. This, as Nobles pointedly remarks, is a stark departure from the transparency and diligence the GOP once claimed to champion.

A Systemic Error in Governance

This episode is more than just a political faux pas; it's a systemic error in governance that undermines the very foundation of legislative responsibility. The failure to read and understand the implications of critical legislation before voting on it is a dereliction of duty that transcends party lines. It's a vivid illustration of the pitfalls of partisan politics, where allegiance to party or individual trumps the commitment to informed decision-making.

The GOP's current predicament is a cautionary tale of the dangers inherent in prioritizing political expedience over principled governance. As they scramble to address the fallout from their legislative oversight, one can't help but wonder if this will serve as a wake-up call or simply be dismissed as another footnote in the annals of political hypocrisy.

In the end, the GOP's selective reading crisis is not just an embarrassment; it's a glaring systemic error that calls into question the integrity of our legislative process. It's a reminder that in the high-stakes game of politics, the consequences of failing to do one's homework can be both profound and far-reaching.
