Elise Stefanik snubbed Trump's pleas not to drop out of the NY governor race: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 25, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Milwaukee, Wisconsin - July 16, 2024: Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-NY) at the Republican National Convention. (Photo credit: Maxim Elramsisy / Shutterstock)

When it became clear that Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-NY) was going to drop out of the race to challenge Democratic New York Gov. Kathy Hochul, allies of President Donald Trump privately reached out begging her to change her mind, but to no avail.

Stefanik, a former moderate Republican lawmaker turned hard MAGA loyalist in the Trump era, announced her campaign to great fanfare and a few widely-mocked stumbles in early November, vowing to defeat Democrats in a staunchly liberal bastion state. But after just a few weeks, she announced she is ending her campaign and retiring from Congress to spend more time with her family.

According to political analyst Rachel Bade, this move triggered chaos behind the scenes.

“I’m told the president and his team always intended to endorse her around the state’s Feb convention, viewing her as the stronger candidate. When she called to say she was bowing out, they made a soft pitch for her to stay in the race, reminding her of the Trump support to come,” Bade wrote on X. However, “it was too late” to get her to change her mind.

With Stefanik out of the race, the only notable Republican candidate is Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman — and according to Bade’s sources, the New York GOP is divided on just how hard to commit to him.

“In upstate New York, some party chairs are fretting that Blakeman doesn’t have the name recognition to turn out the base the way Stefanik could have,” wrote Bade. Furthermore, per a GOP source, “donors are reneging on commitments to contribute millions to turnout efforts because they were set on Stefanik and have questions about Blakeman’s chances. And the bitterness about Stefanik getting a raw deal is dividing the party in a critical midterm year.”

Stefanik’s exit from Congress and suspension of her gubernatorial campaign come amid reports that she is resentful of House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and that his leadership is silencing key voices ahead of a challenging midterm year.

Our Analysis:

The Stefanik Saga: A Symptom of a Fractured GOP

In an almost Shakespearean twist of political fate, Rep. Elise Stefanik’s abrupt departure from the race to challenge Gov. Kathy Hochul has exposed not just the cracks within the New York GOP, but a wider schism threatening the Republican party’s coherence nationwide. Stefanik, once hailed as a moderate Republican, underwent a political metamorphosis, emerging as a hard MAGA loyalist in the Trump era. Her campaign’s commencement was met with both fanfare and derision, an embodiment of the current Republican dichotomy. However, her sudden retreat and the subsequent scramble that ensued amongst Trump’s inner circle highlight a party at war with itself.

The Trump Factor

It’s telling that allies of former President Donald Trump were the ones scurrying to keep Stefanik in the race. This desperation underscores Trump’s lingering influence over the GOP, where his endorsement is still seen as a kingmaker’s touch. The fact that Trump and his team planned to endorse Stefanik only closer to the state’s February convention betrays a strategic miscalculation, perhaps overestimating their sway or underestimating the volatility of political fortunes.

The Blakeman Dilemma

With Stefanik out, Bruce Blakeman’s emergence as the de facto GOP challenger is met with trepidation. The lack of enthusiasm—bordering on outright skepticism—regarding Blakeman’s candidacy from within the party is a glaring red flag. It speaks volumes that in the wake of Stefanik’s withdrawal, the immediate concern isn’t the loss of a potentially strong candidate but the fear of diminished name recognition and donor retreat. This not only illustrates a superficial approach to political campaigns, focusing more on celebrity than substance but also hints at a deeper crisis of confidence within the party.

A Divided House

The mention of Stefanik’s resentment towards House Speaker Mike Johnson and the perceived silencing of key voices under his leadership adds another layer of intrigue. This internal discord, set against the backdrop of a crucial midterm year, could not come at a worse time for the GOP. It’s a manifestation of a party struggling to find its identity post-Trump, torn between its traditional conservative roots and the populist, often divisive rhetoric championed by Trump loyalists.

The Bitter Aftertaste

What’s perhaps most concerning is the “bitterness” within the party over what’s perceived as Stefanik getting a “raw deal.” This victim mentality, where political strategy and personal ambition collide, is symptomatic of a party more concerned with settling scores than addressing the pressing issues facing its constituents.

Conclusion

Stefanik’s saga is more than just a political footnote; it’s a cautionary tale of a party at a crossroads. The GOP’s current predicament—riddled with infighting, a lack of clear direction, and an overreliance on Trump’s waning star power—poses significant challenges as it heads into a critical midterm year. Without a concerted effort to bridge these divides and refocus on substantive policy issues, the Republican Party risks alienating not just its base but the broader electorate. In the end, the saga of Stefanik’s rise and fall may well be remembered as a microcosm of a party grappling with its future, for better or worse.
