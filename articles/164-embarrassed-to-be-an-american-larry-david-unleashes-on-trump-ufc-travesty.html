'Embarrassed to be an American': Larry David unleashes on Trump UFC 'travesty'

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosJune 25, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The White House Turned Into a Fight Card

A Presidency as Stage Prop

The source describes Donald Trump using the White House to host a widely publicized UFC event, prompting Larry David to call it “a travesty” and “embarrassing.” That is the basic event. The political meaning is sharper: the presidency is not being defended as an institution here, it is being converted into branding, spectacle, and personal theater.

Who Holds Power

The only actor in this story with real institutional power is Trump. He is the president. He controls the venue, the symbolism, the access, and the machinery that turns a private celebration into a national image. UFC does not commandeer the White House on its own. Celebrities do not either. The event happened because the office at the top allowed it, wanted it, and used it.

The Real Decision

That matters because the decision is not “people were offended.” The decision was to stage an aggressive entertainment event inside the country’s most loaded political building and to present that as normal. That is deliberate degradation, not accidental tone-deafness. The source tries to frame the reaction as a culture-war dispute between Trump critics, Never Trump conservatives, and comedians. That framing is too soft. It turns an act of institutional vandalism into a debate about taste.

Outrage as Cover

Larry David’s response is blunt, and for once the bluntness is not the point. The point is that elite outrage often stays trapped at the level of personal disgust. It becomes a performance of embarrassment rather than a description of power. Bill Maher’s defense of meeting Trump, the Democratic backlash, the Never Trump metaphor-making, the celebrity commentary all orbit the same center without naming it plainly enough: Trump was not merely being rude. He was using state prestige as a prop for spectacle.

What the Story Hides

The article’s weak spot is that it treats commentary as the event. It makes room for the familiar media comfort zone where political harm gets translated into personality feuds and vibe disputes. That misdirects attention away from the actual institutional issue: a president normalizing the occupation of public power by entertainment logic. When the White House becomes a venue for a fight card, the message is not “look how edgy he is.” The message is that the office itself is available for monetized humiliation.

The Pattern

This is the deeper pattern: authoritarian politics does not always arrive wearing uniforms and issuing decrees. Sometimes it arrives as spectacle, trivialization, and the conversion of civic authority into a fan event. The corruption is not only in what gets done, but in how quickly people are nudged into arguing about tone while the institution itself is being cheapened in plain view.
