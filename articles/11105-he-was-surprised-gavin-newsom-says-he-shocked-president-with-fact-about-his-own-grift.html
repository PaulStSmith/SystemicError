'He was surprised': Gavin Newsom says he shocked president with fact about his own 'grift'

California Gov. Gavin Newsom teased a new plan to troll what he described as President Donald Trump’s “crypto grift” Wednesday following a private meeting he had with the president earlier this year.

“We talked about his crypto grift,” Newsom said of his meeting with Trump, speaking during a Politico summit in Sacramento, California. “He was surprised that the meme coin is not even a coin.”

Trump launched his meme coin, “$TRUMP,” in January of this year, its total value soon exploding to more than $27 billion. In May, Trump held a private dinner at the Trump National Golf Club in Virginia for those with the largest investment in the coin, attended by many hoping to get into Trump’s ear and sway public policy.

Holding 80% of the coin supply, Trump and Trump-linked entities would go on to profit from the coin to the tune of hundreds of millions of dollars, whereas more than 800,000 investors in the coin lost a cumulative $2 billion in what some have described as a traditional ‘pump and dump’ scheme.

Newsom, who has recently launched “The Patriot Shop,” an online storefront selling merchandise mocking Trump’s own online store, said he’d likely be launching his own meme coin, though suggested it would be a novelty item mocking Trump’s “crypto grift,” and not an actual cryptocurrency.

“By the way, I'm about to come out with a coin on my patriot site, so you may want to look into that,” Newsom said. “We're probably adding that, I think we'll add a coin, maybe a Trump corruption coin or something.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Political Satire or a Descent into Petty Showmanship?

In a recent spectacle that feels more like a subplot torn from a satirical novel, California Gov. Gavin Newsom has thrown what can only be described as a political curveball at former President Donald Trump. During a Politico summit in Sacramento, Newsom revealed plans to troll Trump's controversial foray into the world of cryptocurrency, labeling it as a “crypto grift.” This pronouncement comes on the heels of a private meeting between the two, wherein Newsom claims to have broached the subject of Trump's meme coin, “$TRUMP,” which, to the surprise of no one keeping an eye on Trump's ventures, was news to the former president that his coin was not, in fact, tangible currency.

The Spectacle of “$TRUMP”

It's critical to dissect the revelation that Trump's meme coin, launched with fanfare in January, escalated to a valuation of more than $27 billion. This ballooning valuation is not just a number but a testament to the magnetic pull Trump continues to exert on his base and investors, hoping to cash in on the Trump brand. Yet, the grand dinner at the Trump National Golf Club in Virginia—ostensibly a gathering of the coin's most significant investors—paints a disturbing picture of influence and the potential for policy swaying through financial means.

The revelation that Trump and entities linked to him held 80% of the coin supply and profited to the tune of hundreds of millions, while over 800,000 investors faced losses of $2 billion, is nothing short of scandalous. This scenario is emblematic of a classic ‘pump and dump’ scheme, dressed up in the finery of modern cryptocurrency's allure. The brazenness with which this scheme was conducted underlines a troubling disregard for ethical financial practices, leaving a swath of investors in its wake.

Newsom’s Countermove

In what appears to be a retaliatory strike wrapped in mockery, Newsom's announcement of launching a meme coin as a novelty item to mock Trump’s venture is an intriguing development. The proposed “Trump corruption coin” is more than a jab at Trump's cryptocurrency adventure; it's a statement on the broader theme of corruption and the exploitation of political power for personal gain. Yet, one cannot help but question: Is this the high road, or are we witnessing a descent into the mire of petty political showmanship?

Launching “The Patriot Shop” and using it as a platform to sell merchandise that pokes fun at Trump’s merchandise is a bold move, but it also skirts dangerously close to the line dividing clever satire from juvenile taunting. The effectiveness of such tactics in achieving any substantive political discourse is debatable.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Political Discourse

In an era where politics increasingly resembles a reality TV show, the exchange between Newsom and Trump over cryptocurrency ventures is a stark reminder of the theatrics that now dominate the political landscape. While Newsom's initiative to launch a novelty meme coin might score points in the arena of public opinion, especially among his supporters, it also raises questions about the level of discourse we expect from our elected officials.

Are we content with our politicians engaging in tit-for-tat antics, or do we yearn for a return to a more dignified, issue-focused political debate? The Newsom-Trump crypto debacle is a microcosm of the larger, more pervasive issue of how political figures use their platforms and influence. As entertaining as these episodes may be for some, they underscore a systemic error in our political discourse—a shift towards spectacle over substance, where the real losers are not those trading barbs, but the electorate, left to sift through the noise in search of genuine leadership and policy-making.
