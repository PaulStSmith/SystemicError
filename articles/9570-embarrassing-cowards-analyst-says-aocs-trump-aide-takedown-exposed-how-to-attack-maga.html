'Embarrassing cowards’: Analyst says AOC’s Trump aide takedown exposed how to attack MAGA

An analyst on Tuesday revealed the “trick” to Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez‘s (D-NY) mockery of Stephen Miller after the congresswoman called the White House deputy chief of staff “insecure” and warned MAGA followers not to follow the “embarrassing cowards.”

Ocasio-Cortez didn’t mince words last week when she told her Instagram followers to “Laugh at them! Stephen Miller is a clown! I’ve never seen that guy in real life, but he looks like he’s, like, 4′10″.”

An analyst warned that the attack on Miller, who stands 5 feet, 10 inches, could backfire unless it’s done “without making it feel as if elitist liberals and man-hating women are taking aim at all conservative men,” journalist and lawyer Jill Filipovic writes for Slate.

Filipovic argues there is a straightforward way to poke at Miller and shame his followers into reconsidering their support.

“People talk about this toxic masculinity. Let’s put that to the side for just one second—this is about insecure masculinity, and one of the best ways that you can dismantle a movement of insecure men is by making fun of them,” the author writes.

She also emphasizes that Miller deserves ridicule.

“Few people in American politics are as deserving of condemnation and mockery as Miller. He was the impetus behind Donald Trump’s family-separation policy. He has been pushing the outrageous claim that left-leaning groups are ‘domestic terrorist organizations,’ a lie (domestic terrorist organization also is not a legitimate legal designation) that seems to be a pretext for bringing the full force of the federal government down on Trump’s political opponents. It is truly difficult to overstate how authoritarian, dark, and dangerous Miller’s politics are, and how much sway he maintains over the president.”

Filipovic points to fear as a tactic to mobilize MAGA followers, something that opponents can use to their advantage.

“And it seems to stem mostly from the fact that despite actually being closer to 5’10”, he’s a big, scared baby: scared of immigrants, scared of liberals, and begging Big Daddy Trump to send in the troops to protect him. If there were ever a legitimate target for mockery, he’s it,” she writes.

The best tactic to disrupt MAGA’s stronghold on men would be to focus on a key element: manliness.

“And Ocasio-Cortez is right that MAGA is a movement premised on insecure masculinity—and in that, it is like many other authoritarian movements throughout history,” she adds.

“The mockery is merited. And sometimes it works,” she writes. “Revealing men like Miller for who he is—an anxious scaredy-cat dweeb—helps dispel some of the mythology that this administration is trying to create. It also sends a message to men that the leaders of MAGA are not impressive tough guys but embarrassing cowards: men not to emulate but to move away from.”

However, specifically humiliating men — even ones targeting immigrants, children and women — could have repercussions. But there could be a way to approach it.

“That doesn’t mean that liberals have to embrace or court these guys. But it does mean drawing a line between mocking the MAGA men in power and MAGA men writ large. The trick is making MAGA less appealing to men by being honest about how unappealing MAGA male leaders are,” Filipovic writes.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Examination: The Strategy of Mockery in Political Discourse

In the recent article discussing Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s (D-NY) verbal jabs at Stephen Miller, the underpinning argument posits a dangerously simplistic view on handling deeply entrenched political ideologies through mockery. The piece, penned by journalist and lawyer Jill Filipovic for Slate, unpacks Ocasio-Cortez’s strategy while cautioning against potential backlash. However, the analysis itself stumbles into a maze of contradictions and oversimplifications, emblematic of a broader issue within liberal discourse.

The Misguided Missile of Mockery

Firstly, Filipovic’s endorsement of ridicule as a tactic to dismantle MAGA’s stronghold on men is fraught with naivety. To suggest that the key to unraveling a complex web of political and social allegiance is through belittling one’s physical appearance or perceived masculinity is not only superficial but inherently flawed. It ignores the core issues driving individuals towards such movements, which are often rooted in economic disenchantment, cultural alienation, and a profound sense of loss. The assertion that Stephen Miller’s political influence can be diminished by highlighting his “insecure masculinity” is to misunderstand the gravity and appeal of the MAGA ideology to its followers.

The Elitism Trap

Filipovic warns against making the mockery feel as if it’s coming from “elitist liberals and man-hating women,” yet fails to recognize that the mere act of personal ridicule—especially on such superficial grounds—exemplifies this elitism. It’s a tone-deaf approach that likely reinforces rather than diminishes MAGA followers’ disdain for what they perceive as the liberal elite. This strategy not only alienates potential allies but emboldens the opposition by playing directly into their narrative of liberal condescension.

The Oversimplification of Authoritarianism

Linking MAGA’s appeal solely to “insecure masculinity” and suggesting that exposing this insecurity is a silver bullet to disbanding its influence is an oversimplification of authoritarian movements. History is replete with examples where authoritarianism was embraced by societies for reasons far beyond the fragile egos of its male adherents. Economic instability, fear of the other, and a craving for simplistic solutions to complex problems are just a few factors that have fueled such movements. To reduce it to a question of masculinity is to overlook the multifaceted and deep-rooted causes of authoritarianism’s appeal.

A Dangerous Precedent

The article also sets a dangerous precedent by legitimizing personal attacks as a political strategy. While Stephen Miller’s policies may be deserving of condemnation, the path to challenging his and MAGA’s ideology should not be paved with mockery of personal attributes. Such tactics risk descending political discourse into a quagmire of pettiness, diverting attention from substantive policy critiques and debates. Furthermore, it’s a strategy that can easily backfire, as those ridiculed often gain sympathy from onlookers who may see the mockery as unjust or cruel.

Conclusion: The Need for a Deeper Strategy

In conclusion, Filipovic’s article, while attempting to navigate the treacherous waters of political strategy, ends up advocating for a superficial and ultimately counterproductive approach. The fight against authoritarian tendencies within the MAGA movement requires a more profound engagement with the underlying causes of its appeal. It necessitates building bridges rather than burning them, understanding rather than mocking, and engaging in earnest policy debates rather than resorting to ad hominem attacks. The path forward should be grounded in respect, empathy, and the relentless pursuit of truth—qualities conspicuously absent in the strategy of mockery.
