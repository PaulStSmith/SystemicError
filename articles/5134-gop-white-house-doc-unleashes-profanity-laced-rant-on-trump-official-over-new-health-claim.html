GOP White House doc unleashes profanity-laced rant on Trump official over new health claim

U.S. Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. looks on as President Donald Trump (not pictured) makes an announcement about lowering U.S. drug prices, at the White House in Washington, D.C. on Oct. 10, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A doctor who served the George W. Bush administration has called out RFK Jr. for a recent claim on sickness rates in the United States.

The Secretary of Health and Human Services claimed the US has the sickest population on the planet. In an appearance on Theo Von’s podcast, RFK Jr claimed that ultra-processed foods and a lack of disease information are making the country sicker. He posted a two-minute clip of the podcast appearance to his X account.

RFK Jr added in the caption, “Americans didn’t become the sickest people in the world because they’re lazy. We are sick because ultra-processed foods dominate our diets—and because the government misled the public about what drives disease.”

Speaking to Theo Von, the Secretary of Health said, “When my uncle [John F. Kennedy] was president of this country, I was 10, he spent nothing on chronic illness. Zero.

“Today we spend $4.3trillion a year, and it’s about 40 cents out of every tax dollar that is paid by you, to the federal government, that is going towards treatment for chronic disease. It’s unsustainable, and it’s getting worse every year.

“Individuals have a responsibility - when I was a kid, one in five children were obese. Today it’s- overweight is 40%, and adults are even higher. People do not get obese because they’re invalids or lazy, or they don’t want to do exercise. They got that way because they are being mass poisoned.

“They’re being mass poisoned because the government lied to them. And it lied about the food. Now, 70% of the food that our kids eat is ultra-processed food, and it’s no coincidence. It’s just poison.”

Jonathan Reiner, the former cardiologist to Vice President Dick Cheney, disagreed with RFK Jr’s claim that America had become the sickest country on the planet.

He wrote on X, “Americans are not the sickest people in the world. There’s a lot we can do to improve the health of our population, including eating better (and vaccinating our kids) but to call Americans the sickest people in the world, is just bulls---.”

Our Analysis:

RFK Jr’s Blistering Critique on America’s Health: A Deep Dive

In a recent eruption of controversy, RFK Jr. has thrown a grenade into the public discourse with claims that might sound hyperbolic to the untrained ear. However, a closer examination of his statements, juxtaposed against the rebuttal from Jonathan Reiner, the doctor who served the George W. Bush administration, reveals a complex tapestry of truth, hyperbole, and systemic failure. Let’s dissect the layers of this argument, shall we?

The Core of RFK Jr’s Claim

RFK Jr. posits that the United States harbors the “sickest population on the planet,” attributing this dismal badge of honor to the proliferation of ultra-processed foods and a government that has ostensibly misled its populace about what drives disease. The statistics he provides are staggering: a leap from virtually no expenditure on chronic illness during JFK’s era to a current expenditure of $4.3 trillion annually, accounting for 40 cents of every tax dollar. The transition from 20% of children being overweight to a current 40%, with adults faring even worse, paints a grim picture of a nation in the throes of a health crisis.

Jonathan Reiner’s Counterpoint

Enter Jonathan Reiner, who counters RFK Jr.’s claims, dismissing the notion that Americans are the sickest globally as “bulls---.” Reiner concedes that there’s much room for improvement in American health, advocating for better dietary habits and vaccination. His critique, however, lacks the granularity needed to effectively dismantle RFK Jr.’s argument.

Analysis: Between Hyperbole and Harsh Reality

1. The “Sickest” Claim: Calling Americans the “sickest” could be seen as a hyperbolic statement, dependent on the metrics used. While it’s true that the U.S. leads in certain negative health outcomes, such as obesity rates and chronic diseases, it’s also more complex than a blanket label can convey.

2. The Role of Ultra-Processed Foods: RFK Jr. is spot-on in highlighting the dangers of ultra-processed foods. Numerous studies have linked these dietary choices to a host of health problems, from obesity to heart disease. The critique of the government’s role in misleading the public, whether through action or inaction, is a poignant commentary on the failure to protect public health.

3. Government Expenditure on Chronic Illness: The shift from zero expenditure on chronic illness during JFK’s presidency to a staggering $4.3 trillion is indicative of a healthcare system that has become heavily reactive rather than proactive. This isn’t just unsustainable; it’s a ticking time bomb.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Discourse

While Reiner’s dismissal of RFK Jr.’s claim aims for a moderating perspective, it falls short of addressing the systemic issues at play. To write off RFK Jr.’s comments as mere hyperbole is to ignore the substantive critique embedded within: a healthcare system in crisis, a diet that’s killing more Americans than any foreign adversary ever could, and a government that appears, at best, complacent.

The truth, as is often the case, lies somewhere in the middle. America might not be the “sickest” by every measure, but it’s undeniably ailing. The debate shouldn’t pivot on whether RFK Jr.’s claim is an exaggeration, but on how we can collectively address the very real issues he raises. It’s time for a more nuanced discourse that transcends dismissive rebuttals and confronts the uncomfortable reality of America’s health crisis head-on.
