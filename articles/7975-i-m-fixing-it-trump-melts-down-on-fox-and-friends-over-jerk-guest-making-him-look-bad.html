'I'm fixing it!' Trump melts down on Fox and Friends over 'jerk' guest making him look bad

Donald Trump started off his Saturday morning by flipping out on the hosts and producers of Fox and Friends for booking a guest who pushed back at the president’s insistence that he has turned the economy around.

The president, who stayed in snowy Washington, DC, this weekend, took to his Truth Social account to complain about the appearance of Peter Schiff, a financial commentator and radio personality who has been raising alarms about America’s affordability crisis.

That led Trump, who has been calling the concept of affordability a “con job” by Democrats, to snarl about letting Schiff make his case.

“Why would Fox and Friends Weekend (of all things?) put on a ‘Stockbroker’ named Peter Schiff, a Trump hating loser who has already proven to be wrong. Either the show made a mistake, or it is heading in a different direction,” he wrote.

He continued, “He thinks prices are going up when, in fact, they are coming substantially down. Gasoline hit $1.99 a gallon yesterday, in certain states, and is down BIG since Biden. Other prices are almost all down. Biden caused the AFFORDABILITY CRISIS, I’M FIXING IT, along with everything else! Much of it, like the Border, is already fixed.”

“Check out the ‘booker’ who put this jerk on!” he demanded.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: Trump’s Latest Media Meltdown

In a predictable yet audacious display of diversion, Donald Trump has once again commandeered the headlines with a tirade directed at Fox and Friends and their decision to feature Peter Schiff, a financial commentator known for his critical stance on America’s economic trajectory. This knee-jerk reaction from Trump is a textbook example of his incessant need to control the narrative, especially when it veers away from his self-aggrandizing tales of economic revival.

Trump’s Misdirection and Misinformation

First off, Trump’s assertion that he has “fixed” the economy and the border is, at best, a gross oversimplification, if not an outright falsehood. His claim that gasoline prices have plummeted to “$1.99 a gallon” in some states is a misleading statistic without context. Prices fluctuate due to a complex web of factors including global oil prices, geopolitical tensions, and domestic policy decisions. To credit or blame any single administration for short-term price movements is to ignore the fundamentals of how energy markets operate.

Furthermore, Trump’s attempt to pin the so-called “AFFORDABILITY CRISIS” on Biden while simultaneously claiming he, Trump, is the fixer of all things economic is a bold exercise in historical revisionism. The affordability issues facing Americans — from healthcare to housing to higher education — are systemic, deeply rooted in decades of policy decisions, economic shifts, and, more recently, the global pandemic’s repercussions.

The Irony of Attacking Schiff

The choice to attack Peter Schiff, branding him a “Trump hating loser who has already proven to be wrong,” is particularly ironic. Schiff, a commentator with a libertarian bent, has been a consistent critic of government policies that he views as detrimental to the economy’s long-term health, irrespective of the administration in charge. This includes criticism of policies during Trump’s tenure. To dismiss Schiff’s concerns about affordability as a “con job” by Democrats not only undermines legitimate economic anxieties felt by millions but also exemplifies Trump’s aversion to any form of criticism.

Fox and Friends: A Scapegoat?

By lashing out at Fox and Friends, a show that has often been criticized for its uncritical coverage of his presidency, Trump is signaling his intolerance for even the slightest deviation from total loyalty. This episode raises questions about the role of media in holding power to account. When a show known for its favorable view of Trump decides to feature a dissenting opinion, it’s met with public rebuke from the man himself. This is telling of the echo chamber Trump wishes to maintain, where only his version of reality is acknowledged.

The Real Loser Here

To demand the outing of the “booker” who decided to feature Schiff on the program is to encourage a witch hunt against those who dare to present a view that contradicts Trump’s narrative. It’s an attempt to stifle free speech and punish dissent, a tactic that Trump has employed time and again.

In this latest outburst, Trump reveals not just his fragile ego but a dangerous propensity to undermine the very principles of democratic discourse. His unwillingness to engage with or even acknowledge differing viewpoints lays bare a leadership style that is antithetical to the pluralistic values upon which America prides itself.

In the end, the real loser isn’t Peter Schiff or the producers of Fox and Friends. It’s the American public, who are subjected to this ongoing spectacle of diversion and misinformation, all while the substantive issues facing the country — the very “affordability crisis” Trump so readily dismisses — continue to loom large, awaiting genuine solutions rather than scapegoats.
