'Are you listening?' Heated clash on CNN as Republican tries to deny hearing outrage

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A heated CNN anchor gave fierce pushback on Tuesday afternoon to a Republican lawmaker who tried to deny hearing pleas from farmers who said they’re struggling to make ends meet.

CNN’s Brianna Keilar got in a fiery back-and-forth with Rep. Mark Alford (R-MO) after she asked him during a live broadcast about what farmers are saying. Alford claimed that the Trump administration was working to give them more relief when the conversation got heated.

“We are giving the farmers their safety nets through 80% of the farm bill that was passed through the ‘One Big, Beautiful Bill,’” Alford said. “Those safety nets are there an increase in reference prices. We are helping the American farmers, Donald J. Trump knows the importance of feeding America and the world, and we’re gonna get it done.”

Keilar didn’t hold back in response.

“But that’s not what farmers are telling you, right? I know that you’ve heard from them,” she shot back. “They say they’re hurting from tariffs. I know you had a town hall this summer, KMIZ reported about one farmer telling you it’s not just that China was retaliating, they’re not buying soybeans, even though that’s back now, but tariffs actually drove up prices of fertilizer and other farm goods. So, I mean, of this idea of loans, but when you’re paying more for goods that you paid a certain price for before, and your margins are pretty slim. Do you think the administration is understanding this? Do you think they’re getting that message?”

Alford attempted to blame former President Joe Biden for farmers’ current woes.

“They do, and they understand as I do, that it was Joe Biden that drove up the price of fertilizer and fuel with his war,” he said.

“Well, no, that’s not what this farmer told you. So what do you say to him?” Keilar said, pushing back again.

“Well, that’s what I’m telling you. I’m telling you right now,” Alford said.

“But that’s not what this farmer told you, and he’s the farmer who’s telling you... excuse me. This farmer you heard him. He said this to you,” she said.

“Yes, we need to look at fertilizer prices. Fuel has come down and will continue to come down thanks to Donald Trump,” Alford said.

But Keilar still wasn’t satisfied with Alford’s response and pressed further.

“Are you listening to your constituents? Because he was very clear about why his prices were going up,” she said.

“Brianna, I did 15 stops on a town hall tour, one of the few Congress members on the Republican side and the Democrat side to actually do that. Yes ma’am. I listened,” he said. “I took 256 questions and sat there and listened. Listened to insults, listened to yelling, listened to name-calling, and answered when they wanted to be respectful. I remember that farmer. I understand his concern, but the large number of farmers in Missouri believe in Donald J. Trump and that he’s going to get things done. We are doing that with beef prices.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Clash: CNN Anchor vs. Rep. Mark Alford

In a heated exchange that epitomized the current state of political discourse, CNN’s Brianna Keilar and Rep. Mark Alford (R-MO) provided a stark illustration of the chasm between political narratives and the stark realities faced by American farmers. The conversation, ostensibly about agricultural policy and its impacts, quickly devolved into a blame game, highlighting not only the struggles of the farming community but also the broader issue of political accountability.

The Claims and Counterclaims: A Closer Look

Rep. Alford’s assertion that the Trump administration has been a benefactor to farmers through the “One Big, Beautiful Bill” is a classic example of political grandstanding. Alford’s claims about safety nets and increased reference prices conveniently omit the broader context of the situation, notably the adverse effects of tariffs on the agricultural sector. His statement, “Donald J. Trump knows the importance of feeding America and the world, and we’re gonna get it done,” is a bold claim that not only ignores the complexity of global trade but also the very real consequences of these policies.

Keilar’s pushback was not just necessary; it was essential. Her reference to the tangible hardships faced by farmers—increased costs due to tariffs, specifically mentioning fertilizers and other farm goods—paints a much more accurate picture of the agricultural landscape. Farmers operate on razor-thin margins, and policies that increase costs without providing commensurate support can be disastrous. Keilar’s insistence on reflecting the farmers’ voices, rather than political talking points, is commendable.

The Blame Game: Biden vs. Trump

Alford’s attempt to shift blame to former President Joe Biden for the plight of farmers is a redirection that fails to engage with the substance of Keilar’s inquiries. The claim that Biden’s policies are responsible for the increase in fertilizer and fuel prices is a simplification that does not account for the global complexities that influence these costs, including but not limited to trade policies, global demand, and geopolitical tensions.

Moreover, Alford’s response that “fuel has come down and will continue to come down thanks to Donald Trump” is a statement that seems disconnected from the realities of global oil markets and the myriad factors that influence fuel prices. It’s a claim that feels more rooted in political loyalty than in the economic realities facing farmers.

Listening vs. Hearing: A Key Distinction

Rep. Alford’s claim of listening to his constituents—boasting about the number of town hall stops and questions taken—raises an important distinction between listening and hearing. Listening implies an act of passive reception, whereas hearing suggests an active process of understanding and internalizing the concerns presented. The congressman’s defensive posture and redirection suggest a preference for political narratives over the genuine grievances of his constituents.

Conclusion: The Realities of Political Discourse

This exchange between Keilar and Alford is emblematic of the wider issue of political discourse in America. It’s a discourse where talking points often overshadow truths, where the struggles of the many are drowned out by the narratives of the few, and where accountability often takes a backseat to allegiance. The farmers’ plight is real, and it deserves more than to be used as a pawn in political gamesmanship. It requires serious, thoughtful policy discussions that go beyond the surface and tackle the root causes of their hardships.
