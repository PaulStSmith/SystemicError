Trump biographer exposes the 'demented' fantasy driving his endless greed

The Presidency as a Private Revenue Stream

The Real Holder of Power

Donald Trump is not merely surrounding himself with grift. He holds the office that can authorize federal funding, shape foreign policy, and normalize which private interests get a federal stamp of legitimacy. That is the power center here. His sons may be the visible beneficiaries, but the institutional advantage comes from the presidency itself.

The Deal Is the Point

The source details a tungsten project in Kazakhstan tied to a company partially owned by Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump, with as much as $1.6 billion in federal funding attached. That is not some accidental overlap between business and politics. It is the deliberate use of public authority to subsidize a family venture in a strategic mineral sector tied to military projects.

Who Enabled It

The enablers are the people and institutions treating this as merely another Trump story. Michael Wolff’s description of Trump’s psychology is useful only up to a point. The deeper problem is not a private fantasy of revenge. It is an executive system that continues to function while the president and his family convert policy leverage into cash.

The Misdirection

The weakest framing in the source is the suggestion that this is mainly about Trump’s mind: greed, revenge, grievance, ego. Those motives matter, but they can become a distraction if they pull attention away from the machinery. The machinery is what matters: foreign policy blurred into private enterprise, federal financing attached to family ownership, and a political class that keeps calling open corruption “a stunning windfall.”

Foreign Policy for Sale

The New York Times disclosure described a $2.2 billion windfall in 2025, including money tied to a UAE-affiliated wealth fund taking a major stake in World Liberty Financial, plus Trump name licensing in Saudi Arabia and Qatar. That is not incidental commerce around the edges of diplomacy. It is diplomacy itself being monetized. When countries central to U.S. foreign policy are also buyers of the Trump brand, the line has not blurred. It has been priced.

The Systemic Pattern

This is what oligarchic politics looks like when it stops pretending to be embarrassed. The message is simple: access to state power can be converted into family wealth, and the cost is absorbed by the public, which is asked to interpret extraction as personality, corruption as savvy, and state capture as just another day in Washington.
