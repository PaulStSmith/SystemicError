'Red flag to GOP': Irate Republicans reportedly ready to break with Trump to end shutdown

The president called on Senate Republicans to “terminate the filibuster” to pass a continuing resolution to end the record 37-day shutdown, after the GOP suffered its worst electoral walloping in years, but they made clear to reporters they planned to ignore his demand, reported Politico.

“One GOP senator, Mike Rounds of South Dakota, laughed out loud when asked about the anti-filibuster push,” Politico reported. “Welcome to the dawn of Trump’s lame duck era.”

The president remains overwhelmingly popular with Republican voters and remains the party’s most dominant force, but Tuesday’s electoral blowout was, in the words of a retiring congressman, “a red flag to the GOP” about the looming post-Trump era.

“He has zero ability to work across the aisle,” said Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE), who is not seeking re-election next year. “He needs to face reality and learn how to talk to Democrats he can reason with.”

Many Republicans expressed frustration that Trump’s demand to end the filibuster would backfire once Democrats regain the majority, and the prospect excited Democratic senators.

“Last night’s results look like a recipe for them to lose the House and the Senate next fall,” said Sen. Chris Murphy (D-T), “and they’re going to hand us a 50-vote majority gift-wrapped when we show up Day 1?”

Some Republicans are getting ready to defy the president if he won’t allow compromise to get a government funding bill passed because they know that he doesn’t drive up turnout when he not on the ballot – and he won’t be again, despite his musings about running for an unconstitutional third term.

“Many are privately signaling they’re prepared to break with Trump if he doesn’t allow Republicans to negotiate on an extension of the Obamacare insurance subsidies Democrats are demanding,” Politico reported. “Others blamed the president and his top budget aide, Russ Vought, for favoring hardball moves such as canceling blue-state transportation projects and firing federal employees that only served to cause Democrats to dig in further.”

“One irate senior House Republican granted anonymity to speak candidly blamed Trump and Vought for spurring the shutdown with their unprecedented move to unilaterally rescind congressional funding over the summer through a so-called pocket rescission,” the report added. “’That decision is why we’re in this mess,’ the Republican said.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of GOP’s Fracturing Allegiance to Trump Amid Shutdown Crisis

In a recent Politico report, the cracks within the Republican Party regarding its allegiance to President Donald Trump have become glaringly apparent. The article outlines how, in the wake of a 37-day government shutdown and a significant electoral defeat, GOP senators are privately indicating a readiness to break ranks with Trump. This situation illuminates the broader tensions within the party, as well as Trump’s waning influence over his GOP colleagues.

The End of Unquestioned Loyalty?

Trump’s call to “terminate the filibuster” to pass a continuing resolution and end the shutdown was met with outright dismissal and, in the case of GOP Senator Mike Rounds of South Dakota, laughter. This reaction is not just a mere political disagreement but a clear sign of Trump’s diminishing authority within his own party. Rounds’ laughter could be interpreted as a symbol of the GOP’s growing impatience with Trump’s often controversial and unilateral directives.

Electoral Defeat: A Red Flag

The article emphasizes the GOP’s recent electoral losses as a “red flag” and a harbinger of the post-Trump era. Notably, Rep. Don Bacon’s critique of Trump’s inability to work across the aisle underscores a fundamental flaw in Trump’s governance style. It suggests a recognition within the party that Trump’s approach may not be sustainable or beneficial in the long run, especially as the party contemplates its future beyond his presidency.

Fear of Filibuster Backfire

The GOP’s hesitation to end the filibuster, despite Trump’s insistence, is rooted in a strategic concern that such a move would backfire once Democrats regain the majority. This sentiment, shared among Republicans, reflects a more pragmatic approach to governance, contrasting with Trump’s often impulsive and confrontational style. The worry about handing Democrats a “50-vote majority gift-wrapped” speaks volumes about the GOP’s attempt to think several steps ahead, beyond the immediacy of Trump’s demands.

Resistance to Trump’s Hardball Moves

Republicans’ preparations to defy Trump, particularly on the issue of Obamacare insurance subsidies, indicate a significant shift. The willingness to negotiate with Democrats, even in opposition to Trump’s stance, suggests that GOP senators are prioritizing governance and the needs of their constituents over loyalty to Trump. Furthermore, the criticism of Trump and his top budget aide, Russ Vought, for favoring aggressive tactics like canceling blue-state transportation projects, highlights a growing disenchantment with Trump’s divisive strategies.

The Shutdown’s Catalyst

The revelation that Trump’s unilateral decision to rescind congressional funding through a “pocket rescission” was a primary catalyst for the shutdown is particularly damning. It not only underscores the recklessness of such a move but also places the blame squarely on Trump’s shoulders for the ensuing chaos. This acknowledgment from a senior House Republican, albeit anonymously, is a stark admission of Trump’s role in precipitating the government shutdown.

Conclusion

The Politico report paints a picture of a Republican Party at a crossroads, with members increasingly willing to distance themselves from Trump’s more controversial decisions and directives. This readiness to defy the president, especially in the context of a prolonged government shutdown and electoral setbacks, signals a potential shift in the GOP’s internal dynamics. Whether this marks the beginning of a significant realignment within the party or merely a temporary divergence remains to be seen. However, what is clear is that Trump’s grip on the GOP is not as ironclad as it once was, posing significant implications for his influence and the party’s direction moving forward.
