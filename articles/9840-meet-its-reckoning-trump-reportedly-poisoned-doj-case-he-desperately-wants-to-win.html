Former federal prosecutor Shan Wu weighed in on the Trump DOJ’s prosecution of former FBI director James Comey. Trump’s own decision to chime in on the case may have already doomed it, according to the attorney.

In an article called Trump’s Witch Hunt Is About to Meet Its Reckoning: Lawyer, the Daily Beast reports that Wu “says Trump’s public attacks on James Comey may have already poisoned the case against the former FBI director.”

“Donald Trump’s multiple public statements demanding the prosecution of former FBI Director James Comey delivered Comey’s defense team arguments to get the case thrown out,” the article states. “Former federal prosecutor and Daily Beast guest columnist Shan Wu told The Daily Beast Podcast that the president’s public lust to prosecute Comey—which includes a Truth Social post addressed to Attorney General Pam Bondi demanding she prosecute the 64-year-old former FBI director—has poisoned his chances of securing the conviction he desperately wants.”

The piece quotes Wu as saying, “The main argument Comey’s people are probably going to make is selective prosecution, meaning he was picked out because he opposes Trump... Probably not going to go all way to the [Supreme Court]. Usually that would be heard at the district court level, the trial judge. And the judge rules one way or the other, and it’s probably not going to keep going at this point.”

According to the report, “Wu warned that even if the judge in the case, Biden-appointed Michael Nachmanoff, did not throw the case out on the basis of selective or vindictive prosecution, the case would also need to clear another legal hurdle to even make it to trial: finding an unbiased jury.”

Wu added, “There’s no way to find a place where people haven’t heard about this case... And the problem here with that publicity...”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Self-Inflicted Legal Quagmire: The Comey Prosecution Fiasco

In a twist that reads more like a cautionary tale than a chapter in legal strategy, Donald Trump seemingly sabotages his own Department of Justice’s (DOJ) efforts to convict former FBI Director James Comey. This narrative, as outlined in a report featuring former federal prosecutor Shan Wu, showcases a blend of irony and recklessness that could only emerge from the Trump legal playbook.

The Poison Pill: Trump’s Public Outbursts

Trump’s inability to refrain from public commentary on ongoing legal matters has once again reared its head, this time potentially derailing a prosecution he “desperately” desires. According to Wu, Trump’s barrage of public attacks and demands for Comey’s prosecution—culminating in a Truth Social post aimed at Attorney General Pam Bondi—have not only undermined the case but have handed Comey’s defense a potential silver bullet: the argument of selective prosecution.

Selective prosecution, a defense that posits Comey was targeted for his opposition to Trump rather than any legitimate legal failings, could see the case dismissed before it even warms the bench. Wu’s analysis suggests that while this argument might not climb the judicial ladder to the Supreme Court, it poses a significant enough threat at the district court level to potentially stop the prosecution in its tracks.

The Unbiased Jury Conundrum

Even if the case against Comey skirts past the selective prosecution hurdle, Wu highlights another formidable barrier: the quest for an unbiased jury. Trump’s relentless publicity of his vendetta against Comey has so saturated the public sphere that assembling a jury untainted by preconceived notions seems a Herculean task. This scenario begs the question: where can one find jurors untouched by the omnipresent Trump-Comey saga?

Legal Strategy or Political Theater?

What emerges from this debacle is a picture of a prosecution mired not in legal substance but in personal vendetta and political spectacle. Trump’s interventions paint a picture not of a calculated legal maneuver but of a self-sabotaging quest for revenge. The implications of such actions extend beyond the immediate legal outcomes; they erode public trust in the impartiality of the justice system and underscore the potential dangers of politicizing prosecutorial power.

In Conclusion: A Systemic Error

This episode stands as a stark illustration of the systemic errors plaguing Trump’s approach to legal and governmental processes. The pursuit of James Comey, fueled by personal antipathy rather than grounded in solid legal reasoning, exemplifies the chaotic intersection of law and politics that has characterized much of Trump’s modus operandi.

The case against Comey, as it unfolds, will undoubtedly serve as a litmus test for the integrity of the legal system in the face of political pressure. However, the lasting impact of Trump’s actions—undermining the very case he champions through his inability to separate personal vendettas from presidential duty—reveals a deeper systemic flaw: the peril of allowing the whims of political animosity to dictate the course of justice.
