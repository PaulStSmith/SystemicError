Our Analysis:

Decoding Trump's Latest Online Escapade

In a move that's as predictable as it is perplexing, Donald Trump has once again taken to social media to voice his unsolicited advice on international trade relations, this time targeting China. His chosen platform? Truth Social, of course, where he can safely preach to the choir without the inconvenience of fact-checking or dissent. In an early Friday morning post written in his trademark all-caps style (because subtlety is for the weak, right?), Trump declared, "CHINA SHOULD OPEN UP ITS MARKET TO USA — WOULD BE SO GOOD FOR THEM!!!" followed by an equally subtle jab, "CLOSED MARKETS DON’T WORK ANYMORE!!!"

The Context? Good Luck Finding It

Trump's latest foray into the complex world of international economics comes without a shred of context. This isn't surprising; context requires a depth of understanding and nuance that seems to evade the former president. China, as Trump correctly identifies, remains a "noticeable sticking point" in global trade discussions. However, the notion that China, or any nation for that matter, would alter its trade policy in response to a social media post is as laughable as it is ludicrous.

A Follow-Up Post: The 80% Tariff

Not content with just one post, Trump followed up with another gem, suggesting an "80% Tariff on China seems right! Up to Scott B.," presumably referring to Scott Bessent, a key player in global investment circles. The suggestion of an 80% tariff is, to put it mildly, extreme. It demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of how tariffs work and whom they affect. Tariffs are not a one-way street where only the targeted country feels the pain; American consumers and businesses would bear the brunt of such a policy through higher prices and disrupted supply chains.

The Reality of Closed Markets

Trump's proclamation that "CLOSED MARKETS DON’T WORK ANYMORE!!!" is a fascinating insight into his view of global economics. The irony, of course, is that his administration was known for its protectionist policies, including tariffs on steel and aluminum, that moved the U.S. closer to the 'closed market' model he now criticizes. The reality is that global trade is a complex, interconnected web of relationships that require careful negotiation, not bluster and all-caps demands on social media.

The Bottom Line

Trump's posts are a stark reminder of the dangers of conducting diplomacy via social media, especially when done so recklessly. The suggestion of an 80% tariff on China is not only economically nonsensical but could have serious implications for global trade relations and the U.S. economy. It's a classic example of Trump's approach to policy: shoot first, aim later (if at all).

What's truly concerning is not just the content of these posts but the mindset they reveal—a mindset that views complex international relationships as simple transactions that can be bullied into submission. This approach to diplomacy is not only ineffective but dangerous. It's a reminder of the need for informed, nuanced, and responsible leadership in navigating the intricate dance of global trade and politics.
