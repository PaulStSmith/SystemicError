'Not helpful!' Republicans worried by Trump's off-script 'ostrich' affordability routine

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he arrives to deliver remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania, U.S. December 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst/File Photo

President Donald Trump made the first stop this week in a planned nationwide tour to reassure Americans about the cost of living, but his off-script message concerned other Republicans.

The president’s high-profile economic speech Tuesday in Pennsylvania was meant to cheer up voters and reset his economic message, but instead his meandering, 90-minute remarks deepened GOP fears that his inability to connect on the issue would send the party for a midterm election wipeout, reported CNN.

“The first thing in terms of recovery is acknowledging there’s a problem,” said GOP strategist Matthew Bartlett, a first-term Trump official. “Being an ostrich in politics and putting your head in the sand is never a good strategy, much less a winning strategy.”

Trump insisted the economy is booming – and gave himself an “A-plus-plus-plus-plus-plus” grade on the issue in an interview – and claimed prices are falling, and he blamed voter anxiety is a Democratic “hoax” and also encouraged Americans to accept living with less.

“Telling people they don’t know what’s going on in their lives is a mistake, and that won’t work,” said Douglas Holtz-Eakin, president of the right-leaning American Action Forum. “But he’s not going to change his tune, and his tune is not helpful at the moment.”

Trump’s support on economic issues has sharply declined in polling and Democrats have outperformed their 2024 results over and over in elections this year, so Republicans are scrambling to revamp their affordability message as the White House preaches patience.

“President Trump is stamping out Biden inflation for good, and we fully expect it to roll down next year,” said Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent ahead of Trump’s speech. “Where Biden created scarcity, President Trump is creating abundance.”

Trump made brief allusions to the White House’s new message on affordability, acknowledging that prices “were” high at one point but declared “we’re bringing them down,” but that’s not enough for Republicans facing re-election next year.

“A lot of people are still having trouble making ends meet,” said Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-WV).

MAGA Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS) defended the president’s speech, saying Trump was forced to comment on an issue amplified by the media, but he declined to endorse his view on the economy.

“He’s pushing back the other way,” Marshall said.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Economic Delusion: A Masterclass in Misdirection

In an era where political rhetoric often teeters on the edge of reality and fiction, President Donald Trump’s latest escapades on his nationwide tour have set a new standard for disconnection. His recent speech in Pennsylvania, intended as a high-octane boost to reassure Americans about the cost of living, morphed into a bewildering 90-minute ramble that has left even his Republican allies in a state of alarm. This isn’t just a case of going off-script; it’s a deep dive into a political twilight zone where self-assurance trumps reality.

Trump’s Economic Grade: A Fantasy in “A-plus-plus-plus-plus-plus”

Let’s start with Trump’s self-assessment, an “A-plus-plus-plus-plus-plus” grade on the economy. In a world where facts matter, this grade inflates not just the truth but the very parameters of grading. The audacity to claim such excellence in the face of economic turmoil is not just misleading—it’s a slap in the face to Americans grappling with the realities of inflation, job insecurity, and the cost-of-living crisis.

The Blame Game and the “Democratic Hoax”

Trump’s insistence that the economy is booming and his claim that prices are falling are not just inaccuracies; they’re dangerous delusions. Blaming voter anxiety on a “Democratic hoax” is a masterclass in misdirection, a tactic aimed at shifting responsibility rather than addressing the root causes of economic discontent. This isn’t leadership; it’s a dereliction of duty, masked under the guise of political strategy.

The GOP’s Economic Quagmire

The response from the GOP highlights a party at an impasse. Figures like Matthew Bartlett and Douglas Holtz-Eakin offer a glimpse into the internal strife brewing within the party, recognizing the folly in Trump’s approach yet seemingly powerless to alter its course. The fear of a midterm election “wipeout” is palpable, yet the strategy to combat this potential disaster remains elusive.

The White House’s Affordability Mirage

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s pre-speech optimism, claiming Trump is “stamping out Biden inflation for good,” adds another layer of surrealism to the narrative. The promise of creating abundance in place of scarcity sounds like a campaign slogan detached from the harsh economic realities many Americans face daily. This isn’t just wishful thinking; it’s a calculated attempt to paint a rosy picture where the thorns are all too real.

The Bottom Line

Trump’s economic tour and his handling of the economic narrative are emblematic of a broader issue in American politics: the chasm between political rhetoric and the lived experiences of the electorate. This isn’t just about mismanagement or a series of gaffes; it’s a systemic failure to address the genuine concerns of Americans struggling to make ends meet.

As Republicans scramble to recalibrate their message on affordability, the question remains: Can they distance themselves from Trump’s delusions without alienating the base that hangs on his every word? The clock is ticking, and with each passing moment, the GOP’s tightrope walk becomes increasingly perilous.

In the end, Trump’s economic delusions and the GOP’s struggle to address them reflect not just a political dilemma but a profound disconnect with the American people. As the rhetoric ramps up and the 2024 elections loom on the horizon, one thing is clear: The cost of living in a reality fashioned by political convenience is one that many Americans can ill afford.
