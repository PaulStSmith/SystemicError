Trump’s catastrophes skyrocket as he blunders into another hole: report

Trump’s Aluminum Tantrum Is Industrial Self-Sabotage

A Manufactured Crisis

The story here is simple: Trump slapped tariffs on Canadian aluminum, and the result is not strength but avoidable damage. Canada supplies most of the imported aluminum the U.S. actually uses, because it can smelt it with cheaper, more reliable hydropower. The metal feeds American auto, furniture, and beverage production. This is not a rival gaming the system; it is an integrated supply chain being kicked by a president who mistakes disruption for leverage.

Where Power Actually Sits

The institutional power is not with the companies buying aluminum, the workers depending on stable inputs, or the consumers who will eat higher prices. It sits with the White House, which can impose tariffs by decree and then pretend the consequences are someone else’s problem. Trump made the decision. Trump owns the fallout. The administration cannot claim surprise when a policy designed in political theater lands on factories, supply contracts, and pricing.

The Blame Shuffle

The weak framing in stories like this is the suggestion that trade conflict is some kind of abstract economic weather. It is not. Trump is not reacting to a Canadian “advantage” in any morally meaningful sense. He is punishing a country for having the electricity and infrastructure to produce something the U.S. has let become too expensive to make at scale. That is not unfair competition. That is industrial policy failure being monetized as nationalism.

Costs Passed Down the Chain

The source gives the bill in plain numbers: the auto industry has already paid more than $35 billion since 2025 on tariffs across aluminum, steel, car parts, and related goods. That money is not vanishing into a vacuum. It is money that could have gone to research, investment, or lower car prices. Instead, it is being siphoned off by a policy regime that treats American firms as hostages and calls the ransom patriotism.

The Real Constraint Is Infrastructure

Trump’s tariffs also collide with a more serious problem he is doing nothing to solve: the U.S. electric grid is in bad shape, electricity rates are rising, and the administration is discouraging new power sources like renewables. That matters because aluminum is, as the source puts it, electricity in solid form. You cannot bully physics. You cannot tariff your way around the fact that the U.S. has underbuilt the capacity needed to compete.

The Pattern

This is the larger pattern: executive power used as spectacle, economic interdependence recast as betrayal, and long-term industrial planning replaced by impulsive coercion. The damage lands on domestic industry first, then on workers and consumers, while the political message is marketed upward as toughness. That is the logic of authoritarian-adjacent governance in economic form: make the country pay for the leader’s need to dominate the news cycle, then call the wreckage a win.
