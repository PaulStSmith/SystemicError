Trump appears to forget ocean's name live on TV: 'A thing'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Depths of Trump's "Oceanic" Confusion

U.S. President Donald Trump smiles. REUTERS/Umit Bektas

In a call with Fox & Friends on Tuesday, President Donald Trump discussed at length his ongoing efforts to end the war in Ukraine and then appeared to forget the name of a major body of water.

Trump was asked by Fox News’ Lawrence Jones about the several European leaders who attended Trump’s meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy Monday, and whether they had said anything to Trump in private.

“They're very good people, they're very good leaders of their countries and they want to get back to leading their countries,” Trump said of the European leaders, which included French President Emmanuel Macron and UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer, among others.

“They're consumed far more with this than we are because they're right there. You know we have an ocean that’s separating us, right? A thing called… an ocean. A big, beautiful ocean. And, uh, they don't, they're right there. So it's a different kind of a thing for them.”

Russia's east coast and the state of Alaska are separated by the Bering Strait, which is part of the Pacific Ocean. The Pacific separates it from the U.S. mainland. Trump may also have been referring to the Atlantic Ocean, which separates Europe and the U.S.

Trump’s apparent mishap went unnoticed by the Fox & Friends hosts, who attempted to interject with questions twice, unsuccessfully, as Trump continued speaking on the United States’ relationship with Ukraine.

Now the oldest American president to have ever held office, Trump, at 79 years old, has raised questions from medical experts over his potential “mental decline,” largely stemming from his increasingly “incoherent” remarks at rallies or during interviews. Trump has fiercely rejected such claims, claiming to have “aced” a cognitive test back in 2024.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Depths of Trump's "Oceanic" Confusion

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the realm of what is quickly becoming political satire, former President Donald Trump engaged in a telephonic dance with the hosts of Fox & Friends. During this call, Trump not only touted his purported efforts to quell the Ukrainian conflict but also embarked on a geographical odyssey that left more questions than answers about his grasp of basic world geography.

The Core of the Matter: Geography 101

It appears that Trump, in an attempt to underscore the geographical separations that ostensibly buffer the United States from the European concerns about Ukraine, stumbled into a quagmire of confusion over a "thing called… an ocean." This moment of hesitation might have been brushed off as a harmless slip, had it not been for the gravity of the conversation and the person involved. The former president seemed to momentarily forget the name of a significant body of water, a blunder that might have been humorous in a less critical context.

The Oceanic Oversight

This geographical faux pas raises eyebrows for several reasons. First, it's concerning that Trump, once the leader of one of the world's most powerful nations, struggles with naming the Atlantic or the Pacific Ocean, basic elements of global geography that are familiar to most middle school students. The Atlantic Ocean, after all, is the body of water that primarily separates the U.S. from Europe, the context in which his comment was made. His reference to the Bering Strait and the Pacific Ocean only muddles the waters further, painting a picture of a leader who is not just geographically disoriented but also seemingly unaware of the geopolitical landscapes he once navigated.

Uncharted Waters: The Mental Decline Debate

This incident cannot be viewed in isolation but rather as part of a troubling pattern of incoherence and forgetfulness displayed by Trump. At 79 years old, questions about his mental fitness for office are not new, fueled by his own erratic behavior and speech. The fact that medical experts are raising alarms over his potential "mental decline" is a significant concern, not just for the former president but for the political ecosystem that continues to support him.

Trump's defense, that he "aced" a cognitive test in 2024, does little to assuage fears. Cognitive tests are designed to detect severe deficits, not affirm advanced intellectual capabilities or the nuanced understanding required to navigate complex geopolitical landscapes. It's akin to bragging about passing a basic health screening while ignoring symptoms of a more profound malaise.

The Fox & Friends Facade

Equally concerning is the role of Fox & Friends hosts in this saga. Their failure to address or even acknowledge Trump's confusion in real-time speaks volumes about the state of political discourse on the network. It's a glaring example of how media platforms can become echo chambers, where critical analysis and factual accuracy take a back seat to maintaining a narrative that aligns with their political biases.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

Trump's "big, beautiful ocean" comment, while seemingly trivial, encapsulates a much larger issue: the normalization of incompetence and the disregard for factual accuracy in political leadership. It's a stark reminder of the importance of holding public figures accountable for their words and actions, especially when those figures wield significant influence over public opinion and policy.

As observers and participants in the democratic process, it's imperative to demand more from our leaders and the media that cover them. We must navigate these troubled waters with a critical eye, lest we all get swept away by the currents of misinformation and mediocrity.
