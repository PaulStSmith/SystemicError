Scorn as Trump rushes back to DC with 'top advisers' — golfers and a model

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

The Golfers Are a Distraction. The Presidency Is the Story.

A Photograph in Place of Accountability

Donald Trump abruptly left Camp David as conflict involving Iran intensified and U.S. embassies across the Middle East warned Americans about security risks. He returned to Washington aboard Marine One with golfer Gary Player, golfer Bryson DeChambeau, and DeChambeau’s girlfriend, Mikayla Demaiter.

That image is revealing—but not for the easy reason offered by social media mockery. The scandal is not merely that Trump socialized with golfers during a crisis. It is that presidential power remains obscured behind spectacle, while coverage invites readers to scrutinize the guest list instead of the machinery of war.

The President Holds the Power

Golfers do not command the military, direct embassies, or determine whether the United States escalates a conflict. The president and his administration do.

The source identifies no military order issued aboard Marine One and establishes no role for Trump’s companions in national-security decisions. Calling them his “top advisers” is sarcasm, not reporting. It may capture the grotesque optics, but it does not clarify who was actually advising Trump, what decisions were under consideration, or which officials were responsible for the regional posture alarming Americans abroad.

Those are the consequential questions. The photograph cannot answer them, and the article largely does not try.

Spectacle Protects the Decision-Makers

The golfers are politically useful precisely because they are powerless. Their presence offers a vivid symbol of unseriousness without forcing scrutiny of the officials exercising institutional authority.

This is a familiar media failure: substitute personality evidence for an account of government action. Trump’s preference for celebrity company may expose his priorities, but it does not explain the escalation itself. Focusing on who shared the helicopter risks reducing war-making to another episode in the endless reality show surrounding him.

The result is misdirection. Readers are encouraged to sneer at leisure while the administration’s decisions remain indistinct.

Warnings Without an Accounting

The embassy alerts matter because they reveal consequences beyond Washington’s optics. Americans across the region were being warned about danger serious enough to provoke speculation about further escalation.

Yet “fears” and “could certainly” are not findings. The reporting establishes anxiety, alerts, and an abrupt presidential return—not the nature of any impending operation. A responsible political analysis should preserve that distinction while pressing harder on what is knowable: who authorized the relevant actions, which agencies implemented them, and what risks they imposed.

Treating escalation as atmospheric tension allows deliberate state choices to masquerade as weather.

Government by Image Management

Trump’s political method has long depended on collapsing governance into spectacle. In this instance, critics risk reinforcing that method. A cutting joke about “top advisers” travels farther than an institutional account because it is easier to consume and requires less evidence.

But ridicule is not accountability. It can even become a substitute for it. The president appears unserious; the audience laughs; the underlying exercise of executive power escapes examination.

That is institutional cowardice in miniature: coverage recognizes the absurdity surrounding authority while declining to map authority itself.

The Systemic Error

The larger pattern is not that powerful men play golf during crises. It is that American political coverage repeatedly personalizes state violence and depoliticizes the institutions that enable it.

A golfer stepping off Marine One is an image. Embassy warnings, military escalation, and presidential command are power. Confusing the first for the substance of the story leaves the public staring at the entourage while the government acts behind it.
