'We got sacked in that meeting​': Trump's tax cuts in danger as GOP lawmakers back down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Mirage of Fiscal Responsibility: GOP Tax Cut Fiasco

U.S. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (R-LA) arrives at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 11, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan HowardDetail

Republican congressional leaders seem to be scaling back the "big, beautiful bill" sought by president Donald Trump after deficit hawks warned they would vote against the sprawling tax legislation.

The permanent extension of Trump's 2017 tax cuts could be cut, as well as the president's promised tax relief for seniors and exemptions on tips and overtime earnings, as key committees struggle to hit their $2 trillion target for spending cuts to offset those reductions in revenue, reported Politico.

“Republicans talk a big game … about reining in reckless spending,” said House budget chair Jodey Arrington (R-TX). “You won’t get the full permanency in the tax policy on all the provisions if we don’t get to the $2 trillion in savings, and that’s unfortunate.”

GOP leaders are still considering Trump's request to increase income taxes on the highest earners to their pre-2017 levels, from 37 percent to 39.6 percent, to make room for his promised cuts to taxes on Social Security, tips and overtime, and Ways and Means chair Jason Smith (R-MO) will visit the White House on Friday to discuss ways to make that happen.

House speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) told GOP lawmakers that he's scaled back his target from $4.5 trillion in tax cuts to $4 trillion, a half-billion less than many Republicans had hoped, and that's probably below what's needed to permanently extend the 2017 cuts, and he's pushing three key committees vote on their portions of the bill next week, and the Senate is likely to make revisions to anything those panels approve.

“I’m not excited about the proposal, but I have to say, there are a number of people in both the House and the Senate who are, and if the president weighs in favor of it, then that’s going to be a big factor that we have to take into consideration as well,” said Senate Finance Committee Chair Mike Crapo (R-ID).

Johnson's reduced target would force those committees to make some difficult choices, and tax writers say a number of tax provisions would be temporary, and blue-state Republicans shot down a proposal to increase state and local tax (SALT) deduction cap from $10,000 to $30,000 to raise more revenue to pay for Trump's priorities.

“We were on the 25-yard line with about 75 yards to go,” said Rep. Nick LaLota (R-NY). “We got sacked at that meeting. We probably lost five to ten yards.”

The scaled-back tax cuts are seen as a setback to Trump's agenda, but Politico reported that two White House sources are relieved that GOP lawmakers have backed down on cuts to safety net programs, such as Medicaid, and are privately hoping that swing-district moderates to win out over budget hard-liners.

Our Analysis:

The Mirage of Fiscal Responsibility: GOP Tax Cut Fiasco

In a spectacle that's becoming all too familiar, the Republican congressional leadership's recent dance around tax legislation underscores a glaring hypocrisy at the heart of their fiscal policy. The so-called "big, beautiful bill" that President Donald Trump has been touting finds itself significantly trimmed down in the face of opposition from deficit hawks within the party. It seems that the Republican commitment to fiscal conservatism is only as strong as its least popular spending cut.

Scaling Back on Promises

The narrative pushed by Trump and echoed by his administration promised expansive and permanent tax cuts, a move that was heralded as a boon for the middle class, seniors, and workers earning tips and overtime. However, the reality, as reported by Politico, paints a starkly different picture. The GOP's struggle to align their grandiose tax-cut promises with the daunting task of finding $2 trillion in spending cuts has led to a significant scaling back of ambitions.

The Irony of Fiscal Conservatism

House Budget Chair Jodey Arrington's (R-TX) lament that full permanency in tax policy would not be achievable without the targeted savings is a testament to the party's internal conflict. On one hand, there's a desire to deliver on tax cuts; on the other, there's the looming specter of expanding deficits, a monster of their own making. This predicament is emblematic of the GOP's broader ideological crisis: claiming the mantle of fiscal responsibility while championing policies that exacerbate fiscal imbalances.

The Rich Get Richer, and the Promises Get Poorer

While GOP leaders are still toying with the idea of raising income taxes on the highest earners to pre-2017 levels—a move that would, on its face, seem to align with a semblance of equity—their reluctance to permanently extend the 2017 cuts speaks volumes. The prioritization of temporary tax provisions over substantial reform highlights a clear preference for short-term political gains over long-term economic stability.

Blue-State Republicans and the SALT Deduction Charade

The outright rejection by blue-state Republicans of a proposal to raise more revenue by increasing the state and local tax (SALT) deduction cap is particularly telling. It's a microcosm of the larger GOP strategy: tout fiscal discipline in theory, but when practical solutions that might offset some of the cost of their tax-cut fantasies are proposed, suddenly the commitment to fiscal conservatism wanes.

A Setback or a Reality Check?

While some may view the scaling back of Trump's tax cuts as a setback, it could also be seen as a much-needed reality check. The relief expressed by two White House sources at GOP lawmakers' reluctance to slash safety net programs to fund tax cuts suggests that there is, buried deep beneath the rhetoric, an understanding of the potential political and social fallout from pursuing an aggressively regressive fiscal policy.

Conclusion

The GOP's latest tax cut saga is a clear illustration of a party at odds with itself, torn between the desire to appease a tax-cut-hungry base and the realities of fiscal governance. The result is a watered-down version of an already problematic policy, one that likely will neither satisfy the party's fiscal hawks nor adequately serve the American people. In their quest to deliver on Trump's promises, Republican leaders have instead showcased their propensity for systemic error: a failure to reconcile the inherent contradiction between their tax-cutting zeal and the principles of fiscal responsibility they claim to champion.
