'It's that simple': Elizabeth Warren draws line for GOP as Congress hurtles to shutdown

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20256 min read

U.S. Senator Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) arrives for an interview with CNBC on the floor at the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in New York City, U.S., April 17, 2025. REUTERS/Brendan McDermid

On the US Senate floor Thursday, Sen. Elizabeth Warren gave her Republican colleagues a choice: undo the damage they caused to the healthcare of millions of Americans by slashing Medicaid and insurance subsidies, or explain to the public why they refuse to do so—even if it means shutting down the government.

Warren (D-MA) spoke about a proposal released by the Democrats Wednesday night to keep the government running through October 31—averting a shutdown at the beginning of next month—if the GOP agrees to restore the $1 trillion in Medicaid cuts and extend the Affordable Care Act subsidies to keep out-of-pocket premiums from rising by an average of 75% for millions of people who purchase health insurance through the ACA.

A Congressional Budget Office analysis released Thursday found that making the ACA subsidies permanent would increase the number of insured people by nearly 4 million.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) and Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) have said Democrats will not vote for Republicans’ proposal to extend government funding at its current level through November 21, including the cuts in the so-called One Big Beautiful Bill Act, unless the GOP opens bipartisan talks on the legislation.

So far, GOP leaders have not asked the Democrats for input—but the Republicans will need at least 60 votes to pass the spending proposal in the Senate and will require Democrats to vote with them.

On the Senate floor, Warren told the Republicans how they can ensure that result.

“Before working moms go broke from a cancer diagnosis, Congress must act. Before community hospitals are forced to shut down, Congress must act,” said the senator. “That is why Democrats are saying: ‘If Republicans want our votes, they need to restore healthcare for Americans.‘”

While Schumer has demanded bipartisan talks and called for the GOP to make concessions on healthcare, he told The Washington Post Wednesday that the Democrats do not have a “red line.”

Schumer angered progressive lawmakers and many of his own constituents in March when he joined the GOP to advance a spending bill that kept the government open—but cut $13 billion in nonmilitary federal spending and did nothing to rein in President Donald Trump and his then-adviser, Elon Musk, as they eviscerated government agencies.

Lisa Gilbert, co-president of the consumer advocacy group Public Citizen, said Tuesday that the current “alignment of Democratic leadership and appropriators in recognition of this moment of leverage is heartening.”

“A budget deal should be contingent on addressing Americans’ top economic priority—the cost of and access to healthcare. If Republicans refuse to negotiate and move away from their cost-increasing agenda, then they are the ones who will be forcing a government-wide shutdown,” said Gilbert. “There should be no deal without assurances that the budget will be honored and not impounded, and that it will begin to return care to the American people.”

By refusing to meet with the Democrats thus far, said Kobie Christian of Unrig Our Economy, GOP leaders are thus far showing that “if it isn’t about giving the ultrarich another tax break, Republicans in Congress aren’t interested.”

“Every day that Congress does not take action to prevent increases in health insurance premiums, more and more Americans are at risk of facing higher healthcare costs and losing coverage,” said Christian. “It’s time that congressional Republicans come to the table and find a solution to help all Americans, not just the ultrawealthy.”

On her way to the Senate floor Thursday, Warren said that “if Republicans want our votes for this budget, they’ve got to restore healthcare for millions of Americans.”

“It’s really that simple,” she added.

Our Analysis:

The Political Chessboard: Warren's Standoff Over Healthcare

In the latest act of the political theater that is the United States Senate, Senator Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) has thrown down the gauntlet to her Republican colleagues, presenting them with what can only be described as a Hobson's choice: restore the healthcare funding they've gutted or face the wrath of the public for their intransigence. Warren's strategy, delivered on the Senate floor Thursday, underscores a pivotal moment in the ongoing saga of American healthcare, with millions of citizens caught in the crossfire.

The Democratic Proposal: A Lifeline or a Pipe Dream?

The Democrats, led by the likes of Warren, Jeffries, and Schumer, have put forth a proposal that seems like a no-brainer on the surface: keep the government running and restore $1 trillion in Medicaid cuts while extending the Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies to prevent out-of-pocket premiums from skyrocketing. A move that, according to a Congressional Budget Office analysis, could increase the insured populace by nearly 4 million.

Yet, the simplicity of this proposal belies the complexity of its execution. The GOP, having not even sought Democratic input on their so-called "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," seems poised to continue their healthcare austerity unimpeded, needing but a simple majority in the Senate to pass their spending proposal. Warren's plea on the Senate floor, therefore, is not merely a call to action but a stark reminder of the consequences of inaction: working moms facing bankruptcy due to medical bills, community hospitals shutting down, and a general healthcare apocalypse.

Schumer's "No Red Line" and the Ghost of Compromises Past

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer's admission that the Democrats do not have a "red line" in negotiations is particularly galling. It reeks of the same spirit that led Schumer and co. to advance a spending bill earlier in the year that did nothing to curb the administration's gutting of federal agencies, all while cutting nonmilitary federal spending. This lack of a firm stance, especially after such a debacle, is not just disappointing; it's a strategic misfire that undercuts the Democratic position before negotiations have even begun.

The Republican Stalemate and the Specter of Shutdown

The GOP's current stance, as articulated by figures like Lisa Gilbert of Public Citizen and Kobie Christian of Unrig Our Economy, is one of obstinate refusal to engage. By not even entertaining bipartisan talks, the Republicans are signaling a willingness to let the government shut down rather than negotiate on healthcare. This approach, ostensibly aimed at preserving tax breaks for the ultrarich, could not be a clearer demonstration of their priorities.

The implication here is stark: for the GOP, the health and well-being of millions of Americans are acceptable collateral damage in their quest to preserve the status quo of wealth inequality. Warren's framing of the issue, then, as a choice between restoring healthcare or facing public backlash, is not just political maneuvering. It is a reflection of a deeper moral and ethical divide that cuts to the heart of what it means to govern in the public interest.

Conclusion: A Call to Arms or a Cry in the Wilderness?

Senator Warren's ultimatum to her colleagues is more than a tactical ploy; it is a clarion call for sanity in an increasingly insane political landscape. Whether this call will be heeded remains to be seen. The coming days will be a test of not just political will but of the moral fiber of those who claim to represent the American people.

In the end, the choice presented by Warren and the Democratic proposal is simple, yet profound: restore healthcare funding or explain to the American public why their health and well-being are not worth the investment. The ball is now in the GOP's court, and the clock is ticking.
