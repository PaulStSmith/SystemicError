'No way out': Young voters now regret voting for Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 17, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Buyer’s Remorse of Young GOP Voters: A Telling Tale

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 10, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Young voters who pledged their support to Donald Trump and the Republican Party at the last election are suffering from buyer’s remorse.

A collection of young people who voted for the GOP at the 2024 election highlighted the many problems now facing their generation as a result of the party in power. One voter, Joseph, told The Atlantic that his generation feels trapped by the current state of the country under the Trump administration’s watch.

He said, “From an economics factor, so many of the things that I would say are not wants, but instead needs, have just absolutely skyrocketed. And basic families are spending so much on just the cost of living that they don’t have a cost to save, or anything like that. There’s just no financial way out.”

Another voter, Mukesh, has called on the Trump administration to focus on domestic issues rather than foreign policy.

“I think we should just respect it [Greenland], and leave it, and just focus on what’s actually happening inside the nation,” he said.

Lizabel, another voter who backed Trump at the last election, says the state of the country under the president’s team is far from ideal. She said, “I think things are pretty chaotic lately, honestly.”

“You just see all this stuff on the news, and you see a lot of people are struggling to find jobs. A lot of people are feeling kind of pessimistic about what things are going on.”

Kim, a Generation Z Trump voter, agreed, adding, “There are things that are very disappointing and very rough right now.” The writing may be on the wall for Trump and the GOP, with the governor of California, Gavin Newsom, suggesting the president has resigned himself to massive losses in November.

Newsom told CNN, “Trump’s going to get shellacked in the midterms. He knows that. The world is becoming, I think, more and more familiar with that reality.”

Our Analysis:

The Buyer’s Remorse of Young GOP Voters: A Telling Tale

In a revealing piece by The Atlantic, a narrative unfolds that could very well be the epitaph of the Trump era for young Republicans. These voters, who once threw their support behind Donald Trump and his party, are now facing the harsh daylight of regret. Their disillusionment paints a stark portrait of a generation caught in the crosshairs of political promises and the grim realities of their fulfillment—or lack thereof.

Joseph’s Economic Despair: The story of Joseph, a young voter, is emblematic of the broader economic anxieties plaguing this demographic. His words, “so many of the things that I would say are not wants, but instead needs, have just absolutely skyrocketed,” lay bare the economic stranglehold that current policies have on basic family finances. This isn’t just buyer’s remorse; it’s a cry for help from a generation feeling the economic squeeze with no relief in sight. The situation is so dire that the concept of savings seems almost mythical, a relic of a bygone era.

Mukesh’s Plea for Domestic Focus: Mukesh’s commentary shifts the lens to the administration’s foreign policy distractions. His call for the Trump administration to “just focus on what’s actually happening inside the nation” underscores a critical misalignment between the government’s priorities and the urgent needs of its citizens. The mention of Greenland, while specific, serves as a metaphor for the administration’s often bewildering foreign policy pursuits, sidelining pressing domestic issues.

Lizabel’s Observation of Chaos: Lizabel’s reflection on the state of the nation as “pretty chaotic lately” is an understatement. The chaos she mentions isn’t just a series of unconnected events but a symptom of systemic dysfunction under Trump’s leadership. Her comments about job struggles and pervasive pessimism are not mere grievances but a damning indictment of the administration’s failure to provide stability and hope.

Kim’s Disappointment: The sentiment of disappointment expressed by Kim, another Generation Z voter, encapsulates the feeling of many young conservatives. The acknowledgment of current conditions as “very disappointing and very rough” is a testament to the disillusionment felt by those who expected more from their elected leaders.

Newsom’s Prediction of a GOP Downfall: The article concludes with a prediction from California Governor Gavin Newsom, who anticipates a significant defeat for Trump in the midterms. His statement, “Trump’s going to get shellacked in the midterms,” isn’t just political bravado; it’s a recognition of the shifting sands beneath the GOP’s feet as more and more of its young base awaken to the consequences of their electoral choices.

In Conclusion: The collective voices of Joseph, Mukesh, Lizabel, and Kim are more than just individual stories of regret; they are harbingers of a potential political realignment. Their experiences and observations offer a glimpse into the disillusionment and disenchantment of a segment of the electorate once thought to be securely in the GOP’s camp. As these young voters reckon with the impact of their decisions, the Republican Party would do well to take heed. The generational divide, exacerbated by economic despair, domestic negligence, and chaotic governance, may very well be the undoing of Trump’s political legacy.
