A dark new ideology is spreading through the GOP — thanks to the Trump admin

Based on an article by The Conversation

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20263 min read

Masculinism Is Not a Mood. It Is a Power Project.

The Men Running the Story

The source article is useful because it names the thing plainly: masculinism is not just online grievance, not just bad manners, not just a subculture of damaged men posting through their feelings. It is an organized anti-feminist politics that joins influencers, pastors, podcasters, and officeholders around a shared objective: restoring male dominance.

That matters because the people giving this ideology oxygen are not all marginal. Some sit near state power. Doug Wilson was welcomed into the Pentagon’s Christian prayer orbit. Pete Hegseth prayed alongside him. The article also notes allegations that the Trump White House intervened in Andrew Tate’s Romanian case. Whether every allegation is proven or not, the pattern is clear enough: the right’s misogynist fringe does not remain a fringe when institutional gatekeepers decide it is useful.

The Useful Lie of “Culture War”

This is where weak political framing does its usual damage. Stories like this can be treated as if they are about a noisy online style war, or about “extreme ideas becoming more common,” as though that were the main issue. It is not.

The real issue is power. Masculinism is not trying to win an argument about gender. It is trying to normalize hierarchy. It tells men they are entitled to women’s labor, bodies, votes, money, and obedience. That is not a grievance agenda. It is a social order.

The article’s examples are not random personalities. Tate monetized abuse and called women “assets.” Wilson argues for stripping women of the vote and resurrecting a “family vote” that hands political agency to male heads of household. These are not metaphors. They are instructions for domination.

What They Want Back

The movement is blunt about the destination. Women at home. Men in charge. Political power filtered through patriarchal households. Public authority reserved for those imagined as naturally rational, forceful, and fit to rule. The article is right to connect this to older anti-feminist reaction, but it should be said more directly: this is a counterrevolution against mass democratic equality.

That is why the misogyny matters politically. Once women are recast as irrational dependents, it becomes easier to justify excluding them from voting, leadership, and economic independence. Once that logic is accepted, it never stops with women. It slides easily into white supremacy, nationalist fertility panic, and contempt for anyone who does not fit the preferred household order.

The Far Right’s Glue

The article makes its strongest point when it shows that masculinism is not a side issue on the right; it is connective tissue. The right may quarrel over Israel, Big Tech, or tactics, but it keeps finding unity in hostility to feminism. That is not accidental. Patriarchy is one of the easiest common languages for reactionary politics because it offers hierarchy without embarrassment.

The names in the piece make that plain: Tate, Fuentes, Wilson, Hegseth, One Nation, and a broader ecosystem of churches, podcasts, and grievance media. These actors do not need to agree on every policy detail. They only need to agree that male domination is natural, that feminism is blameworthy, and that democratic equality has gone too far.

When Institutions Make Room

The most revealing detail in the source is not the online bile. It is the institutional welcome. A preacher who speaks of women as submitters is not just shouting into a void when he is folded into Pentagon prayer life. A man like Tate is not just a provocateur when his case becomes a matter of high-level political attention.

This is how extremism moves: not always through formal endorsement, but through selective tolerance, access, and excuses. Security agencies may still treat misogyny as a “lesser” concern, but that only reveals their blindness. Violence against women is not an adjacent issue. It is a rehearsal space for authoritarian order.

The Pattern Beneath the Noise

Masculinism thrives because it offers reactionaries a simple story: equality caused decline, women caused disorder, and democracy became too inclusive. That story is false, but it is politically useful because it turns structural crisis into a search for scapegoats.

The deeper pattern is this: whenever the right feels threatened by social equality, it reaches for domestic hierarchy as a political model. First comes contempt for feminism. Then comes nostalgia for “traditional” authority. Then comes a broader attack on voting, rights, and shared citizenship. Masculinism is not a detour from authoritarian politics. It is one of its favorite entry points.
