The Pardoner-in-Chief: How Trump Turned Clemency Into a Blunt Political Weapon

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A Blanket for the InsurrectionistsPardons for Profit, Power, and PropagandaJustice for SaleHistorical Parallels and Future WarningsClemency Without ConscienceWhat Happens Next?

Donald Trump once again proved that for him, the machinery of the American government is not a tool of service but a cudgel of domination. Within 24 hours of reclaiming the White House in 2025, he issued sweeping pardons to over 1,500 individuals convicted in connection to the January 6th Capitol attack. With one stroke of the pen, he didn’t just erase criminal records; he rewrote the moral ledger of American history.

A Blanket for the Insurrectionists

On Day One, Trump obliterated the judicial consequences for the seditious mob that stormed the Capitol in 2021. It wasn’t just a handful of symbolic figures; this was a blanket amnesty for the entire violent movement. This includes people convicted of assaulting Capitol Police, destroying federal property, carrying weapons on Capitol grounds, and conspiring to obstruct the certification of the election. Fourteen prominent leaders of the Oath Keepers and Proud Boys received commutations rather than pardons—likely a legal hedge. But the message was unmistakable: if you commit political violence in Trump’s name, your crimes are patriotic.

Pardons for Profit, Power, and Propaganda

Next came a cavalcade of clemency for ideological martyrs and political cronies:

  • Ross Ulbricht, the Silk Road founder serving a double life sentence for drug trafficking and conspiracy, walked free.

  • Lauren Handy and other anti-abortion activists convicted under the FACE Act had their convictions wiped clean.

  • Devon Archer, Trevor Milton, and other white-collar grifters with GOP ties were quietly absolved.

  • Even the con-artist reality TV stars, the Chrisleys, received a nod from on high.

Each pardon was not a gesture of mercy but of calculation. Trump didn’t consult the DOJ’s Office of the Pardon Attorney. He didn’t weigh remorse or rehabilitation. He consulted his donor list, his war room, and his ego. The pardon power was wielded like a scythe to reward loyalty, generate headlines, and spite the rule of law.

Justice for Sale

Estimates suggest that Trump’s 2025 clemency spree shortchanged victims by over $1 billion in restitution. In many white-collar cases, courts had ordered convicted fraudsters to repay their victims. Trump’s pardons didn’t just wipe clean prison sentences; they obliterated financial penalties. Pensioners swindled out of retirement savings and communities defrauded by corporate charlatans were told, essentially: “Tough luck, they’re on my team.”

We’ve entered a grotesque new frontier in American governance: clemency-as-currency. Loyalty buys absolution. Violence in Trump’s name earns redemption. Political utility overrides human cost.

Historical Parallels and Future Warnings

Presidential pardons have always been contentious, but Trump’s approach is unprecedented in scope, speed, and cynicism. Nixon’s pardon was a singular stain. Clinton pardoned some cronies, sure. But Trump turned the entire clemency process into a transaction.

His apologists call it decisive. The rest of us should call it what it is: autocratic behavior. It is exactly the kind of legalized corruption seen in failed democracies—where laws are optional for the powerful, and their inner circle lives above consequence.

And let’s not pretend this is over. These pardons embolden future extremists, and normalize using the presidency to reward allies who break the law. The next time there is a violent uprising, the participants will remember Trump’s example. Why fear prison when the tyrant-in-waiting carries a rubber stamp?

Clemency Without Conscience

Trump's pardons showcase a man devoid of humility or responsibility. He wasn’t correcting injustice; he was enshrining impunity. He didn’t pardon the poor, the abused, or the over-sentenced. He pardoned the violent, the connected, and the complicit. And he did it with the gusto of a man who believes he's immune.

It is now abundantly clear: Trump’s vision of justice is vengeance. His vision of mercy is conditional. His use of presidential power is nothing short of authoritarian mimicry.

What Happens Next?

The Department of Justice is reportedly "reviewing" the pardons. But let’s be blunt: unless Congress acts, unless the courts intervene, unless voters rebel, this is the new normal. Future presidents may feel empowered to use the pardon power as a tool for personal gain. That poison is in the bloodstream now.

Trump’s clemency spree must be condemned, challenged, and, if possible, reversed where legal avenues exist. But more importantly, we must reassert as a society that the presidency is not a get-out-of-jail-free card dispenser for seditionists and scam artists.

If we fail to do that, the next wave of violence won’t just be predicted. It will be invited.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
