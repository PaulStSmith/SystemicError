Trump in full panic over war restarting – and ‘swearing a lot about it’: insider

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

Trump’s “Peace” Depends on an Occupier’s Permission

The Real Center of Power

The source makes one thing plain: the institutional power still sits with Washington, but the operational leverage is being exercised by Israel. Trump can boast about a tentative deal with Iran all he wants; if Israeli officials can openly defy its terms and keep their military campaign alive, then the supposed peace is already conditional on someone else’s restraint.

Who Enabled the Outcome

Trump’s administration negotiated the memorandum with Tehran. That is the decision that created the political stakes. But the Israeli government is the actor refusing to comply with the agreement’s first condition, which calls for operations in Lebanon to stop while Israeli forces still occupy roughly 10% of the country. That is not confusion. That is a deliberate refusal to let a diplomatic deal constrain military behavior.

The Convenient Misdirection

The article leans hard on Trump’s private anger and fears, which is useful if you want the conflict to look like a matter of mood rather than power. It is not just that Trump is “madder” at Israelis than Iranians. It is that the U.S. president is being positioned as someone reacting to events that stronger allies and partners are still trying to shape for him. That framing softens responsibility on the people actually making escalation possible.

Occupation, Not Misunderstanding

The source notes that Israeli officials are “defiant” and have vowed not to stop the campaign. That matters more than Trump’s swearing. An occupying force does not accidentally continue occupying. A government does not “drift” into vetoing a deal by ignoring its obligations. These are choices, and they carry consequences for Lebanon, for Iran, and for any U.S. claim to be steering events rather than being dragged by them.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

This is the familiar imperial arrangement in miniature: Washington signs the paper, allies test the limits, and the public is asked to focus on personalities instead of enforcement. The weak point is never the rhetoric. It is whether the powerful will punish defiance when it comes from an armed client with political cover. If they will not, then the “peace deal” is not peace. It is theater wrapped around a hierarchy that still rewards escalation.
