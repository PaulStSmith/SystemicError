'He didn’t quit, I fired him!’ Trump lashes out at outgoing appointee in overnight rant

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 19, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

President Donald Trump announced the dismissal of his own appointee for U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia late Friday night, justifying the move in a social media post by alleging that his choice for the position had gained “unusually strong” backing from his political opponents.

“Today I withdrew the nomination of Erik Siebert as U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia, when I was informed that he received the UNUSUALLY STRONG support of the two absolutely terrible, sleazebag Democrat Senators, from the Great State of Virginia,” Trump wrote shortly before midnight Friday on his social media platform Truth Social.

“He didn’t quit, I fired him! Next time let him go in as a Democrat, not a Republican. Thank you for your attention to this matter. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!”

Siebert served the Eastern District of Virginia as an assistant U.S. attorney starting in 2010, and was among those tasked with investigating New York Attorney General Letitia James, who last year launched and won a $464 million fraud case against Trump. Trump’s Justice Department launched a probe into James’ office for alleged mortgage fraud, but Siebert recently declined to seek an indictment against her after finding insufficient evidence.

It was not long after Siebert’s decision not to pursue charges against James in the criminal probe launched by Trump’s Justice Department that the president signaled he wanted Siebert out, telling reporters earlier on Friday “I want him out.”

Trump didn’t name Siebert’s refusal to prosecute James as the reason for his ousting, however, and instead named the support he had received from Virginia’s Democratic senators, Tim Kaine and Mark Warner.

“When I saw that he got approved by those two men, I said, pull it, because he can't be any good," Trump said told reporters earlier Friday, ABC News reported. "When I learned that they voted for him, I said, I don't really want him."

Our Analysis:

The Ousting of Erik Siebert: Trump’s Transparent Vendetta

In a move that surprised no one who has been paying attention to the erratic, often vindictive nature of Donald Trump’s decision-making, the President announced the late-night dismissal of Erik Siebert, his own appointee for U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia. The justification? A paranoid reaction to bipartisan support that would traditionally be viewed as a mark of a candidate's integrity and competence.

Trump’s reason for dismissal is cloaked in the guise of party loyalty but transparently reeks of personal vendetta. Siebert, having served as an assistant U.S. attorney since 2010, recently made the professionally and ethically sound decision to decline seeking an indictment against New York Attorney General Letitia James due to insufficient evidence. James, notably, has been a thorn in Trump’s side, successfully launching and winning a $464 million fraud case against him.

The realpolitik behind Trump’s dismissal of Siebert could not be more transparent. In Trump’s own words on his social media platform Truth Social, he withdrew Siebert’s nomination because he received “UNUSUALLY STRONG support” from Virginia’s Democratic senators, whom he charmingly refers to as “absolutely terrible, sleazebag Democrat Senators.” This statement alone is a masterclass in the kind of divisive, inflammatory rhetoric that has come to define Trump’s political career.

But let’s unpack this further. Trump’s admission that he fired Siebert not because of any professional failing, but because of the bipartisan support he garnered, is alarming. It sends a clear message: loyalty to Trump, not competence or ethics, is the paramount criterion for serving in his administration. This is not just an isolated incident; it is symptomatic of a broader, deeply troubling approach to governance.

Moreover, Trump’s disdain for the judicial process and the principles of justice is laid bare by his reaction to Siebert’s professional judgment in the case against James. The refusal to seek an indictment was not a political decision; it was a legal one, based on insufficient evidence. Yet, in Trump’s view, Siebert’s adherence to the law over loyalty to him personally is a betrayal.

The implications of such a stance are far-reaching. It undermines the independence of the judiciary and erodes public trust in the institutions designed to serve justice impartially. When the President of the United States prioritizes personal loyalty and vendettas over the rule of law, it is a dark day for democracy.

In conclusion, Trump’s dismissal of Erik Siebert is not just about the ousting of a single U.S. Attorney. It’s a case study in how Trump’s leadership style—marked by divisiveness, retaliation, and a flagrant disregard for ethical governance—continues to pose a systemic threat to the very fabric of American democracy. This incident should not be viewed in isolation, but as part of a troubling pattern that warrants rigorous scrutiny and, more importantly, accountability.
