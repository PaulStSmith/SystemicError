Trump’s 'triple threat' poised to crash Boomers' generational party

Atlantic writer Charley Locke says Baby Boomers have caught the very best that America can give them. Now they’re about to catch a case of Trumponomics.

“They were born in an era of unprecedented economic growth and stability,” writes Locke in the Atlantic. “College was affordable, and they graduated in a thriving job market. They were the first generation to reap the full benefits of a golden age of medical innovations: birth control, robotic surgery, the mapping of the human genome, effective cancer treatments, [and] Ozempic.”

But recent policy changes proposed by President Donald Trump are promising to douse that parade.

“If you’re in your 60s or 70s, what the Trump administration has done means more insecurity for your assets in your 401(k), more insecurity about sources of long-term care, and, for the first time, insecurity about your Social Security benefits,” said New School labor economist Teresa Ghilarducci. “It’s a triple threat.”

The biggest threat to Boomers in the second Trump administration is the president’s plan to overhaul Social Security, which provides benefits to nearly nine out of 10 Americans ages 65 and older. DOGE announced it was slicing 12 percent of Social Security staff and close six of its 10 regional offices. Shutting down field offices means seniors can’t get help in person, and small staff means longer wait times. And now that a quarter of the agency’s IT workers have already quit or been fired, they can expect more frequent and longer website crashes.

“When you add hurdles, or cause a slowdown in terms of processing claims, you see losses in terms of benefits,” said Monique Morrissey, a senior economist at the Economic Policy Institute.

But then there’s the money factor. Any cuts to Social Security first hits low-income Boomers, who are the likeliest to rely on benefits to cover their whole cost of living. And anyone “comfortably middle-class in your early 60s,” has no guarantee they’ll stay that way into their 70s, writes Locke.

Trump’s “big, beautiful bill,” now in the Senate, also substantially reduces Medicaid enrollment by about 10.3 million people. And Medicaid pays for more than half of long-term care in the U.S., which 70 percent of adults who live to 65 will require.

On top of all this lies the volatile market under Trump’s tariffs, say analysts.

“[Middle-income seniors] tend to have modest investments and fixed incomes rather than equities,” said Boston College labor market expert Laura D. Quinby. “[That’s] the type of wealth that will erode over a high-inflation period.”

Our Analysis:

The Looming Crisis for Baby Boomers: A Closer Look at Trumponomics

The article by Charley Locke in The Atlantic paints a grim picture for Baby Boomers, a generation once cradled in the comforts of post-war economic prosperity, now facing the specter of financial instability due to recent policy changes under the Trump administration. Locke's narrative, supported by insights from various economists, outlines a scenario where the golden years for Boomers could turn into lead, burdened by insecurity and the potential dismantling of the social safety net they have come to rely on. Let's dissect the key points and the looming crisis it foretells.

The Boomers' Hayday

Baby Boomers, according to Locke, were born into an era of unprecedented economic growth and stability. Affordable college, a booming job market, and breakthrough medical innovations were the backdrop of their formative years. This generation, more than any before, benefited from the fruits of American innovation and economic expansion. However, the tide seems to be turning with the proposed policy shifts under the Trump administration, which Locke and the experts she cites argue, threaten to unravel the fabric of security that Boomers have woven around their lives.

The Triple Threat

Teresa Ghilarducci, a labor economist at the New School, terms the impact of Trump's policies on Boomers as a triple threat — targeting their 401(k)s, long-term care sources, and Social Security benefits. The administration's plan to overhaul Social Security, a lifeline for nearly 90% of Americans aged 65 and older, emerges as the most significant threat. The decision to slash Social Security staff by 12% and close a majority of its regional offices will inevitably lead to reduced accessibility and efficiency, directly harming those dependent on its benefits.

A Tumultuous Future for Social Security

The proposed cuts to Social Security starkly illustrate the potential for increased financial insecurity among low-income Boomers, who rely on these benefits for their entire cost of living. Furthermore, middle-class Boomers in their early 60s face the stark reality that their comfortable lifestyle may not be sustainable into their 70s, challenging the assumption that a middle-class life is shielded from the vagaries of policy changes and economic downturns.

The Medicaid Conundrum

Trump's "big, beautiful bill," with its significant cuts to Medicaid enrollment, threatens to deepen the crisis. Considering that Medicaid funds over half of the long-term care in the U.S., a necessity for 70% of adults who reach the age of 65, the proposed reduction in enrollment could leave many without essential care. This scenario is particularly alarming in the context of an aging population increasingly in need of long-term care services.

Market Volatility and Its Impact

The article also highlights the precarious position of middle-income seniors with modest investments and fixed incomes, who stand to lose the most during periods of high inflation, exacerbated by market volatility under Trump's tariffs. This demographic, which could have previously counted on a stable financial future, now faces the risk of eroding wealth.

Conclusion: A Generation at Risk

The analysis provided by Locke, backed by expert opinions, lays bare the stark reality facing Baby Boomers in the wake of Trumponomics. Once beneficiaries of America's economic golden age, they now stand on the precipice of uncertainty, their financial security and access to essential services under threat. This situation is a clarion call to policymakers and the public alike to recognize the potentially disastrous implications of the proposed policy changes, not just for Boomers, but for the fabric of American society as a whole. The narrative serves as a scathing indictment of the Trump administration's approach to social welfare and economic policy, urging a reevaluation of priorities to safeguard the well-being of one of the most vulnerable segments of the population.
