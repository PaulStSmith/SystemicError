Resignation Is Not Courage: Jerry Greenfield’s Surrender in an Age of Fascism

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Jerry Greenfield’s resignation from Ben & Jerry’s is being spun as a principled stand, but let’s strip away the PR gloss. What we’re witnessing is not an act of courage, but a retreat — a surrender dressed up in the language of moral clarity.

Here’s the truth: Greenfield and Cohen sold Ben & Jerry’s to Unilever in 2000. They didn’t “negotiate a sacred pact to enshrine independence forever.” They cut a deal with a multinational whose only mission is maximizing shareholder value. For decades, they collected the benefits — the money, the spotlight, the ability to call themselves conscience-driven businessmen while cashing checks from one of the largest conglomerates on Earth. And now, with the muzzle tightening around their political stances, Greenfield is out, his resignation letter dripping with self-mythology about “perpetuity” and “values.”

But here’s the bottom line: he sold. He sold the company, he sold the voice, and he sold the independence. No amount of wistful rhetoric about love and justice changes the fact that when you hand your brand over to corporate overlords, you hand over control. It doesn’t matter what clauses you scribbled into the merger agreement. The moment your mission collides with profits or geopolitical interests, the lawyers and shareholders will win. Every time.

Greenfield wants us to see his exit as noble. In reality, it’s the path of least resistance. Walking away with millions in the bank, posting a heartfelt note, and promising to carry the torch “outside” the company costs him nothing. Courage would have been staying inside Unilever’s belly, refusing to be silenced, and daring them to fire him. Courage would have been to fight until the day the jackboots stomped his face — and even then, to spit back with his dying breath. That’s what King did, what Malcolm did, what JFK did in his own way: they fought until they were gunned down. That’s what real sacrifice looks like.

Contrast that with Greenfield. He’s not being jailed, exiled, or assassinated. He’s not even losing his paycheck. He’s simply bowing out when things get hard, and writing his own hagiography on the way out the door. That isn’t courage. That’s abdication.

And abdication is the last thing America can afford right now. Because while Greenfield weeps over ice cream, fascism is metastasizing in the open. Trump is not shy about his aims: he wants to dismantle democracy, neuter the Constitution, and crown himself the American strongman. The GOP has shed even the pretense of independence — they lick his boots while corporations loot the country in broad daylight. And the MAGA faithful? They cheer their own exploitation, almost literally raped by corporate greed while screaming about “freedom.”

Meanwhile, Democrats do what they can, but every effort is throttled by a Republican party that has weaponized obstruction as a strategy. The top echelon of the Democratic Party looks paralyzed, unsure how to deal with an opposition that doesn’t even believe in playing by the rules anymore. Gavin Newsom, out in California, at least makes noise — noise about rights, about resistance, about holding the line. But noise is all it is until he has the national platform, which he won’t until 2028. Until then, he’s a figurehead of frustrated potential, shouting into the void while the machine grinds forward.

The next midterms are more than a year away, a political eternity in this climate. And there’s no guarantee they’ll even happen. Fascists don’t care for elections except as props, and Trump has already shown his hand on January 6th: if democracy doesn’t give him the crown, he’ll take it by force. If he and his cronies decide that democracy itself is expendable, don’t think for a second they won’t scrap it outright. They’ve tested the waters. Next time, they’ll come with better lawyers, more complicit officials, and a base primed for violence.

This is the landscape in which Jerry Greenfield chose to make his stand by… resigning. Think about the absurdity of it. At a time when the fight for democracy, equality, and justice demands relentless defiance, one of America’s most famous “activist businessmen” decided his boldest move was to walk away from the company he built. That’s not resistance. That’s retreat. And it sends exactly the wrong message: that when the pressure mounts, it’s acceptable to step back and salvage your comfort rather than put everything on the line.

We need to be very clear here: symbolic gestures don’t topple fascism. Press releases don’t derail authoritarianism. What stops tyranny is sacrifice — the willingness to fight, to lose, and to keep fighting anyway. The willingness to stand in the fire until it consumes you, because you know the cause is worth more than your own comfort. That’s what our martyrs taught us. That’s why King’s voice still echoes, why Malcolm’s defiance still inspires, why Kennedy’s torch still burns. They fought to the end, not until it got inconvenient.

So let’s stop pretending Greenfield’s resignation is some act of moral bravery. It’s not. It’s a footnote. If he wants to prove otherwise, he has to take the millions Unilever gave him and fund a movement, bankroll resistance, create structures that outlast the ice cream brand he surrendered. He has to turn his exit into a battering ram against the system he profited from. Short of that, this is just another rich man staging his conscience for applause.

Because here’s the bitter truth: we don’t have time for staged conscience. Trump and his enablers are building a dystopia brick by brick. The GOP has already chosen submission. The MAGA cult will happily drag the country into the abyss if it means owning the libs. And the clock is ticking toward elections that may never come. This isn’t the hour for bowing out. It’s the hour for standing tall, even when the jackboots close in.

Courage is fighting until your last breath. Courage is refusing to bend when everything presses down on you. Courage is knowing you might lose, and fight anyway. Anything less is surrender, and surrender is what the fascists are counting on.
