'Near collapse': Insider warns 'month from hell' has left Pentagon in total chaos

A month of “total chaos” has left the top echelons of the Pentagon “near collapse,” a former chief spokesman at the department and an adviser on Donald Trump’s 2016 campaign warned.

The most recent straw was the revelation that department head Pete Hegseth shared confidential attack plans on Signal with his wife and brother — just weeks after he did the same with a journalist.

But John Ullyot wrote in Politico that it was just the latest revelation in a month that has left the USA’s defense operation reeling.

“It’s been a month of total chaos at the Pentagon,” he wrote. “From leaks of sensitive operational plans to mass firings, the dysfunction is now a major distraction for the president.”

Not only is Hegseth at great risk of losing his job, wrote Ullyot, but Trump has a lot of work to do to make sure the department doesn’t fall apart.

Ullyot’s main target in his column was Hegseth’s firing or top officials last week, people he said were loyal and competent and who had become fall guys for the department's director.

“ In the aftermath, Defense Department officials working for Hegseth tried to smear the aides anonymously to reporters, claiming they were fired for leaking sensitive information as part of an investigation ordered earlier this month,” he wrote.

“Yet none of this is true. While the department said that it would conduct polygraph tests as part of the probe, not one of the three has been given a lie detector test. In fact, at least one of them has told former colleagues that investigators advised him he was about to be cleared officially of any wrongdoing.

“ Unfortunately, Hegseth’s team has developed a habit of spreading flat-out, easily debunked falsehoods anonymously about their colleagues on their way out the door.”

He added, “In short, the building is in disarray under Hegseth’s leadership.”

Ullyot voluntarily left the Pentagon last week after what he called a “month from hell.”

Our Analysis:

Pentagon Chaos: Unpacking the Disarray

The recent article detailing what has been dubbed a "month of total chaos" at the Pentagon provides a glaring insight into the disarray within one of the United States' most critical institutions. This chaos, as reported, isn't just a series of unfortunate events but a systematic failure under the current leadership, notably Pete Hegseth. The allegations against Hegseth, including sharing confidential attack plans via Signal with his non-clearance-holding wife and brother—and previously with a journalist, are not only shocking but indicative of a broader issue of incompetence and disregard for national security protocols.

Leadership in Question

Pete Hegseth's alleged actions are a stark violation of basic operational security measures, an offense that in any other scenario, or for any other rank within the Department of Defense, would likely lead to immediate dismissal or severe reprimands. However, the situation unveils a more disturbing trend of accountability—or the lack thereof at the highest levels. It's not just about one individual's recklessness; it's about a systemic failure that allows such breaches to occur.

The Fallout

The fallout from these actions, as John Ullyot, a former chief spokesman at the department, describes, has been profound. The firing of top officials—who Ullyot claims were both loyal and competent—seems to be a desperate attempt by Hegseth to scapegoat others for his inadequacies. This move not only undermines the morale and integrity of the Pentagon's workforce but also jeopardizes national security by dismissing experienced officials for what appears to be personal vendettas or cover-ups.

Smear Campaigns and Falsehoods

Ullyot's allegations that Hegseth's team engaged in smear campaigns against these officials, coupled with the fact that these individuals were not subjected to promised polygraph tests, raises serious concerns about the ethical and operational standards being upheld at the Pentagon. If true, this not only represents a failure of leadership but also a breakdown in the department's internal justice and investigative processes.

Analysis and Commentary

What we are witnessing at the Pentagon under Hegseth's leadership is not just total chaos; it's a microcosm of a larger problem plaguing the current administration. It's a symptom of a government that not only tolerates incompetence but seems to thrive on it. This situation is not merely a distraction for the president, as Ullyot suggests; it's a national security risk that could have dire consequences beyond the Pentagon's walls.

Moreover, the attempt to deflect blame onto loyal officials while engaging in a campaign of misinformation is emblematic of a broader trend of dishonesty and lack of transparency that has become all too common in recent years. The Pentagon, an institution built on discipline, order, and respect for chain of command, is now being subjected to the same level of disarray and disrepute as seen in other facets of the current political landscape.

Conclusion

The situation at the Pentagon, as described by Ullyot, should serve as a wake-up call. It's a testament to the need for a return to competence, integrity, and accountability in leadership. The security of the United States and the well-being of its defense apparatus cannot be left to individuals who, through their actions, demonstrate a blatant disregard for the responsibilities and ethical standards required of their positions. It's time for a systemic overhaul, starting with those at the top who have shown they are not fit to lead.
