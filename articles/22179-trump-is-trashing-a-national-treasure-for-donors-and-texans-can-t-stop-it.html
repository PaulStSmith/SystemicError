Trump is trashing a national treasure for donors —and Texans can't stop it

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

Big Bend Is Not a Border Problem. It Is a Power Demonstration.

A Wall Built on a Lie

The reported facts are straightforward: construction began on Trump’s border wall through Big Bend National Park, over local opposition, on a stretch of border that accounts for only 1% of annual U.S.-Mexico crossings and already has natural barriers that make a wall redundant. The project will bulldoze habitat, sever wildlife routes, and drain money from a chronically underfunded National Park Service.

That is the story. Everything else is camouflage.

Who Actually Decided

Trump holds the institutional power here. Not the conservationists, not the Texas business owners, not the park, not the species whose habitat is being carved apart. The decision is top-down, federally funded, and pushed through by executive force. The contractor does not set the purpose; it cashes the check. The locals object; the state’s ecosystems absorb the damage.

This is what authoritarian governance looks like in plain daylight: a distant executive converts public land into a stage prop and calls it security.

National Security as Cover

The wall is sold as border defense in a place where the border is already difficult terrain and illicit traffic is minimal. That matters. When a policy has no meaningful connection to the problem it claims to solve, the policy is not mistaken. It is performative.

The administration’s environmental waiver makes the fraud even clearer. The source describes it as legally suspect because the waiver authority is supposed to be limited to high-entry areas. Big Bend is the opposite of that. The point is not enforcement. The point is prerogative: do something visibly brutal, then wrap it in patriotic language.

The Contractor State

The money trail is not incidental. Southwest Valley is a subsidiary of Kiewit Corp, and the source notes Kiewit is a Trump donor. That is the real infrastructure of the project: public land converted into private revenue through a political machine that rewards loyalty and monetizes destruction.

This is not a border policy with some corruption around the edges. It is corruption as method. Public authority creates a fake emergency, private firms collect the payout, and the landscape is left to pay the bill for decades.

Misdirection as Governance

The source rightly mocks Trump’s incompetence, but incompetence alone is too small a frame. What matters is that harm on this scale does not require competence. It only requires power, impunity, and a political culture trained to confuse spectacle with seriousness.

The wall in Big Bend is a demonstration that the state can spend billions to solve a problem that barely exists, destroy a protected landscape, and still call itself rational. That is not a policy failure. It is institutional contempt for evidence, law, and stewardship.

The Larger Pattern

Big Bend is one more entry in the same governing logic: manufacture a threat, punish the symbolic target, route money to allies, and leave ordinary people to absorb the damage. The form changes, but the structure does not. Whether the target is a park, a river, a civil right, or a public institution, the method is the same: plunder first, justify later.

Democracy does not fail only when ballots disappear. It also fails when elected power is used to vandalize the commons in broad daylight and call it leadership.
