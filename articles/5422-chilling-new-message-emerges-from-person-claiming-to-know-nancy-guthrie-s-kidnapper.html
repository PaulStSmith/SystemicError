Chilling new message emerges from person claiming to know Nancy Guthrie's kidnapper

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 13, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The FBI Tuesday released an image of a suspect in the Nancy Guthrie kidnapping. (FBI)

An unsettling new message was sent to TMZ on Thursday by a person claiming to know who kidnapped Nancy Guthrie.

TMZ previously received an email on Wednesday asking for 1 bitcoin, worth about $65,000, in exchange for revealing the person responsible for Guthrie’s disappearance. On Thursday, it received a second message — and this time it contained a more chilling tone. No activity or deposit has been made in the Bitcoin account associated with the request.

“Just after 8 AM Pacific Time Thursday ... TMZ received a second email from the same person, who complained, ‘I am not being taken seriously.’ He makes some ominous statements in the email ... TMZ is cooperating with law enforcement by not disclosing specifics. One thing he makes clear ... the situation has changed from Wednesday to Thursday,” the outlet reported.

The person in the email warned that this would be their last attempt to help, TMZ reported.

“As for why he wants a bitcoin ... he says he will need money to lay low after identifying the kidnapper for fear of retaliation. He also worries he might be ‘incriminated like that Carlos [the deliveryman who was detained and released] guy,’” according to TMZ.

Guthrie, 84-year-old mother of “Today” host Savannah Guthrie, went missing from her Tucson, Arizona, home sometime between late Jan. 31 and early Feb. 1. She was last seen following dinner with her family.

The Pima County Sheriff’s Department’s tip line has received more than 18,000 tips since she went missing.

The FBI this week released footage and images of an armed suspect on Guthrie’s porch wearing a ski mask, gloves, jacket and backpack and tampering with her Nest camera.

Investigators have asked neighbors in the area to check their home security footage for two specific days and times, sharing an alert via Ring’s Neighbor App, according to CNN.

“January 11 between 9 p.m. and midnight, more than two weeks before the 84-year-old went missing, and January 31 between 9:30 and 11, flagging a suspicious vehicle in the area around 10 a.m.,” according to the alert.

Our Analysis:

A Disturbing Dance with Digital Demands: The Kidnapping of Nancy Guthrie

In what reads like a script torn from the darkest corners of the internet’s underbelly, the Nancy Guthrie kidnapping saga unfolds with a mixture of modern cyber intrigue and age-old criminal bravado. At the heart of this unsettling narrative is an anonymous individual’s brazen attempt to exploit human fear and desperation for a hefty sum of digital currency, specifically, 1 bitcoin, valued at approximately $65,000. This situation not only highlights the evolving tactics of criminals in the digital age but also casts a glaring light on the media’s precarious role in such crises.

The Bait: A Demand for Bitcoin

The initial contact made to TMZ, a popular entertainment news outlet, sets the stage for this sordid affair. An individual, cloaked in the anonymity afforded by the internet, claims knowledge of the whereabouts or details concerning the disappearance of Nancy Guthrie, the 84-year-old mother of “Today” show host Savannah Guthrie. The price for this information? A single bitcoin. This choice of ransom is telling; it underscores the kidnapper’s (or informant’s) understanding of the anonymity and difficulty in tracing cryptocurrency transactions, a favorite among modern criminals.

The Twist: A Chilling Follow-Up

By the second communication, the tone escalates. The sender expresses frustration at not being taken seriously, hinting at a more ominous development if their demands are not met. This progression from an initial demand to a veiled threat is a classic escalation in kidnapping and extortion cases, yet here it unfolds in the public eye, mediated by a news outlet and the nebulous nature of email communication.

The Media’s Murky Waters

TMZ’s involvement is a double-edged sword. On one hand, their cooperation with law enforcement by withholding specific details from the public eye is commendable. On the other, the mere publication of these demands and the kidnapper’s messages risks emboldening the perpetrator, offering them the notoriety they might crave or encouraging others to mimic such tactics. The ethical tightrope walked by media outlets in these situations is fraught with potential missteps.

Law Enforcement’s Digital Dragnet

The FBI’s release of footage showing a masked suspect tampering with Guthrie’s home security system marks a critical turn in the investigation, leveraging public assistance in the digital age. The request for neighbors to review their security footage is a modern take on community policing, an effort to crowdsource clues from a neighborhood’s digital eyes.

A Reflection on Our Times

This case is emblematic of the challenges and changes in criminal behavior in the digital era. The use of bitcoin as ransom, the exploitation of media platforms for communication with authorities, and the reliance on home security footage for leads all underscore the digital transformation of crime and its investigation.

Yet, the core of this story remains tragically timeless: the anguish of a family missing a loved one, the desperation of authorities to find leads, and the societal fascination with a crime that seems both outlandishly modern and eerily familiar. As the investigation continues, one hopes for a resolution that brings Nancy Guthrie safely home and offers some reflection on the ways our interconnected, online existence can both complicate and aid in the resolution of such deeply human crises.
