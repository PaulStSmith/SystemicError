A revealing photo from Attorney General Pam Bondi’s folder that she kept on hand to insult lawmakers at a Wednesday testimony suggested she was using federal resources to snoop lawmakers’ internet search history.

The Independent is now reporting that House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) wants her to stop.

A photographer captured a page out of Bondi’s briefing binder that appeared to show a document detailing the files that Rep. Pramila Jayapal (D-Wash.) had searched on a DOJ computer. The photo of Jayapal’s apparent search in Bondi’s so-called “burn book,” caused outrage among members of Congress.

“We’re out there trying to do our investigative oversight, and they’ve structured a situation where they’re basically spying on our every move,” Judiciary Committee ranking member Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-Md.) told The Independent. “So the attorney general knows what material we’ve looked at. I would have liked to have known what material she was looking at before she came in.”

Even House Speaker Mike Johnson, an unwavering ally to the administration of President Donald Trump, took issue with the idea of Bondi using federal resources to procure kompromat to retaliate during the Wednesday hearing.

“I’ve heard the allegations I’m looking into that myself,” Johnson told The Independent on Thursday. “It would obviously be an important line that’s crossed, and obviously we can’t allow for that.”

Bondi delivered few answers but considerable acrimony during the combative Wednesday testimony before the Judiciary Committee. Much of the back and forth collapsed into personal attacks and sniping against Democrats. And despite her inside knowledge pertaining to the survivors of Epstein’s abuse in her opening remarks, when victims stood during the hearing for an apology, Bondi did not even turn to face them. Instead she told the committee chairman that apologizing to the victims would be akin to getting “into the gutter” or participating in “theatrics.”

One Epstein victim, Danielle Bensky — who was assaulted by Epstein when she was 17 years old, told reporters that Bondi was “just gaslighting the American people over and over by not answering direct questions.”

“[F]or me it was pure rage,” said Bensky. “... I could feel myself shaking with anger and frustration because it’s bad enough that the redaction process was what it was. I feel like we’ve been re-victimized over and over again.”

Our Analysis:

The Bondi “Burn Book” Saga: A New Low in Political Pettiness

In an era where political theater often overshadows substantive policy debate, Attorney General Pam Bondi’s recent escapade stands out as a particularly disheartening spectacle. A photograph, seemingly innocuous yet profoundly revealing, has unearthed a sordid tale of pettiness, privacy invasion, and the unabashed misuse of federal resources. This isn’t just another day at the office; it’s a glaring symptom of a political system in dire need of introspection and reform.

The Unveiling of a “Burn Book”

The photograph in question, captured during a Wednesday testimony, laid bare the contents of Bondi’s briefing binder. Among various documents was one that seemed to detail the internet search history of Rep. Pramila Jayapal (D-Wash.) on a DOJ computer. This discovery, reminiscent of a high school drama rather than a dignified political proceeding, sparked understandable outrage among lawmakers. The term “burn book,” borrowed from teenage slang, aptly describes the juvenile intent behind this dossier. Yet, the implications are anything but juvenile.

A Clear Violation of Trust and Privacy

The reaction from both sides of the aisle was swift and severe. Judiciary Committee ranking member Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-Md.) expressed his dismay, highlighting the chilling effect such surveillance has on legislative oversight. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.), despite his allegiance to the Trump administration, acknowledged the grave nature of the allegations. His concern underscores a fundamental question: Where do we draw the line?

Bondi’s actions, as alleged, represent more than a breach of etiquette; they signify a gross misuse of federal resources and a brazen invasion of privacy. In her quest to arm herself with kompromat against political adversaries, Bondi appears to have crossed a line that even her allies find indefensible.

The Theater of the Absurd: Bondi’s Testimony

The actual testimony, as reported, devolved into a farce of personal attacks and evasion. Bondi’s refusal to apologize to the victims of Epstein’s abuse, under the guise of avoiding “theatrics,” is particularly galling. This stance is not just tone-deaf; it’s a calculated insult to the survivors and a blatant display of political cynicism.

Danielle Bensky’s words resonate with a raw, unfiltered truth that Bondi’s political machinations can never diminish. The feeling of re-victimization Bensky describes is a damning indictment of Bondi’s callous approach to her office and the individuals it impacts.

The Path Forward

This incident lays bare the systemic rot that can pervade the corridors of power. It’s a stark reminder of the need for vigilance, accountability, and a return to a politics that prioritizes people over petty vendettas. Bondi’s “burn book” is not just a personal failing; it’s a systemic error that demands correction.

In a functioning democracy, there is no room for the weaponization of personal information against political opponents. It’s high time our political institutions reflect that principle, not just in word but in deed. The Bondi debacle should not just fade into the annals of political infamy; it should spur us to demand better from those in power.
