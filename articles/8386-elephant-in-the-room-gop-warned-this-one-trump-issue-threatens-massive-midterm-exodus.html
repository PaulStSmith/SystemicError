The midterm elections could swing massively in favor of Democrat candidates, and it’s because of an issue with Donald Trump the GOP have not addressed.

Greg Sargent of The New Republic and Amanda Marcotte believe the problem of age will weigh heavy on the election cycle next year. Trump, 79, is “not looking good these days” according to Marcotte, who says the “elephant in the room” of the 2026 midterms is the president’s age.

She said, “I will not be surprised if turnout is really high in the midterms. And that’s all before you even get into the fact that the elephant in the room is that Donald Trump is 79 years old, and he is not looking good these days.”

“And I think that his ability to be a strongman leader of the MAGA cults kind of depends on him looking like someone who’s going to survive for the next few years. I think there’s a bet that increasingly few people are interested in taking.”

This is an issue that is, according to Sargent, starting to register with “a lot of MAGA figures.” Sargent said, “A lot of MAGA figures are now questioning Trump’s strength and his ability to hold the movement together. And that’s its own sort of death knell.”

Trump has struggled to bring the GOP back together after the party found itself split on economic issues and healthcare. The economic issue, Marcotte believes, is what is affecting Trump’s second term most of all.

She said, “I do think that the economy is really… I mean, even Trump has been persuaded, obviously, that he should be worried about the bad economic news because he’s trying to stick to talking points. He fails.”

“But it’s clear that whoever sat him down and said, this affordability issue is going to hurt you, got through to him because he’s trying to use the word, even though, you know, affordability sounds as weird in his mouth as saying please or thank you. Our Analysis:

The Age Factor: Trump’s Achilles’ Heel in the 2026 Midterms?

In a recent analysis by Greg Sargent of The New Republic and Amanda Marcotte, a rather unconventional specter looms over the upcoming 2026 midterm elections: the age and physical robustness of Donald Trump. At 79, Trump’s vitality—or the apparent lack thereof—seems to be causing ripples of concern not just among political analysts but within the ranks of his own MAGA base.

Trump’s Image: The Strongman Falters?

Marcotte’s observations are nothing short of scathing. She points out the “elephant in the room”—Trump’s age and health, which might be starting to chip away at his strongman image. The implication here is that Trump’s physical condition could be a deal-breaker for voters who idolize him for his vigorous, albeit divisive, leadership style. It’s a poignant critique; after all, the cult of personality surrounding Trump has always hinged on his larger-than-life persona. If cracks are showing in that facade, what does it mean for his electoral prospects?

MAGA’s Wavering Confidence

Sargent notes a growing sentiment among MAGA figures, hinting at a crisis of confidence in Trump’s ability to galvanize the movement as he once did. It’s telling when your own camp starts questioning your strength and cohesiveness as a leader. This isn’t just idle gossip—it’s a fundamental challenge to Trump’s grip on his base and, by extension, the GOP.

Economic Anxieties: Trump’s Kryptonite

Beyond the issue of age and health, Trump’s handling of economic issues is under the microscope. Marcotte astutely points out Trump’s awkward attempts to address concerns of affordability, suggesting a deep disconnect between his billionaire’s perspective and the everyday struggles of American voters. It’s a critical observation: Trump, despite his financial ups and downs, cannot genuinely empathize with the economic anxieties of the electorate. His purported alienation from the reality of ‘affordability’ speaks volumes about his inability to reconcile his wealth with the experiences of the average voter.

Conclusion: A House Divided?

The article paints a picture of a leader and movement at a crossroads. Trump’s age and health, coupled with his questionable handling of key economic issues, could significantly impact the GOP’s unity and electoral fortunes in the midterms. While age should not be the sole criterion for leadership, in Trump’s case, it exacerbates existing fissures within the party and among his base.

The real takeaway here? The GOP’s future prospects may hinge less on external challenges and more on their internal contradictions and the looming question of leadership viability. Trump’s situation exemplifies a broader dilemma: Can a party rally behind a leader who appears increasingly disconnected from both his base and the practical realities facing voters? Only time—and the 2026 midterms—will tell.
