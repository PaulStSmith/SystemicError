Discouraged Republicans surrender a once hotly-contested political battleground: report

Once a fiercely-contested state supreme court that embroiled the whole state of Wisconsin, Republicans appear to have given up on the possibility of reclaiming the court majority and influencing Wisconsin policy.

Now with Democrats holding the court majority and striking down Republican gerrymandered state maps, the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reports conservative Chief Justice Annette Ziegler will not seek a third term in 2027, she announced Monday.

“After three decades on the bench, now is the right time for me to step away to spend more time with my husband, kids and grandkids,” Ziegler said in a statement.

In 2007, Ziegler beat attorney Linda Clifford for her seat on the Supreme Court in a race that cost about $6 million. The Sentinel reports that, at the time, that was the funding record for a court race.

But last year, billionaire Elon Musk flooded the Wisconsin supreme court election with money, even going so far as to hand out two million-dollar checks to petition signers just days before voters went to the polls. During a Fox News interview, Musk predicted that if the liberal candidate won against his conservative champion, it would be a precursor to Democrats taking back the U.S. House of Representatives and having “nonstop impeachment hearings and subpoenas.“

But Musk came up empty after Dane County Judge Susan Crawford defeated conservative Waukesha County Circuit Court Judge Brad Schimel in April 2025 — in perhaps one of the first backlash votes against President Donald Trump last year.

The Sentinel reports that three days after securing their fledgling majority, the court’s four liberal justices voted to limit Ziegler’s power as chief justice – a move she called at the time “an attempt to gut” her constitutional authority by “rogue justices.”

Last year, Ziegler — now in the court’s minority — argued that the use of a more balanced election map would violate the U.S. Constitution. She cited a 2022 Supreme Court decision, Moore v. Harper, for the proposition that state courts’ role in congressional redistricting is “exceedingly limited.”

But Slate writer Mark Stern walloped that argument, reporting that “Moore said no such thing. That quotation appears nowhere in the ruling. To the contrary, Moore held the opposite, concluding that state courts can play a legitimate, meaningful role in congressional redistricting.”

Stern added that the Badger state’s electorate is split almost evenly between Republicans and Democrats. Yet in 2024, it elected six Republicans and just two Democrats to the House of Representatives thanks to the heavily Republican-favored gerrymandered map it was using at the time.

The increasingly anti-Trump environment in Wisconsin may be coupling with the exorbitant costs of court elections and deterring conservative candidates, even incumbents like Ziegler.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Fall of a Republican Bastion in Wisconsin’s Supreme Court

The political landscape in Wisconsin has undergone a seismic shift, marking a significant pivot in the state’s judiciary power balance. The recent developments within the Wisconsin Supreme Court epitomize the crumbling of Republican dominance, a narrative that should be dissected with the precision of a scalpel, given its profound implications on the state’s policy trajectory.

Republicans Wave the White Flag

In what can only be described as a concession speech without the formalities, Republicans have seemingly surrendered their ambition to reclaim majority control of the Wisconsin Supreme Court. This is not merely a retreat; it’s an admission of defeat in a battleground they once fiercely contested. Chief Justice Annette Ziegler’s decision to not seek a third term in 2027 is the most tangible manifestation of this new reality. Ziegler, a conservative linchpin on the court, cites personal reasons for her departure. However, the timing and context suggest a strategic withdrawal from a lost battle.

The Musk Effect: A Billionaire’s Failed Gambit

Elon Musk’s foray into Wisconsin’s Supreme Court election reads like a political thriller, complete with million-dollar checks and grandiose predictions. Yet, the outcome was a stark reminder that money cannot always sway the scales of justice or public opinion. Musk’s intervention, characterized by the distribution of two million-dollar checks and bold forecasts on Fox News, ended in a spectacular failure. His conservative pick was defeated, and his prophecy of a Democratic resurgence materializing through nonstop impeachment hearings and subpoenas remains unfulfilled. This episode not only highlights the limits of billionaire influence but also signals a backlash against external meddling in state judiciary matters.

The Misinterpretation of Moore v. Harper

Chief Justice Ziegler’s argument against more balanced election maps, citing the Moore v. Harper decision, is a striking example of either a grave misinterpretation or a deliberate distortion of Supreme Court rulings. Ziegler’s claim that the decision limits state courts’ role in congressional redistricting flies in the face of the actual ruling, which affirms the opposite. This misuse of judicial precedent to justify gerrymandering underlines the lengths to which some are willing to go to maintain partisan advantage, even at the expense of democratic principles and electoral integrity.

Gerrymandering and the Illusion of Majority

The stark discrepancy between the electoral representation and the actual political division within Wisconsin’s electorate is a glaring indictment of gerrymandering. Despite a nearly even split between Republicans and Democrats, the heavily skewed maps have resulted in a disproportionate Republican representation in the House of Representatives. This artificial majority not only misrepresents the state’s political landscape but also undermines the foundational principle of representative democracy.

Conclusion: A Turning Tide in Wisconsin

The unfolding drama within the Wisconsin Supreme Court is more than a local legal battle; it’s a microcosm of the broader national struggle over the soul of America’s judiciary. The Republican retreat, Musk’s failed intervention, and the backlash against gerrymandering are indicative of a changing tide. This saga underscores a growing rejection of external influence, judicial distortion, and undemocratic practices in favor of a more balanced, representative, and equitable system of governance.

In the final analysis, Wisconsin’s judiciary upheaval is a cautionary tale of overreach, a testament to the resilience of democratic institutions, and a beacon of hope for the restoration of balance and fairness in American politics.
