Trump's claim he moved 100K books demolished by author: 'Haven't seen anything'

President Donald Trump bragged this week that his endorsement caused sales to skyrocket on a book — but even the author of that book is calling this out as nonsense, The Daily Beast reported on Friday evening.

“Trump boasted at the White House Christmas reception earlier this month that he had boosted sales for Venom and Valor: A White House Physician Assistant’s Battle for Survival in the Amazon, written by his former medical adviser, Dr. James Jones, by promoting it in a Truth Social post,” reported Erkki Forster. Jones himself, who wrote the book about a year ago, was reportedly in the audience during this speech.

“It sold about two copies, but it had a hell of a cover,” Trump said. “I put it out: ‘Great book. Bop bop bop,’ picture of this viper, coming like, boom from the White House. And his publisher calls him, said, ‘Doctor, what happened? What’s going on?’… They sold 100,000 books.”

That’s not true, Jones told NOTUS.

“I don’t think it’s a massive bump, but, you know, I’m sure it didn’t hurt anything. But I haven’t seen anything that’s excessive,” said Jones, who added that Trump never consulted with him before giving his book a boost — something that, according to NOTUS, has been a running pattern with the authors Trump promotes.

While Jones, who also served under former President Barack Obama, did not write his book explicitly about the president, all of this comes amid data that shows the general public is souring on reading about Trump. Sales of books about the president have plummeted in this term, compared to Trump’s first term.

Our Analysis:

Unpacking Trump’s Tall Tale: A Classic Case of Overstatement

In the labyrinth of political discourse, former President Donald Trump’s claims often stand out for their audacity and, occasionally, their tenuous relationship with reality. This week, we’ve been treated to another quintessential Trumpian assertion: his magical ability to boost book sales through mere endorsement. However, as reported by The Daily Beast, even the author at the center of this latest boast, Dr. James Jones, is flagging this claim as farcical.

The Claim: A Sales Phenomenon

Trump’s proclamation, made with the grandiosity we’ve come to expect, was that his endorsement of Venom and Valor: A White House Physician Assistant’s Battle for Survival in the Amazon resulted in a meteoric rise in sales—specifically, from “about two copies” to 100,000. This statement was made at the White House Christmas reception, with Dr. Jones, the author of the highlighted book and Trump’s former medical adviser, supposedly among the audience.

The Reality: A Modest Bump at Best

However, the reality, as per the author’s account, paints a starkly different picture. Dr. Jones himself downplays the so-called “Trump bump,” suggesting that while the endorsement might not have hurt, it certainly didn’t catapult his sales into the stratosphere. This discrepancy between Trump’s grandiose claim and the author’s tempered response is not just a minor blip in the vast expanse of political narratives. It’s emblematic of a broader pattern of overstatement and misrepresentation that seems to follow Trump like a shadow.

The Pattern: Unconsulted and Untrue

It’s worth noting that Dr. Jones was not consulted before Trump decided to boost his book—a running theme with authors Trump chooses to promote. This approach raises questions about the intention behind such endorsements. Is it a genuine attempt to spotlight works Trump finds valuable, or merely another avenue for self-aggrandizement, facts be damned?

The Context: Trump Fatigue?

Adding another layer to this story is the backdrop against which it unfolds. Sales of books about Trump have seen a significant decline during his current term, compared to his first. This trend might suggest a growing public weariness towards the Trump narrative, a saturation point where the once-insatiable appetite for all things Trump is now waning.

Conclusion: A Tale as Old as Time

In sum, Trump’s claim of transforming a book’s fate with a single endorsement is, at best, an exaggeration, and at worst, a fabrication. Dr. Jones’s response—measured and devoid of the excitement one would expect from such a sales spike—speaks volumes. It’s a reminder that in the echo chamber of political egos, truth often becomes the first casualty.

What we witness here is not just a story about a book’s supposed sales figures. It’s a vignette that encapsulates the broader Trump phenomenon: a blend of hyperbole, misinformation, and the relentless pursuit of self-promotion, regardless of the collateral damage to facts. In the grand tapestry of Trump’s narratives, this episode is perhaps a minor thread, but it’s one that is all too revealing of the fabric from which it is woven.
