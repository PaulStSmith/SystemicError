A GOP lawmaker on Wednesday continued to stand by Rep. Tony Gonzales (R-TX) despite growing calls for the embattled Republican to step down over sexual misconduct allegations following the grisly suicide of a House staffer last year.

CNN anchor and congressional correspondent Manu Raju asked Rep. Troy Nehls (R-TX) whether he felt Gonzales should step down. Nehls defended Gonzales and explained why he should not leave his position in the GOP majority House, especially ahead of the high-stakes midterm elections, in an interview shared by Raju on X.

“He’s not been indicted for anything. Does it look good? No, I don’t like the appearance of it. He’s got a problem. Don’t get me wrong. The optics are horrible,” Nehls said. “But I would, in no way, ever resign right now... accusations aren’t enough. If he does that, then you’ve got to give the gavel to Hakeem Jeffries. And I’m sure the Democrats would love that, I would love that, but no I wouldn’t do that for any reason.”

Raju asked, “Aren’t some things bigger than politics?”

“No, no. Not up here, not the way we do what we do in the House,” Nehls said.

Rep. Tony Gonzales (R-TX) was accused by Adrian Aviles, the estranged husband of congressional aide Regina Santos-Aviles, of having an affair with his wife. Aviles discovered the trysts in May 2024 through sexually explicit text messages between his wife and the married congressman.

According to Aviles, Gonzales “abused his power” by seducing Santos-Aviles, the congressman’s regional district director. After the affair was discovered, Aviles claims staff retaliated by isolating his wife. Santos-Aviles, 35, died by self-immolation in September 2025. Aviles now contends her death was a “cry for help” rather than suicide

Our Analysis:

The Unforgivable Entanglement of Politics and Personal Tragedy

In a display of political allegiance trumping moral compass, Rep. Troy Nehls (R-TX) has chosen to stand by Rep. Tony Gonzales (R-TX) amid a storm of sexual misconduct allegations and a related, heart-wrenching suicide. This decision starkly illustrates the degradation of ethical standards within certain corridors of power, where the retention of a GOP majority in the House seems to outweigh any allegations, no matter how severe or consequential.

The Allegations: A Closer Look

Rep. Tony Gonzales stands accused of having an affair with Regina Santos-Aviles, a congressional aide and his regional district director. The affair, uncovered through sexually explicit text messages, has been wrapped into a larger narrative of power abuse by Gonzales. Adrian Aviles, the estranged husband of Santos-Aviles, has pointed to a retaliatory isolation of his wife by staff following the affair’s discovery.

The tragic culmination of these events was Santos-Aviles’ death by self-immolation in September 2025, a drastic act that her husband describes as a “cry for help” rather than a straightforward suicide. This paints a grim picture of a person driven to desperation, potentially as a direct consequence of the alleged misconduct and subsequent institutional betrayal.

Political Defiance in the Face of Ethical Concerns

Rep. Nehls’ defense of Gonzales is disturbingly revealing. By stating, “He’s not been indicted for anything. Does it look good? No, I don’t like the appearance of it. He’s got a problem. Don’t get me wrong. The optics are horrible,” Nehls admits to the problematic nature of the situation but still deems it insufficient for Gonzales to resign. This stance is emblematic of a broader issue where the bar for accountability is set alarmingly high, provided the accused belongs to the right political faction.

Moreover, Nehls’ assertion that “accusations aren’t enough” to warrant a resignation, and his concern that resigning would hand over power to the Democrats, underscores a blatant prioritization of political strategy over ethical integrity. His response to the question of whether some issues transcend politics – “No, no. Not up here, not the way we do what we do in the House” – is a chilling admission of the cynical, power-centric machinations that dominate the legislative body’s moral landscape.

The Real Victims

In the shadow of these political games are real people suffering real consequences. Santos-Aviles’ tragic end screams of a system that not only failed to protect her but may have actively contributed to her despair. The allegations of power abuse and institutional retaliation paint a damning picture of a culture that views individuals as expendable in the grand chess game of politics.

Conclusion

The stance taken by Rep. Nehls, and by extension, those who share his viewpoint, is a testament to the systemic error within our political institutions. When political loyalty is valued over truth, justice, and human dignity, the system is broken. The case of Rep. Gonzales, as defended by Nehls, is not just about the alleged misconduct or the tragic suicide of Regina Santos-Aviles; it’s about a moral rot at the heart of our political system.

It’s high time we demand more from our elected officials – not just the adherence to the law, but a commitment to ethical behavior, compassion, and a genuine prioritization of the welfare of their constituents over the cold calculus of political advantage. Anything less is a disservice to the offices they hold and the people they serve.
