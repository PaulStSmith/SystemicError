CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale made clear Tuesday morning that President Donald Trump is “wildly overstating” when he bashes Canada.

The comments came ahead of Trump’s meeting Tuesday with the new Prime Minister of Canada, Mark Carney.

Dale took aim at the president's claim that Canada imposes high tariffs on its neighbor.

“The facts are that Canada is a low-tariff country,” Dale said. “The last international data we have from 2022, the World Bank published that Canada was 102nd on a list of 137 countries for average tariffs. It had lower average tariffs than the United States.”

He noted Trump’s claims about Canada's agricultural tariffs are also false. “[Trump] does not mention that those high dairy tariffs only kick in after a certain quantity of tariff-free U.S. exports to Canada, a certain quantity negotiated in his own USMCA are hit, and that the U.S. is not even close to hitting those maximum quantities,” Dale said.

“[Trump] also does not mention that the U.S. Department of Agriculture itself says on its website that almost all U.S. agricultural exports to Canada are tariff-free and barrier-free. So the milk stuff, the dairy stuff that exists, but again, there are exemptions. And number two, those are the exception, not the rule.”

He later went on to criticize Trump’s claim that the United States has a massive trade deficit with its neighbor to the north. “[Trump] says the number 200 billion, almost every time he talks about Canada, it is wildly overstated,” Dale said. “So the United States' goods and services trade deficit with Canada in 2024 was under 40 billion.”

Dale shortly after corrected himself, “It's almost about 36 billion, I'm sorry. So he's multiplying it several times. Now, if you only talk about trade in goods and ignore the services trade at which the United States excels, he's still grossly exaggerating it.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Misrepresentations on Canada: A Fact-Check

In a recent expose, CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale brings to light the glaring discrepancies in President Donald Trump's claims about Canada, particularly ahead of his meeting with the new Prime Minister, Mark Carney. Trump's assertions about Canada's tariff policies and trade relations with the United States are, to put it mildly, a creative reimagining of reality. Let's dissect Dale's findings and the broader implications of Trump's rhetoric.

The Tariff Tale: A Fabrication of Facts

Trump's characterization of Canada as a high-tariff villain is starkly at odds with the truth. According to the World Bank's 2022 data, Canada ranks 102nd out of 137 countries in terms of average tariffs, positioning it as a low-tariff country. This fact alone dismantles Trump's narrative, but the misinformation doesn't stop there.

The Dairy Delusion: Trump's specific gripes about Canada's dairy tariffs are misleading. He fails to mention that the high dairy tariffs he decries only apply after a certain quota of tariff-free U.S. exports—agreed upon in the USMCA, which Trump himself negotiated—is met. Moreover, the U.S. hasn't even come close to hitting those limits. The U.S. Department of Agriculture corroborates that almost all U.S. agricultural exports to Canada are tariff-free and barrier-free, making dairy tariffs the exception rather than the rule.

This omission is not just an oversight; it's a deliberate attempt to paint a misleading picture of the trade dynamics between the two nations, undermining the complexity and mutual benefits of the USMCA agreement.

The Trade Deficit Hyperbole

Trump's claim of a $200 billion trade deficit with Canada is nothing short of fantastical. The actual figure, as corrected by Dale, hovers around $36 billion—a far cry from Trump's inflated numbers. By focusing solely on the trade in goods and ignoring the services sector, where the U.S. excels, Trump grossly exaggerates the trade balance to fit his narrative of economic victimhood.

This distortion not only misleads the American public but also jeopardizes the delicate balance of international trade relations. It fosters unnecessary antagonism and undermines the foundation of mutual trust and cooperation that bilateral agreements like the USMCA are built upon.

Conclusion: The Cost of Misinformation

Trump's wildly inaccurate statements about Canada's tariffs and trade deficit serve as a stark reminder of the dangers posed by misinformation at the highest levels of government. By peddling falsehoods, Trump not only distorts public understanding but also risks damaging the intricate and mutually beneficial trade relationships that the U.S. has painstakingly built with its neighbors.

It's imperative that we demand accuracy and truthfulness from our leaders, especially in discussions that affect international relations and economic policy. Fact-checkers like Daniel Dale play a critical role in holding public figures accountable and ensuring that policy debates are grounded in reality, not fiction.

Let's not be swayed by the theatrics of political rhetoric. The facts are clear: Canada is not the tariff-heavy antagonist Trump portrays, and the U.S.-Canada trade relationship is far more nuanced and beneficial than his exaggerated claims suggest. In the realm of international trade, let's stick to the facts, not fiction.
