Trump's 'ginned up panic' threatens to backfire — and strip Republicans of vote: analyst

The SAVE ACT that Donald Trump is desperate to pass through in time for the upcoming midterms could actually be a huge help to the Democratic Party, a New York Times columnist warned the president Wednesday.

Trump has said the number one priority for the Republican Party should be pushing through the bill. If passed into law, it would mean U.S. citizens would require voter ID at the ballot box, and mail-in votes would be hindered massively.

This could affect millions of voters, according to The New York Times‘ Jamelle Bouie — who suggests those hindrances of voter ID could be more of a problem for the GOP’s voter base than the Dems’.

Bouie wrote, “Far from neutering the Democratic Party, the SAVE Act might improve its ability to win big in this year’s midterms as a result of education polarization and a stark difference in enthusiasm between the two parties.

“But Trump and his allies think otherwise, and intent matters. The point of the SAVE Act, for them, is to use a ginned-up panic over noncitizen voting to disenfranchise the tens of millions of Americans who oppose the president and who have, as a result, been placed outside the political community.

“The SAVE Act embodies Trump and the Republican Party’s astonishing contempt for the idea that a fair election is one where you can vote without being hassled by the state.”

Bouie went on to explain that, despite the GOP being hot on the prospect of passing the bill into law, it would actually hinder the voters who may place their vote in the Republican Party’s column.

“The SAVE Act would go beyond simple voter ID to impose a national citizenship requirement. Consider too the millions of American women who, upon marriage, took their husband’s last name and may need to get a new birth certificate to register to vote.

“Now, if we step back and look at the composition of the American electorate, the reality is that the SAVE Act might work against the Republican Party. Married women, and especially those who have taken their husband’s last names, are a Republican-leaning group. So are Americans without passports, who tend not to have college degrees.

“And those Americans most likely to lack the personal or civic resources to obtain documentation on short notice are the low propensity voters that put Trump over the top in 2016 and 2024 — the same voters that Republicans need in November.”

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Strategy Behind the SAVE Act

The political landscape is often a battleground of strategies and counter-strategies, with each party attempting to leverage legislation to its advantage. However, the road to political dominance is fraught with miscalculations and unintended consequences. The SAVE Act, championed by former President Donald Trump, serves as a case study in how political maneuvers can backfire, especially when the architects of such strategies misread the electorate’s composition and voting behaviors.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

Firstly, it’s essential to identify the power dynamics at play. The push to pass the SAVE Act is spearheaded by Donald Trump and his Republican allies within Congress. This group holds significant political influence, particularly within the Republican Party, and their support for the legislation underscores its importance to their midterm election strategy. However, the actual power to enact the bill lies with Congress, which means its fate depends on the legislative process, subject to the dynamics and debates within this institution.

Decision Makers and Their Motives

The decision to prioritize the SAVE Act emerges from Trump and his closest political allies. Their motive, ostensibly, is to secure the integrity of elections by imposing stricter voter ID laws and curbing mail-in voting. Yet, as the article suggests, the underlying intent appears to be the disenfranchisement of voters perceived to be against Trump’s political agenda. This strategic decision, while aimed at consolidating Republican power, is fraught with risks and may be based on a misinterpretation of the voter landscape.

Misdirection and Misunderstanding

The framing of the SAVE Act by its proponents as a necessary measure for election security cleverly misdirects from its potentially disenfranchising effects. However, the article posits that this strategy might inadvertently harm the Republican voter base more than it would the Democrats’. This assertion is grounded in the demographics likely to be affected by the stringent requirements of the SAVE Act—married women who have changed their names, individuals without passports, and those lacking the means to quickly obtain necessary documentation are highlighted as groups that have traditionally leaned Republican.

A Strategic Miscalculation?

The analysis provided by Jamelle Bouie in the New York Times challenges the conventional wisdom that voter ID laws uniformly benefit the Republican Party. By highlighting the specific provisions of the SAVE Act and their potential impact on Republican-leaning demographics, Bouie suggests that the legislation could be a strategic miscalculation. Instead of disenfranchising Democratic voters, the act might hinder a significant portion of the Republican base, thereby complicating the party’s path to victory in the midterms.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale of Political Hubris

The SAVE Act represents a vivid example of how political strategies, especially those predicated on disenfranchisement and voter suppression, can backfire. The belief that such measures will exclusively harm the opposition neglects the complex and varied nature of the American electorate. Furthermore, it underscores a troubling contempt for democratic principles—namely, the right to vote without undue hassle from the state.

In the final analysis, the push for the SAVE Act by Trump and his allies reveals a potent mix of misplaced confidence and a misunderstanding of the electorate. Rather than a masterstroke of political maneuvering, it may well prove to be a self-inflicted wound, demonstrating that the quest for power, when pursued without regard for the foundational values of democracy, can lead to unexpected and undesired outcomes.
