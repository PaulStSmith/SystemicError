'Donald certainly knew': Trump biographer reveals new evidence of intimate ties to Epstein

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump, Epstein, and the Uncomfortable Truths

Donald Trump (Reuters)

One of Donald Trump's biographers shared some intimate details on Sunday about Trump's relationship with disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein.

Michael Wolff, author of "Fire and Fury: Inside the Trump White House," joined Trump's former attorney, Michael Cohen, on his podcast "Mea Culpa" to discuss the latest developments in the Epstein saga. On Thursday, the Wall Street Journal published a blockbuster report detailing a "bawdy" letter Trump allegedly sent to Epstein for his 50th birthday.

"Epstein would often recount his experiences with women, hunting women, pursuing women, with Trump," Wolff told Cohen. "Incuding, at one point, they shared a girlfriend for the better part of a year."

Wolff's comments come at a time when Trump is attempting to distance himself from the Epstein case altogether. He has personally called MAGA influencers to tell them to stop talking about the Epstein files. Trump has also attacked parts of his base that continue to care about the Epstein files as "stupid" and "weaklings."

However, Wolff explained that Trump and Epstein knew each other very well. He added that Trump and Epstein traveled together on multiple occasions.

"Donald certainly knew what was going on at Epstein's house," Wolff said. "In fact, I have seen pictures, a set of Polaroid pictures, that Epstein would sometimes display, of Donald Trump prior to 2004 at Epstein's Palm Beach [Florida] house around the pool with young girls."

Wolff also explained that some of the people in Trump's political orbit also bonded with Epstein over their disdain for Trump.

"[Steve Bannon] bonded with Jeffrey Epstein about this, because they both had this experience, and they both thought they knew more than anybody about who Donald Trump really was," Wolff said.

Our Analysis:

Trump, Epstein, and the Uncomfortable Truths

In the latest episode of "Mea Culpa," hosted by none other than Trump's former attorney Michael Cohen, Michael Wolff, the author behind the explosive "Fire and Fury: Inside the Trump White House," sheds light on the murky waters of Donald Trump's acquaintanceship with the now-infamous Jeffrey Epstein. This conversation is not just a trip down scandal lane; it's a dive into the deep end of Trump's attempt to rewrite his history with Epstein, a man whose name is synonymous with sex trafficking and the exploitation of young women.

The Attempted Whitewash

Trump's efforts to distance himself from the Epstein saga are as transparent as they are desperate. Directly reaching out to MAGA influencers to silence discussions about the Epstein files reeks of a man scrambling to keep the skeletons in his closet. But calling parts of his base "stupid" and "weaklings" for their continued interest in the Epstein files? That's not just desperate; it's derogatory and divisive, even by Trump's standards.

Shared Histories and Bawdy Letters

The details Wolff brings to light are not merely salacious; they are damning. Trump's "bawdy" letter to Epstein for his 50th birthday, as reported by the Wall Street Journal, is the tip of the iceberg. Wolff's recounting of Trump and Epstein sharing a girlfriend for almost a year, alongside their multiple travels together, paints a picture of a camaraderie built on the objectification and exploitation of women. The fact that Trump was allegedly seen in Polaroids at Epstein's Palm Beach house, surrounded by young girls, only deepens the shadow cast over his character.

The Bannon Connection

Even more intriguing, or perhaps disturbing, is the bond formed between Steve Bannon and Jeffrey Epstein over their mutual disdain for Trump. Their alliance, built on the back of shared secrets about who Trump "really was," adds another layer of complexity to the narrative. It suggests a world where political and personal alliances are as interchangeable as they are insidious, bound together by shared secrets rather than shared values.

The Uncomfortable Conclusion

This episode of "Mea Culpa," with Wolff's insights, doesn't just add chapters to the sordid saga of Trump's association with Epstein; it forces us to confront uncomfortable truths about the nature of power, influence, and the societal structures that allow men like Epstein and Trump to thrive.
