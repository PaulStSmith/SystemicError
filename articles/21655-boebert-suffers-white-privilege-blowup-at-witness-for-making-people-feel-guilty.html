Boebert suffers 'white privilege' blowup at witness for 'making people feel guilty'

The Right Is Not Policing History. It Is Purging It.

The Target Was Never Just Anthea Hartig

At a House Oversight subcommittee hearing, Rep. Lauren Boebert attacked Smithsonian National Museum of American History Director Anthea Hartig for supposedly making white people “feel guilty” and for using taxpayer funds to “divide our country by racism and guilt.” The exchange did not happen in a vacuum. It followed more than a year of Trump administration pressure on the Smithsonian, including a March 2025 executive order directing Vice President JD Vance to strip “divisive race-centered ideology” from Smithsonian museums.

That is the real story: not one lawmaker’s rant, but a coordinated attempt by state power to discipline a public institution into ideological obedience.

Who Actually Has Power

Boebert played interrogator, but she was not freelancing. The institutional power sits with the Trump White House, the Republican-controlled oversight machinery, and the federal leverage that comes with both. The White House report titled Saving America’s Story named Hartig dozens of times and branded her an activist with an agenda supposedly hostile to the museum’s “founding purpose of fostering patriotism.”

That language is not neutral oversight. It is administrative warfare. It defines the terms of acceptable history in advance, then sends politicians in to stage the outrage.

Patriotism as Sanitization

The accusation here is revealing. Boebert did not argue that the museum was inaccurate. She argued that history itself should not produce discomfort in white audiences. That is the demand: strip the record of conflict, race, domination, and consequence so the national story can remain flattering to those already centered in it.

Hartig’s answer was plain enough: she said she was using “the power of history” and cared about “the full richness of the American past.” That should be the baseline for a national museum, not something to defend under interrogation. If telling the truth about the country counts as ideological contamination, then the ideology is not in the museum. It is in the chair of the committee.

Misdirection As Strategy

Boebert also dragged abortion into the hearing, demanding to know where the museum’s compassion was for “the babies that were killed.” Hartig refused to follow her into that diversion. She said twice that she was not there to discuss it.

That exchange matters because it shows the method. When the right cannot win on the substance of public history, it switches to moral theater, grievance bait, and whatever unrelated suffering can be weaponized for spectacle. The point is not coherence. The point is to break the frame, flood the room, and turn a public institution into a stage for punishment.

The Pattern

This is the same authoritarian habit in different clothing: attack experts, redefine public truth as bias, and force institutions to choose between honesty and survival. The Smithsonian is being treated not as a repository of national memory but as a suspect that must prove loyalty. That is how authoritarian politics works in practice. It does not merely censor; it recodes censorship as patriotism and calls the purge “restoring balance.”

If Democrats want to understand the fight, they should start there. The issue is not whether museums should be more “inclusive” in the abstract. The issue is whether the state gets to dictate which parts of history can remain visible when they make power uncomfortable.

The Larger Lesson

The story is not about one museum director being grilled by one Congresswoman with a political grudge. It is about a governing coalition using federal authority to enforce amnesia. The target is public memory because public memory can still interrupt lies. That is why they are coming for it.
