'You're going in circles!' Republican's tweet hurled back at his face as CNN gets heated

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

CNN Anchor Brianna Keilar challenged Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) during a live broadcast over his response to Jimmy Kimmel’s suspension and confusing claims about free speech. (Screenshot/CNN)

A CNN anchor blasted a Republican on Thursday during a heated exchange over free speech in the aftermath of Jimmy Kimmel's show getting pulled indefinitely, asking, "Who do you want free speech for?"

Brianna Keilar pressed Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) during a live broadcast, responding to his comments over Kimmel's suspension and calling him out for "going in circles" around the topic and the First Amendment.

"Is this free speech? But only for the right? No free speech for the left? Do you want free speech for all? Who do you want free speech for and why should it be limited, considering the First Amendment is very clear that it's for everyone?" Keilar asked.

Moore had tried to argue that companies can make decisions "to take voices down based on what they're saying" and revenue, then tried to make a comparison to social media companies and their algorithms.

Keilar mentioned that in 2022, he talked about it on X, sharing what he said in the past.

"You said on then Twitter, now X, 'big of their left wing CEOs. Speech isn't truly free if the left dictates every word, now the left is losing their minds that Elon Musk will allow free speech.'"

"Why do you think it's a business decision, considering they haven't said it was?" She pressed.

She called him out for referencing the company's decision, which has not been announced. He also tried to cite the ratings and referred to it as a "business decision."

Keilar talked about the FCC Chair Brendan Carr's stern threat in a podcast interview on Wednesday, pushing back again on Moore's claims.

"That's just not believable," she added.

Carr threatened immediate action against Kimmel, ABC and Disney over his satirical comments Monday night during his show, claiming he deliberately misled the public and said "it appears to be some of the sickest conduct possible" in the wake of conservative influencer Charlie Kirk's killing. He also said that Kimmel should be suspended and that ABC should lose its broadcast license.

Hours later, Kimmel was suspended.

Our Analysis:

The Free Speech Conundrum: A Dissection of Hypocrisy

In a recent fiery exchange on CNN, we witnessed a classic spectacle of political evasion and hypocrisy that merits a scathing analysis. The confrontation between CNN anchor Brianna Keilar and Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) over the suspension of Jimmy Kimmel's show casts a glaring spotlight on the perennial debate over free speech, its beneficiaries, and its supposed guardians.

The Irony of "Free Speech for All"

First and foremost, let's dissect the irony dripping from Rep. Moore's stance. Keilar's pointed question, "Who do you want free speech for?" slices through the facade of Moore's argument with surgical precision. It's a question that should echo in the halls of Congress: Who indeed? Is the sanctity of free speech a selective principle, reserved only for those voices that echo the corridors of power and privilege?

Rep. Moore's argument, suggesting that companies can "take voices down based on what they're saying and revenue", is a thinly veiled endorsement of censorship when it suits one's political agenda. His attempt to compare this situation with social media companies and their algorithms is a red herring, diverting attention from the core issue at hand.

Selective Outrage and the Business Decision Mirage

Moore's selective outrage over Kimmel's suspension, while simultaneously celebrating Elon Musk's takeover of Twitter (formerly X) as a victory for free speech, is hypocrisy at its finest. His past comments, as highlighted by Keilar, reveal a disturbing trend of championing free speech only when it aligns with his political views. This selective advocacy undermines the very foundation of the First Amendment, which does not discriminate based on ideology.

Furthermore, Moore's insistence on framing Kimmel's suspension as a "business decision" without evidence is intellectually dishonest. Keilar's skepticism towards this claim is well-founded. The lack of transparency from the company regarding the decision only fuels speculation and doubt about the true motives behind Kimmel's suspension.

The FCC's Overreach and the Threat to Free Speech

The involvement of FCC Chair Brendan Carr in this saga adds another layer of concern. Carr's stern threat of immediate action against Kimmel, ABC, and Disney for the comedian's satirical comments reveals an alarming willingness to wield government power to suppress dissenting voices. This is not merely about a company's decision to suspend a show; it's about the potential for governmental overreach to silence satire and criticism under the guise of protecting the public.

Carr's assertion that Kimmel's comments constitute "some of the sickest conduct possible" is not only an overreaction but a dangerous precedent for evaluating speech. The swift suspension of Kimmel following Carr's threats raises questions about the independence of media entities and their vulnerability to political pressure.

Conclusion: A Call for Genuine Free Speech Advocacy

This episode serves as a stark reminder of the fragility of free speech in an era of political polarization and selective outrage. The principle of free speech must be defended universally, not selectively based on political convenience.

As we dissect the layers of hypocrisy and evasion in Moore's arguments and the broader political reaction to Kimmel's suspension, it's clear that the battle for free speech is ongoing. It requires vigilant defense against those who would seek to limit it for their own ends.

In the end, Keilar's interrogation of Moore is not just about a single incident of censorship but a microcosm of the larger struggle for the soul of free speech. It's a battle that demands our unwavering commitment to principles over politics, ensuring that the First Amendment remains a shield for all, not a sword for the few.
