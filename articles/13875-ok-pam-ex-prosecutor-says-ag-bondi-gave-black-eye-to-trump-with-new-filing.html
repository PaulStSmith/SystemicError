Ex-federal prosecutor Glenn Kirschner released a new episode of his "Justice Matters" online show on Saturday, in which he "takes apart" Bondi's most recent legal action and flags "weak" spots.

Specifically, Kirschner flagged the federal government's return of the wrongfully deported Kilmar Ábrego García, who was brought back to the United States months after the U.S. Supreme Court ordered the president to "facilitate and effectuate" his return from a brutal El Salvador prison.

Kirschner says that, in order to "save face," Bondi and the Trump administration chose to charge the Maryland father with a two-count indictment involving allegations that García had unlawfully transported illegal aliens between states.

But this indictment makes a weak case, according to Kirschner, who wondered if the charges were "trumped up."

He went on to highlight what he called a major problem with the indictment, namely that it hurts the Trump administration's cause and gives it a "black eye."

That's because the indictment alleges the time frame García conducted these acts overlaps with Trump's first term.

"Trump's Department of Justice alleges in this indictment that Ábrego García and others were just running wild, this big bad conspiracy in the United States of America, transporting undocumented aliens, from 2016 to 2025," Kirschner said. "So for the four years during the first Trump administration, this robust conspiracy was running rampant in the United States and Donald Trump and his Department of Justice apparently did nothing about it."

The analyst then added, "Sounds like Trump was letting bad folk run wild in the United States for the entirety of his first term."

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Bondi Blunder and Trump's Tarnished Legacy

In an episode that could easily be mistaken for a plotline out of a political thriller, U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi has managed to cast a long, dark shadow over the already controversial tenure of Donald Trump's administration. The latest saga, as dissected by former prosecutor Glenn Kirschner, reeks of desperation, legal flimsiness, and, most notably, irony so rich it's almost palpable.

The Case of Kilmar Ábrego García

At the heart of this debacle lies Kilmar Ábrego García, a Maryland father whose wrongful deportation story reads like a harrowing journey of injustice and systemic failure. García's return to the U.S., following a Supreme Court directive, should have been a triumphant moment of correction within the justice system. Instead, it morphed into a spectacle of vindictive legal maneuvering, thanks to Bondi and the Trump administration.

A Questionable Indictment

The decision to slap García with a two-count indictment for allegedly transporting illegal aliens between states smacks of desperation. Why now? One can't help but wonder. Kirschner's assessment of the indictment as "weak" is a polite understatement. It reeks of an attempt to "save face" by an administration notorious for its hardline stance on immigration, yet here lies the monumental irony: the indictment inadvertently implicates Trump's administration in a glaring oversight, or worse, indifference.

The Timing Tells All

The indictment's timeframe is its most damning aspect. The alleged conspiracy, running from 2016 to 2025, covers the entirety of Trump's first term. This revelation isn't just a "black eye" for the Trump administration; it's a self-inflicted wound that exposes a staggering level of incompetence or negligence. Kirschner's observation that this suggests Trump allowed "bad folk run wild in the United States for the entirety of his first term" is a scathing indictment of the administration's purported law and order ethos.

Conclusion: A Legacy Undermined

This sequence of events further complicates Trump's already contentious legacy. For an administration that prided itself on being tough on crime and stricter on immigration, the indictment's implications are a stark contradiction. It's a narrative that undermines the very principles Trump loudly championed, revealing a chaotic, reactive posture to governance.

In their zeal to prosecute García, Bondi and the Trump administration have inadvertently highlighted their failures. This isn't just about a legal case; it's about how the pursuit of vindictive justice can backfire, exposing the systemic errors and moral bankruptcy at an administration's core. The irony is as unmistakable as it is tragic: in their attempt to portray strength, they've only revealed their profound weakness.
