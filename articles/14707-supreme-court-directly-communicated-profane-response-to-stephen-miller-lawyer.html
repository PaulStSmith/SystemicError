Ex-prosecutor Glenn Kirschner over the weekend published a video entitled, "The Supreme Court AGAIN Tells Trump NO UNCONSTITUTIONAL DEPORTATIONS Of Venezuelan Immigrants," in which the legal analyst highlighted a pattern in which at least seven Supreme Court justices have been standing up to the president.

Kirschner zeroed in on the most recent example, which saw seven of nine justices ruling that immigrants are entitled to due process.

According to Kirschner, that particular point was one of the most direct in the entire judicial ruling.

The passage of the opinion, he says, "sounds like it might be a direct rebuke, refutation of Stephen Miller's assertion that due process doesn't really apply to immigrants, particularly immigrants that may be here without status."

After quoting Miller saying due process isn't to protect "an illegal alien," Kirschner flags "one of the most direct assertions" in the seven-justice opinion.

"The Fifth Amendment entitles aliens to due process of law," the justices wrote. That led Kirschner to conclude, "That sure feels like the Supreme Court directly communicating 'F--- you, Stephen Miller."

"Let's take our wins where we can find them," he added.

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court's Clear Message: A Rebuke of Stephen Miller's Views on Due Process

In an illuminating demonstration of judicial integrity, seven of the nine justices of the Supreme Court have once again stood as bulwarks against the erosion of constitutional rights, this time regarding the due process rights of immigrants. The recent ruling, as dissected by former federal prosecutor Glenn Kirschner, sends a crystal-clear message, especially to figures like Donald Trump's White House Deputy Chief of Staff, Stephen Miller, who have notoriously espoused views diminishing the rights of immigrants.

The Direct Rejection of Unconstitutional Views

It's not everyday that the Supreme Court's decisions read like a direct snapback to the executive branch's high-ranking officials. Yet, according to Kirschner, this is precisely what has occurred. The Court's ruling, which underscores that "The Fifth Amendment entitles aliens to due process of law," serves not just as a legal clarification but as a potent rebuke of Miller's previously stated belief that due process does not apply to "an illegal alien."

This ruling is not merely a footnote in the annals of legal proceedings; it is a loud, resonant declaration that the principles enshrined in the Constitution extend beyond the narrow confines of citizenship as defined by the likes of Miller. The Supreme Court's message is unmistakably clear: Constitutional rights are not a buffet from which government officials can pick and choose who is deserving based on status or nativity.

The Pattern of Pushback

What Kirschner highlights, and what is perhaps most significant, is the discernible pattern of the Supreme Court's pushback against the president's administration. It's a narrative thread that weaves through the tenure of an administration marked by attempts to redefine the boundaries of executive power and the rights of individuals under its jurisdiction.

This instance of the Supreme Court standing up for the due process rights of immigrants, including those without legal status, is a beacon of hope in what has often seemed like a relentless darkening of the American commitment to justice and human rights. It's a reminder that, despite the pressures and persuasions of political agendas, the judiciary can and does serve as a check on the excesses of executive power.

A Victory, But Not the End of the Fight

Kirschner's call to "take our wins where we can find them" is a sobering reminder of the ongoing battles facing the nation. This ruling is undoubtedly a victory for the rights of immigrants and the integrity of the Constitution. However, it is also a single battle in a much larger war against a tide of policies and perspectives seeking to undermine the foundational principles of due process and equal protection under the law.

The Supreme Court's decision is a moment of vindication for all who believe in the universality of constitutional rights. Yet, it's essential to recognize that such moments are not guaranteed. They are fought for, argued for, and, in cases like this, ruled on in the face of powerful opposition.

In Conclusion

The Supreme Court's rebuke of Stephen Miller's unconstitutional stance on due process for immigrants is not just a legal ruling; it is a moral stand and a beacon of judicial independence. It reaffirms the Court's role as a guardian of the Constitution and protector of the rights of all individuals, regardless of their immigration status.

In a political era fraught with challenges to the very fabric of American democracy, such rulings are a critical reminder of the resilience of the Constitution and the enduring power of the judiciary to uphold its principles. As we move forward, let us not forget the importance of vigilance, advocacy, and the relentless pursuit of justice for all.
