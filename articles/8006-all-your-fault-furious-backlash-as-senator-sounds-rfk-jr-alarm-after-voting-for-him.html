Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), a gastroenterologist who voted to confirm Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as the U.S. Secretary of the Department of Health and Human Services in February, isn’t exactly garnering praise for his condemnation of Kennedy’s latest controversial health recommendations.

Cassidy, whose support during Kennedy’s confirmation hearings looked tentative at best due to his status as a medical doctor clashing with RFK Jr’s antivax views, ultimately ensured the controversial confirmation by the full Senate.

Cassidy co-founded the Greater Baton Rouge Community Clinic, a clinic providing free dental and health care to the working uninsured and also created a private-public partnership to vaccinate 36,000 greater Baton Rouge area children against Hepatitis B at no cost to the schools or parents.

RFK Jr’s appointed vaccine advisory committee voted to eliminate the longstanding universal recommendation that all U.S. babies receive a hepatitis B shot at birth.

This controversial decision suggests a new approach to the vaccine, which public health experts warn could lead to a resurgence in cases of the incurable virus.

On Friday, Cassidy posted on X, “As a liver doctor who has treated patients with hepatitis B for decades, this change to the vaccine schedule is a mistake. The hepatitis B vaccine is safe and effective. The birth dose is a recommendation, NOT a mandate. Before the birth dose was recommended, 20,000 newborns a year were infected with hepatitis B. Now, it’s fewer than 20. Ending the recommendation for newborns makes it more likely the number of cases will begin to increase again. This makes America sicker. Acting CDC Director O’Neill should not sign these new recommendations and instead retain the current, evidence-based approach.”

The responses to this post were fast and furious, with Meidas Touch replying, “You did this. This is all your fault. You will live with the consequences of your actions forever — and unfortunately, so will all Americans.”

Republicans Against Trump also replied, asking, “Do you regret voting to confirm RFK Jr., who’s implementing these dangerous policies?”

Popular resister account CathyNotToday said, “I watched the confirmation hearing live and was impressed with your line of questioning and thought FINALLY a Republican with a spine It was therefore incomprehensible that you STILL voted to confirm him Either you are grossly incompetent or totally morally bankrupt Or both ‘Severe Consequences: Babies infected at birth have about a 90 percent chance of developing a chronic, lifelong infection, which silently damages the liver for decades and increases the risk of liver failure and cancer’”

“You voted to confirm RFK Jr.! What did you expect to happen[?]” replied writer Sam Deutsch.

Our Analysis:

The Conundrum of Senator Cassidy’s Choices: A Scathing Review

In an almost theatrical turn of events, Senator Bill Cassidy (R-LA), who once donned the cloak of medical professionalism and public health advocacy, finds himself embroiled in a controversy of his own making. His decision to confirm Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as the U.S. Secretary of the Department of Health and Human Services—a figure notorious for his anti-vax views—has rippled through the public health sphere with a decision that could potentially reverse decades of progress in combating hepatitis B. The irony is thick, the consequences dire, and the outcry, rightfully furious.

The Ill-Advised Confirmation

Senator Cassidy, a gastroenterologist by profession, seemingly abandoned the Hippocratic Oath in favor of political maneuvering, ensuring the controversial confirmation of RFK Jr. Despite Kennedy’s well-documented anti-vaccine stance, Cassidy, who has a history of healthcare advocacy, particularly in vaccination, cast a vote that now positions him at the epicenter of a public health backslide. The decision to eliminate the universal recommendation for the hepatitis B vaccine at birth, advocated by Kennedy’s appointed vaccine advisory committee, is not just a controversial move; it’s a public health blunder of epic proportions.

Cassidy’s Attempted Backpedal

Cassidy’s recent outcry on X against the new vaccine schedule recommendations is a classic case of too little, too late. His post, highlighting his experience as a “liver doctor” and the efficacy of the hepatitis B vaccine, reeks of an attempt to distance himself from the consequences of his own actions. The fact remains: Cassidy’s vote was instrumental in empowering an anti-vaxxer to make sweeping and detrimental changes to public health policy.

Public Outrage and Accountability

The responses to Cassidy’s post on X paint a vivid picture of betrayal and frustration. Accusations of incompetence, moral bankruptcy, and direct responsibility for the impending public health crisis are not just digital expressions of rage; they are a testament to the gravity of Cassidy’s miscalculation. The reference to the severe consequences of hepatitis B infection in newborns by the account CathyNotToday underscores the human cost of this policy shift—a cost that could have been mitigated had Cassidy stayed true to his medical roots and public health principles.

The Verdict: Gross Misjudgment

Senator Cassidy’s actions and subsequent condemnation of RFK Jr.’s health recommendations epitomize a failure of judgment that transcends political affiliation. The decision to confirm a known anti-vaxxer to a position of immense influence over public health policy is not just a lapse in judgment; it’s a catastrophic oversight with real-world implications. His attempt to now position himself as a voice of reason and medical authority is not only disingenuous; it’s an insult to the very principles of public health and safety he once claimed to champion.

In the end, the saga of Senator Cassidy and the confirmation of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. serves as a stark reminder of the importance of holding our elected officials accountable for their actions and the decisions they make. The outcry from the public and the pointed questions about regret are not merely rhetorical—they are a demand for accountability and a call to remember the stakes involved in public health policy decisions. Cassidy’s role in this debacle is a systemic error that will haunt his legacy and, more importantly, the health and well-being of countless Americans.
