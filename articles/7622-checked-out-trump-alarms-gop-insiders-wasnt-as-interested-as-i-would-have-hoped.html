Checked-out Trump alarms GOP insiders: 'Wasn’t as interested as I would have hoped'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 16, 20255 min read

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump’s apparent disengagement from advice offered by pollsters and close associates has sparked speculation that he has effectively walked away from his role as leader of the Republican Party, leaving GOP lawmakers and his MAGA supporters uncertain about their path forward.

According to a Washington Post report, during a recent White House luncheon, the lead analyst from the conservative polling firm Rasmussen attempted to urge the president to follow through on his promise to “fight, fight, fight,” but felt the president was not receptive to the message.

Pollster Mark Mitchell told the Post, “To the extent to which we were talking about the economic populism message, he wasn’t as interested as I would have hoped.”

This sentiment is widespread, with the Post reporting a “growing chorus of faithful MAGA supporters who have begun raising concerns over what they see as Trump’s second-term shortcomings.”

Members of the president’s base have accused him of prioritizing foreign affairs over domestic concerns, neglecting to address the cost-of-living crisis he pledged to resolve, developing too close ties with billionaires and tech executives, and resisting the release of investigative files related to Jeffrey Epstein. The Post notes a “steady drumbeat” of reports suggesting the MAGA base may abstain from the 2026 midterms, with little indication the president is troubled by this possibility.

While White House insiders dismiss the concerns as part of the typical “cyclical” nature of media coverage, they acknowledge an increasing volume of complaints may emerge before sentiment improves for the president.

Trump is losing ground with conservative influencers, with some characterizing his second term as “underwhelming.’ TPUSA contributor Savanah Hernandez stated, “All we’ve really seen is punchy tweets, cool video edits, but really no follow-through on any of the promises.”

Hernandez elaborated, “And if he listened to his base and he was connected to us, even just through social media, you would see that the average person is still struggling to buy groceries, that the housing crisis is still on the mind of everybody, that inflation is still a really big issue, and when Americans see billions of dollars going overseas to any country, it really feels like a betrayal when we’re struggling here at home.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Alleged Disengagement: A Symptom of Broader GOP Malaise?

In what reads like a political thriller yet grounded in the grim reality of today’s GOP dynamics, the Washington Post report sketches a portrait of Donald Trump apparently adrift from the very advice and base that hoisted him to the pinnacle of American politics. Trump’s apparent disinterest in the counsel of pollsters and close associates suggests a larger, more systemic issue within the Republican Party—a leadership vacuum that leaves GOP lawmakers and MAGA supporters wandering in a directionless fog.

The Discontent Within

The claim that Trump has turned a deaf ear to the conservative polling firm Rasmussen’s lead analyst during a White House luncheon is not just an anecdote; it’s a metaphor for his broader disengagement with the economic populism that fueled his rise. Mark Mitchell’s lamentation that Trump wasn’t as interested in the “economic populism message” as hoped underscores a critical disconnect between Trump’s actions and the expectations of his base.

This growing discontent among MAGA supporters is painted in broad strokes of betrayal. Accusations of prioritizing foreign affairs over domestic issues, neglecting the cost-of-living crisis, cozying up too closely with billionaires and tech executives, and resisting the release of investigative files related to Jeffrey Epstein, sketch a presidency in its second term that seems to have lost its populist edge.

The Base’s Brewing Storm

The Post‘s report on a possible MAGA base abstention from the 2026 midterms should sound alarm bells within the GOP. It signals not just a temporary disillusionment but a potential seismic shift in voter engagement that could have lasting effects on the party’s future. That Trump is reportedly unperturbed by this prospect is perhaps the most damning indictment of his disengagement.

White House insiders’ dismissal of these concerns as “cyclical” media coverage misses the forest for the trees. It’s not just about cyclical coverage; it’s about a cyclical pattern of neglect and disengagement that has characterized Trump’s second term, according to the report.

The Influencers’ Indictment

Conservative influencers, often seen as the barometer of the base’s mood, have not shied away from expressing their disillusionment. Savanah Hernandez’s comments encapsulate the sense of betrayal felt by many: a president who seems more interested in “punchy tweets” and “cool video edits” than in addressing the tangible struggles of his constituents. Her poignant observation that “when Americans see billions of dollars going overseas to any country, it really feels like a betrayal when we’re struggling here at home” speaks volumes about the perceived misalignment between Trump’s priorities and those of his base.

Conclusion: A Party at the Precipice

What this article underscores is not just Trump’s alleged disengagement but a broader existential crisis within the GOP. The party finds itself at a crossroads, caught between the populist fervor that propelled Trump to the presidency and the apparent drift away from these core principles in his second term. The GOP’s future, much like Trump’s leadership, hangs in the balance, leaving lawmakers and MAGA supporters alike grappling with an uncertain path forward.

If the GOP hopes to recalibrate and reconnect with its base, it must reckon with the realities laid bare by the Washington Post report—a leadership that seems increasingly out of touch with the economic and social grievances of its constituents. Trump’s alleged disengagement may just be the tip of the iceberg, signaling deeper systemic issues within the party that need urgent attention.
