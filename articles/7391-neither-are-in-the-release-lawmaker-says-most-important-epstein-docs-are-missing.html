‘Neither are in the release!’ Lawmaker says 'most important' Epstein docs are 'missing'

Rep. Ro Khanna (right) appears on CNN with Kaitlan Collins (left), Dec. 19, 2025. (Screengrab / CNN)

Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) sounded the alarm late Friday night over two crucial documents he says are “missing” from the Justice Department’s release of files on Jeffrey Epstein, documents he said he knows are held by the agency and described as the “most important.”

“What we found out is the most important documents are missing,” Khanna said, speaking with CNN’s Kaitlan Collins Friday night.

“They’ve had excessive redactions, and the central questions that Americans want to know – who are the other rich and powerful men on the island raping these young girls – has not been answered.”

The DOJ released thousands of files Friday related to Epstein, which it was compelled to by law after the passage of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, introduced by Khanna and Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY). The DOJ has been criticized, however, for withholding hundreds of thousands of files, which some say is a blatant violation of the new law.

And among those withheld files, Khanna claims, are two documents that he said the law was explicitly written to apply to – a criminal indictment of Epstein drafted before he was given his “sweetheart deal” in the late 2000s, and an 82-page DOJ memo of Epstein’s alleged crimes.

“Neither are in the release!” Khanna exclaimed.

Epstein, who died in 2019 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, was first convicted of sex crimes in 2008 after receiving a generous plea deal, one that saw him plead guilty to one count of child prostitution, and despite the FBI having identified as many as 40 potential minor victims. The prosecutor who offered Epstein the deal – Alexander Acosta, Trump’s former labor secretary – allegedly said he offered the deal because he was told Epstein “belonged to intelligence” and that he should “leave it alone.”

“Thomas Massie and I explicitly drafted it to cover those two documents,” Khanna continued. “Now, I know what they’re thinking: they think it’s gonna be Christmas, they’re gonna do this release and people are gonna move on. I would just remind them, that’s what the Trump administration thought when they shut down Congress early before the August recess.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Masterclass in Government Obfuscation

In what can only be described as a déjà vu of governmental evasion, Rep. Ro Khanna’s revelations on CNN about the missing key documents from the Justice Department’s release of Jeffrey Epstein files is not just alarming—it’s a stark reminder of the deep-seated issues plaguing our justice system. The documents, which Khanna describes as the “most important,” are mysteriously absent from the thousands of files the DOJ grudgingly released under the compulsion of the Epstein Files Transparency Act.

The Missing Pieces of the Puzzle

Khanna’s frustration is palpable and justified. Among the missing are a criminal indictment of Epstein that was drafted before he was handed his now-infamous “sweetheart deal” and an 82-page DOJ memo detailing Epstein’s alleged crimes. The absence of these documents is not just an oversight; it’s a blatant disregard for the law, specifically the Epstein Files Transparency Act, designed to bring these very documents to light.

A Sweetheart Deal Shrouded in Secrecy

Epstein’s 2008 plea deal is a case study in judicial malpractice. Pleading guilty to one count of child prostitution, despite the FBI identifying up to 40 potential minor victims, Epstein’s punishment was laughably lenient. The prosecutor behind this deal, Alexander Acosta, later claimed he was pressured because Epstein “belonged to intelligence.” This statement alone should have triggered alarms and demanded transparency. Yet, here we are, years later, still peeling back the layers of secrecy.

The Intelligence Angle: A Convenient Excuse?

Acosta’s claim that Epstein was somehow protected due to his connections with intelligence agencies is as troubling as it is curious. It suggests a level of untouchability that goes beyond mere wealth and social connections. If true, it points to a systemic flaw where individuals can evade justice simply because of their affiliations. This isn’t just a failure of the justice system; it’s a mockery of it.

A Deliberate Act of Obfuscation

The DOJ’s decision to withhold hundreds of thousands of files, and specifically the documents Khanna highlighted, reeks of a deliberate attempt to keep the public in the dark. The claim that these omissions are a violation of the new law is not just a technicality; it’s a significant breach of trust. The government’s role is to serve the people, not protect the powerful at the expense of truth and justice.

A Call to Action

Khanna’s comparison to the Trump administration’s tactics of shutting down Congress to evade scrutiny is apt. It’s a playbook we’ve seen time and again: bury the uncomfortable truths and hope the public’s attention moves on. We must not let this happen. The missing documents are not just pieces of paper; they represent the unanswered questions and the unaccounted victims of Epstein’s crimes. The DOJ’s reluctance to release these documents should not be the end of the story but a rallying cry for transparency, accountability, and justice.

In conclusion, the episode of the missing Epstein documents is a grim reminder of the lengths to which institutions will go to protect their own. It’s a systemic error that requires not just acknowledgment but action. We owe it to the victims and to the principles of justice to demand more.
