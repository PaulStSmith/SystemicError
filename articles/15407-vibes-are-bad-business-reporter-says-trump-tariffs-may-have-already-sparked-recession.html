'Vibes are bad': Business reporter says Trump tariffs may have already sparked recession

The Commerce Department issued a report Wednesday showing the gross domestic product contracted at an annual rate of 0.3 percent in the first quarter, the first drop since 2022, and consumer confidence has fallen for five straight months, all of which has sparked fears of a recession.

"We were talking about the threat of recession," said "CNN This Morning" host Audie Cornish, "and I also remember covering the last president [Joe Biden], where we talked about a 'vibecession,' and it was like always this conversation about, is it real? Is it not?"

Morrow said it wasn't too soon to worry about a recession.

"It is real really hard to know whether we're in a recession [or] whether we're heading toward one," Morrow said. "You know, part of it is semantics and technical economic analysis that gets done by a committee. You know, sometimes we don't know we were in a recession until we're out of it. So the unsatisfying answer is we don't really know right now. But one thing we do know is that the vibes are bad."

The business reporter said consumers and business owners alike were bracing for economic pain associated with the president's tariffs.

"We see a lot of economic data, and sometimes that feels very remote to everyday consumers," Morrow said. "Like, you know, you might still have a job, your coffee and bagel in the morning still costs the same. There aren't shortages, you're not seeing empty store shelves. So maybe you're thinking the media has completely blown this all out of proportion, and there's the reality is that the lagging effects of trade, it takes three or four weeks for a shipment to come from China to California, for instance, which is where a lot of our imports come from."

"So you're not seeing the effects quite yet, but it is happening," she added, "in part because the the steepest tariffs that the Trump administration has rolled out came out on April 2, and then there were heavier tariffs on April 9, and as we get into May, it's going to start showing up more on store shelves and consumers are going to start seeing more effects of it."

Our Analysis:

Tariffs and the Tumult: Dissecting the Economic Anxiety

The current discourse surrounding the American economy is a whirlpool of concern, with tariffs at its vortex. CNN's Allison Morrow brings to light a chilling perspective on President Donald Trump's trade wars, suggesting a grim aftermath that could have already nudged the U.S. economy into the shadows of recession. With the Commerce Department's latest report indicating a contraction in the gross domestic product (GDP) at an annual rate of 0.3 percent in the first quarter - the first downturn since 2022 - the stage seems set for an economic tragedy. Coupled with a consistent decline in consumer confidence for five consecutive months, it's no wonder recession fears are mounting.

Vibecession vs. Recession: A Semantic Quagmire

CNN's Audie Cornish recalls the term 'vibecession' used during Joe Biden's presidency, highlighting the often nebulous nature of economic discussions. Is the economy teetering on the edge of a recession, or is it merely a collective psychological descent fueled by negative sentiment? Allison Morrow's insight cuts through the semantic fog, emphasizing the real difficulty in pinpointing our economic standing until after the fact. The term "vibecession" itself trivializes the underlying economic mechanisms at play, reducing them to mere mood swings rather than tangible, impactful policy outcomes.

The Tariff Tempest and Its Delayed Fury

Morrow adeptly underscores the delayed effects of tariffs on the average consumer. Despite the immediate repercussions felt in the economic data, the everyday American may remain untouched, at least in the short term. This disconnection between macroeconomic indicators and personal experience serves as a breeding ground for skepticism towards media reports on economic downturns. Yet, the warning is clear: the real impact of the Trump administration's aggressive tariff strategy, particularly those implemented in early April, is a slow-burning fuse that is undeniably lit.

The steepest tariffs introduced on April 2, followed by even heavier tariffs on April 9, are ticking time bombs. Their detonation, scheduled as we venture further into the year, promises to manifest more conspicuously on store shelves and, inevitably, in consumer wallets. This looming threat, Morrow suggests, is not to be underestimated.

Conclusion: A Reality Check Amidst Economic Turbulence

In dissecting the swirling concerns around tariffs and the potential for a recession, one thing becomes abundantly clear: the economic anxiety gripping the nation is far from unwarranted. While the debate over whether we are in a recession or skirting its edge continues, the adverse effects of current trade policies are undeniable. The real tragedy lies in the lag between policy implementation and its tangible impact on everyday life, creating a false sense of security that may very well lead to a harsh awakening.

As Morrow and the CNN team navigate the complexities of the current economic landscape, their analysis serves as a crucial reminder of the interconnectedness of global trade, policy decisions, and the individual consumer's reality. The specter of recession, whether fully materialized or not, underscores the urgent need for a nuanced understanding of economic policies and their long-term ramifications. It's a call to action for both policymakers and the public to engage with economic data and trends not as distant, abstract concepts, but as the very fabric of our collective future.
