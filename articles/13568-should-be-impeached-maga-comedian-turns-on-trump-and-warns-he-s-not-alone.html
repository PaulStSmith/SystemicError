'Should be impeached!' MAGA comedian turns on Trump — and warns he's not alone

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20254 min read

Comedian and political commentator Dave Smith, a Libertarian who supported Donald Trump in the 2024 elections, is calling for the president's impeachment due to his handling of nuclear talks with Iran.

Smith appeared Monday on the Breaking Points YouTube series with Krystal Ball and Saagar Enjeti.

"Donald Trump telling [Iran] to come back to the negotiating table, man, was a joke. I mean, what an impotent leader!" Smith declared. "It's like sitting after Pearl Harbor and telling FDR, 'Now is the time to go negotiate with the Japanese.'"

Smith said the time for negotiations was before Israel started trading missile fire with Iran over the theocratic republic's development of nuclear weapons.

"Listen man, I supported him this last year. I apologize for doing so. It was a bad calculation. At the time, it seemed like the right one. But, he should be impeached and removed for this one," Smith said, eliciting surprised reactions from the hosts.

"Of course, the Congress will never do it because they're all a bunch of, you know, corrupt hacks," Smith said before urging MAGA to stand up to the president.

"All of his supporters should turn on him. This is the absolute betrayal of everything he ran and campaigned on and everything that he stood for...He is going to lose his coalition over this. I don't just speak for myself when I say, there are a lot of us who simply won't go along with this."

Smith also declared that Trump should expect to "lose the deportation fight because of this."

"Right now, he needed all the political capital he could have. You have a majority support for deportations, but the minority is very mobilized and they're out and they're protesting all around the country. He needed all of his political capital for that move. So, here's what we've got: the neocons win. More war abroad — bomb the world and invite the world."

Our Analysis:

The Ironic Twist of Political Allegiance: Dave Smith's Call for Trump's Impeachment

In an unexpected turn of events, Dave Smith, a comedian and political commentator known for his libertarian views, has openly criticized Donald Trump—a president he previously supported—in a manner that reveals much about the fickle nature of political allegiances and the complex web of expectations surrounding political leaders.

The Catalyst for Discontent: Nuclear Talks with Iran

Smith's primary bone of contention lies in Trump's handling of nuclear talks with Iran. Likening Trump's call for negotiations to a weak and ineffective strategy, Smith does not mince words: "Donald Trump telling [Iran] to come back to the negotiating table, man, was a joke. I mean, what an impotent leader!" This comparison to 'sitting after Pearl Harbor and telling FDR, 'Now is the time to go negotiate with the Japanese'' is not just a criticism but a scathing indictment of Trump's perceived lack of action.

A Missed Opportunity

Smith articulates a missed opportunity for negotiations before the escalation of hostility between Israel and Iran, suggesting a proactive approach might have circumvented the current crisis. His disappointment is palpable as he apologizes for his previous support of Trump, calling it a "bad calculation."

The Impeachment Call and Political Realities

Calling for Trump's impeachment, Smith highlights a disillusionment not just with the president but with the broader political system, which he views as corrupt and incapable of holding its leaders accountable: "Of course, the Congress will never do it because they're all a bunch of, you know, corrupt hacks." This statement reflects a deep-seated cynicism about the political process and the challenges of enacting meaningful change within it.

MAGA's Dilemma and the Deportation Fight

Smith's appeal to MAGA supporters to turn against Trump underscores a perceived betrayal of the core principles that Trump purportedly stood for. The warning that Trump will "lose his coalition" over his handling of foreign policy speaks to the fragile nature of political loyalty, which can be as volatile as the issues that test it.

Furthermore, Smith predicts Trump will "lose the deportation fight" due to the erosion of his political capital, a battle that requires not just majority support but a mobilized and vocal minority to challenge it. This commentary sheds light on the dynamics of political power and the importance of public perception and support in pursuing controversial policies.

Conclusion: The Neocon Victory?

Smith's concluding remark that "the neocons win" encapsulates a broader critique of American foreign policy—a preference for interventionism that he implies Trump has inadvertently supported. This assertion that "more war abroad" and "bomb the world and invite the world" will prevail is a bleak assessment of the trajectory of U.S. policy under Trump's leadership.

In essence, Dave Smith's sharp critique not only challenges Trump's decisions on a substantive level but also serves as a mirror reflecting the broader disillusionment and ideological rifts within the American right. His commentary is a stark reminder of the complexities and contradictions that often underpin political support, illustrating how quickly admiration can turn into admonishment when expectations are not met.
