Voters 'smell a rat' as swing state's Republicans flail

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Power, Plainly StatedThe Point Is SelectionICE Is Not A FootnoteThe Framing ProblemDemocrats Need To Say MoreThe Larger Pattern

Republicans Are Not “Testing” Democracy. They Are Gaming It.

Power, Plainly Stated

The actual power in this story sits with the Republican Governors Association, Right Direction PAC, and the MAGA donors underwriting them. They are not passive commentators on a Democratic primary. They are outside actors spending nearly $2.2 million to shape the field before Wisconsin voters even cast their ballots.

That is the decision. Not analysis. Not concern. Intervention.

The Point Is Selection

The reporting makes the objective obvious: boost Francesca Hong because Republicans think she is easier for Tom Tiffany to beat in November. That is not a theory about politics at the margins. It is a deliberate attempt to influence who Democrats nominate by manipulating Democratic voters with Republican money.

Call it what it is: election engineering. The party that wraps itself in procedural sanctimony is again trying to choose its opponent instead of competing against one.

ICE Is Not A Footnote

The ad leans hard on Hong’s support for banning ICE cooperation, legal aid for immigrants, and requiring police to identify themselves during arrests. Good. Those are the parts of the story that reveal the moral divide.

ICE is not some neutral civil service that has been unfairly misunderstood. It is a deportation regime with a badge. Any serious democratic politics should move toward abolishing ICE, not trimming its image or pretending its violence can be managed with better messaging. Republican outrage here is not about law and order. It is about preserving a cruelty machine while punishing anyone who names it.

The Framing Problem

The Journal Sentinel framing, and the standard language around these buys, still soft-pedals the basic fact: this is sabotage dressed up as political strategy. “Dems smell a rat” is too polite. The rat is the spending. The motive is the spending. The target is the spending.

When a right-wing network floods a primary with deceptive or manipulative messaging, the issue is not whether the ad is technically clever. The issue is that wealthy partisan actors are trying to overpower ordinary voters and install a weaker general-election opponent. That is not democracy being tested. It is democracy being priced.

Democrats Need To Say More

Joe Zepecki and the Barnes campaign are right to call this out, but the language should be sharper than “meddle.” Republicans and their donor class are not merely meddling; they are exploiting a broken campaign-finance system to interfere in a rival party’s nomination process.

And if Democrats want to be more than the custodians of a compromised status quo, they should stop treating this as an isolated dirty trick. It is the predictable result of Citizens United, dark money, and a political culture that tolerates unlimited outside influence until it becomes impossible to ignore.

The Larger Pattern

This is what modern American politics looks like when power is allowed to buy camouflage. Donors do not just fund candidates; they shape the opponent. Opponents do not just fight for votes; they fight against engineered narratives built by people who will never face the electorate themselves.

The deeper lesson is simple: when the rules allow the wealthy to infiltrate primaries, manipulate perception, and spend millions to narrow voters’ options, the formal act of voting survives while democratic control erodes around it. That is the system working exactly as designed, and exactly why it keeps producing outcomes that serve power before people.
