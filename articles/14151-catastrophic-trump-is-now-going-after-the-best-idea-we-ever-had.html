'Catastrophic': Trump is now going after 'the best idea we ever had'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 2, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump makes an announcement regarding the Golden Dome missile defense shield in the Oval Office of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 20, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump’s push to slash federal spending could have devastating consequences for America’s national parks, with proposed budget cuts threatening to gut the Park Service and dismantle protections for the country’s most treasured landscapes, the New York Times reported Sunday.

In 2023, the latest year with available data, an estimated 325 million people visited national parks, monuments, and historic sites, spending around $26.4 billion in nearby communities. That number grew even higher the following year, with park visits reaching an all-time record of nearly 332 million, per the article by Ted Kerasote, a nature and wildlife writer.

Theresa Pierno, president of the National Parks Conservation Association (NPCA), an advocacy organization, cautioned last month that the park system "would be completely decimated" if the proposed cuts go into effect.

"Even before he took office, the Park Service was running lean, on a slim operating budget of about $3 billion. But since January, an estimated 13 percent of its staff has departed through pressured buyouts, early retirements and deferred resignations," the Times piece noted.

According to the NPCA, the Trump administration’s proposed $900 million cut to the Park Service’s operating budget could force the closure of more than 80 percent of the 433 parks, monuments, historic sites, and other areas under its care.

Such a drastic move would mark the unraveling of a system that for over 100 years has safeguarded the nation’s most iconic natural and cultural treasures. Long seen as a global example of public land preservation, the national parks represent a legacy of conservation and shared heritage that could be severely compromised, Kerasote argued.

"Mr. Trump has proposed hacking the Park Service’s operating budget by roughly 30 percent, which would be catastrophic, and transferring less visited national parks and other Park Service locations to states and tribal governments," he said.

The writer said Trump "seems bent on eroding one of the country’s greatest achievements, a national park system open to all."

He continued: "As the Western writer Wallace Stegner once put it, the national parks are 'absolutely American' and 'the best idea we ever had.'

Will the millions of Americans who cherish their parks allow Mr. Trump to destroy them?"

Our Analysis:

Trump's Assault on America's National Parks: A Legacy in Peril

President Donald Trump's latest budgetary machinations are not merely misguided; they are a frontal assault on one of America's most cherished institutions—the National Park Service (NPS). According to a recent report by the New York Times, Trump's push for draconian cuts could spell disaster for the guardians of our nation's natural splendor, potentially dismantling protections for America's most treasured landscapes.

The Staggering Impact of the Proposed Cuts

In 2023, an estimated 325 million people visited national parks, monuments, and historic sites, injecting approximately $26.4 billion into nearby communities. This figure rose to an all-time high of nearly 332 million visits the following year. These numbers aren't merely statistics; they represent a populace deeply connected to these natural sanctuaries. Yet, Trump's proposed budget cuts, slashing the Park Service’s operating budget by a devastating $900 million, threaten to sever this connection, forcing the closure of over 80 percent of the 433 parks under NPS care.

The Reality of Running "Lean"

The National Parks Conservation Association (NPCA) has sounded the alarm, with its president, Theresa Pierno, stating unequivocally that the park system "would be completely decimated" by the proposed cuts. It's worth noting that even before Trump's tenure, the Park Service operated on a "slim operating budget" of about $3 billion. Since January, the agency has seen an estimated 13 percent of its staff depart due to pressured buyouts, early retirements, and deferred resignations, further exacerbating the strain on its already stretched resources.

An Erosion of Legacy and Heritage

The proposed budget cuts are not just numbers on a page; they are a direct threat to a system that has, for over a century, safeguarded the nation’s most iconic natural and cultural treasures. Trump's plan to slash the Park Service’s operating budget by roughly 30 percent is nothing short of catastrophic, risking the unraveling of a national legacy of conservation and shared heritage. The move to transfer less visited national parks and other Park Service locations to states and tribal governments is a thinly veiled attempt to diminish federal responsibility and potentially open these lands to exploitation.

A Clarion Call to Action

The national parks are not just "absolutely American"; they are a testament to the country's commitment to preserving its natural beauty and historical integrity for future generations. They embody what Western writer Wallace Stegner aptly described as "the best idea we ever had." The question now is whether the American people will stand by as this administration attempts to dismantle this idea piece by piece.

The attack on America's national parks is an attack on America's soul. It’s a blatant disregard for the environment, history, and the countless individuals who derive solace, joy, and inspiration from these public lands. If these proposed cuts proceed, we stand to lose more than just parks; we risk losing a vital part of our national identity. It’s high time for citizens, advocacy groups, and political leaders alike to rally in defense of these national treasures. The legacy of America’s national parks must not be allowed to fall victim to short-sighted fiscal policies and unchecked administrative recklessness.
