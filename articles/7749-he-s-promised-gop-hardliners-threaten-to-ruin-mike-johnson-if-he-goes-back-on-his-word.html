'He's promised': GOP hardliners threaten to ruin Mike Johnson if he goes back on his word

By Paulo Santos

Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (Reuters)

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) finally managed to scrape together the votes to pass the annual defense authorization bill, known as NDAA. But he had to make some key concessions to Republican hardliners — and they’re threatening to raise hell if those promises aren’t kept, Axios reported on Thursday.

If the holdouts perceive Johnson as going back on his word, they could pull out of the next continuing resolution to fund the government, potentially triggering yet another federal government shutdown early next year.

“So we made a deal — and actually we talked to the secretary of state directly,” said Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL), one of the holdouts who demanded a deal. “[Our demand] either happens, or a CR goes down. He’s promised to be with us on that.”

Among the concessions Johnson made are to allow a future vote to ban members of Congress from trading individual stocks — a longtime bipartisan goal of good-government reformers across the political spectrum — and a future provision that would ban the Federal Reserve from creating its own digital currency, a longtime pet project of far-right activists. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) also secured a vote on her own Protect Children’s Innocence Act, which would prohibit certain forms of gender-affirming care for minors.

Essential to the deal, per the report, was a separate promise from Secretary of State Marco Rubio to crack down on funding from NGOs to Afghanistan that GOP hardliners believe is going to Taliban leadership.

All of this comes as lawmakers remember the weekslong government shutdown months before as Democrats demanded a solution to expiring Affordable Care Act tax credits. A vote on extending those subsidies is coming, and Republicans are scrambling to introduce their own alternative to the Democratic proposal.

Our Analysis:

The Political Puppetry of the NDAA Negotiations

In what could only be described as a theatrical display of political maneuvering, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has narrowly averted the collapse of the annual defense authorization bill, known as NDAA, by bowing to the demands of Republican hardliners. This episode is a glaring example of how policy-making in Washington is often held hostage by the most extreme elements within a party, with significant implications for governance and the public good.

Concessions: A Price Too High?

Johnson’s concessions to the hardliners are not just minor adjustments but are indicative of a broader trend where extreme ideologies dictate mainstream political agendas. Let’s dissect these concessions:

  1. Future Vote to Ban Congress Members from Trading Stocks: While this appears to be a win for transparency and ethics in government, it’s buried under the weight of far more controversial concessions. The fact that such a universally appealing reform is used as a bargaining chip reveals the skewed priorities at play.

  2. Ban on Federal Reserve Creating Its Own Digital Currency: This concession leans heavily into the paranoia often peddled by far-right activists about government overreach and economic control. It underscores a worrying push towards policies that are not just conservative but radically libertarian, potentially undermining the stability and adaptability of the nation’s financial systems.

  3. Vote on the Protect Children’s Innocence Act: This move, championed by Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), represents a dangerous encroachment of hardline conservative ideology into the rights and lives of individuals, particularly vulnerable minors seeking gender-affirming care. It’s a stark reminder of the GOP’s willingness to politicize personal freedoms and healthcare in pursuit of an extremist agenda.

  4. Crackdown on NGO Funding to Afghanistan: While accountability in foreign aid is crucial, the singular focus on Afghanistan and the assumption that funds are going to the Taliban without clear evidence smacks of xenophobia and a simplistic understanding of international aid dynamics.

The Threat of Government Shutdown: A Damocles Sword

The hardliners’ threat to trigger another government shutdown if their demands are not met is a reckless gamble with the livelihoods of millions of Americans. It’s a tactic that has become all too common, showcasing a disturbing trend of governance by brinkmanship rather than responsible negotiation and compromise.

A Reflection on the Democratic Response

As Republicans scramble to craft an alternative to the Democratic proposal on extending Affordable Care Act tax credits, it’s crucial to note the contrast in priorities. While Democrats sought a solution to preserve healthcare subsidies for millions, the GOP hardliners are fixated on pushing through an agenda that prioritizes ideological purity over practical governance.

Conclusion: A System Held Hostage

This episode is a microcosm of a larger problem in American politics: the increasing capture of the legislative process by the most extreme voices within the Republican Party. These developments should alarm anyone concerned with the health of American democracy and the well-being of its citizens. The concessions made to pass the NDAA are not just political compromises; they are concessions on the very principles of good governance, transparency, and the public interest.

In sum, the NDAA negotiations reveal a systemic error in our political machinery, where the needs of the many are overshadowed by the demands of the vocally radical few. It’s a sobering reminder that the battle for the soul of American politics is far from over.
