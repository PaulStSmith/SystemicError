Norman Ornstein, an emeritus scholar at the American Enterprise Institute, writes at “The Contrarian” on Substack that it’s time for Democrats to start impeachment proceedings against the five “monsters” in Trump’s Cabinet who are “causing death and destruction, threatening human lives and safety, destroying the rule of law and undermining American national security.”

Ornstein names those whom he says are “the worst Cabinet members in history”: Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Attorney General Pam Bondi, and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

Because of the special power of impeachment, House Speaker Mike Johnson would have no choice but to prioritize those proceedings, offering the American public greater insight into “the brutal reality that the misconduct and outrages of these monsters” have created.

They would also require Republicans to “take a stand,” one way or another on a litany of actions, including: “RFK’s deadly move to block the Covid vaccine from children and other vulnerable populations,” “spreading vile and dangerous conspiracy theories,” “firing competent health professionals,” and “stopping vital research on cancer and other deadly diseases. Also, Republicans would have to take a stand on “endorsing Gabbard outing an undercover CIA agent, eviscerating our intelligence capability, and more.”

Dr. Ornstein also calls for Democrats to create a shadow Cabinet, and suggests a shadow HHS Secretary could give a press conference, “flanked by Rep. Rosa DeLauro, Sen. Patty Murray, and other key members, outlining the case against Kennedy.”

He also calls for “shadow impeachment hearings, bringing in witnesses that include victims of the assault on our health, from measles patients to those kicked out of key clinical trials to those fired by RFK. And field hearings outside clinics and hospitals—in Republican districts and red states—with physicians, nurses, researchers, and patients testifying.”

The same template could be used by a Shadow Intelligence Director, a Shadow CIA Director, a Shadow Secretary of Defense, a Shadow Attorney General, and a Shadow Homeland Security Secretary.

Other experts have called for the United States to adopt a U.K.-style system of “shadow government,” including historian Timothy Snyder. In January, Snyder explained that the “shadow ministers ‘shadowed’ the actual ministers,” akin to U.S. Cabinet secretaries, “in the sense of following their every move, criticizing policy and offering alternatives.”

“Importantly, the shadow minister was always available to offer commentary to the press on his or her area of expertise. This greatly enriched public life. At any point a journalist, and thus the public, had access to an alternative point of view, one which was both pertinently expert and politically relevant.”

In February, Dylan Williams, Vice President of Government Affairs at the Center for International Policy, wrote: “If the Trump admin is going to neglect or eliminate entire US Government functions, Democrats should select a shadow cabinet to fill the void. Send an alt SecState to the G20, have an alt CDC Director tell us what’s really going on with Bird Flu and TB, etc.”

Our Analysis:

The Theatrical Call for Impeachment: A Critical Analysis

In an era where political discourse often mirrors the sensationalism of reality TV, Norman Ornstein's proposition for the impeachment of five of President Donald Trump's Cabinet secretaries reads like a script for the latest political drama. Ornstein, a respected figure in political science, labels these secretaries as "monsters" responsible for "causing death and destruction, threatening human lives and safety, destroying the rule of law and undermining American national security." The charged language sets the tone for a proposal that, while grounded in genuine concern, ventures into the realm of political theatrics.

Impeachment: A Tool or a Weapon?

Impeachment is a constitutional mechanism designed to remove officials who have committed "high crimes and misdemeanors" from office. However, Ornstein's call for the impeachment of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Attorney General Pam Bondi, and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem transforms this grave constitutional remedy into a political weapon. The allegations range from blocking COVID vaccines to endangering national security, painting a dystopian portrait of a government in crisis.

Yet, the proposal begs the question: Is impeachment the most effective course of action? Impeachment proceedings are lengthy, divisive, and consume the political oxygen, potentially detracting from urgent policy issues and governance. The spectacle of impeachment could overshadow the substantive policy critique and reforms that are desperately needed.

The Shadow Cabinet: A Novelty or Necessity?

Ornstein's suggestion to adopt a "shadow Cabinet" is an intriguing nod to a parliamentary tradition not native to the United States. The concept of a shadow Cabinet, where opposition members critique and offer alternatives to the government's policies, could enrich American political discourse. However, the feasibility and impact of such a system in the U.S.'s rigid two-party structure remain questionable.

The idea of a shadow HHS Secretary or a shadow CIA Director engaging in field hearings and press conferences is innovative. Yet, one wonders if this approach might further polarize an already deeply divided nation. The risk is that these shadow figures become mere political antagonists rather than constructive contributors to public policy debate.

A Dual-Edged Sword

Ornstein's proposal, while bold and thought-provoking, is a dual-edged sword. On one hand, it calls attention to the urgent need for accountability and oversight in government. On the other, it risks exacerbating political spectacle and division without offering a pragmatic path to meaningful change.

The suggestion that House Speaker Mike Johnson would have "no choice" but to prioritize impeachment proceedings is a simplification of the complex political calculations involved in such a decision. It underestimates the strategic considerations that leaders must weigh, including the potential backlash and the prioritization of legislative agendas.

Conclusion: Between Idealism and Realism

In the final analysis, Ornstein's call for impeachment and a shadow Cabinet reflects a deep-seated desire for a more accountable, transparent, and effective government. However, the path to achieving these noble goals is fraught with political and practical challenges. The proposal, while making for a compelling narrative, seems to straddle the line between idealism and realism.

As the political landscape continues to evolve, the ideas put forth by Ornstein merit consideration and debate. However, it is crucial to approach such proposals with a critical eye, assessing their practicality, potential for success, and unintended consequences. The quest for a better government is a marathon, not a sprint, requiring strategic thought, patience, and a willingness to engage in constructive, rather than destructive, political discourse.
