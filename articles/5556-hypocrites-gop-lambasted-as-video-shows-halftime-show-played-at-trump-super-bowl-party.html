'Hypocrites': GOP lambasted as video shows halftime show played at Trump Super Bowl party

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 10, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Hypocrisy of Preference: Trump’s Super Bowl Saga

Super Bowl LX - Half-Time Show - New England Patriots v Seattle Seahawks - Levi’s Stadium, Santa Clara, California, United States - February 8, 2026 Bad Bunny performs during the halftime show REUTERS/Carlos Barria TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

Despite the White House insisting that President Donald Trump would “much prefer” to watch the “All American” alternative Super Bowl halftime show, video suggests that the official performance by Puerto Rican artist and Trump critic Bad Bunny was aired “on the big screens” at Trump’s private watch party in Florida on Sunday.

Bad Bunny, whose real name is Benito Antonio Martinez Ocasio, has been the target of MAGA scorn since being announced as the headliner for Super Bowl LX’s halftime show.

In protest, the conservative advocacy organization Turning Point USA revealed that it would be airing its own alternative half-time show starring artist and Trump ally Kid Rock, a performance that White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters last week that Trump would “much prefer” to watch over the official show.

And yet, video has emerged that appears to show Trump, Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) and other attendees at the president’s private Super Bowl watch party watching Bad Bunny’s performance on the big screens, sparking widespread ridicule, Pro Football & Sports Network reported.

“Footage from inside Trump’s golf club Super Bowl party reveals the Bad Bunny half-time show played on the big screens,” wrote X user “Patriot Takes,” a popular political commentary account with more than 460,000 followers. “What a bunch of hypocrites.”

Trump apparently took time away from watching the Super Bowl to rage on social media about Bad Bunny’s halftime show performance, calling it “absolutely terrible” and “one of the worst, ever!”

Meanwhile, the “All American” halftime show alternative with Kid Rock was widely mocked for both its content and technical difficulties, being unable to be streamed on the popular social media platform X — owned by Trump ally Elon Musk — due to “licensing restrictions.”

“Not even Trump wanted to watch the lip sync extravaganza from [Turning Point USA],” wrote journalist Jeff Benjamin in asocial media poston X Monday

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Preference: Trump’s Super Bowl Saga

In an ironic twist that could only manifest in the realm of today’s political theater, President Donald Trump, who vocally expressed a preference for the “All American” alternative Super Bowl halftime show, was caught with the official performance by Puerto Rican artist and Trump critic Bad Bunny blaring on the big screens at his private Florida watch party. This incident is a textbook example of political hypocrisy, wrapped in a layer of comedic irony so thick, one might struggle to find where the truth ends and the farce begins.

“Much prefer” turns to “must watch”: Despite the White House’s insistence that Trump would “much prefer” to watch Turning Point USA’s alternative halftime show featuring Kid Rock, reality painted a different picture. Videos circulating on social media suggest that Trump, along with Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) and other attendees, watched Bad Bunny’s performance instead. This move not only exposes a gap between public statements and private actions but also highlights a deeper inconsistency within Trump’s own camp.

Bad Bunny vs. Kid Rock: The choice between Bad Bunny and Kid Rock for Super Bowl entertainment was loaded with political symbolism. Bad Bunny, a known Trump critic, represents a voice from Puerto Rico—a territory often marginalized in American political discourse. On the other hand, Kid Rock, a vocal Trump supporter, symbolizes the MAGA movement’s ideal “All American” entertainment. By opting for Bad Bunny’s performance (even if by default), Trump inadvertently lent his eyes and ears to the very representation of diversity and dissent he publicly disdains.

The spectacle of failure: To add insult to injury, the alternative halftime show that was supposed to capture the president’s attention faced technical difficulties and could not be streamed on X (formerly known as Twitter) due to licensing restrictions. This fiasco, widely mocked for its content and execution, serves as a metaphor for the broader inefficacies and misalignments within the MAGA movement. Not even the backing of Turning Point USA and an endorsement from the White House could salvage this ill-fated attempt at counterprogramming.

“What a bunch of hypocrites”: The critique from the X user “Patriot Takes” succinctly captures the essence of this debacle. The blatant disregard for consistency between public statements and private actions is a hallmark of Trump’s tenure, both as a president and a public figure. This incident merely adds to a long list of contradictions that undermine the credibility of Trump and his affiliates.

In conclusion, the Super Bowl halftime show saga is a microcosm of the larger political and cultural divides that plague the United States. It underscores the performative aspect of political allegiances and the superficiality of public statements made by those in power. As the MAGA movement grapples with its identity in the aftermath of Trump’s presidency, incidents like these serve as poignant reminders of the chasm between rhetoric and reality. The irony of Trump watching Bad Bunny’s performance, despite his avowed preference for an alternative, is a stark illustration of the duplicity that often characterizes political posturing. In the end, actions speak louder than words, and in this case, the choice of halftime entertainment spoke volumes.
