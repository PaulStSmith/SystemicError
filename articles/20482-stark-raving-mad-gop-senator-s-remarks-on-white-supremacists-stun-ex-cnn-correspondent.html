'Stark raving mad:' GOP senator's remarks on white supremacists stun ex-CNN correspondent

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

The Senator Saw a Nazi March and Blamed Democrats

Source

A white supremacist group marched through Washington, D.C. on Independence Day. Sen. Mike Lee responded by claiming, without evidence, that Democrats were responsible. Fox host Laura Ingraham pushed a similar fantasy, calling the scene “fake” and suggesting it looked like “Antifa in costume.” The story is not that these people failed to understand what they were seeing. It is that they saw it clearly enough to lie about it.

Power Chooses the Lie

The people with actual institutional power here are not the masked marchers. They are the senator with a national platform and the cable host with access to millions of viewers. White supremacist groups thrive when powerful conservatives act as their translation service, laundering public outrage into partisan deflection.

Mike Lee did not stumble into confusion. He made a choice. He used his office to redirect blame away from the movement that produced the march and toward Democrats, the people least responsible for it. That is not a mistake of perception. It is political protection.

The Point Is Misdirection

The masks, Confederate flags, and location made the message unmistakable. That is exactly why the lie matters. Lee and Ingraham were not merely making a false claim; they were trying to overwrite the meaning of the event itself. The goal was to turn a white supremacist demonstration into a generic culture-war hallucination where responsibility dissolves and the right never has to answer for what is in plain sight.

This is a familiar conservative trick: treat deliberate reactionary violence as ambiguity, then accuse Democrats of the crime. It is not accidental that the accusation lands upward at a weaker target and never inward at the right’s own ecosystem.

Republican Cowardice in Public

The source makes clear that the reaction was ridicule and rebuke, including from Adam Kinzinger, who told Ingraham to condemn the march instead of inventing costumes. That basic demand should not have to be novel: when a white supremacist group marches in the capital, the burden is not on Democrats to explain it away. The burden is on Republicans with microphones and Senate seats to admit what their movement keeps inviting, excusing, and normalizing.

Instead, Lee performed the oldest act of institutional cowardice in American politics: he saw the enemy inside the house and pointed at the door.

Why This Keeps Happening

This story is not mainly about one senator sounding unhinged. It is about a political class that cannot acknowledge the far right because too much of its power depends on flirting with it. So when the right’s open extremists show up in public, the establishment version of the right reflexively blames liberals, leftists, masks, antifa, anyone but the people carrying Confederate flags.

That is how authoritarian politics works in practice. Not just through marches and symbols, but through elite cover. Not just through street intimidation, but through the willingness of officeholders and propagandists to lie on cue so the movement never has to confront itself.

The Pattern

The pattern is simple: the right produces the menace, and the right’s institutions manufacture the alibi. Then Democrats are expected to absorb the blame, manage the fallout, and behave as though this is an ordinary disagreement among civic-minded adults.

It is not ordinary. It is a system of denial built to protect reactionary power from its own visible evidence.
