'Unparalleled incompetence and imbecility': Expert staggered by 'perilous' Trump strategy

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump’s strategy to prosecute his political opponents could backfire spectacularly, according to a former federal prosecutor.

Glenn Kirschner, the former Deputy U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia, discussed Trump’s efforts to prosecute former FBI Director James Comey on a new episode of “The Legal Breakdown” with progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen. His comments come at a time when the Trump administration returned an indictment against Comey on two counts of obstruction of justice and lying to Congress for statements he made to a Senate committee in 2020.

“This represents unparalleled incompetence and imbecility,” Kirschner said. “Those are the only words I can come up with.”

Newly minted interim U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia Lindsey Halligan, one of Trump’s former defense attorneys, is leading Comey’s prosecution. Halligan is replacing another Trump appointee, Erik Siebert, who refused to prosecute Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James, according to media reports.

Experts have noted that this move violates a 50-year-old norm that the president cannot order the Department of Justice to prosecute a political opponent.

Kirschner said he has been hearing that Halligan is struggling to find another prosecutor inside the Eastern District of Virginia to prosecute the case, which he said could open them up to court sanctions or losing their law license if the case is ruled to be a vindictive prosecution.

“The rank and file prosecutors who know what it takes to go into federal court in Alexandria, Virginia ... know what they’re getting into, which is why inferentially we’re beginning to hear that they’re refusing to do it,” Kirschner said.

Kirschner noted that Halligan seems to be a key part of Trump’s legal strategy because she is willing to prosecute his political enemies, but said she is “entirely ill-prepared” to handle prosecuting Comey. That puts her in a perilous situation where she could lose her law license, thereby making her unable to continue going after Trump’s foes.

“[Trump’s] going to have to get a political appointee who will be on the hook ethically the way anybody else would,” Kirschner said. “But I have a feeling that may be the only person they may be able to find, some lapdog political appointee to go in and put his or her bar license on the line to please Dear Leader.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Prosecutorial Crusade: A Recipe for Disaster?

In what can only be described as a spectacle of political vendetta, President Donald Trump’s latest gambit to use the federal prosecutorial machine against former FBI Director James Comey has been laid bare. With charges of obstruction of justice and lying to Congress, Trump’s maneuvers seem less about justice and more about settling scores. Glenn Kirschner, a voice of legal reason and experience, has called out this charade for what it is: “unparalleled incompetence and imbecility.”

The Unprecedented Undermining of Justice

It’s not just the charges themselves that raise eyebrows but the personnel maneuvering behind them. Lindsey Halligan, a former Trump defense attorney now leading the prosecution as the interim U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia, steps into a role fraught with conflict of interest. Her predecessor, Erik Siebert, reportedly stepped aside due to his refusal to prosecute Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James, adding another layer of controversy.

This situation marks a clear deviation from a 50-year norm that insulated the Department of Justice from presidential whims of retribution. Trump’s insistence on weaponizing legal processes against perceived political adversaries not only undermines the DOJ’s integrity but also violates foundational principles of American democracy.

The Legal Quagmire and Ethical Minefield

Kirschner’s insights reveal a legal team in turmoil. The difficulty in finding a prosecutor within the Eastern District of Virginia willing to take on Comey’s case speaks volumes. The risk of court sanctions or losing one’s law license looms large over this vindictive prosecution, highlighting the perilous path Halligan and potential prosecutors are being asked to tread.

Halligan’s willingness to prosecute Trump’s “enemies” places her in a precarious position, both legally and ethically. Kirschner’s observation that Trump might be forced to rely on a “lapdog political appointee” to carry out his bidding underscores the desperation and potential recklessness of this strategy.

The Bigger Picture: A Threat to Democracy

Trump’s approach to prosecuting Comey is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger disease eroding the pillars of American democracy. The use of the DOJ as a tool for political retribution is a dangerous precedent that threatens the impartiality of the justice system and the rule of law.

The implications of Trump’s actions extend beyond the legal realm into the very fabric of democratic governance. When a president can order the prosecution of political opponents without consequence, it signals a shift towards authoritarianism and away from the democratic ideals upon which the United States was founded.

Conclusion

In their quest to serve “Dear Leader,” Trump and his appointees like Halligan are not just challenging legal norms but are actively participating in the degradation of American democracy. This isn’t just about Trump versus Comey; it’s about safeguarding the independence of the judiciary and ensuring that the pursuit of justice remains free from political vendettas.

As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant and critical, recognizing these maneuvers for what they are: a dangerous conflation of power and petty politics. The rule of law must stand above political ambitions, and those who seek to undermine it for personal or political gain must be held accountable. In the end, it is the strength of our institutions and the resilience of our democratic principles that will determine the fate of our republic.
