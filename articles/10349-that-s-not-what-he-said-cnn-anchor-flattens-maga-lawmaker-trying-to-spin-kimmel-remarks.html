'That's not what he said!' CNN anchor flattens MAGA lawmaker trying to spin Kimmel remarks

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

CNN’s Brianna Keilar and Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) (Photo: Screen capture)

Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) got into it with CNN's Brianna Keilar on Thursday as he struggled to spin President Donald Trump's administration's comments about late-night host Jimmy Kimmel and the circumstances around his firing.

Federal Communications Commission Chairman Brendan Carr told the far-right Benny Show that his regulators may go after ABC. It took mere hours for the network to announce Kimmel was indefinitely suspended.

On Thursday morning, Carr told CNBC, “It was appearing to directly mislead the American public about a significant fact that probably one of the most significant political events we’ve had in a long time, for the most significant political assassination we’ve seen in a long time."

The exact quote from Kimmel was: "The MAGA gang [is] desperately trying to characterize this kid who murdered Charlie Kirk as anything other than one of them and doing everything they can to score political points from it."

When Trump was asked about it in the U.K. during a press conference, he claimed that Kimmel was canceled as a result of having "no talent" and bad ratings, denying that his government had any involvement or influence in the decision, contradicting Carr.

Keilar confronted Moore with the quotes, but Moore tried to spin Kimmel's remarks.

"Well, apparently he did mislead them — the American public when he said it was a right-wing MAGA person that did the assassination of Charlie Kirk," Moore said of Kimmel's statements on his show.

"That's not what he said. Actually, congressman? Congressman?" Keilar tried to step in to fact-check Moore, but he continued to filibuster over her.

"Let's jump off of this. Let's listen to what Jimmy Kimmel said and then let's have a discussion," she said, playing the clip.

"So, it's more nuanced than what you said. I just want to be clear," she said before quoting Trump's past statements claiming the government will never use its power to silence anyone. "What did he mean by that, then?"

Moore said it was precisely as Trump said it. "He's not labeling disinformation."

The comment came less than 60 seconds after Moore said Carr was going after Kimmel for disinformation.

Our Analysis:

A Carousel of Contradictions: The Kimmel-Carr-Trump Triad

In a display that would make Orwell double-take, Rep. Barry Moore (R-AL) entangled himself in a web of contradictions and attempted spins during an interview with CNN's Brianna Keilar. At the heart of this spectacle lies the controversy surrounding Jimmy Kimmel's suspension from ABC, a move allegedly precipitated by comments from Federal Communications Commission Chairman Brendan Carr. Carr's threat to target ABC, followed by the network's swift suspension of Kimmel, paints a chilling tableau of government influence over media expression. Yet, in a twist befitting a Kafka novel, President Donald Trump denies any governmental involvement, boldly contradicting Carr's own insinuations.

The Misleading Spin Cycle

Moore's attempt to navigate the interview was akin to watching a fish out of water if the fish were also trying to juggle. His efforts to reinterpret Kimmel's words—words that were readily available and clear to any who listened—border on the absurd. Kimmel's critique of the MAGA movement's response to a fictional assassination scenario is twisted into an accusation of misinformation. Moore's insistence on mischaracterizing Kimmel’s comments, even in the face of direct quotes, is a masterclass in political spin—one that would be impressive if it weren't so dishearteningly transparent.

The Irony of Free Speech Advocacy

Trump's assertion, made during a press conference in the U.K., that Kimmel was canceled due to "no talent" and poor ratings, rather than governmental pressure, would be laughable if it weren't so concerning. This claim starkly contrasts with the FCC Chairman's earlier threats, revealing a bizarre disconnect within the administration's ranks. Furthermore, Moore's parroting of Trump's past claims that the government wouldn't use its power to silence anyone comes off as not just ironic but deeply hypocritical. The irony is almost too rich: Moore criticizes Kimmel for alleged disinformation less than a minute after defending Trump's stance on non-censorship.

The Bigger Picture

What this convoluted saga underscores is the alarming potential for governmental bodies to exert pressure on media entities, a scenario with dire implications for free speech and the democratic exchange of ideas. The rapid sequence of events from Carr's comments to Kimmel's suspension suggests a level of influence that should give any free speech advocate pause. It's a stark reminder of the fragile balance between government authority and media freedom, a balance that seems increasingly threatened in today's political climate.

Moreover, the willingness of political figures to engage in blatant misinformation, as Moore does in his interview with Keilar, is a testament to a broader issue of truth decay in political discourse. When facts become malleable to fit political narratives, democracy itself is at risk.

Conclusion

The Kimmel incident, with its layers of contradiction, misinformation, and government interference, is more than just a media scandal. It's a microcosm of a larger, more troubling trend toward authoritarianism and away from the principles of free expression and truth. In the end, Moore's interview does little to exonerate the parties involved; instead, it serves as a damning indictment of the current state of political discourse and a government that appears all too willing to overstep its bounds.
