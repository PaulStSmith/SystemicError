'Don’t be surprised': Conservative George Conway warns of drastic Trump move 'tonight'

By Paulo Santos

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump gestures next to NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg as they attend a working lunch during the NATO leaders summit in Watford, Britain, December 4, 2019. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

Conservative attorney and prominent Trump critic George Conway has warned that President Donald Trump could announce a U.S. withdrawal from NATO as soon as Monday night — or at least claim to do so, given Congressional approval requirements — depending on the outcome of his talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European leaders earlier in the day.

Russian President Vladimir Putin has long expressed interest in weakening NATO, and recent developments following Friday’s Alaska Summit — including President Trump’s decision to step back from his stated goal of securing a Russian ceasefire, his Monday remarks about ending mail-in voting, and his Sunday statement that Ukraine should cede territory for peace and will never enter NATO — have drawn renewed attention to that issue.

“Don’t be surprised if Trump announces on Truth Social tonight that the United States is withdrawing from NATO,” Conway surmised late Monday morning. “I’m not joking. I’m not saying that it’s actually going to happen … today. But I’m not joking. Remember—everything he does is driven by his delicate, thirsty, easily bruised, malignantly narcissistic ego.”

Conway is the founder of an anti-Trump Super PAC, and is the president of the Society for the Rule of Law.

The Lincoln Project’s Rick Wilson appeared to agree with Conway’s assessment, writing: “We live in a world where that is not out of the question.”

Technically, Trump cannot withdraw from NATO by fiat — the Senate must approve a withdrawal from NATO, according to a 2023 law signed by President Joe Biden, but Politico reports that law is not “airtight.”

Last week, former U.S. Ambassador to NATO, Michael McFaul, wrote: “If Trump were serious about ending the war in Ukraine, he should be discussing with Zelenskyy security guarantees for Ukraine, including NATO membership. That’s what the Ukrainians need in return for agreeing to reunify their country only through peaceful means. Thats a real deal.”

The New Republic suggests none of that will happen.

“Donald Trump is ready to allow Russian President Vladimir Putin to block Ukraine from joining NATO—and to keep a slice of stolen land,” TNR’s Edith Olmstead wrote on Monday. “Ahead of his meeting with European leaders Monday, Trump put pressure on Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy to end the invasion of his own country by handing over seized territory and dropping his dreams of belonging to the powerful military alliance.”

Our Analysis:

The Perilous Politics of Trump's NATO Stance

In a recent, rather alarming development, conservative attorney and vocal Trump critic, George Conway, has issued a stark warning: President Donald Trump could potentially announce the U.S.'s withdrawal from NATO—or at least, make an attempt to claim such a move is forthcoming. This speculation isn't without its merits, especially considering Trump's track record and the complex web of international relationships at play. Here, we dissect the layers of this political quagmire, spotlighting the nuances and potential repercussions of such a drastic foreign policy pivot.

Trump's Alignment with Putin's Agenda

At the heart of this controversy is Russian President Vladimir Putin's well-documented ambition to weaken NATO, a cornerstone of European security since the aftermath of World War II. Trump's recent actions and statements, particularly those surrounding the Alaska Summit and his interactions with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, signal a disturbing alignment with Putin's long-term goals. Trump's suggestion that Ukraine cede territory for peace and his assertion that it will never join NATO not only undermine the sovereignty of Ukraine but also play directly into Russia's hands.

The implications of such a stance are far-reaching and deeply troubling. By entertaining the idea of a NATO withdrawal, Trump risks emboldening Russia's aggressive posturing in Eastern Europe and beyond, potentially destabilizing an already volatile geopolitical landscape. The fact that this aligns so seamlessly with Putin's objectives should raise alarm bells for anyone concerned with maintaining the post-World War II international order.

The Legal and Political Quagmire

While George Conway's warnings may seem hyperbolic to some, they underscore a critical aspect of this debacle: the legal and political mechanisms at play. Although Trump cannot unilaterally withdraw the U.S. from NATO due to a law signed by President Joe Biden in 2023, the assertion that this law is not "airtight" introduces a layer of uncertainty into the equation. This ambiguity serves as a reminder of the fragile nature of international agreements when faced with leaders willing to push the boundaries of their executive powers.

Moreover, the commentary from figures like Rick Wilson of The Lincoln Project echoes a broader sentiment of disbelief and concern among political commentators and analysts. The very notion that a U.S. President could even contemplate such a drastic step speaks volumes about the current state of American politics and its impact on global stability.

The Role of Congress and the Rule of Law

The requirement for Senate approval for a NATO withdrawal highlights the constitutional checks and balances designed to prevent rash decisions by a single branch of government. However, the ongoing discourse also raises questions about the effectiveness of these mechanisms in the face of determined executive action, especially when national security and international alliances are at stake.

The role of Congress in this matter cannot be overstated. It serves as the bulwark against unilateral executive actions that can have far-reaching implications for U.S. foreign policy and national security. The debate surrounding Trump's potential NATO withdrawal underscores the importance of maintaining a robust legislative oversight over matters of such grave importance.

Conclusion

In sum, the prospect of a U.S. withdrawal from NATO, as speculated by George Conway, is a stark reminder of the precarious nature of international relations under Trump's administration. It's a potential move that could have catastrophic consequences for global security, undermining decades of diplomatic progress and cooperation. As the situation unfolds, it is imperative for policymakers, analysts, and the public to remain vigilant and engaged in the discourse surrounding these critical issues. The stability of the international order may very well depend on it.

Remember, in the realm of geopolitics, the stakes could not be higher, and the margin for error could not be smaller.
