Musician Ben Folds: Kennedy Center is 'being held hostage' by Trump

Singer-songwriter and legendary musician Ben Folds slammed President Donald Trump’s recent takeover of the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts saying he’s making a good thing awful.

MS NOW host Chris Jansing asked what Folds knew about the future of the institution. Folds said that they are clearly no longer trying to elevate the arts. He added that no one he knows was aware that the Kennedy Center would be closing for two years.

A CNN report earlier this week revealed that the reason it was being forced to close is that there were no performers for the 2026-2027 season.

Folds made it clear that it isn’t the “dump” that Trump said it is. He spent ten years as the artistic advisor to the National Symphony Orchestra in Washington for the institution. He resigned last year, saying that he didn’t want to be complicit in anything involving the Trump administration. He initially thought the effort was to choke off the arts, given the administration’s moves to silence Stephen Colbert and Jimmy Kimmel.

Jansing said that in her experience, everything was lovely and comfortable compared to some Broadway theaters she’s been to. It made her wonder why Trump would lie about the center’s state

“Well, I mean, it’s hard to know what goes on in someone’s mind who will say things like that,” Folds said. “But, I mean, I do think that, you know, the overall like I say, arts administration is quite, quite a skill. It takes a long time to understand. And when you come in, and you take over a place like that, you’re trying to muzzle the arts or control it. And people are art. Even in the Soviet Union, at the height of Stalin, some of the greatest music ever made was coming out.”

He said that Trump will never be successful in silencing the art community with his Kennedy Center takeover, even if it means shutting down the buildings and threatening companies that make or distribute art.

“We’re human beings. And so it’s very frustrating to go, wow, we think we can take over the Kennedy Center and suddenly guess what? You don’t know what you’re doing,” he mocked. “And part of not knowing what you’re doing is not understanding how to get artists there, how to get audiences there. And none of the criticism or the lies that were leveled at the place had — held any water at all.”

He called the place “very diverse,” meaning it was “representative of the USA, including, like rural America and every Christian, everybody, they were all included.”

“There was no — I don’t know what woke even means. There was none of that. All it was was a really well-run place,” he continued. Folds conceded that every place has its challenges, but The “Kennedy Center was in its golden years, you know?”

He noted that in 2023, they were “in the black,” meaning the arts institution was running with a surplus. It’s something he called outstanding to have a federal arts institution making that much money.

“They’ve not closed it for renovations. It’s just gone,” he noted. Folds added that it was a “living monument” and a “great gift to the United States.”

The National Symphony Orchestra is “out in the cold now,” he said, adding that he hope they would soon find a home during the Trump administration.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Takeover: A Discordant Note in the Symphony of Arts

Ben Folds, a name synonymous with musical genius and a decade-long stewardship as the artistic advisor to the National Symphony Orchestra, has cast a searing spotlight on President Donald Trump’s recent maneuvers at the Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts. Folds, in a conversation with MS NOW host Chris Jansing, didn’t mince words: Trump’s orchestration is turning a harmonious haven into a cacophony of chaos. Let’s dissect the dissonance.

The Unforeseen Silence

The Kennedy Center, a beacon of cultural and artistic excellence, faces a two-year closure—a development as shocking as it is abrupt. Notably, a CNN report unearthed the bewildering reason: a stark absence of performers for the 2026-2027 season. Here, Folds articulates a critical question: Why? This isn’t a matter of the center’s inadequacy; Folds himself dismantles Trump’s “dump” narrative, having witnessed the institution’s golden years firsthand.

Art Under Siege?

Trump’s tenure has seen attempts to muzzle voices across the artistic spectrum, with efforts to silence stalwarts like Stephen Colbert and Jimmy Kimmel. Folds initially viewed the Kennedy Center’s predicament as another front in this battle against the arts. However, the true adversary here is not just the silencing but a profound misunderstanding of the arts’ essence and the administration’s inability to foster an environment where artists and audiences converge.

The Misconception of Control

Folds’s insights shed light on a larger, more insidious issue: the misconception that the arts can be controlled, or worse, that they should be. His comparison to the Soviet Union’s artistic scene under Stalin underscores the resilience of art and artists. Trump’s belief that he can dictate the terms of artistic expression is not just misguided; it’s fundamentally flawed.

Inclusion and Diversity: More Than Buzzwords

The Kennedy Center, as per Folds, represented the mosaic of American culture, embracing rural America, various religious communities, and a spectrum of political beliefs. The charge of it being “woke” is not only unfounded but reveals a gross misunderstanding of what the institution stood for: inclusivity and diversity in the truest sense.

The Financial Irony

In 2023, the Kennedy Center was “in the black,” operating at a surplus—an extraordinary feat for a federal arts institution. This financial health underscores the center’s robust management and its importance as a cultural pillar. The closure, then, is not a matter of necessity but a willful disregard for a thriving institution’s value.

A Living Monument Silenced

The Kennedy Center, described by Folds as a “living monument” and a “great gift to the United States,” now faces an uncertain future. The National Symphony Orchestra’s displacement is a glaring testament to the administration’s failure to recognize the arts’ intrinsic value to society’s fabric.

In Conclusion

Ben Folds’s critique of Trump’s takeover of the Kennedy Center is not just about the arts; it’s a clarion call to defend the soul of cultural expression from those who seek to undermine it. The arts, resilient and defiant, will continue to thrive, but institutions like the Kennedy Center are irreplaceable. Their silencing is a loss not just to the artists but to the nation as a whole. It’s a systemic error that demands not just recognition but rectification. The question remains: will the dissonance stirred by this administration catalyze a movement towards harmony, or will it herald an era of muted voices? Only time, and perhaps the resilience of the art community, will tell.
