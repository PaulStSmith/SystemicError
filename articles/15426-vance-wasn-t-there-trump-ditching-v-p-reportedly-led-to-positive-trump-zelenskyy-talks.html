'Vance wasn't there': Trump ditching V.P. reportedly led to positive Trump-Zelenskyy talks

President Donald Trump's meeting with Ukraine president Volodymyr Zelenskyy at the pope's funeral went a lot better than their confrontation in the Oval Office, and sources say there's one big reason for that.

The pair briefly met Saturday at the Vatican while attending Pope Francis' funeral, and Axios reported new details of that tête-à-tête as Trump and Zelenskyy attempt to reach an agreement to end Russia's invasion.

"Zelensky received 'signals' ahead of Pope Francis' funeral that Trump was prepared to meet on the sidelines, the sources say," the website reported. Zelensky's advisers were nervous, and some — still traumatized by the scenes in the Oval on Feb. 28 — told Zelensky they weren't sure it was a good idea."

No plans were finalized ahead of the meeting, which a source said was initially expected to come after the funeral, but the leaders ran into one another upon arrival and met alone in St. Peter's Basilica, where Zelensky told Trump that Vladimir Putin would not change his position unless Trump applies more pressure.

"One source said Trump replied that he might have to change his approach to Putin, as he later stated in his Truth Social post [threatening sanctions]," Axios reported. "Zelensky also pushed Trump to return to his initial proposal of an unconditional ceasefire as a starting point for peace talks, which Ukraine accepted but Russia rejected. One source said Trump seemed to agree."

Zelenskyy made clear that he would not recognize Crimea as Russian, which Trump insisted he was not asking him to do, but the U.S. president again pressed his counterpart to sign the U.S.-Ukraine minerals deal as soon as possible.

Officials for each government declined to comment on the specifics of their conversation, but the sources said it seemed to have gone better than the contentious meeting in February at the White House.

"The sources said one potential reason this Trump-Zelensky meeting was more positive was that Vice President Vance and White House envoy Steve Witkoff — whom the Ukrainians see as more supportive of the Russian position — were not there," Axios reported.

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Trump-Zelenskyy Vatican Meeting: A Closer Look

In a world where diplomatic subtleties often dictate the course of international relations, the recent meeting between President Donald Trump and Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy at the Vatican, of all places, during Pope Francis' funeral, presents a tableau ripe for analysis. The encounter, as reported by Axios, ostensibly marked a departure from their previously tense interaction in the Oval Office. But did it really signify a shift in dynamics, or was it merely a facade of diplomacy, as thinly veiled as the intentions behind it?

The Premeditated "Chance" Meeting

First off, let's dissect the notion of the so-called "signals" Zelenskyy received, suggesting Trump was open to a sideline meeting. The strategic leaking of intentions before such high-profile gatherings is a classic diplomatic maneuver, designed to orchestrate what would appear to bystanders as a spontaneous encounter. The reality is far from it; these are calculated moves on the geopolitical chessboard, with each side trying to outmaneuver the other.

Zelenskyy's advisers, reportedly "traumatized" by their last Oval Office encounter, expressed reservations. This speaks volumes about the lingering shadow of their previous confrontation, highlighting the high stakes involved for Ukraine, which finds itself grappling with an aggressive neighbor in Russia.

The Substance of the Conversation

The discourse between Trump and Zelenskyy, as per the sources, revolved around the critical issue of Russia's invasion. Zelenskyy's plea for Trump to exert more pressure on Putin is a glaring testament to the desperation Ukraine faces. Trump's alleged consideration to "change his approach" is noteworthy, yet it reeks of reactive diplomacy rather than a well-thought-out strategy. His post on Truth Social, threatening sanctions, feels like an afterthought, an attempt to exhibit a stance of strength in the aftermath of public scrutiny.

Zelenskyy's insistence on an unconditional ceasefire as a starting point for peace talks, and his firm stance on not recognizing Crimea as Russian, are commendable. However, Trump's push for the U.S.-Ukraine minerals deal to be signed "as soon as possible" raises eyebrows. It begs the question: Is the U.S. prioritizing its economic interests over the pressing humanitarian crisis at hand?

The Absence of Vance and Witkoff

The report suggests that the meeting's positive tone might be attributed to the absence of Vice President Vance and White House envoy Steve Witkoff, perceived by Ukrainians as leaning towards Russia. This is a critical observation. The presence or absence of certain figures can drastically alter the dynamics of such discussions. It implies that the U.S. administration may have internal divisions on its stance towards Russia and Ukraine, a factor that could significantly impact the effectiveness and coherence of its foreign policy.

Conclusion: A Step Forward or Diplomatic Theater?

While the meeting between Trump and Zelenskyy at the Vatican might have ended on a seemingly positive note, one must ponder whether it was a genuine step towards resolving the conflict or merely a diplomatic theater. The backdrop of Pope Francis' funeral added a layer of solemnity and perhaps urgency to their discussion, yet the real test lies in the actions that follow.

The pressing issue of the U.S.-Ukraine minerals deal, juxtaposed with the existential threat Ukraine faces from Russia, encapsulates the dilemma at the heart of international diplomacy: the balance between moral imperatives and national interests. As the world watches on, the unfolding events will reveal whether this meeting was a harbinger of meaningful progress or just another episode in the saga of geopolitical posturing.
