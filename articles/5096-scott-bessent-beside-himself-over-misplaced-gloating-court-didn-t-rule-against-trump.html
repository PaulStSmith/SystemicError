Scott Bessent beside himself over 'misplaced gloating': 'Court didn't rule against Trump!'

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent reacted on Friday to the Supreme Court’s decision barring the use of emergency economic powers to impose tariffs, during a speech at the Economic Club of Dallas and echoed President Donald Trump’s defiance on the subject.

Much like Trump, Bessent vowed that the tariffs will continue — and the Trump administration will simply use other federal statutes than emergency powers to authorize them.

“I would ... like to take a moment to address today’s Supreme Court ruling, and I would note that I did not change a single word in my speech post the ruling,” said Bessent. “President Trump will always put our national security and Americans first, and as I have said before, the president has multiple tools in his toolbox.”

“Let’s be clear about what today’s ruling was, and what it wasn’t,” Bessent continued. “Despite the misplaced gloating from Democrats, ill-informed media outlets, and the very people who gutted our industrial base, the court did not rule against President Trump’s tariffs. Six justices simply ruled that IEEPA authorities cannot be used to raise even one dollar of revenue. This administration will invoke alternative legal authorities to replace the IEEPA tariffs. We will be leveraging Section 232 and Section 301 tariff authorities that were validated through thousands of legal challenges.”

In fact, Bessent promised, the new tariff plan won’t even result in a meaningful loss of revenue for the federal government.

“Treasury’s estimates show that the use of Section 122 authority, combined with possibly enhanced Section 232 and Section 301 tariffs, will result in virtually unchanged tariff revenue in 2026,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Defiance: Trump Administration’s Tariff Tango

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the current American political theater, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent took to the stage at the Economic Club of Dallas, oozing defiance against the Supreme Court’s latest decision. This ruling, a significant slap on the wrist to the Trump administration, bars the use of emergency economic powers to impose tariffs. Yet, Bessent, echoing the sentiment of President Donald Trump, declared that this is merely a hiccup in their grand plan of economic maneuvers.

“President Trump will always put our national security and Americans first,” Bessent parroted, a line so often used it’s practically a mantra at this point. However, the devil is in the details, or in this case, the lack thereof. The administration vows to continue its tariff tirade but through different legislative paths. This pivot raises the question: Is this about national security, or is it a stubborn refusal to accept defeat?

Bessent’s speech was a masterclass in political double-speak. “Let’s be clear about what today’s ruling was, and what it wasn’t,” he stated, attempting to downplay the Supreme Court’s decision. The Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) cannot be used as a piggy bank to impose tariffs willy-nilly. Yet, Bessent assures us, this is but a minor inconvenience. The administration’s solution? Leverage other sections of federal law to continue their tariff regime.

The Alternative Paths: Section 232 and Section 301

Bessent’s confidence hinges on the administration’s plan to invoke Section 232 and Section 301 tariff authorities, which, according to him, have withstood “thousands of legal challenges.” This raises an eyebrow or two. One must wonder, if these sections are indeed bulletproof, why resort to the IEEPA in the first place? Could it be that these alternatives are not as robust or justifiable as Bessent claims?

Moreover, Bessent’s promise that the new tariff plan “won’t even result in a meaningful loss of revenue for the federal government” by 2026 is both oddly specific and suspiciously optimistic. It’s a bold claim, considering the unpredictable nature of global trade and economic relations. It seems the Treasury Secretary possesses a crystal ball, one that perhaps only shows rosy forecasts and ignores potential retaliatory measures from trade partners.

The Bottom Line

The Trump administration’s defiance in the face of the Supreme Court’s ruling is not just about tariffs. It’s a statement, a declaration that they will not be hindered by checks and balances. It’s an audacious stance, one that skirts dangerously close to autocratic whims rather than democratic deliberation.

Bessent’s speech was less about concrete economic policy and more about political posturing. It’s a reminder that in the current administration, the show must go on, regardless of the Supreme Court’s script. This approach to governance, where alternative legal paths are sought not for the sake of national security but for the sake of defiance, is a systemic error that undermines the very foundations of the United States’ democratic institutions.

In the end, the Trump administration’s tariff tango is a dance around the principles of democracy, and it’s a performance we should all be wary of.
