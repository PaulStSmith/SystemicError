Trump aides aware of president’s repeated gaffe – but are ‘afraid to correct him’: report

President Donald Trump has been ridiculed in recent days for repeatedly referring to his administration’s military siege on Iran as an “excursion,” a potential mix-up with the word “incursion,” but according to a new report from Zeteo, his aides are well aware of the blunder but refuse to correct him out of fear.

“I’m not telling him,” a Trump administration official told Zeteo on Friday, speaking on the condition of anonymity.

The word “excursion,” as explained by MS NOW’s Jonathan Lemire on Friday, generally refers to “a short, organized pleasure trip, à la snorkeling,” whereas an “incursion” is a “sudden, often hostile entrance or invasion into a territory or place,” a far more appropriate term to describe the Trump administration’s operation in Iran.

And yet, despite the obvious blunder, Trump officials told Zeteo that correcting the president would be “a fool’s errand,” and that “doing so would likely get them yelled at,” the outlet reported.

“We say ‘incursion,’ the boss says ‘excursion.’ It’s not a big deal,” a second Trump official told Zeteo, also speaking on the condition of anonymity.

A third source – whom Zeteo described as a person “familiar with the matter” – told the outlet that Trump was aware that the two words had far different meanings, but that the president still “prefers ‘excursion’ and likes that it sounds like ‘incursion.’”

“Some of these sources say that they or their colleagues have used the term ‘incursion’ in front of Trump lately, but then the president just… did his own thing,” Zeteo’s report reads.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Zeteo’s Report on Trump’s “Excursion” vs. “Incursion” Terminology

Identifying Institutional Power

The focus here is on President Donald Trump and his administration’s handling of a military operation in Iran. As President, Trump held the highest institutional power in the U.S. government, directly influencing foreign policy and military decisions. His administration officials, while influential, operate within the power structure defined by Trump’s leadership.

Decision-Making Dynamics

The decision in question is not a policy or strategic military choice, but rather the use of the term “excursion” instead of “incursion” when referring to military operations in Iran. The person who made the decision to use “excursion” is President Trump himself, as per the Zeteo report’s indication that Trump is aware of the correct term but prefers his choice.

Article’s Framing and Responsibility

The article frames the situation as a mix-up or blunder by President Trump, with aides too intimidated to correct him. It implies a dysfunction within the Trump administration, highlighting a culture where officials fear retribution over minor corrections. However, focusing on the terminology blunder could misdirect responsibility or overshadow more substantive issues related to the military operation in Iran.

Analysis

In terms of power dynamics, it’s clear that Trump’s preference dictates the administration’s public messaging, underscoring his ultimate control over the narrative. Aides, despite recognizing the mistake, have limited power to challenge or correct the President’s language choices due to the hierarchical nature of his administration.

Regarding decision-making, Trump’s insistence on using “excursion” reflects his unique approach to leadership and communication. While the choice of word is indeed a decision made by him, it is minor compared to the broader strategic and operational decisions involving military actions in Iran. The focus on this linguistic preference may distract from more critical evaluations of the administration’s foreign policy and military strategy.

The article’s framing, while capturing an interesting anecdote about the internal workings of the Trump administration, risks overemphasizing a relatively trivial issue. The distinction between “excursion” and “incursion” is linguistically significant but pales in comparison to the implications of the military operation itself. The framing potentially misdirects responsibility by focusing on the President’s communication style rather than on the substance and consequences of the military action in Iran.

Conclusion

While the Zeteo report sheds light on the peculiarities of Trump’s presidency and his relationship with aides, it’s crucial to maintain perspective. The real issues at hand involve the use of military force and the strategic decisions leading to such actions, not the semantics of how they are described. Criticizing Trump for his choice of words may offer a momentary distraction but fails to address the deeper, more consequential aspects of his administration’s policies and actions. In this case, the criticism seems misplaced, focusing on a symptom of Trump’s leadership style rather than on the substantive outcomes of his institutional power and decision-making.
