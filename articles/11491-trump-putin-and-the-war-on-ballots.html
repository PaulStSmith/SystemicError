Trump, Putin, and the War on Ballots

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump has never met a democratic institution he didn’t want to bulldoze. His latest tantrum, a promise to issue an executive order to “eliminate mail-in ballots” and “bring HONESTY” to the 2026 midterms, is just the newest entry in his endless war against voting itself. This isn’t about policy. It’s about power. Trump cannot coexist with free elections because free elections reject him.

Let’s start with the obvious: Trump’s claim that mail-in ballots are a “hoax” has already been torpedoed by his own former Attorney General, William Barr, who said in 2020 that there was no evidence of widespread fraud. Trump lost, and his fragile ego couldn’t handle it. Instead of accepting reality, he spun a mythology that he continues to flog today—mail-in voting as the great Democratic plot. The irony? Republicans have benefited from mail-in ballots when they’ve embraced them, particularly in 2024. His party made gains precisely because they finally stopped shooting themselves in the foot and told their voters to use the system.

But Trump doesn’t care about results. He cares about narrative. And the narrative is that democracy is illegitimate unless it crowns him king. That’s why he’s now floating the authoritarian fantasy that states are merely “agents” of the federal government and must do “what the President of the United States tells them.” No. The Constitution puts states in charge of elections. Trump’s version reads like a fascist daydream scribbled on the back of a Truth Social rant.

The rot goes deeper when we see who’s in his corner: Vladimir Putin. Trump gleefully claimed that during their summit in Alaska, Putin agreed with him that the 2020 election was “rigged” because of mail-in ballots. Stop and let that sink in. The president of the United States is quoting the leader of an authoritarian adversary to justify dismantling his own country’s voting system. This isn’t policy—it’s collaboration in effect, if not in formal order. Trump doesn’t need a secret set of “marching orders” from Moscow; by parroting Putin’s line, he does the Kremlin’s work all the same. Whether from sycophancy, alignment of interests, or sheer authoritarian instinct, the outcome is identical: Trump’s words and deeds weaken American democracy and strengthen Russia’s propaganda war.

And then there’s the blatant lie that the U.S. is the “only country in the world” with mail-in voting. Dozens of democracies—from Germany to Canada to Australia—use postal ballots. They manage just fine. But Trump thrives on the Big Lie, and facts are irrelevant when grievance is the fuel. Every fabrication he spits out corrodes trust in the system, and that corrosion is the entire point. Democracy cannot function if its citizens are told—constantly, relentlessly—that the results are illegitimate. That is the very strategy Russia uses abroad, and it is the one Trump has imported to America.

Strip away the caps lock hysteria and the bluster, and what’s left is naked fear. Trump knows that a functioning democracy spells doom for him. He knows his opponents outnumber his devotees. And so, he must shrink the electorate, sabotage trust, and cry “fraud” at every turn. Without voter suppression, disinformation, and foreign applause, Trumpism collapses. His fixation on destroying mail-in voting isn’t about fraud prevention. It’s about suffocating turnout. When fewer people vote, Trump’s odds rise. That is the entire strategy in a nutshell.

Let’s be blunt: Trump doesn’t need to take direct orders from Putin because their objectives already align. Putin wants a fractured, distrustful America that tears itself apart from within. Trump wants unchecked power, regardless of the cost. Put the two together and you have a grotesque partnership of convenience. Every time Trump delegitimizes elections, Putin gains. Every time Trump undermines voting rights, Putin smiles. Whether Trump is consciously serving Moscow or simply indulging his own authoritarian cravings is beside the point. The end result is indistinguishable.

The Republican Party has a choice: embrace democracy or embrace Trump’s fantasyland. But every day that GOP leaders stay silent, they inch closer to the latter. Mail-in ballots aren’t the threat. Trump is. He is the hoax, the fraud, the con. And this latest performance—taking validation from Putin while plotting to shred voting rights—isn’t just anti-democratic. It’s un-American. If he succeeds in normalizing this kind of authoritarian sabotage, America won’t need Putin to dismantle its democracy. Trump will have done the job for him.
