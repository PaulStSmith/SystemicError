'Attention-thirsty troll': Oklahoma's MAGA school chief shocks with new right-wing stunt

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Ryan Walters: The Poster Child for MAGA's Educational Overreach

Oklahoma Superintendent of Public Instruction Ryan Walters. (Official photo)

Ryan Walters, the MAGA-cheerleading state superintendent of Oklahoma, is known for his "attention-thirsty troll tactics," noted Molly Olmstead for Slate — forcing schools to purchase Trump-endorsed "God Bless The USA Bibles" and even ordering teachers to inform kids of conspiracy theories that the 2020 election was rigged, allegedly without the full consent of state officials.

But now he has come up with a new stunt: forcing teachers who move from blue states to effectively swear loyalty to a suite of MAGA beliefs outlined by PragerU.

"Under this plan, teachers from states with 'progressive education policies' would be tested on the U.S. Constitution, American exceptionalism, and 'the fundamental biological differences between boys and girls,'" wrote Olmstead. "The screening is guaranteed to test teachers for a deeply MAGA-inflected understanding of U.S. history, government, and society: PragerU, a right-wing group with a stated mission of countering leftism in education, is already known for its white nationalist-adjacent content for schoolchildren."

Walters' stunts have been wildly successful at building attention for himself, Olmstead wrote. "He has become the face of the Christian nationalist campaigns into public education. A good number of Americans know the name of the school superintendent in the 28th most populous state."

However, it's unclear much of his boasted agenda is actually even being enacted, let alone improving student learning.

Per local experts, "Some of the boldest proposals to come from Walters’ office have foundered, either ensnared in legal challenges or sidestepped by school districts wary of the culture wars," wrote Olmstead. Moreover, Oklahoma officials and parents alike are reportedly exasperated with these stunts.

But Walters appears sincere in his beliefs. He "has attended a number of events with the Heritage Foundation, which has encouraged pushing boundaries and counting on the Supreme Court to back them up." And it could lay the groundwork for a Supreme Court case to allow state policy to discriminate against people from other states.

Even if Walters manages to drum up such a legal case and it goes on to set a precedent for the country, "It will not help the worsening teacher shortage in Oklahoma, where students have some of the worst test scores in the nation. Nor will it necessarily help Waltners’ gubernatorial aspirations in the state, where traditional Republicans remain wary of Walters and where he has polled well behind the state attorney general," Olmstead wrote.

Nonetheless, she concluded, it demonstrates how, "in Trump’s America, impulsive, politically isolated, and farcically combative figures can go far."

Our Analysis:

Ryan Walters: The Poster Child for MAGA's Educational Overreach

In a move that blurs the lines between education and indoctrination, Ryan Walters, Oklahoma's state superintendent, has taken MAGA cheerleading to an unprecedented level. His latest stunt? Forcing teachers relocating from blue states to practically pledge allegiance to a series of MAGA-centric beliefs, as outlined by PragerU. This initiative is not just another footnote in the saga of political overreach into education; it's a glaring neon sign of the times.

The PragerU Litmus Test: Let's dissect this for a moment. Teachers from states with "progressive education policies" are now subjected to a litmus test on the U.S. Constitution, American exceptionalism, and "the fundamental biological differences between boys and girls." This is not education; it's ideological grooming, courtesy of PragerU—a group notoriously cozy with white nationalist-adjacent content. Walters' alignment with PragerU is not just a red flag; it's a red banner, unfurled with pride, signaling a deep dive into the MAGA rabbit hole.

Walters' Attention-Grabbing Antics: It's no secret that Walters thrives on controversy. From mandating schools to purchase Trump-endorsed "God Bless The USA Bibles" to pushing conspiracy theories about the 2020 election, his tactics scream desperation for the spotlight. But at what cost? Molly Olmstead's article for Slate rightly points out that while Walters has become the "face of the Christian nationalist campaigns into public education," the efficacy and enactment of his proposals are questionable at best.

A Mirage of Success: Despite Walters' claims, many of his boldest initiatives have either been mired in legal challenges or outright ignored by districts wary of engaging in culture wars. This raises the question: Is Walters more interested in stoking the flames of division rather than fostering an environment conducive to learning? Oklahoma's worsening teacher shortage and abysmal student test scores suggest as much.

The Supreme Court Gambit: Walters' ambitions might not just be limited to Oklahoma. With ties to the Heritage Foundation, he seems to be playing a long game, potentially aiming to set a Supreme Court precedent that would allow state policy to discriminate against individuals from other states. However, even if successful, such a strategy is unlikely to address the pressing issues facing Oklahoma's education system or bolster Walters' gubernatorial aspirations.

Conclusion: A Farce in the Guise of Reform: Walters' actions, as outlined by Olmstead, are emblematic of a broader trend in Trump's America, where impulsive and isolated political maneuvers are mistaken for genuine leadership. His alliance with PragerU and insistence on pushing a deeply MAGA-inflected curriculum highlight a dangerous conflation of education with political indoctrination. As Oklahoma grapples with a dire educational crisis, one can't help but wonder: Is Ryan Walters the symptom or the disease?

In the end, Walters' legacy, regardless of his intentions, may very well be a cautionary tale of how not to approach educational reform. As educators, parents, and policymakers sift through the aftermath of his tenure, the real work of repairing and rejuvenating Oklahoma's education system will require more than just political grandstanding. It will require a return to principles of inclusivity, critical thinking, and genuine educational excellence—values currently in short supply in Walters' playbook.
