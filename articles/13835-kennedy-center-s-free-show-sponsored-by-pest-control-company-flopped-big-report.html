Kennedy Center's free show sponsored by pest control company flopped big: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures while he poses for a picture at the presidential box at the Kennedy Center, in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 17, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

The MAGAfied Kennedy Center hosted a free animated film about Jesus, but only a little more than half of the seats were filled, according to a new report.

Trump avoided the Kennedy Center in his first term, but has since vowed to remake it in MAGA's image.

Already, reports have abounded about canceled bookings, a dropoff in donations, and stars like legendary cellist Yo-Yo Ma dropping out of performances ever since Trump promised to rebuild the Kennedy Center into a MAGA bastion.

Raw Story reported over the weekend on how Kennedy Center officials have purportedly discussed speaking to Qatar about funding a VIP renovation project.

The same Wall Street Journal report that revealed the potential Qatari funding had an anecdote about a recent film flop.

"Richard Grenell, the White House special envoy who was named executive director of the Kennedy Center in February, said the president has taken a direct interest in the center and agreed it needed additional funding for upkeep and repairs after a recent visit," the report states before quoting Grenell.

"The back of the house and the front of the house have been left in an embarrassing state. I am proud to be a small part of a team where the boss eschews partisan politics in favor of putting American culture, heritage, and excellence first,” Grenell reportedly said.

The report states, "Grenell requested a June 1 public screening of 'The King of Kings,' an animated feature film about the story of Jesus, as told by the character of Charles Dickens. Grenell ordered that the free event take place in the center’s biggest venue, a 2,500-seat concert hall, at a projected cost of $29,000 for staffing, gratis popcorn and other expenses."

"The event featured a prayer wall where visitors could post their written prayers for the nation, and was sponsored by the Museum of the Bible and Moxie Pest Control, whose founder made an unsuccessful run at a Republican U.S. Senate seat in Utah last year," The report continues. "When advance sign-ups for tickets indicated a full house, Kennedy Center leaders added a second screening, increasing the total cost of the event."

But things didn't go as planned, according to the WSJ.

"Employees, who said there is typical attrition for free events, said the actual turnout left the hall 55% full for the first screening and 58% full for the second," the report states. "The Kennedy Center spokesperson said the free screenings were sold out."

Our Analysis:

The MAGAfication of the Kennedy Center: A Tale of Hubris and Mismanagement

In what can only be described as a theatrical flop of biblical proportions, the once revered Kennedy Center has found itself at the center of a political and cultural storm. The recent attempt to host a free animated film about Jesus, which struggled to fill seats, is emblematic of the broader issues plaguing the institution under the shadow of Trump's MAGA movement. This debacle, as reported, is not just a story of empty seats but a cautionary tale of misguided priorities and the alienation of the art community.

Trump’s MAGA Crusade Hits the Arts

The Kennedy Center, historically a beacon of cultural achievement and bipartisan support, has seemingly fallen victim to the polarizing MAGA agenda. Trump's avoidance of the venue in his first term was a clear snub, but his subsequent vow to remake it in MAGA's image is where the real tragedy begins. The fallout has been swift and severe: canceled bookings, a dropoff in donations, and the loss of stars like Yo-Yo Ma. These are not mere coincidences but a direct consequence of a misguided attempt to politicize a space that thrives on inclusivity and diversity.

The Qatar Connection: A Desperate Bid for Funding

Perhaps most alarming in this saga is the reported dalliance with Qatar for funding a VIP renovation project. If true, this move reeks of desperation and raises serious questions about the integrity and autonomy of the Kennedy Center. It's one thing to seek additional funding for upkeep and repairs, a necessity for any aging institution, but quite another to potentially compromise the center's values and independence in the process.

A Flop of Biblical Proportions

The choice to screen "The King of Kings," an animated film about Jesus, as a grand gesture of this new MAGA direction, was puzzling at best. The decision by Richard Grenell, the White House special envoy turned executive director of the Kennedy Center, to host this event in the center’s biggest venue, underscores a profound misunderstanding of the center's audience and mission. The resulting poor attendance — with the hall less than 60% full for both screenings — is a stark indicator of this disconnect.

The event's sponsorship by the Museum of the Bible and Moxie Pest Control (whose founder had a failed Republican Senate run) further muddies the waters between art, religion, and politics. The inclusion of a prayer wall might have been intended as a heartfelt gesture, but in this context, it feels more like a prop in a larger political performance.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Judgment

The saga of the Kennedy Center under Trump's MAGA influence is a cautionary tale about the dangers of politicizing cultural institutions. The arts have long been a refuge from the divisiveness of politics, a space where diverse voices and perspectives can come together in a celebration of humanity. By attempting to co-opt this space for a narrow political agenda, the MAGA movement has not only alienated artists and patrons but has also undermined the very essence of what makes the Kennedy Center special.

As the Kennedy Center struggles to navigate these choppy waters, one thing is clear: art cannot thrive when it is shackled to the whims of political ambition. The center must find a way to reclaim its independence and reestablish itself as a beacon of cultural excellence, free from the taint of partisan politics. Only then can it hope to fill its halls once more — not just with people, but with the spirit of unity and creativity that once defined it.
