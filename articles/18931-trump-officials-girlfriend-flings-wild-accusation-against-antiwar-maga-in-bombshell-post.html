Trump official’s girlfriend flings wild accusation against antiwar MAGA in bombshell post

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 26, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

(REUTERS)

Alexis Wilkins, the girlfriend of former podcaster and now FBI head Kash Patel, accused the anti-war enclave of MAGA of being under the influence of foreign nations on X Tuesday, notably Russia.

“A foreign-linked influence network has been running coordinated operations against the Trump administration for 22 months,” said Wilkins, who recently garnered criticism for having “a security detail made up of elite FBI agents usually assigned to a SWAT team.” I know it’s real because they ran one against me. I was targeted in something I knew was far from organic. This level of media is isolating, unwanted, and unwarranted.”

Wilkins, who is a country singer, claimed she “spent the last few months learning to build programs to utilize publicly available information to prove that this is way bigger than me,” and said the effort involves Trump former advisor Michale Flynn, as well as MAGA influencers Tucker Carlson, Joe Kent and Candace Owens — all of whom disapprove of what appears to be Trump’s fealty to Israel in the Iran invasion.

“This is about creating chaos in the Republican Party. It’s about the organized effort to lose Republicans the midterms and subvert President Trump’s agenda, and I have the data for you to see,” said Wilkins, who said she was “falsely labeled a Mossad spy” in a coordinated campaign designed to isolate a Trump official by “using the person publicly nearest to him.”

What follows in Wilkins’ posts is an exhaustive trail of X submissions claiming to reveal “the trail” of the foreign influence network.

One notable step in the trail involves the September 2025 assassination of MAGA influencer Charlie Kirk and the claim by influencer Candace Owens publicly attributing his death to Israel and her targeting of Kirk’s widow for incrimination.

Wilkins noted many of the same accounts “in the July 2025 honeypot” activated immediately to amplify Owens’ claims in a manner that converted “natural Republican unity … into one of the most sustained fracture points of the year.”

“The goal of this operation is not to win a political argument, but to make the fractures feel permanent. To make Republicans believe their movement is over. To make soldiers feel the war isn’t worth fighting. To make the government and its officials look unstable and create media that creates more problematic media,” said Wilkins. “Each chapter hits a different pressure point. The network, the timing, and the Russian amplification are consistent across all five. That consistency is the signature.”

“This doesn’t even scratch the surface of who might be getting paid by whom - this is all matrixed publicly available information,” insisted Wilkins. “MAGA doesn’t have an approval problem. It has an infiltration problem.”
