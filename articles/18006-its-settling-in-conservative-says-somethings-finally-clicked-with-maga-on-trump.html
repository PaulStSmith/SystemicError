'It’s settling in': Conservative says something’s finally clicked with MAGA on Trump

By Paulo Santos

The Teflon Cult of Trump: Unpacking the Loyalty Paradigm

The Illusion of Defection

Recent remarks from conservative commentators suggest a brewing discontent within the MAGA ranks, spurred by Donald Trump’s blasphemous self-depictions and contentious policies. Yet, this narrative of potential defection needs scrutiny. Trump’s core support has weathered far more egregious scandals and policy flops. The real question isn’t whether a few disillusioned voices signal a breaking point, but why, despite everything, the structure of his support remains largely intact.

Cult of Personality Over Policy

The phenomenon of Trump’s enduring appeal is not rooted in his policy achievements, which are often divisive or outright failures, but rather in his skill in maintaining a cult of personality. Critics and commentators like Jim Geraghty from The Washington Post recognize that Trump’s support hinges less on substantive policy and more on his pugnacious attitude and a few “immovable north stars” like immigration and tariffs. This configuration allows him to navigate through numerous controversies with his base largely unscathed.

Misdirection and Media Manipulation

Trump’s portrayal as a quasi-messianic figure and the subsequent backlash it generated on platforms like Truth Social highlight another layer of his strategy: media manipulation. The imagery and its inherent controversy serve as a distraction from more substantive policy critiques and failures. By focusing on the spectacle, both the media and the public may overlook deeper, more systemic issues within his leadership and the policies he promotes.

The Real Inputs and Outputs

Amanda Carpenter’s suggestion of a FOIA request to uncover the origins of Trump’s Christ-like imagery underscores the calculated nature of these media stunts. They are not random acts of hubris but deliberate attempts to provoke and command the media narrative. Whether these tactics are the brainchild of Trump or his advisors, they are intentional acts designed to reinforce his constructed deity status among followers.

Why Defections are Unlikely

Despite occasional ripples of dissent, mass defections from Trump’s base are improbable. The allegiance to Trump is less about allegiance to conservative ideology and more about fealty to an individual who has successfully positioned himself as the irreplaceable leader of a movement. As Geraghty and others have pointed out, Trump’s assertion that “MAGA is me” is not just a boast but a precise articulation of how deeply personal loyalty and identity are intertwined in his political brand.

Conclusion: The Durability of the Trump Phenomenon

The discussions among conservative figures about potential cracks in Trump’s support base often miss the larger point: Trump’s political survival strategy is not grounded in traditional political maneuvering but in cultivating a deep-seated identity cult. This approach ensures that even as scandals emerge and policies fail, his core base remains loyal, viewing any criticism as an attack on their collective identity rather than a legitimate political critique. The real systemic issue is not whether Trump can maintain his base but what this says about the evolving nature of political loyalty and leadership in American politics.
