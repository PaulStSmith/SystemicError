'Treasonous': Naomi Klein reveals how billionaires are 'provoking' societal collapse

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor tell The Guardian that corporate city-states are back in fashion thanks to Trump politics indulging tax-hating millionaires and billionaires to start their own high-tech fiefdoms.

A “glorified gated communit[ies]” like Próspera on a Honduran island and Elon Musk’s distant proposal of “a handful of people and robots eke[ing] out survival on two barren orbs (a radically depleted Earth and a terraformed Mars) appear to be catching on with ultra-wealthy members of the Trump circle.

End-time communities were on the rise in 2023, when a campaigning Donald Trump promised to hold a contest that would lead to the creation of 10 “freedom cities” on federal lands. And since election, “would-be country starters have been on a lobbying blitz, determined to turn Trump’s pledge into reality.” Próspera Chief of Staff Trey Goff called the new energy in DC “absolutely electric, and legislation “paving the way for a bevy of corporate city-states” complete by the end of the year, Klein writes.

It’s a fantasy word evolved from the “doom-prepping” movement of the first Trump administration, only now fueled by ultra-wealthy “religious end-timers.” It also hastens the degradation of the only world humanity realistically has, Klein and Taylor say.

“Indeed, in a strange twist on the Old Testament tale, Musk and his fellow tech billionaires, having arrogated god-like powers to themselves, aren’t content to just build the arks. They appear to be doing their best to cause the flood. Today’s right-wing leaders and their rich allies are not just taking advantage of catastrophes, shock-doctrine and disaster-capitalism style, but simultaneously provoking and planning for them,” says Klein, a Canadian author.

There is no second planet to occupy, however, and reducing humanity to a tiny dome on a distant island or mountain peak requires a commitment to letting a huge swathe of humanity die. So, how do you break the apocalyptic fever?

“… We help each other face the depth of the depravity that has gripped the hard right in all of our countries,” Klein writes. “We must first understand … [that] we are up against an ideology that has given up not only on the premise and promise of liberal democracy but on the livability of our shared world – on its beauty, on its people, on our children, on other species. The forces we are up against have made peace with mass death. They are treasonous to this world and its human and non-human inhabitants.”

Second, Klein advises countering “their apocalyptic narratives with a far better story about how to survive the hard times ahead without leaving anyone behind. A story capable of draining end times fascism of its gothic power and galvanizing a movement ready to put it all on the line for our collective survival. A story not of end times, but of better times; not of separation and supremacy, but of interdependence and belonging; not of escaping, but staying put and staying faithful to the troubled earthly reality in which we are enmeshed and bound.”

Our Analysis:

Corporate Fiefdoms: The Ultra-Wealthy's New Playground

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor's conversation with The Guardian sheds light on a disturbing trend that's been gaining traction among the ultra-rich, thanks in no small part to the political climate nurtured by Donald Trump. These modern-day corporate city-states, like Próspera and Elon Musk's Mars ambitions, are not just the latest fad in luxury living—they are a bold statement of separatism and elitism, dressed up in the guise of innovation and progress.

The Return of the Feudal Lords

The concept of billionaires creating their own high-tech enclaves is reminiscent of nothing so much as medieval feudal lords carving out their domains. Only instead of castles and knights, we have gated communities and robots. It's a glorified gated community on steroids, where the rich can isolate themselves from the world's problems—problems they often had a hand in creating.

Trump's "Freedom Cities" initiative isn't just a political promise; it's a dangerous endorsement of this separatist ideology. It's as if the U.S. government is rolling out the red carpet for these would-be kings and their private kingdoms, complete with legislation to seal the deal. Trey Goff's comment about the "absolutely electric" energy in DC speaks volumes about the eagerness with which these projects are being pursued.

Engineering the Apocalypse

Klein's assertion that these tech billionaires, Musk included, are not just preparing for the apocalypse but actively hastening its arrival is chilling. This isn't just about doom-prepping; it's about creating a self-fulfilling prophecy. The environmental and social degradation that results from this kind of mega-wealth hoarding and isolationism is not a byproduct—it's a feature.

The idea that there is no "Plan B" planet for humanity is lost on these modern-day Noahs, who seem hell-bent on floating away in their arks while the world drowns. Their commitment to "letting a huge swathe of humanity die" is not just selfish—it's genocidal.

Breaking the Fever

Klein's call to action is more than just a plea for sanity; it's a battle cry against an ideology that sees the world and its inhabitants as expendable. The antidote to this "apocalyptic fever" isn't just understanding the depth of the depravity we're facing but actively countering it with a narrative of hope, interdependence, and collective survival.

The vision Klein and Taylor propose—one of interdependence and belonging rather than separation and supremacy—is not just aspirational. It's necessary. In a world where the ultra-wealthy plot their escape from a disaster of their own making, the rest of us are left to either succumb to despair or band together and fight for a better future.

Conclusion

The emergence of corporate city-states is a symptom of a much larger disease: a society that glorifies wealth and power at the expense of community and sustainability. If we are to survive the hard times ahead, we must reject the feudal fantasies of the ultra-rich and embrace a future where no one is left behind. The battle against "end times fascism" is not just ideological—it's existential.
