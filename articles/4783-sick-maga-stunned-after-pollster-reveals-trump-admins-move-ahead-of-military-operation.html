‘Sick’: MAGA stunned after pollster reveals Trump admin’s move ahead of military operation

Data journalist and pollster Richard Baris claimed on Saturday that ahead of the unprecedented U.S. attack on Iran, the Trump administration had reviewed polling on how many American casualties voters would be “willing to accept in a war with Iran,” the findings of which rattled a number of MAGA loyalists.

“Polling reviewed by the administration asked how many American casualties voters were willing to accept in a war with Iran,” Baris wrote in a social media post on X Saturday morning, several hours after President Donald Trump had authorized strikes on Iran.

“The answer was ZERO. Americans demanded ZERO casualties because Americans did NOT support a war. That’s how stupid this was.”

Marjorie Taylor Greene, the former Republican member of Congress from Georgia and devout MAGA faithful, responded to the apparent revelation with shock, claiming that Trump’s efforts to enact regime change in Iran were counter to what MAGA stood for.

“The Trump admin actually asked in a poll how many casualties voters were willing to accept in a war with Iran???” Greene wrote Saturday in a social media post on X. “How about ZERO you bunch of sick f------ liars. We voted for America First and ZERO wars.”

Efforts are currently underway to halt the Trump administration’s military operation in Iran – dubbed Operation Epic Fury – including a War Powers Resolution – a legislative tool to check the president’s authority to declare war – that has steadily grown support among both Democratic and Republican lawmakers.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Irony of Polling Wars

In a move that reeks of both desperation and detachment, the Trump administration reportedly delved into the murky waters of public opinion polling to gauge American tolerance for casualties in a potential conflict with Iran. Richard Baris, a data journalist, blew the whistle on this egregious approach to war-making, revealing that the administration sought to quantify the acceptable number of American lives to be sacrificed on the altar of foreign policy objectives. This revelation, however, does more than just expose the administration’s callousness; it underscores a profound misunderstanding of the American public’s appetite for war.

Zero Casualties: The Impossibility of a Bloodless War

The findings, as reported by Baris, laid bare an undeniable truth: Americans demanded zero casualties. This expectation is both a testament to the public’s aversion to conflict and a damning indictment of the administration’s disconnect with reality. War, by its very nature, is destructive and deadly. The concept of a “bloodless” military engagement is a fantasy, particularly in a scenario as volatile as a conflict with Iran. The administration’s pursuit of such polling is not just “stupid,” as Baris aptly puts it; it’s a dangerous fantasy that belies a reckless disregard for human life.

MAGA and the Anti-War Stance

The reaction from Marjorie Taylor Greene, a figure synonymous with the MAGA movement, is particularly noteworthy. Her shock and condemnation echo a sentiment that might surprise some observers: a portion of the MAGA base’s opposition to overseas military engagements. Greene’s assertion that “We voted for America First and ZERO wars” serves as a reminder that the America First rhetoric, at least in theory, prioritizes domestic well-being over international adventurism. Yet, the administration’s actions, as exposed through this polling endeavor, starkly contradict this principle.

Operation Epic Fury and the War Powers Resolution

Amidst this controversy is the unfolding saga of Operation Epic Fury, a military operation that has ignited bipartisan opposition in Congress. The growing support for a War Powers Resolution is a clear indication of the legislative branch’s increasing unease with the executive’s unilateral military actions. This development is significant, not just as a check on this specific operation, but as a broader statement against executive overreach in matters of war and peace.

The Role of Pollsters in War

Baris’s role in bringing this information to light should not be understated. As a respected figure in polling and data analysis, his revelation adds a layer of credibility to the claims. Yet, it also raises questions about the ethical implications of polling on matters of life and death. The fact that such a poll was even considered speaks volumes about the administration’s priorities and its approach to governance.

Conclusion: A Stark Reminder

The revelation that the Trump administration sought to poll the American public on their willingness to accept casualties in a war with Iran is a stark reminder of the dangers of politicizing military action. It highlights a fundamental misunderstanding of both the nature of war and the will of the American people. As efforts to halt Operation Epic Fury continue, one can only hope that this episode serves as a cautionary tale for future administrations: war is not a matter for polls, but for grave deliberation and, above all, a last resort.
