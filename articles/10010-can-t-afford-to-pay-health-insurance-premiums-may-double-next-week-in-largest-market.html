'Can't afford to pay': Health insurance premiums may double next week in largest market

Americans in the largest health insurance market could see their premiums double in price virtually overnight, depending on what Congress does over the next few days.

Politico reported Friday that hundreds of thousands of people in California could see a significant spike in their health insurance premiums at the end of the month, due to the possibility of both a federal government shutdown and a funding bill being passed without an extension of Affordable Care Act (ACA, or Obamacare) subsidies. When open enrollment begins in November, those buying health insurance in the Golden State could experience significant sticker shock should those subsidies not be renewed.

“It’s going to cause a lot of grief, a lot of anxiety,” Martha Santana-Chin, who is the CEO of the L.A. Care insurance plan, told Politico. “Quite frankly, there’s not a whole lot we can do about that if people just simply can’t afford to pay.”

Because the subsidies keep plans affordable, those subsidies expiring could lead to as many as 400,000 Californians — almost a quarter of all enrollees in the Golden State – dropping their health insurance plans over a matter of months. This would cause premiums for those still on their insurance to spike, as most people who drop their insurance are typically younger and healthier. With that population gone, the risk pool for insurers automatically becomes older and sicker by default.

Jessica Altman, who is the executive director of Covered California (the state-run health insurance exchange) said there is roughly $190 million set aside to help state residents cover insurance funding gaps. However, she lamented that even that amount would be a drop in the bucket compared to the estimated $2.5 billion in lost funds should Congress not extend the ACA subsidies.

“I feel very privileged to be in a state that’s putting that amount forward for affordability, particularly in the context of a challenging fiscal situation in the state budget,” she said. “But we shouldn’t pretend it’s going to fully fill the hole ... we will lose a lot of people.”

There could be even greater numbers of people dropping off of the insurance rolls in the coming years, thanks to language in President Donald Trump’s “One Big Beautiful Bill Act.” That law will make it so Americans have to manually opt into insurance coverage, as opposed to the current system that automatically enrolls Golden State residents. Politico reported that roughly 70 percent of Covered California customers were automatically enrolled.

Republicans have indicated a willingness to discuss extending the subsidies, though not as part of the must-pass funding bill due on Trump’s desk by Tuesday. The subsidies will permanently expire by the end of the calendar year if no action is taken.

Our Analysis:

The Precipice of a Healthcare Catastrophe: ACA Subsidies Hang in the Balance

In an alarming development that puts hundreds of thousands of Californians at risk of losing their health insurance, Politico sheds light on a looming healthcare crisis. As Congress dithers, the fate of Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies hangs in the balance, threatening to catapult insurance premiums to astronomical heights.

A Ticking Time Bomb

The report paints a dire picture: without congressional action to extend ACA subsidies, premiums in California’s health insurance market could double overnight. This isn’t just about numbers on a page; it’s about the real, tangible anxiety and hardship that will befall those who find healthcare suddenly out of reach. Martha Santana-Chin’s words capture the essence of the impending disaster, underscoring the powerlessness felt by many in the face of governmental inaction.

The Domino Effect

The potential expiration of subsidies doesn’t just mean pricier premiums for the few but heralds a systemic unraveling that could see 400,000 Californians—almost a quarter of all enrollees—forced to abandon their health plans. This exodus, largely comprising the younger and healthier demographic, will distort the risk pool, leaving insurers covering an older, sicker population. The result? Even higher premiums for those left, spiraling into a vicious cycle of unaffordability and uninsurance.

A Drop in the Ocean

The state’s effort to mitigate this catastrophe through a $190 million stopgap is commendable but ultimately insufficient against the staggering $2.5 billion shortfall anticipated. Jessica Altman’s acknowledgment of this grim reality highlights a broader issue at play: the systemic underfunding and piecemeal approach to healthcare in America.

Legislative Lethargy and Partisan Politics

The article points to a legislative landscape marred by inertia and political gamesmanship. The mention of President Trump’s “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” and its provisions for manual insurance enrollment adds insult to injury, setting the stage for even greater disenrollment and instability in the insurance market.

It’s particularly telling that Republicans express a theoretical willingness to discuss extending the subsidies, yet balk at the idea of including such measures in a must-pass funding bill. This detachment from the urgency of the situation reveals a disturbing disregard for the well-being of millions.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The Politico report serves as a stark reminder of the fragility of healthcare access in the United States. The potential doubling of health insurance premiums in California is not just a state issue but a national emergency that underscores the need for comprehensive healthcare reform.

As the deadline looms and Congress remains at an impasse, one thing is clear: the cost of inaction is measured in human lives. It’s a price too steep to pay. We must demand more from our leaders, insisting on immediate and decisive action to extend ACA subsidies and safeguard the health of millions. The time for political brinkmanship is over; the stakes are simply too high.
