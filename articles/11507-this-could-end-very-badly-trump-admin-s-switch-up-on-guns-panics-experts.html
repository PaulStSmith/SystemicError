'This could end very badly': Trump admin's switch up on guns panics experts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Militarization of Washington, D.C.

A person shooting a gun (image via Shutterstock).

The Wall Street Journal reported on Saturday that the Trump administration had switched its position on National Guard soldiers carrying guns as they take control of Washington, DC, prompting analysts to panic.

According to the conservative news outlet's reporting, "The shift comes after defense officials said the soldiers deployed to the capital wouldn’t be armed."

"More National Guard troops are heading soon to Washington, D.C., and they are preparing to start carrying weapons in the coming days, officials say, a major shift that comes days after President Trump said he was deploying them to 'take back' the capital from what he described as violent criminals," the report states. "Defense officials had previously said the 800 National Guard soldiers deployed wouldn’t be armed, unlike many federal law-enforcement agents sent to the capital. They also weren’t to have weapons in their vehicles. 'Weapons are available if needed but will remain in the armory,' the U.S. Army said in a press release Thursday."

That report caught the attention of legal analyst Ryan Goodman, who is a chaired professor at NYU Law and a former chaired professor at Harvard Law, as well as a former special counsel for the Department of Defense.

"This could end up very badly given what we know of Rules on Use of Force (RUF) for Guard in DC," Goodman said Saturday. "And different training regimens compared to local police."

Liberal commentator Wajahat Ali also chimed in, warning that "Trump and MAGA will overreach."

"This is already happening. Americans are going to see our soldiers rough up and potentially shoot innocent civilians," Ali wrote. "Republicans think people won't care. As the economy turns, people are terrorized, and Epstein files aren't released...they will."

MAGA accounts, on the other hand, celebrated the news. JOHN Golden Maga, for instance, said, "I REALLY hope this is true."

"The National Guard NEEDS to be armed in a vioIent[sic] city like Washington. No way to restore law and order otherwise!" the MAGA user added.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Militarization of Washington, D.C.

The Wall Street Journal's report on the Trump administration's abrupt policy shift regarding the arming of National Guard soldiers in Washington, D.C., is a stark testament to the escalating militarization under the guise of restoring order. This decision, veiled in the rhetoric of combating "violent criminals," marks a dangerous pivot towards an increasingly armed federal presence in the nation's capital. Let's dissect the layers of hypocrisy, fear-mongering, and potential consequences embedded in this development.

The Shift in Policy: A Dangerous Precedent

Initially, defense officials assured the public that the 800 National Guard soldiers deployed to the capital would not be armed, a stance that underscored a commitment to peacekeeping over aggression. This promise was quickly overturned, betraying a disturbing readiness to escalate the military's role in domestic law enforcement. The justification? An ambiguous need to "take back" the capital, as declared by President Trump. This language not only exaggerates the threat but also dangerously blurs the lines between military and police roles.

Expert Warnings: Ignored at Our Peril

The concerns raised by legal analyst Ryan Goodman and liberal commentator Wajahat Ali spotlight the potentially catastrophic consequences of this policy shift. Goodman's warning about the mismatch between the National Guard's use of force rules and their training compared to local police speaks volumes about the risks of confusion, misapplication of force, and unintended violence. Ali's grim prediction of soldiers "rough[ing] up and potentially shoot[ing] innocent civilians" is not an exaggeration but a plausible outcome of deploying armed military personnel with potentially limited restraint in civilian contexts.

MAGA Cheerleaders: Misplaced Enthusiasm or Willful Ignorance?

The celebration among MAGA supporters, as exemplified by JOHN Golden Maga's comments, reveals a disturbing disregard for the principles of democracy and civil liberties. The eagerness to see the National Guard armed in Washington, D.C., underlines a troubling inclination towards authoritarian measures. The belief that arming the National Guard is the only way to restore "law and order" is a myopic view that ignores the broader implications for civil rights and the dangerous precedent it sets for using military force against American citizens.

The Bigger Picture: A Divided Nation

The Trump administration's decision to arm the National Guard in Washington, D.C., is a manifestation of a broader strategy to stoke fear, divide the nation, and consolidate power by portraying the capital as under siege by "violent criminals." This narrative, unsupported by evidence, serves to justify extreme measures that threaten to erode civil liberties and further militarize the public space. The implications are dire: a country where the military is used not just to protect but to intimidate, where the line between peacekeeping and military occupation blurs, and where the voices of peaceful protesters are drowned out by the sound of gunfire.

In conclusion, the Wall Street Journal's report sheds light on a troubling escalation towards militarization in response to civil unrest. The decision to arm the National Guard in Washington, D.C., is not only a dangerous escalation but also a profound mistake that could have lasting repercussions for American democracy. It is imperative that we scrutinize this shift critically, recognize the potential dangers it poses, and demand accountability for decisions that push us further from the ideals of justice and closer to the edge of authoritarianism.
