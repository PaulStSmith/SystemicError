The Department of Homeland Security has acquired two luxury jets at a cost of $172 million to be used by DHS Secretary Kristi Noem and other top agency officials, new documents reviewed by The New York Times revealed Saturday.

According to the documents, the two jets are used Gulfstream G700s, which its manufacturer touts as having the “most spacious cabin in the industry.” They seat up to 13 passengers, include a “whisper-quiet cabin with 100% fresh, plasma-ionized air” and the largest windows “in business aviation.”

A DHS spokesperson told the Times in its report Saturday that the purchase was made as “a matter of safety” given that the agency’s existing jet was “well beyond operational usage hours for a corporate aircraft.”

For two Democratic lawmakers, however, the purchase was inappropriate, particularly amid the ongoing government shutdown, and without transparency around where the funding had come from.

“In addition to raising serious questions about your ability to effectively lead an agency whose procurement strategies appear to vary on a whim, the procurement of new luxury jets for your use suggests that the [United States Coast Guard] has been directed to prioritize your own comfort above the U.S.C.G.’s operational needs, even during a government shutdown,” reads a letter sent to Noem and signed by Reps. Rosa DeLaura (D-CT) and Lauren Underwood (D-IL).

“We are deeply concerned about your judgment, leadership priorities, and responsibility as a steward of taxpayer dollars.”

The two lawmakers go on in the letter to demand more information of the purchase, including the timeline for the jets’ delivery, the source of funding for the purchase, and the names of those who reviewed or approved the purchase.

“Indeed, from choosing to live rent-free in the Commandant’s quarters, to defending Mr. Lewandowski’s refusal to provide basic information about his special government employee status, to your frequent appearances at ongoing operations which require a diversion of resources to focus on your security, it appears you are routinely prioritizing yourself and those closest to you over the needs of the USCG servicemembers who protect this nation,” the letter reads.

Our Analysis:

Lavish Spending in the Lap of Luxury: DHS’s $172 Million Jet Fiasco

In what can only be described as a staggering display of fiscal irresponsibility, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has splurged a whopping $172 million on two used Gulfstream G700 jets. These aren’t just any jets; they’re the epitome of luxury, boasting features that scream excess, from the “most spacious cabin in the industry” to a “whisper-quiet cabin with 100% fresh, plasma-ionized air” and what are claimed to be the largest windows in business aviation. This revelation, brought to light by The New York Times, isn’t just a story about jets. It’s a narrative of misplaced priorities, a disdain for transparency, and a blatant disregard for the taxpayers footing the bill.

The Safety Excuse

A DHS spokesperson, perhaps realizing the absurdity of defending such an extravagant purchase, trotted out the defense that this was all “a matter of safety.” They claim the agency’s existing jet was “well beyond operational usage hours for a corporate aircraft.” This argument might have held water, had the replacement been a modest, function-over-form choice. Instead, DHS opted for the Gulfstream G700s—jets that are more synonymous with billionaire moguls than government officials concerned with safety.

Democratic Lawmakers Cry Foul

Reps. Rosa DeLaura (D-CT) and Lauren Underwood (D-IL) have been vocal in their criticism, and rightfully so. Their scathing letter to DHS Secretary Kristi Noem highlights not just the questionable timing amid a government shutdown but also the complete lack of transparency regarding the funding source for these jets. It’s a slap in the face to the principles of accountability and fiscal responsibility. Their words cut to the heart of the issue: “...the procurement of new luxury jets for your use suggests that the [United States Coast Guard] has been directed to prioritize your own comfort above the U.S.C.G.’s operational needs, even during a government shutdown.”

A Pattern of Self-Prioritization

This isn’t an isolated incident. The letter from DeLaura and Underwood paints a damning picture of Noem’s tenure, marked by a series of decisions that seem to place her comfort and convenience above the needs of the servicemembers she’s supposed to lead. From living rent-free in the Commandant’s quarters to defending dubious government employee statuses, it’s a troubling pattern of behavior for someone at the helm of the DHS.

The Bottom Line

The DHS’s decision to purchase these luxury jets is more than just a questionable allocation of resources; it’s a symptom of a deeper malaise within the agency—a culture that prioritizes the whims of its top officials over the operational needs of its servicemembers and the fiscal responsibility owed to American taxpayers.

In a time when transparency and accountability should be at the forefront of every government action, this episode stands out as a stark reminder of how easily those principles can be cast aside. As taxpayers, we must demand better. As citizens, we should expect nothing less than a full accounting of not only this purchase but also a commitment to reversing this pattern of self-prioritization at the expense of public trust and fiscal responsibility.
