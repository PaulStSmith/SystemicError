Trump tariffs get crippling blow in Supreme Court ruling

The Supreme Court delivered a significant setback to President Donald Trump on Friday, ruling that he exceeded his constitutional authority when imposing broad tariffs under emergency powers legislation.

The justices voted 6-3 that Trump’s tariff approach violated the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, a 1977 law governing presidential emergency authority over economic matters.

Chief Justice John Roberts authored the majority opinion, joined by three liberal justices and two conservative colleagues, Justices Neil Gorsuch and Amy Coney Barrett.

Roberts stated: “The president asserts the extraordinary power to unilaterally impose tariffs of unlimited amount, duration and scope.” He noted that the Trump administration “points to no statute” in which Congress previously authorized IEEPA language to apply to tariffs.

Justices Clarence Thomas, Brett Kavanaugh, and Samuel Alito dissented.

The decision marks a rare Supreme Court defeat for the Trump administration, which has faced minimal judicial obstacles during its second term despite the court’s 6-3 conservative majority.

The ruling’s scope is limited. Tariffs imposed on steel and aluminum under different statutory authority remain intact. However, the decision invalidates two major tariff categories: country-specific “reciprocal” tariffs ranging from 34 percent for China to 10 percent baseline rates for other nations, and a 25 percent tariff on selected Canadian, Chinese, and Mexican goods justified by fentanyl trafficking concerns.

Trump retains the option to reimpose the struck-down tariffs under alternative legal authorities.

Our Analysis:

Supreme Court Checks Trump’s Tariff Power: A Closer Look

In a landmark decision, the Supreme Court has effectively clipped the wings of President Donald Trump’s tariff regime, declaring that his broad application of tariffs under the guise of emergency powers breaches constitutional bounds. The ruling, emerging from a 6-3 vote, not only underscores the limitations of presidential authority but also signals a significant judicial rebuke to the Trump administration’s often unilateral approach to economic policy.

The Core of the Ruling

At the heart of the Supreme Court’s decision is the assertion that Trump overstepped the boundaries outlined in the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) of 1977. This act, which was designed to provide checks on the president’s emergency economic powers, became the fulcrum of the court’s rebuke. Chief Justice John Roberts, leading the majority opinion, didn’t mince words, stating, “The president asserts the extraordinary power to unilaterally impose tariffs of unlimited amount, duration, and scope.” This assertion, as Roberts pointed out, lacked any congressional backing, especially concerning the application of tariffs.

The Dissent and Its Implications

As expected, the court’s ruling wasn’t unanimous. Justices Clarence Thomas, Brett Kavanaugh, and Samuel Alito stood in opposition, marking a clear ideological divide within the court. This dissent, however, does little to mask the broader significance of the ruling: even a Supreme Court with a 6-3 conservative majority can find fault with the Trump administration’s expansive view of executive power.

A Rare Judicial Setback

It’s crucial to highlight the rarity of this defeat for Trump. The administration, particularly in its second term, has navigated through a sea of judicial challenges with relative ease, buoyed by the conservative tilt of the Supreme Court. This decision, therefore, isn’t just a setback; it’s a resounding statement that there are still judicial limits to the executive’s reach, especially in the realm of economic policy.

The Scope and Future Implications

While the ruling draws a line in the sand regarding the use of IEEPA for imposing tariffs, it’s important to note its limited scope. Tariffs imposed under different statutory authorities, such as those on steel and aluminum, remain unaffected. However, the decision does strike down significant tariff categories, particularly those targeting China, Canada, and Mexico, under various justifications including fentanyl trafficking concerns.

Trump’s response to this legal check on his authority could potentially include attempts to reimpose these tariffs under alternative legal frameworks. Such maneuvers would not only invite further judicial scrutiny but also signal a continued commitment to a unilateral approach to trade policy.

Conclusion

The Supreme Court’s ruling is a stark reminder of the checks and balances that define the American political system. By rebuking Trump’s overreach, the court has not only asserted its role as a constitutional safeguard but also highlighted the contentious nature of the administration’s tariff policies. As the dust settles, the implications of this decision will undoubtedly resonate, both in the halls of power and across the global economic landscape.
