Trump is trapped and losing — and lashing out like an enraged child

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 30, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Tantrum Is the Policy

The Setup

The source story centers on Trump’s latest humiliation spiral: a war in Iran he launched without Congress, a retaliatory trade war against Canada, and a growing list of institutions refusing to bend. That is the surface. The deeper story is simpler and uglier: an imperial presidency is being used as a personal grievance machine, and the consequences are being exported to everyone else.

Power Is the Point

Trump is not a powerless man “lashing out.” He is the person with the institutional power to start a war, impose tariffs, threaten broadcasters, and pressure public institutions. That is the decisive fact. The damage does not come from his mood. It comes from the office he occupies and the machinery around him that still treats his impulses as policy.

The Decoy of Personality

The article’s weakest move is its obsession with Trump’s psyche. Calling him childish, loony, or humiliated may feel accurate, but it risks misdirecting attention from the structure that lets childishness govern. The problem is not that he is a bully in the abstract. The problem is that the state has been adapted to reward bullying, and too many actors keep waiting for him to self-correct.

Who Pays for His Ego

Canada retaliated with tariffs aimed largely at Republican states. That detail matters because it shows how authoritarian vanity gets translated into material harm. Trump can treat trade as a stage prop; workers, exporters, and consumers absorb the cost. The same pattern runs through the Iran escalation: unilateral force, no credible consultation, no coherent exit, then a scramble to control the fallout after the damage is already done.

The Institutions That Are Still Resisting

The courts, Congress, universities, ABC, the bond market, and even foreign governments appear in the source as separate sources of friction. Taken together, they are not random obstacles. They are the remaining institutions refusing to normalize open abuse. The story is not that Trump is uniquely chaotic in a vacuum. It is that the system is finally encountering the limits of its own earlier obedience.

Systemic Error

The real political lesson is that authoritarian politics does not require competence. It only requires a leader willing to weaponize state power against anyone who denies him the appearance of dominance. Trump’s rage is not a distraction from governance; it is how he governs. The editorial instinct to mock him is understandable, but the sharper truth is more dangerous: if institutions keep absorbing that rage as mere spectacle, they will keep converting personal humiliation into public ruin.
