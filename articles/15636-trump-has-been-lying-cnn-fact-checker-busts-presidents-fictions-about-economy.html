'Trump has been lying': CNN fact-checker busts president’s 'fictions' about economy

During the 2024 presidential race, Donald Trump hammered then-President Joe Biden and then-Vice President Kamala Harris relentlessly on the economy and blamed them for inflation. The messaging worked: Trump narrowly defeated Democratic nominee Harris and is now back in the White House, where he is claiming that prices are plummeting under his second presidency.

But CNN's Daniel Dale debunked Trump's claims in a comprehensive fact-check aired early Friday morning, April 25.

Trump claimed that the prices of eggs "has come down like 93, 94 percent" since Biden left office on January 20. But Dale countered that egg prices are increasing, not decreasing, on Trump's watch.

"The price of eggs isn't down even close to 93 percent or 94 percent, nor even 87 percent since Trump returned to office in January," Dale explained. "Let's look at the prices consumers are actually paying. now, we only have this data through March, but the March data was bad. it showed the national average price for a dozen eggs hit a record high, about $6.23, and that was up 26 percent from January. It is very possible that these retail egg prices have come down in April. We'll see when those numbers come out next month, but anybody who has shopped for groceries this month can tell you they're certainly not down 93 percent from Trump's Inauguration Day, because if they were down that much, eggs were currently be selling for less than 38 cents a dozen. they clearly are not."

Dale added, "Now, the White House has been pointing to wholesale egg prices. Those prices are way down since trump's inauguration, about 52 percent. That's big. But again, those aren't the prices actually paid by consumers. And of course, 52 percent isn't 87 or 93 percent."

The CNN fact-checker also debunked Trump's claims about gas prices.

"Gas, eggs and groceries — President Trump has been lying about the price of all of them," Dale noted. "Trump has been trying to play down widespread concerns that his tariffs will fuel inflation. And listen to what he said this week and last week about the price of all of them."

Trump claimed that gas prices "hit $1.98 a gallon." But according to Dale, "This $1.98 figure is fiction. On the days the president specified he was talking about, April 16 and 17, zero states had an average gas price even close to $1.98. The lowest state average each day, according to AAA, was $2.70. Now, it's true the president didn't say he was talking about average prices. So you might think, ' Well, maybe he meant there were a bunch of individual gas stations selling $1.98 gas.' That isn't true either. Gas Buddy, a company that tracks tens of thousands of stations nationwide, told me it found zero stations anywhere in the country, selling at even close to $1.98 on either day. In fact, the lowest price it found at any station was $2.19."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Exaggerations: A Fact-Check

In the aftermath of the 2024 presidential race, we've seen Donald Trump's return to the White House, accompanied by a flurry of claims about plummeting prices under his administration. Yet, a recent fact-check by CNN's Daniel Dale has pulled the curtain back on these assertions, particularly regarding egg and gas prices, revealing a reality starkly different from the one Trump has painted.

The Egg-aggeration

Trump's claim that egg prices have nosedived "like 93, 94 percent" since he reclaimed the presidency is nothing short of fantastical. According to Dale, not only have egg prices not plummeted to the absurd depths Trump suggests, but they have actually climbed to a record high. With the national average price for a dozen eggs hitting approximately $6.23 in March, up 26 percent since January, the numbers starkly contradict Trump's narrative. Even considering the potential for price drops in April, the notion that prices have fallen by more than 90 percent is ludicrous—a claim so blatantly false it borders on the comical.

Gaslighting on Gas Prices

Moving on to gas prices, Trump's claims fare no better under scrutiny. Trump's assertion of gas prices hitting $1.98 a gallon turns out to be a work of fiction, with data from AAA and Gas Buddy showing the lowest state average and station prices well above this figure in mid-April. This fabrication is particularly egregious given the tangible impact of gas prices on everyday Americans, making Trump's misinformation not just misleading, but harmful.

The Bigger Picture

Trump's attempt to paint a rosy picture of the economy under his watch by distorting facts is a tactic we've seen before. It's a strategy that relies on the hope that bold claims will overshadow the reality of the situation. However, the discrepancy between Trump's assertions and the facts not only underscores a disconcerting detachment from reality but also a blatant disregard for the intelligence of the American public.

The White House's focus on wholesale egg prices, while ignoring the retail realities faced by consumers, further highlights this disconnect. While a 52 percent drop in wholesale prices is significant, it's a moot point for the average American if it doesn't translate to savings at the checkout. This cherry-picking of data is misleading and fails to provide a truthful or comprehensive overview of the economic landscape.

Conclusion: A Fact-Free Zone?

The fact-check by Daniel Dale serves as a crucial counterpoint to Trump's narrative, emphasizing the importance of scrutinizing claims made by those in power. It's a reminder that in an era of misinformation, fact-checking is not just valuable but vital. Trump's claims about plummeting prices under his administration unravel under the slightest examination, revealing a reality far removed from the rosy picture he attempts to paint.

In a political climate rife with misinformation, the truth often becomes the first casualty. It's our responsibility to cut through the noise, challenge unfounded claims, and hold leaders accountable for their words and actions. As we move forward, let us not be swayed by falsehoods, but rather, be guided by facts, evidence, and reality.
