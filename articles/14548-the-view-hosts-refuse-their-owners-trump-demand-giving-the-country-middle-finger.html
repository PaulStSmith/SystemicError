The View hosts refuse their owners' Trump demand: 'Giving the country middle finger'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Whoopi Goldberg whips out pocket Constitution (Photo: Screen capture via ABC News video)

A co-host of "The View" doesn't appear to be backing down from requests to quiet their attacks on President Donald Trump.

"The View" is one of the most-watched daytime talk shows, but ABC News recently asked the women to back off of criticizing Trump, according to a Daily Beast report Wednesday.

On Thursday, in the middle of their first segment, which attacked Trump for accepting a $400 million plane from Qatar, Whoopi Goldberg whipped out a pocket Constitution to complain that the plane violates the Emoluments Clause.

Without referencing the requests from ABC executives, Goldberg explained that some things needed to be said.

ALSO READ: Top Dem has new plan for Trump's Qatar plane

The comment began with Sunny Hostin saying that the plane was allegedly going to be gifted to the Trump Presidential Library after.

"But we all know that that's BS. He's not giving that to his presidential library. He's going to take it and fly and use it, but he's not supposed to have it!" Goldberg explained, pulling out a pocket Constitution to audience applause. "I got the Constitution right here. He's not supposed to have the plane, okay?"

She added a comment that could refer to the request from the ABC bigwigs.

"You know, and if you — you know, people always want us to be respectful and do the things and talk about the stuff we talk about. We can do all of that. But when you are blatantly giving the country the middle finger, that is blatant. You're not supposed to do that. You're not supposed to take the plane," Goldberg said emphatically.

Our Analysis:

The Unyielding Spirit of Critique: Whoopi Goldberg vs. The Gilded Trump

In an era where the line between political allegiance and media representation blurs more with each passing day, Whoopi Goldberg's audacious stance on a recent episode of "The View" is nothing short of revolutionary. Amidst pressures from ABC News—a request veiled in corporate diplomacy but reeking of censorship—Goldberg, with a constitution in hand, exemplifies the fierce tenacity required to challenge power structures, particularly those embodied by Donald Trump.

The Emoluments Clause: A Thorn in Trump's Side

The segment in question, a scathing critique of Trump's acceptance of a $400 million plane from Qatar, wasn't just another episode in the ongoing saga of Trump's controversies. It was a vivid illustration of the corruption and blatant disregard for constitutional mandates that have characterized Trump's presidency. By highlighting the violation of the Emoluments Clause, Goldberg didn't merely point out an ethical lapse; she showcased a systemic failure to uphold democratic principles.

The Emoluments Clause, for those unfamiliar, is designed to prevent government officials from receiving gifts or payments from foreign states without Congressional consent, a clear conflict of interest designed to ensure the purity of American democracy. Trump's acceptance of such a lavish gift not only spits in the face of this clause but, as Goldberg adeptly pointed out, gives the country "the middle finger."

ABC's Muffled Cries for 'Respect'

The backdrop to this defiant display of journalism is a request from ABC News executives, urging the co-hosts of "The View" to tone down their criticism of Trump. This thinly-veiled attempt at silencing dissent under the guise of respectability politics is emblematic of the broader issue of media outlets prioritizing profit over principle. It's a disturbing reminder of how corporate interests often seek to muzzle the voices of those daring to speak truth to power.

Goldberg's response, a masterclass in principled resistance, didn't just reject this request; it tore it apart, live on air, to audience applause. This wasn't just about a plane; it was a statement, a line drawn in the sand, declaring that some values—like integrity, truth, and democracy—are non-negotiable.

The Misguided Critique and the Real BS

What's particularly intriguing is the strategic dismissal of the plane's intended destination: the Trump Presidential Library. Goldberg, with a sharpness that cuts to the core, labeled this as "BS." And rightly so. This isn't merely skepticism; it's an informed understanding of a pattern of behavior. Trump's history of leveraging his position for personal gain casts a long shadow of doubt over any claims of altruistic intent behind the acceptance of such gifts.

In Conclusion: A Beacon of Hope in Dark Times

Whoopi Goldberg's actions on "The View" serve as a beacon of hope and a model of journalistic integrity. In times when the press is under attack and truth is a commodity, Goldberg reminds us of the power of steadfast, fearless critique. It's a reminder that in the face of attempts to silence dissent, speaking out is not just a right; it's a duty.

In a world where the lines between right and wrong, truth and falsehood, are increasingly blurred, Goldberg's commitment to truth, her refusal to back down in the face of corporate pressure, is not just commendable—it's essential. The struggle for a free and fair discourse demands nothing less.
