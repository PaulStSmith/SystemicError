Trump Pentagon secretly amassing ‘troll army’ as report exposes 'scandal of the century'

Hegseth Is Building a State-Sponsored MAGA Media Machine

The Power Is Not on X

The reporting here is not about a few loud online loyalists discovering politics. It is about the Defense Secretary using the Pentagon’s institutional muscle to build an influencer network around himself. Pete Hegseth holds actual power. The people boosting him online do not. That is the whole story.

The Bulwark’s reporting makes clear that Jennica Pounds, a right-wing influencer known as “Data Republican,” has worked as a temporary Pentagon special government employee since July, while other pro-Hegseth personalities have direct or alleged Pentagon ties. The Defense Department confirmed Pounds’s employment and then declined to say what she does. That silence is not incidental. It is the method.

Influence Is the Product

This is not ordinary communications work. It is political patronage dressed up as staffing. Hegseth is allegedly assembling an online army of MAGA personalities who can defend him, attack critics, and launder administrative choices through a partisan media ecosystem. That turns government employment into a loyalty pipeline.

The key fact is not that these influencers exist. The key fact is that the Pentagon appears to be giving them access, status, and possibly pay while refusing basic transparency about their duties. That is not confusion. That is deliberate insulation from scrutiny.

Misdirection by Design

The camouflage here is familiar: rebrand propaganda as “messaging,” rebrand partisans as “special government employees,” and rebrand public accountability as a nuisance. The story is not that unnamed online users are being unfair to Hegseth. The story is that Hegseth is using institutional authority to recruit, empower, and shield political allies.

Thomas R. Anderson, a retired colonel posting as “Cynical Publius,” defended Pounds by claiming she had no obligation to disclose her government role. That argument is exactly backward. When government power is being mixed with partisan influence, disclosure is the minimum requirement, not an optional courtesy. Adam Ortona, an active-duty Army lieutenant colonel, reportedly vowed to help Hegseth battle the bureaucracy and attacked Democrats. That is not neutral service. It is factional alignment inside the uniformed state.

The Pentagon as a Partisan Asset

This is where the story becomes larger than one secretary’s taste for ideological allies. The Pentagon is being treated as a partisan asset, not a public institution. Hegseth is not merely managing a department; he is cultivating a media flank inside it. That is how institutions get bent toward private political survival.

The most revealing part of the report is the refusal to explain Pounds’s job. Once officials stop describing what public employees do, the public is no longer the audience. The audience becomes the loyalty network itself. The government then operates as both employer and shield.

The Scandal Is Structural

Adam Kinzinger calling this the “scandal of the century” is hyperbole, but the underlying alarm is justified. The scandal is not one influencer or one appointment. It is the convergence of state power, partisan media, and bureaucratic secrecy into a single machine designed to protect a political faction.

That is how authoritarian politics advances in plain sight. Not always through dramatic decrees, but through the quiet conversion of public office into a distribution system for propaganda, cover, and inside access. Hegseth’s Pentagon is not just managing communications. It is helping build a government-backed MAGA ecosystem, one taxpayer-funded slot at a time.
