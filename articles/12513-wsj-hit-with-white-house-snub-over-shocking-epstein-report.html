WSJ hit with White House snub over shocking Epstein report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the press as he arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 13, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

The Trump administration has excluded the Wall Street Journal from the press pool in Scotland following their release of last week's explosive reporting on the president's former friendship with deceased wealth manager and child sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, Politico reported on Monday.

“As the appeals court confirmed, the Wall Street Journal or any other news outlet are not guaranteed special access to cover President Trump in the Oval Office, aboard Air Force One, and in his private workspaces,” said White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt. “Due to the Wall Street Journal’s fake and defamatory conduct, they will not be one of the thirteen outlets on board. Every news organization in the entire world wishes to cover President Trump, and the White House has taken significant steps to include as many voices as possible.”

The story detailed how Trump allegedly sent a salacious letter to Epstein for his birthday in 2003, proclaiming that "We have certain things in common, Jeffrey," and featuring a drawing of a naked woman.

Trump has filed a libel lawsuit against the Journal, a conservative-leaning paper under the umbrella of media tycoon Rupert Murdoch.

So far, the lawsuit is lacking in details, but Trump has publicly denied writing the letter and claims he doesn't even make drawings, despite the existence of several of his drawings that were put up for auction over the years.

The president has already used exclusion from the press pool as a punishment against news outlets that defy his wishes; the Associated Press has been locked out of White House press briefings for months after not updating its style guide to adopt Trump's "Gulf of America" language. A federal judge ordered the White House to stop the lockout, but that decision was put on hold by an appeals court panel last month.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's Assault on Press Freedom: A Case Study

The Trump administration's decision to exclude the Wall Street Journal (WSJ) from the press pool in Scotland, as reported by Politico, marks a significant, albeit unsurprising, escalation in its ongoing war against the free press. This move comes on the heels of the WSJ's "explosive reporting" on the president's alleged former friendship with Jeffrey Epstein, a convicted child sex trafficker. The administration's rationale? Accusations of "fake and defamatory conduct" by the WSJ, as stated by White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt. However, this is not just about the WSJ or Epstein. It's a glaring testament to a broader, more dangerous pattern of behavior.

"Due to the Wall Street Journal’s fake and defamatory conduct, they will not be one of the thirteen outlets on board." This statement by Leavitt is a masterclass in Orwellian doublespeak, suggesting that the administration is the arbiter of truth, deciding who gets to report on it based on their loyalty or subservience. The notion that every news organization in the world wishes to cover President Trump and that the White House is taking "significant steps to include as many voices as possible" is laughable when juxtaposed with their actions.

The Allegations and the Lawsuit

The WSJ's reporting detailed an alleged salacious letter from Trump to Epstein, complete with a drawing of a naked woman. Trump's response? A libel lawsuit lacking in details and a denial of ever writing such a letter or making drawings, despite evidence to the contrary. This lawsuit, then, seems less about seeking justice and more about punishing dissent and intimidating those who dare to scrutinize the president's past—a tactic familiar to autocrats.

A Pattern of Punishment

This isn't the first time the Trump administration has used exclusion as a weapon against the press. The Associated Press (AP) was previously locked out of White House press briefings for refusing to adopt Trump's preferred "Gulf of America" terminology, an Orwellian attempt at rewriting geographical realities to suit political narratives. Although a federal judge ordered an end to this lockout, an appeals court panel has put that decision on hold, illustrating the judiciary's complicity in undermining press freedom.

The Bigger Picture

What we're witnessing is an administration that views the press not as an essential pillar of democracy but as an enemy to be subdued. The exclusion of the WSJ from the press pool is not an isolated incident but part of a concerted effort to control the narrative, silence criticism, and erode public trust in the media. This strategy is not just about avoiding scrutiny; it's about creating a media landscape where only the most sycophantic or unthreatening voices are heard.

In times like these, the role of independent journalism becomes even more critical. It's incumbent upon us, the public, to support those outlets that dare to speak truth to power, to question the official narrative, and to hold the powerful accountable. The Trump administration's actions are a stark reminder of what's at stake when the free press is treated as the enemy: nothing less than the health of our democracy itself.
