President Donald Trump shared an AI-generated video of himself playing hockey for Team USA after the actual national team won a gold medal over Canada in the Olympics.

The American team won its first gold medal in men’s hockey since 1980 thanks to 24-year-old Jack Hughes’ overtime goal to notch a 2-1 win Sunday in the 2026 Milan Cortina Olympics.Trump celebrated the following morning by posting a one-minute clip created by X chatbot Grok that showed him scoring two goals while wearing a suit and tie.

“What a play by Trump,” the fictional announcer calls out as Survivor’s 1982 song “Eye of the Tiger” plays over the video.

The fictional Trump, who appears to be at least a decade younger than he is, dominates the game in the phony highlight reel, and his artificial avatar is banished to the penalty box for removing his gloves and pummeling an opponent on the ice.

“We’ll get Kash or we’ll get the military to get you guys over,” Trump told the players.

“Boys, we’re going to the State of the Union!” Patel replied.

Our Analysis:

The Absurdity of AI-Generated Grandeur: Trump’s Hockey Fantasy

In a move that oscillates between the surreal and the downright ludicrous, President Donald Trump has once again blurred the lines between reality and fiction, and not in the subtle manner of a skilled magician, but with the gracelessness of a bull in a china shop. Sharing an AI-generated video of himself playing hockey for Team USA—after the actual national team’s significant win over Canada in the Olympics—marks a new peak in Trump’s ceaseless quest to insert himself into narratives of success, irrespective of their connection to reality.

The Context of the Controversy

The American men’s hockey team clinched its first gold medal since 1980, a momentous occasion that deserves its spotlight, unshadowed by the ego of political figures. The victory, secured by 24-year-old Jack Hughes’ overtime goal, is a narrative about perseverance, skill, and the culmination of years of dedication. Yet, Trump’s response was to celebrate by posting a one-minute clip created by the X chatbot Grok, depicting him—dressed in a suit and tie, no less—scoring goals and engaging in a fistfight on the ice.

The Dissonance of Delusion

The video, complete with a fictional announcer’s praise and the strains of “Eye of the Tiger,” would be humorous if it didn’t underscore a deeper, more disconcerting trend: Trump’s relentless pursuit of adulation and his propensity to fabricate achievements. The AI-generated Trump, “at least a decade younger,” dominating the game, is an unsettling glimpse into the vanity-driven politics that prioritize personal glory over genuine achievement and collective success.

Blurring the Lines Between Reality and Fiction

What is equally troubling is the casual manner in which this fictionalized account is presented alongside actual achievements and real-life events. Trump’s invitation to the hockey team to attend the State of the Union address, and his instructions to FBI Director Kash Patel—apparently now a liaison for beer-fueled celebrations—to arrange their travel, reads like a script from a satirical sketch, blurring the lines between governance and spectacle.

The Role of AI in Political Narratives

This incident also raises critical questions about the role of artificial intelligence in crafting political narratives. AI, with its ability to generate convincingly realistic images, videos, and sound bites, holds a dangerous potential when wielded by those seeking to rewrite reality in their image. The ethics of AI use in politics must be scrutinized, especially as the technology becomes more sophisticated and accessible.

Conclusion: The Spectacle Over Substance

Trump’s AI hockey fantasy is emblematic of a broader issue: the spectacle over substance, where the allure of media attention and personal glorification overshadows genuine achievement and the real issues at hand. It’s a stark reminder of the need for a political culture that values truth, celebrates real achievements, and respects the intelligence of its citizenry. The American people deserve better than to be spectators at the absurd theater of vanity politics.
