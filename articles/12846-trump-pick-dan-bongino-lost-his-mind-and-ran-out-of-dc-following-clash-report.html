Trump pick Dan Bongino 'lost his mind and ran out of DC' following clash: report

FBI deputy director Dan Bongino took off work Friday following a clash with attorney general Pam Bondi over her handling of the Jeffrey Epstein case.

Two sources told Axios that Bongino was increasingly unhappy of Bondi's handling of the matter because he felt she had overpromised and underdelivered information about the disgraced financier's alleged "client list," and three sources said the deputy director was blamed internally for the "missing minute" from surveillance video recorded outside Epstein's jail cell.

"The fact is, Dan was for releasing the information with the video and had no problem until he got heat online," a senior administration official told Axios. "Bongino found the video with the missing minute. He vouched for it after a 'thorough review,' he said, and he thought this would end the matter. When that didn't work, he lost his mind and ran out of D.C."

Bongino angered President Donald Trump's MAGA base by publicly stating in May that the surveillance video proved Epstein had not been murdered as he awaited trial on sex trafficking charges, and he, Bondi and FBI director Kash Patel have each complained to others that they're being blamed unfairly for the Epstein furor and being hung out to dry.

"Dan is not the bad guy here," said a pro-Bongino source. "He shouldn't take the fall."

The situation came to a boil Wednesday at the White House, where Bongino was confronted about a NewsNation article that reported he and Patel wanted more details released about the Epstein case but were held back, and the deputy director denied leaking that information in a heated discussion in front of the FBI director, White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and deputy chief of staff Taylor Budowich.

"Pam said her piece, Dan said his piece," said one person briefed on the confrontation. "It didn't end on friendly terms."

MAGA influencer Laura Loomer, who first reported that Bongino had taken off the day from work, said the deputy FBI director was "seriously" considering resignation over the matter.

"Bongino took the day off from work to evaluate whether or not he wants to continue his position with the @FBI following the Sunday night release of a memo from the DOJ that claims no more information about Jeffrey Epstein will be released," Loomer tweeted. "Source tells me it’s very likely that Bongino resigns from the FBI unless Pam Blondi (sic) is FIRED by President Trump or resigns from the DOJ."

Our Analysis:

The Bongino-Bondi Clash: A Deep Dive into Dysfunction

In a tale as twisted as it is troubling, recent developments around FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino’s clash with Attorney General Pam Bondi over the handling of the infamous Jeffrey Epstein case have surfaced, painting a vivid picture of internal discord and dysfunction at the highest levels of American law enforcement and justice. This saga, as reported by Axios, is not just a narrative of personal disagreements; it's a glaring spotlight on the systemic failures and political maneuvering that seemingly plague our institutions.

Bold Promises, Bolder Failures

At the heart of the controversy is Bongino's growing dissatisfaction with Bondi's approach to unveiling the so-called "client list" of disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein. Bongino’s angst stems from a classic political blunder: overpromising and underdelivering. This isn't merely a bureaucratic hiccup; it's a significant breach of trust and competence, particularly in a case as sensitive and far-reaching as Epstein's.

The Missing Minute Mystery

Adding to the mire is the baffling case of the "missing minute" from surveillance video outside Epstein's cell. This isn't just a minute; it's a monumental lapse in the chain of custody and integrity of evidence. Bongino, who initially vouched for the video's thorough review, found himself in the crosshairs of internal blame. The narrative that he “lost his mind and ran out of D.C.” when his efforts didn't quell the controversy is not just dramatic; it's demonstrative of a deeper, systemic rot.

Clashing Titans and Political Theater

The public spat between Bongino and Bondi, culminating in a confrontational spectacle at the White House, is the kind of political theater that distracts from the core issues at hand. While they play the blame game, the quest for truth and justice in the Epstein saga takes a backseat.

The MAGA Factor

Bongino's public declaration that Epstein was not murdered, contrary to popular conspiracy theories, might have been an attempt to set the record straight. However, it served as fuel to the fire of his fallout with the MAGA base, a testament to the volatile mix of politics and law enforcement—a combination that often leads to explosive outcomes.

The Resignation Rumor Mill

Laura Loomer's reporting on Bongino’s potential resignation over the debacle adds another layer of intrigue. The suggestion that Bongino's departure hinges on Bondi's firing or resignation is a stark reminder of the personal vendettas and power plays that seem to overshadow the pursuit of justice.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

The Bongino-Bondi clash is not just a story of two high-profile figures at odds; it's a microcosm of the broader issues that bedevil our justice and law enforcement systems. The saga underscores the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and a depoliticization of the justice process. As it stands, the handling of the Epstein case serves as a disheartening example of systemic error—one that demands not just attention but action.

In a world where justice should be blind, it seems caught in the all-seeing eye of political expediency and personal agendas. The real victims, sadly, are the principles of justice and the public's trust in it.
