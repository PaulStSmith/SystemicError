“Well, Santa’s now in Sweden,” said Trump. “So Santa’s got quite a trip to get to your great area, your great part of the world, because you live in a terrific place. But I will say Santa will make it in record time. Probably in about five hours. Five hours from now, Santa will be coming down your chimney and will have a beautiful present for you. “Uh... not coal?” said the child.

“Not coal, you don’t want coal. Well coal is — you mean clean, beautiful coal,” said Trump. “I had to do that, I’m sorry. Coal is clean and beautiful, just remember that. But you don’t want clean, beautiful coal, right?”

This exchange comes as the Trump administration has declared war on renewable energy, with a move to shut down offshore wind installations underway, ostensibly for national security reasons.

Our Analysis:

A Coal-Stained Christmas Tale: Trump’s Yuletide Diversion

In what could only be described as a bizarre twist to a Christmas Eve tradition, President Donald Trump managed to veer wildly off course during a seemingly innocent interaction with a child. The setting: Mar-a-Lago, Trump’s Florida bastion of opulence. The occasion: a Christmas Eve event, presumably filled with the kind of festive cheer that welcomes whimsical questions about Santa’s ETA. Yet, in a moment that should have been steeped in the innocent anticipation of Santa’s visit, Trump inexplicably pivoted to an endorsement of fossil fuels. Yes, you read that right. Amid the jingle bells and holiday spirits, coal—not as a lump for the naughty but as an energy source—found its moment in the spotlight.

“Well coal is — you mean clean, beautiful coal,” said Trump in response to a child’s hope that Santa wouldn’t bring coal. This unsolicited pivot to coal advocacy is emblematic of a broader, more troubling trend: the Trump administration’s unabashed embrace of fossil fuels at a time when the clarion call for renewable energy has never been louder.

The War on Renewables

This peculiar exchange between Trump and the child comes against the backdrop of the administration’s aggressive stance against renewable energy. The move to shut down offshore wind installations, ostensibly for national security reasons, is a stark reminder of the lengths to which the administration is willing to go to hamstring the renewable energy sector. It’s a decision that flies in the face of scientific consensus, economic sense, and environmental necessity.

Coal’s Dubious “Beauty”

Trump’s characterization of coal as “clean and beautiful” is, at best, a gross misrepresentation. The notion of “clean coal” has been debunked time and again, with the process of capturing and storing carbon emissions from coal plants proving to be both costly and rarely implemented. Meanwhile, the environmental and health impacts of coal mining and combustion are well-documented, casting a long shadow over Trump’s glowing adjectives.

A Missed Opportunity for Leadership

In this off-the-cuff endorsement of coal, Trump missed an opportunity to demonstrate genuine leadership. Imagine, instead, if he had seized the moment to inspire the next generation about the potential of renewable energy. A mention of solar-powered sleighs or wind-powered workshops could have served as a powerful metaphor for a sustainable future. Instead, we got a lament for coal.

Conclusion: A Coal-Stained Legacy

Trump’s Christmas Eve conversation is a microcosm of his administration’s larger environmental policy stance. It’s a stance marked by a perplexing allegiance to the past, a disregard for scientific evidence, and a troubling indifference to the global consensus on climate change. As the world looks to the future, embracing innovation and sustainability, the Trump administration’s policies—much like coal itself—seem destined to be relegated to the ash heap of history.

In a season of giving, the greatest gift our leaders could offer is a commitment to a sustainable, clean energy future. Sadly, this Christmas tale suggests we’re more likely to get coal.
