'Big freaking mistake': Trump's DOJ launches probe into MAGA influencer's arrest

President Donald Trump‘s Department of Justice issued a response Friday after a MAGA influencer’s arrest in Portland, Oregon, criticizing the “woke cops” who arrested him.

Harmeet K. Dhillon, Assistant Attorney General for the Civil Rights Division, announced via X that an investigation into the arrest of Nick Sortor was underway, sharing a letter with demands for City Attorney Robert Taylor and Bob Day, chief of police.

“Acting at the direction of our @AGPamBondi, we @CivilRights have launched an investigation into Portland, OR’s highly questionable police practices. Stay tuned. We will not leave the people of Portland at the whims of criminals and woke cops. Govern yourselves accordingly!” Dhillon wrote.

In the letter, she argued that police arrested Sortor but not others who may have been involved in the altercation. She also claimed that police did not arrest the “individuals who allegedly assaulted Katie Daviscourt,” another MAGA influencer, and alleged that the city and police have blocked ICE’s access to the facility.

“The City of Portland reportedly has attempted to use its policing powers through a zoning enforcement action to limit ICE’s use of its Portland facility,” she wrote.

Early Friday morning, MAGA influencer Nick Sortor was released from a Portland jail cell after allegedly getting in a street fight with anti-ICE protesters late Thursday night near an Immigration and Customs Enforcement facility, reports CNN.

In a video posted to X and Bluesky, Sortor can be seen being put in restraints by Portland police officers on bikes as onlookers taunted him with “You dumbs---,” and “What a f------ loser.”

According to the CNN report, local police were dispatched to the area over reports of skirmishes and “continued to monitor the situation and responded after seeing additional fights break out. A bureau Rapid Response Team arrested Sortor and the two Oregonians, police said.”

Sortor has reportedly been involved in other altercations in the past while reporting. Daviscourt was apparently hit in the eye with a flag pole and had a black eye.

Sortor wrote on X after he was released that he kept the burning flag he grabbed altercation and warned police they made a “big freaking mistake.”

“You PROVED what we’ve all been saying for years: you’re CORRUPT and CONTROLLED by vioIent Antifa thugs who terrorize the streets. You thought arresting me would make me shut up and go away. You couldn’t have been more wrong. Stay tuned.”

Our Analysis:

The Department of Selective Outrage: Analyzing the DOJ’s Response to a MAGA Influencer’s Arrest

In what reads like a script lifted straight from the annals of political theatre, the Department of Justice’s recent escapade into the streets of Portland, Oregon, under President Donald Trump’s administration, reeks of hypocrisy and selective outrage. At the heart of this farce is the arrest of MAGA influencer Nick Sortor, an incident that has seemingly ruffled the feathers of the DOJ to an astonishing degree.

The Alleged Crime and Arrest

Nick Sortor, a name now synonymous with the latest MAGA martyrdom tale, found himself in handcuffs after an alleged street fight with anti-ICE protesters. This skirmish, conveniently occurring near an Immigration and Customs Enforcement facility, was enough to dispatch local police and eventually lead to Sortor’s arrest. But it’s not the arrest itself that’s the meat of this story; it’s the DOJ’s laser-focused response, which shines a stark light on their priorities.

The DOJ’s Reaction: A Study in Bias

Harmeet K. Dhillon, Assistant Attorney General for the Civil Rights Division, took to X (formerly known as Twitter) with a fervor, announcing an investigation into Portland’s police practices. This move is curious, to say the least. It’s not the investigation that’s problematic—it’s the selective indignation. The DOJ’s sudden concern for civil rights in Portland, specifically in defense of a MAGA influencer, is as transparent as it is partisan.

Dhillon’s claims—that the police arrested Sortor but not others, and the allegation that the city has tried to block ICE’s access—paint a picture of a government body more concerned with political allegiance than with justice. The insinuation that Portland police are “woke cops” controlled by “violent Antifa thugs” is not only a gross oversimplification but a dangerous narrative that stokes the fires of division.

The Bigger Picture: What’s Really at Stake

This incident and the DOJ’s response to it are emblematic of a broader issue: the politicization of justice. When the Department of Justice cherry-picks its battles based on political affiliations and uses its considerable power to intervene in local law enforcement matters, it sets a dangerous precedent. The message is clear: if you’re aligned with the administration’s politics, you’re more likely to merit its protection or outrage.

Moreover, the DOJ’s swift action in Portland starkly contrasts with its silence or sluggishness in matters involving civil rights violations against marginalized communities. This discrepancy is not lost on the observant public. It begs the question: where is this level of concern when unarmed Black individuals are killed by police? Where is the rapid response when ICE facilities themselves are accused of human rights abuses?

Conclusion: The Real Losers in This Political Game

Nick Sortor’s arrest and the subsequent DOJ outcry are but a microcosm of a larger, more troubling trend of politicized justice. The real losers here are not Sortor, Dhillon, or even the DOJ; it’s the American people. When justice is wielded as a weapon of political convenience rather than a universal principle, trust in our institutions erodes. The Department of Justice, under the guise of protecting civil rights, has instead showcased a blatant disregard for impartiality and equity.

As we watch this saga unfold, let us not be distracted by the theatrics. Instead, let’s demand a justice system that upholds the rights of all citizens, not just those who wield the right political slogans. In the end, selective outrage from our highest law enforcement body does not protect civil rights—it undermines them.
