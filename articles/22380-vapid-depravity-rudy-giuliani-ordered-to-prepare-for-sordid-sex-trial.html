'Vapid depravity': Rudy Giuliani ordered to prepare for 'sordid' sex trial

Based on an article by Adam Nichols

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Giuliani’s Collapse Is Not the Story. His Power Was.

The Case, Briefly

A New York judge has ordered Rudy Giuliani to prepare for trial after rejecting his attempts to kill Noelle Dunphy’s lawsuit. Dunphy alleges that while working for him from 2019 to 2021, she was subjected to sexual harassment, coercion, unpaid wages, and threats tied to Giuliani’s political connections and private investigators. Giuliani denies the claims and has tried to recast the relationship as dating; the court has not let him escape discovery or trial.

Power Was the Instrument

The central fact is not Giuliani’s embarrassment. It is the asymmetry of power. Dunphy alleges that a man with political reach, money, and a long public life used that position to control a worker and threaten retaliation. That is not a private scandal in the cheap tabloid sense. It is institutional domination dressed up as personal misconduct.

Giuliani was not some detached celebrity drifting through the story. He was a former mayor, a Trump lawyer, and a man who allegedly invoked political connections as leverage. That matters. The alleged harm was not only sexual; it was enforced by status, access, and the expectation that powerful men can make consequences disappear.

The Familiar Defense

Giuliani’s legal posture follows a well-worn script: deny the employment relationship, reduce the abuse into a romance, then claim illness and poverty when the court refuses to look away. The point of that sequence is obvious. It is not truth-seeking. It is delay, dilution, and escape.

The source also notes his hospitalization, bankruptcy filing, disbarments, and earlier defamation judgment. Those details are relevant, but not because they inspire pity. They show a political operator trying to convert the wreckage of accountability into a shield. Fragility becomes theater when it is convenient and disappears when the cameras move.

What the Story Tries to Blur

The article’s framing leans toward spectacle: disgrace, fall from grace, sordid details, the shock of a once-famous man collapsing in public. That is a familiar media trick. It turns structural abuse into character drama.

But character is not the point. The point is who had leverage, who could retaliate, and who spent years fighting to get into court. If Dunphy’s allegations are proven, the story is not that Giuliani became a fallen icon. It is that a politically protected man allegedly treated access to power as permission to exploit someone dependent on him.

The Political Pattern

This is the same old elite arrangement: men with institutional power wrap themselves in public service, then behave as if their office, fame, and networks exempt them from ordinary consequences. When they are exposed, they are treated as tragic figures instead of what they are: repeat offenders in a system built to absorb their damage.

Giuliani’s name was once sold as a symbol of law-and-order seriousness. What this case shows instead is the rot inside that mythology. A political class that celebrates strongmen, excuses misconduct as roughness, and mistakes dominance for leadership inevitably produces men who think accountability is for other people.

No More Mythology

The lesson here is not that one disgraced figure fell farther than expected. The lesson is that American political power routinely shelters men who mistake prominence for impunity. The court process can still force facts into daylight, but the broader system has already done its work: elevate, protect, rationalize, and delay.

That is the real scandal. Not the fall. The long protection that made the fall necessary.
