Trump secretly panicking about Stephen Miller — but won't do anything to stop him: analyst

White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller speaks to the media, at the White House, in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 19, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump is privately panicking that longtime far-right adviser Stephen Miller is steering his presidency into the ditch, per recent reports — but doesn’t actually seem to be doing anything to stop that, Greg Sargent wrote for The New Republic on Tuesday.

This comes amid reporting from the Wall Street Journal that Trump wants to sideline Miller, and put a softer messaging spin on his mass deportation agenda, making it clearer to the public he wants to target hardened criminals and not simply snatch random people up off the street.

The problem, wrote Sargent, is that Miller hasn’t really been leashed in any meaningful way — and is coming up with even more ways to undermine civil rights.

“To be clear, this report deserves serious skepticism,” wrote Sargent. “It very much bears watching whether ICE will actually end up deprioritizing the removal of noncriminal immigrants. Trump mostly wants the appearance of a pivot: According to the Journal, he wants a focus on ‘criminals’ in GOP ‘messaging.’ But recalibrating the ‘messaging’ won’t address the public’s broad rejection of Trumpism’s deeper anti-immigrant project. And all signs are that this project is fully forging ahead.”

For instance, this comes amid reports that Miller is pressuring GOP state legislators to kick the citizen children of unauthorized immigrants out of public schools — which would directly contradict a 1982 Supreme Court ruling, and set up a massive fight that, if Republicans won, would create a huge lower class of citizens with no right to an education.

“Trump and Miller are aiming at something very profound, if maliciously so,” wrote Sargent. “As legal scholar Akhil Reed Amar writes, the ‘big idea’ animating the Fourteenth Amendment’s citizenship clause, its ‘moral north star,’ is that birthright citizenship enshrines a guarantee that all persons are born free and equal — their status is not dictated by blood. The goal of undoing this, Amar notes, is to make the constitutional order more ‘hereditary’ and ‘caste-like.’”

In other words, they want a country in which “heritage, not adherence to creedal ideals, makes one American.”

“Trump can dress this up with spin about targeting ‘criminals’ all he likes. But until all the ethnonationalist, civilizational-emergency-mongering nonsense is exorcised, the deeper problem will fester,” Sargent concluded. “Trump believes all those ideas himself, but the depth of his commitment to them has never been all that clear. One doubts he’ll be so inclined, but should he ever want to end this madness, only one move on his part — a big personnel move — can truly put an end to it.”

Our Analysis:

The Puppeteer Behind the Curtain: Examining Trump’s Relationship with Stephen Miller

In the swirling vortex of political maneuvering and media narratives, it’s crucial to dissect the dynamics at play between President Donald Trump and his adviser Stephen Miller. Greg Sargent’s piece for The New Republic casts a spotlight on this relationship, framing it as a one-sided manipulation where Miller is the puppeteer and Trump, the seemingly reluctant puppet. However, a closer inspection reveals a more complex interplay of power, decision-making, and responsibility.

Who Holds the Power?

First and foremost, it’s imperative to identify who actually wields institutional power in this scenario. As President, Donald Trump occupies the highest executive office, granting him an almost unrivaled platform to shape policy and public discourse. Stephen Miller, while influential as a senior adviser, operates within the boundaries of power delineated by Trump. The ultimate authority rests with the President, making him the primary architect of his administration’s policies and narratives.

Decision-Making Dynamics

The article suggests that Miller is steering the presidency towards a more extreme anti-immigrant stance, yet Trump is depicted as oddly passive, seemingly trapped by Miller’s influence. This portrayal overlooks the fundamental nature of their relationship: Trump has the final say. If Miller’s advice does not align with Trump’s vision or interests, the President has the institutional authority to reject it. The decision to follow Miller’s counsel, therefore, is Trump’s alone. The narrative that Trump is somehow a victim of Miller’s machinations absolves the President of his responsibility and agency.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

Sargent’s piece, while critical of Miller, inadvertently shifts the blame away from Trump, who, as President, is ultimately accountable for the actions of his administration. By focusing on Miller’s role, the article risks underplaying the President’s complicity and active participation in the administration’s policies. This misdirection of responsibility diverts attention away from the individual with the most power to effect change, thereby diluting the accountability that should be attributed to Trump.

Conclusion: The Real Story

The true story here is not just about an adviser’s influence but about a President’s choices and priorities. Trump’s alignment with Miller’s hardline anti-immigrant stance is a reflection of his own political beliefs and objectives, not merely the result of being swayed by an adviser. The framing that positions Trump as being at Miller’s mercy overlooks the President’s capacity and responsibility to set the course of his administration. In the end, Trump’s decisions — to either embrace or distance himself from Miller’s ideas — are what truly shape the administration’s policies.

It’s critical, then, to question narratives that place undue emphasis on advisers at the expense of holding the President accountable. While advisers like Miller undoubtedly play a significant role in shaping policy discussions, the ultimate responsibility for the administration’s direction lies with Trump himself. Any analysis that suggests otherwise not only misreads the dynamics of power within the White House but also inadvertently absolves the President of his own agency and responsibility.
