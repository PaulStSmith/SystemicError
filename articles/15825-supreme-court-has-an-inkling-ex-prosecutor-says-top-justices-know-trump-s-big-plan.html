'Supreme Court has an inkling': Ex-prosecutor says top justices know Trump's big plan

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 21, 20254 min read

Supreme Court 2022, Image via Fred Schilling, Collection of the Supreme Court of the United States

The Supreme Court is clued in to Donald Trump's plan to undercut their power, an ex-prosecutor said on Saturday.

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance over the weekend posted an essay explaining the various deportation cases in which Trump's administration is involved. As part of that explainer, Vance sounded the alarm about what she sees as Trump's agenda.

" Trump is trying to break the government. To control all its levers, he needs a complicit judiciary to go along with a complacent Congress," she wrote on Substack. She then added, "The Supreme Court seems to have an inkling of the fix they’ve put themselves in, with Trump trying to accumulate power at the courts’ expense."

Vance went "into the weeds" to describe the numerous deportation cases involving the Trump administration, and then issued a clarification:

"But keep in mind that while the substance of this dispute centers on the policy goal of deporting people Trump calls criminal illegal aliens, it is also a vehicle this administration is using to undercut the ability of the courts to act as a check on the executive branch and make it easier for Trump to range beyond the authority the Constitution affords to the president."

After following the cases winding their ways through the courts, Vance summarized the most recent development in a major deportation case.

"That’s how we got a middle-of-the-night ruling from the Supreme Court, with ACLU lawyers racing to the Court for emergency protection for their clients, which the Supreme Court ultimately granted. At least for now, their clients cannot be deported, unless the Trump government wants to directly violate the Supreme Court’s order," the attorney wrote.

She then concluded, "I’m tempted to point out that the Supreme Court brought this upon itself, with the ruling in [J.G.G. v. Trump], but that would be petty on my part." J.G.G. is the case in which "the ACLU challenged the government’s deportation of two planeloads of Venezuelans to El Salvador under the Alien Enemies Act," according to Vance.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Judiciary Juggernaut: A Deep Dive

In a world where political maneuvers often resemble chess games, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance has cast a spotlight on what could very well be Donald Trump's attempt to checkmate the American judiciary system. With a sharp pen, Vance outlines the implications of Trump's administration's entanglement in various deportation cases. However, the crux of her argument isn't just about the policy itself but the underlying strategy to erode judicial checks on executive power. Let's dissect her claims with the precision they demand.

The Power Play

At the heart of Vance's alarm is the assertion that Trump is attempting to dismantle the government's balance of power. The strategy? Ensuring a judiciary that bends the knee to executive whim while operating under a Congress that watches from the sidelines, possibly with popcorn. This isn't just about deportation; it's about the erosion of judicial authority. The Supreme Court, according to Vance, is starting to realize the quagmire it's in, with Trump's tactics potentially reducing the court to a mere puppet in the grand scheme of his power consolidation.

The Deportation Decoy

Vance's foray "into the weeds" of deportation cases is more than just legal analysis; it's a revelation of method. The administration's focus on deporting "criminal illegal aliens" isn't just a policy position; it's a Trojan horse. By Vance's account, these cases serve a dual purpose: furthering a controversial policy goal while simultaneously chipping away at the judiciary's ability to act as a constitutional check on the presidency. This isn't just playing fast and loose with executive authority; it's akin to taking a sledgehammer to the foundational principles of American governance.

The Midnight Ruling

The narrative crescendo reaches its peak with a "middle-of-the-night ruling" from the Supreme Court, a dramatic affair with ACLU lawyers in a race against time. This moment, though it ended with the Court granting emergency protection against the deportations, serves as a stark illustration of the precarious state of affairs. The Trump administration's willingness to push these boundaries to the limit—and the Supreme Court's reactive stance—highlights the potential for a constitutional crisis.

Self-Inflicted Wounds?

Vance's closing remarks hint at a poetic justice. The Supreme Court, in her view, may have set the stage for its own predicament through past rulings, though she stops short of diving into the pettiness of "I told you so." The reference to J.G.G. v. Trump—a case where the ACLU challenged the government's deportation actions under the Alien Enemies Act—serves as a chilling reminder of the stakes involved.

Conclusion

Vance's essay isn't just a critique; it's a clarion call. The implications of Trump's strategy, if accurately depicted, represent a seismic shift in the balance of power within the U.S. government. It's a warning that the judiciary, and by extension, the American people, may be at risk of losing an essential safeguard against executive overreach. The Supreme Court's realization of its predicament, as Vance suggests, may be the first step in a much-needed course correction. But the question remains: Is it too late?

In the grand chess game of American politics, Trump's maneuvers could either be a masterstroke or a catastrophic miscalculation. What's clear from Vance's analysis is the need for vigilant, robust judicial and legislative checks on presidential power. Without them, the game might well be over for the American experiment in democracy.
