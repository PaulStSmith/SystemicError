Trump's massive new economic boast exists only inside his head

Based on an article by Dean Baker

Paulo SantosSeptember 3, 20263 min read

Trump’s Venezuelan Oil Fantasy Is Imperialism Without Mechanism

The Story Beneath the Boast

The reported claim is simple: Trump is promising control over 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan oil, selling it as a way to cut gas prices and replenish a Strategic Petroleum Reserve he has already drained. The rest is the usual smoke machine, with Trump inflating imaginary foreign investment numbers while the actual U.S. factory picture weakens and Venezuelan oil production remains constrained by sanctions, decayed infrastructure, and missing capital.

Who Actually Holds Power

The power here is not in Trump’s rhetoric, but in the U.S. state itself. Venezuela’s oil is being treated as an object of U.S. leverage, not a sovereign resource governed by Venezuelans. Trump is not presenting a workable market plan; he is advertising a command fantasy rooted in American military and economic dominance. The deal’s logic depends on power the United States has used for years to squeeze Venezuela, then pretends the same coercion will magically produce stability, investment, and barrels.

A Deal Built on Fiction

The article’s central misdirection is obvious: it treats an imperial claim as if it were an energy policy. There is no immediate spigot, no realistic path to rapid output, and no plausible way to use heavy, acidic Venezuelan crude to refill reserves designed for light oil. That is not a technical footnote. It is the whole story. The promised benefits are not merely delayed; they are structurally mismatched to reality.

Trump’s version of economic leadership is not administration. It is improvisational theft dressed up as abundance. He tells the public that foreign investment is surging by the trillions even as factory construction falls. He now applies the same method to oil: assert ownership, announce bounty, ignore the engineering, and hope the lie is large enough to survive contact with actual institutions.

Venezuela as a Target, Not a Partner

The piece is right to note that people in Venezuela are likely to see this as a joke. They should. This is not a partnership offer. It is an attempt to convert U.S. coercive power into a future asset claim over another country’s resource base. And because the scheme is so transparently tied to a U.S.-installed political order, it is unlikely to survive any change in government. That is not a flaw in the margins; it is the built-in collapse mechanism.

The deeper point is that the plan assumes Venezuelan sovereignty is negotiable if Washington wants the oil badly enough. That is the old imperial habit: break a country, blame the country for the wreckage, then demand the spoils once the damage has made extraction harder and slower.

The Strategic Reserve Is Not a Prop

Trump’s promise to refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve with Venezuelan oil is especially revealing because it combines spectacle with incompetence. The reserve has already been drained, and the proposed replacement is the wrong kind of oil. That should end the story. Instead, it becomes another stage prop in a domestic propaganda performance aimed at making extraction sound like competence.

This is not just dishonesty. It is a governing style that treats state capacity as something you can fake with volume. The more impossible the claim, the more it functions as political theater for people trained to reward domination over execution.

The Pattern

The pattern is familiar: America wrecks the conditions for stable development, then frames that wreckage as evidence that a stronger hand is needed. Trump takes that logic and strips away the last remnants of realism. He does not offer policy. He offers conquest language, then asks the public to confuse it with economic planning.

That is the actual political meaning of the deal. It is not about oil supply. It is about an authoritarian imagination that sees other countries as inventory, domestic institutions as props, and facts as an inconvenience to be outshouted.
