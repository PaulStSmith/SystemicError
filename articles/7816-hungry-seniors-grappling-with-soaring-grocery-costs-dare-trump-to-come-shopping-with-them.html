Hungry seniors grappling with soaring grocery costs dare Trump to come shopping with them

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 11, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump has attempted to claim the concerns about affordability undermining his presidency are a “hoax” and a “con job.” Still, in a new piece from the Los Angeles Times, seniors struggling with food insecurity pushed back against this idea, citing their increasingly harsh circumstances.

“I would say, ‘Mr. President, please travel with me,” Los Angeles retiree Frank McRae said when asked what he would tell the president. “So that you can experience firsthand… what it means to have to search for affordable food.”

McRae, an 81-year-old veteran, spoke to the Times at a community center run by Jewish Family Service LA, which offers food and other resources to what the paper described as “growing legions of people in need.” He provided a straightforward explanation for why he personally believes that the cost-of-living crisis is worsening, despite Trump’s claims.

“I know more people are coming in here now, because I’m seeing people I’ve never seen,” McRae explained.

McRae further detailed how he makes the most of what little he has, taking as many as three buses to get from his apartment to a McDonald’s he says has the cheapest coffee he has found, $0.87 with a senior discount. The apartment, which lacks a kitchen of its own, also recently saw a rent increase to $900 a month from $863.

Another struggling senior at the community center, Christina Shannon, detailed how she’s had to cut back on some of her favorite foods as the costs of all around her have gone up.

“Instead of a pack of bacon,” she explained, “I go to the deli and get like four slices... I go buy medicine, and it’s a little bit higher. And I went to the drugstore to get toothpaste and washing powder. It’s all so high.”

Affordability troubles are widely credited with driving major electoral gains for Democrats this year, with candidates using economic platforms to win big races in places like New Jersey, Virginia and New York City last month. Despite campaigning on cost-of-living issues in 2024, Trump has now dismissed affordability concerns as a “hoax” perpetuated by Democrats to make him look bad, while falsely claiming that prices on everything from groceries to gas to utilities have gone down in the last year.

In his piece for the Times, Steve Lopez gave two guesses as to why Trump might be saying such things despite overwhelming voter sentiments about the economy.

“I can think of two explanations as to why Trump said ‘just about everything is down’ pricewise, and that affordability is ‘a Democrat hoax’ and ‘the greatest con job,’” Lopez wrote. “Either Trump truly has no idea what things cost, other than, say, the $300-million ballroom he’s having built after bulldozing the East Wing of the White House. Or, after striking out on a fundamental campaign promise (’From the day I take the oath of office, we’ll rapidly drive prices down and make America affordable again… You just watch’), Trump is once again creating his own reality.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Ignorance: Trump’s Affordability Hoax

In what can only be described as a brazen display of disconnect, Donald Trump has called the affordability crisis a “hoax” and a “con job.” This claim, as reported by the Los Angeles Times, flies in the face of the harsh reality experienced by seniors struggling with food insecurity. Their stories, far removed from Trump’s rhetoric, paint a starkly different picture of America—one where the cost-of-living crisis isn’t just real; it’s debilitating.

Frank McRae, an 81-year-old veteran, offers a glimpse into this grim reality. McRae’s plea for Trump to witness firsthand the struggle for affordable food is a powerful indictment of the former president’s detachment from the lives of ordinary Americans. McRae’s testimony, alongside others at a community center run by Jewish Family Service LA, highlights a growing populace grappling with basic needs—a far cry from Trump’s claims of declining prices.

The narrative of Christina Shannon, who now buys four slices of bacon from the deli instead of a pack to save money, further underscores the absurdity of Trump’s assertions. The everyday experiences of McRae, Shannon, and countless others directly contradict the fantasy world Trump seems to inhabit, where prices are falling, and affordability is a non-issue.

It’s particularly galling to see Trump dismiss the affordability concerns as a fabrication by Democrats to tarnish his image, especially when his presidency did little to mitigate the economic challenges faced by the most vulnerable. The audacity to claim that prices on essentials like groceries, gas, and utilities have decreased over the last year is not just misleading; it’s a slap in the face to those who live the reality of stretching every dollar to survive.

Steve Lopez of the Times posits two explanations for Trump’s denial: either a genuine ignorance of the cost of living for the average American or a deliberate creation of an alternate reality to mask his failure in fulfilling a crucial campaign promise. Both explanations are damning, reflecting either an unfathomable disconnect or a manipulative attempt to reshape facts to fit a preferred narrative.

The Los Angeles Times piece is a sobering reminder that the affordability crisis in the United States is not a political talking point but a lived experience for many. Trump’s dismissal of these concerns as a “hoax” not only showcases his detachment from reality but also highlights a dangerous penchant for misinformation. As the nation grapples with the economic fallout, the voices of McRae, Shannon, and millions like them serve as a stark rebuke to the former president’s claims. In the face of such undeniable hardship, one thing is clear: the only “con job” is the narrative Trump is trying to sell.
