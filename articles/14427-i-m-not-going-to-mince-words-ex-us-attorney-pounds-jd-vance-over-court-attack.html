'I'm not going to mince words': Ex-US attorney pounds JD Vance over court attack

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 26, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Vice President JD Vance participates in a discussion at a Munich Security Conference event in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 7, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Vice President J.D. Vance was slapped down on MSNBC Sunday morning over comments he made earlier in the week about the Supreme Court where he suggested the Donald Trump administration is beyond the court's control.

In an interview with the New York Times' Ross Douthat, Vance lashed out at Chief Justice John Roberts by stating, "I saw an interview with Chief Justice Roberts recently where he said the role of the court is to check the excesses of the executive. I thought that was a profoundly wrong sentiment.”

He then added, "That’s one-half of his job. The other half of his job is to check the excesses of his own branch. You cannot have a country where the American people keep on electing immigration enforcement and the courts tell the American people they’re not allowed to have what they voted for. That’s where we are right now.”

Appearing on MSNBC's "The Weekend," former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance, no relation to the VP, set Donald Trump's right-hand man and attack dog straight.

"So I'm not going to mince words here," she began. "J.D. Vance is a Yale-educated lawyer; he understands how judicial review works. And since early in the history of our country, a landmark Supreme Court case, Marbury versus Madison, just after the founding the Supreme Court has had the power of judicial review."

"It can review conduct by either the legislative branch or the executive branch, the president, for constitutionality," she bluntly elaborated. "It is not supposed to execute the will of the American people and the vice president knows that."

"It's supposed to compare actions taken by other branches of government for consistency with the Constitution," she told host Jonathan Capehart. "And it has the ability to strike them down when they run afoul of that."

"End of story," she concluded.

Our Analysis:

Vance vs. The Supreme Court: A Misguided Crusade

Vice President J.D. Vance, in a recent outburst that could only be described as astonishingly ill-conceived, attempted to cast a shadow over the fundamental principles of the United States judiciary system. During an interview with the New York Times' Ross Douthat, Vance took aim at Chief Justice John Roberts, criticizing Roberts' acknowledgment of the Supreme Court's role in checking the excesses of the executive branch. Vance, with a perplexing mix of arrogance and ignorance, stated, "I thought that was a profoundly wrong sentiment.”

Let's Unpack the Absurdity

First and foremost, Vance's comments betray a fundamental misunderstanding—or perhaps a willful ignorance—of the role of the judiciary in a balanced system of governance. His claim that the Supreme Court's job is not only to check the excesses of the executive but also of its own branch, while correct in part, misses the larger point. The judiciary's primary function is to interpret the Constitution and ensure that neither the executive nor the legislative branches exceed their constitutional authority. It's not, as Vance seems to imply, a tool for the executive branch to wield unchallenged power.

A Yale-Educated Lawyer, Really?

What makes Vance's comments even more bewildering is his background as a Yale-educated lawyer. This isn't a case of not knowing better; it's a case of choosing to ignore the foundational tenets of American democracy for political expediency. As former U.S. Attorney Joyce Vance (no relation) pointed out on MSNBC, the power of judicial review has been a cornerstone of the American legal system since the landmark Supreme Court case Marbury vs. Madison. This isn't a controversial opinion; it's a fact.

The Real Grift

The article also teases an "EXCLUSIVE" about a new grift involving Donald Trump that apparently "puts Qatari plane in shade," a tantalizing tidbit meant to draw readers deeper into the web of Trumpian controversies. However, this mention feels like a distraction from the more pressing issue at hand: the Vice President's troubling views on the judiciary's role in American government.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

Vice President J.D. Vance's comments represent more than just a misunderstanding of judicial review; they signify a dangerous attempt to undermine the checks and balances that preserve the integrity of the United States government. The Vice President, armed with a Yale Law degree and a platform of significant influence, should know better. But as history has often shown us, knowing better doesn't always equate to doing better. In Vance's case, it seems to be a deliberate choice to eschew constitutional principles in favor of a more authoritarian, unchecked executive power—a stance that is as reckless as it is wrong.

The Bottom Line

At the end of the day, the Vice President's assertions are not just profoundly wrong; they are profoundly dangerous. They reflect a growing trend among certain political figures to question and undermine the judiciary for their own gain. This isn't just a systemic error; it's a systemic threat.
