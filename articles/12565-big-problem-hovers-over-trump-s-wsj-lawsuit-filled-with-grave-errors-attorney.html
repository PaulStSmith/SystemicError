'Big problem' hovers over Trump's WSJ lawsuit filled with grave errors: attorney

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

President Donald Trump's legal team made several grave errors in their new $10 billion lawsuit against the Wall Street Journal, NewsCorp and Rupert Murdoch, according to an attorney who specializes in First Amendment law.

In a thread posted to his X account Friday, Atlanta, Georgia-based lawyer Andrew Fleischman predicted that Trump's lawsuit will be promptly thrown out due to multiple glaring issues. Chiefly, Trump filed the lawsuit in federal court in the Southern District of Florida. While he may have done so as a means of drawing U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon — the Trump appointee who officially scuttled former DOJ Special Counsel Jack Smith's classified documents case against Trump last July — Fleischman pointed out that Florida has an anti-SLAPP statute [Strategic Lawsuits Against Public Participation] that will doom Trump's litigation.

Florida's libel and defamation laws also require that plaintiffs give defendants at least five days' notice that they plan to sue them before officially submitting the filing in court. According to Fleischman, Trump "made that impossible" by suing the Journal just a day after the paper published its bombshell report about a lewd birthday message Trump allegedly sent Epstein in 2003.

"The remedy is dismissal," Fleischman tweeted. "And I suspect that means Trump pays the WSJ's legal fees."

"That's not the only big problem with the lawsuit," he continued in a subsequent tweet. "But it's the one that an 11 year old with a Lexis subscription could figure out in 5 minutes."

Fleischman cited a lawsuit filed in Florida last year in which a plaintiff failed to abide by the required five-day notification rule, which resulted in the judge promptly dismissing the suit. In the Faller v. Beasley Broadcasting Group., Inc case, U.S. District Judge Sheri Polster Chappell of the Middle District of Florida repeatedly referenced the five-day rule in her decision to dismiss plaintiff James Stuart Faller's defamation case.

"The rule does seem very clear," Fleischman wrote.

The Georgia-based attorney went on to opine that Trump should be aware of that rule already, since he abided by it in his unsuccessful 2023 lawsuit against CNN (which Fleischman noted was still "dismissed as frivolous"). He further observed that the Journal covered its tracks by acknowledging that it remains unknown how the note to Epstein was prepared.

Fleischman also said that Trump's legal team is unable to prove "actual malice" in the Journal's reporting, which is a requirement for any defamation lawsuit. In order to win a defamation claim, Trump would have to convince the court that the Journal knew the information it was publishing about him was false.

"This lawsuit is meant to punish a newspaper for fair reporting," he tweeted. "Any lawyer who tells you it has merit is talking out his a--."

Our Analysis:

Trump's $10 Billion Lawsuit: A Comedy of Legal Errors

In the latest chapter of what could be generously described as performance art but more accurately as a legal blunder, President Donald Trump's legal team has launched a $10 billion lawsuit that seems doomed from the start, against the Wall Street Journal, NewsCorp, and Rupert Murdoch. Andrew Fleischman, an attorney specializing in First Amendment law, has dissected this legal action with the precision of a scalpel, revealing a series of errors so fundamental that they border on the absurd.

Location, Location, Location... and Legislation

The choice of venue is the first glaring mistake in this legal saga. Trump's team filed the lawsuit in the federal court of the Southern District of Florida, likely aiming for U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon, a Trump appointee known for scuttling a previous case against him. However, in what can only be described as an oversight, Florida's anti-SLAPP statute, designed to prevent strategic lawsuits against public participation, is poised to be the Achilles' heel of this litigation. It's akin to choosing to fight on a battlefield mined by your adversary.

A Rush to Judgment

Adding to the comedy of errors, Trump's lawsuit violates Florida's libel and defamation laws, which mandate that defendants receive at least five days' notice before a lawsuit is filed. Trump, in his impetuous rush, filed a mere day after the Wall Street Journal published its report. This procedural faux pas is not just a minor hiccup; it's a fatal flaw that mandates dismissal. It's as if Trump's legal playbook was written in invisible ink — clear for all to see except, apparently, his legal team.

The Burden of Proof and the Specter of Malice

Fleischman's critique doesn't stop at procedural missteps. He dives into the substantive heart of defamation law, highlighting the insurmountable challenge Trump faces in proving "actual malice" on the part of the Wall Street Journal. In defamation suits, the plaintiff must demonstrate that the defendant knew the information was false or showed reckless disregard for the truth. The Journal, by acknowledging the uncertainty surrounding the note's preparation, has effectively armored itself against such claims. Trump's lawsuit, therefore, appears less a quest for justice and more an attempt to penalize free press.

A Warning to the Wise

Fleischman's analysis, scathing and meticulous, serves as a stark reminder of the legal system's complexities and the pitfalls awaiting those who navigate it recklessly. His commentary, especially the pointed observation that any lawyer asserting the lawsuit's merit is "talking out his a--," underscores the frivolous nature of Trump's legal action. It's a cautionary tale of what happens when litigation is used as a weapon, not a tool for justice.

In Conclusion

The $10 billion lawsuit filed by Trump is a masterclass in what not to do in a legal battle. From its choice of venue to its disregard for procedural requirements, it reads more like a cautionary tale than a serious legal strategy. Fleischman's critique is not just an analysis of a doomed lawsuit; it's a broader commentary on the misuse of the legal system. As this lawsuit likely stumbles towards dismissal, one can't help but ponder the real motives behind it and the implications for the American legal and political landscape. In the theater of the absurd that is Trump's legal strategy, the only winners are those who understand the punchline.
