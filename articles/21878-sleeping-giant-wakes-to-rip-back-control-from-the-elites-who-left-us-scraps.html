Sleeping giant wakes to rip back control from the elites who left us scraps

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Rich Did Not “Win” Democracy. They Rewired It.

The Wealth Is the Story

The source lays out the central fact plainly: the top 1% now holds $55 trillion in wealth, roughly matching the entire bottom 90%. That is not background inequality. That is political architecture. When wealth concentrates at that scale, it does not merely buy comfort. It buys the terms on which reality gets described, funded, and governed.

The story is strongest when it stops pretending this is an accident of markets and names the winners: billionaires, mega-corporations, and the media systems they control. What looks like public debate is often just elite preference with better packaging.

Media Was Never Neutral

The article correctly identifies the public square as a battleground, not a forum. Its main target is the collapse of shared factual constraints, first through the 1987 repeal of the Fairness Doctrine and then through the post-2010 broadcast of unlimited money into politics after Citizens United.

That is the key pattern: not censorship, but capture. Right-wing media outlets, major digital platforms, and opinion engines were not simply “biased.” They were built into a profit model that rewards grievance, repetition, and loyalty over accuracy. The result is a political culture where voters are fed identities instead of information, and then blamed for the choices that follow.

Money Became Government

Citizens United is not an abstract legal dispute in this story. It is the mechanism that translated economic power into governing power. The source is right to connect dark money, Super PACs, donor anonymity, and election spending with the broader corrosion of democracy.

Elon Musk’s $250 million in the 2024 election, followed by another planned $120 million after SpaceX won a $1.6 billion federal contract, is not a curiosity. It is the business model in public. The same people who profit from deregulation, tax gifts, and public contracts then pay to preserve the conditions that enrich them. That is not participation. It is leverage.

Trump Was the Delivery System

The source treats Trump as both endpoint and proof of concept. That is fair. He did not invent the system. He exploited one that had already been prepared for him by decades of media radicalization, donor capture, and institutional weakness.

What matters is not just Trump’s personal corruption, but the structure that made it useful. According to the source, his presidency became a wealth machine: tax evasions, foreign hotel bookings, overseas ventures, crypto schemes, settlements, and family enrichment. That is the real scandal. Trump was not an aberration from a healthy order. He was a stress test that the order failed.

The Framing Problem

The piece is right about power, but it sometimes hides its own argument under a pile of moralized language. The phrase “low education voters” is a tell. It smuggles contempt into an analysis that should be focused on elite manipulation. The problem is not that workers are stupid. The problem is that institutions built by the rich are designed to overwhelm attention, flatten truth, and make exploitation feel like belonging.

It is also worth saying plainly: this is not a story about “bad messaging” or a media ecosystem that got a little too loud. It is about deliberate political sabotage carried out through ownership, funding, and narrative control. Confusion is not the cause. It is the product.

The Systemic Lesson

The larger pattern is simple enough to be infuriating: wealth concentrations created media concentrations, media concentrations created political concentrations, and political concentrations produced governance that serves capital first and everybody else later, if at all.

If there is a democratic answer, it does not begin with nostalgia for a cleaner press or a nicer billionaire class. It begins with the recognition that the present order was built to convert money into rule. Undoing that means breaking the machinery that lets private wealth set public terms. Until then, the country will keep calling oligarchy “debate” and theft “policy.”
