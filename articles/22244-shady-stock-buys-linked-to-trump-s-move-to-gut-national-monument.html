Shady stock buys linked to Trump's move to gut national monument

Based on an article by Leia Larsen, Grist

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Hand on the LeverThe Timeline Is the StoryNational Security as CoverTribes Pay the PriceWhat Democrats Are Actually ChasingThe Pattern

Public Land as a Private Signal

Congressional Democrats have opened an investigation after Energy Fuels executives bought stock days before Trump slashed Bears Ears National Monument and reopened land for mining. That is the short version. The longer truth is uglier: this is what happens when presidential power, extractive industry, and “national security” rhetoric are fused into one machine.

The Hand on the Lever

The decisive actor here was Trump. He removed 1.24 million acres from Bears Ears, shrinking the monument by 90 percent and making land available again for mining. Whatever Energy Fuels knew, hoped, or positioned itself to benefit from, the actual institutional power belonged to the White House. That matters because too much coverage treats these moments like weather. They are not weather. They are choices.

The lawless elegance of the arrangement is the point: a president redraws the map, and private actors with exposure near that map suddenly stand to gain.

The Timeline Is the Story

Ross Bhappu bought 74,000 shares on July 7, 2026. Bruce Hansen bought 4,000 more the next day. Less than a week later, Trump cut the monument loose. That sequence is enough to raise questions without pretending the investigation has already proven criminal conduct.

But the larger issue is not just whether executives traded on advance knowledge. It is that the structure of U.S. politics repeatedly turns public land into a speculative asset. Bears Ears is not being managed as a living cultural landscape first and a balance-sheet opportunity second. It is being treated as a place where someone can cash in if they are close enough to power.

National Security as Cover

The administration’s language about critical minerals and domestic supply chains is doing the usual laundering work. Uranium, vanadium, rare earths, battery projects, data centers, robotics: the vocabulary is industrial modernity, but the governing logic is still extraction. Public benefit is invoked to excuse private enrichment.

Energy Fuels is already feeding from the government spigot. The Department of Defense gave the company a $725 million loan for rare earth development. The company tells investors that “strong government support” is a competitive advantage. That is not subtle. It is a business model built around state favoritism dressed up as strategic necessity.

The administration calls it national security. The tribes living with the consequences know better: this is a permission structure for dispossession.

Tribes Pay the Price

The Bears Ears Commission, representing five tribal nations, said the downsizing weakens protections for a sacred ancestral homeland and damages a rare model of tribal-federal co-management. That is the reality buried under corporate statements and investor slides. The land is not empty. It is not “available.” It is occupied by memory, obligation, and survival.

Energy Fuels’ defenders insist the company has no property interests inside the monument and follows regulation. That is beside the point. The question is not whether it can recite compliance language. The question is whether a company operating a mill less than a mile from the boundary has profited from repeated political efforts to weaken protections around Indigenous land, while communities face accusations of radioactive transport and environmental risk.

That is not responsible stewardship. It is industrial predation with a permit.

What Democrats Are Actually Chasing

The House Democrats are right to investigate. They should demand the communications, the lobbying contracts, and every federal contact that could show how the boundary shift was prepared, sold, and monetized. But let’s not pretend oversight alone is a solution. Oversight is what institutions reach for after the damage has already been engineered.

The deeper problem is that Trump did not invent this. Energy Fuels pressured the first Trump administration to shrink Bears Ears in 2017. Biden restored the monument in 2021. Trump then returned to the same playbook. That is the cycle: extractive interests lobby, presidents redraw boundaries, and Indigenous communities are left to litigate the leftovers.

The Pattern

This story is not about one suspicious trade. It is about how American power protects the people positioned to profit from dismantling public protections. The market gets the signal. The company gets the advantage. The tribes get the loss. And the state calls it strategy.

That is the system. Bears Ears is simply where it is visible again.
