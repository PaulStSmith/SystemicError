Trump 'doing little' to fulfill one major campaign promise: WSJ

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Reality Behind the RhetoricThe Consequences of InactionThe Dysfunction of CongressIn Conclusion

Donald Trump attends an event at the White House. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump appears to be "doing little" to make good on one of his central campaign promises, according to a new editorial.

The conservative Wall Street Journal Editorial Board argued on Wednesday that Trump is not doing enough to make America's military great again, as he promised to do on the campaign trail. Instead, he appears to be allowing foreign adversaries to form strong alliances. One example of those opportunities is the recent meeting between global leaders like Chinese President Xi Jinping, North Korean Dictator Kim Jong Un, and Russian President Vladimir Putin outside of Beijing, according to the editorial.

The editors said the military parade should be a "wake-up call."

"Mr. Trump keeps bragging about the great American military while doing little to make it even all that good again," the editorial reads in part. "He isn’t proposing a real defense buildup or a multiyear increase in spending. It’s an open question this month whether a dysfunctional Congress will get the Pentagon a full budget, rather than more stopgap funding that carves up military readiness."

"If Mr. Trump doesn’t get serious, he’s putting the U.S. in a position to lose a shooting war that this axis of adversaries seems increasingly willing to entertain," it adds.

Not only would these alliances threaten U.S. national security, they would also threaten the security of American allies across the globe, the editorial contends.

"Mr. Xi showed off everything from undersea vehicles to new intercontinental ballistic missiles," the editorial reads. "He’s building a military that can get what it wants when it decides to move, and don’t think his ambitions stop at swallowing the vital U.S. friend Taiwan."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Military Promises: Loud Thunder, Little Rain

In a recent scathing critique from the Wall Street Journal Editorial Board, President Donald Trump is taken to task for what appears to be a glaring chasm between his bombastic campaign promises regarding the United States military and the reality of his actions—or lack thereof. Trump's pledge to "make America's military great again" now seems to be nothing more than campaign trail bluster, a hollow echo fading into the political ether.

The Reality Behind the Rhetoric

The Wall Street Journal, hardly a bastion of liberal critique, lays bare the incongruity between Trump's grandiose declarations and the stark reality. The editorial points out Trump's failure to initiate a "real defense buildup" or propose any significant multiyear increase in defense spending. This is particularly alarming as we witness foreign adversaries not just forming alliances but parading their military capabilities with brazen confidence. The recent rendezvous of Xi Jinping, Kim Jong Un, and Vladimir Putin, flagged by the editorial, is not merely a photo-op but a "wake-up call" for the United States and its military posture on the global stage.

"Mr. Trump keeps bragging about the great American military while doing little to make it even all that good again," the editorial notes. This sentence alone encapsulates the essence of Trump's tenure—a presidency marked more by what is said than what is done, a leadership style that prioritizes rhetoric over substantive policy implementation.

The Consequences of Inaction

The consequences of Trump's inaction are far-reaching. Not only does it embolden U.S. adversaries, setting the stage for a potential "shooting war" that the current American military posture is unprepared for, but it also threatens the security of U.S. allies globally. The editorial underscores the advancements in military technology displayed by Xi Jinping, from undersea vehicles to new intercontinental ballistic missiles, highlighting a strategic buildup aimed not just at regional dominance but at challenging the U.S. on the world stage.

The reference to Taiwan is particularly poignant. Xi's military ambitions, as pointed out by the Wall Street Journal, do not stop at mere saber-rattling but extend towards real geopolitical objectives like "swallowing" Taiwan, a vital U.S. ally. This should be a clarion call for a reassessment of U.S. military readiness and strategy, yet Trump's administration seems woefully disengaged from the reality of the evolving global power dynamics.

The Dysfunction of Congress

The editorial also casts a spotlight on the dysfunction within Congress, which is complicit in this scenario by failing to secure a full budget for the Pentagon. The reliance on "stopgap funding" rather than committing to a comprehensive defense budget further erodes military readiness and undermines the capability of the U.S. armed forces to respond to emerging threats. This legislative paralysis is not just a reflection of a divided government but indicative of a broader abdication of responsibility towards national security.

In Conclusion

The Wall Street Journal's editorial is a damning indictment of Trump's failure to fulfill one of his cornerstone campaign promises. It reveals a presidency that is all too comfortable with the spectacle of military might but lacks the conviction or the policy acumen to ensure the U.S. military is capable of meeting the challenges of a rapidly changing global landscape. As our adversaries coalesce and demonstrate their growing military capabilities, the question remains: Will Trump's legacy be one of strengthening America's military, or will he be remembered for his contribution to its potential decline? The evidence, as laid bare by the Wall Street Journal, suggests the latter.
