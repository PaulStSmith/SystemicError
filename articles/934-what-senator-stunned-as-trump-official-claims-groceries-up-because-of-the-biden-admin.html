'What?' Senator stunned as Trump official claims groceries up 'because of the Biden admin'

Blame the Outgoing President, Starve the Present

The Real Power Is in the Room

Brooke Rollins is not an outside commentator guessing at grocery pain. She is the Agriculture Secretary, speaking for the administration that controls USDA policy, food aid, and the public story around both. When she tells struggling families to blame Joe Biden for prices, she is not making an observation. She is redirecting responsibility away from the government currently holding the levers.

The Source, Briefly

In a Senate Agriculture Committee hearing, Sen. Raphael Warnock pressed Rollins on rising grocery costs, USDA projections, cuts to $500 million in food bank funding, and Rollins’ own count of 4.3 million Americans removed from SNAP. Rollins responded by blaming Biden for high prices, citing a few falling item prices, and calling the SNAP reduction “a celebration of work and the dignity of work.”

Misdirection as Policy

Rollins’ answer is the oldest trick in modern governance: treat present damage as somebody else’s inheritance. Grocery bills are up, families feel it immediately, and the administration’s response is to wave around selective price drops on avocados and eggs as if isolated items erase the broader burden. That is not analysis. It is a rhetorical screen designed to obscure who is actually governing now.

Cruelty With a Moral Gloss

The food bank cuts and SNAP removals matter more than the verbal dodge because they show how this administration handles hardship: it does not merely ignore it, it reframes it as virtue. Taking 4.3 million people off SNAP and cutting food bank support while families struggle to buy groceries is not a “celebration” of work. It is the familiar political alchemy of converting deprivation into a sermon.

What Warnock Exposed

Warnock did what oversight is supposed to do: force the official to answer for the present, not hide behind the past. His disbelief landed because Rollins’ defense was sterile in exactly the way he said it was. A family in Georgia cannot pay for groceries with a lecture about Biden, and they cannot eat selective statistics from a secretary’s talking points.

The Pattern

This is how power protects itself under pressure: blame the predecessor, trim the safety net, and dress austerity up as moral seriousness. The public is told to absorb higher prices, accept fewer benefits, and applaud the cruelty as discipline. The name of the game is not confusion. It is institutional evasion, with the administration asking poor people to carry the cost and the blame at the same time.
