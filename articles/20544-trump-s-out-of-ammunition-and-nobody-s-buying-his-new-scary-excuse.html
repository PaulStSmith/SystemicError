Trump's out of ammunition — and nobody's buying his new 'scary' excuse

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Trump’s Communist Panic Is a Confession of Weakness

The Usual Trick, Retired and Recycled

Trump does not launch a “communist menace” campaign because he has discovered an actual communist threat. He does it because he has run out of defensible achievements. The source material makes the structure plain: prices are still outpacing wages, his Iran war is a debacle, his tariffs have failed, and his immigration crackdown is unpopular. When the record is this bad, the enemy list becomes the product.

Who Has Power Here

The institutional power sits with Trump and the Republican apparatus around him. He is not an aggrieved outsider warning from the margins; he is the president using the machinery of state symbolism, including Mount Rushmore and the 250th anniversary stage, to launder partisan panic into national identity. The goal is not persuasion in a neutral sense. It is to shift attention away from the consequences of his own decisions and toward a familiar ideological scare.

That is the real move: convert governance failure into cultural warfare.

Red-Baiting as Political Waste Disposal

The “communist” label is not analysis. It is political waste disposal. It gets dumped on any Democrat who talks about Medicare for All, childcare, public college, or taxing the super-rich. Those are not fringe fantasies in the source text; they are popular positions with broad public support. Trump’s problem is that the policies he wants to demonize are anchored in material life, not in an abstract ideology he can easily caricature.

So he reaches for the oldest trick in the right-wing handbook: if you cannot defend your agenda, criminalize your opponent’s vocabulary.

The Real Target Is Democratic Renewal

The people Trump is trying to smear are described as younger Democrats with actual political energy: Zohran Mamdani, AOC, Katie Wilson, Melat Kiros, and others winning primaries by taking on corporate power and political corruption. That matters. The attack is not really against “communism.” It is against a Democratic wing that is trying to reconnect politics to rent, wages, health care, and the price of simply living.

This is why the smear is revealing. Trump is not afraid of Marxism. He is afraid of Democrats who can name the class structure without apologizing for it.

The Historical Pattern Is The Point

The source text correctly places this in the lineage of McCarthyism: scare politics used to discipline the left, protect elite power, and punish multiracial democratic coalitions. That history matters because the tactic never disappeared. It just changed costume. Then, as now, red-baiting is what the right reaches for when it needs to turn public frustration away from the people actually making the decisions.

And the earlier chapters of that story were never clean. Southern segregationist Democrats helped drive the anti-communist frenzy when it served white supremacy and labor suppression. The lesson is not that all parties are equally rotten. It is that authoritarian tactics spread wherever elites think fear will do the work that policy cannot.

What This Story Actually Shows

The deeper pattern is simple: when a governing coalition cannot defend its economic and political record, it reaches for enemies it can invent. Trump’s “communist menace” talk is not strength. It is the sound of a ruling faction stripping the varnish off its own weakness and hoping panic will cover the stain.

The anti-democratic impulse is not hidden here. It is the point. Trump’s politics depend on false enemies, broken institutions, and a public kept too distracted to notice who is holding power and what they are doing with it.
