Trump raked over the coals for ‘embarrassing’ meltdown about American Olympic skier

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to reporters, on his way to the Marine One helicopter to depart the White House for travel to Detroit, Michigan, in Washington, D.C. U.S. January 13, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump was widely ridiculed Sunday after targeting American Olympic skier Hunter Hess in a blistering attack, an attack labeled by some critics as “genuinely embarrassing.”

Hess, a 27-year-old Oregon native, was asked in Italy on Friday about what it meant to represent the United States in this year’s Winter Olympics, to which he said it was “a little hard,” and that he wasn’t the “biggest fan” of “everything that’s going on in the U.S.,” Reuters reported. Trump had apparently seen Hess’s remarks by Sunday, and attacked him as a “real loser” that was “hard to root for.”

The unprecedented instance of a U.S. president publicly attacking an American Olympic athlete was met with exhaustion from critics, such as X user “Gandalv,” a political commentator who’s amassed nearly 60,000 followers on the platform.

“From a European perspective, this is genuinely embarrassing. You would have to search far and wide to find a country that manages to look this ugly in its politics and public behavior, and a president who so perfectly mirrors it,” they wrote in a post on X.

“In most democracies, a leader would rise above it, defend the country’s values without acting personally wounded, and avoid turning a citizen into a national punching bag. This is not strength and the MAGA regime drags the entire country’s reputation down.”

The closest parallel in American history occurred during the 1968 Summer Olympics in Mexico, when two Black American athletes – Tommie Smith and John Carlos – raised their fists during a performance of the National Anthem in solidarity with the ongoing fight against racial inequality. And, while Smith and Carlos were ultimately expelled from the Olympic Games for their act of protest, they were not attacked by the sitting president, then Lyndon B. Johnson.

“Trump is a Disgrace: He just called US Olympic Skier, Hunter Hess ‘a real loser,’” wrote liberal political commentator Brian Krassenstein, who’s amassed nearly 1 million followers on X.

“Imagine if Joe Biden called a US Olympian ‘a real loser.’ Imagine the meltdowns that MAGA would be having right now, calling him a traitor. The President of the US is a pathetic clown.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tirade Against Olympian Hunter Hess: A New Low in Presidential Decorum

In what can only be described as a spectacle of unpresidential behavior, President Donald Trump’s recent attack on American Olympic skier Hunter Hess has once again illuminated the depths to which political discourse in the United States has sunk. The incident, as reported, is not just a fleeting moment of embarrassment on the international stage but a glaring example of how far the standards of leadership have plummeted.

The Incident

Hunter Hess, a 27-year-old Olympian, made a rather mild comment about the difficulty of representing the United States given the current state of affairs. His remarks, which were less an indictment and more a reflection of a sentiment felt by many Americans, were met with an astonishingly disproportionate response. Trump, in a move that redefines pettiness, labeled Hess a “real loser,” making it “hard to root for” him.

This isn’t just about an athlete expressing a personal view or a president disagreeing with it. This is about the leader of the free world choosing to publicly deride an individual representing the nation on a global stage. The implications are as clear as they are concerning.

Historical Context and the Echoes of 1968

The article draws a parallel to the 1968 Summer Olympics, where athletes Tommie Smith and John Carlos were expelled for their protest against racial inequality. Yet, they were not subjected to direct verbal attacks from then-President Lyndon B. Johnson. This comparison not only highlights the unprecedented nature of Trump’s outburst but also serves as a stark reminder of how presidential responses to dissent have evolved - or, more accurately, regressed.

The International Perspective

The commentary from “Gandalv,” a European political observer, brings an international lens to the debacle, labeling it “genuinely embarrassing.” This perspective is invaluable as it underscores the damage such incidents do to the United States’ reputation abroad. It’s one thing for domestic politics to be mired in polarization and pettiness, but it’s another for these issues to so vividly tarnish the nation’s image on the world stage.

The MAGA Regime’s Impact on National Discourse

The article points to a broader, more systemic issue: the impact of Trump and the MAGA movement’s rhetoric on national discourse. This isn’t merely about Trump’s penchant for controversy or his unique brand of leadership. It’s about how the norms and values that underpin democratic dialogue and presidential conduct are being eroded. The “MAGA regime,” as it’s aptly called, has not only dragged down the country’s reputation but has fundamentally altered the expectations of presidential behavior.

The Double Standard

Brian Krassenstein’s commentary highlights the glaring double standard at play. Imagine, if you will, the uproar had Joe Biden made a similar remark about an Olympian. The outcry from MAGA supporters would be deafening, with accusations of treason and calls for respect for national representatives. This hypocrisy is not just notable; it’s a damning indictment of the current political climate’s partisanship.

Conclusion: The Pathetic Clown and the Loss of Respect

The president’s role, historically, has been one of leadership, dignity, and respect - both given and received. Trump’s actions, as described in the article, are a far cry from these ideals. Calling a U.S. Olympian “a real loser” is not just undignified; it’s unbecoming of the office of the President.

This incident is a symptom of a larger disease - a systemic error in American politics where divisiveness has replaced dialogue, and where the highest office in the land can be used to bully rather than to unite. Trump’s behavior is not just an embarrassment on the international stage; it’s a crisis of leadership at home. As we move forward, the question becomes: How does a nation recover its dignity when its leaders seem intent on discarding it?
