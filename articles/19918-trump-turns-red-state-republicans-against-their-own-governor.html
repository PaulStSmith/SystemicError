Trump turns red state Republicans against their own governor

Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt — a Republican who also serves as chair of the National Governors Association (NGA) — is losing the support of his fellow Oklahoma Republicans as they side with President Donald Trump in the president’s public feud with him. At issue: Stitt is fighting Trump over the president’s refusal to allow specific Democratic governors (Wes Moore of Maryland and Jared Polis of Colorado) to attend the NGA’s annual White House meeting.

Atty. Gen. Gentner Drummond said he agreed with Trump in calling Stitt a “RINO” (Republican In Name Only) for arguing that all America’s governors should be invited to the annual event, as is tradition for the NGA. Drummond is running in the upcoming GOP primary to replace Stitt as governor.

“Governor Stitt misrepresented the President’s invitation to the governors and has often taken baffling positions that are out of step with Oklahoma republicans,” Drummond said.

Chip Keating, Oklahoma’s former Secretary of Public Safety, said that Stitt and Keating’s other opponents in the gubernatorial race “were never known as ‘Trump guys’ before they announced for governor. These office-hoppers are only loyal to their next election, not to the Oklahomans who elected them and overwhelmingly voted for President Trump all three times.”

Trump and Stitt began feuding over the NGA event when the White House allegedly withheld invitations to Democratic governors. Stitt responded by withdrawing the NGA from business breakfast and a follow up black tie dinner. On Wednesday Stitt publicly stated that all 55 governors and territory leaders were invited, characterizing the earlier dispute as a “misunderstanding.”

Trump replied by going on his Truth Social platform to claim that there had been no misunderstanding, but rather that Stitt “incorrectly stated” Trump’s position. He later described Stitt as a “very mediocre” governor who “called for help” during the 2022 gubernatorial election.

“I Endorsed him, and he won – Sorry, my cherished Oklahoma, to have done that to you!” Trump said.

Stitt initially was a very vocal supporter of Trump, but has recently split with the president on his National Guard deployments and immigration policies. He has also feuded with Ryan Walters, the former Oklahoma State Superintendent of Public Instruction.

Our Analysis:

Oklahoma’s GOP Rift: Stitt vs. Trump and the Battle Over Bipartisanship

In an unfolding drama that reads like a Shakespearean tragedy set in the heartland of America, Oklahoma Governor Kevin Stitt finds himself increasingly isolated within his own party—embroiled in a public feud with none other than former President Donald Trump. The root of their discord? A seemingly principled stance by Stitt against Trump’s refusal to invite Democratic governors to the National Governors Association’s (NGA) annual White House meeting—a move that has sparked outrage among Trump loyalists and led to accusations of Stitt being a “RINO” (Republican In Name Only).

The Allegations Fly

Atty. Gen. Gentner Drummond didn’t mince words when he sided with Trump, echoing the former president’s derogatory label for Stitt and criticizing him for being out of step with Oklahoma Republicans. Drummond’s critique is not just a personal attack; it’s a calculated political maneuver as he eyes Stitt’s gubernatorial seat. His statement, “Governor Stitt misrepresented the President’s invitation to the governors and has often taken baffling positions that are out of step with Oklahoma Republicans,” is a direct jab at Stitt’s leadership and decision-making.

Chip Keating, Oklahoma’s former Secretary of Public Safety, joins the chorus of dissent, painting Stitt and his allies as opportunistic politicians who are loyal only to their own ambitions. This narrative, while scathing, underscores a deeper issue within the Republican party: the litmus test of loyalty to Trump as a determinant of one’s political legitimacy and future.

Stitt’s Stand and Trump’s Tantrum

The feud reached a boiling point when Stitt publicly challenged the exclusion of Democratic governors from the NGA event, withdrawing from significant portions of the event in protest. Stitt’s insistence that all 55 governors and territory leaders were invited—which he characterized as rectifying a “misunderstanding”—was met with derision by Trump. The former president took to his Truth Social platform to refute Stitt’s claims, labeling him as “very mediocre” and expressing regret for endorsing him in the 2022 gubernatorial election.

A Deeper Divide

What’s at stake here is more than just an interpersonal feud or a power struggle within the Oklahoma GOP. This situation mirrors the larger schism within the Republican Party at the national level—a divide between traditional conservative principles and the cult of personality surrounding Trump. Stitt’s falling out with Trump over issues such as National Guard deployments and immigration policies signals a broader ideological battleground where party loyalty is often conflated with loyalty to Trump.

The Implications

This episode raises critical questions about the future direction of the Republican Party, both in Oklahoma and nationally. Will the party continue to ostracize members who challenge Trump’s authority, or will there be room for dissenting voices that advocate for a more inclusive and principled conservatism? The outcome of this feud may well serve as a bellwether for the GOP’s identity crisis as it grapples with its allegiance to Trump versus its foundational conservative values.

Furthermore, the public spat between Stitt and Trump, and the ensuing fallout, highlight the precarious nature of political alliances and the volatile dynamics of party politics. As the GOP navigates these turbulent waters, the eyes of Oklahoma—and indeed, the nation—will be watching closely to see whether principles or personalities prevail.
