Trump is 'in a fix' as his 'babyish obsession' sends his supporters fleeing: conservative

The qualities and tools Donald Trump used in his first term to enamor his fans are falling apart during his second term as voters and congressional supporters are increasingly leaving his camp.

That is the opinion of long-time Wall Street Journal columnist Peggy Noonan, who observed that the president’s “mood swings” as well as those of voters is signaling that exhaustion has set in.

It’s not just plummeting poll numbers that are plaguing the president, she wrote, but it is what comes out of the president’s mouth that is helping the exodus along, as his vulgar attacks on perceived enemies have ramped up and turned off fans who used to find him charming.

“There is the matter of his mouth. The president’s supporters have for 10 years put up with his babyish obsession with insulting people. They think of it as the Trump Tax, the price you pay for getting someone bold and tough,” she wrote. “But his hate-stoking now, in an era of political violence, is going to get someone hurt.”

“In his Truth Social post Tuesday night he used criminal language about the press—news outlets and reporters are ‘seditious, perhaps even treasonous,’ They ‘libel and demean THE PRESIDENT OF THE UNITED STATES.’ They are true Enemies of the people, and we should do something about it.’ Like what?” she asked before advising, “It isn’t 2015, we’re more on edge. In a darker time, he’s going to find in the polls fewer people willing to pay the Trump Tax.”

Adding to his woes was the loss of support from high-profile former MAGA supporter Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) with Noonan predicting, “She’s leaving Congress but not looking like someone who lost her battles with Mr. Trump. His problem: Once someone makes a successful jailbreak, all the other prisoners know a jailbreak is possible. This changes the conversation in the prison yard. Guards are eyed differently, the warden’s mystique is diminished.”

An inflection point for Trump will arrive after the first of the year when he delivers the traditional State of the Union speech, which the columnist suggested may be his last chance to stem the bleeding.

“Those addresses don’t have the power they once had but still retain some,” she cautioned. “He might focus on things people are really thinking about—AI, inflation and how Americans in their 30s and 40s can get it together to buy a house and have a baby and keep this whole lumbering thing called America going.”

Our Analysis:

The Decline of Trump’s Enchantment: A Critical Analysis

In an era where political discourse is more polarized than ever, the observations of Peggy Noonan in her recent column for the Wall Street Journal cut through the noise with a precision that deserves more than a cursory glance. Her analysis of Donald Trump’s dwindling charm among his base and the broader electorate is not only timely but speaks volumes about the changing dynamics within American politics. Let’s dissect her arguments with the critical eye they warrant, peeling back the layers of what could be heralding a significant shift in the political landscape.

The Waning of the Trump Tax

Noonan’s characterization of the “Trump Tax” – the metaphorical price paid for enduring the former president’s incessant insults in exchange for a leader perceived as “bold and tough” – is a stark reminder of the bargain many of his supporters believed they were entering into. However, as Noonan points out, the currency of this tax is inflating, with the cost becoming too steep for even his staunchest supporters. This is not merely about plummeting poll numbers; it’s about a fundamental shift in what the electorate is willing to tolerate. The increase in Trump’s vulgar attacks on perceived enemies, especially in an era marked by political violence, is not just unpalatable; it’s dangerous.

The Dangerous Rhetoric in a Fragile Era

Noonan’s analysis of Trump’s rhetoric, particularly his labelling of the press as “seditious” and “treasonous,” hits a nerve. This is not about freedom of speech; it’s about inciting violence against a cornerstone of democracy – the free press. The question she poses, “Like what?” in response to Trump’s call to “do something about it,” is chilling in its implications. It serves as a reminder that words, especially those from a figure of authority, carry weight that can tip the scales towards chaos.

The Significance of Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Defection

The defection of Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, once a high-profile MAGA supporter, is emblematic of a broader trend. Noonan’s metaphor of a “jailbreak” is particularly apt. Greene’s departure signals to others that the walls of the MAGA fortress are not impregnable. This is not just about one individual’s change of heart; it’s a crack in the facade that could lead to a structural collapse. Trump’s influence, once seemingly unassailable, is showing signs of erosion, suggesting a diminishing fear among Republicans to publicly break ranks.

The State of the Union: A Last Stand?

Noonan’s speculation about the upcoming State of the Union address as a potential inflection point for Trump is intriguing. This address could indeed be his last chance to reverse the tide, to show that he can rise above the divisive rhetoric and focus on critical issues facing Americans. However, given his track record, skepticism is warranted. The ability to pivot to substantive discussions on AI, inflation, and societal challenges requires a level of nuance and empathy that has been conspicuously absent from Trump’s political playbook.

Conclusion: A Diminishing Return on the Trump Investment

The analysis provided by Noonan, and further dissected here, points to a broader theme: the diminishing return on the investment in Trump’s brand of politics. As the cost of the “Trump Tax” rises, the returns – in the form of bold, effective leadership – are in decline. The political landscape is shifting, and with it, the tolerance for leaders who prioritize division over discourse, and insults over solutions. The upcoming State of the Union address may indeed be Trump’s last opportunity to change course, but if history is any guide, it’s an opportunity he is likely to squander.

In the end, Noonan’s column serves as a mirror to the current state of American politics, reflecting a reality that many have sensed but few have articulated with such clarity. The question that remains is whether this reflection will spur change or merely be admired for its accuracy before being disregarded in the next news cycle. Only time will tell, but the clock is undoubtedly ticking.
