‘This is nuts!’: Internet explodes over Marjorie Taylor Greene's sudden resignation

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unraveling Spectacle of Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Resignation

Marjorie Taylor Greene. (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

The internet erupted Friday after Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene announced she will resign from Congress effective Jan. 5 following escalating tensions with President Donald Trump and increasingly frequent breaks from the Republican Party.

The resignation followed weeks of public infighting with the MAGA leader and a sudden attempt by Greene to revamp her public image and apologize for past remarks. Online, the announcement was widely described as both shocking and bizarre – and in many corners, a cause for celebration.

“Good riddance and goodbye,” MAGA influencer Laura Loomer wrote in one of a series of seething social media posts targeting the Georgia Republican. “I hate when women pretend like they are done so you will chase them and beg them to stay. It’s such female behavior. MTG wants Trump to chase her. When someone leaves, open the door for them and then change the locks so they can’t return. Goodbye!”

Loomer later called Greene “a terrible person,” told her followers that she gets “a lot of joy in watching my enemies fall,” and predicted Greene would run for president in 2028.

RealClearNews White House reporter Philip Melanchthon Wegmann, wrote: “Trump runs his most loyal ally, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, out of office the same day he bears hugs a democratic socialist, Mayor-elect Zoran Mamdani.”

Independent journalist Aaron Rupar piled on: “Trump feuding with Marjorie Taylor Greene but being in love with Zohran Mamdani was not on my November 2025 bingo card.”

Writer Charlotte Clymer voiced confusion felt across the political spectrum: “I don’t pretend to know exactly what’s going on with Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation, but I sure…know we’re not getting the full story. None of this makes sense.” In a follow-up post, she called the move “bizarre” and part of “one of the weirdest days of this era.”

Clymer also noted a detail others flagged: Greene’s resignation date may be timed to qualify her for a congressional pension.

“A House Member has to win three consecutive terms and serve at least five years,” the veteran and progressive activist added. “January 3rd marks her fifth year in Congress. She can’t start collecting until she’s 62, but that does qualify her for a pension. From what I can tell, that’s about $15k annually in today’s dollars.”

Rep. Malcolm Kenyatta (D-PA) joked: “Trump glazed Mamdani so hard Marjorie Taylor Greene resigned from Congress.” While former federal prosecutor Ron Filipkowski wrote: “So the new version of MAGA is that Marjorie Taylor Greene is a traitor, Rand Paul is a RINO, and Zohran Mamdani is great. Got it.”

Writer and social media commentator Brian Krassenstein perhaps summed it up best with his X post: “This is nuts!

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling Spectacle of Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Resignation

The recent announcement of Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation from Congress is a spectacle that reads more like a plot twist in a political drama than a chapter in American governance. The internet’s explosion following her resignation is a testament to Greene’s contentious and polarizing career. But let’s slice through the sensationalism and break down the facts and implications of this political bombshell.

The Shockwaves of Resignation

Marjorie Taylor Greene’s departure from Congress is painted with broad strokes of shock and confusion. The reasons cited—escalating tensions with President Donald Trump and a series of breaks from the Republican Party—highlight the chaotic nature of current political alliances. Greene’s sudden pivot towards revamping her public image and issuing apologies for past remarks adds an additional layer of unpredictability to her political narrative.

MAGA’s Mixed Reactions

The reaction from the MAGA corner is particularly telling. Laura Loomer’s acidic commentary branding Greene’s move as “such female behavior” is not just an attack on Greene but a reflection of the deep-seated misogyny that often permeates political discourse. Loomer’s subsequent remarks, where she revels in the downfall of perceived adversaries, underlines the cutthroat nature of political loyalty within MAGA ranks.

Philip Melanchthon Wegmann’s observation that Trump’s actions—ousting a loyal ally while embracing a democratic socialist—signifies a bewildering realignment of political allegiances. This sentiment is echoed across the board, with numerous commentators expressing disbelief and confusion over the evolving dynamics within the Republican Party.

The Quest for a Rational Explanation

Charlotte Clymer’s statements encapsulate the general bewilderment surrounding Greene’s resignation. The timing of her resignation, strategically positioned to qualify her for a congressional pension, is a detail that injects a dose of cynicism into the narrative. This move, while legal, raises questions about the motivations behind Greene’s decision to resign, suggesting a calculated exit strategy rather than a purely ideological rift.

The Bizarre Politics of MAGA

The commentary from figures like Malcolm Kenyatta and Ron Filipkowski underscores the absurdity now inherent in MAGA politics, where the lines between ally and adversary are constantly redrawn. The notion that Greene is now considered a traitor, Rand Paul a RINO, and Zohran Mamdani somehow a figure of admiration, speaks volumes about the volatile and often contradictory nature of political loyalties within this group.

Conclusion: A Maelstrom of Motivations

Greene’s resignation is more than just a political event; it’s a vivid illustration of the tumultuous and unpredictable nature of contemporary American politics. The responses it has elicited reveal deep divisions, not just between but within political factions. As spectators and analysts, we must tread carefully, distinguishing between genuine ideological shifts and strategic maneuvering.

In the end, Marjorie Taylor Greene’s resignation is a reminder of the complex interplay of personal ambition, political loyalty, and public spectacle. It’s a chapter that, while bizarre, is entirely fitting for the era we find ourselves in—a period marked by systemic errors, where the unexpected has become the norm.
