Bush White House cardiologist flags new Trump health claim oddity: 'This makes no sense'

A former White House cardiologist believes a recent health claim made by Donald Trump has little grounding in fact.

Jonathan Reiner, the former doctor to Vice President Dick Cheney, responded to a claim made by the 47th POTUS that he takes aspirin against his doctor’s orders. In an interview with NBC, Trump confirmed he is still taking aspirin and “I don’t want to change”. He added, “I want that blood to be nice and thin running through my heart.”

Reiner, director of the cardiac catheterization laboratory at The George Washington University Hospital, has since shared that Trump’s logic here makes little sense.

In a post to X, he wrote, “This still makes no sense.” Earlier this year, Reiner suggested he is concerned about the president’s health, and not just because of the aspirin intake. Speaking to The Lead host Phil Mattingly, Reiner says the public has received almost no “meaningful” information on Trump’s health.

In summer 2025, Trump had swollen ankles and another series of tests were conducted to check in on the president’s health. Cognitive tests were also carried out on the president across 2025.

Reiner said at the time, “Now we learn the president didn’t have an MRI. He actually had a CT scan, which explains why the president’s physician didn’t describe the test after the president disclosed his MRI, because he didn’t have an MRI.”

Reiner would also comment on Trump’s use of aspirin as making “no sense”. He said, “It’s not like changing something from gumbo to chicken soup.”

The former White House cardiologist has since called for an inquiry into Trump’s mental fitness, with Reiner suggesting the president’s reaction to not winning the Nobel Peace Prize is ground for an investigation.

He wrote at the time, “This letter, and the fact that the president directed that it be distributed to other European countries, should trigger a bipartisan congressional inquiry into presidential fitness.”

The letter had been written by Trump and sent to Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre, where the president told Norway he no longer felt “an obligation to think purely of peace” when it came to the country.

Our Analysis:

The Aspirin Anecdote: Unraveling Trump’s Medical Missteps

In a tale that teeters between bizarre and bewildering, Donald Trump, the 47th POTUS, has once again fluttered the dovecotes of medical professionals and the public alike with his recent health claim. According to Trump, his daily regimen includes aspirin, consumed against his doctor’s advice, to keep his blood “nice and thin.” This revelation, met with skepticism from Jonathan Reiner, a former White House cardiologist, underscores a broader narrative of perplexity and concern surrounding Trump’s approach to his health.

Trump’s Aspirin Assertion: A Pill Too Hard to Swallow

Trump’s cavalier attitude towards aspirin consumption, as detailed in an NBC interview, is a classic example of his often unorthodox approach to health and medicine. “I don’t want to change,” Trump declared, embedding his personal medical philosophy with a simplicity that is as alarming as it is audacious. Reiner, a seasoned medical professional, responded with a succinct yet powerful rebuttal: “This still makes no sense.”

Given the context, Trump’s self-prescribed aspirin regimen not only flouts conventional medical wisdom but also showcases a troubling disregard for expert advice. Aspirin, while beneficial under specific circumstances, is not a one-size-fits-all solution, especially for someone of Trump’s profile. The potential risks associated with unnecessary aspirin intake, including bleeding, cannot be understated, making Trump’s defiance a matter of genuine concern.

The Transparency Conundrum: Trump’s Health Under Scrutiny

Reiner’s critique extends beyond the aspirin issue, touching upon a more systemic problem: the opacity surrounding Trump’s health. Despite a series of tests conducted in 2025 to assess his well-being, including cognitive evaluations, the public remains largely in the dark. The revelation that Trump underwent a CT scan, as opposed to an MRI, only after he himself disclosed undergoing an MRI, further muddles the waters, highlighting a lack of straightforward communication.

This lack of clarity is not a trivial matter. For a figure as polarizing and influential as Trump, the health of the president is of paramount public interest. The absence of “meaningful” information, as Reiner rightly points out, is not just a personal privacy issue but a question of national concern.

Mental Fitness for Office: A Question Unanswered

Perhaps most alarmingly, Reiner’s call for an inquiry into Trump’s mental fitness, spurred by his reaction to not winning the Nobel Peace Prize, paints a picture of a leader whose behavior and decision-making could potentially be impaired. The distribution of a letter to European countries, expressing a diminished “obligation to think purely of peace” following this personal setback, is not only petulant but deeply troubling. It underscores a volatility and impulsiveness ill-suited to the office Trump once held.

Conclusion: The Aspirin Allegory and Beyond

The narrative surrounding Trump’s aspirin intake, while seemingly trivial, is emblematic of a larger, more concerning trend. It illustrates a disregard for expert medical advice, a lack of transparency regarding personal health, and questions of mental fitness for office. These issues, when compounded, underscore the need for a more rigorous examination of the health and fitness of those in the highest echelons of power.

In the end, Trump’s aspirin anecdote is more than just a medical misstep; it’s a cautionary tale of hubris, opacity, and the imperative for accountability. As observers and constituents, we must demand more than just thin blood from our leaders; we need thick skin, clear minds, and an unwavering commitment to transparency and sound judgment.
