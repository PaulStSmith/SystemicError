'Off the rails': WSJ's conservative editors tear down Trump's 'biggest overreach'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a House Republican members conference meeting in Trump National Doral resort, in Miami, Florida, U.S. January 27, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

The Wall Street Journal’s conservative editorial board went after President Donald Trump for what they framed as an unconstitutional attempt “to run Harvard” University after the Ivy League school rejected his demands for a MAGA-inspired policy overhaul.

Trump’s funding freeze of $2.2 billion in retaliation for the university’s snub drew the ire of the Journal’s board, which scorched the action in a Tuesday opinion piece that detailed the “good reasons to oppose this unprecedented attempt by government to micromanage a private university.”

While the editorial board noted that some of Trump’s demands fit the government’s civil rights purview and the MAGA leader's own ban on diversity, equity, and inclusion programs, it found little else to support the presidential actions.

“The Administration runs off the legal rails by ordering Harvard to reduce ‘governance bloat, duplication, or decentralization.’ It also orders the school to review ‘all existing and prospective faculty . . . for plagiarism’ and ensure ‘viewpoint diversity’ in ‘each department, field, or teaching unit,’” according to the board.

“These reforms may be worth pursuing, but the government has no business requiring them,” they told readers. The conservative editorial board grew particularly concerned about what they called Trump’s “biggest overreach” – which it slammed the administration for requiring "viewpoint diversity" – while failing to offer a definition.

“Does this mean the English department must hire more Republican faculty or Shakespeare scholars?” the Journal’s board of editors asked as it fired off a set of its own questions. “Must Harvard ask applicants if they support Mr. Trump and impose ideological quotas in hiring and admissions?"

The board concluded by telling readers that Congress can pass legislation if it wants to advance Trump’s reforms to higher education.

“But the Administration can’t unilaterally and retroactively attach strings to grants that are unrelated to their purpose,” the board wrote Tuesday. “President Trump has enough balls in the air without also trying to run Harvard.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Harvard Hysteria: A Masterclass in Overreach

In a recent and rather astonishing turn of events, The Wall Street Journal's conservative editorial board has taken a firm stand against President Donald Trump's latest escapade, which could easily be dubbed his attempt to "run Harvard." This saga unfolds as Trump demands an overhaul at the prestigious Ivy League school, inspired by his MAGA ideology, only to be rebuffed. The response? A funding freeze of a whopping $2.2 billion. Such a figure isn't petty cash, even by Ivy League standards, but the rationale—or lack thereof—behind this move is where the plot thickens.

Unconstitutional Attempts and Micromanagement

Let's break down the audacity it takes to wield federal power as a club against an institution for not aligning with one's personal political creed. The Journal rightly points out the glaring issue here: Trump's demands step far beyond the bounds of constitutional propriety, veering into a territory that's not just uncharted but outright alarming. It's one thing to advocate for changes within the scope of civil rights; it's entirely another to dictate how a private university should manage its internal affairs—ranging from faculty reviews for plagiarism to enforcing "viewpoint diversity."

"Viewpoint Diversity": An Undefined Mandate

The vagueness surrounding the demand for "viewpoint diversity" is particularly troubling. What does it mean in practice? Does it translate to ideological quotas in hiring and admissions, as the Journal questions? The imposition of such a standard—without clear guidelines or rationale—smacks of a thinly veiled attempt to politicize academic institutions along partisan lines. The specter of having to pass a political litmus test to teach Shakespeare is as ludicrous as it is dangerous.

Legislative Action vs. Executive Overreach

The editorial board's call to Congress is a reminder of the constitutional separation of powers that underpins the United States government. Legislation, not executive fiat, is the appropriate vehicle for enacting sweeping reforms. The idea that the Trump Administration could, or should, unilaterally attach strings to unrelated grants is not just overreach; it's a fundamental misunderstanding of how governance should work in a democratic society.

Trump's Juggling Act: Too Many Balls in the Air

The closing remark from the Journal serves as a poignant critique of Trump's governance style: a chaotic juggling act with too many balls in the air. The attempt to micromanage Harvard, to dictate its policies and priorities from the Oval Office, is emblematic of a broader problem within the Trump administration—a penchant for overreach, a disregard for established norms, and a troubling inclination to insert political ideology into arenas where it has no place.

In essence, the Wall Street Journal's conservative editorial board has laid bare the absurdity and danger of Trump's Harvard gambit. It's a stark reminder of the need for checks and balances, for respect for the autonomy of private institutions, and for the adherence to constitutional principles. Trump's attempt to "run Harvard" is not just misguided; it's a glaring example of systemic error in judgment and governance.
