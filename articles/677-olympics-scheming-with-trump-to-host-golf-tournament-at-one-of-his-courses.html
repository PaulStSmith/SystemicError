Olympics scheming with Trump to host golf tournament at one of his courses

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20262 min read

The White House as a Booking Agent for Trump’s Private Course

The Setup

Politico’s reporting is simple enough: LA28 is discussing a pre-Olympics pro-am golf event at Trump National Golf Club Los Angeles, with the Trump administration in the loop. The detail that matters is not the ceremony around it. It is that a president is once again using a public event with federal dependencies to route prestige, access, and likely money toward his own property.

Where the Power Sits

The institutional power here is not with LA28, and not with the anonymous “sports bodies” politely orbiting the story. The real leverage sits with the White House. The Olympics depend on federal security, transportation, immigration, and visa processing. That means the administration can shape whether the Games run smoothly or become administratively painful. When one side controls the choke points, “partnership” is just a softer word for pressure.

Who Benefits From the Arrangement

Trump is the decision-maker with the clearest incentive. He values the course, the branding, and the revenue. The proposed event is not even an official Olympic competition, which makes the setup more revealing, not less: a side event near the Games is being treated as a prestige vehicle for a presidential family business. This is not accidental proximity. It is the conversion of public spectacle into private advantage.

The Misdirection

The weak framing is the familiar one: organizers are “working with” the administration, the situation is “fluid,” and everyone is allegedly just navigating logistics. That language smears responsibility across a cloud of process and unnamed sources. It treats a deliberate conflict of interest like a scheduling complication. The real story is not confusion. It is compliance under unequal power.

The Pattern

This fits a broader pattern already visible in the World Cup reporting: major international events held in the United States become bargaining chips inside Trump’s personal and political economy. Travel exemptions, access, and federal approvals turn into leverage points; foreign athletes and event organizers are forced to negotiate with a president who sees global sports as a venue for self-dealing. The institution is no longer merely hosting. It is being used.

The Bottom Line

What Politico documents is not just another Trump nuisance around a sporting event. It is the normalizing of state power serving private brand extraction. The Olympic movement needs federal cooperation, and Trump knows exactly how to monetize that dependence. When public authority and private property become interchangeable in the same transaction, corruption does not look exceptional. It looks administrative.
