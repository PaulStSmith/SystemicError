This 'necrotizing cancer' just made NBC defend Charlie Kirk's 'appalling movement': expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 15, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:NBC's Integrity Crisis: A Scathing Analysis of a Fallen Peacock

NBC was forced to defend Charlie Kirk's "appalling movement" because it lost its integrity, according to an independent political strategist who worked on former President George W. Bush's campaign.

Political strategist Steve Schmidt, who has criticized Trump in the past, pointed to a letter that Comcast and NBC released to their employees. The letter, which was publicized over the weekend, said in part, "The tragic loss of Charlie Kirk, a 31-year-old father, husband, and advocate for open debate, whose faith was important to him, reminds us of the fragility of life and the urgent need for unity in our nation."

The letter also justified the firing of an analyst who pointed out that Kirk lived his life spreading division, and noted that he died in a similar manner.

"You may have seen that MSNBC recently ended its association with a contributor who made an unacceptable and insensitive comment about this horrific event. That coverage was at odds with fostering civil dialogue and being willing to listen to the points of view of those who have differing opinions. We should be able to disagree, robustly and passionately, but, ultimately, with respect. We need to do better," it states.

But according to Schmidt, this letter shows how far NBC has truly fallen.

"This is a story of a letter written by a peacock, who was once proud. It is now abused by the owners of a new zoo that have drooped its tail and dulled its plume. Known once for integrity, the bird is now riddled with a necrotizing cancer, which has sentenced it to a slow, miserable, degrading and public decomposition in front of the glazed eyes of the last customers who stare at the peacock, and wonder why," he wrote. "How did the peacock lose its feathers and write this letter, which would have made all of the other peacocks that came before so very, very sad?"

Schmidt also attempted to explain the origin of the letter:

"Once upon a time, I was the vice chairman of the world’s largest public relations firm, and I know a thing or two about how letters like the one this story is about come to life. Emails were firing back and forth between Comcast/NBCUniversal/MSNBC executives and their PR teams about the raging extremist reaction to the murder of a racial supremacist and demagogue, who debased the public discourse with conspiracies, bigotry and sophistry at the expense of debate, discourse and reason," he wrote. "The emails would undoubtedly have shown panic and incoherence by the leadership of Comcast, a $123 billion company. There were no adults in the room to comprehend the moment and understand what was happening. The moral failure at Comcast was absolute, and it was profound."

According to Schmidt, the letter ignores that Kirk had "served an appalling political movement, and was an apologist for political violence."

"He was a denier about the outcomes of legitimate elections, which lit the fuse for the violence that has metastasized and grown ever since political extremists tried to overthrow the 2020 election during a violent coup. It is not only now denied, but celebrated by the pardoned criminals Trump incited to attack the Capitol," he then added.

Our Analysis:

NBC's Integrity Crisis: A Scathing Analysis of a Fallen Peacock

In what can only be described as a theatrical tragedy, NBC finds itself in the midst of a storm, not of nature's making, but of its own. Steve Schmidt, a veteran political strategist with a frontline view of political warfare, has cast a merciless spotlight on NBC's latest debacle. His analysis? The network has not just stumbled; it has plummeted from the heights of journalistic integrity into a pit of moral and ethical decay.

The Letter at the Heart of the Controversy

NBC, in a move that can only be seen as bewildering, issued a letter defending Charlie Kirk, a figure synonymous with divisive politics and extremism. This letter, dripping with a tone of reconciliation and unity, paradoxically defends a man who, by many accounts, dedicated his life to undermining those very ideals. The dismissal of an analyst for calling out Kirk's divisive legacy only adds a layer of irony thick enough to cut with a knife.

Schmidt's Scathing Metaphor

Schmidt's analogy of NBC as a once-proud peacock now suffering from a "necrotizing cancer" is not just vivid; it's visceral. His words paint a picture of a network that has lost its way, sacrificing its plumage—its integrity and purpose—on the altar of appeasement and misguided attempts at fostering unity. This is not just a fall from grace; it's a nosedive into ignominy.

The PR Disaster Unveiled

Schmidt's insider knowledge of the PR world allows him to dissect the chaos that likely ensued behind the scenes. His description of "emails firing back and forth" amidst "panic and incoherence" by Comcast's leadership paints a damning picture. It's a scenario where the blind lead the blind, culminating in a public relations disaster that does more to expose the company's moral failings than to mitigate any damage.

Ignoring the Elephant in the Room

The letter's failure to acknowledge Kirk's controversial legacy is perhaps its most egregious fault. Kirk's history of denying legitimate election outcomes and his role in fueling political violence are not just footnotes in his biography; they are central to understanding the impact of his actions. NBC's omission of these facts is not just oversight; it's a deliberate act of obfuscation.

The Bigger Picture: A Media in Moral Crisis

This episode is more than just a scandal; it's a symptom of a larger disease plaguing the media landscape. The quest for neutrality has led networks down a path where the truth becomes negotiable, and figures like Kirk are sanitized in death as they never were in life. The result? A media unable to confront the very forces threatening the fabric of democracy.

Conclusion: A Call for Journalistic Integrity

NBC's handling of the Charlie Kirk debacle is a stark reminder of the importance of journalistic integrity. Schmidt's analysis, while scathing, is a necessary medicine. It's a call to action for media outlets to rediscover their spine, to stand up for truth, and to refuse to whitewash the legacies of those who seek to divide us. The peacock must regrow its feathers, not through empty gestures of unity but through a steadfast commitment to the truth, no matter how uncomfortable it may be.
