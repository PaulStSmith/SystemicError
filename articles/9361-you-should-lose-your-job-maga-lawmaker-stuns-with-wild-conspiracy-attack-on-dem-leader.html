“Reptilian,” Higgins wrote on X Monday night along with a photo of Jeffries, referencing the conspiracy theory popularized by British Holocaust denier David Icke, who’s argued that a race of “inter-dimensionary“ reptilian humanoids occupy many powerful positions in governments across the world.

The stunning claim was widely condemned online by critics, with some, like X user “Chippy Dizzle,” arguing that Higgins should be removed from office over the remark.

“You SHOULD lose your job for this... but as we’ve learned, there are no consequences for Republicans anymore,” the user wrote, who frequently posts content critical of the Trump administration and has amassed more than 4,200 followers.

Higgins has been a strong ally to President Donald Trump, supporting efforts to overturn the 2020 election in his favor and embracing much of Trump’s rhetoric. Trump endorsed Higgins for his re-election bid in 2024, calling him an “America First warrior” in a recorded phone call that Higgins promoted in his campaign.

Accusing his House colleague of being a “reptilian,” however, shocked even those seemingly on the right, including X user “Jessica,” who’s followed by Trump ally Rep. Mike Lee, shares right-wing content and has amassed nearly 7,000 followers.

“Weird times we are in,” she wrote on X.

Others called the accusation a bridge too far for sitting congressman.

“This is unbecoming of a congressman,”wroteBrian Eskow, a podcast host with more than 44,000 followers.

Our Analysis:

The Reptilian Accusation: An Analysis of Rep. Clay Higgins’ Bizarre Claim

In a move that defies political norms (yet seems increasingly characteristic of today’s political circus), Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) has catapulted himself into the spotlight with a claim so outlandish, it would be laughable if it weren’t so concerning. Higgins, on social media platform X, labeled House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) as “Reptilian,” aligning himself with the fringe conspiracy theory that suggests a race of reptilian humanoids controls world governments. This theory, notably popularized by British Holocaust denier David Icke, is not just a harmless fantasy; it’s a dangerous narrative that undermines rational discourse and feeds into the paranoia that plagues our political landscape.

The Reaction: Shock and Condemnation

The online response was swift and overwhelmingly negative. Critics, both from the left and surprisingly from the right, condemned Higgins’ statement. One user, “Chippy Dizzle,” rightly pointed out the glaring issue in today’s political accountability, stating, “You SHOULD lose your job for this... but as we’ve learned, there are no consequences for Republicans anymore.” This sentiment echoes a broader frustration with the current state of political accountability, where sensationalism often overshadows substance, and outrageous statements go unchecked.

Higgins, a staunch ally of President Donald Trump and a supporter of efforts to overturn the 2020 election, is no stranger to controversy. Trump’s endorsement of Higgins as an “America First warrior” only serves to highlight the growing divide within the Republican Party, between those who embrace extreme rhetoric and conspiracy theories, and those who adhere to a more traditional conservative ideology.

The Implications: Beyond the Bizarre

Labeling a political opponent as a “reptilian” is not just a bizarre sideshow; it’s indicative of a deeper, more insidious problem in American politics. When elected officials engage in such baseless accusations, it not only detracts from meaningful policy debate but also erodes public trust in governmental institutions. The willingness of a sitting congressman to embrace and propagate conspiracy theories is a stark reminder of the normalization of extremism in our political discourse.

Furthermore, the reaction from some corners of the right, including a user followed by Trump ally Rep. Mike Lee, suggests a growing discomfort with the direction some elements of the party are heading. The comment “Weird times we are in” from a right-wing user underscores the surreal nature of current political debates, where the line between reality and conspiracy theory increasingly blurs.

Conclusion: A Bridge Too Far?

Rep. Higgins’ claim raises serious questions about the state of political rhetoric in America. While freedom of speech is a cornerstone of democracy, the propagation of dangerous and unfounded conspiracy theories by those in power poses a significant threat to the integrity of our political system. The backlash from both sides of the aisle offers a glimmer of hope that there are still boundaries that should not be crossed, even in today’s polarized environment. However, the incident also serves as a grim reminder of the challenges facing American democracy, where sensationalism often drowns out reason, and accountability seems to be a relic of the past.

As we navigate these “weird times,” it is crucial for the electorate to demand higher standards from their elected officials. The health of our democracy depends on it.
