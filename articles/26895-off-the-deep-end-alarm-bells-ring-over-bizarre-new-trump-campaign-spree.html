'Off the deep end': Alarm bells ring over 'bizarre' new Trump campaign spree

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosOctober 31, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedThe power is not diagnosticSubmission as political theaterA headline stronger than its evidenceThe pattern beneath the spectacle

What happened

AlterNet reports that author Jennifer Welch called President Donald Trump’s recent campaign appearances “bizarre” and “unacceptable,” citing strange noises, forgotten names, comments about male politicians’ bodies, and public mockery of Republican allies. The supposed “alarm bells” in the headline refer chiefly to Welch’s commentary, not to a newly reported medical finding or organized warning from multiple experts.

Welch highlighted Trump’s praise of Ohio Sen. Jon Husted’s backside, his insult about Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton’s appearance, and his ridicule of Sen. Ted Cruz while discussing a hypothetical Supreme Court appointment. AlterNet also cites psychologist Alan D. Blotcky, who told the outlet earlier in October that, in his opinion, Trump acts nihilistically and cares primarily about chaos, personal acclaim, and financial gain.

The power is not diagnostic

The consequential fact is not whether commentators can assign Trump a psychological label from afar. It is that the president used campaign appearances to objectify, insult, and humiliate political allies—and those allies remained publicly accommodating.

Trump held the institutional power in every encounter described. Husted, Paxton, and Cruz were not helpless citizens confronting an erratic stranger. They were Republican officials operating inside a political structure dominated by a president whose approval, endorsements, and retaliation could affect their careers.

Submission as political theater

Welch’s strongest observation concerns the men who “race up” to accept humiliation or objectification. Cruz’s reported response is especially revealing: Trump said Democrats and Republicans alike hated him, and Cruz stood, smiled, and waved.

That exchange does not establish Trump’s mental condition. It does demonstrate a hierarchy. The leader dispenses ridicule; the subordinate converts degradation into loyalty theater. What looks merely strange at the level of personality becomes politically legible once power enters the frame.

A headline stronger than its evidence

AlterNet packages the episode as experts sounding alarms over a campaign “spree,” but the article supplies one author’s podcast commentary and one psychologist’s sweeping opinion from an earlier interview. It provides no clinical evaluation, no response from Trump or the officials discussed, and no evidence establishing Blotcky’s claims about Trump’s ultimate intentions or finances.

That weakness matters. Speculation about whether Trump is “off the deep end” shifts attention from conduct the reporting actually describes to a diagnosis it cannot establish. The behavior does not need amateur medical certification to warrant scrutiny. Public humiliation, personal fixation, and enforced deference are political facts when practiced by the person who controls the executive branch and dominates his party.

The pattern beneath the spectacle

The familiar misdirection is to treat Trump’s conduct as a puzzle of temperament while Republican compliance becomes background scenery. That framing turns a power arrangement into a personality story.

The larger pattern is institutional submission: elected officials accept insult because proximity to presidential power remains more valuable than dignity or independence. The bizarre remarks attract attention, but the consequential event is the room’s response. A political system degrades when powerful people learn that smiling through humiliation is the price of access—and decide the price is worth paying.
