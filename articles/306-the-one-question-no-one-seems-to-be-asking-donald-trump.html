The one question no one seems to be asking Donald Trump

Based on an article by Tom Engelhardt, Common Dreams

Paulo SantosJune 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

War as Budget, Budget as War

The Reporting, Stripped Down

The source argues that Donald Trump is normalizing a rolling cycle of threats, interventions, and open-ended militarism, with Iran, Venezuela, Cuba, Greenland, and Somalia all treated as interchangeable theaters. It also ties that posture to a ballooning Pentagon budget, fossil-fuel politics, climate damage, and the grotesque concentration of wealth embodied by Elon Musk’s billionaire status. The piece’s main claim is clear: this is not drift. It is a governing logic.

Who Holds Power

The actual institutional power sits with the presidency, the Pentagon, and the congressional machinery that still labels this spending “defense.” Trump can posture; Pete Hegseth can announce readiness for “any possible contingency”; Congress can ratify the bill. That is the chain. The source correctly identifies the executive branch as the driver, but the deeper point is that the war system is not a Trump invention. He is an accelerant inside an existing machine built to turn fear into appropriations.

That is why the budget figure matters. A possible $1.5 trillion yearly Pentagon outlay is not a side note. It is the political prize. War talk is the sales pitch.

The Misdirection Game

The strongest part of the source is its refusal to let the language of “peace through strength” stand unchallenged. That phrase is not a doctrine. It is marketing. In practice, it means “peace” gets defined as perpetual militarization, and “strength” gets defined as whatever price tag Congress will tolerate this year.

The article also exposes a familiar form of misdirection: presenting deliberate escalation as unpredictability. Trump is framed as erratic, but erratic is too soft a word when the pattern is repeated. The repeated target list is the point. The scattershot naming of countries creates the appearance of confusion while hiding the basic fact that power is being used to threaten weaker states and consolidate domestic political advantage.

Climate as Collateral

The source does something useful by linking war policy to fossil-fuel dependence and climate harm. That connection is not rhetorical decoration; it is political structure. A government that treats military expansion as normal also protects the energy system that feeds it. The result is a feedback loop: more conflict, more fuel consumption, more emissions, more instability, more pretext for intervention.

Pakistan’s record heat and the NOAA El Niño warning are not random climate inserts. They are evidence that the planet is already under strain while U.S. policy keeps pouring accelerant on the fire. The cruelty here is not abstract. It is administrative. The people who profit from war and fossil fuels are insulated from the costs they impose on everyone else.

Billionaires and Bombs

The mention of Elon Musk and the “first trillionaire” is not incidental either. It points to the same political economy that makes endless war seem affordable and social breakdown seem inevitable. The source is right to call the economy rigged. That rigging is visible in the overlap between concentrated wealth, military spending, and the public language used to sanitize both.

This is where the story stops being about Trump alone. Trump is not the anomaly. He is what happens when oligarchy, militarism, and climate denial stop bothering with restraint. The result is a government that can find money for bombs, propaganda, and permanent readiness, while treating environmental collapse and mass displacement as somebody else’s problem.

The Real Pattern

The larger pattern is authoritarian cruelty wrapped in patriotic vocabulary. A state that cannot promise peace sells readiness for endless conflict. A political class that benefits from that system calls it stability. And a press culture that still indulges euphemism helps keep the machinery running.

The source is messy in style, but the underlying diagnosis is sound: war is being used as a governing instrument, climate destruction is being normalized as collateral damage, and institutional language is being abused to disguise both. That is not confusion. It is how a decayed political order protects itself while asking everyone else to pay for the consequences.
