'Goes too far': GOP senators raging over Trump admin attack on clean energy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 19, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump's administration is poised to attack the clean energy industry with new permitting rules that would affect "scores of projects on private land," and "even Republicans say it goes too far," wrote Joshua Siegel and Zack Colman for Politico on Friday.

The memo, issued on Wednesday, requires "Secretary Doug Burgum’s personal approval for even the most routine activities related to wind and solar projects on federal lands" — which would also require any project that is primarily on private land that must run through or link up with infrastructure on land managed by the federal government to go through the same burdensome process.

In another stealth change to federal rules, Burgum also banned wind and solar developers from using the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Information for Planning and Consultation portal, which is used to automatically screen proposed projects that may overlap with the habitats of endangered species. Without access to this tool, wind and solar projects will have to undergo a much lengthier and more difficult environmental review than any other kind of project.

All of this follows Republicans in Congress approving a provision in Trump's tax cut megabill that repeals most federal green energy credits, which already threatens to slow the development of an industry that has ironically created most of its new infrastructure and jobs in Republican-controlled states like Texas, Oklahoma, and Indiana.

But even many of these same Republicans draw the line at these new regulations on wind and solar power, said the report.

Sen. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK), who only voted for Trump's bill in part because she negotiated a one-year delay in phasing out credits for wind and solar projects, said this memo “is like putting the final nail into” the coffin of that compromise. Sen. John Curtis (R-UT), who was also in on that compromise, said he disagrees with putting new restrictions on solar because "we need every electron we can get" to be energy dominant.

Even Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-WV), whose state economy depends heavily on the fossil fuel industry, told Politico she believes there must be a "fair playing field" and “every energy project needs to be permitted as fast as possible regardless of resource."

Democrats, too, are warning that if this memo is not rescinded or struck down in court, it could come back to haunt Republicans.

"I would warn them if they create this as a precedent and it survives, a future administration could play the same game with oil and gas pipelines and leases,” said Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-NM).

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's Assault on Clean Energy: A Dangerous Game

In a move that reeks of disdain for both the environment and economic progress, the Trump administration has unveiled a draconian set of permitting rules aimed squarely at the burgeoning clean energy sector. This latest maneuver, detailed by Joshua Siegel and Zack Colman in Politico, not only threatens to stifle innovation but also pits the administration against members of its own party, revealing a chasm in Republican ranks over the future of energy in America.

An Unprecedented Power Grab

At the heart of this controversy is a memo that demands Secretary Doug Burgum's personal approval for all wind and solar projects on federal lands — a requirement that absurdly extends to any clean energy project that interacts with federal infrastructure, even if primarily situated on private property. This is not just a bottleneck; it's a stranglehold on an industry that has become a vital part of the nation's energy infrastructure, particularly in states that lean heavily Republican.

But the administration didn't stop there. In a move that can only be described as a stealth attack on environmental considerations, it has also cut off access to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Information for Planning and Consultation portal. This tool, essential for evaluating the impact of projects on endangered species, is now off-limits to wind and solar developers, putting them at a significant disadvantage compared to other energy sectors. This isn't regulation; it's sabotage.

The Irony of Republican Resistance

What makes this situation particularly galling is the irony of its impact. The clean energy sector, a significant source of jobs and infrastructure development, has thrived in Republican-controlled states like Texas, Oklahoma, and Indiana. Yet, this hasn't shielded it from the administration's attack. Even more striking is the resistance from within the GOP ranks. Senators like Lisa Murkowski (R-AK), John Curtis (R-UT), and Shelley Moore Capito (R-WV) have voiced their opposition, underscoring a rare but critical fracture in the party's stance on energy policy.

Murkowski's comment that the memo puts the "final nail into" the coffin of a previous compromise on green energy credits is particularly telling. It suggests a betrayal of not just the clean energy industry but of the very legislators who sought to find a middle ground. Curtis's pragmatic assertion that "we need every electron we can get" to achieve energy dominance further highlights the short-sightedness of restricting clean energy development.

A Dangerous Precedent

Democrats, for their part, have been quick to warn of the long-term consequences of such heavy-handed regulation. Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-NM) points out the potential for this precedent to be turned against the fossil fuel industry under future administrations. This isn't just about clean energy; it's about the very principles of fair and efficient regulation.

The Trump administration's latest actions are more than an attack on wind and solar power; they are an assault on common sense, economic progress, and environmental responsibility. By prioritizing political vendettas over pragmatic energy policy, the administration not only harms the clean energy sector but also undermines the nation's energy security and economic future.

In a world increasingly moving towards sustainable solutions, these regulations are not just a step backward; they are a leap into the abyss. It's not just clean energy that's being throttled by these policies — it's the very spirit of innovation and progress that has driven America forward. The administration would do well to remember that energy policy should light the way towards the future, not extinguish the flames of innovation.
