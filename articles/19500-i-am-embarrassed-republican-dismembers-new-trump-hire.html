'I am embarrassed ': Republican dismembers new Trump hire

U.S. President Donald Trump, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Interior Secretary Doug Burgum and U.S. Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., attend a cabinet meeting at the White House (Reuters)

Politico reports a Republican congressman, who is also a tax writer, destroyed President Donald Trump’s pick for head of the IRS and the Social Security Administration.

“This is unacceptable,” Rep. Max Miller (R-Ohio) told IRS CEO Frank Bisignano after Bisignano offered vague answers to lawmakers’ inquiries about tax-filing season and Trump’s Big Beautiful bill’s impact on it, and other issues.

Bisignano got his dressing down at a Wednesday hearing before the House Ways and Means Committee.

“You really need to come in here and answer the questions that these members ask you directly, and saying ‘I’ll come see you in your office,’ even to me, on very basic questions that I’m asking you, is really upsetting,” continued Miller. “I am very embarrassed right now for my side.”

Trump created Bisignano’s position as “CEO” of the IRS to circumvent the Senate confirmation process for agency nominees. But now, even GOP lawmakers appear concerned that Bisignano’s appointment should have been scrutinized. Unlike other IRS leaders, Politico reports that Bisignano does not have a tax background, and he is currently also serving as head of the Social Security Administration.

When nominating the former Fiserv CEO as Social Security administrator last year, Trump argued Bisignano “takes troubled entities and turns them around.” But current Fiserv chief Mike Lyons warned that Bisignano had made major missteps as CEO, overinflating sales projections and relying on short-term cost-cutting before selling his stock for $500 million.

ABC News reported Bisignano claiming to having to “Google Social Security” when Trump offered him the position, according to leaked audio.

On Wednesday, however, Miller was vexed by Bisignano’s non-answers to inquiries about the IRS agency’s plans to tax digital assets. But Bisignano’s empty responses to lawmakers’ questions on less arcane IRS issues drove Miller to fury. Bisignano, for example, appeared to be unacquainted with Trump’s signature tax break, despite the bill’s controversial passage last year through the budget reconciliation process to bypass Senate filibusters.

Politico reports Bisignano told lawmakers he was also ignorant of a so-called “marriage penalty” built into a new deduction for tips, and he confessed to being unable to identify occupations ineligible for a new deduction for overtime pay.

Miller also dismantled Bisignano’s advisers accompanying him to the hearing while appearing to contribute nothing to his answers.

“You need to do a better job of educating the IRS commissioner about the questions that he’s coming here to answer,” Miller told them. “If I was working for a principal, I would never let them walk into a hearing like this.”

Our Analysis:

The Bisignano Debacle: A Stark Illustration of Trump’s IRS Misstep

In a political landscape that often reads like a farce, the recent performance of IRS “CEO” Frank Bisignano before the House Ways and Means Committee plunges to new depths of absurdity. The Politico report detailing Rep. Max Miller’s (R-Ohio) frustration with Bisignano’s nebulous responses—and the broader implications of his appointment—underscores a glaring misjudgment in the Trump administration’s approach to key federal roles. Let’s dissect this fiasco with the precision of a tax auditor on a mission.

The Farce of “CEO” as a Title in Public Service

Firstly, the creation of a “CEO” position for the IRS by President Donald Trump is a mockery of public service and governance. The IRS, a cornerstone of the federal government’s functioning, is not a corporation. Its leader should not be a CEO, immune to Senate scrutiny and chosen for reasons other than proven competence in taxation. This maneuver to circumvent the Senate confirmation process not only undermines democratic checks and balances but also sets a dangerous precedent for accountability in government appointments.

Bisignano’s Ill-equipped Tenure

Frank Bisignano’s tenure as both the IRS “CEO” and head of the Social Security Administration is riddled with red flags. His admission of Googling Social Security upon his nomination is not just a shocking revelation; it’s a slap in the face to the American people. The cavalier attitude towards managing agencies critical to millions of Americans’ livelihoods is unacceptable.

Moreover, Bisignano’s background as the former CEO of Fiserv, where he reportedly inflated sales projections and engaged in short-term cost-cutting, hardly inspires confidence. His financial windfall from selling stock, as highlighted by current Fiserv chief Mike Lyons, paints a picture of a man more concerned with personal gain than public service.

A Performance of Incompetence

The Politico report on Bisignano’s inability to answer basic questions regarding tax-filing season, Trump’s “Big Beautiful bill,” and other significant tax policies is damning. It’s not just incompetence; it’s a gross dereliction of duty. For a tax writer of Rep. Miller’s caliber to express embarrassment is telling. Miller’s frustration with Bisignano’s vague responses and his critique of Bisignano’s advisers’ apparent lack of preparation underscore a systemic failure.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is symptomatic of a larger issue within the Trump administration’s approach to governance: the prioritization of loyalty over expertise, the disdain for procedure, and the undermining of institutional integrity. The appointment of a figure like Bisignano to oversee the IRS and the Social Security Administration without the requisite background or understanding is not just an error; it’s an affront to the American system of governance.

In conclusion, the saga of Frank Bisignano is a stark reminder of the consequences of placing unqualified individuals in positions of significant power. It’s a cautionary tale that underscores the need for rigorous vetting, respect for institutional norms, and a commitment to public service over personal profit. The American people deserve better than Google searches and nebulous answers; they deserve competent, accountable leadership.
