Kindergarten girls targeted for invasive exams by new GOP-led ballot measure

The State Is Turning Pediatrics Into a Purity Test

A Ballot Initiative Built on Bad Faith

Washington voters are being asked to approve IL26-638, a measure that would force girls from kindergarten through high school to undergo sex-verification testing before playing school sports. The proposal would require a health-care provider to certify a child’s sex assigned at birth through genetic screening, testosterone testing, or even examination of reproductive anatomy. That is not a sports policy. It is state-sponsored intrusion dressed up as concern.

The reporting makes the central fact plain: this is being sold as protection for girls while the burden lands on girls. The measure does not explain who would perform these exams, who would pay for them, or how schools would carry them out. That vagueness is not an accident. It is the point. The initiative creates a coercive framework first and leaves the humiliation, confusion, and logistical mess for children, parents, schools, and doctors to absorb later.

Power Is Not With the Children

The people with actual power here are not the athletes, and not the school staff who would have to enforce this nonsense. The real power sits with Brian Heywood, the hedge-fund manager financing the campaign through Let’s Go Washington, and with the political machinery that turns his money into a ballot measure. This is what manufactured moral panic looks like when it has a donor class behind it.

Heywood claims the 2007 policy allowing kids to join teams matching their gender identity comes “at the expense of female athletes and their safety.” The article gives no evidence for that claim, only the assertion. What it does show is that the measure is designed to stigmatize children and force institutions to police their bodies. That is not athlete safety. That is political sadism with a paperwork trail.

Medical Language as Political Cover

Doctors quoted in the piece are blunt: this is not medically necessary, and it is not how sports physicals work. Dr. Gina Martin says the essential parts of a physical are musculoskeletal and cardiac health, not sex verification. She also says she has never run those tests on a patient and does not know how such a process would even work.

That matters because the measure wraps itself in the authority of medicine while violating basic medical norms. It hijacks the annual sports physical, a routine meant to assess whether a child can safely play, and turns it into a gatekeeping ritual. The state would be conscripting clinicians into a political spectacle that serves no medical purpose. The likely result is obvious: unnecessary testing, embarrassment, and pressure to expose children’s bodies in settings that have no business demanding that information.

The Target Is Bigger Than Trans Kids

The official target is transgender athletes. The operational target is every girl who would have to prove she belongs. The source notes that the measure leaves open whether girls could be required to disrobe, possibly in group settings where schools already offer free physicals. That possibility alone reveals how reckless the proposal is: it normalizes invasive scrutiny of children under the banner of fairness.

The article also makes clear how small the supposed problem is. The Washington Interscholastic Activities Association says only about 10 transgender athletes participate among more than 200,000 students in middle and high school sports. The initiative is not responding to a major factual crisis. It is weaponizing an almost nonexistent one to justify surveillance and exclusion. That is classic reactionary politics: inflate a tiny number into an existential menace, then use the panic to reorganize power over vulnerable people.

Litigation as Delay, Not Justice

Legal experts warn the measure would be difficult to challenge if passed, especially after the Supreme Court’s June ruling in West Virginia v. B.P.J. upholding similar bans. Chris Erchull of GLAD Law says any privacy or discrimination challenge would be slow and costly, meaning girls could be forced into testing or out of sports long before a court catches up.

That is the deeper structure here. The initiative is built to win in the short term by making harm immediate and relief delayed. Even if the policy eventually falls in court, the damage already happens: children are screened, humiliated, excluded, or discouraged from participating. This is how bad-faith lawmaking works now. It does not need to survive scrutiny forever. It only needs to inflict consequences fast enough to make the cruelty usable.

What This Story Actually Shows

This is not a story about concern for girls’ sports. It is a story about donor-funded harassment, institutional cowardice, and the political usefulness of scapegoating children. The framing is weak because it lets the initiative pretend to be a policy disagreement when it is really an attempt to normalize state power over bodies that should never have been made targets.

Maria, the recent graduate organizing against the measure, names the truth more clearly than the people funding it: “It is villainizing children.” That is exactly right. The broader pattern is bigger than Washington state. Reactionary politics keeps finding ways to turn schools into battlegrounds, children into symbols, and private anatomy into public discipline. When a campaign needs a hedge-fund bankroll, medical theater, and court-proof cruelty to sell itself, it is not defending fairness. It is laundering control through panic.
