'Closest I've come to tears': Dems take floor to call out senator's manhandling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:An Unprecedented Assault on Democracy and Oversight

Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA) (Photo: screen capture via CSPAN video)

U.S. Senators took to the floor on Thursday afternoon after Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) was shoved out of a Homeland Security news conference, pushed to the ground, and handcuffed by the FBI.

"We have a constitutional obligation to provide oversight to the executive, a constitutional obligation to ask questions, to make inquiries, a constitutional obligation to check and balance the authority of the executive," Sen. Cory Booker (D-NJ) said from the Senate floor.

"And so here we saw before our eyes a senator in their district, in their state asking questions, and we see him being shoved out of a room. But it did not stop there. Shoved to the ground, shoved onto his belly, and have his arms pulled behind his back. This should outrage every United States Senator."

Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA) similarly spoke out, her voice shaking as she confessed she was near tears.

"I have come to this floor often to speak out, to be angry, to be a voice for my people. I have never come this close to having tears in my eyes, as I speak to both sides of this aisle, about this horrendous incident that occurred," said Murray.

"We are a democracy, but we can lose that democracy. It can be gone, unless all of us speak out and forcibly reject what happened to United States Senator, and to send the message that in this democracy it is just, it is right, it is part of our responsibility to speak up, to ask questions, and to be able to have the knowledge we need to represent the people that we come here for," she added.

"We use our voices, Mr. President. We use our votes, Mr. President, to be a part of this democracy. Not violence," Murray continued. "When violence is done by someone representing this administration, in a forceful way, against a United States senator, how does anyone of us go home and tell our constituents that they can be part of a democracy, speak out about what they believe in? This is so wrong."

Our Analysis:

An Unprecedented Assault on Democracy and Oversight

In an event that should have sent shockwaves through the very foundations of American democracy, Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA) was not just removed from a Homeland Security news conference but was subjected to a level of physical aggression that is as alarming as it is unprecedented. Shoved to the ground and handcuffed by the FBI, Padilla's treatment is not just an isolated incident but a glaring red flag about the state of democratic checks and balances in the United States.

Sen. Cory Booker's (D-NJ) remarks from the Senate floor underscore a fundamental principle that seems to have been grossly violated: the constitutional obligation of congressional oversight over the executive. This principle isn't a mere formality; it's the bedrock of American democracy, ensuring that power is not concentrated unchecked in the hands of the few. When Booker emphasizes that a senator, "in their district, in their state asking questions," is met with such force, it's not just an outrage; it's a threat to the very essence of democratic governance.

Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA) echoed this sentiment, her voice shaking as she described the incident as "horrendous." Murray's near tears are indicative of the emotional and democratic gravity of the situation. Her words, "we are a democracy, but we can lose that democracy," should not be taken lightly. This is not hyperbole but a stark warning. The physical manhandling of a senator for performing their duty is not just an attack on an individual but an attack on the institution of democracy itself.

Murray's assertion that "violence is done by someone representing this administration, in a forceful way, against a United States senator" is particularly damning. It implies that the incident is not just an overzealous act by law enforcement but could be indicative of a broader, more disturbing trend of using force to suppress democratic functions and oversight. If representatives of the people can be manhandled for asking questions, what message does that send about the state of free speech and democratic engagement in the country?

The senators' calls to action are not just about seeking justice for Sen. Padilla but a call to defend the very principles of democracy. The incident is a litmus test for America's commitment to its foundational values. If such acts can happen to a United States senator with impunity, one shudders at the thought of what could happen to the average citizen expressing dissent or seeking accountability from their government.

In essence, this incident should not just outrage every United States Senator, as Booker suggests, but every American citizen. It's a stark reminder that the freedoms and democratic principles we often take for granted are far from guaranteed. They must be actively protected, preserved, and defended against all forms of encroachment.

The treatment of Sen. Padilla is a call to action. It's a reminder that democracy is not just about voting but about ensuring that those elected to represent the people can do so without fear of physical reprisal. The silence or inaction of those in power in the face of such blatant disregard for democratic norms and the rule of law signals complicity in its erosion. This incident should not and must not be swept under the rug. It demands a forceful and unequivocal response from all corners of the political spectrum. After all, today it's Sen. Padilla; tomorrow, it could be any one of us.
