Trump officials crash and burn with bizarre excuse for failing policies

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Government in Power Is Blaming a President 580 Days Gone

At an event with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, Vice President JD Vance and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin kept reaching backward to Biden, blaming the previous administration for everything from “fraud in commercial trucking” to global vulnerabilities and munitions being drained. The timeline is the story: Biden left office 580 days ago, and the people talking now are the ones holding office now.

Power Is the Point

This is not an argument from the outside. It is the governing class speaking from inside the machinery of state. Vance is the vice president. Mullin is the secretary of homeland security. They are not commentators, and they are not a frustrated opposition looking for leverage. They are the men with the authority to act, explain, investigate, and govern.

That is why the Biden fixation matters. It is not just lazy rhetoric. It is an admission that the current administration does not want to be judged as the current administration. When power cannot defend its own record, it tries to drag the last occupant back into the room.

The Dodge Is the Message

The source text shows the basic trick clearly: when asked about present-day vulnerabilities, Vance answered with a sentence that began in the past tense and stopped there. When asked about trucking fraud, he blamed Biden again. Mullin did the same thing. This is not confusion. It is a strategy.

The strategy is to convert responsibility into noise. If everything is “Biden’s fault,” then nothing the Trump administration does has to be explained on its own terms. Every failure becomes inherited, every disaster becomes someone else’s residue, every present-tense decision gets laundered through stale partisan reflex.

The Misleading Frame

The article’s core frame is almost too polite for what is happening. It presents the comments as “curious” and notes the mockery they drew, but the sharper truth is that these are deliberate evasions from officials who should be defending current policy, not embalming old scapegoats.

The weakness of this framing is that it treats the behavior like a gaffe with comic value. It is worse than that. It is a governing tactic: blame a departed president for conditions that now sit on your desk. That is how political accountability gets hollowed out. Not through one grand lie, but through repetitive, childish deflection until the public is supposed to forget who actually signed the orders.

What They Are Hiding

The source gives away the deeper anxiety in the topic shifts. Global vulnerabilities, drained munitions, bombs in Iran, trucking fraud, administration failure: these are not random talking points. They are the kind of problems that expose whether the Trump administration has a plan or only a grievance.

So Vance and Mullin reach for Biden because Biden is safer than scrutiny. It is easier to say “the previous administration” than to say what this administration has chosen, what it has broken, what it has failed to repair, and who benefited from that failure. Republicans in power do not want a public accounting. They want a permanent alibi.

The Pattern Beneath It

This is the broader political pattern: authoritarian politics loves a fog machine. It prefers a universe in which accountability is always displaced, where the people with the levers of power pose as victims of their predecessors, and where the public is expected to tolerate incompetence as long as it is performed with confidence.

That is the real scandal here. Not that Vance and Mullin were mocked, but that they felt entitled to govern by memory hole. A party in power should be answering for the state of the country it now runs. Instead it is trying to keep Biden alive as a rhetorical corpse because it has so little to offer in the living present.
