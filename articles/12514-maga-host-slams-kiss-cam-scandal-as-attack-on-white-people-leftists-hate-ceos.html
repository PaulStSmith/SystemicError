MAGA host slams kiss cam scandal as attack on 'white people': 'Leftists hate CEOs'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Distraction: A Masterclass by Jack Posobiec

Real America’s Voice/screen grab

Pro-MAGA host Jack Posobiec argued that backlash against a CEO who was caught having an affair at a Coldplay concert was an attack on "straight white" people.

During an interview with Human Events editor Kenny Cody on Monday, Posobiec likened the ousting of Astronomer CEO Andy Byron to the plans of Democratic candidates.

"You look at some of the candidates now that are being embraced, whether it be Zohran Mamdani, in New York City or whether it be this guy, Omar Fateh, who just won the endorsement of the Democrats in the Minneapolis mayor's race, which essentially means, you know, he's well on his way to becoming the next mayor," the host opined. "They offer full-on free stuff. They offer, hey, we are going to go to all the white people, take away their stuff, and the rich CEOs."

"This is important," he continued. "All those CEO memes that were going around this weekend, the, you know, oh, this guy, he had an affair, Coldplay, et cetera, et cetera. Keep in mind that, once again, all those CEO memes that were running around this weekend were going viral because leftists hate CEOs. And specifically, they hate straight white CEOs."

"If they were not straight, if that was not a white couple, guess what, I guarantee you you would have not seen the response that you did this weekend."

Cody acknowledged the CEO was wrong for having an affair but insisted that the media would not have reported on "any other race of people" or "any other sexuality of people."

"I don't feel sorry for him at all, just as a person or as an influencer, but when it comes to the class of people that always get attacked, it's always the same rich, white men that are getting attacked by the mainstream media because they hate them," he remarked.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction: A Masterclass by Jack Posobiec

In a stunning display of deflection and victimhood, Jack Posobiec, a pro-MAGA host, has launched an argument that could only be conceived in the deepest recesses of the right-wing echo chamber. Posobiec’s contention that the backlash against Astronomer CEO Andy Byron, caught in the act of having an affair at a Coldplay concert, is an attack on “straight white” people, is not just a leap—it's an Olympic-sized vault over the realms of logic and reason.

Distraction and Deflection: The first tool in Posobiec’s kit. By likening Byron’s personal scandal to an orchestrated attack on straight white CEOs, Posobiec attempts to shift the narrative from a story about personal failure and betrayal to a grand conspiracy of leftist hatred against white men. This leap of logic is so vast it should come with its own air traffic control.

Victimhood as a Shield: Posobiec, in a move as predictable as it is disingenuous, uses the victimhood of “straight white” men as a shield to deflect legitimate criticism. The insinuation that leftists only dislike CEOs because of their race and sexual orientation is not just laughably reductive; it's an affront to the intelligence of his audience. This argument conveniently ignores the myriad of reasons one might criticize a CEO, ranging from issues of wealth inequality, corporate malfeasance, to environmental negligence.

False Equivalence: In an attempt to bolster his argument, Posobiec draws comparisons to Democratic candidates Zohran Mamdani and Omar Fateh, suggesting that their policies are aimed at taking from white people to give to others. This gross simplification of progressive policies into a zero-sum racial game is not just misleading; it's dangerously divisive.

Selective Outrage: Posobiec and his interviewee, Kenny Cody, express selective outrage, focusing on the supposed unfair treatment of Byron due to his race and sexual orientation. Cody’s concession that he doesn’t feel sorry for Byron “as a person” but rather as a representation of “rich, white men” who are “always getting attacked,” highlights the inherent bias in their outrage. It's not the morality of Byron’s actions that concern them but the perceived attack on their demographic.

Conclusion:

The argument presented by Posobiec and Cody is a masterclass in the art of distraction, victimhood, false equivalence, and selective outrage. It seeks to transform Andy Byron from a man accountable for his personal actions into a martyr of an imagined war against straight white men. This narrative is not just flawed; it's dangerous. It perpetuates a sense of unjust persecution among those who are, by most measures, among the most privileged in society.

In a world rife with genuine injustice and inequality, Posobiec’s focus on defending the honor of a CEO caught in a personal scandal by framing it as a racial and sexual orientation issue is not just a missed opportunity for meaningful discourse—it's a deliberate obfuscation designed to stoke division and distract from real issues. Let's not fall for it.
