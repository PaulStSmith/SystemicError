Fox host gets Trump aide to admit he can't name single country with trade deal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Liberation Day or Delusion Day ? Trump’s Erratic Tariff Tango

President Donald Trump declared April 2, “Liberation Day” and launched a series of erratic, wildly fluctuating, country-specific tariffs, citing a “national emergency” over the U.S. trade deficit—a trend that has persisted since 1975.

He has also vowed to make 90 deals in 90 days, something experts say is an impossibility, given that trade negotiations often take months if not years to complete. The deadline is fast approaching: July 9, he has said.

The President has yet, as far as we know, to have any country sign an actual trade deal—despite having claimed to already have struck 200—and the clock is ticking. He has claimed to have closed two deals, one with the UK, and one with China. The UK deal has been described as an “outline.”

“Who goes first, if anyone, as of today?” Fox News host Bill Hemmer asked (video below) top Trump economic advisor, and tariff and bitcoin advocate, Stephen Miran.

“You mean, who goes first in terms of countries?” Miran asked. “Yeah, so there’s—look, there’s a number of deals in the works, with a number of our trading partners, particularly in Asia and in Europe as well.”

Miran, Trump’s Chair of the Council of Economic Advisers. said he expected a “flurry” of deals to come “as we get close to the deadline because of the process in which these deals are proceeding, where there’s steps that everyone is taking to move the deal forward.”

“And those steps are designed to yield success around the time of the deadline,” he continued before Hemmer interrupted.

The Fox host appeared less than satisfied.

“Every time we do these segments, I’m just trying to find the name of a country—who will be first in line? Can we say today which country that will be?” Hemmer asked.

“I cannot,” Miran replied.

Back in April Trump had claimed, “I’ve made 200 deals.”

“Pressed on which countries he had made deals with,” Politico reported, “Trump refused to say, nor did he clarify the terms of the agreements. He added that he would announce them ‘over the next three to four weeks,’ once the negotiations are ‘finished.'”

Also in April, Miran told CNN, “We’ve received written offers from almost 20 countries.”

Our Analysis:

Liberation Day or Delusion Day? Trump’s Erratic Tariff Tango

In what can only be described as a fever dream of economic policy, President Donald Trump has declared April 2 as "Liberation Day," a moniker as grandiose as it is perplexing. The day marks the launch of a series of erratic and wildly fluctuating country-specific tariffs under the guise of addressing a "national emergency" over the U.S. trade deficit—a saga that has been unfolding since 1975. Trump's solution? A promise to conjure 90 deals out of thin air in 90 days, a feat that experts have promptly labeled an impossibility. Trade negotiations, known for their glacial pace, often take months, if not years, to bear fruit, making Trump’s deadline of July 9 seem more like a fantasy than a feasible target.

The Art of the Impossible Deal

Trump's ambitious pledge to make 90 deals in 90 days would be laudable if it weren't so detached from reality. As the deadline races closer, the president has yet to secure a single country's signature on an actual trade deal, despite his claims of having already struck 200. This discrepancy between Trump's assertions and reality is not just a gap; it's a chasm. He has claimed to have closed two deals, one with the UK and one with China. However, the UK deal has been described merely as an "outline," a term so vague it could mean anything from a handshake to a wishful thought scribbled on a napkin.

During an interview, Fox News host Bill Hemmer's quest for clarity from Trump's top economic advisor, Stephen Miran, felt like a journey into a bureaucratic abyss. Miran's talk of a "flurry" of deals materializing "as we get close to the deadline" was as reassuring as a mirage in a desert. The promise of impending success, based on unspecified "steps" being taken by unnamed countries, is as nebulous as Trump's overall strategy.

A Mirage of Economic Strategy

Trump's and Miran's rhetoric is a masterclass in obfuscation. The president's claim of having made 200 deals, when pressed for details, evaporates into a commitment to announce them "over the next three to four weeks," a timeline as elastic as his trade policies. Miran's reference to "written offers from almost 20 countries" is equally baffling, offering no substance, no specifics, and certainly no signed agreements.

The administration's approach to trade negotiations seems to be built on a foundation of wishful thinking rather than strategic planning. The notion that a flurry of deals will suddenly materialize as the deadline approaches is reminiscent of a student cramming for an exam the night before, hoping for a passing grade through sheer willpower rather than preparation.

Conclusion: A Strategy Built on Quicksand

As the July 9 deadline looms, the Trump administration's "Liberation Day" is shaping up to be anything but liberating. The erratic tariff strategy, coupled with grandiose but unfounded claims of imminent deals, paints a picture of a policy approach that is not only detached from reality but dangerously impulsive. The economic future of the U.S. cannot be secured through bluster and baseless promises. As the clock ticks down, one thing becomes clear: this administration's trade strategy is less about liberation and more about a desperate gamble, with the American economy as the stake.
