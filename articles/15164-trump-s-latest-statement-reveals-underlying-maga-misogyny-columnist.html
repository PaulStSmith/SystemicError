President Donald Trump's repeated claims that girls will have to get along with fewer dolls as his tariffs work their economic magic reveals an underlying MAGA misogyny that reinforces the beliefs of the manosphere, according to Salon's Amanda Marcotte.

A week ago, Trump seemed to speak off the cuff when he said, "maybe the children will have two dolls instead of 30 dolls." But Marcotte wrote that the "doll" talk became an actual talking point, as evidenced by the fact he told NBC's Kristen Welker, "I don't think a beautiful baby girl that's 11 years old needs to have 30 dolls. I think they can have three dolls or four dolls," then told reporters , "All I’m saying is that a young lady, a 10-year-old-girl, 9-year-old girl, 15-year-old-girl, doesn’t need 37 dolls."

Marcotte argued that "Trump defended his trade-destroying tariffs by lecturing parents about not spoiling their daughters" who are inherently "frivolous" due to their gender, a point that speaks directly to the MAGA base that helped Trump get elected.

Marcotte noted with interest that the the only children Trump talked about were girls, never boys or toys "more stereotypically associated with boys, like Legos or toy trucks."

"This is likely not an accident, but part of a larger effort to sell the otherwise indefensible tariffs to the MAGA base by invoking the same misogynist resentment that helped Trump get elected," Marcotte wrote. "The image of 'too many' dolls taps right into this ugly worldview that overly indulged girls are growing into 'selfish' women who think they're too good to settle for Mr. Tweeting Incel."

In fact, Marcotte wrote that "Trump superfans on X started arguing that the tariffs were the key to restoring male dominance. They falsely claim that tariffs will drive women out of the workforce and force them to get married to survive."

Marcotte wrote that Fox News picked up on the sexist talk "and tried to argue that tariffs will restore the patriarchal gender order by bringing back manly manufacturing jobs, while destroying 'email jobs' that are primarily held by women in MAGA fantasy version of reality."

Marcotte noted that whether or not Americans pick up on Trump's misogyny in delivering his tariff message, they do hear him "admitting prices are going up — and most people are smart enough to know it won't be limited to dolls."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Tariff Tirade: A Cloak of Misogyny?

In an era where every presidential utterance can spark controversy, President Donald Trump’s comments linking tariff policies to the number of dolls a girl should own is not just an off-the-cuff remark but a calculated maneuver steeped in misogyny. According to Amanda Marcotte of Salon, Trump’s fixation on girls and their dolls in discussing the impacts of his economic policies reveals a deeper, more insidious agenda aimed at appeasing his MAGA base by tapping into and reinforcing age-old gender stereotypes and misogynistic resentments. Let’s dissect this with the precision of a scalpel going through butter.

The Doll Debacle: More Than Child's Play

At first glance, Trump’s comments might seem like a benign, if not clumsy, attempt at humor. Yet, upon closer scrutiny, it becomes apparent that there is nothing innocent or humorous about them. The repeated reference to girls specifically, and the fixation on a triviality such as the number of dolls a girl should have, speaks volumes. It's not about economic policy; it's about pushing a narrative where women and girls are seen as frivolous and their desires and interests as trivial and unworthy of serious consideration.

A MAGA Misogyny Masterclass

Marcotte’s analysis hits the nail on the head when she identifies these comments as part of a larger, more disturbing trend of MAGA misogyny. The underlying message? That girls are being overly indulged, leading to a generation of 'selfish' women with inflated self-worth. This narrative conveniently ignores the societal pressures and gender-based expectations placed on girls from a young age, reducing their identities to stereotypes.

Manufacturing Misogyny

The leap from tariffs on imports to the number of dolls a girl owns is a giant one, yet Trump and his supporters seem to make it with Olympic ease. The insinuation that tariffs will somehow restore a lost patriarchal order is not just ludicrous; it's downright dangerous. Marcotte aptly points out the absurdity of the claim that tariffs will drive women out of the workforce and back into the domestic sphere, reliant on marriage for survival.

Fox News: The Echo Chamber

Fox News' role in amplifying this narrative cannot be overstated. By framing tariffs as a means to restore 'manly' manufacturing jobs at the expense of so-called 'email jobs' predominantly held by women, they further entrench gender stereotypes and support a regressive agenda that seeks to roll back decades of progress towards gender equality.

The Real Cost of Tariffs

Amidst this gendered rhetoric, the actual impact of tariffs on the economy and on the everyday American seems to be lost. As Marcotte points out, Trump inadvertently admits that prices will rise as a result of his policies. This admission, buried under layers of misogynistic messaging, is perhaps the most telling. It reveals a willingness to sacrifice economic sensibility on the altar of gender politics.

Conclusion: A Call for Awareness and Action

Trump’s comments and the subsequent discourse it has sparked among his supporters and in the media serve as a stark reminder of the enduring strength of misogynistic undercurrents in American politics. It is imperative that these narratives are challenged, dissected, and debunked with the same fervor with which they are propagated. The cost of failing to do so is too high, impacting not just the current generation of girls and women but those that follow.

In sum, Trump's tariff rhetoric is not just an economic policy discussion; it's a window into a worldview that seeks to diminish and control women under the guise of national interest and economic reform. It's a narrative we must not only question but vehemently oppose.
