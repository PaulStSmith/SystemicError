Americans crushed this same Trump con Republicans pushed 100 years ago

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

Robber-Baron Politics with a Better Marketing Budget

The Mansion Is the Message

The Biltmore Estate is used here as evidence, not nostalgia: 175,000 square feet of inherited wealth built by 1,000 laborers in six years, adorned with imported luxury while the people who built it lived in squalor. That contrast is the point. The source is arguing that Trump-era politics, backed by corporate wealth, is not an anomaly but a return to the same governing logic that made this kind of spectacle possible.

Power Wears the Crown

The people with actual power in this story are not the voters being flattered by patriotic branding. They are the officeholders, donors, corporate beneficiaries, and court allies who can turn public institutions into private advantage. The source names Trump, his corporate backers, and corporate-aligned Supreme Court justices as the decisive actors. That is the correct frame. The harm is not accidental drift; it is policy, design, and permission.

The Blame Is Doing Misdirection Work

The article correctly notes that MAGA voters are absorbing the damage, but that truth can become a convenient diversion if it ends there. The weaker actor is not the one producing the policy. The weaker actor is the person being manipulated by propaganda into defending it. Fox News and other disinformation channels do not merely “confuse” people; they help launder elite intent into class resentment, culture-war panic, and loyal submission. The blame belongs upward, where the incentives are set and the profits are collected.

Oligarchy in Casual Clothes

The source is strongest when it names the structure: deregulation, legal evasion, union suppression, tax giveaways, and institutional sabotage. Trump’s attacks on the judiciary and press are not side behavior; they are part of the operating system. So are the family profits, the Albanian resort on protected land, the Pentagon loan, the drone-company windfall, the billion-dollar self-enrichment, the algorithmic megaphone, and the warehouse surveillance economy. These are not isolated scandals. They are the contemporary version of robber-baron extraction, updated for a financialized, media-saturated state.

Hunger Is the Lever

The piece’s weakest move is its faith that enough pain will automatically generate political clarity. History does not guarantee that. But the basic point is sound: when SNAP and Medicaid are cut, the damage stops being abstract. It becomes bodily. That is why austerity politics relies on distance, delay, and denial. It needs consequences to arrive after the election, after the headline cycle, after accountability has been buried under branding. The cruelty is deliberate because delayed suffering is politically useful.

The Pattern Beneath the Pageantry

What this story reveals is not just inequality, but the method by which inequality is defended: concentrate wealth, capture institutions, flood the zone with distraction, and call the result freedom. The Biltmore is a monument to the fantasy that exploitation can be made beautiful. Trumpism, Musk-style platform power, Bezos-style labor domination, and court-backed deregulation all serve the same project: make extraction look inevitable and resistance look vulgar. That is the system. The pageant is just the wallpaper.
