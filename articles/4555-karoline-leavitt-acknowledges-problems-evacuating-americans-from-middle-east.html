Karoline Leavitt acknowledges 'problems' evacuating Americans from Middle East

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Tangled Web of Unpreparedness and Deflection

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt holds a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 4, 2026. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Press secretary Karoline Leavitt appeared to struggle with the messaging from the White House on Wednesday when she claimed that President Donald Trump had decided to strike Iran before he spoke to Israel about Saturday’s location of the Iranian president. Her statement prompted questions as to why the U.S. didn’t begin evacuating Americans sooner.

NBC News reporter Garrett Haake asked Leavitt about the timeline and where Israel fit in with the intelligence. A follow-up came from the reporter next to him, who asked why more wasn’t done to help Americans abroad.

“Just in the context of what you just told Garrett about the president’s decision on a timeline, if he had decided to do this strike before receiving the specific intelligence about the meeting on Saturday, should there have been more done in advance of the strikes to try to get those Americans in the region resources and information to get home before day three of the war?” she asked.

Leavitt said that there were “many signs” that a war was coming from the State Department and that Americans should get home. She claimed that they issued travel advisories in late 2025 to some Middle Eastern countries and outright “do not travel” notices to other countries like Iran itself.

However, multiple countries, including the United Arab Emirates, didn’t issue a “do not travel” warning until after the bombing. According to older estimates, there are over 50,000 Americans living in the UAE. In fact, at least 88 percent of residents of the UAE are not from the UAE, a Pew Research Center survey said.

Leavitt was asked for another follow-up, saying that as late as last night, the State Department was telling people “we can’t help you” when they called to leave the area.

“Well, the line was updated,” Leavitt said, before demanding journalists in the room fulfill their duty to report that the State Department is no longer telling Americans abroad that it can’t help them get home or to safety.

“I saw many people in this room very quick to do selfie videos with the [phone] line yesterday, but not so quick to report on the corrected message today. So, that is something the administration quickly worked to address. And we have fixed that problem. However, we’re also pointing people to the website, again, to register so that we know exactly where you are. So, when there are commercial flights moving into that area, when there are chartered flights moving into that area, we can get Americans on planes as quickly as possible,” Leavitt said.

She did not address why it took so many days for the State Department to “fix that problem” if the administration knew that they were going to bomb Iran last week.

One reporter later compared the situation to Trump’s complaints that former President Joe Biden flubbed his evacuation of Afghanistan.

Our Analysis:

A Tangled Web of Unpreparedness and Deflection

In a display of what can only be described as a staggering mishandling of critical information dissemination and citizen protection, Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt’s recent press briefing has laid bare the chaotic underpinnings of the Trump administration’s approach to a significant military operation against Iran. The briefing was rife with inconsistencies, evasions, and a concerning lack of foresight that prompts serious questions about the administration’s competence and priorities.

Pre-Strike Confusion and Lack of Coordination

Leavitt’s attempt to navigate the timeline of events leading up to the strike on Iran was nothing short of bewildering. Claiming that President Donald Trump had made the decision to strike Iran before consulting with one of the U.S.’s key allies in the region, Israel, immediately raises red flags about the administration’s decision-making process. The lack of coordination and consultation with allies prior to such a consequential military action is not only reckless but also undermines the very fabric of the international alliances that the U.S. has painstakingly built over decades.

The Ghost of Evacuation Failures Past

The specter of the botched evacuation from Afghanistan under former President Joe Biden loomed large over the briefing, yet it seems the current administration has failed to learn from past mistakes. The pressing question of why more wasn’t done to evacuate or assist Americans in the Middle East prior to the commencement of hostilities was met with deflections and the pointing to “many signs” that war was imminent. Leavitt’s reference to travel advisories issued in late 2025 falls embarrassingly short of a coherent plan for safeguarding American citizens abroad.

It’s particularly egregious that despite these advisories, countries like the United Arab Emirates, home to over 50,000 Americans, did not receive a “do not travel” warning until after the bombings began. This oversight—or worse, negligence—exemplifies a disturbing lack of proactive measures to protect U.S. citizens.

A Reactive, Not Proactive, Approach to Citizen Safety

Leavitt’s comments on the State Department’s initial inability to assist Americans seeking to leave the region, followed by a hasty assurance that the problem has been “fixed,” is a testament to the administration’s reactive stance on issues of paramount importance. The delay in rectifying the assistance line’s message, and the failure to address why it took so long, speaks volumes about the administration’s prioritization of citizen safety.

Moreover, Leavitt’s chastisement of journalists for not promptly reporting on the corrected message smacks of an attempt to shift blame and deflect from the administration’s own shortcomings. The responsibility to protect and assist American citizens abroad is not contingent upon media reporting but is a fundamental duty of the government.

Conclusion: A Pattern of Disregard and Disarray

This press briefing has illuminated a pattern of disregard for the intricacies of international diplomacy and a disarray within the administration’s ranks when it comes to safeguarding American lives. The lack of foresight, coordination, and timely action in the lead-up to and aftermath of the strike on Iran is a stark reminder of the grave implications of leadership failures.

In the final analysis, it is clear that the Trump administration’s handling of this situation has been anything but strategic or empathetic. The American public, especially those with loved ones abroad, deserve better than this haphazard approach to international conflict and citizen safety.
