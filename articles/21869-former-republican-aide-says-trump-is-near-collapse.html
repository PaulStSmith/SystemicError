Former Republican aide says Trump is near 'collapse'

Rotting by Design

Not Collapse, Selection

Steve Schmidt’s argument is that Trump’s administration is “collapsing” under the weight of incompetence. That is too neat. The more important fact in the source is not failure in the abstract, but who Trump chose to put in place: Pete Hegseth at Defense, Scott Bessent at Treasury, Tom Homan on immigration, with Kristi Noem and Corey Lewandowski circling the same machinery of cruelty and self-dealing. This is not an administration accidentally drifting into chaos. It is a personnel strategy built to produce damage.

The People Trump Empowered

The source names the actual operators: the officials holding executive power, the people deciding policy, the people carrying out the crackdown, the people turning government into a stage for grievance and enrichment. Schmidt’s insults are colorful, but the underlying point is concrete. These are not random failures of temperament. They are appointees selected because they fit the Trump system: loyalty over competence, aggression over legality, spectacle over governance.

That matters because responsibility sits with the man making the appointments and the party apparatus that continues to protect him. Trump is not being misled by subordinates. He is the central author of the structure.

Misdirection as a Governing Tool

The source also shows how political degradation gets narrated away. When Schmidt says Trump officials lie about immigration incidents and act with a “license to delude,” he is describing more than spin. He is describing an operating method: manufacture confusion, flood the zone with contempt, and force the public to argue over the lie instead of the abuse behind it.

That is the trap of calling this “incompetence” alone. Incompetence is often the alibi. The damage is real, but so is the intent to normalize it. A government full of “imbeciles and scumbags,” as Schmidt puts it, is still a governing coalition. The fact that it looks clownish does not make it harmless.

Democrats Still Are Not Acting Like an Opposition

Schmidt’s sharpest useful line is his complaint that Democrats are not doing enough to hold Trump and his officials accountable. He is right on the premise, even if his framing remains too passive. The problem is not merely that Democrats need to “react more harshly.” The problem is that they still too often behave as if outrage is a substitute for power.

Trump’s movement is sustained not just by voters, but by institutional cowardice around him. If Democrats hold democratic power at all, it has to be used as power: investigations, oversight, legal pressure, exposure of the money, exposure of the violence, refusal to let deliberate cruelty be filed under “controversy.” Anything less leaves the field to the people running the machinery.

The Real Pattern

The deeper pattern in this story is not that MAGA is messy. It is that authoritarian politics can be both vulgar and disciplined. It rewards frauds, brutes, and grifters because their usefulness is not competence but permission. Permission to lie. Permission to enrich. Permission to threaten. Permission to turn public office into a loyalty market.

That is why the collapse narrative is incomplete. What the source actually describes is a system that deforms government by design, then dares observers to mistake sabotage for incompetence. The scandal is not that Trump’s circle is full of trash. The scandal is that this is the operating principle.
