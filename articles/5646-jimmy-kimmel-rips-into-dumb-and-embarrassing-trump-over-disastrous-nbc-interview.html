Jimmy Kimmel rips into 'dumb and embarrassing' Trump over disastrous NBC interview

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Jimmy Kimmel has torn into Donald Trump after the president’s NBC interview, where the president refused to rule out a third term in office.

Talk show host Kimmel highlighted the many shortcomings of the president from the interview, including a comment on how the deaths of Renee Good and Alex Pretti were deemed “bad publicity” for the government by Trump. Speaking during the opening monologue of his talk show, Kimmel highlighted a handful of tough moments from the president in the sit down with NBC.

Kimmel said, “Trump believes that, ultimately, what we have here is a simple failure in communicating. That’s the real tragedy, the impact on publicity. Nobody ever talks about the tens of thousands of people we don’t murder at these protests.

“Trump said so many dumb and embarrassing things during the interview, he may have to sue himself for another $10billion dollars. The good news is we only have three more years of this left.”

A clip of Trump refusing to rule out being president in 2029 then played, where the president says it would be “less exciting” if people knew whether he would run for a third term.

Kimmel added, “I feel like I speak for the vast majority of Americans when I say we are ready for our lives to be so much less exciting. I think we would enjoy living in a country where we don’t all feel like being on the back of the bus from Speed morning, noon, and night.”

The family of Renee Good, who was shot and killed last month by an ICE agent, have since criticized the government. Her brother, Luke Ganger, said, “The deep distress our family feels because of (Renee’s) loss in such a violent and unnecessary way is complicated by feelings of disbelief, distress and desperation for change.”

“The completely surreal scenes taking place on the streets of Minneapolis are beyond explanation,” he added. “This is not just a bad day or a rough week or isolated incidents — these encounters with federal agents are changing the community and changing many lives, including ours, forever.”

Our Analysis:

The Mockery of Democracy: Trump’s Third Term Ambition and the Kimmel Critique

In a recent tirade that can only be described as both necessary and alarmingly humorous, Jimmy Kimmel has once again proven why the court jesters of our era wield the sharpest swords. His target? None other than the former President Donald Trump, following an NBC interview that can only be characterized as a circus act if the circus was on fire and the clowns were in charge.

Trump’s refusal to rule out a third term is not just a flirtation with autocracy; it’s a full-blown love affair with tyranny. The notion is as laughable as it is terrifying, demonstrating a blatant disregard for the U.S. Constitution and the very foundation of American democracy. But then again, expecting adherence to democratic norms from Trump is like expecting a fish to climb a tree.

The Casualties of Trump’s Ego: Renee Good and Alex Pretti

Kimmel’s spotlight on Trump’s comments regarding the deaths of Renee Good and Alex Pretti as “bad publicity” for the government is a stark reminder of the former president’s inability to see beyond the mirror. To reduce human life to mere PR inconvenience is not just cold; it’s inhumane. Yet, Trump’s insensitivity is as unsurprising as it is unforgivable. His presidency was a masterclass in placing optics over ethics, and Kimmel’s critique slices through this facade with the precision of a scalpel.

The Real Tragedy: A Failure in Communicating

Trump’s assertion, as highlighted by Kimmel, that the real tragedy lies in a “failure in communicating” the impact on publicity is a grotesque understatement. It’s not a failure in communication; it’s a failure in leadership, empathy, and basic human decency. Joking about the “tens of thousands of people we don’t murder at these protests” is not just poor taste; it’s a horrendous trivialization of the systemic violence and racial injustice plaguing America.

The Desire for Dullness

Kimmel’s yearning for a less “exciting” life underlines a profound truth. The constant chaos, the never-ending cycle of outrage, and the persistent threat to democratic norms under the Trump administration were exhausting. It’s a sentiment echoed by a vast majority of Americans who crave normalcy, stability, and a government that doesn’t resemble a reality TV show on the brink of cancellation.

A Community Changed Forever

The words of Luke Ganger, Renee Good’s brother, paint a harrowing picture of the real-world consequences of Trump’s governance. The “deep distress,” “disbelief,” and “desperation for change” felt by Good’s family is a microcosm of the nation’s collective trauma. The “completely surreal scenes” in Minneapolis and the encounters with federal agents are not isolated incidents but symptoms of a systemic disease that flourished under Trump’s watch.

Conclusion: The Jester’s Clarity

Jimmy Kimmel, in his role as the modern-day jester, has once again provided a clear-eyed view of the absurdity and danger of Trump’s presidency and the potential for a third term. His monologue is not just comedy; it’s a critical commentary on the state of American politics, a call to action, and a reminder of the power of satire to speak truth to power.

In the end, Kimmel’s critique is a scathing indictment of Trump’s tenure and a rallying cry for change. As the nation moves forward, it must not forget the lessons of the past four years. Democracy is fragile, and the cost of complacency is too high. The jesters among us are not just making jokes; they’re sounding the alarm. It’s time we listen.
