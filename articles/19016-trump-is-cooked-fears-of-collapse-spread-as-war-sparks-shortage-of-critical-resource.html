‘Trump is cooked’: Fears of ‘collapse’ spread as war sparks shortage of critical resource

The United States’ war against Iran has triggered a shortage of a critical resource, one so crucial to the economy that one expert predicted a “horrendous” and imminent “collapse,” while another said that President Donald Trump would bear the blame.

That resource is Helium, which plays a critical role in applications ranging from semiconductor manufacturing to medical equipment. Shortly after Trump first authorized strikes on Iran late last month, Qatar, which supplies a third of the world’s helium, was forced to halt production of the critical resource.

Helium is critical for the function of MRI machines, a technology widely used in medical facilities across the country, including at Walter Reed National Military Medical Center in Washington, D.C. where Trump received an MRI scan last year that raised questions surrounding his health. It’s also used in the space, defense and scientific research sectors.

Helium’s applications are so far spread that financial crises and geopolitical expert Tuomas Malinen issued a bleak prediction for what might be in store for the United States in the near future.

“I keep warning you that the collapse will be horrendous,” Malinen wrote in a social media post on X in response to reports of the growing helium shortage.

As for what – or who – will bear the blame for the fallout of a helium shortage, policy analyst Matt Stoller didn’t mince words.

“Stuff is starting to break. Trump is cooked,” Stoller wrote in a social media post on X. “His base will abandon him.”

And Sara McGee, a Democratic candidate for the Texas House of Representatives, offered her own take on the impact of Trump’s war against Iran.

“There are so many downstream elements to this war that people don’t even know about yet way beyond gas prices,” McGee wrote in a social media post on X. “Donald Trump chose to do this, and Congress chose to let him. Betrayal.”

Despite initiating the war, Trump is reportedly seeking an exit as global stability falters and “tremors” ripple through the world economy. Even so, such an off-ramp may not exist anymore, according to at least one former Trump security advisor, with Iran having vowed to keep fighting in response to the U.S.-Israeli attack

Our Analysis:

The Helium Crisis: A Closer Look at Accountability and Power Dynamics

The recent article detailing the helium shortage as a consequence of the United States’ war against Iran quickly points fingers at President Donald Trump for both the initiation of the conflict and the ensuing critical resource deficit. While on the surface, this allocation of blame may seem straightforward, a nuanced analysis reveals a more complex web of institutional power, decision-making, and potential misdirection of responsibility. Let’s dissect the layers to understand where accountability truly lies.

Who Holds the Power?

At the heart of this issue is the institutional power wielded by the President of the United States, Congress, and international suppliers and allies. President Trump, as the commander-in-chief of the U.S. military, indeed holds significant power in authorizing military actions. However, the power to declare war constitutionally resides with Congress. This distinction between military action and formal war declaration underscores a complex balance of power and responsibility.

Decision-Making and Outcomes

The decision to engage militarily with Iran, as the article notes, directly impacted Qatar’s ability to supply helium, highlighting a clear line from decision to outcome. Trump’s authorization of strikes is presented as the precipitating action for the helium shortage, a critical link in this chain of causality. Yet, the broader geopolitical and economic systems that facilitate or constrain access to resources like helium also play a crucial role, suggesting that while the President’s decisions are pivotal, they are not the sole factors at play.

Misdirected Responsibility?

Critiques from experts and political figures in the article focus sharply on Trump, with policy analyst Matt Stoller and Democratic candidate Sara McGee attributing the fallout of the helium shortage and broader war consequences directly to him. While presidential decisions should rightly be scrutinized, the framing here risks oversimplifying the narrative. It implies a level of unilateral control by Trump that overlooks the roles of Congress, international partners, and market dynamics in the crisis. This framing could mislead readers into attributing full responsibility to an individual rather than acknowledging the systemic and procedural complexities involved.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating

The article’s approach borders on scapegoating by concentrating blame on Trump without equal examination of Congress’s role or the broader geopolitical context. While Trump’s actions are central to the narrative of the helium shortage, the lack of critical analysis regarding Congress’s acquiescence or the strategic calculations of Qatar and other nations in halting helium production reveals a gap in the article’s critique. This oversight embodies a false equivalence by equating the decision-making power and responsibility of one individual with the collective responsibility of national and international actors.

Conclusion

The helium crisis, precipitated by the U.S. conflict with Iran, undeniably showcases the significant impact of presidential decisions on global resources and stability. However, a comprehensive critique must consider the multifaceted power dynamics and decision-making processes that contribute to such outcomes. While President Trump’s actions are undoubtedly at the forefront of this crisis, the narrative that excludes the roles of Congress, international allies, and systemic factors in both the conflict and its economic repercussions fails to capture the full spectrum of accountability. Misplaced blame not only obscures the reality of institutional power but also undermines the pursuit of nuanced solutions to complex global issues.
