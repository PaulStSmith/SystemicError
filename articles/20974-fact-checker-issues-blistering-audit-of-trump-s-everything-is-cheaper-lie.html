Fact-checker issues blistering audit of Trump's 'everything is cheaper' lie

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20263 min read

Trump Didn’t Fix Prices. He Repackaged Them.

The Egg Is the Prop

Donald Trump’s latest economic boast is a familiar fraud: pick one visible price, declare victory, and hope the public won’t look at the rest of the receipt. The source material makes the problem plain. CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale audited Trump’s claim that “everything” is cheaper and found the opposite: prices have risen across far more categories than they have fallen.

Eggs are the showpiece because they are easy to talk about and easy to remember. That is exactly why they are useful to a politician trying to turn inflation into a personal success story. But a single grocery item is not an economy, and a lucky dip in one category is not leadership.

Power Made the Claim

The institutional power here sits with Trump. He is not a commentator describing prices from the outside. He is the president claiming credit for outcomes he did not produce and obscuring the policy environment he controls. Dale’s reporting strips away the performance: overall prices are up 4.3 percent since Trump took office, with food up 4.2 percent, housing up 4.6 percent, energy up 13.1 percent, medical services up 4.9 percent, clothing up 4.5 percent, and childcare up 3.8 percent.

That matters because power is not just about causing harm. It is also about narrating harm away. Trump is doing what strongman politics always does: seize the microphone, rewrite the ledger, and demand that people treat repetition as evidence.

The Misdirection

The article’s core trick is not subtle. It presents eggs as proof of general recovery when Dale and Matt Stiles found the opposite across 332 products and categories: about five times as many went up as went down. Only about one-sixth declined at all, and eggs were the standout exception.

That is not a broad improvement. It is selective storytelling built on a narrow anomaly. The source also notes that eggs had been driven by bird flu, with tens of millions of chickens dying. In other words, the price movement Trump wants to claim is the product of a separate biological shock, not a presidential miracle. The confusion is deliberate: take a rare dip, strip it of context, and sell it as proof that everything is under control.

The Real Cost

The deeper story is not about eggs. It is about the material pressure building across ordinary life while the White House markets fantasy. Food is still up. Housing is still up. Energy is up sharply. Childcare is more expensive. Medical services are more expensive. That is the actual economy Americans live in, not the cartoon version Trump uses to flatter himself.

The source also points to tariffs and Trump’s Iran war as part of the surrounding economic strain. Whether every price movement can be pinned to one cause is not the point here. The point is that the administration is governing inside a stack of consequences and then pretending the stack does not exist. That is not confusion. It is evasion with a press office.

The Pattern

This is the old authoritarian habit in a fresh grocery-store costume: take a system-level failure, isolate a symbol, and declare the system healthy because the symbol moved in a convenient direction. It depends on public exhaustion, media asymmetry, and the assumption that people will not check the data.

They should not have to become economists to notice the con. The fact-check does that work already. Far more prices are up than down. Trump did not “save the economy.” He is trying to dominate the story about an economy he cannot honestly defend.

The Broader Lesson

The political pattern is simple: when governing produces pain, reactionary power does not apologize or adapt. It manufactures a simpler lie and asks the public to live inside it. That lie works best when it can point to one cheap anecdote and hide the larger ledger of deprivation, inflation, and institutional irresponsibility behind it.
