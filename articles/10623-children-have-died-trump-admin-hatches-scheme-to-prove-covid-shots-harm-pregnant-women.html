'Children have died!' Trump admin hatches scheme to prove Covid shots harm pregnant women

President Donald Trump's administration has hatched a new scheme to determine whether the Covid-19 vaccine harms pregnant women, according to a new report.

The Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday that top officials under Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. are working to "waive privacy protections" around data related to the Covid vaccine, citing "people familiar with the matter." The new scheme was revealed just days after Kennedy's Senate hearing, where he repeated falsehoods about vaccines.

"The push to collect the data is an extension of a broader effort," the report states. "Last week, Makary said he is preparing a report on child deaths he said were caused by the Covid vaccines."

Food and Drug Administration Commissioner Marty Makary and vaccines chief Vinay Prasad are leading what Makary described to CNN as a "proper investigation."

“There have been children who have died from the Covid vaccine. We’re doing a proper investigation,” Makary told CNN. “We think the public deserves to have that information. It wasn’t released in the last administration and it should have been.”

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of the Latest Trump Administration Antics on COVID-19 Vaccine Data

In a move that can only be described as both breathtakingly irresponsible and alarmingly intrusive, the Trump administration appears to have concocted yet another controversial scheme surrounding the COVID-19 vaccine—this time, with a focus on pregnant women. According to a report by The Wall Street Journal, officials under Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. are pushing to waive privacy protections around vaccine-related data. This revelation comes hot on the heels of Kennedy's Senate hearing, where, in a display of staggering misinformation, he reiterated various vaccine falsehoods.

The Scheme Unveiled

The Wall Street Journal's uncovering of this scheme is particularly troubling, not least because of the sensitive nature of the data in question. The purported goal is to determine if the COVID-19 vaccine poses any harm to pregnant women—a demographic already fraught with anxieties about the vaccine, thanks to a relentless stream of misinformation. Yet, the method—to waive privacy protections—is not just ethically questionable; it's a gross invasion of privacy under the guise of public health.

Misinformation Masquerading as Concern

Kennedy's performance at the Senate hearing, coupled with this latest endeavor, paints a clear picture of an administration more interested in fueling vaccine hesitancy than in safeguarding public health. The rhetoric used by Kennedy and his officials, including Food and Drug Administration Commissioner Marty Makary and vaccines chief Vinay Prasad, is particularly insidious. Makary's comments to CNN about children dying from the COVID vaccine and the promise of a "proper investigation" are not just misleading but dangerous. These statements not only lack a solid foundation in the vast body of scientific evidence supporting the vaccine's safety and efficacy but also serve to undermine public trust in these life-saving measures.

A Dubious Investigation

The mention of Makary preparing a report on child deaths allegedly caused by the COVID-19 vaccines adds another layer of concern. The phrasing—"he said were caused by"—betrays a predetermined conclusion rather than an objective investigation. It's a classic case of putting the cart before the horse, with Makary seemingly more interested in validating his own narrative than in conducting a rigorous, unbiased inquiry. The claim that such information "wasn’t released in the last administration and it should have been" is a thinly veiled attempt at deflecting responsibility and fostering mistrust in both the vaccine and the previous administration's handling of the pandemic.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Path

This latest scheme by the Trump administration is not just a breach of privacy; it's a calculated assault on public confidence in vaccines at a time when such confidence is crucial to overcoming the pandemic. The administration's willingness to compromise the privacy of pregnant women, under the guise of protecting them, is emblematic of a broader pattern of misinformation and malfeasance. As with so many of their endeavors, the purported concern for public health is overshadowed by a clear agenda to undermine science, sow division, and prioritize political gain over the well-being of the populace.

In sum, this initiative is not a genuine effort to ensure the safety of the vaccine for pregnant women. Instead, it's a dangerous ploy that threatens to erode the already fragile trust in public health measures, with potentially catastrophic consequences. It's a stark reminder of the importance of critically evaluating such claims and the motivations behind them, and the need for vigilance in protecting both our privacy rights and the integrity of public health discourse.
