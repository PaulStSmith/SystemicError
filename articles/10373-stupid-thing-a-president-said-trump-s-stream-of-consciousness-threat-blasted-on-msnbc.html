'Stupid thing a president said': Trump's 'stream of consciousness' threat blasted on MSNBC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analysis: The Unraveling of Rational Discourse

Donald Trump (MSNBC screenshot)

Any hope that Donald Trump might try to calm the waters as the finger-pointing continues after the murder of far-right conservative gadfly Charlie Kirk was blown to pieces on Tuesday as the president headed off to his trip to London.

On Wednesday morning, the co-hosts of MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” showed a clip of the president’s confrontation with Jonathan Karl of ABC, where the president threatened the journalist with being investigated by the DOJ and told him he had “hate" in his heart.

That led co-host Joe Scarborough to laugh, at first, and point out, “So really, there's so much to sort through there. First of all, Donald Trump goes from sort of this stream of consciousness situation where he is –– goes from sort of WWF fighter mode to Mar-a-Lago concierge.”

Mocking Trump acting like a concierge telling a guest, “You should take your beautiful wife down —,” he switched gears and exclaimed, “He knows Jon [Karl]. He's the first person who's ever said Jon Karl's heart is filled with hatred. He knows it's not. He knows Jon Karl and likes Jon Karl.”

“But this is what happens in a day's time,” he added. “You have an attorney general who says, 'I'm going after people who say things that we don't like, that we call hate speech' when prodded along by the interviewer saying, ‘Hey, when do we start putting handcuffs on people for saying things that they think are hatred?'"

“And you, you see how quickly this is, this is the most extreme version of what law professors talked about when they warned us of a slippery slope that this was a slip-and slide like going down from like Mount Kilimanjaro,” he continued. “It went very quickly from a stupid thing an attorney general said to a stupid thing that a president said.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Unraveling of Rational Discourse

In an article detailing a recent episode following the murder of far-right conservative figure Charlie Kirk, we're given a front-row seat to the circus that is current American political discourse, helmed by none other than former President Donald Trump. The narrative, soaked in the absurdity of Trump’s responses and the reactions it incites, paints a vivid picture of a political landscape teetering on the brink of farce.

The Spectacle of Deflection

Trump's strategy, if one can grace it with such a term, is reminiscent of a magician's sleight of hand. As he heads off to London, any faint hope that he might adopt a tone of reconciliation evaporates. Instead, Trump escalates tensions, threatening journalist Jonathan Karl with an investigation by the Department of Justice and accusing him of harboring "hate" in his heart. This maneuver is classic Trump: divert, deflect, and attack. It's a tactic designed not to clarify his stance or to contribute to any semblance of productive dialogue but to muddy the waters further and rally his base around a perceived enemy.

The Misuse of Power

The threat to use the DOJ as a weapon against a journalist for alleged "hate" speech is not just troubling; it's a glaring misuse of presidential power. It's a stark reminder of how Trump views the levers of government: not as mechanisms for justice or to serve the public good, but as tools to be wielded against his adversaries. This is not just undemocratic; it's dangerously authoritarian. The implications of such a stance are chilling and serve as a stark warning of the ease with which democratic norms can be eroded.

The Slippery Slope Argument

Joe Scarborough’s commentary on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” captures the essence of the slippery slope the nation finds itself on. What begins as an absurd comment from the attorney general quickly snowballs into an equally ludicrous statement from the president. This "slip-and-slide" from an already questionable standpoint to outright authoritarian threats is alarmingly rapid. It underscores the fragile state of American democracy and the perilous path it's on when the checks and balances intended to prevent such abuses are ignored or undermined.

The Laughter That Conceals Despair

Scarborough's initial laughter in response to Trump's antics is telling. It’s the kind of incredulous reaction that has become all too common in the face of the Trump administration's continual assault on reason and decency. Yet, beneath the mockery and disbelief lies a sobering truth: this is not merely a comedic sideshow. It's a distressing spectacle of power being wielded irresponsibly and vindictively at the highest levels of government.

Conclusion: The Farce and the Fury

As we dissect the events and rhetoric surrounding the murder of Charlie Kirk and Trump's subsequent reactions, it's crucial to recognize the broader implications. Trump's behavior and the normalization of such discourse in the political arena are not just about one man's ego or personal vendettas. They're symptomatic of a deeper, systemic malaise that threatens the very foundations of democracy. The farce masks a fury; a fury against rational discourse, against democratic norms, and against the principle of accountability. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant and critical, lest the slide into authoritarianism becomes a freefall.
