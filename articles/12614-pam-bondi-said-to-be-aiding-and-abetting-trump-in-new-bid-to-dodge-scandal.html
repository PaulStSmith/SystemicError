Pam Bondi said to be 'aiding and abetting Trump' in new bid to 'dodge' scandal

Donald Trump's plan to publicize court documents in the Jeffrey Epstein case is actually a calculated "strategy" to avoid the scandal, and Pam Bondi is "aiding and abetting" him, according to a former federal prosecutor.

Trump recently announced he would direct his attorney general to release all "pertinent" grand jury files related to Epstein's case, but the problem is that option is not within Trump's power, but with the courts. Bondi immediately said she would ask the court for such a release.

Some say this was an attempt to finally provide transparency to the MAGA base, which is riddled with conspiracy theories about Epstein, but ex-prosecutor Joyce Vance says it's a calculated "strategy" to ride out the storm and "dodge" the scandal.

Vance asked, "Why the shift?"

"Release of the grand jury material, or as Trump would have it, only the bits that are 'pertinent,' isn’t automatic. Federal prosecutors need a court order to disclose grand jury testimony; they can’t just do it on their own," she wrote, adding that there are specific categories under which the request might fit. "Even if the government can shoehorn its request into one of these categories or another provision of the rule, it will take time. There may be objections from Ghislaine Maxwell, whose case is still on appeal. And, DOJ would have to make a 'strong showing of particularized need' that 'outweighs the public interest in secrecy' in order for the release to be ordered."

Vance went as far as to say this is all part of the plan.

"Grand jury secrecy is designed to protect the integrity of the investigation, prevent witnesses from being intimidated, and safeguard the reputations of those not indicted. To get grand jury material disclosed, a party has to demonstrate a specific need for the information that outweighs the public interest in maintaining secrecy. So why land on an option that is so limited and so fraught? All of the possible impediments to releasing grand jury material may well be the point for Trump. He can say, yet again, that he tried and the courts stood in his way," she wrote. "Even a delay, while lawyers brief the matter and a judge schedules a hearing, could work in Trump’s favor if the fickle public loses interest in the issue and moves on, and he lives to fight another day, yet again. That could explain this strategy."

Vance adds that, "A lot of ink has been spilled on how unfit to serve Pam Bondi is. But it was not enough to convince the Senate to reject her nomination."

"And now we pay the price for it, with an attorney general who is incapable of acting independently of the president to do the right thing instead of the expedient thing. Bondi is giving Trump cover so he can try to ride this dispute out," she added. "She acceded to his order that she ask the court to release what is just a small fraction of the material from the Epstein investigation, only testimony of witnesses prosecutors brought before the grand jury when they sought their indictment. And she does it knowing that it’s unlikely to succeed or could take some time."

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump's Epstein Document Drama: Transparency or Tactic?

In the latest twist of political theater, Donald Trump has announced intentions to publicize court documents related to the Jeffrey Epstein case, a move that has been dissected as less about shedding light and more about dodging shadows. Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance has called out this maneuver as a "calculated strategy" to sidestep scandal, with Pam Bondi, accused of being complicit in this scheme, right at the heart of the controversy.

The Illusion of Power

Trump's declaration to direct his attorney general to release all "pertinent" grand jury files from the Epstein case strikes as a bold move until one realizes it's a play without a stage. The power to release such documents does not lie with Trump or his attorney general but with the courts. This fundamental misunderstanding (or deliberate obfuscation) of how legal procedures work is alarming but unsurprising. Bondi's quick jump to support Trump's call, promising to ask the court for the release, adds another layer of complexity—or perhaps, complicity—to the narrative.

A Strategy of Distraction

The essence of Vance's argument is that this move is a smokescreen. It's a tactic designed to appease a base riddled with conspiracy theories while knowing full well the hurdles in actually achieving such a release. Grand jury material is protected with good reason: to preserve the integrity of investigations, protect witnesses, and safeguard the reputations of those not indicted. For Trump to suggest a selective release of these documents, then, reeks of a strategy not to enlighten the public but to distract and delay.

Why It's Problematic

The request to release grand jury material is a legal minefield that requires demonstrating a "particularized need" that outweighs the public interest in maintaining secrecy. Given these requirements, the likelihood of Trump's attempt succeeding is slim—but that might be the point. Vance suggests that even the delay caused by the legal wrangling could benefit Trump, allowing public interest to wane and letting him "live to fight another day."

Bondi's Role: Enabler or Enforcer?

Pam Bondi's willingness to follow Trump's directive, knowing the improbability of success, paints her as less an independent attorney general and more an enabler of Trump's tactics. Her actions raise questions about the independence of her office and her ability to act in the public interest rather than serving the whims of political allies. The criticism isn't new—doubts about her fitness for office have circulated before, but they've done little to sway her trajectory or influence.

Conclusion: Transparency as a Tool of Obfuscation

Trump and Bondi's actions, as outlined by Joyce Vance, reveal a calculated attempt to use the guise of transparency as a strategic tool of obfuscation. By promising the release of documents he likely knows cannot be legally disclosed, Trump is playing a game of smoke and mirrors designed to distract and deflect. This maneuver is not about shedding light on the Epstein case but about managing a political narrative and avoiding scandal. It reveals a cynical exploitation of legal processes and a disregard for the principles of justice and transparency. In the end, the public is left navigating a maze of half-truths and misdirection, a tactic that has become all too familiar in this administration's playbook.
