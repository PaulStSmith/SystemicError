'MAGA's mad': Far-right activists said to be upset 'Trump made them look stupid'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump supporters and protesters gather outside a campaign rally (and accompanying anti-Trump protest) for President Trump and US Senate candidate Martha McSally. (Eric Rosenwald / Shutterstock.com)

Donald Trump and his administration really stepped in it when it comes to their recent handling of a MAGA conspiracy theory, a conservative strategist said.

Conservative anti-Trump activist Rick Wilson, who co-founded the Lincoln Project and hosts the group's podcast, wrote Friday about the explosion of the Jeffrey Epstein conspiracy theory. As Wilson calls it, "the Holy Grail of MAGA and Q-addled rage, the long-promised Rosetta Stone that would finally expose the deep state, the Clintons, Tom Hanks, your local kindergarten teacher, and maybe even the ghost of Antonin Scalia."

When Trump and his attorney general, Pam Bondi, announced that there was no Epstein client list, the was a "meltdown," Wilson said.

"This wasn’t just a betrayal. This was heresy. This was J.D. Vance getting to the Vatican and realizing the Pope is playing beer pong with Beelzebub and Hunter Biden. MAGAworld didn’t just expect the Epstein Revelations would destroy every Democrat, journalist, and celebrity in their pantheon of petty hatreds: they demanded it, had built their identity around it. And now? Trump torched the stage" and told them to move on, according to the ex-GOP strategist. "And oh, they’re moving on all right…right into a frothing civil war with each other."

Wilson said Bondi's role stood out.

"The best part of a cult unraveling is the backbiting. MAGA world didn’t even pause at first to blame the Deep State™. No, they went straight for the nearest traitor they could find: Pam Bondi," Wilson wrote. "Posobiec called her a liar. Loomer demanded her resignation. Fox News hosts started asking the kinds of questions they usually reserve for Hillary Clinton’s liver enzyme panels. Jesse Watters, with the same gravitas he brings to rating women’s Instagram bios, declared the whole thing 'reeking.'"

According to the activist, Trump offered "no apology. No explanation. Just more hot air and a wet fart of misdirection. Because that’s what he does: breaks the toys and walks away like a toddler with diplomatic immunity."

"But MAGA’s mad. Not entirely because they didn’t get what they wanted, but also because Trump made them look stupid. And you can lead a cult to Kool-Aid, but you can’t tell them the flavor was just LaCroix," he added.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Meltdown: A Spectacle of Betrayal and Infighting

In a riveting display of political theater that would make even the most seasoned dramatist blush, Donald Trump and his administration have managed to ignite a firestorm within their own ranks over the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein conspiracy theory. Rick Wilson, a conservative strategist turned anti-Trump activist, offers a scathing critique of this debacle, painting a picture of betrayal, heresy, and a MAGA movement at war with itself.

The Holy Grail of Conspiracy Theories: Wilson doesn't hold back in his description of the Epstein conspiracy as the MAGA and QAnon movements' most coveted prize. A theory so outlandish, it implicates everyone from the Clintons to Tom Hanks, making it the perfect fodder for those inclined towards the fantastical narratives of deep state machinations. The anticipation for a client list was palpable, with supporters convinced it would be their ultimate weapon against perceived enemies.

The Great Letdown: However, the revelation—or lack thereof—by Trump and his Attorney General, Pam Bondi, that no such client list existed, served as a cold shower to the inflamed expectations of MAGA followers. Wilson likens the disappointment to a religious betrayal, evoking imagery of a disillusioned follower finding the Pope in league with the devil. The reaction? A frenzied scramble to assign blame, with Bondi becoming the unfortunate scapegoat in the eyes of prominent MAGA personalities and their followers, showcasing the movement's propensity for cannibalistic infighting when faced with disillusionment.

Bondi: The Scapegoat of the Hour: The backlash against Pam Bondi is depicted with a certain glee by Wilson, who seems to relish the "backbiting" that follows the collapse of a cult-like group's narrative. The rapid shift from blaming the omnipresent Deep State to attacking one of their own highlights a fundamental characteristic of the MAGA movement: the need for a villain, even if it means turning on their allies.

Trump’s Role: The Toddler in Chief: Wilson's portrayal of Trump in this saga is unapologetically contemptuous, likening him to a toddler wreaking havoc without a second thought. Trump's response, characterized by a lack of apology or explanation, is seen as a hallmark of his leadership style—leaving chaos in his wake and moving on without concern for the fallout.

The Bitter Aftertaste: Perhaps the most stinging observation made by Wilson is the notion that MAGA supporters are not just angry over the failure to procure the damning evidence they so desperately sought. Their fury is compounded by the realization that they were led to look foolish, duped by their own leader. Wilson's closing analogy, comparing the MAGA movement to cult followers disillusioned by the revelation that their Kool-Aid was nothing more than flavorless LaCroix, encapsulates the blend of betrayal, embarrassment, and internal conflict that defines this episode.

In summary, this article serves as a profound commentary on the fragility of populist movements built on conspiracy theories and the volatile nature of their loyalty. Wilson’s analysis, while biting and filled with contempt, underscores a crucial lesson: when reality fails to align with fervently held beliefs, the fallout can be both vicious and self-destructive. The MAGA movement's reaction to the Epstein list saga illustrates the dangerous consequences of entrusting one's political and moral compass to figures and narratives that promise much but deliver little beyond discord and disillusionment.
