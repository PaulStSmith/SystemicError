Carping Trump gets a dose of comeuppance after election shocker

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

The Revolt Is Not Against “Socialism.” It’s Against Theft.

Abdul El-Sayed’s win in Michigan is being used as a panic trigger by Republicans and a convenient spectacle for pundits, but the source material makes the real conflict plain: voters are not lunging toward utopia or ideology-for-its-own-sake. They are rejecting an economy and political order built to protect the rich, entrench corruption, and punish everyone else. The article’s own language is a blunt indictment of oligarchy, not a neutral report on a leftward mood.

Who Actually Runs the System

The people with real institutional power are not the voters the commentary is mocking. They are the billionaire class, the CEOs, the court majority that kept widening the legal rights of money, and the political operators who turned public government into a private service channel. The source names the chain clearly: Lewis Powell’s think-tank-and-media strategy, the Bellotti decision, Citizens United, and the flood of outside money that now shapes elections at every level.

That is the power structure. Not “the economy.” Not “the market.” Specific people and institutions made specific decisions that shifted authority upward and outward from citizens.

The Blame Game Is the Point

What the source gets right is that this system survives through misdirection. Republicans scream “communism” when people demand healthcare, schools, housing, and a functioning democracy. Corporate media then flattens the whole fight into a personality contest or a culture-war tantrum. That is not analysis. It is cover for the same class of actors who caused the damage in the first place.

The article also takes a swipe at “corporate Democrats,” and that criticism matters only if it sharpens the democratic coalition instead of dulling it. Democrats are not the symmetrical threat here. But when Democratic elites accept donor discipline, normalize wealth concentration, or speak in managerial fog instead of material terms, they help preserve the very order progressive voters are trying to break.

The Real Issue Is Material Power

The demands listed in the source are not radical abstractions. They are ordinary democratic expectations: healthcare that does not bankrupt families, education that is not a scam, voting that is actually accessible, a tax code that does not privilege billionaires, and an economy where working people can still buy a home and retire.

That this now gets treated as extremism says more about the ruling class than the electorate. The article is right to frame billionaire hoarding as political pathology. When a tiny fraction of the country can buy elections, dominate courts, and write the rules of taxation, the problem is not public anger. The problem is that theft has been normalized as competence.

ICE Is Not Broken. It Is Doing Its Job.

The immigration section is especially clear-eyed: masked agents, children held in camps, civil violations treated like criminal threats, and racialized arrests are not accidents or isolated misconduct. They are the operational logic of a coercive system built to terrify migrants and discipline everyone else.

That is why reform language is too small. Train them better is a dodge. Oversight is a delay tactic. The democratic position is abolition: abolish ICE and replace the entire enforcement regime that depends on secrecy, cruelty, and state violence.

What the Win Actually Means

El-Sayed’s victory matters because it shows that voters can still read the class map even when elites try to blur it. They know who has been taking from them for 45 years, and they are not impressed by red-baiting or elite hand-wringing. The source is strongest when it treats this as a struggle over power, not branding.

The larger pattern is simple: when people demand a government that serves them, the oligarchy calls it extremism. That inversion is the modern American scam. The fight is not over whether democracy is too ambitious. It is over whether money keeps getting to veto it.
