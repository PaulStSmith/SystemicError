'Trump does not like this': Expert sees president in tough spot after Cabinet member's act

President Donald Trump is not happy with the recent firing of Centers for Disease Control Director Susan Monarez but came to her ouster’s defense Monday out of a sense of debt to one of his top cabinet members, political commentator and journalist Jonah Goldberg said on Tuesday.

Monarez, a Trump appointee, was removed from her position last week by Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. — reportedly after she refused to do something “illegal” — sparking a walkout of hundreds of CDC staffers in protest. And on Monday, Trump appeared to come to RFK Jr.’s defense in a lengthy social media post.

But behind the scenes, Trump isn’t pleased with the chaos at the CDC brought on by RFK Jr., but feels compelled to come to his defense due to a perceived debt of gratitude, Goldberg suspected.

“It's beyond vandalism what [RFK Jr.] is trying to do because I think he has bought into crackpottery at a very high level, and the problem is that Donald Trump feels like he owes RFK Jr. in ways he doesn't owe other Cabinet secretaries,” Goldberg said.

“It was one of the only times in the campaign that Trump's numbers actually went up, when RFK Jr. endorsed, and that's, I think, the background of that Truth Social post, that Trump does not like this. It's very rare [that] I agree with Truth Social posts because they're basically wacky Japanese game-show weirdness.”

For years, RFK Jr. has floated thoroughly debunked conspiracy theories on vaccines, including that they cause autism. His delving into conspiracy theories on vaccines often clashes with one of Trump’s defining achievements during his first term, Operation Warp Speed, the policy initiative that saw the rapid development of the COVID-19 vaccines that Trump regularly touts as one of his signature accomplishments.

And, while Goldberg would go on to say he had been describing RFK Jr. as “a crackpot crank for 25 years,” the HHS secretary brought with him to the MAGA movement his own base of support, one that Goldberg suspects Trump is well aware of.

Our Analysis:

The Tangled Web of Politics and Pseudoscience: Trump, RFK Jr., and the CDC Debacle

In a twist that reads like a script from a political thriller yet sadly unfolds in the realm of reality, the recent shakeup at the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) under the Trump administration spotlights the chaotic interplay between loyalty, pseudoscience, and the raw exercise of power. At the center of this storm are President Donald Trump, Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and the ousted CDC Director Susan Monarez.

The Unlikely Alliance of Trump and RFK Jr.

The allegiance between Trump and RFK Jr. is as bewildering as it is concerning. This partnership seemingly thrives on a mutual benefit scheme, overshadowed by the dubious honor of RFK Jr.'s endorsement boosting Trump's campaign numbers. Political commentator Jonah Goldberg nails it when he underscores the peculiar sensation of Trump's numbers swelling upon RFK Jr.'s endorsement, a rare phenomenon that hints at the unconventional underpinnings of Trump's political strategies.

The Dismissal of Susan Monarez: A Symptom of a Greater Malaise

Susan Monarez's firing by RFK Jr. — allegedly for her refusal to engage in illegal activities — and the subsequent protest walkout by hundreds of CDC staffers is not just a sign of internal discord but a glaring red flag on the administration's governance style. It reeks of an authoritarian approach to leadership, where dissent is not tolerated, and legality is a negotiable concept. The specifics of the "illegal" requests remain shrouded in mystery, a deliberate obfuscation that does nothing but fan the flames of speculation and distrust.

RFK Jr.'s Controversial Stance on Vaccines

RFK Jr.'s flirtation with thoroughly debunked vaccine conspiracy theories starkly contrasts with one of the Trump administration's crowning achievements: Operation Warp Speed. This initiative, which expedited the development of COVID-19 vaccines, stands as a testament to scientific achievement and is regularly heralded by Trump as a pinnacle of his tenure. The irony is palpable — on one hand, Trump celebrates a landmark scientific endeavor, while on the other, he shields and promotes a cabinet member known for espousing anti-vaccine falsehoods.

The Political Calculus of Trump's Support

Trump's public defense of RFK Jr., despite private misgivings, is a calculated move rooted in a sense of indebtedness and political strategy rather than principle. It underscores a Machiavellian landscape where alliances are forged not on shared values but on mutual benefits and where loyalty is prized above competence or legality. Goldberg's assertion that Trump's backing is driven by a perceived debt rather than genuine support for RFK Jr.'s actions or beliefs unveils the transactional nature of political endorsements and their disproportionate impact on leadership decisions.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Leadership and Accountability

The unfolding saga at the CDC, underpinned by the controversial firing of Susan Monarez and the dubious alliance between Trump and RFK Jr., serves as a stark reminder of the dangers posed by intertwining pseudoscience with political power. It also raises pressing questions about leadership accountability, the ethical responsibilities of those in power, and the long-term ramifications of sacrificing scientific integrity on the altar of political expediency.

In an era where truth and science are increasingly under siege, the events surrounding the CDC's recent upheaval are not just a cautionary tale but a clarion call for vigilance, demanding a reevaluation of the criteria by which we judge and uphold our leaders.
