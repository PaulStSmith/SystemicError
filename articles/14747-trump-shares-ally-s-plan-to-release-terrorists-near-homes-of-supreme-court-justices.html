Trump shares ally's plan to 'release terrorists near homes of Supreme Court justices'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Depths of Desperation: Trump, MAGA, and the Supreme Court

Official White House photo by Andrea Hanks

Donald Trump has been lashing out at the Supreme Court since it handed him a loss on the issue of immigration, and on Saturday he went as far as to distribute a MAGA lawyer's plan to "release 'terrorists' near the homes of Supreme Court justices."

Former GOP staffer Mike Davis has made headlines for his social media comments in the past, and was rumored to be on Trump's list for attorney general. Recently, he posted a plan to get back at the Supreme Court justices for not ruling in line with MAGA.

"The Supreme Court still has an illegal injunction on the President of the United States, preventing him from commanding military operations to expel these foreign terrorists," Davis wrote. "The President should house these terrorists near the Chevy Chase Country Club, with daytime release."

Chevy Chase Club is "an elite country club that counts Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. among its members," the Washington Post reports.

Trump "re-Truthed" that remark on Saturday.

This led former Obama speechwriter Jon Favreau to say, "The President re-posts a suggestion from an adviser that he release 'terrorists' near the homes of Supreme Court justices who’ve merely ruled that the government can’t send people to a foreign gulag without due process."

Davis weighed in on Favreau's statement, writing, "Yes."

"We should send these 'Maryland fathers' where they will feel safe and protected: Wealthy white liberal enclaves, like Chevy Chase and Martha’s Vineyard," he added. "Instead of working-class minority neighborhoods, like Aurora. Then let’s see how much 'due process' you liberals want."

Analyzing the Depths of Desperation: Trump, MAGA, and the Supreme Court

In a recent display of unhinged political strategy, Donald Trump, alongside MAGA lawyer Mike Davis, has stooped to new lows in an attempt to retaliate against the Supreme Court. This comes after the court delivered a decision on immigration that did not align with Trump's agenda. The plan, as audacious as it is reprehensible, involves "releasing 'terrorists' near the homes of Supreme Court justices." This proposal isn't just a breach of ethical conduct; it's a glaring testimony to the lengths to which Trump and his allies are willing to go in their crusade against the judiciary.

Trump's Validation of Vengeance

Trump's endorsement of Davis's plan by re-Truthing the remark on social media is not just a tacit approval but a loud proclamation of his vindictive intentions. It's a move that reeks of desperation and disregard for the rule of law, pushing the boundaries of political retribution into the realm of personal vendetta. The target? An "elite country club" where Chief Justice John G. Roberts Jr. is a member, a detail that's as petty as it is telling of Trump's modus operandi.

The MAGA Lawyer's Menacing Blueprint

Mike Davis, a former GOP staffer and a figure who has lingered on the fringes of Trump's legal sphere, has emerged with a plan that is as dangerous as it is delusional. His proposition to house "foreign terrorists" near the Chevy Chase Country Club under a "daytime release" program is fraught with legal and ethical red flags. It's a stark reminder of MAGA's willingness to compromise national security and community safety for political gains.

The Fallacy of "Due Process" According to MAGA

Davis's comments, especially his cynical suggestion to re-locate these so-called "Maryland fathers" to "wealthy white liberal enclaves" instead of "working-class minority neighborhoods," unveils a disturbing strategy to weaponize human lives for political spectacle. The irony in his challenge to liberals about wanting "due process" is palpable and profoundly hypocritical, considering the MAGA camp's evident disdain for the very foundation of due process when it doesn't serve their agenda.

The Dangerous Precedent

Trump and Davis are not just attacking the Supreme Court; they are undermining the very pillars of American democracy and justice. Such tactics threaten to erode trust in the judiciary and fuel divisions within the country. The suggestion to use human lives as pawns in a political vendetta is not only morally bankrupt but sets a dangerous precedent for political discourse in the United States.
