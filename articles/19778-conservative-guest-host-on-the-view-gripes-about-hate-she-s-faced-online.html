Conservative guest host on The View gripes about hate she's faced online

“The View” co-host Sunny Hostin and reality TV personality Savannah Crisley (Photo: Screen capture)

Savannah Crisley joined the co-hosts of “The View” on Friday at the “Hot Topics” table. She has been invited to the show to fill in for Republican Alyssa Farah Griffin, who is on maternity leave.

Crisley’s parents are the reality television personalities and former felons who were jailed after being found guilty of federal bank fraud and tax evasion. It was thanks to her that her parents were able to get a pardon from President Donald Trump.

“I want to thank you all for giving me a voice and allowing me to sit at the table and believe totally differently, but have conversations that are hard and respectful,” Crisley said.

During a conversation about the social media trial in which Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg was called to testify, Crisley lamented that her platforms have been particularly awful, allowing trolls to say whatever they want on her page.

“As an adult sitting here on ‘The View’ this week, the amount of hateful messages I have gotten on—‘fat,’ ‘MAGA supporter’... it affects me at almost 30 years old,” she whined about the world of social media bullying she’s faced.

Co-host Joy Behar told Crisley not to read the comments.

“I’m trying not to,” she said.

“You’re addicted to reading them, aren’t you?” Behar quipped.

Chrisley quipped, “Next topic.”

The Daily Beast reported that having Crisley on the show was a kind of Hail Mary to the Trump administration after reports claimed the FCC was scrutinizing the show more amid censorship threats. Earlier this week, “Late Show” host Stephen Colbert was told by CBS lawyers that if he aired an interview with Democratic Texas Senate candidate James Talarico, the administration would come after them. The network later denied the allegations, only for FCC Chairman Brendan Carr to agree that he would have come after them.

It was then that Carr announced that they were investigating “The View.”

In her first week, Crisley has faced criticism for some of her comments, including the claim that Trump “isn’t racist” and the false declaration that Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez was the Democratic nominee for 2028.

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of “The View” and the Political Chess Game

In a move that feels like a blend between a desperate grasp for relevancy and a thinly veiled political maneuver, “The View” has introduced Savannah Crisley, a figure whose claim to fame is as nebulous as the criteria for selecting guest hosts on daytime TV. Her parents, reality TV stars turned convicted felons, were pardoned by President Donald Trump—a fact that hangs over Crisley’s guest appearance like a dark, unspoken cloud.

The Irony of the Token Conservative

Crisley’s role seems to fill the void left by Republican Alyssa Farah Griffin, but the choice is peculiar and laden with subtext. It’s as if the producers at “The View” were scouring the earth for a guest host who could embody the intersection of reality television sensationalism and the murky waters of political nepotism. “I want to thank you all for giving me a voice,” Crisley stated, a sentiment that rings almost hollow when one considers the backdrop of her invitation.

The Social Media Trial and Error

Her lamentation about the cruelty of social media, while valid, comes across as a somewhat tone-deaf whine when one considers the platform and privilege she occupies. Yes, the vitriol online is real and harmful, but for someone who has directly benefited from the very system that perpetuates these divisions, Crisley’s complaints feel like missing the forest for the trees. Joy Behar’s advice and subsequent quip about addiction to reading comments serve as a momentary comedic relief in an otherwise dissonant conversation.

A Desperate Ploy for Ratings or Political Favor?

The Daily Beast’s revelation that Crisley’s hosting gig might be a “Hail Mary” to the Trump administration amidst FCC scrutiny adds a layer of intrigue and, frankly, cynicism to the entire affair. The suggestion that “The View” is somehow caught in a larger battle over censorship and political favoritism, with Crisley as a pawn, is both fascinating and deeply troubling.

The article’s mention of Stephen Colbert and the FCC’s alleged threats against CBS paints a picture of a media landscape under pressure, where the lines between entertainment and political leverage blur. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr’s involvement only thickens the plot, suggesting a level of political intrigue and manipulation that should alarm any observer of media and politics.

Missteps and Misinformation

Crisley’s comments, such as the denial of Trump’s racism and the false claim about Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s future nomination, exemplify the dangers of providing a platform to voices ill-equipped to engage with the complexities of political discourse. These statements are not just misinformed; they are potentially harmful, perpetuating falsehoods and distorting public perception.

Conclusion

In sum, Savannah Crisley’s guest appearance on “The View” is a microcosm of the larger systemic issues plaguing American media and politics. It’s a spectacle that is at once absurd, revealing, and deeply concerning. The intertwining of reality TV culture, political nepotism, and the battle for freedom of expression underscores the need for a critical examination of the media we consume and the powers that shape it.

The question remains: Are we simply spectators in a game where the rules are written by those in power, or can we demand a media ecosystem that serves truth, integrity, and the public good? One thing is clear: As long as shows like “The View” continue to blur the lines between entertainment and political advocacy, we must remain vigilant in our consumption and critique of media.
