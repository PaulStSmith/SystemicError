“They realized that too many people were too happy in America,” he said of Jackson and other civil rights leaders. “Even the poor people, the downtrodden people, like even they’re like chilling out. They’re having a great, great time.”

“They had to find something else,” he continued. “The color of your skin. So they went like, I mean, you know, they went full ret--d, and they’re like, hey, how about we break it down to like skin color?”

According to Johnson, Jackson was behind a plot to give Black people “special privileges.”

“We’ll piss off all the classes. We’ll piss off every race. We’ll pit every race against each other. And Jesse Jackson was part of that. “Wouldn’t be the first time there was mass white slavery,” he added. “And that’s what they want. Ultimately, their communist revolution will come through race antagonism to the point of white people being second-class citizens. Does that sound crazy to you?”

“And Jesse Jackson, to his eternal shame, was absolutely part of that dehumanization.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Unhinged: Benny Johnson’s Rant on Jesse Jackson

In the latest episode from the MAGA influencer echo chamber, Benny Johnson has served up a concoction that’s less political analysis and more a dive into the deep end of conspiracy theory. His reaction to the death of civil rights icon Jesse Jackson is not just over the top; it’s a leap into a bizarre alternate reality where facts are optional, and inflammatory rhetoric is the main course.

MAGA Influencer’s Wild Claims: Johnson’s rant is a masterclass in misinformation, as he links Jesse Jackson to an alleged plot of “mass white slavery” and white “genocide” with a confidence that is inversely proportional to the evidence he provides - which is none. His narrative twists the legacy of a civil rights leader into a caricature so distorted it would be laughable if it weren’t so dangerously misleading.

The “Trigger” of Jackson’s Death: According to Johnson, the mere death of Jesse Jackson “triggered” him, leading to a tirade where Johnson accuses Jackson and other civil rights leaders of fabricating racial issues to sow discord. This gross mischaracterization ignores the systemic inequalities these leaders fought to address, choosing instead to paint them as villains in a sinister plot against happiness and unity.

Skin Color Conspiracy Theory: In a particularly egregious leap, Johnson suggests that civil rights leaders, including Jackson, “went full retard” by focusing on skin color, insinuating a deliberate ploy to divide Americans. This statement not only employs an offensive slur but also grossly simplifies and distorts the complex history of racial struggles in America into a nefarious agenda.

A Dystopian Vision of “Race Marxism”: Johnson’s narrative crescendos with the claim that the goal of what he calls “race Marxism” is nothing short of white genocide, facilitated by creating “second-class citizens” of white people through a communist revolution fueled by racial antagonism. This fantastical leap would be a curious plot in a dystopian novel but presented as analysis, it’s a reckless and baseless scare tactic.

The Reality Check: - Historical Ignorance: Johnson’s comments betray a fundamental ignorance or willful distortion of both Jesse Jackson’s legacy and the broader civil rights movement. Far from seeking to oppress, these efforts have been about lifting barriers and seeking justice. - Dangerous Rhetoric: By framing civil rights activism as a plot against white people, Johnson not only misrepresents reality but also stokes the flames of division and racial animosity. - No Evidence, No Problem?: At no point does Johnson offer concrete evidence for his wild claims, relying instead on inflammatory rhetoric. This is not just intellectually lazy; it’s ethically reprehensible.

In summary, Benny Johnson’s rant is a case study in how not to engage with the complex legacy of a civil rights leader. It’s a blend of conspiracy theory, misinformation, and divisive rhetoric that serves no purpose other than to inflame and mislead. Jesse Jackson, whatever one’s view of his activism, deserves a more nuanced and fact-based appraisal. In the end, Johnson’s comments contribute nothing to meaningful discourse; they are, to borrow his own triggered response, a regrettable detour into the realm of the unhinged.
