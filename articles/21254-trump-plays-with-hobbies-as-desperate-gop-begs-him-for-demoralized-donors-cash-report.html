Trump plays with hobbies as desperate GOP begs him for 'demoralized' donors' cash​: Report

Trump Is Not Trapped. The GOP Is.

The real story is party capture, not bad luck.

Axios reports that Republican leaders have stopped expecting Trump to help them with the midterms, financially or otherwise. The numbers are ugly: approval at 32 percent, 68 percent saying he is not focused on the problems most Americans face, and donors getting nervous as MAGA Inc.’s $400 million war chest stays mostly untouched. The source frames this as Trump being “trapped by decisions.” That is too generous. This is what it looks like when a party hands its power to one man and then discovers he plans to spend it on himself.

Who Actually Holds Power

Trump still holds the most important power in this story: agenda-setting power, money power, and symbolic power over a party that has made itself dependent on him. Republican leadership is not directing him. It is pleading for scraps from him.

That is the central institutional fact here. The party is not trying to govern around Trump. It is trying to survive his moods, his obsessions, and his refusal to treat the midterms as anything larger than a backdrop for his own performance.

Decisions, Not Mysteries

The report points to an unpopular war in Iran, controversial tariffs, and an obsession with monuments and renovations. Those are not accidents. They are decisions, and they have political consequences.

Yet the source language still flirts with the idea that Trump is merely distracted or stuck in his habits. No. The pattern is clearer than that. He chose the fight, chose the tariffs, chose the vanity projects, and then chose not to pivot when the numbers turned. Calling that “trapped” turns responsibility into psychology. It softens deliberate conduct into a character quirk.

The Base Is Not Confused

The quote from one Trump insider is more revealing than any polished political analysis: he “concentrates on the hobbies that distract from his terrible polling numbers.” That is not just a jab. It is an admission that the president is governing as if political damage is something to evade, not solve.

The damage is concrete. His own party says the war, tariffs, and monuments are demoralizing the base. Donors are demoralized too. This is not because Republican elites suddenly developed a conscience. It is because they are watching their investment get burned by a leader who treats state power like a personal stage set.

The Money Is the Point

The refusal to deploy MAGA Inc.’s $400 million in competitive races tells you what this arrangement really is. Trump’s movement was sold as a vehicle for the party. In practice, the party became the vehicle for Trump, and the cash is there to preserve his leverage, not necessarily to help down-ballot Republicans win.

That is why the complaint from Republican leaders sounds so thin. They are not objecting to the concentration of power itself. They are objecting to being shut out of the spoils. Their frustration is less about democracy than distribution: who gets funded, who gets abandoned, and who gets blamed when the machine stalls.

The Pattern Beneath It

The deeper pattern is authoritarian style without administrative discipline: loyalty demanded from below, accountability rejected above, and public consequences passed off as bad optics. Trump’s stubbornness is not the flaw in the system. It is the system working as designed.

Republicans built a party around one man’s vanity, grievance, and immunity from internal consequence. Now they want the advantages of centralized control without the humiliation of centralized failure. That is not leadership. It is surrender with bookkeeping.
