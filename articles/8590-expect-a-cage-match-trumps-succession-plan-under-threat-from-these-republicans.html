'Expect a cage match': Trump’s succession plan 'under threat' from these Republicans

Although President Donald Trump has previously floated Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio as his heirs apparent in 2028, that plan is under threat as new Republicans mull a 2028 run, Newsweek reports.

Recent cracks in the MAGA base over the release of the files pertaining to late convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein and support of far-right influencer Nick Fuentes, among other things, may make it “difficult for Trump to rally his supporters around whoever he favors,” Newsweek explains.

And while Trump has previously floated the unconstitutional idea of running for a third term, that notion has been dismissed, and instead, he is “likely to attempt to cement his influence on U.S. politics by choosing a successor, and whoever follows him will be responsible for guiding the Republican Party through future elections and continuing MAGA policies and the president’s legacy,” Newsweek says.

A most recent poll by Polymarket shows Vance has a 56 percent chance of becoming the Republican nominee in 2028, while Rubio is a distant second, with an 8 percent chance. But now that others may enter the field, that could change.

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), a former Trump loyalist turned foil, has dismissed rumors she will seek the presidency despite a recent poll showing her chances improving.

Her odds, Newsweek reports, have increased from 2.7 percent in August to 4 percent in November, according to Polymarket.

Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX), who has emerged as a vocal critic of Tucker Carlson, is also reportedly weighing a run, despite poor early polling in which he currently has 4 percent support, trailing Vance, who polls at 42 percent according to a separate YouGov poll.

In that same poll, Trump’s eldest son, Donald Trump Jr., came in distant second at 13 percent. Florida’s Republican Gov. Ron DeSantis, whose 2024 presidential campaign was eviscerated by Trump, sits at 7 percent and Rubio is narrowly ahead of Cruz at 5 percent.

Calvin Jillson, a politics professor at Southern Methodist University in Texas, tells Newsweek that Vance is still poised to be the frontrunner.

“The 2028 presidential race, with no incumbent in the fight, will likely draw at least a dozen contenders, maybe many more on the Democrat side. Though things could change in the coming months, Trump’s vice president, JD Vance, is a strong favorite for the Republican nomination” Jillson says.

“Rubio, Cruz, maybe Tucker Carlson, will wrestle him for it, but the question for Vance and Rubio is how they can stay close enough to Trump to win the nomination without staying so close that Trump’s baggage becomes theirs in the general election,” he adds.

Vance’s path to the nomination may not be smooth, though, Jillson says, adding that “only if the Trump administration founders will Cruz and Carlson become relevant. But if it happens, expect a cage match, as Trump might say, ‘like no one has ever seen before.’”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Heir Apparent: The Trump Succession Saga

The political landscape as painted by a recent Newsweek article provides a vivid illustration of the ongoing turmoil within the Republican Party as it grapples with the legacy of President Donald Trump and the looming 2028 election. At the core of this saga are the emerging fissures within the MAGA base, the speculative dance of potential heirs, and the shadow cast by Trump’s own political machinations.

The Fragility of the MAGA Coalition

The article highlights the “cracks in the MAGA base” stemming from controversies such as the release of files related to Jeffrey Epstein and the support of far-right influencer Nick Fuentes. This points to a deeper issue: the MAGA movement’s cohesion is more fragile than its outward bravado suggests. The movement, once seen as a monolithic block rallying unequivocally behind Trump, now shows signs of fragmentation. It’s a telling development that may significantly hamper Trump’s ability to anoint a political successor who can unify the base.

The Succession Candidates: A Closer Look

JD Vance and Marco Rubio have been floated by Trump as potential successors, with Vance seemingly the favored one with a “56 percent chance of becoming the Republican nominee in 2028” according to a Polymarket poll cited by Newsweek. Rubio, on the other hand, is a “distant second” at 8 percent. Yet, the entrance of other players like Marjorie Taylor Greene, Ted Cruz, Donald Trump Jr., and possibly Tucker Carlson, complicates the narrative.

The mention of Greene and Cruz is particularly interesting. Greene’s transformation from a Trump loyalist to a potential rival underscores the volatile nature of political alliances within the GOP. Cruz’s consideration of a run, despite his early poor polling, is a testament to the unpredictable dynamics of Republican politics.

Donald Trump Jr.‘s inclusion in the discourse, with a not-insignificant 13 percent in a separate YouGov poll, raises questions about the Trump dynasty’s future in American politics. Meanwhile, Ron DeSantis, once a rising star, now appears sidelined, a casualty of Trump’s political vendettas.

The Trump Conundrum: Legacy vs. Liability

The article succinctly captures the conundrum facing potential successors: how to remain close enough to Trump to benefit from his support without being so close that his controversies become a debilitating liability. This balancing act is crucial. The MAGA movement, while a potent force, is also a double-edged sword. Its power to elevate is matched by its capacity to alienate broader swaths of the electorate.

Conclusion: A Battle Royale in the Making

Calvin Jillson’s commentary predicts a “cage match” of political ambition and strategy, suggesting that the 2028 Republican primary could be an all-out war for the soul of the party. The underlying question is whether the GOP can navigate these tumultuous waters without capsizing. The struggle to both embrace and escape Trump’s shadow will define the party’s trajectory, for better or worse.

In essence, the Newsweek article offers a glimpse into a GOP at a crossroads, with its future leadership in flux. The coming years will reveal whether the Republican Party can consolidate its ranks behind a single figure or if it will continue to be torn by internal strife and competing visions for America’s future. The only certainty is that the battle for the 2028 nomination will be fought not just on policy or charisma, but on the ability to wield the double-edged sword that is Trump’s legacy.
