'Next AG will prosecute': Pam Bondi put on notice of legal reckoning ahead of Epstein drop

WASHINGTON — A rebel House Republican openly questioned top Trump administration officials on Thursday about their handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files after House Democrats released dozens of new photos from Epstein’s estate, amid anticipation for a Justice Department file dump on Friday.

These images included disturbing quotes from Vladimir Nabokov’s “Lolita” written on a woman’s body, alongside other unclear-context photos of Epstein and redacted women. The novel contains the theme of an adult man’s obsession with a 12-year-old girl, mirroring allegations against Epstein.

Reps. Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Robert Garcia spoke to reporters on Thursday about the case.

“I think they will be [released]. We’ll see,” he said of the files expected to be released by the Justice Department on Friday. “It’ll be interesting if they’re not.”

Fifteen days after Friday’s release, the Justice Department must fork over a report to lawmakers with a list of the politicians and government officials in the files.

When asked if he thought there would be a cover-up, Massie noted that the officials appointed to review the Epstein files — namely, FBI Director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi — are not implicated in them. As such, he said there’s no reason for them to be reluctant about releasing them.

“Why would they be reluctant?” he asked.

Massie warned that this is a law that “lasts forever.”

“So the next attorney general will prosecute this attorney general or this FBI director if they do become involved in a cover-up by not being in compliance with this law,” he said.

Massie also accused House Speaker Mike Johnson of lying about the law.

“Three federal judges cite the Transparency Act, but, more importantly, they cite that they’re going to redact the victims’ names, which was a lie that was told about this bill by the Speaker himself. He said that victims would be exposed by this bill… but all three judges said no, there are sufficient protections,” said Massie.

When asked if Mike Johnson would remain in power long term, Massie said Johnson would remain in place as long as President Donald Trump wants him there.

“As long as he’s on his good side, he’s still the Speaker,” said Massie.

Garcia repeated his call to reporters on Thursday to release all the files, and also put Bondi on notice.

“Let’s be very clear that the Department of Justice has to release all of the files tomorrow, and I also want to remind the attorney general that she cannot use any excuse that somehow the law says that ‘if there’s an investigation happening, we can, you know, partially not release everything.’ The subpoena that’s in place, that’s essentially with the law that Oversight passed, does not include that provision. They have to release everything.”

Garcia threatened that Democrats will use every tool available to them, including going to the courts and taking legal action to get the files released.

“We’re prepared to do that, but we’re gonna see what happens tomorrow,” he said.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Saga: A Test of Transparency and Integrity

The recent developments surrounding the Jeffrey Epstein case have once again thrown a sharp spotlight on the inner workings of our justice system and the political machinations that often accompany high-profile cases. The anticipation surrounding the Justice Department’s impending release of Epstein-related files is palpable, not just for the lurid details they promise but for the potential exposure of systemic failings and possibly, high-level complicity.

The Disturbing Revelations

The House Democrats’ release of new photos from Epstein’s estate, featuring disturbing quotes from Vladimir Nabokov’s “Lolita” on a woman’s body, is not just sensational; it’s profoundly unsettling. These images serve as a visceral reminder of the allegations against Epstein, mirroring the novel’s theme of an adult man’s obsession with a young girl. The release of these photos should have been a moment of somber reflection on the horrors of exploitation and abuse. Instead, it feels like another chapter in the ongoing spectacle, with the true gravity of the crimes risking being overshadowed by political posturing.

Political Posturing and the Call for Transparency

Reps. Thomas Massie and Robert Garcia have emerged as vocal figures in the call for transparency, challenging their own party’s handling of the Epstein files. Massie’s assertion that there’s no reason for FBI Director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi to be reluctant about releasing the files, given they are not implicated in them, is a pointed reminder of the expectation of integrity that should be fundamental to these roles. However, Massie’s confidence in the law as a safeguard against potential cover-ups—”a law that lasts forever”—feels naively optimistic in the face of historical precedents of legal manipulation and evasion.

Furthermore, Massie’s accusation against House Speaker Mike Johnson of lying about the law’s implications for victim anonymity underscores a troubling lack of cohesion and trust within the political apparatus. This discord is emblematic of a broader systemic issue: the weaponization of legal and legislative processes for political gain.

The Role of Oversight and Accountability

Garcia’s insistence on the release of all files, without exemption, speaks to a fundamental demand for accountability. His readiness to use “every tool available,” including legal action, to ensure the files’ release is commendable. Yet, it also highlights a systemic dysfunction where oversight mechanisms are so easily disregarded or circumvented that they necessitate threats of legal action to be enforced.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

The handling of the Epstein files is more than a litmus test for the Justice Department and the political figures involved; it’s a referendum on the state of our justice system and the political establishment’s willingness to confront uncomfortable truths. The demand for transparency and accountability in this case is not just about the pursuit of justice for Epstein’s victims but a stand against the systemic failures that allowed his crimes to go unchecked for so long.

As we await the release of the files and the subsequent report to lawmakers, we must remain vigilant in scrutinizing the actions of those in power. The integrity of our institutions and the principle of accountability hang in the balance. In the end, the true measure of our system’s robustness will be how it addresses not just the crimes of individuals like Epstein but the structures that enable such crimes to flourish.
