'Hell of a 90 minutes': Gavin Newsom reveals ominous hints from private Trump meeting

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump 2028 Illusion: A Threat to Democracy or Political Theater?

California Governor Gavin Newsom speaks to the media after announcing a $750 million film and TV tax credit to help boost one of California’s hallmark industries and the local economy from The Ranch, a Warner Bros. production facility in Burbank, California, U.S., July 2, 2025. REUTERS/Mike Blake

California Gov. Gavin Newsom revealed a telling detail Wednesday of a private meeting between him and President Donald Trump earlier this year that he said made clear the president’s plans for 2028.

“He showed me a painting; he said ‘turn around,’ and I said 'oh, there's (former President Franklin D. Roosevelt),’” Newsom said, speaking at a summit in Sacramento, California hosted by Politico.

“Then he went on and on about the third term…I don't think Donald Trump wants another election. I have two dozen Trump 2028 hats his folks keep sending me.”

Newsom said the meeting, which he described as “a hell of a 90 minutes,” made clear that Trump intended to serve a third term in the White House come 2028. The crowd at the summit could be heard laughing at some of Newsom’s remarks, to which he urged them to “wake up” to what he characterized as a serious threat to democracy.

“Who spends $200 million on a ballroom at their home and then leaves the house?” Newsom said.

“This is serious guys… He tried to light democracy on fire, he dialed for almost 12,000 votes, now he's doing it in plain sight and people say 'oh, just Trump being Trump.' People actually think this guy's serious about having another election? You think he's joking about 2028? He brings foreign leaders to the Oval Office and he goes to the White House store and he shows them the 2028 hats.”

Trump is already selling “Trump 2028” hats on his official “Trump Store” website, priced at $50 each, and has hinted at a third term in office on numerous occasions.

"A lot of people want me to do it," Trump said back in April, regarding the idea of serving a third term. "There are methods which you could do it."

Newsom, taking the president at his word, said Trump’s history spoke for itself as far as whether the president’s potential bid for a third term should be taken seriously.

“This guy doesn't believe in free and fair elections, he tried to wreck this country,” Newsom said. “Were you there Jan. 6?”

Our Analysis:

The Trump 2028 Illusion: A Threat to Democracy or Political Theater?

In a recent revelation that sounds more like a scene from a dystopian novel than a snippet from a political summit, California Governor Gavin Newsom has thrown a spotlight onto what could either be the most alarming threat to American democracy since the Civil War or the most elaborate ego trip of the 21st century. During a Politico-hosted summit in Sacramento, Newsom detailed a private meeting with President Donald Trump, painting a picture so absurd it's almost hard to believe. And yet, here we are, analyzing the potential implications of a Trump 2028 campaign.

The Third Term Temptation

Trump's alleged obsession with a third term is not just a breach of the U.S. Constitution’s two-term limit for presidents; it's a direct challenge to the very foundations of American democracy. The 22nd Amendment is clear, yet Trump’s fixation on Franklin D. Roosevelt, the only U.S. president to serve more than two terms, signals an unsettling desire to emulate a historical exception—a move that could only be pursued through constitutional manipulation or an outright disregard for democratic norms.

The Merchandise of Monarchy

Selling “Trump 2028” hats as if they were mere campaign souvenirs is a bold move, but it's also a brilliantly sinister marketing ploy. At $50 a pop, these hats serve not only as fundraising tools but as symbols of Trump's enduring influence over his base and a disturbing testament to his disregard for constitutional limits. The merchandise, coupled with his flippant remarks about finding methods to secure a third term, should not be dismissed as mere bravado. This is a man, after all, who has shown a remarkable ability to bend the Republican Party, and to some extent, the country's political norms, to his will.

The Democracy at Stake

Governor Newsom's comments, while easy to mock for their almost comical portrayal of Trump's ambitions, underscore a grave concern. Trump’s past behavior, particularly his actions surrounding the 2020 election and the infamous January 6th insurrection, reveal a pattern of challenging the electoral process and undermining the pillars of democracy. To dismiss these ambitions as mere bluster would be to ignore the clear and present danger Trump poses to the sanctity of free and fair elections.

The Call to Action

Newsom's plea to the audience to “wake up” is more than a call to awareness; it's a call to action. The threat to democracy, as Newsom suggests, is not looming—it's already at the doorstep. Trump's casual musings about a third term, his mobilization of merchandise, and his manipulation of political narratives are not isolated incidents but part of a larger strategy to reshape American democracy in his image.

In conclusion, while some may be tempted to laugh off Trump's 2028 aspirations as the fantasies of a man unwilling to relinquish power, the reality is far from amusing. This is a calculated attempt to undermine democratic principles and extend a legacy marked by division and autocracy. Whether or not Trump truly intends to pursue a third term is almost irrelevant. The damage to democratic norms and the emboldening of autocratic tendencies among his followers are already in motion. The real question now is not whether Trump can actually secure a third term, but whether American democracy can withstand the erosion of its foundational principles.
