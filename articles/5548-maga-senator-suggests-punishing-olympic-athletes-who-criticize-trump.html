MAGA senator suggests punishing Olympic athletes who criticize Trump

By Paulo Santos

The United States has 232 athletes representing the country on the world stage at the 2026 Winter Olympics in Italy. Now, one Republican senator is calling for some of those athletes to be booted from the team for expressing a negative opinion about President Donald Trump’s administration.

The Daily Beast reported Monday that Sen. Rick Scott (R-Fla.) wants to strip American athletes of their uniforms should they speak negatively about the current government while competing for Olympic gold. Scott wrote on X that the U.S. stood for “freedom and democracy” and then pivoted to calling for removing athletes from Team U.S.A. who expressed their First Amendment rights at the Winter Olympics.

“Any person who goes to the Olympics to represent the United States and then says they don’t want to represent the United States should be immediately stripped of the Olympic uniform,” Scott said in a video.

While Scott didn’t name any specific athlete he felt should be stripped of their uniform, his comment comes on the heels of Olympic skier Hunter Hess giving his own nuanced view of patriotism. Hess — who is from Bend, Oregon — said over the weekend he had “mixed emotions representing the U.S. right now,” and that he was “not the biggest fan” of the administration’s policies.

“I think for me, it’s more I’m representing my friends and family back home, the people that represented it before me, all the things that I believe that are good about the U.S.,” Hess said.

In response to Hess’ interview, President Donald Trump called Hess “a real Loser” on his Truth Social platform. The president also suggested that Hess shouldn’t be on Team U.S.A. given his attitude toward the administration.

“U.S. Olympic Skier, Hunter Hess, a real Loser, says he doesn’t represent his Country in the current Winter Olympics,” Trump wrote. “If that’s the case, he shouldn’t have tried out for the Team, and it’s too bad he’s on it. Very hard to root for someone like this.”

Hess didn’t directly respond to Trump, but posted a follow-up message to his Instagram account on Monday expressing gratitude for living in a country that values freedom of speech.

“There is so much that is great about America, but there are always things that could be better. One of the many things that makes this country so amazing is that we have the right and the freedom to point that out,” he wrote. “The best part of the Olympics is that it brings people together, and when so many of us are divided we need that more than ever. I cannot wait to represent Team USA next week when I compete.”

Skier Chris Lillis also expressed criticism of the administration, with CNN reporting he was explicitly upset about the recent killings of U.S. citizens Renee Good and Alex Pretti at the hands of federal agents in Minneapolis.

“A lot of times, athletes are hesitant to talk about political views and how we feel about things,” Lillis said. “I feel heartbroken about what’s happening in the United States.”

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Demanding Silence in the Name of Freedom

In a stunning display of cognitive dissonance, Senator Rick Scott (R-Fla.) has taken it upon himself to become the arbiter of what constitutes acceptable patriotism, suggesting that American athletes critical of the Trump administration should be stripped of their Olympic uniforms. This suggestion, reported by The Daily Beast, starkly contrasts with the very principles of “freedom and democracy” that Scott claims the U.S. stands for.

The Irony of Suppressing Free Speech

Scott’s suggestion is an affront to the First Amendment rights that form the bedrock of American freedoms. By advocating for punitive measures against those who exercise their right to free speech, particularly when representing their country on an international stage, Scott inadvertently champions a form of political censorship more akin to authoritarian regimes than the democratic ideals he purports to defend.

Hunter Hess: A Case Study in Patriotism

Olympic skier Hunter Hess’s nuanced take on patriotism — expressing “mixed emotions” about representing the U.S. under the current administration while also acknowledging the aspects of the nation he values — has drawn the ire of both President Trump and Senator Scott. Trump’s response, labeling Hess “a real Loser” on Truth Social, is a textbook example of the administration’s intolerance toward dissenting voices. Hess’s stance, grounded in a love for his country tempered by a critical eye towards its leadership, embodies a mature, thoughtful form of patriotism far removed from the blind allegiance Scott and Trump demand.

Freedom of Speech: The Ultimate Patriotism

Hess’s follow-up message, celebrating the freedom of speech as one of America’s greatest attributes, underscores the vital role dissent plays in a healthy democracy. His anticipation to represent Team USA, despite his reservations about its current leadership, speaks to a commitment to the nation’s values over its politics.

Chris Lillis: Speaking Out Against Injustice

Skier Chris Lillis’s comments on the tragic killings of U.S. citizens by federal agents highlight another dimension of the issue: the importance of athletes using their platform to draw attention to national injustices. Lillis’s heartbreak over the events in Minneapolis — and his willingness to speak on them — challenges the notion that athletes should remain apolitical, instead showcasing the potential for sports figures to contribute to important national conversations.

Conclusion: The True Spirit of the Olympics

Senator Scott’s call to silence athletes critical of the U.S. administration not only flies in the face of the constitutional rights he is sworn to protect but also betrays a fundamental misunderstanding of what the Olympics represent. The Games are a celebration of humanity’s diversity, bringing together individuals from all walks of life to compete in the spirit of friendship, respect, and fair play. Attempting to enforce a uniform political stance among Team USA’s athletes undermines this spirit, turning what should be a unifying event into yet another battleground for partisan squabbles.

In their courage to speak out, athletes like Hess and Lillis embody the true spirit of the Olympics — one where the freedom to express one’s beliefs is as celebrated as the athletic achievements themselves. Their stance should be applauded, not condemned, for it is in the crucible of dissent that democracy is tested and strengthened.
