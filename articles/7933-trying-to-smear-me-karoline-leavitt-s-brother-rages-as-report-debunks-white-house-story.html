'Trying to smear me!' Karoline Leavitt's brother rages as report debunks White House story

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 8, 20255 min read

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt walks over to speak with reporters on the West Wing driveway at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 24, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst/File Photo

Michael Leavitt, the brother of White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, attacked the Washington Post Sunday as his and the White House’s official narrative on a lengthy custody battle and immigration arrest was challenged by the outlet’s reporting.

In 2014, Michael Leavitt had a child with Bruna Ferreira, a migrant who obtained a work permit and limited immigration protections in 2012 and would later make Karoline Leavitt her child’s godmother. Ferreira was arrested by immigration officials last month, and the White House has painted her as a “criminal” and an absent mother.

But, as the Post reported Sunday, “court records, family photos and Ferreira’s account tell a different story,” and one that sparked Michael Leavitt’s outrage.

“[The Washington Post is] trying to use this whole situation to push a narrative to smear me,” Michael Leavitt wrote to the Post in a text message, having declined to speak with the outlet by telephone.

When Ferreira’s arrest was first reported in mid-November, the White House tried to distance Karoline Leavitt from the mother of her brother’s child, insisting that Karoline Leavitt hadn’t spoken to Ferreira in years. The Post’s reporting, however, cast doubt on that claim.

“[Ferreira] selected [Karoline] Leavitt to be her son’s godmother. She signed off on her son’s trip to the White House Easter egg hunt this spring,” the Post writes. “And she said she ‘moved mountains’ to ensure he could attend Leavitt’s wedding in January.”

Micheal Leavitt’s insistence that he had no involvement with her immigration arrest was also challenged by Ferreira, who told the Post that both Michael Leavitt and his father, Bob Leavitt, had told Ferreira’s sister that Ferreira should “self deport,” something that Ferreira’s attorney, Todd Pomerleau, described to the Post as “a trap.”

Ferreira would go on to say that while her relationship with Karoline Leavitt wasn’t intimate, telling the Post “obviously we’re not BFFs and we’re not FaceTiming,” she outright rejected the White House’s assertion that no relationship between the two existed.

“Ferreira said she could not recall when they last spoke, but said she met Karoline Leavitt’s now husband and is constantly in the family’s orbit because of their son,” the Post reported. “Her lawyer, Pomerleau, said it is false that they have not spoken in years.”

Our Analysis:

The Leavitt Saga: Unraveling the Spin

In a tale that seems ripped straight from the pages of a political thriller, the Leavitt family, intertwined with the highest levels of White House communications, finds itself embroiled in a narrative of immigration, family drama, and media battles. At the heart of this story, as reported by the Washington Post, is Michael Leavitt’s vehement denial and the White House’s attempt to paint Bruna Ferreira, a migrant and the mother of Michael’s child, in broad strokes of criminality and neglect. Yet, the details emerging from the Post’s investigation suggest a reality far more complex and less convenient than the official narrative would have us believe.

The Official vs. The Real

The White House, with Karoline Leavitt as its press secretary, seems to have taken a page from the age-old playbook of political damage control: distance, deny, and discredit. The initial attempt to sever any ties between Karoline Leavitt and Bruna Ferreira reeks of a strategic maneuver designed to mitigate political fallout. However, the facts unearthed by the Washington Post paint a starkly different picture - one of ongoing, albeit complicated, relationships and personal connections that directly contradict the administration’s claims.

Michael Leavitt’s outrage at the Post’s reporting is indicative of a broader trend within political circles to vilify the messenger rather than address the substance of the allegations. His accusation that the Post is attempting to “smear” him by leveraging a personal dispute for political narratives is a classic redirection, a feeble attempt to shift focus from the intricacies of the case to a perceived media bias.

The Trap of ‘Self-Deportation’

The narrative takes a darker turn with the revelation that Michael Leavitt and his father, Bob Leavitt, allegedly advised Ferreira to “self deport” - a suggestion her attorney, Todd Pomerleau, labels as a “trap.” This insidious piece of advice, whether it was given with genuine intent or as a strategic ploy, underscores the perilous landscape migrants navigate, often at the mercy of personal vendettas that can have profound legal and emotional ramifications.

The Godmother Connection

Karoline Leavitt’s role as the godmother to Ferreira’s son adds layers of personal connection and responsibility that directly contradict the White House’s portrayal of Ferreira as an “absent mother” and their attempts to downplay her relationship with the Leavitt family. The involvement of Ferreira’s son in family events, such as the White House Easter egg hunt and Leavitt’s wedding, does not suggest a severed relationship but rather a continued, if complex, familial entanglement.

Conclusion: The Uncomfortable Truth

The Leavitt-Ferreira saga, as dissected by the Washington Post, exposes the uncomfortable truth about how personal narratives are manipulated for political gain. The White House’s attempts to cast Ferreira in a negative light, based on her immigration status and her relationship with the Leavitt family, underscores a troubling propensity to exploit individual stories for broader political narratives.

In this instance, the facts laid bare by investigative journalism challenge the official narrative and demand a reevaluation of the roles and responsibilities not only of those directly involved but also of the institutions that support them. The real story here is not just about the complexities of a custody battle or an immigration arrest; it’s about the power dynamics at play and the lengths to which political entities will go to protect their own, often at the expense of the truth.
