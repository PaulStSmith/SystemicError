Raw Story reported on the “bombshell ruling,” in which Judge Karin Immergut, a Trump appointee, ruled that Trump’s National Guard call-up to Portland was based on false claims about unrest in the liberal city. That ruling also made waves online.

Enter Soviet-born Parnas, who worked closely with Rudy Giuliani during Trump’s first administration, and was purportedly sent to Ukraine to help Trump and Giuliani make contacts there for the purposes of digging up negative info on Hunter Biden. Parnas was later convicted of campaign finance violations, and today the analyst reports on Trump from outside the administration.

“I’m writing with good news — real good news — and as soon as I heard it, I knew I had to share it with you right away,” Parnas wrote on Substack after the ruling. “A federal judge — one that Trump himself appointed — just ruled that Trump cannot deploy the National Guard into Portland, at least for the next two weeks.”

According to Parnas, this is more than just a procedural win in court. It could be a sign that Trump’s policies are beginning to meet checks and balances, the analyst suggested

“Think about that: even judges he hand-picked are starting to push back against his most dangerous power grabs,” according to Parnas. “This isn’t just a legal win — it’s a signal that the walls around Trump’s authoritarian playbook are beginning to crack.”

Parnas continued, highlighting what he sees as an important quote from the ruling.

“In her ruling, Judge Karin Immergut — a Trump appointee — wrote, ‘This is a nation of constitutional law, not of martial law.’ Those words matter,” he wrote. “They carry the weight of history. She made it clear that no president, no matter how powerful or angry, can bypass the Constitution to use the U.S. military against American citizens. Coming from someone he appointed, this is more than a courtroom decision — it’s a line drawn in the sand.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Trump’s Own Appointee Ruling Against Him

In what can only be described as a delicious twist of fate, Donald Trump has been handed a legal defeat by a judge he appointed, Judge Karin Immergut. This scenario is rich with irony and indicative of a deeper issue within Trump’s presidency and his approach to governance. The ruling in question, which found Trump’s call-up of the National Guard to Portland based on false claims of unrest, is a stark reminder of the dangers of misinformation and the abuse of power.

The False Claims of Unrest

Trump’s narrative of Portland being a city under siege by liberal anarchists was not only exaggerated but, as Judge Immergut’s ruling points out, baseless. This manipulation of facts to justify the deployment of the National Guard is a dangerous precedent. It’s an attempt to use military power to quell dissent and manage public perception, a tactic that is worryingly authoritarian.

A Signal of Checks and Balances

Parnas, a former Trump insider turned analyst, sees this ruling as a sign that Trump’s policies are beginning to encounter the checks and balances that are fundamental to the American political system. It’s heartening to see that even judges appointed by Trump are willing to uphold constitutional values over loyalty to the person who appointed them. This speaks volumes about the resilience of the American judiciary and should serve as a reminder that no one is above the law, not even the President.

“This is a nation of constitutional law, not of martial law.” This quote from Judge Immergut’s ruling is a powerful rebuke of Trump’s authoritarian tendencies. It underscores the principle that the military should not be used against American citizens and that the Constitution cannot be bypassed for political gain. Coming from a Trump appointee, this statement carries an added weight of betrayal against Trump’s authoritarian playbook, marking a significant blow to his perceived infallibility.

The Bigger Picture

This scenario is emblematic of a larger problem within Trump’s presidency and political strategy: the reliance on misinformation and authoritarian tactics to maintain power and silence opposition. The ruling by Judge Immergut is a beacon of hope, a sign that despite the attempts to erode democratic norms, there are still safeguards in place to protect the principles of democracy.

In Conclusion

The ruling against Trump’s deployment of the National Guard in Portland is not just a legal defeat; it’s a symbolic victory for constitutional law and a testament to the strength of America’s democratic institutions. It shows that even those appointed by Trump are not blindly loyal and that the judiciary remains a critical check on presidential power. This should serve as a warning to Trump and others who seek to abuse their power: the Constitution and the rule of law will always prevail.
