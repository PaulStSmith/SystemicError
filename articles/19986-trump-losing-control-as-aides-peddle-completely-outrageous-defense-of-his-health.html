Trump 'losing control' as aides peddle 'completely outrageous' defense of his health

By Paulo Santos

During Joe Biden’s four years in the White House, Donald Trump and his allies repeatedly questioned his mental fitness and attacked him as “Sleepy Joe.” But now, more than a year into his second presidency, it is Trump’s fitness — both mental and physical — that is being questioned.

In a February appearance on the “Today, Explained” podcast, New York Magazine’s Ben Terris examined the questions surrounding Trump’s health — a subject he previously addressed in an article published on January 26. And he explained why it can be difficult to determine how well-off the president is.

“Instead of just saying he’s healthy for an almost 80-year-old, that he’s slowing down a little bit, but he’s certainly healthy enough to be president, people talk about him in these terms that are just completely outrageous: superhuman, the healthiest man alive,” Terris explained. “He told me he was healthier than he was 40 years ago. The guy doesn’t exercise; he doesn’t eat well. He drinks enough Diet Coke to fill a football stadium. And you just can’t quite trust the people around him. And I felt like the story I published said a lot about Trump’s America, not just Trump’s health.”

Trump’s bluster, according to Terris, may obscure questions about his physical and mental wellbeing.

“Donald Trump is about to be 80 years old,” Terris argued. “Just by dint of that fact, it’s a worthwhile story to cover. One reason I think that Trump is able to ‘get away’ with some things that could be signs of aging is that they could also just be signs of Donald Trump being Donald Trump. He has been a chaotic figure for a long time. He’s got this rambling way of talking. He says unhinged, outrageous stuff. He did that 15 years ago. He does that now.”

Terris added, “Are there differences in the way that he communicates between now and then? Sure, of course. But it’s not as stark as if and when Biden starts to show signs of deteriorating. Because Biden was such a serious guy who kind of spoke in your traditional politician way, as soon as there was slippage, you could notice it a lot easier.”

Trump, according to Terris, is having a hard time “controlling the narrative” about his health and other matters.

“Donald Trump does seem to be losing his ability to control his story,” Ferris observed. “His poll numbers are not what he wants them to be. The midterms are trending in the wrong direction. The immigration story is not even going the way he wants to go, and that was kind of a top issue for him. The way that he tries to control the narrative, so to speak, of his health is sort of akin to how he’s trying to control everything, and I just feel like he’s sort of losing some of that control.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Health and Politics: A Tale of Two Presidencies

In the realm of political discourse, the tables have turned with a delicious irony that could only be savored in the unpredictable theater of American politics. Donald Trump, once the maestro of mocking Joe Biden’s mental fitness with the moniker “Sleepy Joe,” now finds himself in the glaring spotlight of health scrutiny. This twist of fate is not just a tale of political karma but a reflection of a deeper malaise in the body politic.

Trump’s Health: Between Superhuman Claims and Stark Reality

Ben Terris of New York Magazine delves into the murky waters of Trump’s health, revealing a narrative fraught with superhuman claims that defy the reality of an almost 80-year-old man who shuns exercise and harbors a penchant for Diet Coke. This dichotomy between the image projected and the reality is a stark reminder of the cult of personality that often clouds rational judgment in political loyalty. It’s as if Trump’s followers are asked to believe in a fountain of youth, miraculously discovered on a golf course in Mar-a-Lago.

The narrative constructed around Trump’s health doesn’t just raise eyebrows for its departure from plausibility; it underscores a troubling trend where facts are malleable, and truth is a matter of convenience. Terris’s observation that “you just can’t quite trust the people around him” speaks volumes about the echo chamber that sustains these myths. It’s a tale as old as time, but with a modern twist where misinformation can spread faster than a Diet Coke spill.

The Art of Distraction and the Age of Unreason

Trump’s approach to his health queries mirrors his broader strategy of obfuscation and distraction. As Terris rightly points out, distinguishing the symptoms of aging from Trump’s inherent chaotic persona becomes a challenging task. This chaos, however, is not merely a personal trait but a calculated political tool. By keeping the waters muddy, Trump manages to evade scrutiny, blending signs of potential health decline with his baseline of erratic behavior. It’s a cunning strategy, leveraging his history of outrageous antics as both a shield and a smokescreen.

Yet, as Terris notes, the armor is beginning to show cracks. Trump’s dwindling ability to “control the narrative” is symptomatic of a larger issue at play — the erosion of credibility. With poll numbers waning and political tides turning, the story of Trump’s health is emblematic of his broader struggle to maintain dominance in a narrative he once authored with unchallenged authority.

The Stark Contrast: Biden vs. Trump

The comparison between Biden and Trump in terms of health scrutiny reveals a deeper commentary on political image management. Biden, with his more traditional demeanor, presents a clear canvas where deviations are easily spotted. Trump’s canvas, however, is a Jackson Pollock painting — chaotic, colorful, and open to interpretation. This contrast not only highlights the different standards to which we hold our leaders but also reflects our collective tolerance for ambiguity in the face of charisma.

Conclusion: The Unhealthy State of Political Discourse

In the end, the saga of Trump’s health is less about the man and more about what he represents — a polarized nation where truth is often a casualty of allegiance. It’s a reminder that in the high stakes game of politics, the health of our leaders can sometimes become a pawn in a larger battle for power. As we navigate these turbulent waters, let’s remember that transparency, integrity, and truth should be the guiding principles, not just in assessing the physical and mental fitness of our leaders but in upholding the health of our democracy itself.
