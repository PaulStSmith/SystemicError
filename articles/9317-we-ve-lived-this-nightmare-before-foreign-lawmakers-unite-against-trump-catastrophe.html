‘We've lived this nightmare before': Foreign lawmakers unite against Trump 'catastrophe'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:An Analysis of Trump’s Controversial “Boat-Bombing Spree” in Latin America

Donald Trump in the Oval Office. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Dozens of political leaders throughout Latin America are condemning US President Donald Trump’s boat-bombing spree, which began in the Caribbean last month but has since spread to the Pacific Ocean.

In a letter posted by Progressive International on its X account, the Latin American leaders from across the region said that Trump’s campaign of extrajudicial killings of purported drug traffickers is threatening peace and stability in Latin America, and is likely a pretext for further military intervention in the region.

“The Trump administration is escalating a dangerous military buildup off the coast of Venezuela, deploying naval forces in the Caribbean in preparation for potential armed intervention,” they wrote. “The pretext is familiar. President Trump justifies intervention in Venezuela as a means to combat ‘cartels,’ celebrating lethal strikes against fishermen accused of carrying drugs.”

The lawmakers then linked the Trump administration’s current militarism to past US actions that had destabilized their nations.

“We have lived this nightmare before,” they emphasized. “US military interventions of the 20th century brought dictatorships, disappearances, and decades of trauma to our nations. We know the terrible cost of allowing foreign powers to wage war on our continent. We cannot—we will not—allow history to repeat itself.”

They called upon “all organized political forces across Latin America and the Caribbean” to unite in the name of preventing another “catastrophe” from occurring.

“Across our political contexts, we share a common cause: the sovereignty of our nations and the security of our peoples,” they concluded. “We must stand together now.”

The US military has carried out at least nine attacks on boats in the Caribbean and in the Pacific Ocean over the past seven weeks, killing at least 37 people so far. Although the administration has claimed that these boats were engaged in illegal drug smuggling, it has provided no evidence to justify this claim—and both Trump and Vice President JD Vance have jokingly said it would be dangerous “to even go fishing” in the Caribbean, suggesting civilians could be killed in the strikes

Colombian President Gustavo Petro this past weekend said that the Trump administration had “committed a murder” after one of its boat attacks killed a Colombian citizen named Alejandro Carranza, who had been out on a fishing trip when the US military attacked his boat.

The Trump administration’s boat strikes have been condemned not only by leaders in Latin America but also by multiple US-based legal experts who have accused the administration of going on an extrajudicial murder spree. Experts have noted that the US has long approached drug trafficking as a criminal offense—not an act of war to be responded to with military force.

The Center for Economic and Policy Research (CEPR), a US-based think tank, announced on Thursday that it was launching a new project aimed at tracking “US militarism, aggression, and intervention in Latin America and the Caribbean,” including “US strikes on boats, threats against Venezuela and Colombia, and other aspects of US interventionism in the region under the second Trump administration.”

Alexander Main, CEPR’s director of international policy, said that the Trump administration’s recent actions across Latin America represent “a dangerous new escalation with an open disregard for international law and agreements” that could have “profound implications beyond the region.”

Our Analysis:

An Analysis of Trump’s Controversial “Boat-Bombing Spree” in Latin America

In what reads like a script torn from the darkest annals of Cold War interventionism, the Trump administration has embarked on a contentious and bloody campaign across the Caribbean and Pacific Ocean. This operation, characterized by the extrajudicial killing of individuals purported to be drug traffickers, has not only sparked outrage across Latin America but also prompted serious questions regarding its legality, its morality, and the actual intentions behind these military maneuvers.

The Pretext of Drug Cartels: A Cloak for Military Expansion?

The administration justifies its actions with the threadbare argument of combating drug cartels, a rationale that is as old as it is suspicious. Deploying naval forces off the coast of Venezuela, Trump’s actions beg the question: Is this genuinely about tackling the drug trade, or is it a pretext for a larger strategic objective, perhaps one involving military intervention in Venezuela? The brazen celebration of lethal strikes against fishermen, accused without evidence of trafficking drugs, seems to underscore a cavalier attitude towards life and law, with both Trump and Vice President JD Vance making light of the potential for civilian casualties.

Echoes of a Dark Past

Latin American leaders, in a searing indictment of these actions, have drawn parallels to the harrowing era of U.S. military interventions during the 20th century—an era that left deep scars across the continent in the form of dictatorships, disappearances, and generational trauma. The collective voice of these leaders, emphasizing the sovereignty and security of their nations, stands as a potent reminder of the region’s dark history with foreign intervention—a history they are determined not to repeat.

A Dangerous Escalation in Military Aggression

The Trump administration’s conduct represents a dangerous pivot from treating drug trafficking as a criminal issue to an act of war. This marks a significant escalation in U.S. military aggression, one that disregards international law and norms. The killing of at least 37 individuals in a span of seven weeks, with flimsy justification and a disturbing levity about potential civilian casualties, is nothing short of alarming. The death of Alejandro Carranza, a Colombian citizen, further exemplifies the reckless disregard for civilian life, prompting Colombian President Gustavo Petro to accuse the Trump administration of murder.

A Violation of International Norms

This campaign, devoid of evidence or accountability, has not only been condemned by leaders within Latin America but has also drawn sharp criticism from legal experts in the United States. The narrative being spun by Trump and his administration is dangerously reminiscent of past U.S. policies that have led to widespread destabilization and suffering in Latin America. The Center for Economic and Policy Research’s initiative to track U.S. militarism in the region is a crucial step towards holding the administration accountable and shedding light on these aggressive maneuvers.

Conclusion: A Call for Unity and Sovereignty

The collective outcry from Latin American leaders is a clarion call for unity against what they perceive as an existential threat to their sovereignty and the well-being of their citizens. This episode not only challenges the international community to scrutinize the actions of the Trump administration but also to reflect on the broader implications of such military adventurism. The disregard for international law, the flippant attitude towards civilian casualties, and the shadow of ulterior motives cast a long and ominous shadow over Trump’s “boat-bombing spree.” It’s a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance, accountability, and international solidarity in the face of actions that threaten peace, stability, and the rule of law across regions.
