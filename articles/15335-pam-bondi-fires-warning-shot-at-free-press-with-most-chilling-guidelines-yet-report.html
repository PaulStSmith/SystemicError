Pam Bondi fires 'warning shot' at free press with 'most chilling' guidelines yet: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Chilling Move Against the Free Press by Attorney General Pam Bondi

FILE PHOTO: U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi, then as U.S. President-elect Donald Trump’s nominee, testifies at a Senate Judiciary Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 15, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz/File Photo/File Photo

“Attorney General Pam Bondi fired a warning shot at the free press last Friday,” according to the Editorial Board of the Washington Post.

They are concerned with the removal of a Justice Department regulation, which limited the government's ability to see the phone or email records of journalists.

Bondi replaced this guideline with ones they call “most chilling.”

This is because Bondi is allowing investigators to go after members of the media who obtain “privileged and other sensitive information,” not just classified material, “that undermine President [Donald] Trump’s policies.”

The regulations Bondi removed were put in place by Attorney General Garland.

Additionally, the House of Representatives voted unanimously to codify the regulation, which was known as the Press Act (Protect Reporters and Exploitative State Spying Act) last year.

But Trump called on Senate Republicans to kill the bill after his election.

“The effort had bipartisan support because both parties have an interest in exposing wrongdoing inside government, especially when they are in the opposition,” according to the Post.

“A lasting stain on Obama’s presidential legacy is his administration’s ruthless pursuit of journalists’ records,” the Post wrote.

“Including seizing Associated Press phone records in 2013 and conducting electronic surveillance of a Fox News journalist. The Obama administration also brought more leak prosecutions than all previous administrations combined.”

Bondi’s memo focused on federal employees who “intentionally” leak sensitive information to the media.

The outlet noted Bondi’s promise that her department will limit subpoenas and claims she must personally “approve efforts to question or arrest members of the news media.”

“Importantly, obtaining a subpoena for records will also continue to require showing a judge that there is probable cause to think a crime has been committed,” the Post said.

“Leaking negative information about the government is not necessarily against the law.

The Board made it clear, their newspaper “routinely relies on confidential sources to bring readers important stories.”

Later adding that keeping “sources’ identities confidential will always be a top priority — under Trump, and every president who follows him.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Chilling Move Against the Free Press by Attorney General Pam Bondi

The Assault on Press Freedom

Attorney General Pam Bondi's recent actions represent nothing short of a frontal assault on the free press and the very cornerstone of democracy. By dismantling regulations that limited the government's capacity to seize journalists' communication records, Bondi has opened the door to a level of state surveillance reminiscent of the darkest chapters in modern history. The Washington Post's Editorial Board has rightly sounded the alarm on this dire turn of events. Bondi's decision to replace these guidelines with far more invasive ones is not just alarming; it's chilling.

The Erosion of Safeguards

The regulations removed by Bondi were not arbitrary bureaucratic red tape; they were vital safeguards against the abuse of power. Instituted by Attorney General Garland, these guidelines were a bulwark protecting journalists from unwarranted invasions of privacy and ensuring the flow of information to the public remained unimpeded. The House of Representatives' unanimous vote to codify these protections into law, via the Press Act, underscores the bipartisan understanding of their importance. Yet, in a move that reeks of authoritarianism, Trump and his Senate allies chose to quash this legislation, revealing a blatant disregard for the principles of transparency and accountability.

A Dangerous Precedent

Bondi's memo, focusing on federal employees who leak "sensitive information," is a thinly veiled threat against whistleblowers and the journalists who give them voice. While she claims that the department will limit subpoenas and that she must personally approve efforts to question or arrest members of the media, these assurances ring hollow. The requirement of showing probable cause to a judge, though maintained, is small comfort when the overarching policy is so aggressively aimed at stifling dissent.

Bipartisan Concerns and a Stained Legacy

The Washington Post correctly highlights that the effort to protect journalists and their sources has historically enjoyed bipartisan support. This consensus recognizes that exposing government wrongdoing is vital, regardless of which party is in power. The editorial also reminds us of the Obama administration's troubling record of pursuing journalists' records, illustrating that the impulse to control the narrative can infect any administration. However, Bondi's actions, set against the backdrop of Trump's antagonism towards the press, represent a significant escalation in this ongoing battle.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

The commitment of the Washington Post, and indeed all reputable news organizations, to protecting their sources is a testament to the enduring value of press freedom. As these events unfold, it is crucial for the public and policymakers alike to recognize the grave threat posed by Bondi's policy shift. This is not merely a matter of partisan politics; it is a fundamental issue of civil liberties and the health of our democracy. Under Trump, Bondi, or any future administration, the need for a free, unfettered press remains paramount. We must not allow these recent developments to become normalized. The stakes are simply too high.
