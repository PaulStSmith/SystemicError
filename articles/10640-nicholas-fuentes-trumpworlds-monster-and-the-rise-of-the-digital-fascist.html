Nicholas Fuentes: Trumpworld’s Monster and the Rise of the Digital Fascist

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 10, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump’s political project was always a Frankenstein’s experiment in rage, paranoia, and resentment. Now, that experiment has slipped its shackles and come alive in the form of Nicholas J. Fuentes, a 27-year-old incel demagogue whose blend of white nationalism, misogyny, and online swarm tactics terrifies even Trumpworld insiders. The New York Times profile, amplified by The Daily Beast, reveals a grotesque irony: the political class that once rode hate like a cheap carnival ride now cowers from a man who turned hate into a career.

Fuentes doesn’t command armies in the streets. He commands digital mobs, armed with harassment campaigns, doxing tactics, and relentless amplification in the toxic echo chambers of Elon Musk’s X and Rumble. This is the new right-wing power base: not think tanks or Super PACs, but a network of radicalized young men addicted to livestreams and grievance. And make no mistake—Fuentes is their prophet.

The Tweet That Defined Him

On Election Night 2024, as Trump’s campaign flailed, Fuentes unleashed one of the most vile posts in recent memory: “Your body, my choice. Forever.” A deliberate inversion of reproductive rights slogans, it dripped with ownership, control, and domination. This wasn’t just anti-choice rhetoric; it was the incel fantasy made explicit. The message was clear: women’s bodies belong to men, and to him in particular, forever.

It went viral. Over 100 million views. In a sane society, that would be the death knell of his influence. But in this society—where hate is entertainment and cruelty is clout—it became his coronation.

Fuentes doesn’t bother with dog whistles. He doesn’t need to. His followers, self-styled “Goypers,” don’t want polite euphemisms. They want the raw feed of racism, misogyny, antisemitism, and violence. They crave domination framed as political authenticity. Where Trump once said “quiet part loud,” Fuentes screams it into a megaphone and livestreams the echo.

Trumpworld’s Cowardice

According to insiders quoted in the Times and The Daily Beast, members of Trump’s circle—both current and former—refused to speak publicly about Fuentes “out of fear.” Not fear of physical retaliation, but fear of his zealous digital army. Three insiders specifically cited the ubiquity of Fuentes-related clips flooding their feeds whenever his name surfaced.

Let’s pause on this: the architects of the most reactionary government in modern American history are terrified of a man barely out of his twenties because he commands a legion of meme warriors. That’s how fragile they are. That’s how far Trumpworld has fallen.

This is poetic justice, of course. The GOP mainstream normalized harassment of journalists, feminists, immigrants, and anyone outside their reactionary circle. They cultivated digital outrage as a weapon, a way to silence dissent. Now the weapon is turned inward, and suddenly they don’t like the rules of the game.

Alligator Alcatraz and the Turning Point

When a federal judge ordered the Trump administration to dismantle “Alligator Alcatraz”—a grotesque Florida immigrant detention scheme that sounded like dystopian satire—Fuentes pounced. “It’s just getting embarrassing atp [at this point],” he sneered.

For Trumpworld, this was humiliation. For Fuentes, it was oxygen. Every Trump stumble feeds his narrative: that the MAGA movement has been hijacked by consultants and donors, drained of revolutionary zeal, and reduced to a clown show of incompetence. Fuentes positions himself as the heir who won’t make the same mistakes—someone willing to strip away the thin veil of respectability and embrace cruelty in its purest form.

This is why he terrifies establishment Republicans. Not because he’ll out-fundraise them, but because he’ll out-cruel them. And in the zero-sum carnival of the far right, cruelty is currency.

The Roots of the Goyper Cult

Fuentes didn’t appear from nowhere. He was at Charlottesville in 2017, shoulder to shoulder with tiki-torch fascists chanting “Jews will not replace us.” He egged on January 6, riling up his audience before and during the Capitol riot. He cultivated a digital militia of disaffected young men—bored, angry, addicted to grievance, and desperate for belonging.

The so-called “Goypers” represent a generational shift within the American right. They’re not interested in tax cuts or deregulation. They don’t read National Review. They’re not impressed by Mitch McConnell or Paul Ryan. They want identity politics of the blood-and-soil variety. They want a movement that promises domination of women, minorities, and immigrants. They want what Fuentes sells: pure, unfiltered fascism with an internet-native spin.

The cult thrives on irony-poisoned rhetoric, where the line between “joke” and “serious threat” doesn’t exist. That ambiguity is itself a weapon—it gives plausible deniability to Fuentes while radicalizing his followers. When challenged, they can say, “It’s just trolling.” But the trolls showed up in Charlottesville. They showed up on January 6. They will show up again.

Elon Musk’s Megaphone

Fuentes’s resurgence owes much to Elon Musk’s stewardship of X. Musk’s obsession with “free speech absolutism” has always been a Trojan horse for platforming bigotry. By reinstating Fuentes, Musk didn’t just hand him a megaphone—he handed him legitimacy. Every viral post, every engagement metric, every trending hashtag fuels the perception that Fuentes is not fringe but central to the conversation.

Meanwhile, voices targeted by his hate campaigns—women, minorities, journalists—are silenced through harassment or outright bans. The platform amplifies the abuser and suppresses the abused. This is not neutrality. It’s collaboration.

The Monster They Built

It’s tempting to call Fuentes an “aberration.” He isn’t. He’s the distilled essence of Trumpism and the Republican Party’s decades-long courtship of hate. He is the monster they built, stripped of pretense. Trump cracked the door open. Fuentes is kicking it off the hinges.

Trump gave white nationalists permission to come out of the shadows. Fuentes gave them a brand, a community, a livestream to gather around. And unlike Trump, he doesn’t have the instincts of a showman tempered by years of mainstream media attention. He has only the instincts of cruelty, sharpened by algorithms.

Why This Matters

Fuentes is not just some online crank. He represents a potential future for American fascism: younger, meaner, less encumbered by political institutions, and more fluent in the digital culture that shapes young voters. Trump may fade. DeSantis may stumble. But Fuentes is building a movement that doesn’t rely on elections at all. It relies on cultural capture, harassment, and intimidation. It thrives in the shadows and metastasizes online.

That’s why establishment Republicans whisper about him but don’t confront him. They know his army will destroy them online. They know they helped create the conditions that made him possible. And they know there is no easy way to put this monster back in the cage.

The Path Ahead

What happens when a figure like Fuentes decides that politics isn’t moving fast enough? When his followers, primed for violence and addicted to rage, decide to take matters into their own hands? We’ve already seen the preview at Charlottesville and on January 6. The difference is scale and coordination—and Fuentes is providing both.

The longer the GOP indulges or ignores this cancer, the more it spreads. Every silence, every refusal to confront him head-on, is complicity. Every media platform that hands him reach is fueling the fire. Every politician who pretends he’s irrelevant is lying to themselves.

Nicholas Fuentes is not irrelevant. He is the canary in the coal mine, except the canary isn’t dying—it’s thriving. And the gas is still filling the chamber.

Conclusion

The story of Nicholas Fuentes is the story of Trumpism’s logical endpoint. A movement built on resentment and cruelty will always produce someone younger, crueler, and more extreme. Fuentes embodies that truth. He terrifies Trumpworld because he proves their loss of control. He terrifies the rest of us because he shows where this is heading.

This isn’t just about one man. It’s about a political culture that rewards hate, a media ecosystem that monetizes outrage, and a party that unleashed a monster it cannot contain. Nicholas Fuentes is not the future we want. But unless he is confronted directly, forcefully, and relentlessly, he may well be the future we get.
