'Painfully divided' GOP shows 'cracks' amid in-fighting over looming shutdown: insider

Republican lawmakers are showing “visible cracks” as the party scrambles to find solid footing in its strategy to negotiate a budget ahead of a looming government shutdown, a senior House GOP aide admitted in a Politico report Thursday.

“There have been some unforced errors, clearly,” the senior House GOP aide said, speaking with Politico on the condition of anonymity.

Congress has until Oct. 1 to pass a new annual spending bill but, so far, Republicans have been “painfully divided” as to how to approach the ongoing budget fight, particularly with whether or not to include funding for subsidies for the Affordable Care Act, also known as Obamacare. This division has led to inter-party fights on the GOP’s strategy, fights that have begun to raise “internal concerns” within the party.

“Taken together, the visible cracks in the GOP front are raising internal concerns as party leaders face off against Democrats who are largely united behind a plan to focus on health care – particularly an extension of expiring insurance subsidies,” the Politico report reads.

Those “internal concerns” came to a head after President Donald Trump cancelled a previously-planned meeting with Democratic congressional leaders Tuesday at the behest of House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and Sen. John Thune (R-SD), alleging Democrats in a social media post of wanting to “create transgender operations for everybody,” an accusation that “has puzzled even some fellow Republicans,” the Politico report reads.

And, according to four people familiar with the situation who spoke with Politico on the condition of anonymity, Johnson and Thune urged Trump to cancel the meeting, in part because they were worried they might not be included in the meeting.

Trump’s strategy of tying the extension of Obamacare subsidies to transgender surgeries – with there being five states that require health insurance plans to cover gender-reassignment procedures – has left some GOP lawmakers “confused and caught off-guard,” according to Politico, particularly those in vulnerable districts where Obamacare is overwhelmingly popular.

Trump further muddied the water after his White House circulated a draft memo with instructions for federal agencies to create plans for mass firings in the event that a budget is not adopted by Oct. 1, a memo that only created more confusion among GOP lawmakers.

“That alarmed some Hill Republicans who saw it as an unnecessary provocation that, in the words of one, ‘would give Democrats an excuse to vote against’ the GOP-led stopgap – and muddy their message that it was Democrats, not Republicans, who were unreasonable hostage-takers,” the Politico report reads.

Our Analysis:

GOP’s Budget Battle: A Masterclass in Mismanagement

The recent Politico report unveils a Republican Party in disarray, a spectacle of visible cracks and unforced errors that could serve as a case study in political mismanagement. With the clock ticking towards a government shutdown, the GOP’s inability to unite behind a coherent strategy not only threatens the nation’s fiscal stability but also lays bare the internal schisms that have long plagued the party.

The Art of the Unforced Error

“There have been some unforced errors, clearly,“ admits a senior House GOP aide, in a rare moment of candor that encapsulates the party’s current predicament. The phrase ‘unforced errors’ is generally reserved for sports, referring to mistakes made without any external pressure. Applied here, it aptly describes a party shooting itself in the foot, scrambling to find solid footing in budget negotiations. The Republicans, it seems, are their own worst enemy.

Divided We Fall

The GOP’s division over whether to include funding for the Affordable Care Act (Obamacare) subsidies in the budget is more than a mere policy disagreement; it’s a symptom of a deeper ideological rift. This painful division has escalated into inter-party fights, raising internal concerns about the party’s strategy—or lack thereof. When your strategy is so fractured that it begins to concern even those within the party, it’s a glaring signal that a course correction is urgently needed.

The Theater of the Absurd

Adding to the GOP’s self-inflicted woes, President Donald Trump’s decision to cancel a meeting with Democratic leaders—allegedly at the urging of House Speaker Mike Johnson and Sen. John Thune—over fears of being excluded and to propagate bizarre claims about Democrats wanting to “create transgender operations for everybody” veers into the realm of the absurd. This accusation, which has left even some Republicans puzzled, exemplifies a party struggling not just with policy and strategy, but with basic coherence.

Misguided Strategies and Muddled Messages

Trump’s attempt to link Obamacare subsidies to transgender surgeries, a contentious and confusing strategy, has left GOP lawmakers, especially those in vulnerable districts, confused and caught off-guard. This move, devoid of political tact, not only alienates members within his own party but also jeopardizes the GOP’s standing in districts where Obamacare enjoys popularity.

Moreover, the White House’s draft memo instructing federal agencies to prepare for mass firings in the event of a budget non-adoption by October 1st has only added to the chaos. This move, seen by some Republicans as an unnecessary provocation, risks handing Democrats the moral high ground, portraying Republicans as the unreasonable party in this fiscal standoff.

Conclusion: A Party at Crossroads

The Politico report paints a picture of a Republican Party at a crossroads, torn between its ideological factions and hamstrung by a lack of strategic vision. As the GOP stumbles towards a potential government shutdown, one thing becomes clear: the party’s greatest adversary lies not across the aisle, but within its own ranks. Without a unified front and a coherent strategy, the Republicans risk not just fiscal turmoil, but a significant erosion of their political capital. In this high-stakes game of political chess, the GOP seems perilously close to checkmate by its own hand.
