'Let all hell break loose': What's our plan for Trump's military parade?

Thousands gathered in Chicago, chanting anti-ICE and anti-Trump slogans while marching through the city.

This coming Saturday, in response to Trump’s display of military might in Washington (ostensibly to honor the 250th anniversary of the creation of the U.S. Army but also to honor himself and his own birthday), many of us will be protesting in “No Kings Day” events across the nation. See here.

Trump says anyone who protests during his military parade in Washington will be met with “very big force.”

All of which raises some basic questions: What is the purpose of our protests, in concrete political terms? What should our strategy now be in the face of Trump’s emerging police state? How do we avoid playing into Trump’s hands?

I’ve heard four basic answers:

1. Do nothing. Roll over and play dead, and let Trump and his authoritarian regime overreach on their own. (This is the strategy suggested by James Carville, former political operative of Bill Clinton.)

2. Protest peacefully but in large numbers. This will show the rest of America — especially Republican lawmakers — our overwhelming numbers, and therefore our potential political power. Build on these demonstrations to create a network ready for state and local elections later this year and for the 2026 midterms, designed to mobilize and get out the vote.3. Engage in massive civil disobedience. Link arms around courthouses and around Trump’s troops, sit in strategic places, wear Trump masks during his military parade, block bridges and intersections. Force Trump’s troops to arrest us and haul us off. As with Martin Luther King Jr.’s and Mahatma Gandhi’s uses of civil disobedience, these acts can reveal the regime’s helplessness in the face of a disciplined opposition.

4. Let all hell break loose. Escalate the conflict. Don’t recoil from violence. Make Trump’s troops use overwhelming force. Enlarge the conflict so much that it consumes America — necessitating that Americans take sides for or against the regime. Advocates of this strategy say it will reveal the fascism at the core of Trumpism, and hopefully provoke a huge backlash against it.

So: What should be our game plan?

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Strategy Against Trump's Alleged Police State

The article under the microscope today serves as a prime example of the fever-pitched rhetoric and strategic planning that's become emblematic of opposition to the Trump administration. It outlines a growing concern among demonstrators nationwide regarding what's described as an "emerging police state" under Trump, with a focus on the reactions and proposed strategies to counteract this development. Let's delve into the overarching themes and dissect them with the clarity they demand.

The Narrative of Escalation

The narrative starts with a vivid depiction of nationwide protests against Trump's governance, particularly emphasizing demonstrations in Los Angeles, New York, and Chicago. The article then swiftly transitions into the portrayal of Trump's military parade as a vanity project, doubling as an ominous display of authoritarian power. This framing is crucial as it sets the stage for the subsequent call to action, painting protests not just as acts of dissent but as a moral imperative against tyranny.

The Four-Pronged Strategy

The core of the article presents us with four distinct strategies proposed to counter Trump's regime, each escalating in intensity and potential consequences. Let's analyze these one by one:

  1. Do Nothing: This strategy is quickly dismissed within the article as passive acceptance. However, it's essential to recognize the underlying assumption that inaction equals complicity, a notion that oversimplifies the complex calculus of political resistance.

  2. Protest Peacefully but in Large Numbers: This approach is grounded in the belief in the power of mass mobilization to signal political strength. It's arguably the most traditional form of democratic expression, yet the article implies it may be insufficient on its own, given the gravity of the perceived threat.

  3. Engage in Massive Civil Disobedience: Here, the article draws parallels to historical figures like Martin Luther King Jr. and Mahatma Gandhi, invoking the moral high ground of nonviolent resistance. While civil disobedience has a storied place in effecting social change, the article seems to overlook the potential for such actions to be misconstrued or co-opted by opposing narratives.

  4. Let All Hell Break Loose: The most controversial strategy suggests embracing conflict to expose the regime's true nature. This proposal is fraught with ethical and practical dilemmas, seemingly advocating for a scenario where the end justifies the means, a dangerous precedent in any democratic society.

The Unspoken Fifth Strategy: Critical Engagement

What the article fails to address is perhaps the most crucial strategy of all: the necessity for critical engagement and dialogue within the democratic framework. By focusing solely on oppositional tactics, the piece overlooks the importance of fostering a broader understanding of the issues at hand and working within the system to enact sustainable change.

Conclusion: A Call for Strategic Clarity

In the fervor to oppose what's viewed as an authoritarian slide, the article presents a spectrum of resistance that ranges from passive to provocatively active. However, it misses a vital opportunity to advocate for a more nuanced approach that combines protest with policy change, public engagement, and the strengthening of democratic institutions. As we navigate these turbulent political waters, clarity of purpose and strategy will be paramount. Let's not forget that in the pursuit of justice, the means are as important as the ends.
