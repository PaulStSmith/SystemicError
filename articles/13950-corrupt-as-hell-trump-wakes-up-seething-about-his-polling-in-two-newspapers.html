'Corrupt as hell': Trump wakes up seething about his polling in two newspapers

U.S. President Donald Trump gives a speech, during the commencement ceremony at West Point Military Academy in West Point, New York, U.S., May 24, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump lobbed another attack against the nation's two leading newspapers over their polling.

The president started off Friday morning with a fresh complaint about his approval rating measured by the New York Times and the Washington Post, suggesting that "real polls" showed he was actually more popular than ever.

"The Failing New York Times and the Washington ComPost, two 'papers' that have ruthlessly fought me for years, have each done Polls where a vast majority of those sampled are Democrats," Trump posted on Truth Social at 7:20 a.m. EST. "In other words, the Polls, just like their writings, are RIGGED - It is not possible for a Republican to do well in such a Poll! They are 'corrupt as hell.'"

Trump claimed November's election, more than six months ago, offered the true measure of his popularity, although a variety of polls have shown his approval rating drooping steadily since his inauguration, with a few rebounds along the way.

"The real Polls out there have me doing better than ever before, JUST LIKE ELECTION DAY, 2024, where I WON seven out of seven Swing States, the Popular Vote, and Districts by 2,750 to 505," Trump posted Friday. "I won the Election in a landslide! The Fake News Media is Failing Our Nation at levels never seen before. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!"

Our Analysis:

Deconstructing Trump's Attack on Polling and the Press

In a recent spectacle that only he could orchestrate, President Donald Trump has once again launched an attack against the media, specifically targeting the New York Times and the Washington Post, over their polling methods and results. This isn't just another tantrum thrown by the man who once held the highest office in the land; it's a calculated move to undermine public trust in two of the nation's most reputable newspapers. Let's dissect Trump's claims with fierce clarity and biting commentary.

The Allegation of Rigged Polls

Trump's assertion that the polls conducted by these newspapers are "RIGGED" because they allegedly sample a "vast majority of Democrats" is a classic Trump move. It's a claim made without evidence, designed to discredit the pollsters and, by extension, the publications themselves. This tactic isn't new; it's been a part of Trump's playbook since his 2016 campaign. But let's be clear: reputable polling organizations use methodologies designed to accurately reflect the demographics of the national electorate, not to skew results in favor of one party.

The Claim of Unprecedented Popularity

Trump's boast that "real polls" show him "doing better than ever before" and his claim of winning the 2024 election in a "landslide" are, at best, delusional. First, the 2024 election is still in the future, making his claim of victory not just premature but fictitious. Second, a variety of credible polls have consistently shown Trump's approval rating fluctuating, often showing him in an unfavorable light compared to other presidents at similar points in their terms.

The Attack on the Media

Trump's characterization of the New York Times and the Washington Post as "failing" and "corrupt as hell" is not just an attack on these institutions but an attack on the free press as a whole. This rhetoric is dangerous. It seeks to delegitimize the press and sow doubt among the public about the veracity of any news that doesn't come directly from Trump or his surrogates. In Trump's view, any negative coverage is "fake news," a term he has weaponized to an unprecedented degree.

The Irony of the Situation

It's ironic that Trump, who has been caught in numerous falsehoods and misleading statements throughout his career and presidency, accuses reputable news organizations of rigging polls and spreading disinformation. The truth is that rigorous journalism is more important than ever in holding those in power accountable for their words and actions.

Conclusion

Trump's recent outburst against the New York Times and the Washington Post over their polling is nothing more than a continuation of his long-standing feud with the media. By attacking these institutions, Trump is attempting to undermine the very foundation of democracy: a free and independent press. It's a tactic straight out of the authoritarian playbook, designed to erode public trust in any source of information that doesn't align with Trump's narrative.

The real issue here isn't about the accuracy of polls or the integrity of the press. It's about Trump's relentless assault on the truth. As readers and citizens, we must remain vigilant, question sources, and seek out facts. Only then can we hope to navigate the turbulent waters of misinformation that figures like Trump are all too eager to churn.
