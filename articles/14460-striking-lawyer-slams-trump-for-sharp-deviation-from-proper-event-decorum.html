Wearing a red "Make America Great Again" hat, Trump delivered what strategist and lawyer Aaron Parnas called a "strikingly unconventional" school address.

"Trump delivered a strikingly unconventional commencement address today to the graduating class of the United States Military Academy at West Point, blending political rhetoric, off-the-cuff remarks, and personal anecdotes into a speech that deviated sharply from the traditional tone of such events," Parnas wrote on Saturday.

Parnas went on to call out Trump's hat, which he called a "striking departure from typical presidential decorum."

Further commenting on Trump's "trophy wives" comment, Parnas added, "The anecdote set the tone for a speech that oscillated between political commentary and meandering reflections."

Parnas also flagged an instance that "raised eyebrows" among those watching.

"In a moment that drew raised eyebrows among observers, Trump shifted to question the logic of stealth aircraft technology, musing aloud: 'The whole stealth thing—I’m sort of wondering. We shape a wing this way, they don’t see it, but the other way they see it? I’m not so sure,'" Parnas wrote.

The lawyer concluded his Substack post by commenting on the "overly political" action by the president.

"The address, while cheered by many of his supporters, drew criticism from some observers who viewed it as overly political for a military graduation. Others praised the President’s off-script style and emphasis on military pride and national strength," he wrote in the post. "The speech will undoubtedly fuel further debate over the blending of politics and military traditions—a topic that may shape headlines well beyond West Point’s parade grounds."

Our Analysis:

The Unconventional Commencement: Trump at West Point

Donald Trump's recent speech at the West Point graduation has once again shredded the fabric of presidential decorum, replacing it with the coarse weave of political rally cries and off-the-cuff musings. Trump, donning his campaign's iconic red "Make America Great Again" hat, made it clear that the solemnity and tradition expected at such an occasion were to be secondary to his personal brand of political showmanship.

Trump's Misplaced MAGA Rally

Aaron Parnas, a strategist and lawyer, aptly described the event as a "strikingly unconventional" commencement address. It was an event that blurred the lines between a solemn graduation ceremony and a Trump rally. By choosing to wear his MAGA hat, Trump didn't just deviate from "typical presidential decorum"; he drove a bulldozer through it. This was not an oversight or a minor faux pas. It was a calculated move to embed his political identity into an environment that should be apolitical and dignified.

Trophy Wives and Stealth Tech: A Confused Mélange

In his speech, Trump veered into topics as varied as "trophy wives" and the questionable logic behind stealth technology. Such remarks are not just "striking departures" from expected decorum; they are bewildering choices for a commencement speech to a class of newly minted military leaders. The reference to "trophy wives" is particularly jarring, reducing a moment of academic and personal achievement to the level of a tabloid headline. Meanwhile, questioning the utility of stealth technology, in an almost comical bewilderment, reveals a lack of understanding or respect for the advancements and strategies that these graduates might one day be responsible for deploying.

An Overly Political Affair

Parnas also notes that the speech was "overly political" for a military graduation. This observation hits the nail on the head. Military graduations should be a time to honor the hard work, dedication, and sacrifices of the graduates, not a platform for political grandstanding. Trump's inability to separate his political persona from his role as a commencement speaker not only overshadowed the achievements of the graduates but also injected unnecessary divisiveness into what should have been a unifying event.

Conclusion: Blurring Lines and Igniting Debate

The address at West Point, as Parnas concludes, is likely to fuel further debate over the blending of politics and military traditions. It should. The encroachment of political agendas into military spaces is a dangerous precedent. Trump's speech was not just unconventional; it was a stark illustration of his continual disregard for the boundaries and norms that have long governed presidential behavior. The reaction to his speech—ranging from cheers from supporters to criticism from observers—mirrors the broader polarization that has come to define his presidency.

In essence, Trump's West Point address was a missed opportunity to honor the future leaders of America's military. Instead, it served as another reminder of Trump's relentless focus on himself and his political fortunes, even at the expense of time-honored traditions and the accomplishments of others.
