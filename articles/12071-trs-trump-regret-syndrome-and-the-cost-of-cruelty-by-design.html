TRS: Trump Regret Syndrome and the Cost of Cruelty-By-Design

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There is no shortage of political sob stories, but few sting quite like the one captured in a viral video circulating on TikTok and across social media. In it, a young woman, sobbing and broken, recounts her father's struggle to survive stage 4 cancer while detained by ICE. Despite his terminal condition and a prognosis of just six months left to live, ICE officials denied him conditional medical release. She pleads to the camera: "NO ONE deserves to go through this. I truly wish I had made a different choice."

The tragedy here is undeniable. But so is the bitter irony. Because this woman voted for Trump.

This is Trump Regret Syndrome—a phenomenon where people only comprehend the consequences of their vote when those consequences punch through the bubble of privilege and slam into their personal lives. It's the political version of setting the house on fire and screaming when the smoke reaches your bedroom.

Under Donald Trump's presidency, the Department of Homeland Security, and specifically ICE, was weaponized into a machine of dehumanization. Backed by the venomous policy guidance of Stephen Miller, Trump's administration advanced a brutal immigration agenda: family separations, mass raids, prolonged detentions, and a callous disregard for humanitarian standards. And unlike the convenient myths people used to rationalize their votes—"he only targets criminals," "he's just enforcing the law"—the cruelty was never collateral. It was the point.

To those of us watching this TikTok with clenched jaws and blood boiling, it's not just that this woman is crying. It's that her tears are late. Her suffering, however sincere, is the byproduct of a vote cast in complicity. She supported the system that built the cage her father is now dying in. And now, that system has turned its gaze on her.

This is what people mean when they say "elections have consequences." It’s not a slogan. It's a funeral dirge for the voiceless. Every policy, every law, every appointment becomes a tool. And under Trump, those tools were forged into weapons against the most vulnerable. So when someone votes for the candidate who gleefully demonizes immigrants, then turns around and cries when their immigrant father is chewed up and spit out by the very system they empowered—it is not unfair to say: What did you expect?

Let’s talk plainly. Trump didn't hide his immigration agenda. He campaigned on walls, bans, and ICE raids. He mocked asylum seekers. He painted undocumented people as rapists, drug dealers, and animals. He called for Muslim bans and described entire nations as "shithole countries."

So for any voter to plead ignorance after the fact is not only hollow—it's insulting. The information was there. The policies were spelled out. The cruelty was public, deliberate, and cheered by his base. To act shocked when those policies come home to roost isn't just naive—it's willful.

But there’s a deeper layer to this. The regret captured in this video isn’t just about politics—it’s about belief systems unraveling. It’s about the moment a person realizes the system they trusted never had their back. It’s the gut-wrenching clarity that comes when your citizenship doesn’t immunize your family, when your vote doesn’t buy you protection, and when the policies you shrugged off as "not my problem" show up at your doorstep.

The woman in the video pleads for unity, compassion, and action. That’s admirable. But where was that unity when immigrant activists begged for solidarity? Where was that compassion when families were torn apart at the border, or when children died in ICE custody, or when entire communities lived in fear of deportation?

Where was that action when her vote was needed to stop this from happening in the first place?

It is not vindictive to ask these questions. It is necessary. Because if we don't interrogate the pipeline from ignorance to regret, from complicity to victimhood, then we allow the cycle to continue. We allow future voters to believe they can support cruelty without consequence—that they can vote for the boot and only cry out when it lands on their neck.

There are thousands of people—fathers, mothers, children—suffering in ICE detention right now. Many of them will never go viral. Many won’t get TikToks made about them. They will die quietly in cells. Their suffering won't make it into anyone's feed. They didn't vote for Trump. They didn't ask for this. But they’re living it—or dying from it.

So yes, this woman deserves sympathy. But she also deserves scrutiny. Because with that scrutiny comes understanding. And with understanding, maybe—maybe—comes a chance to break the cycle.

But let’s be crystal clear: this system is not broken. It is functioning exactly as intended. Which is why it must be dismantled.

Abolish ICE.
End detention-based immigration enforcement.
And stop electing monsters.
