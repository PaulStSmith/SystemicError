'Mind-boggling': Trump DOJ officials threatened with impeachment over Epstein file drop

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi looks on as she testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C. on Oct. 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) argued Friday in no uncertain terms on MS NOW that the new release of Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files proves that Attorney General Pam Bondi lied to the American people — and separately threatened both Bondi and another Justice Department official with impeachment.

“So the survivors themselves have illuminated the problem, in their view,” anchor Nicolle Wallace said. “With that approach, some of what is in the files are their 302s, their interviews with the FBI. Is it your understanding that the survivors themselves have not been contacted about their own 302s, if they’ve been released?”

“To my knowledge, some of the survivors have not been contacted. Survivors, lawyers,” said Khanna, who co-authored the bill to compel the files’ release in the first place. Now, I don’t know if they have contacted some of the survivors. As you know, there were over a thousand survivors in this case. I certainly haven’t talked to all of them, but at the very least, you would say in a typical process, the Justice Department would say anyone who’s a survivor or their lawyers, we want to consult with you before we’re going to be releasing these files, and we want to hear your interest.”

“I’m less concerned that they haven’t talked to Thomas Massie or me, the authors of the bill. I’m less concerned that they haven’t talked to the Oversight Committee,” he continued. “I’m flabbergasted that they haven’t talked to the survivors or the survivors’ lawyers. And if they have, they should be transparent about this.”

“Look, the reason Pam Bondi has no credibility is six months ago, she stood up there and said, ‘There’s nothing more to see. There are no more documents. We’ve released everything,’” said Khanna. “And now, six months later, her deputy is going on TV saying, we’re releasing hundreds of thousands of documents. And by the way, there are more documents we will be releasing. Well, what changed? Did she lie back then, or what changed? How did they discover all these documents? She needs to answer. If the answer is, well, now Congress has compelled us and we’ve gotten judges who ordered the release of documents that previously we were protecting because of DOJ policy, well, she needs to explain that. But what’s mind-boggling to me is she’s not taking a press conference. She hasn’t explained anything. And this is just eroding trust.”

“Massie and I have never made this against Trump or against the DOJ,” Khanna added. “We literally have spent hours with the survivors. We just want to see justice. We want to see transparency. I’m less hung up if everything is going to come out today. I just want it all to come out and I want them to be heard. That’s really what I want to see happen. And it’s disappointing to me that that’s not the goal of everyone in this process.”

According to The New York Times, Khanna has also warned that the House could hold impeachment hearings for Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, “if it comes to that.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Rep. Ro Khanna’s Statements on the Epstein Case File Release

In a recent interview on MS NOW, Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) did not mince words regarding the newly released Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files, taking a direct shot at the credibility of Attorney General Pam Bondi and the Department of Justice’s handling of the case. Khanna’s commentary cuts through the bureaucratic obfuscation to highlight a disturbing pattern of misinformation and a blatant disregard for the survivors of Epstein’s crimes.

Khanna’s Critique of DOJ’s Transparency

Khanna’s assertion that Pam Bondi “lied to the American people” is a damning indictment of the Justice Department’s operations under her oversight. The revelation that “hundreds of thousands of documents” are being released, despite Bondi’s previous claims that all documents had already been made public, is not just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring contradiction that raises questions about what else might be hidden within the DOJ’s vaults. The discrepancy between Bondi’s statements and the reality of the situation is stark, suggesting either a monumental blunder or a deliberate attempt to withhold information from the public.

The Disregard for Survivors

Khanna’s emphasis on the survivors’ lack of contact and consultation regarding the release of their own interview transcripts (302s) is a critical point that should not be understated. The idea that the DOJ has not prioritized communication with the victims or their legal representatives is, as Khanna rightly points out, “flabbergasting.” It speaks volumes about the department’s priorities and their consideration for the individuals most affected by Epstein’s heinous acts. This oversight, whether intentional or not, is a failure of empathy and duty on the part of the DOJ.

The Question of Credibility and Trust

Khanna also challenges Bondi’s credibility and, by extension, that of the entire Justice Department. The issue at hand is not just about the mishandling of documents or poor communication; it’s about the eroding trust between the government and its citizens. When officials like Bondi are caught in what appears to be a lie, it undermines the public’s faith in their government’s willingness and ability to uphold justice, especially in cases involving powerful figures like Epstein.

The Political Implications

Khanna’s statement that he and Rep. Thomas Massie (who co-authored the bill to compel the files’ release) are not making this a partisan issue against the Trump administration or the DOJ is a noteworthy attempt to keep the focus on justice and transparency. However, the potential for impeachment hearings for Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche highlights the severity of the situation and the political ramifications that could ensue. This is not merely a bureaucratic snafu; it’s a matter that could lead to significant legal and political consequences for those involved.

Conclusion

Rep. Ro Khanna’s scathing critique of Pam Bondi and the Department of Justice reveals a troubling narrative of deceit, neglect, and potential obstruction of justice. The Epstein case, with its connections to high-profile individuals and disturbing allegations, necessitates a level of transparency and sensitivity that, according to Khanna, the DOJ has failed to provide. This situation is a stark reminder of the ongoing battle for justice and accountability, not just for the survivors of Epstein’s crimes, but for all who seek to hold the powerful to account. Khanna’s call for transparency and justice is a rallying cry that should not be ignored, as it underscores the fundamental principles that should guide our justice system.
