‘He didn’t listen to you’: Trump torched for being openly defied in ceasefire demand

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 5, 20255 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the press before boarding Marine One to depart for Quantico, Virginia, from the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 30, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

President Donald Trump is being torched online after being openly defied by Israeli leadership in his call Friday for the Middle Eastern nation to “immediately stop the bombing of Gaza,” with 20 Gazans killed in Israeli strikes over the past 12 hours according to CNN.

“Mr. Trump, it has been one hour since you order Netanyahu to stop bombing Gaza and he didn’t listen to you,” wrote X user “The Palestinian,” who describes themselves as a Palestinian living in Gaza and has amassed nearly 120,000 followers. “Do [you’re] job with him!”

In a Friday social media post, Trump demanded that Israel halt its siege on Gaza based on Hamas’ response to the White House’s proposed peace plan, unveiled on Tuesday alongside Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu from the Oval Office. While the plan saw some last-minute changes at the behest of Netanyahu, changes that reportedly infuriated Arab officials, it was received favorably by Hamas, with some caveats.

However, Israeli strikes on Gaza persisted well into Saturday morning according to major news outlets, strikes that critics online argued were in open defiance of Trump’s demands.

“The most intense bombardment of the last two [weeks] is happening right now in direct defiance of what you just posted,” wrote X user “Assaf,” whose profile says they’re Palestinian and has amassed nearly 19,000 followers.

Others pointed to the leverage Trump has in being able to pressure Israel to halt its bombardment, such as X user “Empire of Lies,” who frequently shares content critical of the Trump administration and has amassed nearly 48,000 followers.

“Trump could immediately end the bombing of Gaza with one phone call to Netanyahu,” they wrote. “Trump has enormous leverage. The only question is? Will he use it.”

Netanyahu was reportedly “surprised“ by Trump’s demand for Israel to halt its strikes, according to an Israeli official who spoke with Axios reporter Barak Ravid on Friday, and viewed Hamas’ response as an outright rejection of Trump’s proposed peace plan.

Trump issued an apocalyptic threat to Hamas earlier on Friday before his call for Israel to halt its strikes in which he set a Sunday deadline for the organization to either accept the peace plan, or face “hell like no one has ever seen.”

Our Analysis:

The Charade of Diplomacy: Trump’s Ineffectual Call for Peace

In a display of political theater that would make even the most cynical observer blush, President Donald Trump’s call for an immediate cessation of hostilities in Gaza was met with the kind of defiance that underscores his waning influence on the global stage. The Israeli leadership, led by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, openly flouted Trump’s demand to halt the bombing of Gaza, leading to a further 20 Gazan casualties over a mere 12-hour period, according to CNN. This blatant disregard for Trump’s directives not only highlights the deteriorating sway of the Trump administration but also paints a grim picture of the so-called peace processes in the Middle East.

The Illusion of Control

Trump’s impotence was laid bare for the world to see when a user, known as “The Palestinian”, pointed out the obvious: Netanyahu had ignored Trump’s command. The expectation that Netanyahu would simply fall in line after a social media post from Trump is as laughable as it is tragic. It reveals a profound misunderstanding of the dynamics at play or, perhaps, a deep-seated delusion about the extent of America’s influence under Trump’s stewardship.

The Farce of the Peace Plan

The irony of the situation is palpable. Trump’s peace plan, unveiled with much fanfare alongside Netanyahu, was supposed to be a landmark achievement. Instead, it has been anything but peaceful. Despite being favorably received by Hamas, with some reservations, the Israeli strikes on Gaza have not only continued but intensified. This contradiction lays bare the farcical nature of the peace plan and the capriciousness of its architects. The last-minute amendments made at Netanyahu’s behest, which reportedly “infuriated” Arab officials, only add to the comedy of errors that this peace initiative has become.

The Misplaced Threats

Trump’s approach to diplomacy, characterized by bluster and intimidation, was on full display when he issued an apocalyptic threat to Hamas, setting a Sunday deadline to accept the peace plan or face “hell like no one has ever seen.” This grandiose posturing is emblematic of Trump’s style: all bark and, as it turns out, no bite. The “most intense bombardment of the last two weeks,” as reported by Assaf, a Palestinian user, happened in “direct defiance” of Trump’s posturing. It’s a stark reminder that threats and ultimatums are poor substitutes for genuine diplomacy and understanding.

The Question of Leverage

The situation raises an important question, one that X user “Empire of Lies” succinctly put: “Will he use it?” referring to Trump’s supposed leverage over Israel. The truth is, leverage is only as good as its application, and in this instance, Trump’s leverage appears to be a figment of his imagination. Netanyahu’s surprise at Trump’s demand further illustrates the disconnect between what the Trump administration perceives its influence to be and the reality on the ground.

In Conclusion

The unfolding events in Gaza are a sobering reminder of the complexities of Middle Eastern politics and the inadequacies of the current U.S. administration’s approach. Trump’s demands for peace, juxtaposed with the ongoing violence, serve as a microcosm of his presidency’s broader foreign policy failures. The open defiance of Israeli leadership not only embarrasses Trump on the world stage but also exposes the inherent flaws in his administration’s understanding and handling of delicate geopolitical dynamics. As the situation develops, one thing remains clear: Trump’s brand of diplomacy is not only ineffective but dangerously naive.
