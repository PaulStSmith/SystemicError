Gulf countries 'raging' they're 'collateral damage' in Trump's Iran bombing campaign

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 4, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Calculated Chaos: Iran’s Strategic Pushback

An Emirates plane with German tourists evacuated from the Middle East arrives from Dubai, amid the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran, at the airport in Frankfurt, Germany on March 3, 2026. REUTERS/Kai Pfaffenbach

Gulf countries targeted by Iran after the U.S. and Israel attacks were reportedly frustrated by the escalating regional conflict, an analyst revealed Tuesday.

CNN’s political and national security analyst David Sanger described how Iran’s objective is to put financial pressure on the countries allied with the United States and Israel in the fallout over the military strikes, all while midterm elections loom for President Donald Trump.

“Look, the Iranians have one big mission here: it’s survive,” Sanger said. “If they’re going to survive, they have to wait out President Trump and Prime Minister Netanyahu. If they’re going to wait out President Trump, it means they need to make it painful enough, long enough that he’s beginning to get toward the elections. You’re beginning to see significant casualties. And it gets back to that question that Steve Witkoff, his chief envoy, asked about a week before the war broke out. During the negotiations, he said, ‘The president is curious about why the Iranians haven’t capitulated.’ Well, the answer is that the whole meaning and purpose of the current Iranian state is to stand in opposition to the U.S., and so it’s not as if, even with the loss of the Supreme Leader, that they’re just going to say, ‘well, this is over.’”

He also suggested what other types of attacks could come next from Iran.

“They’ve got other techniques, cyber [attacks]. We haven’t begun to see anything here in the United States. I hope we don’t. But I wouldn’t be shocked if we did.”

Several Gulf nations, which were not reportedly prepared for the attacks, have also had furious reactions to the conflict behind the scenes, Sanger said. Iran could also be looking at what countries are important to the American economy and aiming to target those economic hubs.

“Well, look, the U.S. is their major patron,” Sanger added. “You know eight months ago, what were we discussing with UAE? With the UAE, how many data centers we’re going to build there? Right. So the Iranian strategy at this point is not necessarily to go after U.S. bases. It’s to go after the financial engines of these countries and say the price for sticking with the U.S. is high in public. They have been very supportive of the U.S. so far. In private, they have been raging, angry that they weren’t consulted on the start of this war to begin with, right? Many of them didn’t even know what the start date was going to be, so they feel like they are sort of collateral damage in President Trump’s confrontation with the Iranians, which many of them don’t think is timed right.”

Our Analysis:

The Calculated Chaos: Iran’s Strategic Pushback

Once again, the geopolitical chessboard is alight with tension, and at the center of this intricate web of conflict and strategy lies Iran’s calculated response to U.S. and Israeli military aggressions. As elucidated by CNN’s David Sanger, Iran’s maneuvers in the Gulf region underscore a sophisticated blend of survival strategy and political pressure, all aimed at the heart of the American and Israeli alliance. But let’s dissect the underpinnings of this situation with the clarity it demands and the scrutiny it deserves.

Iran’s Survival Game: A Matter of Endurance

Firstly, Sanger’s commentary sheds light on Iran’s primary objective: survival. This isn’t merely about weathering a storm; it’s about outlasting adversaries in a geopolitical endurance race. The Iranian strategy, as described, isn’t just reactive—it’s deeply embedded in the fabric of its political identity. Iran’s refusal to capitulate in the face of U.S. pressure isn’t a temporary stance; it’s a fundamental aspect of its statehood. The notion that Iran would simply fold with the loss of a single leader underestimates the resilience and complexity of its political structure. In essence, Iran’s resistance is not a chapter but the theme of its story.

The Double-Edged Sword of Economic Warfare

Sanger’s analysis extends to Iran’s tactical shift towards economic warfare, targeting the financial lifelines of Gulf countries aligned with the U.S. This strategy reveals a nuanced understanding of contemporary warfare, where battles aren’t solely fought with missiles but through economic pressure points. Cyber attacks, as mentioned by Sanger, represent a foreboding cloud of potential disruption that could extend far beyond the Gulf, reaching into the heart of American economic and technological infrastructure.

However, one must question the sustainability and ethics of such a strategy. While it’s clear that Iran aims to leverage economic instability as a tool against U.S. allies, the broader implications for global economic security and civilian welfare cannot be ignored. Economic warfare, especially in the form of cyber attacks, poses a significant threat to the interconnected global economy, potentially affecting billions of lives.

The Gulf’s Silent Fury

The frustration of Gulf countries, as articulated by Sanger, points to a larger issue of communication and consultation—or the lack thereof—between the U.S. and its allies. The reported fury of these nations isn’t just about being targets; it’s about being sidelined in strategic decisions that directly impact their security and economic stability. This sentiment of being “collateral damage” in the U.S.’s confrontation with Iran highlights a critical flaw in coalition politics: the failure to foster genuine partnerships based on mutual respect and transparent communication.

A Misguided Confrontation?

Lastly, the timing of this conflict, as critiqued by Gulf nations, raises fundamental questions about the strategic calculus of the Trump administration. Launching a confrontation without the consensus or preparedness of allied nations not only undermines the collective strength of the alliance but also demonstrates a troubling disregard for the broader implications of such actions. It’s imperative to question whether this path of escalation serves the long-term interests of regional stability or if it merely satisfies short-term political objectives.

In Conclusion: A Call for Strategic Reevaluation

As we peel back the layers of Iran’s strategic response and the broader geopolitical dynamics at play, it becomes evident that the path forward requires more than just military might. It demands a deep reevaluation of how conflicts are initiated, how allies are engaged, and how adversaries are understood. The Gulf region stands at a precipice, and the decisions made today will echo through the annals of history. Let’s hope those in power heed the lessons of the past, recognizing that true strength lies in strategic foresight, diplomatic engagement, and a unwavering commitment to global stability.
