'Sellout': MAGA in meltdown mode after Trump-appointed judge defends First Amendment

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Myth They NeedGorsuch Said What the Text SaysVance and the Clergy of National IdentityThe Noise Is the PointThe Currency of MythsThe Real Lesson

The Panic Begins When the Constitution Is Read Out Loud

Neil Gorsuch, a Trump-appointed conservative justice, said the quiet part that Christian nationalists cannot tolerate: the United States was not founded as a one-religion state, and the First Amendment exists for a reason. That was enough to trigger MAGA meltdown, because the movement does not want constitutional interpretation. It wants sanctified obedience.

The Myth They Need

The source of the outrage is simple: Christian nationalism depends on a fake founding story. If the country was built for pluralism, then the claim that Christianity is the state creed collapses. That is why Gorsuch’s reminder mattered. He pointed to the First Amendment, to religious freedom, and to the obvious fact that the early colonies and the founding were not the property of one sect.

That is not a fringe historical correction. It is a direct threat to a political project that uses selective memory as a governing tool.

Gorsuch Said What the Text Says

The irony is brutal. A justice appointed by Donald Trump, a president who spent years feeding the movement’s grievance machine, had to remind the right that “no established religion” is not an optional decoration. It is law. He even named the principle plainly: equal protection under law, freedom of speech, freedom of religion.

That is what enraged MAGA. Not because the statement was radical, but because it was boringly constitutional. The movement survives by treating constitutional limits as betrayal whenever those limits restrain its preferred hierarchy.

Vance and the Clergy of National Identity

JD Vance’s claim that “Christianity is America’s creed” is not accidental rhetoric. It is an attempt to convert a pluralist republic into a moral hierarchy with a preferred faith at the top. Once that language is normalized, political exclusion becomes a religious duty. Minorities do not just disagree. They become suspect.

That is the deeper logic here. Christian nationalism is not about spiritual revival. It is about legitimizing power by wrapping it in divine language. It gives politicians a ready-made excuse for coercion and a ready-made enemy list for everyone who does not fit.

The Noise Is the Point

The MAGA reactions quoted in the source are not confused overreactions. They are infrastructure. They reveal a movement that cannot survive contact with reality, so it manufactures persecution, invents treason, and declares every contradiction proof of sabotage. One commenter raged that “traitors” are overthrowing the nation. Another claimed the country is already ruled by “anti Americans.” Another said the whole system should be ignored.

This is what authoritarian politics sounds like when it slips its mask. The target is not just Gorsuch. The target is any institution that refuses to certify the myth.

The Currency of Myths

The “In God We Trust” detour is revealing for what it tries to do: smuggle a later political slogan into the founding era and pretend that historical timing does not matter. The source notes the slogan appeared on coins nearly a century after the Declaration and on paper currency only in 1955. That matters because the movement is not arguing from history. It is laundering present-day power grabs through invented tradition.

That is the game: rewrite the record, then accuse anyone who notices of hostility to the nation.

The Real Lesson

This story is not about one justice’s offhand remark. It is about how fragile the Christian nationalist project is when confronted with the actual constitutional order. The movement needs historical fraud, judicial intimidation, and perpetual grievance to keep its coalition together. The moment someone with institutional authority states the obvious, the reaction is not debate. It is panic.

The larger pattern is plain: authoritarian politics cannot govern honestly, so it governs through myth. The antidote is not civility theater or institutional nostalgia. It is naming the power structure accurately, refusing the false founding story, and treating pluralism as a core democratic fact rather than a negotiable preference.
