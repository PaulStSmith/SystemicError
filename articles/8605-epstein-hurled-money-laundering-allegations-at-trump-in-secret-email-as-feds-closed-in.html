Epstein hurled 'money laundering' allegations at Trump in secret email as feds closed in

A newly unearthed 2019 email shows that Jeffrey Epstein was aware that President Donald Trump may have been misrepresenting the value of his assets — an allegation that would get president hit with a $454 million fraud judgment in 2024.

“Trump was a frontman for money laundering, according to Epstein,” wrote Mykhailo Golub, the general manager for the consultancy firm TLFRD, in a social media post on X Sunday. Golub shared an email unearthed in the latest release of a trove of Epstein messages last week.

Trump would be successfully prosecuted in civil court in New York for having inflated the value of his company’s assets, and was ordered to pay $454 million. But in 2019, Epstein appeared to be aware of Trump’s alleged financial crimes.

“Donald doesn’t really own very much, he rents out his name. He puts it on buildings projects etc. for a royalty or piece of the upside. he then claims the asset is,” wrote Epstein in an email to himself, dated Jan. 15, 2019, just months before his eventual death in prison.

“He represents his ‘income’ as the GROSS receipts of his clubs. means nothing at all ZERO. no income as we [know] it. He lists his ‘assets’ and their VALUE – but not the corresponding loans, against it. So no net number, hence meaningless.”

As to why Epstein would write emails to himself, investigative journalist Barry Levine recently suggested that Epstein may have been aware that “feds would be closing in on him,” and that such emails were designed to document his connection to Trump in the event that his computers were seized by law enforcement, and hence, gain leverage should he be arrested.

Trump’s alleged inflation of the value of his assets would come to a head around three years after Epstein’s email in a New York courtroom when New York Attorney General Letitia James brought civil charges against Trump for allegedly inflating the values of his company’s assets to receive more favorable loan agreements.

While an appeals court would eventuallythrow outTrump’s multi-million dollar penalty, the Trump administration targeted James with fraud charges over allegations that she misrepresented her income and assets on a loan document, acriminal probethat critics have described as retaliatory andpolitically motivated

Our Analysis:

Decoding the Depths of Deception: Trump, Epstein, and the Alleged Web of Financial Fraud

In a revelation that reads like a script torn from the pages of a political thriller, a 2019 email has surfaced, casting a long, dark shadow over former President Donald Trump, courtesy of none other than Jeffrey Epstein, a figure synonymous with scandal. This email not only illuminates Epstein’s awareness of Trump’s potentially dubious financial practices but also foreshadows the $454 million fraud judgment that would later rock Trump’s world in 2024. But let’s dissect this with the precision it deserves, shall we?

Epstein’s Email: A Prelude to Scandal

Jeffrey Epstein, in his own words, paints Trump not as a titan of industry, but as a mere frontman in a grand illusion of wealth and ownership. According to Epstein, Trump’s empire was less about tangible assets and more about the allure of the Trump name—a brand leased out for a price, inflating his perceived wealth. Epstein’s assertion that Trump treats “gross receipts of his clubs” as income and lists assets without accounting for corresponding liabilities exposes a financial facade, one that would eventually crumble under legal scrutiny.

The fact that Epstein was emailing himself these observations is a curious detail, suggesting a man preparing for the worst, ensuring that his connections and insights could serve as leverage or a bargaining chip if the federal hammer were to come down on him. It’s a move that smacks of desperation and cunning in equal measure.

The Legal Labyrinth

Fast forward to a New York courtroom, circa 2024, and Trump’s alleged financial wizardry comes under legal fire, with Attorney General Letitia James at the helm. The charges? Inflating the value of his company’s assets to secure more favorable loan terms. It’s a scenario that seems ripped straight from Epstein’s premonitory email, underscoring a potential pattern of deceit in Trump’s financial dealings.

However, in a twist worthy of a courtroom drama, an appeals court later nullifies Trump’s multi-million dollar penalty. But not before the Trump administration launches a counterattack, targeting James with fraud charges in what appears to be a clear cut case of retaliatory politics. Critics have lambasted this move as a transparent attempt to deflect and discredit, a tactic as old as politics itself.

Conclusion: The Mirage of Power and Wealth

This entire saga—spanning from Epstein’s cryptic email to the subsequent legal battles—reveals a tangled web of power, deception, and alleged financial fraud. It’s a stark reminder that in the upper echelons of wealth and influence, appearances can be both everything and nothing; a mirage of success constructed on a foundation that, when scrutinized, threatens to collapse under the weight of its own pretensions.

Trump’s alleged financial maneuvers, as detailed by Epstein and examined in a court of law, serve as a cautionary tale about the perils of overreach and the illusion of invincibility. In the end, the truth, much like the devil in the details of Epstein’s email, finds a way to surface, leaving us to ponder the real cost of wealth and power pursued at any price.
