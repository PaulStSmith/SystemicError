'Stuck with a loser!' Trump sends venomous message to Republican's new wife

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Venomous Vow: Trump’s Wedding Note to Massie

U.S. President Donald Trump talks to members of the press on board Air Force One en route to Florida, U.S., November 14, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

A rebel Republican and his new wife received a venomous note Friday night from President Donald Trump following their wedding announcement.

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), a frequent thorn in Trump’s side, recently announced his marriage to Carolyn Grace Moffa, a former staffer for U.S. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY).

In a Nov. 3 post, Massie wrote on Facebook, “Carolyn and I would like to announce our recent marriage! We were legally married in Kentucky on October 19th by the pastor of the church we have been attending for several months. This weekend we celebrated with close friends and family at a Christian wedding ceremony in Pennsylvania where Carolyn is from.”

Massie said he met Moffa more than a decade ago when she worked for Paul.

Rather than send flowers, Trump — who has repeatedly attacked the Republican over clashes on the Epstein files and the national debt — went on the offensive.

“Did Thomas Massie, sometimes referred to as Rand Paul Jr., because of the fact that he always votes against the Republican Party, get married already??? Boy, that was quick! No wonder the Polls have him at less than an 8% chance of winning the Election. Anyway, have a great life Thomas and (?). His wife will soon find out that she’s stuck with a LOSER!”

Our Analysis:

The Venomous Vow: Trump’s Wedding Note to Massie

In what can only be described as the epitome of pettiness, former President Donald Trump has once again demonstrated his unique flair for nurturing grudges. This time, his target was Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), and the occasion was none other than Massie’s wedding announcement. Yes, folks, you read that correctly: a wedding announcement.

Massie, a Rebel With a Cause

Rep. Thomas Massie, often branded as a rebel within his party, has the peculiar habit of sticking to his principles, a rarity in the swampy mire of Washington politics. His recent marriage to Carolyn Grace Moffa, a connection forged in the libertarian crucible of Sen. Rand Paul’s office, should have been a moment of personal joy and celebration. Instead, it became the latest battleground for Trump’s ongoing war against those who dare to defy him.

Trump’s Tacky Tirade

Trump’s response to the wedding announcement was not a bouquet of flowers, nor a tastefully penned note wishing the couple well. Instead, it was a venomous barb, dripping with the kind of vitriol we’ve come to expect from the former president. “Did Thomas Massie, sometimes referred to as Rand Paul Jr., because of the fact that he always votes against the Republican Party, get married already??? Boy, that was quick! No wonder the Polls have him at less than an 8% chance of winning the Election. Anyway, have a great life Thomas and (?). His wife will soon find out that she’s stuck with a LOSER !”

Let’s unpack that, shall we?

Firstly, the moniker “Rand Paul Jr.” is a feeble attempt at belittlement, suggesting that Massie is nothing more than a copycat of Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), another figure who often finds himself at odds with party line politics due to his libertarian leanings. The insinuation here is clear: Massie is not his own man but merely a shadow of another.

Secondly, the jab at Massie’s electoral prospects (”No wonder the Polls have him at less than an 8% chance of winning the Election”) is both baseless and irrelevant to the matter at hand. Massie’s wedding has nothing to do with his political career, yet Trump couldn’t resist the urge to forecast doom for his adversary.

Lastly, the sign-off is as graceless as it gets, casting aspersions not just on Massie but on his new wife as well. It’s one thing to engage in political sparring; it’s entirely another to drag family members into the fray.

Conclusion: A New Low

In the grand tapestry of Trump’s transgressions, this incident might seem minor, but it is emblematic of a larger, more troubling trend: the utter erosion of civility in our political discourse. Trump has not only refused to elevate the conversation but has actively dug its grave, standing proudly with a shovel in hand.

Rep. Thomas Massie’s wedding should have been an occasion for joy, not a platform for political pettiness. Yet, here we are, analyzing a former president’s spiteful comments about a personal milestone. It’s a sad state of affairs when such venom is spewed so casually, and it speaks volumes about the character of the man behind the words.

In the end, Massie and Moffa’s marriage will likely thrive far beyond the fleeting shadows of Trump’s contempt. As for Trump, his legacy continues to be one of division and discord, a testament to the power of pettiness over grace.
