'MAGA Fyre Festival': Trump voters reportedly starting to notice he doesn't keep promises

The Atlantic's Yair Rosenberg wrote Thursday that President Donald Trump's voters are starting to notice that his promises during the 2024 election aren't manifesting.

" Trump never meant to keep his promises. His voters are starting to notice," wrote Rosenberg after Trump announced that families must make do with less.

Trump announced at a Cabinet meeting that children may have fewer presents during the holiday season.

“Maybe the children will have two dolls instead of 30 dolls, you know, and maybe the two dolls will cost a couple of bucks more than they would normally,” the president said.

Rosenberg recalled Trump's Aug. 2024 rally in Montana: “Starting on day one, we will end inflation and make America affordable again, to bring down the prices of all goods,” he promised.

Just a few days later, Trump claimed, “They’ll come down, and they’ll come down fast."

However, as Rosenberg explained, those promises can't "be reconciled" with his pledge to impose tariffs on consumer goods. At one point, Trump called "tariff" one of “the most beautiful words I’ve ever heard."

The Bulwark's Will Sommer characterized it as the "MAGA Fyre Festival," a reference to the disastrous "luxury" music festival that was heavily promoted but failed to deliver.

"These two pledges could not be reconciled, and once elected, Trump was forced to choose between them," wrote Rosenberg. "The results have disillusioned many of those who voted for him."

He cited CNN polling analyst Harry Enten, who noted just how far underwater Trump's polling is on an issue where he once reigned supreme.

"By pursuing populist protectionism over free trade," Rosenberg noted that Trump has "betrayed" some of his biggest donors. Amazon CEO Jeff Bezos donated $1 million to the inauguration committee and paid $40 million to license and distribute a documentary on Melania Trump. Amid the tariff increase, Amazon is facing "rising prices on many of its products."

The oil and gas sector is another world where Trump garnered support in 2024.

"In 2024, oil and gas interests gave an estimated $75 million to elect Trump," wrote Rosenberg. Trump promised to "drill, baby, drill." The tariffs have hit oil and gas hard, however. The higher costs of components add to the "cratering ... price of oil amid an anticipated economic downturn."

Rosenberg closed by saying that after the first 100 days of Trump's second term, his "approval rating stands at a historic low, imperiling his party’s chances in the midterms, as more and more of the president’s backers realize that his impossible promises were never meant to be kept."

Our Analysis:

The Mirage of Trump's Promises: A Scathing Analysis

In an incisive piece for The Atlantic, Yair Rosenberg lays bare the ever-widening chasm between Donald Trump's grandiloquent promises and the stark, unyielding reality of his actions. Trump's proclamations, once a beacon of hope for his fervent base, now seem as hollow as a drum, echoing with the disillusionment of those who once championed his cause.

Trump's Economic Alchemy: Turning Promises into Disappointment

Trump's audacious pledge to "end inflation and make America affordable again" at a rally in Montana is a masterclass in political overpromise. His subsequent assertion that prices would "come down fast" is now a bitter pill for his supporters to swallow, especially in light of his contradictory embrace of tariffs—a move as economically savvy as trying to extinguish a fire with gasoline.

The analogy of the "MAGA Fyre Festival" posited by Will Sommer is as hilarious as it is apt. Just like the infamous festival promised a luxury experience and delivered chaos, Trump's economic promises have proven to be a facade, leaving his voters stranded on the island of disillusionment.

The Tariff Trap: A Self-Inflicted Wound

Trump's affection for tariffs, described as "the most beautiful words I’ve ever heard" by the man himself, is a policy choice with repercussions that reverberate through the homes and wallets of the American populace. This approach, masquerading as economic nationalism, is in reality a tax on consumers, inflating the prices of goods and undercutting the purchasing power of American families.

The juxtaposition of Trump's tariff policy with his promise of economic relief is a study in contradiction. It's akin to promising to lose weight by adopting a diet exclusively comprising fast food: the outcome is predictably disastrous, and the logic, fundamentally flawed.

The Fallout: Broken Dreams and Economic Backlash

The betrayal felt by Trump's donors and supporters is palpable. Figures like Amazon's Jeff Bezos, who seemingly bet on Trump's economic acumen, now face the repercussions of a tariff-induced price hike—a cruel return on investment. The oil and gas sector, once buoyed by Trump's "drill, baby, drill" enthusiasm, now grapples with the dual specters of increased costs and a plummeting oil price, a scenario as favorable as a hurricane to a beachfront property.

Trump's plummeting approval rating, as Rosenberg notes, is a testament to the widening gulf between his promises and his presidency's tangible outcomes. The mirage of prosperity and affordability has evaporated, leaving in its wake a constituency grappling with the harsh light of day.

In Conclusion: The Emperor Has No Clothes

Yair Rosenberg's analysis cuts to the heart of the Trump presidency's fundamental paradox: the promise of prosperity, delivered through policies that undermine economic stability. The realization among Trump's base that they were sold not a dream, but a fantasy, marks a critical juncture. As Trump's approval ratings sink into the mire, one is left to ponder: how long until the fervor that once fueled his ascent burns itself out?

In the grand scheme of things, Trump's economic policies, characterized by a misguided reliance on tariffs and a blithe disregard for their consequences, stand as a monument to the folly of placing hope in the hands of those who promise much but plan little. The disillusionment of his base is not just a political turning point; it is a stark reminder of the cost of misplaced trust in the realm of politics.
