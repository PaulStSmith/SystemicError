Retired general rains hell on 'obscene' Ashli Babbitt military honor plan

Ashli Babbitt, the pro-Trump Air Force veteran who was fatally shot by Capitol Police during the Jan. 6 Captal riot, is now set to receive full military funeral honors, a decision that has left one retired general livid, and condemning the move as “obscene.”

“I am infuriated that the Air Force plans to grant military funeral honors to Ashli Babbitt,” wrote Retired Army Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling in an op-ed published Saturday in The Bulwark. “She did not die defending the Constitution. She died trying to overturn it.”

Hertling, whose active duty service spanned from 1975 to 2013, recounted his own history of swearing his oath to defend the Constitution, done many times throughout his career. Babbitt, having served in the Air Force for 12 years, and was deployed at least eight times, would have taken that same oath.

Hertling went on to recall his time serving in Iraq, with one memory in particarly standing out to him: the death of his colleague, a “young soldier,” who died by suicide bomber while standing at his post at a military base entry gate.

“The soldier died at his post, saving lives by giving his own; that is service, that is sacrifice,” Hertling wrote.

“...(Babbitt) was not protecting lives at a gate in Iraq; she was forcing her way through windows in the Capitol to stop the peaceful transfer of power, one of the most sacred traditions of our Republic. To pretend that her death deserves the same recognition as the young soldier at the gate is obscene. It is a betrayal of the oath she once swore and a desecration of the sacrifice made by so many who kept faith with theirs.”

Babbitt has become something of a martyr for the MAGA movement, including for President Donald Trump himself, who called the Capitol Police officer who shot Babbitt – Lt. Michael Byrd – a “thug.” Other MAGA-aligned figures have also condemned her shooting.

The military funeral honors for Babbitt were previously denied under the Biden administration, a decision that Air Force Secretary Matthew Lohmeier, appointed by Trump and confirmed by the Senate last month, called an “incorrect determination.”

“After reviewing the circumstances of Ashli’s death, and considering the information that has come forward since then, I am persuaded that the previous determination was incorrect,” Lohmeier wrote in a letter to Babbitt’s family, and shared on social media. “Additionally, I would like to invite you and your family to meet me at the Pentagon to personally offer my condolences.”

The Trump administration has also agreed to pay Babbitt’s familynearly $5 millionto settle their wrongful death suit filed against the federal government.

The Controversial Honor: Analyzing the Decision to Award Military Funeral Honors to Ashli Babbitt

In a move that has ignited fierce debate, Ashli Babbitt, the pro-Trump Air Force veteran who was fatally shot during the January 6 Capitol riot, is slated to receive full military funeral honors. This decision has sparked outrage among certain sectors, with Retired Army Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling labeling it as “obscene” in a recent op-ed for The Bulwark. Hertling's condemnation is not just a whisper in the wind but a roaring gale that questions the very foundations of military honor and the values it seeks to uphold.

The Core of the Controversy

At the heart of this controversy lies a stark dichotomy between the actions that led to Babbitt's death and the principles traditionally associated with military honors. Military honors are a symbol of respect and gratitude for service and sacrifice—a recognition typically reserved for those who have died in the line of duty or after having served honorably. Babbitt, who served in the Air Force for 12 years and was deployed at least eight times, undeniably contributed to her country. However, the circumstances of her death—participating in a riot aimed at overturning the results of a democratic election—stand in jarring contradiction to the oath she once swore to defend the Constitution.

Retired Army Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling's op-ed draws a poignant comparison between Babbitt and a young soldier who died defending his post in Iraq. This comparison is not just a narrative device but a sharp critique of the decision to equate Babbitt's actions with those who have made the ultimate sacrifice in the line of duty. Hertling's argument is grounded in a fundamental truth: the essence of military service is to protect and defend—principles that Babbitt's actions on January 6 actively undermined.

The Political Dimension

The decision to grant Babbitt military funeral honors, previously denied under the Biden administration, has been overturned under the influence of Air Force Secretary Matthew Lohmeier, a Trump appointee. This reversal is emblematic of the politicization of military honors, a development that undermines the integrity of the armed forces and erodes public trust. Furthermore, the Trump administration's agreement to pay nearly $5 million to settle a wrongful death suit filed by Babbitt's family adds another layer of complexity and controversy to the narrative.

This situation is not merely about the allocation of military honors but about the message it sends to the nation and the world. By awarding such honors to someone who participated in an insurrection, the decision potentially sets a dangerous precedent that could dilute the meaning of military service and sacrifice.

Conclusion: A Betrayal of Values?

The decision to grant Ashli Babbitt full military funeral honors is a contentious one, deeply intertwined with political interests and the ongoing struggle over the narrative of January 6. Retired Army Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling’s condemnation of this move as “obscene” reflects a broader concern that this decision betrays the very oath that service members swear to uphold.

In the final analysis, the controversy surrounding Babbitt's military funeral honors is not just about her but about what values and actions we, as a society, choose to honor and remember. It raises profound questions about the nature of service, the definition of heroism, and the sacredness of the democratic principles that underpin the American Republic. As this debate continues, one thing remains clear: the decision has far-reaching implications that extend well beyond the confines of military tradition, touching the very core of America's ongoing political and cultural divides.
