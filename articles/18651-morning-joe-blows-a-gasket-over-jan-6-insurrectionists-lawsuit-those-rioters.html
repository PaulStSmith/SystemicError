Morning Joe blows a gasket over Jan 6 insurrectionists' lawsuit: 'Those rioters?'

By Paulo Santos

The Inversion of Victim and Aggressor in the Jan. 6 Insurrectionist Lawsuit

In a stunning display of audacity that seems almost too surreal to believe, a group of January 6th insurrectionists have taken legal action against the very Capitol police officers they assaulted. This news, revealed in a recent Politico report and subsequently covered with palpable fury by Joe Scarborough on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” represents not just a legal anomaly but a deeply troubling distortion of justice and accountability.

Unpacking the Lawsuit

The lawsuit, filed in Florida, audaciously seeks to represent a class of plaintiffs that includes dozens who participated in the storming of the U.S. Capitol. This group claims grievances against the officers tasked with defending the Capitol—and by extension, the democratic process itself—on that harrowing day. The plaintiffs, it appears, are attempting to flip the script, casting themselves as victims in a narrative where they were unmistakably the aggressors.

Joe Scarborough’s response, a mix of outrage and disbelief, captures the absurdity and the danger of this legal maneuver. As Scarborough highlighted, these are the same rioters responsible for the death of officers and the severe injury of over 140 others. His rhetorical questioning underscored the grotesque nature of the lawsuit: suing the families of deceased officers is not just a legal action; it’s a moral outrage.

The Misdirection of Responsibility

This legal action raises critical questions about the framing of responsibility and power. Firstly, the institutional power at play here—U.S. law and its courts—is being invoked by those who actively sought to undermine it. The decision to file this lawsuit, made by the insurrectionists, attempts to redirect the narrative from their criminal actions to purported grievances that hold no basis in the reality of those events.

The framing of the lawsuit and its coverage potentially shifts the focus from the accountability of those who incited and carried out the attack to those who defended the Capitol. It’s crucial to dissect such narratives to prevent the distortion of the historical record and to maintain focus on the preservation of democratic institutions.

Media’s Role in Reframing the Narrative

Scarborough’s vehement reaction on “Morning Joe” also plays a pivotal role in the media landscape. It serves not only as a counter to the lawsuit’s narrative but also as a necessary call to reason. By vehemently condemning the lawsuit, Scarborough and co-host Mika Brzezinski reject the audacious repositioning of the rioters as victims. This kind of media response is vital in challenging attempts to rewrite the events of January 6th.

Conclusion: Guarding Against False Narratives

The lawsuit filed by the January 6th insurrectionists against the Capitol police is more than a legal oddity; it’s a test of our collective capacity to recognize and reject twisted narratives. In this instance, those who hold institutional power—the courts—must not only judge the merits of the case but also consider the broader implications of allowing such a perversion of justice.

As observers and as members of the democratic society that was directly attacked on January 6th, our role is to stay informed, to question attempts to misdirect responsibility, and to hold steadfast in our understanding of who truly holds the power and responsibility in such critical moments. The absurdity of the aggressor playing the victim must be recognized for what it is: an attempt to undermine the very foundations of accountability and truth in our system.
