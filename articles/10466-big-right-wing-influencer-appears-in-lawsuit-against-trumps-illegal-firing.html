Big right-wing influencer appears in lawsuit against Trump’s 'illegal' firing

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts during an event to announce that the Space Force Command will move from Colorado to Alabama, in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Federal prosecutor Maurene Comey, daughter of former FBI director James, has named right-wing influencer and so-called "Trump Whisperer" Laura Loomer in a lawsuit contesting her abrupt firing, for which "The Justice Department gave no reason," reports The New York Times.

Comey, who was working on cases involving Jeffrey Epstein, Ghislaine Maxwell, and Sean "Diddy" Combs when she was fired from the U.S. attorney's office for the Southern District of New York in July, "calls her firing ... illegal," the Times reports.

The lawsuit, in which Comey seeks her job, back pay and legal fees, names several defendants, including Attorney General Pam Bondi, the Office of the President and Loomer. Loomer called for Comey's firing via a successful social media campaign.

After Comey was let go, Loomer took a victory lap for what she deemed "a pressure campaign."

“This comes 2 months after my pressure campaign on Pam Blondi to fire Comey’s daughter and Comey’s son-in-law from the DOJ,” Loomer boasted in a X post, referring to Bondi.

On Monday, Will Sommer, senior reporter at The Bulwark, posted on X, "One NSC official stood between what appears to be a wildly corrupt deal involving the UAE, AI chips, and China. Then Laura Loomer intervened and got him fired. (She says it had nothing to do with the chip deal)."

“Laura is more trouble than she’s worth,” a White House official told The Free Press in July.

In the lawsuit, Comey said that the U.S. attorney, Jay Clayton, was unable to provide her with a reason for her termination.

“All I can say is it came from Washington,” Clayton told her, according to the lawsuit. “I can’t tell you anything else.”

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Maurene Comey's Firing and the Influence of Laura Loomer

In an age where the lines between political activism, social media influence, and the mechanisms of justice dangerously blur, the story of Maurene Comey’s dismissal from the U.S. attorney's office for the Southern District of New York reads like a cautionary tale. Comey, a federal prosecutor and daughter of former FBI director James Comey, found herself unceremoniously ousted from her position, a move she deems “illegal” according to a lawsuit reported by The New York Times. The grounds of her firing? Murky at best, with the Justice Department providing no clear rationale. Yet, lurking in the shadows of this legal drama is right-wing influencer Laura Loomer, whose involvement adds a layer of intrigue—or perhaps, insidiousness—to the entire affair.

The Defendants: A Who’s Who of Political and Social Media Power

Among those named in Comey's lawsuit seeking reinstatement, back pay, and legal fees are Attorney General Pam Bondi, the Office of the President, and notably, Laura Loomer. Why Loomer? Because she openly called for Comey’s firing via a "successful" social media campaign, later celebrating her departure as the result of her "pressure campaign." This isn’t just about a personal vendetta; it’s a glaring example of how social media can be weaponized to influence decisions within the highest offices of justice and governance.

Loomer's Victory Lap: A Sign of the Times

Loomer's public victory lap on the platform X, previously known as Twitter, upon Comey's firing is emblematic of a deeper, more systemic issue. It's a stark reminder of the power social media influencers wield in shaping political narratives and, more alarmingly, affecting real-world outcomes within our justice system. When a social media influencer can boast about influencing the termination of a federal prosecutor working on high-profile cases, one must pause and question: where do we draw the line?

The Ripple Effects of Influence

The situation becomes even murkier with the mention of another incident involving Loomer, where her intervention reportedly led to the firing of an NSC official who was a roadblock to a "wildly corrupt deal" involving the UAE, AI chips, and China. This is not just about the unjust firing of a prosecutor; it's a glimpse into a potential pattern of interference and influence that has tangible consequences on national and international affairs.

"More Trouble Than She’s Worth"

A White House official's quote to The Free Press in July, stating that “Laura is more trouble than she’s worth,” might be the understatement of the year. This isn’t merely about the trouble caused by controversial statements or political positions; it’s about the real, substantive impact that such influencers can have on the mechanisms of justice and governance.

Conclusion: A Call for Clearer Boundaries

Maurene Comey's lawsuit is not just a personal fight for reinstatement; it's a spotlight on a dangerous precedent where influence, social media, and politics intersect in ways that can undermine the very foundations of justice and accountability. The Justice Department's silence on the reason behind Comey's firing only adds to the opacity of this troubling scenario. In a world where social media campaigns can lead to significant legal and political repercussions, the need for clearer boundaries and transparency has never been more critical. The integrity of our justice system—and indeed, our democracy—may very well depend on it.
