'May be the last time you ever see me': Actor Rob Reiner makes ominous prediction on CNN

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Rob Reiner's Stand Against Trump and Carr: A Battle Cry for Democracy or a Hollywood Hyperbole?

Rob Reiner attends the Los Angeles Premiere of ‘‘Spinal Tap II: The End Continues’’ at The Egyptian Theatre in Los Angeles on Sept. 9, 2025. REUTERS/Aude Guerrucci

Actor and director Rob Reiner told CNN anchor Brianna Keilar "this may be the last time you ever see me" in response to speaking out against President Donald Trump and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr in the aftermath of comedian Jimmy Kimmel's suspension.

"This may be the last time you ever see me because ... there's only a couple of us that are speaking out in this hard way," Reiner said. "And I, we, hope and we know that there will be others. And so I don't think you can destroy a whole industry."

The award-winning director and author of "A Fine Line Between Stupid and Clever: The Story of Spinal Tap," said that it's time for people to stand up against censorship and to protect First Amendment rights in the U.S. He also recently appeared on Kimmel's show to promote his film "Spinal Tap II: The End Continues."

"I'm a friend of Jimmy's," he said. "And what happened to him is unconscionable. It just can't happen."

Kimmel was suspended by ABC and its parent company Disney for comments he made about murdered right-wing activist Charlie Kirk. The networks were widely criticized as bowing to pressure from Trump.

Reiner added: "What's happening now goes way beyond Jimmy Kimmel."

The stakes are high, he said.

"We're in a war. We're in a war right now for our democracy," Reiner said. "Trump has declared war on this democracy."

"He throws people out of the country without due process," he said. "He takes funds that were appropriated by Congress, refuses to distribute them, in clear contradiction to the Constitution, the separation of powers. He's browbeating businesses and universities and law.

"This is way beyond Jimmy Kimmel. This is a man who's a convicted felon, an adjudicated sex offender and business fraud, and he's decided that our 250 years of American democracy is going to be wiped out — and is doing it within the last eight months."

He said that people are going to organize to fight back because Trump's attacks are now personal.

"Beside the fact that I'm in show business, I'm already a libtard, I'm already a pariah... with the Trump administration."

He described how his family, including his wife — a descendant of Holocaust survivors — and his veteran uncle, fought against Nazis and fascism during WWII.

"There are a lot of people who are going to be standing up in the near future ... and you're going to see a big pushback. There's no question about it. He's declared war on the Constitution, on the First Amendment, and we're not going to take it."

He anticipates that Kimmel could also fight back in a major way and that this moment would not just impact Hollywood but communication overall. He warned that if people don't speak up, it could lead to state-run media and further authoritarian rule.

"If I'm Jimmy Kimmel, I'm suing the Trump administration," Reiner said. "I'm suing them for abandoning my First Amendment rights. We're going to see what recourse he has and where what recourse he has and where this goes."

Our Analysis:

Rob Reiner's Stand Against Trump and Carr: A Battle Cry for Democracy or a Hollywood Hyperbole?

Rob Reiner, a figure whose contributions to the cinematic world are undeniable, has recently positioned himself on a battleground far removed from the silver screens - the fiercely contested arena of American politics. In an era where hyperbole often supersedes substance, Reiner's comments to CNN's Brianna Keilar about potentially facing existential repercussions for criticizing President Donald Trump and FCC Chairman Brendan Carr warrant a dissecting analysis.

"This may be the last time you ever see me," Reiner ominously predicted, framing his and a select few others' outspokenness as a rare but vital resistance against what he perceives as encroaching censorship and an assault on First Amendment rights in the U.S. This statement, while evoking a sense of dire urgency, leans heavily into the melodramatic. It suggests a landscape where dissent equals disappearance, a narrative that, while resonant of authoritarian regimes, might be an overreach in describing the current American political climate.

Reiner's defense of Jimmy Kimmel, who faced suspension from ABC and Disney over comments about murdered right-wing activist Charlie Kirk, is painted in broad strokes of a war for American democracy. While the suspension itself is a contentious issue, potentially indicative of corporate overreach or a failure to uphold free speech principles, Reiner's leap to framing this as part of a larger war led by Trump against democratic values is a bold claim. It elevates a specific incident into a symptom of systemic democratic decay.

"We're in a war. We're in a war right now for our democracy," Reiner asserts, attributing to Trump actions that he believes undermine the Constitution and the foundational separation of powers. Here, Reiner's argument gains some traction by highlighting genuine concerns over the treatment of due process, the misallocation of appropriated funds, and attempts to intimidate various institutions. However, the assertion that Trump, described by Reiner as "a convicted felon, an adjudicated sex offender and business fraud," aims to obliterate 250 years of American democracy is a fiery charge that necessitates a careful unpacking of fact versus hyperbolic fear-mongering.

Reiner's personal anecdotes, referencing his family's fight against Nazism and fascism, serve to underscore his emotional investment in the fight against what he views as a slide into authoritarianism under Trump. This emotional appeal is potent, yet it risks conflating historical and contemporary threats in a manner that could dilute the specificity and uniqueness of each.

The call to action, predicting a "big pushback" and suggesting Kimmel could or should sue the Trump administration for infringing on his First Amendment rights, ventures into speculative legal strategy. While it's an interesting proposition, it simplifies the complex interplay between individual rights, corporate decisions, and government influence.

In conclusion, Rob Reiner's impassioned plea is indicative of a larger, deeply polarized discourse in America. His comments, steeped in a mixture of genuine concern and potentially exaggerated foreboding, reflect the heightened stakes perceived by many in the current political climate. However, the effectiveness of his message is contingent upon one's willingness to navigate the fine line between critical vigilance and alarmist rhetoric. In the quest to protect democratic values, the challenge lies in balancing fierce advocacy with factual accuracy—a balance that must be meticulously maintained to avoid veering into the realm of Hollywood hyperbole.
