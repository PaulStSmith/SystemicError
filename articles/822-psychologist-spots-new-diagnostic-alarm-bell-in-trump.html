Psychologist spots new diagnostic alarm bell in Trump

Based on an article by Adam Nichols

Trump’s Pet Projects Are Not a Distraction. They Are the Job Description.

The Source of the Story

A psychologist told the Daily Beast that Trump’s repeated detours into White House construction fantasies, arches, pools, and self-memorializing projects may reflect cognitive decline. The reporting says he has returned to these obsessions 17 times in a little over a month, including during a meeting with Wisconsin farmers worried about commodity prices. The White House responds with insult and denial; Trump’s physician says he is in excellent health.

Who Actually Holds Power

The central fact is not whether Trump is “rambling” in some clinical sense. It is that the president controls the agenda, the microphones, the symbolism, and the machinery of federal attention. When he turns a meeting with farmers into a tour of his decorative grandiosity, that is not an interruption of power. It is power operating exactly as designed: public business subordinated to personal vanity.

The people around him are not correcting the problem. They are laundering it. A spokesman mocks the doctor. The physician certifies fitness. Staff and allies keep the machinery moving. That is institutional complicity, not confusion.

What the Article Nearly Lets Him Get Away With

The reporting leans on the language of symptom and diagnosis, which can be useful, but it also risks softening the political damage. The problem is not only whether Trump’s fixation reflects dementia, narcissism, or both. The problem is that the presidency is being used as a personal stage set while real issues are pushed aside.

This is where media framing often gets lazy. A six-minute detour about the Reflecting Pool can be treated as comic eccentricity, or as medical curiosity. It should also be read as governance by ego: farmers arrive with economic distress, and the president answers with architectural fantasies about himself. That is not miscommunication. It is misprioritization with state power behind it.

Fitness Theater and Loyalty Enforcement

The White House line is familiar: he is “sharp,” “energetic,” and “fully fit for duty.” Trump boasts about cognitive tests as if repetition makes them proof. None of this addresses the substance. It performs certainty to keep the loyalist ecosystem intact.

That ecosystem matters. In a functioning system, public signs of disorganization would trigger internal restraint, rigorous scrutiny, and real accountability. Instead, the incentives run the other direction. Staff preserve access, allies repeat praise, and the president’s own behavior becomes untouchable because the political cost of telling the truth is higher than the cost of pretending.

The Real Pattern

The deeper pattern is not just one aging strongman talking too much about vanity projects. It is a system built to absorb obvious dysfunction without changing course. The stronger the evidence of disarray, the more aggressively the apparatus insists everything is fine. That is how authoritarian-adjacent political culture works: reality becomes a branding problem, and loyalty becomes more important than competence.

If the source is right about decline, then the scandal is not only neurological. It is institutional. A president can drift into fixation, the White House can turn denial into policy, and the public can be handed a reassuring script while the machinery of government bends around one man’s obsessions.

The Larger Lesson

This story is about a presidency that has collapsed the distinction between public office and private self-regard. Whether the cause is illness, narcissism, or both, the effect is the same: state power is used to glorify the ruler, while aides, doctors, and spokespeople provide the cover. That is the system. The symptom is only what makes it visible.
