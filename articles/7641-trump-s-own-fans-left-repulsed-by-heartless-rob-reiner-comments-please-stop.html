Trump's own fans left repulsed by 'heartless' Rob Reiner comments: 'Please stop!'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 16, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Deep Dive into Trump’s Controversial Post on the Deaths of Rob Reiner and Michele Singer

Donald Trump (Photo by Jonathan Ernst for Reuters)

Donald Trump’s Monday morning post on Truth Social about the shocking death of Hollywood star Rob Reiner and his wife Michele Singer started out calling it, “A very sad thing.”

But it quickly took an ugly turn in an attack that left onlookers sickened — including many of the president’s own fans.

Just hours after the two were found in their Brentwood, California home, the president wrote, “A very sad thing happened last night in Hollywood. Rob Reiner, a tortured and struggling, but once very talented movie director and comedy star, has passed away, together with his wife, Michele, reportedly due to the anger he caused others through his massive, unyielding, and incurable affliction with a mind crippling disease known as TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME, sometimes referred to as TDS.

“He was known to have driven people CRAZY by his raging obsession of President Donald J. Trump, with his obvious paranoia reaching new heights as the Trump Administration surpassed all goals and expectations of greatness, and with the Golden Age of America upon us, perhaps like never before. May Rob and Michele rest in peace!”

That did not sit well with some of the president’s fans who urged him to delete the post, with one calling the president “heartless.”

Charlie Kirk supporter Ant fired back, “Didn’t agree with Rob’s views or what he said but no one deserves to go out like that”

“Very tacky Mr. President,” wrote Lighthouser, whose Truth Social account is littered with attacks on Muslim immigrants.

“Terrible post and I am a big Trump supporter. Take it down!!” commented @Laurengerbino.

Gracelover wrote, “I respect you so much, but this was heartless and uncalled for,” which led Medgirl to add, “You could have just said RIP. Love you Mr. President, but none of the other stuff was needed. It’s very sad that we are living in the world we live in right now.

MAGAHarleyChick offered, “Please stop President Trump. You’re my president and I will vote for you always but this is not a good idea or a very smart comment. I don’t know who’s advising you but they are not very smart.”

Mike D who had previously reposted Trump writing, “We must all pray for Charlie Kirk, who has been shot. A great guy from top to bottom. GOD BLESS HIM!” snapped, “This is absolutely insane to write.”

“There are no words for this post, no good words anyway,” offered Iworkforangels.

WMMark suggested, “Dear President Trump. Sometimes saying nothing is better. Don’t let your ego get the best of you. You are just giving the enemy more ammunition and you will alienate fringe supporters. Come on, you are smarter than this.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into Trump’s Controversial Post on the Deaths of Rob Reiner and Michele Singer

Donald Trump’s recent post on Truth Social, regarding the tragic deaths of Hollywood luminary Rob Reiner and his wife Michele Singer, encapsulates a grotesque blend of condolence and vitriol that only serves to highlight the former president’s unyielding penchant for controversy. Initially masquerading as a lament, Trump’s message morphs into an appalling tirade, attributing their deaths to “TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME,” a fictitious malady he accuses Reiner of suffering from due to his political opposition. This repugnant statement not only disrespects the deceased but also starkly illustrates Trump’s inability to transcend personal grievances, even in death.

“A very sad thing happened last night in Hollywood...” thus begins Trump’s post, a sentence that, for a fleeting moment, seems to promise a rare glimmer of empathy and respect. However, this façade rapidly crumbles as he proceeds to assail Reiner’s character and posthumously blame him for his own death, a move that is as baseless as it is insensitive. The audacity to diagnose someone with a so-called “mind crippling disease” like “TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME” in the wake of their death is not only ludicrous but profoundly disrespectful. It trivializes mental health and weaponizes it for political score-settling.

The backlash from Trump’s own supporters underscores the egregiousness of his comments. Even those who align with him politically found themselves repelled by the unnecessary cruelty of his words. From calling the post “heartless” to suggesting it was “tacky” and “terrible,” the responses from his base reveal a rare crack in the unwavering support Trump usually enjoys. Such reactions from his own cohort hint at the deep discomfort with using the death of individuals as a platform for political point-scoring.

Among the critics was a supporter who pointedly remarked, “Didn’t agree with Rob’s views or what he said but no one deserves to go out like that.” This sentiment, echoed by others urging Trump to delete the post, signifies a line that even his most ardent followers are unwilling to cross. The criticism wasn’t just confined to calls for decorum; some highlighted the strategic folly of Trump’s words, with one advising, “Sometimes saying nothing is better. Don’t let your ego get the best of you.” This counsel not only calls out Trump’s narcissism but also pragmatically warns against alienating potential supporters.

This incident lays bare a troubling pattern in Trump’s rhetoric: the exploitation of any event, no matter how tragic, for personal aggrandizement or to settle scores. The invocation of “TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME” serves as a particularly insidious example of how Trump deflects criticism and delegitimizes dissent. By framing opposition to his policies and behavior as a pathological condition, he not only absolves himself of any responsibility but also pathologizes political discourse, further polarizing an already divided nation.

In the grand scheme of things, Trump’s post is more than just a distasteful aberration; it is symptomatic of a broader cultural malaise where political tribalism and personal vendettas overshadow basic human decency. The mourning of deaths should transcend political divides, serving as a moment of unity and reflection rather than an opportunity for derision and divisiveness. Trump’s failure to grasp this basic tenet of empathy and respect is not just a personal failing but a stark reminder of the corrosive effect of his brand of politics on the societal fabric.

In conclusion, the controversy surrounding Trump’s comments on the deaths of Rob Reiner and Michele Singer reveals not just a momentary lapse in judgment but a deeper, more concerning disregard for decency, empathy, and the societal norms that bind us. It’s a stark reminder that in the world of Trump, no tragedy is too sacred to exploit for political gain.
