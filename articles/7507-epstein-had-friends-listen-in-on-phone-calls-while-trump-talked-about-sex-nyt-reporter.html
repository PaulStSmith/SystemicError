Epstein had friends listen in on phone calls while Trump talked about sex: NYT reporter

Jeffrey Epstein and Donald Trump (MS NOW screencap)

Convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein had a habit of letting friends and associates listen in on what were supposed to be private phone conversations with Donald Trump where the two shared tales of “sexual conquests.”

Appearing on MS NOW hours after the New York Times published a bombshell report that detailed a closer relationship between the late Epstein and the current U.S. president years ago than was previously reported, the Times’ Nick Confessore highlighted some of the more sordid details his investigation turned up.

With the Times article zeroing in on the Trump and Epstein “bonding” over their sexual escapades, Confessore told host Chris Jansing that the disgraced Epstein used his relationship with Trump, a wealthy real estate developer, to gain more influence and expand his circle of friends.

“I think that Jeffrey Epstein thought he was Donald Trump’s closest friend for a period,” he told the host. “They spent a lot of time talking on the phone about women and sexual conquests. They spent time at Trump Tower or over at Jeffrey Epstein’s offices nearby on Madison Avenue. They flew on each other’s planes and they partied together a lot. And a lot of parties with women, people who are younger than they are.”

Jansing interrupted to say, “We have no proof that Donald Trump did anything that was criminal or related to the criminal activities that put Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell in prison,” which led the Times reporter to elaborate.

“So we spoke to several former assistants and employees of Jeffrey Epstein’s. And he had a habit of putting Donald Trump and other famous friends on the phone. It was partly a power play and partly a way that he groomed people. ‘See how powerful I am. I can put this person on the phone. I can get them on the phone, I can put them on speaker. We’re friends. That’s how powerful I am.’”

“It was a way that he groomed people and he would put Donald Trump on the phone. They would talk about sex,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Alliance: Trump, Epstein, and the Spectacle of Power

In the latest expose that reads more like a script from a sordid daytime drama than real life, the New York Times has unearthed details of a relationship between Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein that is as disturbing as it is revelatory. The bombshell report, dissected with fervor on MS NOW, highlights not just a friendship but a partnership in depravity, where tales of “sexual conquests” were not just shared but broadcasted to an audience, courtesy of Epstein.

Epstein’s Power Play and the Cult of Celebrity

Jeffrey Epstein, a name now synonymous with the vilest forms of sexual trafficking, apparently saw Donald Trump, then a wealthy real estate developer, not just as a friend but as a trophy. Nick Confessore from the Times points out that Epstein viewed his relationship with Trump as a tool to gain influence and widen his sinister circle. This wasn’t friendship; it was a calculated power play. Epstein, by flaunting his association with Trump, essentially used it as a way to groom and impress others about his clout. “See how powerful I am,” Epstein seemed to say, as he put Trump on speakerphone to discuss their sexual exploits. This was not just about bonding; it was a chilling display of how societal elites can manipulate their status for nefarious purposes.

The Spectacle of Misogyny

The discussions between Trump and Epstein, as detailed by the Times, weren’t mere locker room banter; they were public performances of misogyny. These were powerful men, using their status not just to objectify women but to do so as a form of entertainment for those in their immediate circle. It’s a grotesque reminder of how women are viewed in certain echelons of power: not as individuals, but as conquests to be discussed and discarded at will.

A Complicit Society?

What is equally disturbing about this revelation is the environment that allowed it to flourish. Epstein’s employees, aware of these conversations and the nature of these gatherings, were part of a system that normalized this behavior. The societal structures that enable men like Epstein and Trump to boast about their sexual conquests with impunity are the same structures that silence their victims. It’s a glaring indictment of a society that often turns a blind eye to the excesses of the wealthy and powerful, especially when women are the collateral damage.

The Teflon Men

While the Times reporter made it clear that there is no proof Donald Trump engaged in criminal activities akin to Epstein’s, the very nature of his association with Epstein is damning. It’s a testament to the Teflon nature of men like Trump, who, despite being embroiled in countless scandals, manage to dodge significant repercussions. It raises a critical question: How do men like Trump continue to evade accountability? The answer, though complex, lies in the intersection of wealth, power, and gender dynamics that continue to dominate the American societal landscape.

In Conclusion

The bond between Trump and Epstein, as illustrated by the Times, is not just a story of friendship gone awry; it’s a window into a world where power corrupts absolutely, and where women’s bodies are the currency of male bonding. It’s a stark reminder that in the quest for influence, morality is often the first casualty. And while Epstein may no longer be a part of this world, the system that enabled him and his “closest friend” to thrive remains very much intact.
