Last month, the White House published a presidential memorandum titled “Restoring Law and Order in Memphis,” which orders 13 federal government agencies and offices to coordinate. It states the “objective shall be to end street and violent crime in Memphis to the greatest possible extent,” and lists actions including “hypervigilant policing, aggressive prosecution, complex investigations, financial enforcement, and large-scale saturation of besieged neighborhoods with law enforcement personnel.”

Memphis is among the most violent cities in the country.

“The Memphis Safe Task Force began operations on September 29, with the National Guard joining efforts this past Friday,” Action5 News reported on Monday.

Lee told reporters on Tuesday, “We’ve just begun,” and said the program will last “for months,” before stating that in some form it will continue “forever.”

“We do know that this is gonna last for months, and we have just begun. In fact, I will tell you that it will last forever,” he said. “Because what we believe will happen is the numbers of law enforcement agents from different agencies will change, depending on the mission at the moment.”

He described a “collaboration that is happening right now between U.S. Marshal Service and the Memphis Police Department, and the FBI, and the DEA, and the Tennessee Highway Patrol.”

Our Analysis:

The “Memphis Safe Task Force”: A Permanent Solution or a Perpetual Problem?

In a move that sounds ripped from the pages of a dystopian novel, Tennessee Republican Gov. Bill Lee has declared the “Memphis Safe Task Force” a never-ending operation. This decision comes in the wake of a presidential memorandum “Restoring Law and Order in Memphis,” which mandates a militaristic and heavy-handed approach to crime in one of America’s most violent cities. The plan, backed by an unholy alliance of 13 federal agencies, including the National Guard, promises “hypervigilant policing, aggressive prosecution, and a large-scale saturation of besieged neighborhoods with law enforcement personnel.” In essence, it’s a blueprint for a police state, with Memphis as its testing ground.

Let’s dissect this draconian strategy and the unsettling rhetoric surrounding its implementation.

The Promise of Permanence

Gov. Lee’s assertion that this program “will last forever” is not just troubling; it’s a stark admission that the government is willing to implement an unending loop of surveillance and control over its citizens. This isn’t a policy aimed at addressing the root causes of crime, such as poverty, education, or lack of employment opportunities. Instead, it’s an endorsement of brute force and perpetual occupation as a means to maintain order.

Collaboration or Occupation?

The governor boasts of a “collaboration” between various law enforcement agencies, including the U.S. Marshal Service, the Memphis Police Department, the FBI, DEA, and the Tennessee Highway Patrol. However, one must question whether this is truly a collaborative effort aimed at enhancing community safety or a heavy-handed occupation designed to suppress and control.

The Critic’s Corner

Criticism of Gov. Lee’s announcement has been swift and severe, with accusations of authoritarianism and fascism being levied by experts and commentators. Authoritarianism expert Ruth Ben-Ghiat’s characterization of the initiative as a “domestic forever war” and political scientist Dr. Norman Ornstein’s blunt assessment of it as “pure fascist” cannot be taken lightly. These are serious charges that reflect deep concerns about the erosion of civil liberties and the militarization of law enforcement.

A Forever Occupation?

Columnist and “recovering attorney” Wajahat Ali’s comment about a “forever occupation” encapsulates the fear that Memphis could become a city under siege by its government, a place where the presence of military and police forces becomes a permanent fixture. This scenario raises profound questions about the balance between ensuring public safety and preserving the freedoms and rights of citizens.

Conclusion

The “Memphis Safe Task Force” and the rhetoric of perpetual law enforcement presence sets a dangerous precedent. It suggests a future where heavy-handed policing and military involvement in civilian life become normalized under the guise of fighting crime. The implications for civil liberties, social justice, and the very fabric of community life in Memphis and potentially beyond are dire.

As we continue to monitor this situation, it’s crucial to remain vigilant against the encroachment of authoritarian practices wrapped in the flag of public safety. The question remains: at what point does the cure become worse than the disease? In Memphis, it seems, we are dangerously close to finding out.
