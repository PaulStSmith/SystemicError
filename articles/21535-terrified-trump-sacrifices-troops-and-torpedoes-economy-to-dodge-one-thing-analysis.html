Terrified Trump sacrifices troops and torpedoes economy to dodge 'one thing': analysis

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s War by Ego, Backed by State Power

Trump Owns the Escalation

The source is straightforward about the basic fact pattern: Donald Trump has kept escalating an unpopular war against Iran even after his own top military adviser warned that the plan was failing. He then tried to recast Gen. Dan Caine’s view, while reporting elsewhere said Caine privately believed the opposite. That is the context. The real story is not confusion. It is a president using institutional power to force a failing course forward.

The Lie Is the Policy

Trump’s claim that the Joint Chiefs chairman believed the war would be “easily won” matters because it shows how the White House launders failure into bravado. According to the source, Trump was told the plan had failed and responded by ordering more of it and lying about it. That is not a strategic disagreement. It is an attempt to overwrite reality with authority.

Power, Not Misunderstanding

The article’s sharpest point is also its most important one: stopping would require Trump to admit he was wrong, and he would rather keep escalating than do that. That is the governing logic here. The decision-maker is not trapped by events; he is trapped by vanity. And because he controls the machinery of state, his private refusal to absorb embarrassment becomes public policy, with consequences for the economy and for U.S. casualties.

The Cost Gets Externalized

Valdes-Rodriguez’s language is blunt, and the underlying charge is accurate: the burden of Trump’s self-protection is dumped onto everyone else. Families bear the risk. The economy absorbs the damage. Soldiers and civilians live with the consequences. This is what authoritarian politics looks like in practice: the leader’s emotional fragility is treated as a national operating principle.

The Pattern Behind the Story

This is bigger than one president’s inability to say “I was wrong.” It is a demonstration of how personal grievance becomes state violence when institutions fail to constrain it. The military can warn. The press can report. The evidence can accumulate. None of that matters if the person at the top can still order escalation and call it resolve. The danger is not just bad judgment. It is a political system that keeps giving one man the power to convert ego into catastrophe.
