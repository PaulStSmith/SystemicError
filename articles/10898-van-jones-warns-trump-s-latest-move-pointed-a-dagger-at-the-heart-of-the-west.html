Van Jones warns Trump's latest move pointed a 'dagger' at the 'heart of the West'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump speaks as Benjamin Netanyahu waves at the White House. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

President Donald Trump's lack of action toward some global strongmen has created a situation where a dagger is pointed "at the heart of the West," according to one analyst.

Van Jones, a former White House advisor in the Obama administration, joined CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip" to discuss a meeting held on Tuesday between the leaders of Russia, India, and China. Jones warned that the meeting should concern every American, and that Trump's praise of the leaders could become historically significant.

"I think we're going to look back on today, historically, as a very big deal because that image of Xi Jinping with Putin with Modi from India, with the leader of Iran with the leader of North Korea, that should send a chill down the spine of every American," Jones said. "That is a new world order."

"They call it multipolar," Jones said. "I call it 'the West is now in a box.'"

On Tuesday, China's Xi Jinping, Russia's Vladimir Putin, and North Korea's Kim Jong Un jointly attended a military parade in Beijing that some analysts have described as a rebuke of the Western world. The parade capped off a week of meetings between global leaders.

Trump commented on Truth Social about the meeting during the parade.

"May President Xi and the wonderful people of China have a great and lasting day of celebration. Please give my warmest regards to Vladimir Putin, and Kim Jong-un, as you conspire against The United States of America,” Trump wrote.

Jones warned that Trump may be too late to respond to what's coming.

"That image is a feather in the cap of Putin, and a dagger pointed at the heart of the West," Jones said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's "Dagger at the Heart of the West"

In what can only be described as a chilling display of geopolitical negligence, President Donald Trump's recent non-actions and bewildering accolades towards some of the world's most notorious strongmen have, according to Van Jones, former White House advisor in the Obama administration, placed a "dagger at the heart of the West." This commentary emerged during a segment on CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip," focusing on a high-stakes meeting between the leaders of Russia, India, and China, alongside the inclusion of Iran and North Korea's top figures.

A New World Order?

Jones's alarm over the gathering of Xi Jinping, Vladimir Putin, Narendra Modi, alongside the leaders of Iran and North Korea is not just theatrical rhetoric. It is a stark warning against a burgeoning alliance that could destabilize the already fragile balance of global power. Describing the scenario as a "new world order," Jones succinctly captures the essence of a multipolar world increasingly indifferent, if not outright hostile, to Western interests and values. The imagery of these leaders, shoulder to shoulder, is not just a diplomatic photo op but a signal of shifting allegiances and emerging threats.

Trump's Peculiar Response

In an almost surreal twist, Trump's response to this geopolitical chess move was to offer "warmest regards" via Truth Social to Xi and Putin, and even to Kim Jong-un, as they "conspire against The United States of America." This statement, dripping with a blend of naiveté and misplaced camaraderie, fails to grasp the gravity of the situation. It is a stark reminder of Trump's often criticized cozy relationship with leaders who represent the antithesis of democratic values.

Too Little, Too Late?

Jones's assertion that Trump may be "too late" to counteract the implications of this alliance is a dire prognosis for Western influence and security. The feather in Putin's cap, as Jones puts it, is not merely a diplomatic win but a strategic advantage in the global power play. Trump's lackadaisical approach to these developments, coupled with his bizarre expressions of goodwill towards authoritarian leaders, serves to embolden adversaries at the expense of Western unity and strength.

Conclusion

The scenario described by Van Jones and the subsequent response by Trump underscore a troubling trend in the erosion of American influence on the global stage. This isn't just a question of politics; it's a matter of national and global security. The formation of a bloc comprising China, Russia, India, Iran, and North Korea, met with Trump's ineffective and inappropriate reactions, is a red flag for the future of international order and democracy.

In essence, Trump's actions, or lack thereof, coupled with his perplexing statements, do not just risk alienating allies and emboldening foes but potentially signal a seismic shift in global power dynamics that could have lasting, unfavorable consequences for the West. It's a stark reminder that leadership on the world stage requires more than platitudes; it demands strategic foresight, unwavering commitment to democratic principles, and a robust response to authoritarian advances.
