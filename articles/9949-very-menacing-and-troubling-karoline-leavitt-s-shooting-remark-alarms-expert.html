'Very menacing and troubling': Karoline Leavitt's shooting remark alarms expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Reaction to the Mormon Temple Attack: A Dive into Dangerous Rhetoric

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt speaks during a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Comments made by White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt after a supporter of Donald Trump attacked a Mormon temple on Sunday alarmed an expert on Christian nationalism who spoke with The New Republic’s Greg Sargent.

In an interview published Tuesday morning, Sargent singled out Leavitt for doubling down on Donald Trump’s knee-jerk response that it was an attack on Christianity, that led her to tell reporters a day earlier, “And as the president rightfully put, in his Truth Social yesterday, this appears to be yet another targeted attack on Christians and the Trump administration is fully committed to not only investigating these crimes, but prosecuting them to the fullest extent of the law.”

According to author Sara Posner, “There’s a lot of problems with the top official in the United States—the president—and his surrogate, Karoline Leavitt, saying these things publicly before we know more about what happened.”

Noting the president’s war on free speech in academia and in the media, Posner suggested a new war may be in the offing for any criticism of Christians or Christianity.

“After saying this appears to be yet another targeted attack on Christians, she then said the Trump administration is fully committed to not only investigating these crimes, but prosecuting them. I think that’s highly suggestive language,” Sargent prompted his guest.

“Well given their track history, so given how during his campaign he promised a task force to combat anti-Christian bias, and I think they established such a task force, although I’m not sure what it has been doing—it’s very menacing and troubling,” Posner admitted. “Because we know from past things that they’ve done in their supposed combating of anti-Semitism, for example, that they are keen on violating people’s First Amendment rights in their supposed quest to end anti-Semitism, right?”

She elaborated that it goes deeper than that.

“And so if they’re going to use law enforcement to combat what they see as anti-Christian bias—and they see that as preventing crimes motivated by anti-Christian bias—I mean, you can’t… you know, somebody saying something that you think is anti-Christian. And remember, they think it’s anti-Christian to, say, disagree with a pastor who’s against same-sex marriage. They think that that’s anti-Christian to say that,” she explained.

Admitting she doesn’t know exactly what they have in store, she suggested, “Given their track record, and given how they’re over-policing and militarizing the policing of cities, given how they have no compunction about violating people’s First Amendment rights—taking these things together—it’s very troubling that they are talking about this.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Reaction to the Mormon Temple Attack: A Dive into Dangerous Rhetoric

The White House press secretary, Karoline Leavitt’s comments in the wake of an attack on a Mormon temple, as dissected by Greg Sargent of The New Republic, spotlight a disturbing trend in the rhetoric emanating from the highest offices of power. By framing the assault as an attack on Christianity and pledging the Trump administration’s full force in prosecuting such crimes, the administration not only jumps the gun but potentially signals a broader, more menacing agenda.

The Premature Conflation with Christian Persecution

Leavitt’s echo of Donald Trump’s “knee-jerk” response to label the incident as an attack on Christianity before the facts are fully known is more than just an impulsive misstep; it’s a dangerous conflation. Sara Posner’s observations highlight the peril in such high-level officials making sweeping declarations that frame the incident within a narrative of Christian persecution without a thorough understanding of the specifics. This isn’t just about mischaracterizing events; it’s about the implications such characterizations carry.

The Veiled Threat of a New War on Criticism

The administration’s commitment to “investigating” and “prosecuting” these perceived attacks on Christians to the “fullest extent of the law” might sound like standard law-and-order rhetoric. However, as Posner points out, given the historical context of how this administration has previously weaponized its power against perceived enemies, this language is not only suggestive but downright menacing. The reference to a previously promised task force to combat anti-Christian bias, while seemingly benign, takes on a sinister tone when viewed through the lens of past actions that have shown little regard for First Amendment rights.

The Slippery Slope of Defining Anti-Christian Bias

Posner’s concerns about the broad and often arbitrary definition of what constitutes anti-Christian bias under this administration are well-founded. The leap from actual hate crimes to labeling dissenting opinions on theological issues as anti-Christian not only muddies the waters of what constitutes bias but also threatens the very core of free speech. The implication that disagreeing with a pastor’s stance against same-sex marriage could be criminalized under the guise of combating anti-Christian bias is a chilling thought.

The Troubling Context of Over-Policing

The mention of over-policing and the militarization of law enforcement in cities adds another layer of concern to the administration’s rhetoric. When coupled with a history of disregarding First Amendment rights, the prospect of using law enforcement to fight so-called anti-Christian bias suggests a move towards an even more authoritarian approach to governance. This isn’t just about protecting citizens; it’s about silencing dissent.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Path Forward

The analysis of Leavitt’s comments and the administration’s stance in the aftermath of the Mormon temple attack reveals a troubling trajectory. By framing the incident within a narrative of Christian persecution and hinting at a hardline approach to combating perceived anti-Christian bias, the administration is not just overstepping but is potentially setting the stage for a broader assault on free speech and dissent. The implications of such rhetoric, coupled with a track record of weaponizing power against perceived adversaries, should alarm anyone concerned with the principles of democracy and the protection of individual rights.
