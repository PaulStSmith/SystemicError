‘That’s a little different’: GOP strategist gets stumped defending Trump boat strikes

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 19, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Rationale Behind Trump’s Military Strikes: A Critical Analysis

Republican strategist Tim Parrish (right) appears on CNN, Oct. 18, 2025. (Screengrab/CNN)

Republican strategist Tim Parrish struggled to defend President Donald Trump’s military strikes on suspected drug trafficking boats Saturday after being confronted with facts from CNN’s Abby Phillip that flew in the face of the Trump administration’s purported rationale for the military campaign.

“Any family in this country who’s lost someone to fentanyl use would absolutely agree with President Trump that we are, in fact, at war with the cartels, and they would appreciate the president taking every action he can to stop the flow of drugs into this country,” Parrish said during an appearance on CNN’s “Table for Five” Saturday.

Trump has ramped up attacks on suspected drug traffickers, announcing this week that another six people were killed after he ordered a strike on a sea vessel in the Caribbean Sea heading toward the United States. This follows other military strikes in the region ordered by Trump in what critics have condemned as amounting to murder.

The Trump administration’s rationale for the strikes is that Trump had designated drug cartels as foreign terrorists via an executive order, and therefore, the execution-style killings – without affording the suspected drug traffickers due process – is warranted, given that they pose an immediate threat to national security.

Phillip, however, accurately noted that Mexico is the overwhelming “dominant source” of fentanyl in the United States, the leading cause of overdose deaths in the nation in 2024.

“If Trump is saying we are in a war against the cartels to stop drug trafficking, that war with be with the Mexican cartels, not with fishermen coming on boats out of Venezuela, so I’m just asking for a real justification that I think we ought to ask for every president when they start dropping bombs around the world for what is the actual reason?” Phillip said.

“Do we actually know who these people are, do we know that they’re even Venezuelans? Because some of them, reportedly, were from other countries, and when we start bombing citizens of other countries, we don’t want other countries bombing our citizens in international waters, so what are the rules of engagement here?”

Parrish attempted to dismiss Phillip’s statement in arguing that Americans weren’t “bringing drugs from the United States into South America by boat.”

“On the Mexican border, the president has designated several Mexican drug cartels as a terrorist of our country, and there are special forces elements on the ground addressing those,” Parrish said.

“But we’re not bombing them!” Phillip fired back.

“Well, because that’s a little bit of a different scenario when we talk about bombing right next door in Mexico,” Parrish said.

Our Analysis:

The Rationale Behind Trump’s Military Strikes: A Critical Analysis

In a recent CNN interview, Republican strategist Tim Parrish found himself in a precarious position trying to defend President Donald Trump’s military actions against suspected drug trafficking boats. This move by Trump has sparked considerable controversy and debate, highlighting a significant disconnect between the administration’s stated objectives and the reality of the drug crisis in the United States.

The Justification for Military Strikes

Parrish’s defense hinged on the argument that the United States is “at war with the cartels,” a statement aimed at justifying the president’s decision to order military strikes without due process. This rationale is based on Trump’s designation of drug cartels as foreign terrorists via an executive order. However, the logic crumbles under scrutiny. The administration’s actions, which include execution-style killings in the Caribbean Sea, have been criticized as tantamount to murder. The fact that these strikes have been carried out without affording the suspects due process raises serious ethical and legal questions.

Misdirected Focus?

CNN’s Abby Phillip astutely points out the discrepancy in the Trump administration’s approach. If the so-called war is against drug cartels to stop the flow of drugs like fentanyl into the United States, then the focus should arguably be on the dominant source of these drugs: Mexico. Instead, the Trump administration has targeted individuals on boats coming from Venezuela, a move that seems misaligned with the stated objective. Phillip’s questioning of the real justification behind these military strikes is both necessary and revealing. It underscores the need for transparency and accountability in the administration’s actions.

A Troubling Approach to International Engagement

Phillip’s inquiries about the rules of engagement and the implications of bombing citizens of other countries in international waters are particularly poignant. These actions not only raise ethical concerns but also jeopardize international norms and relationships. Parrish’s attempt to dismiss these valid concerns by contrasting the situation with drug trafficking across the Mexican border is unconvincing. The argument that bombing is not pursued in Mexico because it’s “a little bit of a different scenario” is a flimsy justification for a highly aggressive and potentially unlawful military strategy.

Conclusion: A Call for Clarity and Accountability

The Trump administration’s military strikes against suspected drug traffickers are a contentious issue that raises more questions than answers. The justification for such actions, as defended by Parrish, is tenuous at best and ignores the complex realities of international drug trafficking. The focus on Venezuela, as opposed to the more significant threat posed by Mexican cartels, suggests a misalignment between stated objectives and actual tactics. Furthermore, the disregard for due process and international norms is alarming.

As we dissect the administration’s approach to combating drug trafficking, it becomes evident that a more transparent, reasoned, and lawful strategy is needed. The fight against drug cartels and the opioid crisis in the United States is a serious issue that demands serious solutions. Military strikes without clear justification or adherence to legal standards are not the answer. The administration owes the American public, and the international community, a more coherent and ethical strategy in addressing this pressing challenge.
