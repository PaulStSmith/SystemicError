This scumbag's shame shows how low the GOP has plunged

Based on an article by John Casey

Paulo SantosOctober 1, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

RawStory reports that Sen. Eric Schmitt used a Senate Judiciary hearing to call former special counsel Jack Smith a “total dirtbag,” a “villain,” and an alleged perjurer. Schmitt presented messages that he said showed Smith traveling to Atlanta to coordinate with local prosecutors, but the purported evidence confused the Atlanta Hawks with the Iowa Hawkeyes; according to the outlet, Smith had attended a college basketball game in Maryland.

Sen. Amy Klobuchar said Schmitt should apologize. He later refused during an appearance on The Charlie Kirk Show. RawStory places the episode alongside other recent cases of lawmakers publicly cursing at witnesses, reporters and constituents, arguing that conduct which once provoked institutional backlash has become routine.

Power behind the insult

Schmitt was not a heckler outside the building. He was a United States senator using a Judiciary Committee hearing, with its authority and public reach, to accuse a former federal prosecutor of misconduct.

That distinction matters. The central failure was not vulgarity. It was a powerful official converting an evidentiary mistake into a public allegation of collusion and perjury. The insult helped sell the charge, but the institutional setting gave it weight.

The evidence collapsed; the accusation remained

Once Schmitt’s mascot mix-up was exposed, the responsible act would have been to withdraw the allegation and correct the record. His reported refusal to apologize preserved the attack after its stated foundation had failed.

That is how political disinformation often survives correction: the dramatic accusation receives the hearing, the poster board and the epithet; the factual collapse becomes a punchline. Smith remains associated with wrongdoing in the audience’s memory even though the episode, as RawStory describes it, did not establish the alleged Atlanta coordination.

Vulgarity is the camouflage

RawStory emphasizes the Republican Party’s descent into routine profanity, citing episodes involving Reps. Lauren Boebert, Nancy Mace and Derrick Van Orden, as well as Sens. Bernie Moreno, Markwayne Mullin and John Kennedy. It also notes that Democratic Rep. Mark Pocan frequently used the same expletive on social media.

But a profanity count can flatten the political stakes. Cursing online is not equivalent to using congressional authority to brand a witness a criminal on defective evidence. Treating every obscenity as interchangeable misdirects attention from who holds power, where the statement was made and what official accusation it was meant to advance.

Contempt as political method

The pattern described here is larger than deteriorating manners. Insults replace substantiation, humiliation replaces examination, and refusal to correct an error becomes proof of partisan loyalty. The objective may be inferred from the conduct even when motive cannot be established: dominate the proceeding, discredit the target and ensure that accountability looks like surrender.

RawStory also cites Schmitt’s earlier declaration that America belongs to “us,” not “them,” and notes that the Missouri NAACP characterized his 2022 campaign advertising as racist. Those facts do not prove precisely whom Schmitt meant by “them,” despite the article’s confident interpretation. They do show a recurring politics organized around an entitled in-group and a suspect out-group.

The institutional collapse

In 1998, House Republican leaders blocked Democratic efforts to discipline Rep. Dan Burton after he called President Bill Clinton a “scumbag,” but the insult still generated a serious backlash. The contrast is not evidence of a more virtuous past; it shows that institutions once recognized reputational costs they now barely attempt to impose.

The deeper decay is therefore not that politicians discovered profanity. It is that legislative power can be used to broadcast an unsupported accusation, the factual premise can disintegrate, and the official responsible can simply refuse repair. The obscenity is memorable. The impunity is systemic.
