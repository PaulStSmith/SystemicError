WSJ reporters smell 'blood in the water' despite Trump's relationship with Murdoch: expert

Short of some type of editorial interference coming from the powers that be, one media expert expects the reporting side of the Wall Street Journal to keep breaking stories on Donald Trump and his Jeffrey Epstein entanglement.

In a report on the split personality exhibited by Rupert Murdoch's media empire, with the Journal dropping bombshells on the Trump White House over the Epstein files, and Fox News hosts avoiding the hot topic as best they can, there are questions about how it will all come out the end.

According to the Guardian's Ben Makuch, while the Epstein firestorm has consumed the major news networks, Fox News has focused it's attention on accusations from DNI Tulsi Gabbard that former President Barack Obama has been guilty of treason.

His report notes that on Fox, "In one of the days following the fallout from Epstein, mentions of Obama’s name reportedly drowned out that of the convicted pedophile and suspected spy, by a score of 117 to two."

Despite Trump's threat to sue Murdoch and the Journal for $10 billion over accusations that have proven to be true, one media watcher claimed there are no indications that someone is calling off the dogs at the WSJ.

Edward Ongweso Jr, a senior researcher at Security in Context, told the Guardian, “Clearly, Trump wants to distract from the fact that he had a close and intimate friendship with Epstein, a billionaire pedophile that seemed to have set up a global trafficking ring. And he wants to distract from the obvious implication of his about-face here (going from insisting the Epstein files will be released to insisting they never did and were invented by Democrats to take him down): that he’s in them.”

He conceded, "It is hard to imagine how any of his tactics will work, but then again he has gotten out of almost every single situation that would’ve doomed anyone else, hasn’t he?”

Having said that, he stated that he believes the Journal will be relentless.

"WSJ reporters might smell blood in the water for the president and report on him accordingly," Makuch wrote with Ongweso, explaining, "There has been a trickle of additional Epstein-Trump material, most recently the resurfacing of photos showing Epstein at Trump’s 1993 wedding to Martha Maples. There is certainly more that WSJ reporters will uncover and unless there’s editorial interference, I can’t see how Murdoch’s empire can stop itself from uttering his name again.”

Our Analysis:

The Dichotomy of Murdoch's Media Empire: A Closer Look

In an era where the lines between journalistic integrity and political allegiance blur, the recent report highlighting the contrasting approaches of the Wall Street Journal (WSJ) and Fox News towards Donald Trump's entanglement with Jeffrey Epstein is nothing short of a journalistic thriller unfolding in real-time. The Wall Street Journal, known for its investigative prowess, has been breaking story after story on Trump's connections with the disgraced financier, while Fox News, another jewel in Rupert Murdoch's media crown, has been conspicuously silent, if not evasive, about the whole affair.

The WSJ's Unyielding Pursuit

The WSJ's relentless reporting on the Trump-Epstein saga is commendable. Despite Trump's blustering threats to sue the Journal for $10 billion over accusations that have proven to be true, the publication shows no signs of backing down. This steadfastness in journalism, especially when reporting on figures with immense power and influence, is a beacon of hope in an otherwise murky media landscape. The fact that additional material continues to surface, including photos of Epstein at Trump’s 1993 wedding, suggests that there's much more to this story waiting to be uncovered.

Fox News' Selective Blindness

On the other end of the spectrum, Fox News' handling—or rather, mishandling—of the situation is a glaring example of editorial cowardice. According to the Guardian's Ben Makuch, Fox News has been actively diverting attention away from the Epstein scandal, focusing instead on baseless accusations against former President Barack Obama. The disparity in coverage is not just disconcerting; it is a deliberate attempt to obscure the truth and mislead its viewership. This selective blindness is a disservice to journalism and a blatant display of the network's political biases.

The Power of Editorial Independence

The dichotomy within Murdoch's empire raises critical questions about editorial independence and the integrity of news reporting. While the WSJ appears to operate with a degree of journalistic freedom that is both rare and necessary, Fox News seems to be under a spell of political partisanship that undermines its credibility. The contrasting approaches to the Epstein-Trump connection within the same media conglomerate underscore the complex dynamics of power, media, and politics.

Edward Ongweso Jr's commentary to the Guardian hits the nail on the head: Trump's attempts to distract from his unsavory connections are transparent and desperate. Yet, his historical Teflon-like resilience to scandals poses a peculiar challenge to the press. The WSJ's determination to continue its investigations despite potential repercussions is a testament to the crucial role of the free press in holding the powerful accountable.

Conclusion: The Importance of Media Vigilance

As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the importance of media vigilance cannot be overstated. In an age where misinformation can spread like wildfire, the commitment of publications like the WSJ to uncover the truth is a vital public service. At the same time, the disheartening editorial choices made by Fox News serve as a stark reminder of the dangers of media partisanship.

In the battle for truth, let us not forget the words of Edward R. Murrow: "To be persuasive we must be believable; to be believable we must be credible; credible we must be truthful." It is high time for all media outlets to take this message to heart and act accordingly.
