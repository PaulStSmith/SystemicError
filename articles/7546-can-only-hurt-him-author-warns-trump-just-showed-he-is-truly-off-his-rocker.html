'Can only hurt him': Author warns Trump just showed he is truly 'off his rocker'

A prominent author who has written about President Donald Trump for decades warned on Tuesday that the president just showed the world that he is genuinely “off his f------ rocker.”

Journalist Michael Wolff, who has written four books about Trump, discussed Trump’s response to director Rob Reiner’s murder on a new episode of “Inside Trump’s Head,” a podcast that he co-hosts with Joana Coles of The Daily Beast. Trump posted on Truth Social that Reiner, a frequent critic of Trump’s, suffered from “Trump Derrangement Syndrome,” and suggested that this may have led to his death.

“As anyone has gone through this, just because your older parent, let’s say, is showing signs of losing it, does not mean that they will not continue down the road of losing it for quite some time,” Wolff said. “We do have three years to go.”

Reiner and his wife, Michele, were murdered in their Los Angeles home over the weekend. Authorities have indicted the couple’s son, Nick, on two counts of first-degree murder related to the incident.

Wolff said Trump’s response “can only hurt him,” but the president doesn’t seem phased by that notion.

“You fall asleep, okay, not a good look, but it’s not really your fault,” Wolff said, referring to Trump’s age. “ But this is something to have said this, and it was unnecessary for him to say anything, to have dived in. And so proactively done something that will create nothing ... That’s derrangement.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Unhinged Response to Tragedy: A Deep Dive into Derangement

In an episode that seems ripped straight from the pages of the most dystopian fiction, former President Donald Trump has once again catapulted himself into the center of controversy with remarks that can only be described as utterly reprehensible. Michael Wolff, a journalist with a long track record of covering Trump’s escapades, has called him out for being “off his f------ rocker,” and frankly, it’s hard to disagree.

The Unsettling Comments on Reiner’s Murder

Rob Reiner, a filmmaker and vocal critic of Trump, was tragically murdered along with his wife, Michele, in what can only be described as a heinous act of violence. Authorities have charged their son with the crime, adding a layer of tragedy to an already heartbreaking story. Yet, in the wake of this, Trump’s reaction was not one of sympathy, condolence, or even basic human decency. Instead, he took to Truth Social to postulate that Reiner’s “Trump Derangement Syndrome” might have led to his death.

Analyzing Trump’s Deranged Logic

Let’s dissect the malicious absurdity of Trump’s statement. To suggest that vocal criticism or opposition to his presidency could be a contributing factor to someone’s murder is not just distasteful; it is dangerously unhinged. It reflects a mindset so deeply entrenched in its own narrative that it loses all contact with reality, empathy, or reason.

Wolff’s Righteous Indignation

Wolff’s commentary on Trump’s behavior sheds light on a profound and disturbing truth: the former president’s actions are not merely the ramblings of an elderly man slightly out of touch. They are indicative of a more severe, pervasive issue. Wolff warns, “just because your older parent, let’s say, is showing signs of losing it, does not mean that they will not continue down the road of losing it for quite some time.” This is a stark reminder that we might be witnessing the tip of the iceberg when it comes to Trump’s erratic behavior.

The Dangers of Trump’s Rhetoric

What makes Trump’s commentary on the Reiner tragedy so alarming is not just its insensitivity, but its potential to incite further division and possibly even violence. His words are not just the musings of a private citizen; they are the proclamations of a man who once held the highest office in the land and may seek it again. This is not about age or occasional gaffes; this is about a persistent pattern of deranged rhetoric that has real-world consequences.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

In sum, Trump’s response to the Reiner murder is a grotesque display of his inability to offer anything resembling appropriate or presidential commentary in times of tragedy. It underscores a dangerous penchant for inflaming situations and deflecting personal responsibility. Wolff’s assessment, harsh as it may be, is a necessary wake-up call to those who might still, inexplicably, give Trump the benefit of the doubt. It’s high time to recognize the dangerous implications of his rhetoric and the critical need for accountability.

In a world teeming with genuine issues requiring thoughtful consideration and empathy, Trump’s comments serve as a stark reminder of why his return to the political arena would not just be a step backward, but a leap into an abyss from which recovery might prove impossible.
