A Kat Abughazaleh (D-IL) campaign sign sits outside of a polling station in Evanston, Illinois, U.S., March 14, 2026. Abughazaleh is running for Congress in Illinois’ 9th district. REUTERS/Jim Vondruska

MS NOW’s John Heilemann analyzed the state of the Democratic Party after an Illinois primary that saw left-wing candidates lose hotly contested races among a seemingly fired-up electorate.

“Morning Joe” host Joe Scarborough focused on 26-year-old newcomer Kat Abughazaleh, a left-wing influencer who centered her campaign on opposition to Israel’s war in Gaza and promised to challenge the Democratic establishment, but ultimately fell short of Evanston Mayor Daniel Biss in a House race in the Chicago suburbs.

“Regardless of what you think of her politics and people are attacking her on all sides, Kat – and I will not mess up her last name, it starts with an A, I’ll just call her Kat A. — had some of the most interesting commercials, and when I looked at them, I said, ‘Okay, this is a good view into the future of campaigning in American politics,” Scarborough said.

“John Heilemann, I heard you laugh. I know, you know, she started with a smear, an attack ad against herself, and here’s somebody who just moved into the district recently, had been thrown around by ICE agents and protests, and nobody really gave her a chance to win, and she came very close to winning the Democratic primary and being a member of Congress in illinois 9, but the story of the night really was not just that progressives, a lot of progressives, lost. It was also the fact that AIPAC played such a huge role in tipping the scales.”

The powerful pro-Israel group backed two winners but lost in two races where it spent the most, but its social media account boasted about taking down Abughazaleh, whom Heileman said had once worked for him on another project.

“Yeah, Kat Abughazaleh, who was a former employee of mine at The Recount, by the way,” he said. “Super bright, super social media and digital media savvy. She is going to have a big future in Democratic politics if she wants to have it, whether you like her politics or not. I’m not endorsing candidates, I’m just saying she is young and smart and gets the way the world of media now works, and it took AIPAC doing what you just, what you just said, Joe.

“I think she would probably win that primary over the Evanston mayor, Daniel Biss, who is also a very progressive candidate. He’s not, he’s not a moderate by any means. I think she probably would have won that primary if it hadn’t been, she got it on the negative side coming from AIPAC.”

The big takeaway was voter enthusiasm, Heilemann said, which has been a recurring theme in special elections and Democratic primaries since Donald Trump won the 2024 election.

“The big, big headlines out of this primary is turnout for Democrats, again off the charts, rarely do you see a midterm primary that equals, as this one did, the turnout for the last time there was a competitive Democratic presidential primary, which was in 2020,” Heilemann said.

“So that number is huge. We’re seeing enthusiasm on the Democratic side that’s completely off the charts.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Illinois Primary: Power, Influence, and Future Campaigns

The aftermath of the Illinois primary has provided a fertile ground for political analysis, with MS NOW’s John Heilemann and “Morning Joe” host Joe Scarborough dissecting the defeat of left-wing candidates, notably Kat Abughazaleh, and the role of the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) in influencing election outcomes. However, a closer examination reveals a nuanced landscape of power dynamics, institutional influence, and the challenges of framing in political narratives.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

At the core of this analysis is the identification of who holds institutional power and who made critical decisions affecting the outcome. In this scenario, AIPAC emerges as a significant institutional force, wielding its influence through financial backing and public endorsements of candidates. Their role in the election, particularly in tipping the scales against Abughazaleh, underscores the power external organizations can exert in electoral politics.

However, it’s also crucial to acknowledge the electorate’s power in the decision-making process. The high voter turnout signals a deeply engaged and participatory base, suggesting that while external groups like AIPAC can influence elections, the ultimate decision rests with the voters.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article frames AIPAC’s involvement as pivotal in the defeat of left-wing candidates, especially Abughazaleh. While it’s undeniable that AIPAC played a significant role, attributing the outcomes solely to its influence risks oversimplifying the electoral dynamics. Other factors, such as candidate appeal, campaign strategy, and voter priorities, undeniably play crucial roles. Therefore, the framing potentially misdirects responsibility toward AIPAC, overshadowing the multifaceted nature of electoral politics and the agency of voters and candidates themselves.

False Equivalence and Lazy Narratives

The story also veers dangerously close to promoting a false equivalence by suggesting that the influence of external groups like AIPAC is the sole determinant of electoral outcomes. This simplification negates the complexities of political campaigns, where a myriad of factors, including grassroots support, candidate visibility, and policy positions, contribute to the results. Additionally, the narrative risks becoming a lazy scapegoat by focusing excessively on AIPAC’s role, potentially absolving other actors, such as the Democratic Party establishment and the candidates’ campaigns, from their share of responsibility in the outcomes.

Conclusion

The Illinois primary’s analysis provides a compelling glimpse into the current state of Democratic politics, highlighting the tension between left-wing candidates and established political forces. While AIPAC’s influence is undeniable, attributing the election outcomes solely to its efforts oversimplifies the electoral landscape. It’s essential to recognize the electorate’s agency, the complexity of campaign dynamics, and the multifaceted nature of political power.

As political observers and participants look toward the future, understanding these dynamics becomes crucial. The rise of candidates like Kat Abughazaleh, despite their defeats, signals a shifting terrain in Democratic politics where digital savviness, policy positions, and grassroots support will play increasingly significant roles. Equally, the involvement of powerful external groups like AIPAC will continue to spark debates about influence and fairness in the electoral process.

In dissecting these narratives, it’s vital to avoid convenient scapegoats and to challenge lazy narratives that oversimplify complex political phenomena. Only through such rigorous analysis can we hope to understand the evolving landscape of American politics.
