'Embarrassing': MAGA prosecutor gets brutal smackdown in feud with AZ attorney general

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Kris Mayes speaking with attendees at an Attorney General candidate forum. (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

The pro-Trump attorney of Pinal County, Arizona, is not backing down from his illegal efforts to criminally investigate a state senator from another county who posted the locations of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials — but his arguments aren't being well-received.

Last week, Brad Miller, who represents a largely rural and exurban area southeast of Phoenix, stated in a radio interview that he wanted the GOP-controlled state legislature to appoint him a special prosecutor to investigate Sen. Analise Ortiz, even though reporting on the locations of ICE agents isn't illegal.

State Attorney General Kris Mayes, a Democrat, responded with a furious letter warning him he had no authority to do this and demanding he explain himself.

"When you ran for Pinal County Attorney, your campaign platform included fighting 'extreme leftist ideologies,'" she wrote. "But now that you are the elected official you have a duty to execute your authority fairly and impartially. Why under A.R.S. § 12-751, would it not be an anti-SLAPP violation for you to initiate the investigation of a Democratic legislator given your campaign statements and current zealous interest to deter, prevent, or retaliate against Senator Ortiz from exercising her First Amendment Rights?"

Miller has now hit back with his own letter to the attorney general's office, accusing her of "embrac[ing] ideology over support for law enforcement" and doubling down on his plan.

"I represent the State of Arizona. A.R.S. § 11-532(A)(1). My authority extends to the entire State of Arizona. A.R.S. § 11-532(E). I am neither required nor will I ever ask your permission to handle a matter outside of Pinal County," he continued. "An investigation is meant to gather facts to determine if a crime was committed ... When leadership is absent, others will step in the [sic] fill the void. My offer to assist still stands."

This letter was promptly mocked by legal analyst and talk radio host Barry Markson.

"This is embarrassing to Brad Miller and anyone believing him," Markson posted to X. "@AZAGMayes was right to call him out on his self serving and silly request for the legislature to appoint him as a special prosecutor. Even the statute Miller cites, IN THIS LETTER, specifically limits his authority to the his county and nothing else. This is nothing more than an embarrassing political document, and the 'act tough' act may work with some MAGA folks, but this is truly pathetic."

Our Analysis:

The Political Theater of Pinal County: A Case Study in Overreach

In the latest twist of political drama emanating from Pinal County, Arizona, we're witnessing a spectacular display of overreach by pro-Trump attorney Brad Miller, whose misguided crusade against State Senator Analise Ortiz is not just ill-conceived but teeters on the edge of the absurd. Miller's attempt to appoint himself as a special prosecutor to investigate Ortiz, for the non-crime of posting locations of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officials, is a brazen exhibition of political muscle-flexing that flies in the face of legal norms and democratic principles.

Miller's Misguided Missile

At the heart of this saga is Miller's radio interview declaration, a move so audaciously out of bounds that it prompted a swift and scathing response from State Attorney General Kris Mayes. Mayes, wielding the written word like a rapier, pointed out the glaringly obvious: Miller's actions smack of a retaliatory witch hunt rather than a legitimate legal inquiry. Her letter didn't just question Miller's authority; it underscored the dangerous precedent his actions could set, framing his motivations as a direct assault on Senator Ortiz's First Amendment Rights.

Miller's rebuttal to Mayes is a study in political doublespeak and legal obfuscation. His claim of representing the "State of Arizona" and possessing authority "extends to the entire State of Arizona" is a masterclass in missing the point. The issue here isn't about geographical jurisdiction but about the selective application of the law based on political affiliations and perceived ideologies.

A Clash of Ideologies or A Misuse of Power?

Miller's attempts to position himself as the lone sentinel guarding against "extreme leftist ideologies" would be laughable if the implications weren't so deeply troubling. This isn't about left versus right; it's about an elected official attempting to weaponize his office to silence political opponents. The irony of accusing Mayes of "[embracing] ideology over support for law enforcement" while engaging in a blatantly ideological pursuit is rich, to say the least.

Legal analyst and talk radio host Barry Markson's critique of Miller's actions strikes at the heart of the issue. Markson rightly identifies Miller's letter as "an embarrassing political document," pointing out the glaring contradiction in Miller's cited statutes that, in reality, do the exact opposite of supporting his argument. This isn't just about legal incompetence; it's about a dangerous precedent where political vendettas are pursued under the guise of law enforcement.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

The saga unfolding in Pinal County is more than a mere political squabble; it's a cautionary tale of what happens when the lines between legal authority and political ambition become blurred. Brad Miller's pursuit of Analise Ortiz under the thinly veiled guise of law enforcement is not just misguided; it's a dangerous game that threatens the very foundations of democratic governance.

As this situation continues to evolve, one thing remains clear: the need for vigilance against the misuse of power. The actions of individuals like Miller, who seek to leverage their positions for political retribution, must be scrutinized and opposed at every turn. In the end, this isn't just about protecting the rights of a state senator but safeguarding the principles that underpin our democracy.
