'Clear signal of defiance' shows Trump attacks are backfiring: analyst

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump holds a Cabinet meeting, Wednesday, April 30, 2025, in the Cabinet Room. (Official White House Photo by Molly Riley)

The appointment of a new ayatollah shortly after the death of Ali Khamenei highlights a major shortcoming in Donald Trump’s plot for Iranian regime change, an analyst wrote Tuesday.

Despite the president’s team carrying out a series of strikes against the Middle Eastern country earlier this month, the only changes made so far, according to CNN analyst Stephen Collinson, are beneficial to Iran. Trump may have succeeded in removing the country’s former head, but his replacement, Collinson believes, is far worse for the US and the current administration.

He wrote, “No one from the outside can know its true state after days of aerial pounding of government facilities and heavy loss of life.

“But so far, the operation has succeeded only in replacing an aged supreme leader — who was already close to his eternal rest and had no succession plan — with a younger version with the same last name.

“The choice of Mojtaba Khamenei to succeed his martyred father as supreme leader was a signal of defiance from the theocracy and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps that rule Iran with an iron fist. It remains unclear how Khamenei will be able to consolidate power in such extreme circumstances.”

Trump has also failed to take into account the activity needed from the Iranian people to successfully topple and ultimately replace the regime, Collinson argued.

“There’s no outward sign that the uprising of Iranians against their corrupt and repressive rulers that Trump sought to trigger is about to materialize,” he wrote. “Perhaps US and Israeli attacks on Iranian economic and energy infrastructure could so weaken the regime’s foundation that a revolt could materialize in the months and years ahead, even if the clerics cling on for now.

“But this requires Iranian civilians taking to the streets against ruthless security forces pining for revenge following the US onslaught.

“Only weeks ago, thousands were killed in a previous thwarted uprising. It seems just as likely that the unintended result of the war will be more repression rather than a flowering of freedom.”

Trump may also be forced to play his hand sooner than he had hoped. Collinson wrote, “Trump also faces pressing strategic dilemmas. Will he use force to try to open the Strait of Hormuz — the world’s vital oil conduit, which has been all but closed by Iran?

“And would the survival of the regime lead to an almost permanent state of simmering warfare between the US and Israel and Iran that requires regular escalations to prevent the Islamic Republic rebuilding its threat?”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Iranian Gambit: A Miscalculated Adventure?

The recent appointment of a new ayatollah in Iran, following the death of Ali Khamenei, throws a stark light on what can only be described as a glaring miscalculation in Donald Trump’s strategy towards Iranian regime change. This development, as noted by CNN analyst Stephen Collinson, not only underscores the shortsightedness of Trump’s approach but also highlights a fundamental misreading of the political landscape within Iran.

Trump’s Misguided Strategy

The strategy employed by Trump’s administration, involving a series of strikes against Iran, appears to be fundamentally flawed. Collinson’s analysis suggests that these efforts have paradoxically strengthened Iran’s position rather than weakening it. The replacement of Ali Khamenei with a “younger version with the same last name” is hardly the outcome the U.S. administration had hoped for. This transition signals not just continuity within the Iranian theocracy but also a potential hardening of its stance against the U.S.

A Failure to Understand Iran’s Internal Dynamics

One of the most critical oversights in Trump’s strategy is the failure to appreciate the complexities of Iranian politics and society. The expectation that military action would trigger a popular uprising against the regime demonstrates a superficial understanding of Iran’s political landscape. Collinson rightly points out that there is “no outward sign” of the mass protests Trump hoped to inspire. This oversight is particularly egregious considering the recent “thwarted uprising” that resulted in thousands of deaths, suggesting a populace weary of conflict and repression.

The Unintended Consequences of Military Action

The consequences of Trump’s actions extend beyond the immediate political fallout. The attacks on Iranian economic and energy infrastructure, while intended to weaken the regime, could have the opposite effect. Collinson posits that such actions might inadvertently solidify the regime’s control, as the civilian population, facing increased repression, may be less inclined to revolt. This scenario underscores the inherent risk of using military force as a tool for political change, particularly in a region as volatile as the Middle East.

Strategic Dilemmas and the Future of U.S.-Iran Relations

Trump’s approach leaves the U.S. facing several “pressing strategic dilemmas,” including the potential for an almost permanent state of conflict with Iran. The question of whether to use force to open the Strait of Hormuz highlights the precarious nature of this strategy. Such actions could lead to an escalation of hostilities, requiring continuous military engagement to counter Iranian influence.

Furthermore, the survival of the Iranian regime, far from being weakened by Trump’s actions, might now be emboldened to pursue its agenda with renewed vigor. This outcome could necessitate a reevaluation of U.S. strategy towards Iran, moving away from military intervention towards more nuanced diplomatic and economic measures.

In Conclusion

The appointment of Mojtaba Khamenei as Iran’s new supreme leader is a testament to the failure of Trump’s Iranian policy. This development not only challenges the effectiveness of military intervention as a means of regime change but also highlights the need for a more informed and strategic approach towards dealing with Iran. The path ahead requires a careful reassessment of U.S. objectives and strategies, with a focus on fostering stability and peace in a region long marred by conflict.
