Trump repeatedly struggles to stay awake during live broadcast of Board of Peace summit

Donald Trump was, once again, seen nodding off during a major public event, this time at the much-hyped first meeting of his “Board of Peace” initiative.

Several instances of the president looking “extremely drowsy” and closing his eyes were caught on a Fox News broadcast of the event, and shared in a report by The New Republic on Thursday. He appeared to struggle with staying awake during addresses from visiting leaders and officials, as seen in split-screen shots.

“His eyelids grew heavy during Major General Jasper Jeffers III’s presentation, and if he didn’t fall asleep completely, he at least looked incredibly disinterested in his own creation,” The New Republic’s report detailed, later quipping, “The ‘bored of peace’ jokes write themselves.”

These instances at the Board of Peace meeting are just the latest examples in a months-long trend of Trump appearing to nod off while on camera during a public event. In April of last year, onlookers caught the president, 78 at the time, seemingly asleep and snoring while attending the funeral of Pope Francis. A few weeks later, a Fox broadcast caught him appearing to nod off while visiting Saudi Arabian leaders in Riyadh.

The trend has continued even when Trump has not been in the middle of a taxing overseas trip. Last month, as The New Republic noted, his “eyes were completely shut at multiple points of his whole-milk legislation signing ceremony...”

“He struggled to stay awake during a marijuana rescheduling executive order signing, looked absolutely exhausted at his own Cabinet meeting in December and fell asleep once again at a Rwanda–Democratic Republic of the Congo peace agreement signing,” the outlet continued.

Trump, who frequently mocks his predecessor as “Sleepy Joe” Biden, has appeared to nod off during events so often that the trend has become a key piece of evidence for his declining condition. Currently 79, he is the oldest person ever elected president, and frequently exhibits behaviors that cause observers to question his physical health and mental acuity.

Some, however, have expressed sympathy with Trump’s latest incident, albeit in a tongue-in-cheek manner,

“The one time I feel some empathy with Trump falling asleep,” economist Tony Yates wrote in a post to BlueSky. “During a 2.5 hour ceremonial to launch a pointless Board of Peace, how many of us would be able to stay awake?”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Snooze Fest: A Symptom of a Wider Malaise?

In an era where the bizarre has become the norm, the latest incident involving Donald Trump nodding off during the inaugural meeting of his “Board of Peace” initiative barely registers as a blip on the radar of political oddities. Yet, it’s a blip that’s symptomatic of a larger, more concerning trend—not just about the man himself, but about the state of political leadership and accountability.

The Nap Heard Around the World

Trump’s penchant for dozing off at critically public moments has morphed from a one-off occurrence to a disturbing pattern. Whether it’s snoozing through Major General Jasper Jeffers III’s presentation, as detailed by The New Republic, or catching Z’s at the funeral of Pope Francis, these are not mere moments of tiredness; they are emblematic of disengagement at the highest level of office. The fact that these incidents of somnolence have been caught on camera, including on a Fox News broadcast—usually a friendly platform for Trump—adds a layer of undeniable visibility to the issue.

The Irony of “Sleepy Joe”

The irony is as thick as it is unavoidable. Trump, who has often mocked his predecessor with the moniker “Sleepy Joe,” is now the protagonist in a series of sleepy sagas that starkly juxtapose his intended image of vitality and vigor. At 79, his frequent lapses into sleepiness have become a de facto exhibit A for discussions about his physical health and mental acuity. It’s a stark reminder that age and stamina are indeed factors worth considering when electing national leaders—a point Trump himself has made, albeit directed at others.

A Symptom of Disinterest?

Beyond the chuckles and meme-worthy moments these incidents provide, there’s a deeper, more concerning issue at play: a seeming disinterest in the very initiatives Trump spearheads. Describing the president as looking “incredibly disinterested in his own creation,” The New Republic’s report highlights a lack of engagement that’s troubling for someone at the helm of national and global initiatives. Whether it’s the “Board of Peace” or legislation signings, the expectation is for the president to be the most engaged, not the most likely to doze off.

Empathy in Unexpected Places

Interestingly, some have found a sliver of empathy for Trump amidst the criticism. Economist Tony Yates’s tongue-in-cheek commentary on the “pointless Board of Peace” ceremony as something many would struggle to stay awake through, underscores a broader point: the performative nature of many political initiatives. It begs the question—are these events truly about fostering change, or are they merely ceremonial, designed for appearances rather than results?

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call Needed

Trump’s latest nap is more than just a funny anecdote; it’s a wake-up call to the realities of aging, engagement, and the genuine interest required to lead effectively. It also prompts a reflection on the nature of political spectacle—on what is done for show versus what is done for real change. In the end, the “Board of Peace” initiative, and Trump’s snooze through it, may just be another chapter in the ongoing saga of contemporary political theater. But it’s a chapter that should prompt both concern and introspection about the state of our leadership and the expectations we hold for those in the highest offices.

In a world craving genuine progress and leadership, perhaps it’s time for political figures to stay awake—both literally and figuratively—to the needs and challenges we face.
