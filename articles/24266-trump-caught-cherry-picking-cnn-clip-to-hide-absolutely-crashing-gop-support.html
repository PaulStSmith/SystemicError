Trump caught cherry-picking CNN clip to hide 'absolutely crashing' GOP support

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosSeptember 30, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

RawStory reports that President Donald Trump posted a 43-second CNN clip emphasizing his 98 percent approval among MAGA Republicans while omitting the rest of analyst Harry Enten’s 14-minute-plus segment, which described steep erosion elsewhere in the Republican coalition ahead of the Nov. 3 midterms.

According to Enten, Trump’s standing among non-MAGA Republicans had fallen from a 56-point margin in the 2024 election to a 7-point net approval margin. He also cited weak voting enthusiasm in that group, sharply negative approval among independents, and widespread independent disapproval of Trump’s handling of gas prices amid the war with Iran. The White House did not immediately comment.

The edit was the message

Trump did not dispute CNN’s unfavorable figures. He selected the one fragment that showed his core supporters remaining loyal and presented it without the analysis that made the fact politically ominous.

That distinction matters. Stable support inside MAGA says little about whether Trump can sustain the broader coalition required to govern or help Republican candidates win. The omitted material addressed exactly that vulnerability.

Power controls the frame

Trump holds the presidency and commands a personal media platform capable of turning a cropped television segment into political messaging. CNN produced the analysis; Trump repackaged it. His institutional power and public reach allowed him to promote the network’s authority while discarding its conclusion.

Republican candidates appear to be making their own calculation. Enten reported that fewer than 1 percent of Republican ads aired during the first half of September mentioned Trump. That does not prove why individual campaigns avoided him, but it shows a striking separation between presidential self-promotion and the party’s advertised electoral strategy.

Loyalty is not expansion

A 98 percent approval rating among MAGA Republicans is evidence of durability within a faction, not strength across the electorate. Enten’s figures suggest that Trump’s political position is becoming more concentrated: nearly unanimous loyalty at the center, paired with severe deterioration among non-MAGA Republicans and independents.

The economic numbers are especially damaging. Trump had held a 22-point advantage over Kamala Harris on the economy among pure independents in fall 2024; Enten said his net approval with that group had since fallen to minus 77. Whatever message sustains the base, it does not erase voter judgments about material conditions.

The weaker actors are not the cause

Coverage can misdirect attention by treating alienated Republicans and independents as fickle blocs whose changing attitudes create a problem for Trump. They do not hold presidential power. They did not choose the administration’s policies or manage its response to rising gas prices.

The politically relevant agency sits with the president and his administration. Voters’ disapproval is a consequence to be explained, not a disruption to be blamed on them. RawStory’s account points toward that distinction, although its “caught” framing risks reducing a structural propaganda practice to a moment of personal embarrassment.

A shrinking coalition can still dominate

The larger pattern is rule through factional intensity: preserve overwhelming loyalty among the most committed supporters, circulate selectively edited evidence of strength, and minimize signs that the wider coalition is breaking apart.

That strategy can protect Trump’s authority inside the Republican Party even as it becomes an electoral liability outside MAGA. The systemic error is confusing control of a party’s internal narrative with broad public consent. A leader can lose reach while retaining command—and use that command to insist the loss is not happening.
