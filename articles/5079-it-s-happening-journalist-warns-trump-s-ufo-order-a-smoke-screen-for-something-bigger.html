'It's happening': Journalist warns Trump's UFO order a smoke screen for something bigger

President Donald Trump’s “bonkers” announcement Thursday that he plans to release government files related to alien life raised an immediate red flag for some journalists, and two of whom warned that the announcement was a clear smoke screen to distract the public from something far more nefarious.

Writing on his social media platform Truth Social, Trump announced that he would be ordering federal agencies to “begin the process” of releasing government files “related to alien and extraterrestrial life” and “UFOs.”

The announcement, made amid the United States inching closer to a “full-fledged war” with Iran, immediately caught the attention of journalist Lee Fang, who highlighted a historical CIA document that bore a striking resemblance to current events.

Fang flagged one CIA document in particular: a 1954 telegram from the CIA that was issued amid the CIA’s Operation PBSuccess, a covert effort to overthrow the democratically elected president of Guatemala, Jacobo Árbenz, after his administration had enacted land reforms that cut into the profits of the United Fruit Company, which the CIA described as an “attack“ on the American company.

In the telegram, the CIA lists methods of de-emphasizing media coverage of the covert operation, among them being a method involving “flying saucers.”

“If possible, fabricate big human interest story, like flying saucers, birth sextuplets in remote area to take play away,” the CIA telegram reads.

Given the timing of Trump’s UFO announcement – issued as the Trump administration has mobilized a colossal military force near Iran, with an all-out war possibly imminent – journalist and author Max Blumenthal bemoaned what he suggested was Trump’s tactic to draw attention away from the potentially catastrophic consequences of a wider regional war.

“It’s happening,” Blumenthal wrote bluntly in asocial media poston X Thursday night. “Again.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s UFO Distraction: A Classic Play from the CIA’s Playbook

In a move that can only be described as astonishingly bonkers, President Donald Trump announced plans to release government files related to alien life and UFOs. This announcement, however, isn’t just a random musing from the depths of Trump’s often unpredictable social media strategy. It’s a calculated distraction, a smokescreen designed to divert attention from the looming shadows of a potentially devastating conflict with Iran.

The Smoke Screen

Trump’s timing is as suspect as it is impeccable. Amid escalating tensions that could drag the United States into a “full-fledged war” with Iran, this announcement serves as a perfect decoy. It’s not every day that the possibility of extraterrestrial disclosures from the government comes up, and the allure of such revelations is undeniable. This is a classic technique of misdirection, a principle magicians have used for centuries: look here at this dazzling mystery, so you won’t notice the disaster brewing right there.

Journalists Lee Fang and Max Blumenthal swiftly identified the tactic. Fang, in particular, shed light on a fascinating piece of historical context that suggests Trump’s move is not just a distraction but a play straight out of the CIA’s playbook. A 1954 CIA telegram detailed methods to de-emphasize media coverage of Operation PBSuccess, a covert operation in Guatemala, suggesting fabricating a “big human interest story, like flying saucers” to divert attention. The parallels between then and now are chillingly clear. Trump, whether consciously or not, is deploying a strategy eerily reminiscent of a bygone era of covert operations and media manipulation.

The Historical Echo

The reference to the 1954 CIA telegram is particularly damning. It highlights a long-standing pattern of using sensational stories to overshadow governmental actions that might be controversial, unethical, or outright illegal. The CIA’s involvement in Guatemala, overthrowing a democratically elected president under the guise of protecting American business interests (namely, the United Fruit Company), is a stark reminder of the lengths to which the U.S. government has gone to secure its interests abroad, often at the expense of democratic principles and human lives. The suggestion that similar tactics are being employed today is a grave warning that the past is not as distant as it might seem.

A Distracted Public

Max Blumenthal’s blunt statement, “It’s happening. Again,” encapsulates the cyclical nature of these distractions. It’s a dire warning that history may be repeating itself, with the public’s attention being masterfully diverted from a potentially catastrophic military escalation. The role of social media platforms, like Trump’s Truth Social, in propagating these distractions only adds another layer of complexity to the modern iteration of this tactic. The speed and reach of social media amplify the potential of such distractions to an unprecedented degree.

Conclusion: Beyond the Smokescreen

As seductive as the prospect of government disclosures on UFOs and alien life may be, it’s imperative to look beyond the smokescreen. The real story here isn’t about extraterrestrials or flying saucers; it’s about the potential for a new, destructive conflict in the Middle East, the manipulation of public attention, and the eerie repetition of history’s darker chapters. Trump’s announcement is not just “bonkers”; it’s a calculated move in a much larger game of global politics and public perception.

In the end, the responsibility falls on journalists, analysts, and the public to peel back the layers of distraction and focus on the underlying issues at stake. As much as we might yearn for the excitement of alien revelations, the reality on our planet, particularly the specter of war, deserves our undivided attention. Let’s not allow sensational stories to overshadow the gravity of real-world consequences – consequences that could shape the future in ways far more profound than any UFO disclosure ever could.
