'Crime is not out of control': Fox News pundit fact-checks Trump on D.C. takeover

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Irony of Political Expediency: Trump's D.C. Law Enforcement Crusade

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts after signing an executive order to create a White House Olympics task force to handle security and other issues related to the LA 2028 summer Olympics in the South Court Auditorium on the White House campus in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 5, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Fox News contributor Ted Williams, a former member of the Washington D.C. Metropolitan Police Department, challenged President Donald Trump ahead of a possible move to federalize law enforcement in the district.

In a post to Truth Social on Sunday, Trump said that homeless people would have to move "far" away from the nation's capital and that criminals would be imprisoned.

"I found it troubling because I also practiced law in the District of Columbia is that the president of the United States would say that crime is out of control," Williams told host Jon Scott on Sunday. "I take exception to that. Crime is not out of control in the District of Columbia."

Williams said he suspected that Trump decided to take action in D.C. after the attempted carjacking of a former DOGE staffer.

"I would like to ask Mr. Trump. Where were you last month when a three-year-old child, Honesty Cheadle, was shot and killed as the result of crime in the District of Columbia?" Williams remarked. "Don't use this as a pretext to actually eradicate home rule. And that seemed to be what Mr. Trump is interested in."

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Political Expediency: Trump's D.C. Law Enforcement Crusade

In a rather astonishing turn of events, we find ourselves peering into the heart of political maneuvering at its most transparent. Ted Williams, a Fox News contributor with a background in both law enforcement and legal practice, has thrown down the gauntlet in front of President Donald Trump’s recent musings on federalizing law enforcement in Washington D.C. Trump's proposal, ostensibly aimed at addressing homelessness and criminality in the nation's capital, has ignited controversy, not least for its implications on home rule and civil liberties.

Trump's Simplistic Solution: Out of Sight, Out of Mind

Trump’s approach to solving D.C.'s problems, as revealed in his post on Truth Social, is both simplistic and draconian. Suggesting that homeless individuals be moved "far" away and criminals be imprisoned is a band-aid solution to deeply rooted societal issues. It's akin to sweeping the dirt under the rug and pretending the room is clean. It's not just an oversimplification of complex issues; it's a complete evasion of responsibility and a lack of empathy for the human condition.

Crime in D.C.: A Convenient Excuse?

Williams' rebuttal to Trump’s claims about rampant crime in D.C. is particularly telling. By challenging the narrative of an out-of-control crime wave in the capital, Williams not only exposes Trump’s exaggeration but also questions the timing and motives behind such a drastic proposal. The reference to the tragic shooting of three-year-old Honesty Cheadle is a poignant reminder of the selective outrage often displayed by politicians. Why now, Trump is asked, do you decide to act, when crime has been a persistent, albeit manageable, issue in D.C.?

The Real Agenda: Eradicating Home Rule

Perhaps the most chilling aspect of Trump’s proposal is its potential impact on D.C.'s autonomy. Williams astutely identifies this as a veiled attempt to undermine home rule. By federalizing law enforcement, Trump would not only strip the district of its ability to self-govern but also set a dangerous precedent for federal intervention in local matters. This isn’t just about crime; it’s about control.

Conclusion: A Call for Genuine Solutions

Williams’ challenge to Trump is more than a critique; it’s a call to action for genuine solutions to the issues facing D.C. and other urban areas. Homelessness and crime are symptoms of larger systemic failures that require thoughtful, comprehensive approaches, not authoritarian overreach.

In the end, Trump’s proposal serves as a reminder of the dangers of using complex social issues as pawns in political gamesmanship. It underscores the necessity for informed, compassionate leadership that seeks to heal rather than divide. Williams, in his condemnation, not only defends the integrity of D.C.’s governance but advocates for the dignity of its residents. In doing so, he highlights the critical difference between leadership that exploits and leadership that empowers.
