'That’s Trump’s problem now' as he faces 'double-whammy' of bad news: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Adding to Donald Trump’s problems that already include the Jeffrey Epstein files, his inability to end the Russia-Ukraine war after promising to do so immediately after taking office, he is now confronted with the same harsh economic news that dogged President Joe Biden.

According to a report from David Lynch of the Washington Post, the president has about 14 months to course-correct the economy or face the prospect that the Republican party will get blown out in the 2026 midterms if he doesn’t get prices down and employment up.

As Lynch wrote, “Consumer prices rose 2.9 percent over the past year, the fastest annual pace since January. Shopping for groceries, in particular, is becoming a traumatic experience,” before adding, “The combination of price pressures and employment worries is reminiscent of the double-whammy that kneecapped Vice President Kamala Harris’s presidential bid last fall and threatens Republican hopes of retaining control of Congress in the 2026 elections.”

Should Republicans lose majority control of one or both of the House and the Senate, Trump will find himself handcuffed as a lame duck president for the last two years he is in the Oval Office, which has the White House concerned.

Despite the Trump White House claims that Biden’s economy was a “disaster” for workers, Lynch wrote, “what the new [job] numbers actually showed, economists said, was that the labor market last year was starting to downshift to a level where fewer new jobs were needed each month to prevent the unemployment rate from rising — a development that has intensified under Trump.”

According to former Biden economic adviser Jared Bernstein, “The problem with this affordability crisis is that every incumbent gets blamed for it, and not just here, but in lots of other countries as well.”

He then dryly added, “And that’s Trump’s problem now.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Quagmire: A Critical Review

In an era where political narratives often spin out of control, it's crucial to dissect the facts with the precision of a scalpel. The recent article highlighting Donald Trump's burgeoning economic challenges makes for an intriguing, albeit distressing, read. As we peel back the layers, it becomes evident that Trump's presidency is marred by a series of unfulfilled promises and economic missteps that could potentially lead to a Republican washout in the 2026 midterms.

The Promise vs. The Reality

Trump, a figure synonymous with grandiose claims, promised to end the Russia-Ukraine war "immediately" after taking office—a pledge that has since dissolved into the ether, much like his assurances of economic prosperity. The article points to a report by David Lynch of the Washington Post, highlighting a grim economic forecast that could spell doom for the Republican party come 2026.

Echoes of Biden's Struggles

It's ironic, if not entirely surprising, that Trump now faces the same economic hurdles that he once lambasted President Joe Biden for. Under Trump's watch, consumer prices have soared by 2.9 percent over the past year, marking the fastest annual pace since January. The act of grocery shopping, once a mundane task, has transformed into a "traumatic experience" for the average American. This juxtaposition between Trump's rhetoric and the harsh economic reality is a testament to the cyclical nature of political accountability—where yesterday's accuser becomes today's accused.

The Double-Whammy

The article adeptly draws parallels between the current economic climate and the factors that undermined Vice President Kamala Harris’s presidential bid. The combination of price pressures and employment worries—aptly described as a "double-whammy"—is not just a specter haunting Trump's presidency but also a looming threat over the Republican party's aspirations to retain control of Congress in the 2026 elections.

A Looming Lame Duck Presidency

Should the Republicans lose majority control in one or both chambers of Congress, Trump would be relegated to a lame duck president—a scenario that the White House is purportedly concerned about. This concern is not unfounded, considering the potential political paralysis and diminished ability to enact or block legislation.

The Misinterpretation of Job Numbers

The Trump administration's portrayal of Biden's economy as a "disaster" stands in stark contrast to the reality depicted by the job numbers. Economists have clarified that the labor market under Biden was adjusting to a sustainable level, where fewer new jobs were needed to keep the unemployment rate from rising—a trend that has only intensified under Trump's presidency.

The Affordability Crisis: Trump's New Nemesis

Jared Bernstein, a former Biden economic adviser, succinctly captures the essence of the political fallout from economic downturns. The "affordability crisis" is a universal scapegoat, indiscriminately blamed on incumbents across the globe. Trump, despite his attempts to deflect responsibility, now finds himself squarely in the crosshairs of this crisis.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

The article serves as a cautionary tale about the perils of unkept promises and economic mismanagement. Trump's predicament underscores a fundamental truth in politics: accountability is inescapable. As the 2026 midterms approach, the Republican party, helmed by a beleaguered Trump, stands at a precipice. Whether they can course-correct in time remains to be seen, but one thing is clear—their fate is inexorably tied to the whims of an increasingly disillusioned electorate.
