'Not how the law works': Experts slam 'dinkus' Tom Homan's attacks on wrongly deported man

On Wednesday, President Donald Trump's advisor Tom Homan attacked Kilmar Ábrego García, the Maryland man the government admits was wrongfully deported to a prison in El Salvador.

Homan serves as Trump's "border czar," a position the president invented. Speaking to the press, Homan claimed of García, "He got more due process than Laken Riley got."

Riley was a Georgia student killed by a 26-year-old Venezuelan man named José Antonio Ibarra, not by García.

The comments infuriated legal analysts and others who fact-checked Homan, but they also lobbed insults of their own.

Wonkette managing editor Evan Hurst commented, "Nazi PE teachers like Tom Homan dutifully bark out the name of a murdered white woman in response to every suggestion that immigrants are human beings. Play a different song, dips---."

Military historian Jill Sargent Russell, Ph.D., proposed a question: "Mr. Homan, what standard of due process do you want applied to yourself?"

"Homan and others say this line *a lot* but here's the thing: Laken Riley was never accused of a crime and the guy who attacked her was not an agent of the state, so the comparison doesn't apply!" posted Ian Carrillo, an author and sociologist studying race, class, and the environment.

Immigration lawyer Matt Cameron wrote on Blue Sky, "He has been using this line for at least a month now and it just doesn't make any more sense no matter how many times he says it."

Former Justice Department appointee Eric Columbus remarked, "Homan says Ábrego García was 'ordered deported twice.' But he omits that the immigration judge ordered that he could not be deported to El Salvador for fear he would be harmed there. Due process isn’t enough — the executive has to follow court orders issued during that process."

"If this were a normal administration—one not bent on defying an order of the Supreme Court—Homan would just say 'We disagree with the court’s ruling but we’re going to comply with it and we’ll do what we can to facilitate Mr. Abrego Garcia’s return to the United States,'" said Lawfare legal analyst Anna Bower.

Reuters crime and justice reporter Brad Heath questioned, "It is true that the government, which is bound by the Constitution's requirement that it provide due process, is expected to behave better than criminals?"

National security lawyer Bradley Moss said simply, "That’s not how the law works, dinkus."

"Toss Homan in prison and remind him he got more due process than the BTK killer's victims," quipped ESL teacher Jean-Michel Connard.

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of Tom Homan's Distorted Logic

In a recent display of twisted rhetoric, Tom Homan, President Trump's self-styled "border czar," launched a scathing and baseless attack on Kilmar Ábrego García, a Maryland man wrongfully deported to El Salvador. This case not only highlights the flagrant misuse of power within the Trump administration but also reveals a disturbing trend of dehumanizing rhetoric against immigrants. Let's dissect the layers of misinformation and outright fallacies peddled by Homan, and why his statements are not just wrong, but dangerously misleading.

The Misleading Comparison

Homan's comparison of the due process received by García to that of Laken Riley, a completely unrelated case involving a tragic death caused by José Antonio Ibarra, a Venezuelan man, is not just apples to oranges; it's deceitful. This comparison is a gross manipulation intended to deflect from the government's admitted mistake, attempting to muddy the waters by invoking an emotional and unrelated incident. This tactic, as noted by Ian Carrillo, highlights an egregious misunderstanding (or intentional misrepresentation) of what due process entails, especially since Riley's case and García's deportation are unrelated legally and contextually.

The Unwarranted Attacks

Homan's comments have rightly drawn ire and ridicule from various quarters. The response from Evan Hurst, calling out Homan's tactic as a repetitive and dehumanizing strategy against immigrants, strikes at the heart of the matter. The caustic sarcasm from commentators like Jean-Michel Connard, suggesting that Homan deserves a taste of his own medicine, although extreme, underscores the frustration and anger towards Homan's flippant disregard for legal norms and human dignity.

The Ignored Due Process

Eric Columbus and Anna Bower pointedly highlight a crucial detail that Homan deliberately omits: the court's decision that García could not be deported to El Salvador due to credible fears for his safety. This omission is not just a rhetorical sleight of hand; it's a glaring example of the administration's tendency to flout judicial orders when they contradict their agenda. The executive branch's blatant disregard for the judiciary's role is alarming, hinting at a deeper erosion of the checks and balances that underpin the United States' democratic framework.

The Legal Misconception

Legal experts and analysts, including Brad Heath and Bradley Moss, have criticized Homan's flawed understanding of the law and due process. The suggestion that the government, bound by constitutional obligations, should somehow mirror the behavior of criminals in its treatment of individuals is absurd and legally unfounded. These critiques shine a light on the Trump administration's broader disdain for legal norms and the constitutional rights of individuals, especially those caught in the immigration enforcement machinery.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Tom Homan's comments are not just a series of unfortunate statements; they represent a dangerous precedent in the discourse surrounding immigration and due process in the United States. The attempt to justify wrongful actions by drawing false equivalences, coupled with a blatant disregard for judicial orders, sets a troubling tone for the rule of law under this administration.

What we are witnessing is not merely a systemic error; it's a calculated assault on the very principles of justice and human dignity. In an era where facts are under siege, it's imperative that such distortions are met with rigorous scrutiny and public condemnation. The fight for truth and justice must continue, louder and more determined than ever.
