Conway joined The Bulwark's publisher, Sara Longwell, on the podcast "George Conway Explains It All (to Sarah Longwell)" to discuss Trump's recent moves since the press conference survivors of Epstein's crimes held at the U.S. Capitol this week. Since then, Trump has called the Epstein files a "Democrat hoax that just won't go away." He's also made a series of other seemingly rash decisions that Conway argued meant he was feeling the heat.

"I think also the pressure that he's feeling because of these files, the fear of being exposed," Conway said. "I don't know what exactly is in these files. I think it makes him more manic."

"And it also makes the people around him get the understanding that they can get more out of him and they can please him by saying, 'Let's do this,' 'Sign this,' and he gets kind of hyper," he continued.

Longwell argued that Trump's manic state could help explain his recent threats to send National Guard troops to Chicago, redistricting Texas' election map, and declaring "emergencies that don't exist."

"As far as I'm concerned, part of deploying the National Guard to D.C. when there was absolutely not an emergency happening was to change the conversation away from Epstein," Longwell said.

Conway added that Trump seems to be navigating a "mixed motive" because of his health.

"I do think he's worried about his own mortality," Conway said. "This bit about I want to go to heaven and I think he's feeling the pressure in a lot of different ways that's causing him to become more, I don't know if manic is really the right word, but hyper."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tumult: The Epstein Files Frenzy

In a recent episode of "George Conway Explains It All (to Sarah Longwell)", conservative lawyer George Conway unpacks a narrative so convoluted, it could only belong to the political circus surrounding President Donald Trump. The focus? The renewed scrutiny of the Jeffrey Epstein files and its apparent psychological toll on Trump. Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned journalist.

Trump's "Democrat Hoax" Defense: A Classic Redirect

Trump's dismissal of the Epstein files as a "Democrat hoax" is as predictable as it is laughable. It's his go-to move—when in doubt, blame the Democrats. However, this isn't just any scandal; it's the Epstein scandal, a saga so sordid it makes House of Cards look like child's play. The fact that Trump is feeling the heat is as clear as day. His attempt to label it a hoax is a poorly veiled attempt to deflect and minimize a potentially explosive situation.

Rash Decisions: A Sign of Desperation?

George Conway and Sara Longwell suggest that Trump's recent erratic decisions are symptoms of his distress. Sending National Guard troops to Chicago, meddling with Texas' election map, and declaring non-existent emergencies are not the actions of a stable leader. They are, as Longwell points out, distractions—smoke bombs designed to shift the public's gaze away from the Epstein files. It's a classic Trump maneuver: when the going gets tough, the tough get going... in a completely different direction.

Manic Moves and Mixed Motives

The alleged manic state Trump finds himself in, according to Conway, is both a cause for concern and a reflection of his fear of exposure. The idea that Trump is making decisions based on a mix of fear, a desire to please his inner circle, and concerns about his mortality is deeply troubling. It paints the picture of a president not guided by policy or the well-being of the country, but by personal insecurities and existential dread.

Reality Check: The Bigger Picture

The discussion between Conway and Longwell, while insightful, only scratches the surface. It raises more questions than it answers. What exactly is in the Epstein files that has Trump so spooked? How deep do the connections run? And most importantly, how can the leader of the free world be so easily rattled by the potential for personal scandal?

As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: Trump's handling of the Epstein files controversy is emblematic of his presidency—chaotic, defensive, and ever so revealing. The implications of what these files might contain and how they relate to Trump remain to be seen. But for now, it's evident that the pressure is mounting, and Trump's facade is cracking.

In conclusion, while Trump tries to navigate this political minefield, his actions betray a man desperately trying to keep his secrets buried. However, as history often shows, the truth has a way of coming to light.
