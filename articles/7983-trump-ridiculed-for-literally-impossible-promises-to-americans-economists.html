Trump ridiculed for ‘literally impossible’ promises to Americans: economists

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on during the announcement of new fuel economy standards, in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 3, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump has repeatedly boasted that revenue from his tariffs will be so substantial that it could fund $2,000 rebate checks for Americans and even eliminate income tax altogether, though even under the rosiest scenarios, economists are saying Trump’s promises are “impossible.”

“We are talking complete fantasy here,” said Daniel Shaviro, a professor at NYU Law, who called Trump’s claim that tariff revenue could replace income taxes “not feasible at all,” speaking with Newsweek in its report Saturday. “Tariffs probably can’t supply even as much as 10 percent of the revenues derived from U.S. individual income tax revenues alone.”

On Tuesday, Trump claimed that revenue from his tariffs would be “so enormous that you’re not going to have income tax to pay,” having repeated a similar claim last month. According to the Treasury Department, around $195 billion in tariff revenue was collected during fiscal year 2025, and the Trump administration has projected annual tariff revenues reaching “towards a trillion-dollar number” in the future.

Yet, even with these projections, tariff revenue still didn’t come close to plugging the hole that would be created by eliminating federal income tax, which accounts for more than half of all government revenue.

“If you pushed tariffs to their revenue-maximizing limit – which would be very unwise for a host of considerations – they would bring in less than $400 billion a year, a small fraction (about one sixth) of what the income tax raises,” said economist Kimberly Clausing, calling Trump’s claims a “mathematical impossibility,” speaking with Newsweek.

Trump’s tariff policy is at a crossroads this month with the Supreme Court potentially ruling it unlawful as soon as this month. With justices having already heard arguments, and the majority of them responding to the Trump administration’s arguments for the tariffs with skepticism, the president’s key foreign policy could be flipped on its head in a matter of weeks.

“[It is] literally impossible for tariffs to fully replace income taxes,” said Alan Wolff, a senior fellow at the Peterson Institute for International Economics, speaking with Newsweek.

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Fantasy: Trump’s Impossible Promises

In the latest episode of political fantasy, President Donald Trump has made assertions so bold and detached from reality, they would almost be comedic if they weren’t so fundamentally misleading. Trump’s grandiose claim that revenue from his tariffs could not only fund $2,000 rebate checks for Americans but also eliminate income tax altogether is a fiscal fairy tale that defies basic economic principles.

The Realm of Impossibility

According to Daniel Shaviro, a professor at NYU Law, Trump’s suggestion that tariff revenue could replace income taxes is “not feasible at all.” This isn’t just an opinion; it’s grounded in stark economic reality. The notion is described as “complete fantasy,” and for good reason. In fiscal year 2025, the Treasury Department collected approximately $195 billion in tariff revenue, a figure that pales in comparison to the gargantuan sum required to abolish federal income tax.

Trump’s administration, ever the optimist, projects annual tariff revenues reaching “towards a trillion-dollar number.” Yet, even this ambitious forecast falls drastically short of the mark. Federal income tax is the government’s bread and butter, accounting for more than half of all revenue. To think that tariffs could plug the hole left by eliminating this tax is not just optimistic; it’s delusional.

A Mathematical Impossibility

Economist Kimberly Clausing cuts through Trump’s rhetoric with the precision of Occam’s Razor, calling his claims a “mathematical impossibility.” If tariffs were pushed to their revenue-maximizing limit, a strategy fraught with its own myriad of economic hazards, they would still only generate less than $400 billion a year. This is about one-sixth of what the income tax raises, highlighting the chasm between Trump’s promises and fiscal reality.

Supreme Skepticism

Adding to the drama, Trump’s tariff policy is under scrutiny by the Supreme Court, with justices already expressing skepticism towards the administration’s arguments. The potential for these tariffs to be ruled unlawful looms large, which could render Trump’s claims even more moot than they already are.

The Bottom Line

Trump’s vision of a U.S. funded by tariffs, sans income tax, is not just a pipe dream; it’s a dangerous misrepresentation of economic feasibility. Alan Wolff, a senior fellow at the Peterson Institute for International Economics, succinctly captures the essence of Trump’s claims: “literally impossible.” This episode serves as a stark reminder of the importance of grounding policy discussions in fact rather than fantasy. As we navigate the complex world of economic policy, let’s leave the fairy tales for bedtime stories, not fiscal strategy.
