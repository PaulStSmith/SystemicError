4 Trump-voting states have 'most egregious water quality' — as DOGE moves to 'delete' safety regulations

A recent study has identified that the United States counties with "the most egregious water quality violations" are mainly found in four states: West Virginia, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, and Oklahoma, all of which supported President Donald Trump in the last presidential election.

According to a study highlighted by The Hill, Wyoming County in West Virginia emerged as the worst offender, having the highest number of violations within a single water system. The study was published on Tuesday in the international journal Risk Analysis.

The authors of the study also found that around 2 million Americans — roughly the same as the entire population of Nebraska —lack access to running water, and this issue tends to be concentrated in specific areas.

They wrote that this high number is neither equally nor proportionally distributed across the population.

The study identified various violations, including non-compliance with the Safe Drinking Water Act, such as exceeding maximum contaminant limits, failing to follow mandated water treatment procedures and lacking monitoring schedules or communication with customers.

“Our results suggest that privatization alone is not a solution,” lead author Alex Segrè Cohen, said in a statement, per The Hill.

The author hoped that lawmakers would take note of the study and regulators would be able to utilize the research to improve their water management strategies.

However, the administration appears to be expediting its efforts to cut the size of the federal government, including doing away with safety regulations. Over 400 federal agencies, officials appointed Trump are reportedly collaborating with tech billionaire Elon Musk's Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE). Their goal is to initiate a significant new phase in their effort to reduce the size of the federal government through "deregulation on a mass scale."

According to a New York Times report published Tuesday, the president has devised a way to reverse regulations "swiftly and permanently" without going through the lengthy legal process that usually takes place before deregulations.

The agencies being targeted govern "almost every aspect of American life," the report said.

“Many people don’t realize how high the American quality of life is because of the competent and stable enforcement of regulations, and if that goes away a lot of lives are at risk,” Steve Cicala, co-director of the National Bureau of Economic Research’s Project on the Economic Analysis of Regulation, told the Times.

“This affects airplane safety, baby formula safety, the safety of meat, vegetables and packaged foods, the water that you drink, how you get to work safely and whether you’re safe in your workplace," he added.

Sparky's Analysis:

The Toxic Brew of Neglect and Deregulation: A Deep Dive into America's Water Crisis

In a revelation that is as shocking as it is unacceptable, a recent study has laid bare the grim reality of water quality violations across the United States. Concentrated in West Virginia, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, and Oklahoma, the findings underscore a systemic failure that disproportionately affects regions that lent their support to President Donald Trump in the last presidential election. These are not mere coincidences but a reflection of a broader, more disturbing trend of environmental neglect and political abandonment.

The Dire State of Water Quality

Wyoming County, West Virginia, has been flagged as the worst offender, leading a dismal pack with the highest number of violations within a single water system. This isn't just a statistic; it's a damning indictment of the state of public utilities and the oversight meant to safeguard them. The study, published in the international journal Risk Analysis, points to a failure that is as widespread as it is severe, affecting around 2 million Americans. This figure is not just a number but a stark representation of the population of an entire state like Nebraska being denied access to a basic human necessity: clean running water.

Violations and Non-Compliance: A Pattern of Neglect

The study identifies a series of egregious violations, including exceeding maximum contaminant limits, failing to adhere to mandated water treatment procedures, and a lack of monitoring or communication with customers. These aren’t mere oversights; they're a blatant disregard for the health and safety of millions. The conclusion that privatization is not a panacea should hardly come as a surprise. Instead, it serves as a cold reminder that the solution to systemic issues requires more than a simplistic change of ownership. It calls for a comprehensive, regulated approach that prioritizes public health over profit.

Deregulation: A Dangerous Path Forward

The current administration's rush to dismantle safety regulations only adds fuel to the fire. With over 400 federal agencies and officials appointed by Trump collaborating with Elon Musk's Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) to cut down the size of the federal government, the implications are chilling. This isn't just about reducing bureaucratic bloat; it's a concerted effort to strip away the very protections that ensure the safety and well-being of the American populace.

The endeavor to deregulate "on a mass scale," bypassing the usual legal safeguards, is not just reckless—it's a calculated risk with human lives. The areas targeted for deregulation span "almost every aspect of American life," from airplane and baby formula safety to the safety of meat, vegetables, packaged foods, and critically, the water we drink. This isn't merely an administrative reshuffling; it's a fundamental threat to public safety.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The findings of this study are a wake-up call that cannot be ignored. The connection between political allegiance and environmental neglect is a narrative that needs to end. The pursuit of deregulation and government reduction at the expense of essential safety regulations is a dangerous game, one that risks the health and safety of millions of Americans. It’s time for regulators, lawmakers, and the public to demand better, to insist on a government that prioritizes the well-being of its citizens over the interests of a select few. The water crisis and the broader implications of deregulation are a systemic error that we can and must correct. The quality of life in America, indeed the very lives of its citizens, depends on it.
