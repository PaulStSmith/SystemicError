Trump pushing 'unusual' GOP move over worries he 'is going to get impeached': conservative

The White House is driving the showdown between Texas Democrats and Republicans over a gerrymandering scheme to protect President Donald Trump from getting impeached for a third time, according to a reporter from a conservative publication.

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott has asked the state Supreme Court to remove state Rep. Gene Wu from office as the so-called "ringleader" of the Democrats who fled the state to deprive the Republican-led legislature of a quorum needed to pass a controversial redistricting plan, and National Review correspondent Audrey Fahlberg told "CNN This Morning" why the president's team was pushing the move.

"The White House is driving this because clearly they are worried about losing the midterms," Fahlberg said. "They're convinced that if House Democrats flip the House, that Trump is going to get impeached again, right. The 'big beautiful bill' is not polling super well right now, so they're going on offense here. They're driving this into motion in Texas. They're looking at other states, as well. We may see this continue in states like Florida, Indiana. Vice President [JD] Vance is meeting with state legislators there and their Republican governor."

Redistricting is typically done after each 10-year census, so the Texas legislature's mid-decade redraw of the congressional map to favor Republicans is unusual, and so is the impetus behind the plan.

"This is unusual," said CNN's Edward-Isaac Dovere. "I would say try to find an eighth-grader who's finished their government and civics class and explain to them that this is what's going on – it would be impossible. We have a president who has said explicitly that this is not about some problem with the map, other than he wants more seats that would be Republican-leaning and has told the governor of Texas to do this and the Republican legislators in Texas to do this, and they have just said, sure. The time between when he asked for it and when they called the special sessions [was] a couple of weeks, right, and then Democrats, who have no other option but to flee the state, they would, if they sat there, they would be they would have to be part of this passing. They don't want to be, so they ran away, and now we're talking about law enforcement coming after them and and being removed from office."

"This is this is just not the way the government is supposed to work," Dovere added, "and it is part of the reason why redistricting, when it happens, as messed up as it is when Republicans do it, when Democrats do it, and all the gerrymandering goes on, at least it only happens usually once every 10 years."

Our Analysis:

The Gerrymandering Gambit: A Calculated Chaos

In an audacious move that reeks of desperation and disregard for democratic norms, the White House is reportedly spearheading an initiative that pits Texas Democrats against Republicans in a high-stakes battle over redistricting. According to Audrey Fahlberg of National Review, the impetus behind this calculated chaos is a fear of impeachment repercussions for President Donald Trump, should the Democrats seize control in the midterms.

The Texas Tango: Governor Greg Abbott's request to the state Supreme Court to strip state Rep. Gene Wu of his office, labeling him the "ringleader" of the Democrats' exodus, is a glaringly transparent attempt to quash opposition. This exodus was a last-ditch effort to stymie a Republican-led legislature from passing a contentious redistricting plan, a move that smacks of desperation and a blatant disregard for democratic process.

A Fear-Driven Strategy: The White House's involvement, as Fahlberg suggests, is not about ensuring fair political play or addressing constituents' needs but is a fear-driven strategy to maintain power. The claim that the "big beautiful bill" is floundering in polls and the consequent offensive stance in Texas—and potentially other states—reveals a presidency more concerned with self-preservation than governance.

Unusual and Undemocratic: CNN's Edward-Isaac Dovere hits the nail on the head: explaining the current scenario to an eighth-grader would be an exercise in futility. The President's explicit demand for a map redraw to create more Republican-leaning seats, followed by a swift and compliant response from Texas' governor and Republican legislators, is a stark deviation from democratic norms. The rapid succession from request to special session, and the subsequent pursuit of fleeing Democrats, underscores a troubling willingness to bypass ethical considerations for political gain.

A Distorted Democratic Process: The fleeing of Democrats, while dramatic, underscores a fundamental truth: they were left with no viable alternative. Participating in the session would mean becoming complicit in a scheme that undermines the very essence of democracy. The threat of law enforcement and removal from office for these legislators is not only an intimidation tactic but also a dire reflection of the lengths to which the administration is willing to go to secure its objectives.

A Decade of Distortion: As Dovere aptly notes, while redistricting and gerrymandering are not new and are practiced by both parties, the frequency and brazenness with which it is being pursued currently are unprecedented. The every-decade occurrence has morphed into a tool wielded with alarming regularity, threatening the integrity of the electoral process and eroding public trust in governmental institutions.

In conclusion, the unfolding drama in Texas is not just a local or state issue but a national alarm bell. It signals a disturbing shift towards power consolidation at the expense of democratic principles. The actions of the White House, Governor Abbott, and the Republican legislators in Texas are not just maneuvers in a political game but a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy when those in power prioritize their survival over the sanctity of the electoral process. As observers and participants in this democracy, it is incumbent upon us to recognize these tactics for what they are: desperate attempts to cling to power by any means necessary.
