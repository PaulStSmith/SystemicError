President Donald Trump’s recent firing of Bureau of Labor Statistics Commissioner Erika McEntarfer following the agency’s publication of an abysmal jobs report is just the latest example of “Trump’s evil super power,” said journalist Garrett Graff Wednesday while speaking on CNN.

“I think certainly this is part of what is to me Donald Trump's evil superpower, which is the way that he makes us focus in on and care about minor government jobs that most Americans should never have to think about; postmaster general, (or) the person who's running the Treasury payment system,” Graff said.

“BLS is as technocratic a job as there possibly could be in the U.S. government, and it's one where much of our finance sector, much of our economy sort of runs on being able to have unimpeachable data coming out of that system. The moment we begin to undermine the possible integrity there, it's to our detriment.”

Published on Aug. 1, the latest jobs report out of the BLS saw job creation for May and June revised down by 258,000 less jobs than initially assessed, marking the lowest monthly job growth since December 2020. The report “really got under Trump’s skin” according to White House officials, who went on to fire McEntafer just hours after its publication.

Trump would go on to tap Heritage Foundation economist E.J. Antoni to lead the BLS, a pick that has drawn scrutiny over Antoni’s lack of experience in labor statistics and partisan past for a position that Graff argued should be apolitical.

Lulu Garcia-Navarro, a CNN contributor, said that in many cases, Antoni was the worst choice Trump could have made for leading the BLS, citing him as the “worst-case scenario” for those across the political spectrum.

“He is, for many people, the worst-case scenario,” Garcia-Navarro said. “He has very strong critics on the right because they see him as a partisan who really doesn't have the background, he comes from this sort of mediocre university, he hasn't published a lot of papers, he is not considered to be an economic giant if you will. And of course on the left, people see him as just highly partisan.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Latest Move: Political Interference or Accountability?

In a move that has sent shockwaves through the corridors of power and the very foundations of governmental integrity, President Donald Trump's decision to fire Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Commissioner Erika McEntarfer starkly illustrates what journalist Garrett Graff aptly describes as "Trump’s evil superpower." This phrase isn't just a catchy soundbite; it encapsulates the disturbing trend of politicizing ostensibly non-partisan roles within the federal government. Let's dissect the layers of this latest affront to governmental norms and the implications it holds.

The Sinister Art of Distraction

First off, Graff's observation hits the nail squarely on the head. Trump has, indeed, mastered the art of misdirection—turning the public's attention towards minor bureaucratic positions that, in the normal course of governance, should operate quietly in the background. However, by thrusting these roles into the contentious arena of political debate, Trump isn't just seeking to distract; he's undermining the bedrock of professional integrity and non-partisanship that these positions require. The role of BLS Commissioner, as Graff rightly points out, is quintessentially technocratic, designed to function away from the political limelight, ensuring the objectivity and reliability of crucial economic data.

The Danger of Undermining Integrity

The crux of the matter lies in the deleterious effects of undermining the BLS's integrity. The agency's output isn't just numbers on a page; it's the lifeblood of economic policy, investment decisions, and public confidence. The moment these figures become tainted by suspicion of political interference, the fallout extends beyond mere partisan bickering—it strikes at the heart of economic stability and global confidence in U.S. data. The downward revision of job creation numbers in the latest BLS report, though a natural and not uncommon occurrence in statistical analysis, became the pretext for McEntarfer's dismissal, a move that smacks not of accountability but of retaliation.

The Controversial Successor

Enter E.J. Antoni, Trump's choice to lead the BLS. The selection of Antoni, with his thin résumé in labor statistics and overtly partisan background, is not just questionable; it's a frontal assault on the principle of apolitical governance. Critics from both sides of the aisle have voiced concerns, highlighting Antoni's lack of requisite expertise and his partisan leanings as fundamentally incompatible with the role's demands for unbiased economic stewardship. Lulu Garcia-Navarro's characterization of Antoni as the "worst-case scenario" isn't hyperbole—it's a stark warning about the potential degradation of the BLS's credibility and effectiveness.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Trump's dismissal of McEntarfer and the appointment of Antoni represent more than just another episode in the ongoing saga of political turbulence; they signify a dangerous precedent. The manipulation of key government positions for political gain not only erodes trust in those institutions but also jeopardizes the very fabric of democratic governance. As the BLS faces its own moment of truth under Antoni's leadership, the broader question looms: Will the American public continue to stand by as the integrity of its institutions is systematically dismantled?

In this instance, Trump's "evil superpower" isn't just his ability to divert attention—it's his capacity to reshape the government's apolitical machinery into tools of partisan advantage. And that, dear readers, is a systemic error we cannot afford to ignore.
