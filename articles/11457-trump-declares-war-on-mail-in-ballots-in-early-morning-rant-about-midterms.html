Trump declares war on mail-in ballots in early morning rant about midterms

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to reporters aboard Air Force One on his way to Alaska meet with Russian President Vladimir Putin on to negotiate an end to the war in Ukraine, U.S., August 15,2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump vowed to eradicate mail-in ballots and “controversial voting machines” via an executive order ahead of the 2026 midterm elections Monday in an early-morning rant on social media.

“I am going to lead a movement to get rid of mail-in ballots, and also, while we’re at it, highly ‘inaccurate, very expensive, and seriously controversial voting machines, which cost ten times more than accurate and sophisticated watermark paper,” Trump wrote in an online post on his social media platform Truth Social. “The mail-in ballot hoax, using voting machines that are a complete and total disaster, must end now!”

Trump has long been a critic of mail-in voting, arguing that the practice is fraught with fraud, despite no evidence supporting his claims. He aggressively attacked mail-in voting in the lead-up to the 2020 election, rhetoric that led to significant party splits by vote methods, with Republican voters leading in Election Day voting, and Democrats, in mail-in voting.

In his Monday morning rant, Trump also falsely claimed that the United States was “the only country in the world that uses mail-in voting,” despite dozens of nations regularly using the practice — and with less restrictions than in the United States.

Trump went on to pressure state leaders not to oppose his “movement” to eradicate mail-in voting, declaring that states were “merely an agent” of his office, and should do what the office of the presidency “tells them.”

“Remember, the states are merely an ‘agent’ for the federal government in counting and tabulating the votes; they must do what the federal government, as represented by the president of the United States, tells them, for the good of our country, to do,” Trump wrote.

“Democrats are virtually unelectable without using this completely disproven mail-in scam. Elections can never be honest with mail-in ballots/voting, and everybody, in particular the Democrats, knows this. I, and the Republican Party, will fight like hell to bring honesty and integrity back to our elections.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump's Assault on Mail-In Voting: A Fact-Checked Analysis

In what seems to be an unyielding crusade against the democratic process, President Donald Trump has once again taken to his platform of choice, Truth Social, to launch an attack on mail-in ballots and voting machines. His proposition? An executive order to eliminate these voting methods ahead of the 2026 midterm elections. This move, cloaked in the guise of promoting "honesty and integrity" in elections, is riddled with misinformation, a blatant disregard for the constitution, and a perilous aversion to democratic principles.

The Myth of Mail-In Voting Fraud

Trump's tirade against mail-in voting is nothing new. It's a continuation of a baseless narrative that has been categorically debunked by numerous studies and election security experts. The claims of widespread fraud in mail-in voting are not just unfounded; they are dangerous. They serve to undermine public trust in the electoral process, without any evidence to support such claims. It's imperative to note that mail-in voting has been a part of the American electoral system for decades, utilized by millions of voters across the political spectrum, and has proven to be both secure and effective.

The Misrepresentation of Voting Machines

The attack on "controversial voting machines" is similarly unfounded. Trump's assertion that these machines are "inaccurate, very expensive, and seriously controversial" and that they "cost ten times more than accurate and sophisticated watermark paper" is misleading at best. Voting technology undergoes rigorous testing and certification processes to ensure accuracy and security. While no system is perfect, the portrayal of voting machines as fundamentally flawed and overly expensive is a gross exaggeration that ignores the realities of modern electoral systems.

A Dangerous Misunderstanding of Federalism

Perhaps most alarming is Trump's claim that states are "merely an 'agent' for the federal government" in the context of elections. This statement reveals a profound misunderstanding (or intentional misrepresentation) of the U.S. Constitution and the principle of federalism. The Constitution explicitly grants states the power to regulate their own elections. The idea that the president could, or should, dictate how states conduct elections is not only undemocratic—it's unconstitutional.

The Global Context Ignored

Trump's assertion that the United States is "the only country in the world that uses mail-in voting" is patently false. Many democracies around the globe utilize mail-in voting to various extents, and with high levels of success and voter satisfaction. This claim is not just factually incorrect; it's a misleading attempt to isolate the U.S. as an outlier in electoral practices, when in fact, it's in good company.

The Real Agenda: Disenfranchisement

Underneath the veneer of concern for electoral integrity lies a more sinister motive: voter disenfranchisement. By attacking mail-in voting and spreading misinformation about voting machines, Trump and his allies aim to restrict the accessibility of the voting process. It's a tactic designed to suppress turnout, particularly among demographics that may not vote in their favor. The rhetoric of "fighting like hell" to bring back "honesty and integrity" to elections is a smokescreen. The real battle being waged is against the foundational principles of democracy itself.

Conclusion

Trump's call to eradicate mail-in ballots and demonize voting machines is not about safeguarding democracy; it's about undermining it. It's a strategy rooted in misinformation, fear-mongering, and an alarming willingness to subvert the democratic process for political gain. As we move closer to the 2026 midterm elections, it's critical that the public and policymakers alike recognize these tactics for what they are: a direct attack on the ability of the American people to vote freely and fairly.
