'Trump is exposed': Columnist speculates who 'will deliver the final blow' to president

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 4, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Pot Calling the Kettle Frail: Trump’s Decline Sparks Déjà Vu and Hypocrisy

President Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C. on Dec. 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

A columnist Wednesday described the “awkward déjà vu” in Washington, D.C. as President Donald Trump shows visible decline and the question arising: who, or what, will push him out or succeed him.

The Daily Beast‘s Joanna Coles, Chief Creative and Content Officer, described in a column how Trump, who has repeatedly attacked former President Joe Biden‘s cognitive abilities, frequently suggested that Biden was mentally unfit to serve as president, mockingly portrayed Biden as senile, referenced his age and apparent moments of confusion, has now faced the same concerns and criticism over his own mental and health capacity.

Only this time it’s not quite the same.

“But here’s the real difference between Biden then and Trump now: Biden was hidden; Trump is exposed,” Coles wrote. “Biden’s aides did the talking; Trump insists on ad-libbing his own decline. Biden’s true deterioration emerged during the debate when he was isolated from all the people who routinely covered for him.”

Biden’s team described watching the former president “diminish in real time,” she said.

“They managed him like a fragile museum artifact: curated angles, limited exposure, a protective choreography the press reluctantly accepted,” Coles added. “Trump offers no such choreography.”

Between the physical signs — bruised hands, swollen ankles, dragging his right leg — and the verbal signs — saying the wrong words, repeating stories and grunting, it’s clear Trump is slipping, she explained. And in the past, it was viewed as impolite to comment on someone’s health and to avoid appearing ageist, but this hasn’t been the case with Trump.

“But it’s not nasty to remark on what we see. It’s accurate. Acting presidential isn’t a feeling, it’s a function. And right now the man running the country is showing all the signs of someone flailing at a job that he believes he’s performing flawlessly,” Coles wrote.

Coles compared this particular moment to a bullfight, as a picador on a horse waits for the next bull’s next move before it charges, pants and circles.

“The picador has taken away the bull’s escape, its dignity, its power,” Coles wrote. “The crowd knows. And the bull, swaying under the weight of its own failing strength, knows it, too.”

Trump, like the bull, she argued, will have to face what comes next.

“Agitated and angry, the bull may still have some fight left in him,” Coles wrote. “But the outcome is already a certainty. The only question left is which matador–Marjorie Taylor Greene? Mitch McConnell? Jeffrey Epstein? JD Vance?–will deliver the final blow.”

Our Analysis:

The Pot Calling the Kettle Frail: Trump’s Decline Sparks Déjà Vu and Hypocrisy

In a striking commentary that mirrors the political theater’s most ironic twists, Joanna Coles of The Daily Beast lays bare the disconcerting parallels and contrasts between the current scrutiny of President Donald Trump’s health and the past critiques he levied against former President Joe Biden. Trump, once the vocal critic of Biden’s cognitive faculties and physical stamina, is now the center of similar concerns. This situation reeks of irony so thick, it’s almost suffocating. Coles’s analysis, while sharp, perhaps doesn’t push hard enough on the accelerator of accountability. Let’s dive a bit deeper, shall we?

The Hypocrisy of Health Critiques

Firstly, the sheer irony of Trump’s predicament cannot be overstated. A man who built a significant part of his campaign narrative mocking Biden’s mental and physical fitness is now under the microscope for the very same reasons. The fact that Trump’s own health concerns are becoming visible and discussed openly marks a significant shift in the public discourse. Previously, it was ‘impolite’ to comment on such matters, but as Coles points out, there’s nothing ‘nasty’ about observing the apparent. However, what’s particularly nasty is the hypocrisy at play. Trump’s readiness to attack Biden on these grounds, only to find himself in a similar spotlight, is a rich tapestry of karma.

The Difference in Handling

Coles astutely contrasts the handling of Biden’s supposed decline with Trump’s current situation. Biden was ‘hidden,’ managed carefully by his team, a strategy that, while criticized, shielded him to some extent from public scrutiny. Trump, on the other hand, is as exposed as ever, his decline on full display for public consumption. This openness, whether by choice or circumstance, strips away any semblance of dignity from the office he holds. It’s a stark reminder that acting presidential is not just about feeling in command but demonstrating competence and stability, qualities Trump is apparently struggling to exhibit.

A Bull in Decline

The metaphor of the bullfight Coles employs is both vivid and telling. Trump, portrayed as the bull, is losing his fight, his power waning under the scrutiny and the weight of his office. Yet, the anticipation of who will deliver the final blow is a macabre reflection on the state of American politics. Names like Marjorie Taylor Greene, Mitch McConnell, and even Jeffrey Epstein are thrown into the mix - a commentary on the bizarre and unpredictable nature of political alliances and enmities. This analogy, while effective in illustrating Trump’s plight, also uncomfortably glamorizes the downfall, framing it as a spectacle rather than a serious concern for the country’s leadership and direction.

Conclusion: More Than Just Déjà Vu

What Coles brings to light is more than just an ‘awkward déjà vu.’ It’s a glaring spotlight on the cyclical, hypocritical nature of political critique and the fragility of those we elect to lead. While her commentary slices through the surface, revealing the irony and the spectacle, one must ponder deeper. The real question isn’t just about who will succeed Trump or push him out; it’s about the systemic issues that allow such a cycle of unfitness and hypocrisy to perpetuate. It’s about why we, as a society, are content to watch this bullfight unfold, year after year, election after election, without demanding more from our leaders and ourselves.

As we look towards the future, one can only hope for a break in this cycle. For a political arena that values capability over spectacle, dignity over decline, and truth over hypocrisy. But until then, we’re just spectators at a bullfight, waiting for the next chapter of this ongoing political saga to unfold.
