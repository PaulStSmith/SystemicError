The brutal truth about who swallowed the American middle class

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Jamie Dimon Is Not a Commentator. He Is the System.

The source story is about America’s wealth shift, but its real subject is power: who gets to shape policy, who gets excused for doing it, and how elite interests dress themselves up as public-minded expertise. Jamie Dimon is the useful example because he is not a fringe villain. He is the banker Democrats treat as credible, the executive who speaks in the language of civic concern while helping finance the conditions that keep most people stuck.

The Power Map

The decisive actors here are not “the market” in some abstract sense. They are billionaires, giant corporations, Wall Street banks, and the political officials who depend on them. The article lays out the mechanism plainly: big money floods elections, lobbyists multiply, taxes on the wealthy fall, antitrust weakens, and public policy bends toward the people who already have the most.

That is not confusion. It is governance by accumulation. The wealthy do not merely benefit from the system. They shape it, then point to the outcome as proof they were right all along.

Dimon’s Real Job

Dimon’s contradictions are the point, not a side note. He talks like a civic steward, but the source shows JPMorgan doing what a profit-maximizing giant bank does: lobbying for Trump tax cuts, financing fossil fuels, settling discrimination claims, processing transactions for Epstein, and paying workers salaries that leave them short of survival.

That is not hypocrisy in the casual sense. It is institutional role-playing. Dimon can appear on CNBC or in congressional testimony as a reasonable adult while his bank does the work of wealth extraction underneath. The performance of responsibility is part of the business model.

The Democratic Halo

The most revealing detail is not that Dimon flatters Trump when it suits him. It is that he is described as “the Democrats’ favorite CEO.” That tells you how much of the party establishment still mistakes access to capital for seriousness.

This is where Democrats earn criticism from inside the democratic coalition: too many leaders treat men like Dimon as wise guardians instead of what they are, which is concentrated private power with a public-relations department. If your political project depends on billionaires being comfortable, you are already negotiating against democracy before the fight starts.

Misdirection as Strategy

The article is right to identify the propaganda function of racial resentment, anti-immigrant fear, and anti-socialist panic. Those tactics are not accidental noise. They are useful cover. They keep working people fighting each other while the oligarchs keep the tax cuts, the subsidies, the deregulation, and the influence.

That is the deeper fraud in the political conversation. The public gets culture-war theater; the ruling class gets legislative transfer. The harm is real, deliberate, and organized. Calling it a misunderstanding only helps the people doing it.

What This Pattern Reveals

The larger pattern is oligarchy learning how to speak democracy fluently enough to survive it. It keeps elections, donor dinners, op-eds, and corporate social responsibility statements. It even keeps Democratic friendships. What it does not keep is any meaningful constraint on private wealth.

The answer is not to plead for better behavior from the same class of people who profit from the damage. It is to reduce their leverage. Big money in politics has to go. Monopolies have to be broken. Corporate welfare has to end. Labor power has to rise. And the country has to stop mistaking banker-friendly moderation for democratic seriousness.

If the richest people can finance the rules, write the rules, and then hire the country to praise the rules, democracy is just a ceremonial name for oligarchy.
