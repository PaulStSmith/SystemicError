MAGA lawyer urges Trump to use military to bust election denier from prison

President Donald Trump claimed to have pardoned an ally serving a prison sentence related to his efforts to contest the 2020 election results — a move Colorado Democrats called “meaningless” due to his lack of authority to release those convicted by the state.

But now her lawyer is urging the use of military forces to help get his client out of prison, The Independent reported.

Tina Peters, 70, a former Colorado election official, is currently serving a 9-year prison sentence on state-level charges, having been convicted in August 2024 of facilitating a security breach after she let prominent election denier and MyPillow salesman, Mike Lindell, enter a secure room and download data containing 2020 election results, as part of his effort to find evidence of fraud and contest Joe Biden’s defeat of Trump.

Following a series of federal pardons and dropped cases, Peters is now the only person serving prison time for actions related to Trump’s denial of the 2020 election results. Despite Trump claiming to have pardoned her this week, the president is only capable of commuting federal charges, and Colorado officials have dismissed the possibility of releasing Peters from custody at his command.

Appearing on Steve Bannon’s War Room podcast, her lawyer, Peter Ticktin, agreed with Bannon’s assertion that Trump should dispatch the 101st Airborne Division to free Peters from prison.

“Do I think it should be done? For who I am, yeah – I’d love to see that happen,” Ticktin said.

Ticktin had previously issued pleas for Trump to pardon his client, despite the president lacking the necessary authority. In response to the earlier pleas, Phil Weiser, the Democratic Attorney General for Colorado, said any attempt by Trump to pardon Peters “will not hold up.”

“One of the most basic principles of our constitution is that states have independent sovereignty and manage our own criminal justice systems without interference from the federal government,” Weiser said.

Shad Murib, chair of the Colorado Democratic Party, also said that Trump “has no legal authority” to facilitate Peters’ release from prison, and called the notion of a pardon from him “meaningless.”

“We’re not surprised by President Trump shouting into the wind and issuing a meaningless pardon for his friend and fellow election denier Tina Peters,” Murib said.

Though some state governors lack complete pardon powers for state-level charges, Colorado Gov. Jared Polis, a Democrat, does have complete pardon power, and has not indicated that he has any intention to pardon Peters.

Our Analysis:

The Pardon That Wasn’t: Trump’s Latest Political Theater

In a move that can only be described as a mixture of desperation and delusion, former President Donald Trump has claimed to have pardoned Tina Peters, a staunch ally and former Colorado election official, currently serving a nine-year sentence on state-level charges. The charges stem from Peters facilitating a security breach by allowing Mike Lindell, a well-known election denier and MyPillow salesman, to access and download data containing 2020 election results in a misguided attempt to prove fraud.

The Pardon with No Power

Trump’s claim of pardoning Peters is not just flawed; it’s fundamentally impossible. The president’s pardon power does not extend to state convictions. This is not merely a technicality but a foundational principle of the United States’ federal system, which recognizes the sovereignty of state governments in managing their own criminal justice systems. Colorado Democrats, including Attorney General Phil Weiser and Colorado Democratic Party Chair Shad Murib, have rightly called Trump’s move “meaningless” due to his lack of authority to release those convicted by the state.

A Dangerous Proposal

Adding fuel to the fire, Peters’ lawyer, Peter Ticktin, has taken to Steve Bannon’s War Room podcast, suggesting that the use of military force — particularly the 101st Airborne Division — could be employed to extricate Peters from prison. This suggestion is not just reckless; it’s a dangerous flirtation with the idea of using military power to override the legal processes and decisions of a state. It’s a stark reminder of the lengths to which some are willing to go to undermine the principles of democracy and rule of law in favor of political loyalty and conspiracy theories.

The Reality of State Sovereignty

The response from Colorado officials underscores a critical aspect of the United States’ constitutional framework: the independence of state sovereignty. This principle ensures that states have the authority to manage their criminal justice systems without interference from the federal government. It’s a safeguard against overreach and an essential component of the country’s checks and balances.

The Misguided Pursuit of a Pardon

The entire episode underscores the ongoing challenges facing the United States in the wake of the 2020 election. Despite a series of federal pardons and dropped cases related to efforts contesting the election results, Peters remains the only individual serving prison time for actions directly connected to these efforts. This fact alone should prompt a reflection on the nature of these actions and their consequences.

The Final Verdict

Trump’s purported pardon of Tina Peters is not just a legal impossibility; it’s a stark illustration of the ongoing attempts to manipulate public perception and undermine legal and democratic processes. It’s a reminder that the battle for truth and democracy is far from over and that vigilance against such baseless assertions is more crucial than ever.

The suggestion to use military force to free Peters is a dangerous escalation of rhetoric that threatens the very foundations of the United States’ democratic system. It’s a moment that should alarm all Americans, regardless of political affiliation, and prompt a unified stand against such undemocratic impulses.
