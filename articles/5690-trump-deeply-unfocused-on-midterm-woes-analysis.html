Trump 'deeply unfocused' on midterm woes: analysis

Donald Trump remains “deeply unfocused” on actual voter concerns as the GOP’s midterm prospects continue to dim, according to a new analysis from MS NOW, and his recent decision regarding a controversial statue is another example of his actual priorities.

Writing for MS NOW on Thursday, journalist Hayes Brown broke down Trump’s recent announcement that a statue of explorer Christopher Columbus would be installed on the grounds of the White House, delving into the statue’s history and the president’s questionable motivations for installing it. Above all, he concluded that the move continued to show Trump’s disregard for addressing the issues voters actually care about.

The statue itself is a recreation of one that was previously damaged when it was thrown into Baltimore’s Inner Harbor by protesters in the summer of 2020. The recreation was completed by the son of the original sculptor, based on 3D scans of the original. Trump had reportedly considered adding the statue and others to the White House grounds near the end of his first term as part of a proposed “National Garden of American Heroes,” but the concept was abandoned when he lost his reelection bid.

In announcing the statue’s move on Wednesday, the White House proclaimed Columbus to be a “hero.” As Brown noted, Trump has maintained a consistent fixation on the reputation of Columbus, signing an executive order in October to bring Columbus Day “back from the ashes.” The federal government had never stopped recognizing that holiday, with Trump’s order mostly demoting the status of Indigenous Peoples Day. Speaking at a press briefing last month, Trump suggested that this move ought to endear him and the GOP to Italian-American voters in the midterms.

“Remember when you go to the voting booths, I reinstated Columbus Day,” the president said. “That was an easy one, Christopher Columbus.”

However, as Brown argued, this latest pro-Columbus move will not “materially” help anybody and will not sway many voters in a year when material conditions, like the cost of living, are the most pressing political issues. Trump’s past comments claiming that “affordability” was a “scam” perpetuated against him by Democrats did little to sway voters, many of whom do not believe that he actually cares about the problems they face.

“Trump is nothing if not deeply transactional, even when the deal he’s offering makes sense to no one else. But despite his suggestion otherwise, it’s hard to see how the Columbus statue could sway the midterms,” Brown wrote. “What it does is highlight how much focus Trump has dedicated to everything but materially helping Americans. Nobody will be better off once the statue is in place at the White House, but Trump clearly believes that a small number of people might feel better enough to vote for a Republican this fall.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Misguided Priorities: A Scathing Review

In the latest circus act from the political arena, Donald Trump has once again demonstrated his remarkable ability to remain “deeply unfocused” on the actual concerns of voters. According to an analysis by MS NOW, Trump’s recent decision to install a statue of Christopher Columbus on the White House grounds is nothing more than a glaring testament to his skewed priorities. This move, dissected with incisive clarity by journalist Hayes Brown, serves as an emblematic distraction from the pressing issues that Americans face today.

A Statue Over Substance

The statue in question is not merely a piece of art; it is a recreation of one previously damaged during protests in 2020, symbolizing a deep-seated controversy over historical narratives and their implications on current societal dynamics. Trump’s decision to resurrect this particular statue, under the guise of honoring a “hero,” blatantly ignores the nuanced debates surrounding Columbus’s legacy and the indigenous peoples’ suffering.

Trump’s fixation on Columbus is not new. His executive order to resuscitate Columbus Day, under the pretense of it being “back from the ashes,” was a needless gesture that demoted the status of Indigenous Peoples Day. The federal holiday never ceased to exist, making Trump’s move a redundant attempt to appeal to Italian-American voters, as he himself suggested. “Remember when you go to the voting booths, I reinstated Columbus Day,” Trump declared, as if reinstating a holiday that never actually went away is a monumental achievement.

Misreading the Room

The core issue with Trump’s Columbus statue gambit is its utter irrelevance to the material conditions affecting Americans today. In a year where the cost of living soars and economic uncertainties loom large, the installation of a statue does nothing to alleviate the burdens on the average citizen. Trump’s past comments, dismissing “affordability” as a “scam” by Democrats, only add insult to injury, showcasing his disconnection from the realities of those he seeks to govern.

Brown’s analysis hits the nail on the head: Trump is “deeply transactional”, yet this transaction—this trade-off of focusing on symbolic gestures over substantive policy—benefits no one. It’s a stark reminder of Trump’s propensity to prioritize personal vendettas and cultural wars over the pressing needs of the electorate.

Conclusion: A Misallocation of Focus

The decision to install the Columbus statue, therefore, is not just a misstep; it is a glaring example of Trump’s misallocation of focus. It underscores a presidency more concerned with symbolic victories and appeasing certain voter segments than with addressing the material hardships faced by many Americans.

Hayes Brown’s critique in MS NOW is not just a condemnation of a singular decision by Trump; it’s an indictment of a broader pattern of behavior that prioritizes personal legacy and cultural battles over genuine, material aid to Americans. In the grand scheme of things, the statue is inconsequential. But as a symbol of Trump’s priorities, it is profoundly telling.

In a time when leadership requires attentiveness to the material and existential crises facing the nation, Trump’s preoccupations are a systemic error, a flaw in the priorities that should define the office he once held. As the GOP’s midterm prospects continue to dim, one can’t help but wonder: when will the focus shift from statues to stability, from Columbus to the cost of living? With Trump at the helm, it seems, never.
