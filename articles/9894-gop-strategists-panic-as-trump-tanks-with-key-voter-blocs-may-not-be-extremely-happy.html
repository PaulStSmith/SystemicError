GOP strategists panic as Trump tanks with key voter blocs: 'May not be extremely happy'

Trump overperformed last year with both Black and young voters; his support among Black voters increased to 15% from 8% in 2020, the highest rate for a Republican candidate in decades, and among young voters under 30, secured 39% of the vote, a 35% increase over 2020.

However, as Trump’s economy continues to struggle, with the latest job numbers published Wednesday being “the worst in quite some time,” these key voter blocs are turning on Trump, and leaving GOP strategists in a panic.

“Their overall performance [with] Black men, younger voters will holistically depend upon the overall health of the economy,” said Shermichael Singleton, GOP strategist and former adviser to Ben Carson, speaking with Politico in a report published Thursday.

“In terms of whether or not we can rely on those groups in November? I hope to God we can. And I’m hopeful that the economy will continue to improve. But I don’t know.”

The economy was among the largest factors that played into Trump’s newfound support among Black and young voters, though in the eight months of his second term, both groups have seen unemployment skyrocket. Unemployment among young Americans increased to 10.5% from 9% since Trump was sworn in back in January, and Black unemployment soared to 7.5% in August from 6.2% in January.

And, with both groups also seeing a drop in wages this year, their support for Republicans in the upcoming midterm elections is anything but guaranteed.

“There isn’t an alternative that is being presented in a unified manner by Democrats,” said Ron Bonjean, GOP strategist and co-founder of ROKK Solutions, a public affairs firm, speaking with Politico. “[Voters] may not be extremely happy about where the economy is at this point, but they can see that Trump is trying – and that the other side isn’t bringing a lot to bear.”

While GOP strategists couched their concerns with either criticism of Democrats or optimism for Trump’s economy to get back on the rails, those not aligned with the GOP were far more blunt in the bleak outlook for Republicans heading into the midterm elections, such as Milan Singh, founder and director of the Yale Youth Poll.

“There’s a good bit of evidence that Trump is underwater on certain economic issues and underwater with young people,” Singh said, speaking with Politico. “The question is whether those two things are connected. My guess is that they are.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Waning Support: A Closer Look at the GOP’s Dilemma

In an era where political fortunes can shift with the wind, the recent report from Politico sheds light on an increasingly precarious situation for former President Donald Trump and the Republican Party at large. The core of the issue? Trump’s dwindling favorability among two pivotal demographics: Black and young voters. These groups, which showed an unexpected tilt towards Trump in the past election, are now seemingly retracting their support amidst a struggling economy.

The Numbers Don’t Lie

Trump’s performance among Black voters and young voters under 30 during the last election was nothing short of a statistical anomaly for a Republican candidate. Climbing to 15% support from Black voters and securing 39% of the vote from young voters under 30, Trump and the GOP had reasons to be optimistic about breaking traditional voting patterns. Yet, as the saying goes, “What goes up must come down.”

The latest job numbers spell trouble, being described as “the worst in quite some time,” a direct quote that leaves little to the imagination regarding the state of Trump’s economy. Unemployment rates for young Americans and Black Americans have seen significant increases, with young Americans at 10.5% and Black Americans at 7.5%. With both demographics also experiencing a drop in wages, the alarm bells are ringing loud and clear for GOP strategists.

Strategists’ Spin Cycle

Shermichael Singleton, a GOP strategist, encapsulates the party’s hope mingled with desperation, banking on the economy’s recovery to retain these key voter blocs. Yet, hope is a strategy that even Singleton seems to realize is thin ice to tread on.

Ron Bonjean’s remarks strike a chord of defensive optimism, suggesting that despite economic dissatisfaction, Trump’s efforts might still be viewed more favorably compared to a perceived lack of alternatives from the Democrats. This perspective, however, feels more like clutching at straws rather than a robust strategy, especially considering the underlying economic discontent among these crucial voter groups.

Democrats: The Silent Spectators?

The GOP’s concerns are juxtaposed with criticisms towards Democrats for not presenting a “unified manner” alternative. This criticism, albeit a strategic deflection, raises a pertinent question about the opposition’s role and strategy in capitalizing on this disenchantment among young and Black voters.

The Bleak Outlook

The analysis from Milan Singh, of the Yale Youth Poll, cuts through the political obfuscation with a clear message: Trump is facing significant headwinds with young voters on economic issues. This acknowledgment from an external observer adds weight to the concerns raised by GOP insiders, suggesting a broader consensus on the challenges ahead for Trump and the Republican Party.

Conclusion: A Tumultuous Road Ahead

In sum, the article from Politico paints a stark picture of a party at a crossroads. The GOP’s reliance on Trump’s expanded voter base is now under serious threat due to the economic downturn. With unemployment rates rising and wages dropping for key demographics that showed Trump unprecedented support, the Republican Party finds itself in a quagmire of its own making.

The GOP strategists’ alarm, coupled with a lack of a coherent counter-strategy from Democrats, sets the stage for an unpredictable and potentially tumultuous midterm election cycle. Yet, the underlying message is clear: economic discontent among young and Black voters is a ticking time bomb for the Republican Party, one that requires more than hopeful rhetoric and defensive posturing to defuse.
