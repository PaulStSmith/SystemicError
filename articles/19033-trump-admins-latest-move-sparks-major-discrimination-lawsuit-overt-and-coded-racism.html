Trump admin’s latest move sparks major discrimination lawsuit: ‘Overt and coded racism’

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media as he departs the White House for Florida, in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 20, 2026. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

The Trump administration was hit with a major discrimination lawsuit after having cancelled a $250,000 federal grant for a New York museum celebrating Black history, a move lawyers for the museum described as “overt and coded racism.”

The grant was awarded to the museum – the Underground Railroad Education Center in Albany, New York – in January of 2024, and despite Congress having already approved the funding, the Trump administration axed the grant as part of its dismantling of diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) programs.

Lawyers for the museum struck back, however, having filed a lawsuit against the Trump administration for what they say was a blatant violation of the Constitution’s discrimination protections, The Washington Post reported Saturday.

“[The Trump administration] systematically targeted grantees and programs that sought to increase the public’s understanding of Black history and cultures,” wrote Nina Loewenstein, a lawyer for the museum, in a brief filed in court, according to the Post. “Numerous statements of the current Executive Branch leadership reflect overt and coded racism supporting white supremacy and denigrating Black history in America.”

The Trump administration officiallyended fundingfor “racially discriminatory discretionary grant programs at minority-serving institutions” last September, but has seenpushbackfrom members of his own party on occasion.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Grant Cancellation

In the recent controversy surrounding the Trump administration’s cancellation of a $250,000 federal grant intended for the Underground Railroad Education Center in Albany, New York, the narrative quickly escalated into accusations of “overt and coded racism.” This cancellation, part of a broader initiative to dismantle diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) programs, has sparked a significant legal and public backlash. To dissect this situation accurately, we must scrutinize the institutional power dynamics, decision-making authority, and the framing of the narrative.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Firstly, it’s imperative to acknowledge that the Trump administration, at the time of the cancellation, holds significant institutional power. This power includes the ability to influence, redirect, or cancel federal grants, even those previously approved by Congress. The decision to axe the grant, as stated, aligns with the administration’s broader policy agenda to end funding for what it deems “racially discriminatory discretionary grant programs at minority-serving institutions.”

The direct decision-maker in this scenario is the Trump administration, specifically those departments or officials tasked with overseeing federal grants. This action is not an isolated bureaucratic hiccup but a deliberate policy choice consistent with the administration’s publicly stated goals.

Misdirection of Responsibility

The framing of the article suggests a binary conflict: the Trump administration’s policies versus the values embodied by the Underground Railroad Education Center. While the narrative centers around the cancellation of a single grant, the underlying issue extends into a systemic dismantling of DEI programs. Critics might argue that focusing solely on this incident could misdirect responsibility away from the broader, more systemic agenda at play.

However, the lawsuit filed by the museum’s lawyers targets the correct locus of decision-making power by addressing the constitutionality of the Trump administration’s actions. This legal challenge correctly identifies the administration as the entity with both the power and the responsibility for the decision, rather than dispersing blame across the broader political or bureaucratic landscape.

The Article’s Framing

The article’s framing, while potentially sensational, does not misdirect responsibility. It clearly places the onus on the Trump administration, highlighting a pattern of behavior that supports the museum lawyers’ accusations of “overt and coded racism.” By connecting the grant’s cancellation to the administration’s larger policy directives, the article correctly positions this incident within a systemic context rather than as a standalone event.

Conclusion

The cancellation of the $250,000 federal grant to the Underground Railroad Education Center by the Trump administration is a manifestation of its wider agenda to dismantle DEI programs, framed within a context of institutional power and deliberate policy decision-making. The lawsuit filed by the museum’s lawyers rightly targets the administration, holding it accountable for actions that are alleged to violate the Constitution’s discrimination protections.

In assessing this scenario, criticism of the administration’s actions is well-placed, resting on a clear understanding of where responsibility lies. This is not a case of misplaced blame or an oversimplified narrative; it is an instance where the actions of those in power directly impact the preservation and education of Black history in America. The broader implications of such policy decisions warrant close scrutiny and public debate, underscoring the importance of holding powerful actors accountable for their decisions within the legal and societal frameworks that govern us all.
