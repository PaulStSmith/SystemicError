'You got everything wrong': Judge's knockdown of DOJ leaves expert taken aback

Legal analysts and researcher Seamus Hughes uncovered the docket for the Epstein grand jury unsealing in the Southern District of Florida, noted national security expert Marcy Wheeler.

Judge Robin L. Rosenberg already denied the request from President Donald Trump's Justice Department, and the 12-page ruling walks through how the Justice Department's Todd Blanche wrote the filing without taking the laws of the Southern District of Florida into account.

Wheeler characterized it as a dozen pages of the judge saying, "You dumba--es, you got everything wrong."

The filing begins with the judge explaining to the government, "11th Circuit law does not permit the Court to grant the government's request." She adds, "The Court's hands are tied — a point that the Government concedes."

The DOJ cited the Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e), however, the judge writes, "the Government's Petition to unseal the grand jury transcripts is not based on any of the exceptions in Rule 6. Instead, the Government makes two arguments outside Rule 6."

"Contrary to the Government's stated basis and the Second and Seventh Circuits, the Eleventh Circuit has directly held that a district court 'do[es] not possess the ... power to order the release of grand jury records in instances not covered by' Rule 6(e)(3)(E)," continued Rosenberg in another section.

Another exception the DOJ attempted to deploy was that there are special circumstances that exist and that the policy around grand jury secrecy has "expired." The judge explained that those aren't exceptions to Rule 6 either. She repeated that she can't "grant a request for disclosure unless one of the five exceptions" in that rule applies.

Our Analysis:

Anatomy of a Judicial Smackdown: The Trump Administration's Legal Blunder

In a ruling that reads like a masterclass in judicial rebuke, the Trump administration's attempt to unseal the grand jury transcripts related to the Epstein case was not just denied but demolished. The 12-page ruling, unearthed by legal analysts and researchers like Seamus Hughes and commented upon by national security expert Marcy Wheeler, serves as a stinging indictment of the administration's legal acumen, or rather, the glaring lack thereof.

A Lesson in Legal Basics Gone Wrong

At the heart of this debacle is the Department of Justice's (DOJ) Todd Blanche, who, in an astonishing display of legal incompetence, penned a filing that seemed to ignore the fundamental laws of the Southern District of Florida. Judge Robin L. Rosenberg’s response? A detailed explanation of why the administration’s request was essentially a non-starter. The judge’s ruling began with a straightforward dismissal: "11th Circuit law does not permit the Court to grant the government's request." This was not just a rejection; it was an elementary lesson in legal jurisdiction that one would assume the DOJ should have mastered.

The DOJ's Misguided Attempts

The DOJ's misguided reliance on the Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e) was a critical misstep. The administration's petition to unseal the grand jury transcripts failed to align with any of the exceptions within Rule 6, showcasing a baffling disregard for basic legal standards. What's more, the DOJ's argument hinged on purported "special circumstances" and the notion that the policy around grand jury secrecy had "expired." Judge Rosenberg’s ruling lays bare the folly of these arguments, emphasizing that such exceptions to Rule 6 simply do not exist.

A Scathing Characterization

Marcy Wheeler's characterization of the ruling as the judge effectively calling the DOJ "dumba--es" for their comprehensive failure is not just a casual dismissal. It's a scathing critique of a fundamental misunderstanding of legal principles by those at the highest levels of government. This episode is not merely a procedural misstep; it's a glaring reflection of the Trump administration's often cavalier attitude towards legal norms and procedures.

The Bigger Picture

This ruling is emblematic of a broader pattern of behavior by the Trump administration: a seeming disregard for legal boundaries and a penchant for pushing the limits of executive power. The administration's legal strategies, often characterized by bluster and overreach, here meet the immovable object of judicial scrutiny and precedent.

Conclusion: A Judicial Dressing Down

The takeaway from Judge Rosenberg’s ruling is clear: the law is not a tool of convenience that can be bent to suit the whims of power. It is a structured framework designed to ensure fairness, accountability, and transparency. This episode serves as a stark reminder of the importance of legal literacy, especially for those tasked with the highest levels of national governance. The Trump administration's attempt to unseal the Epstein grand jury transcripts was not just a legal failure; it was a profound misjudgment of the very foundations of the American legal system.

In sum, the administration's legal maneuver was not just rebuffed; it was laid bare as a case study in how not to approach the law. The ruling stands as a testament to the judiciary's role as a guardian of legal norms and principles—an unglamorous but essential bulwark against the misuse of power.
