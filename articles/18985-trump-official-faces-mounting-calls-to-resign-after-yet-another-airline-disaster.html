Trump official faces mounting calls to resign after yet another airline disaster

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy attends a press conference on the impact of the ongoing federal government shutdown on air travel, at LaGuardia Airport in the Queens borough of New York City on Oct. 28, 2025. REUTERS/Shannon Stapleton

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy was facing criticism and growing calls to resign on Monday after two pilots were killed and dozens of people were injured during a collision involving an Air Canada plane and a fire truck at LaGuardia Airport.

This was the second fatal crash under Duffy’s leadership and in President Donald Trump‘s second administration, The Daily Beast noted. Duffy has been accused of putting safety at risk by not addressing the low staffing among air traffic controllers in the United States.

The Federal Aviation Administration told The Beast that the low staffing concerns were part of the investigation.

Duffy posted on X that he was headed to LaGuardia Airport following the incident, which drew a number of responses and critiques of the Trump administration official.

“Sean Duffy, Elon Musk, Trump cut FAA staff. Now we have three airline disasters in 14 months. None in the previous 16 years. Is there anything Trump touches that doesn’t turn to s---?” Doug Dillman, former biochemist, wrote on X.

“You should be doing your job, not running around the country to make Trump praise content for this page,” Russell Foster, former Democratic congressional candidate in Texas, wrote on X. “Spend more time working and less time sucking up.”

“Ok great. And then what?? How about you all stop fighting with each other so these airports can be fully staffed for the safety of the employees and the traveling public. But who gives a s---right? Keep spreading everyone so thin till they break or something tragic happens,” sports reporter Nikki Gist wrote on X.

“You need more controllers. You need to pay them more. And you need to stop the practice of 6-day duty periods with mandatory overtime. The regulations that govern pilot rest should be applied to controllers, too. Outside of that, let’s see what the NTSB says about contributing factors, especially those related to the firetruck crossing an active runway,” Thomas Larsen, data expert, wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Real Culprits Behind The LaGuardia Tragedy

In the wake of the devastating collision at LaGuardia Airport that resulted in the tragic loss of two pilots and injuries to dozens, the spotlight has turned fiercely onto Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy. However, a closer examination of the institutional powers at play, the decision-making processes involved, and the framing of the narrative reveals a more complex and systemic issue than the immediate backlash suggests.

Institutional Power and Decision Making

Who Holds the Power? The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), an agency within the Department of Transportation, is primarily responsible for the oversight of civil aviation within the United States. While Secretary Duffy, as the head of the Department, holds significant influence, the operational and regulatory decisions come down to the FAA, which operates with a certain degree of autonomy. The President and the administration also possess overarching authority and influence, particularly in matters of budgeting and staffing levels.

Who Made the Decision? The criticism centers on alleged decisions to cut FAA staff, including air traffic controllers, which purportedly compromised safety. If staffing decisions contributed to the tragedy, then the responsibility is twofold: it lies with the FAA for its implementation and with the administration, including President Trump and potentially Secretary Duffy, for any directives or budgetary constraints that led to reduced staffing.

Misdirection and Misplaced Blame?

The article’s framing suggests Duffy is directly to blame for the incident due to insufficient action on staffing concerns. However, this oversimplifies the issue and perhaps unfairly targets Duffy without acknowledging the broader context of governmental budgeting and FAA operational autonomy.

  1. Institutional Power: While Duffy represents the Department of Transportation, the FAA’s internal management and operational decisions, such as staffing levels, are more directly under the purview of the FAA Administrator.

  2. Decision-Making: If the Trump administration indeed pushed for reductions in FAA staffing, the blame should be more broadly distributed among all involved in such policy decisions, including President Trump himself and Congress, which approves federal budgets.

  3. Article Framing: The critiques, largely drawn from social media comments, focus heavily on Duffy and, by extension, the Trump administration. This personalizes the issue, potentially obscuring systemic failures or long-standing issues within the FAA or the broader context of federal staffing and budgeting policies.

Conclusion

While Secretary Duffy and the Trump administration should not be absolved of responsibility—particularly if their policies contributed to reduced staffing levels and compromised safety—the narrative that places Duffy as the primary antagonist is overly simplistic and potentially misleading. The real story likely involves a complex interplay of administrative decisions, bureaucratic inertia, and systemic underinvestment in critical public infrastructure and personnel.

The tragedy at LaGuardia demands a thorough investigation and a nuanced understanding of the failures that led to it. Scapegoating a single official or administration, while politically convenient, does nothing to address the underlying issues or prevent future tragedies. True accountability requires identifying all points of failure—both individual and systemic—and taking the necessary steps to rectify them.
