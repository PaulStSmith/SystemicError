President Donald Trump appears to be staring down lame-duck status as the 2026 midterm election approaches, but that doesn’t mean his administration will be any less dangerous, even if the GOP gets the thrashing experts expect, according to one ex-GOP analyst.

The president’s overall approval rating his at 40% as of November 23, which is down 16 points from when he took office, according to The Economist. Similarly, Americans have largely soured on Trump’s domestic and economic policies. Republicans also handed Trump a stern rejection by overwhelmingly supporting legislation to force the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, something the administration tried to prevent by pressuring people like Rep. Lauren Boebert (R-CO) to vote against the bill.

Rick Wilson, a co-founder of The Lincoln Project and a former Republican strategist, argued in a new Substack essay that these issues can lead one to believe things are about to return to normal. However, Wilson warned, Trump is likely to become more dangerous as his support fades.

“Trump is not planning a quiet sunset,” Wilson wrote. “He is planning a siege.”

The president appears to be laying the groundwork for this siege by appointing people who challenged the legitimacy of the 2020 election to key posts within the federal government.

One such individual is Heather Honey, Trump’s deputy assistant secretary for election integrity at the Department of Homeland Security. Honey worked for a state anti-voting group in Pennsylvania and continues to spread the debunked theory that Trump lost the 2020 election because of widespread voter fraud, according to Democracy Docket.

“Trump is already aiming the federal machine at the midterms...If you think a lame duck can’t cause chaos, you didn’t learn the lesson of 2020,” Wilson wrote. The lame-duck period then gave Trump and his allies space to organize a coup attempt...Now imagine that same playbook with four years of institutional capture, a more radical staff, and a president who knows he can’t be reelected and doesn’t care who he burns.”

“Watch what happens in red state legislatures and courts when Trump calls on them to throw out the election results, or when Mike Johnson declines to seat newly elected members.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Lame-Duck Period: A Prelude to Chaos?

As the 2026 midterm elections loom over President Donald Trump’s administration, the narrative spun around his potential lame-duck status masks a far more sinister reality. According to an analysis by former GOP strategist Rick Wilson, Trump’s decreased approval ratings and apparent GOP rebukes do little to temper his capacity for disruption. Wilson’s insights, previously laid out in a Substack essay, highlight a dangerous trajectory for Trump’s final days in office that should alarm any observer of American democracy.

Approval Ratings and GOP Defections: A False Dawn?

Trump’s approval rating, as pointed out in the article, has plummeted to 40%, a significant 16-point drop since his inauguration. This decline, coupled with the GOP’s legislative pushback against Trump’s attempt to suppress the Jeffrey Epstein files, might suggest a party ready to distance itself from its controversial figurehead. However, the reality is far more complicated and, frankly, more ominous.

A Siege, Not a Sunset

Wilson’s assertion that “Trump is not planning a quiet sunset; he is planning a siege” cuts through any optimistic projections of Trump’s waning influence. The president’s actions—specifically, the strategic placement of loyalists like Heather Honey, who perpetuates baseless claims of election fraud, into critical positions—signal an intent not just to linger in the political landscape but to actively undermine it.

The Danger of Underestimating a Lame Duck

The concept of a lame-duck president traditionally suggests diminished power and influence. Yet, Trump’s administration, as Wilson rightly points out, has been anything but traditional. The parallels drawn between the post-2020 election period and the potential for chaos in the upcoming midterms are particularly chilling. Trump’s previous attempts to subvert the election outcome, using both federal power and the influence over state legislatures and courts, serve as a harrowing prelude to what might be attempted with an even more “radical staff” and a president unburdened by future electoral prospects.

The Role of State Legislatures and Courts

Wilson’s warning about red state legislatures and courts being potential instruments in Trump’s efforts to discard election results or obstruct the seating of newly elected members cannot be overstated. This tactic, if employed as suggested, would not only be a gross misuse of power but a direct assault on the democratic process itself.

In Conclusion

The article, through Wilson’s analysis, paints a grim picture of the potential dangers posed by Trump’s administration in its final days. It’s a stark reminder that the mechanisms of democracy are only as strong as the will to protect them. Trump’s lame-duck period, rather than being a time of diminished threat, could very well be the opposite. As observers and participants in this democracy, it’s incumbent upon us all to remain vigilant and resist complacency. The risk of underestimating Trump’s capacity for chaos is a luxury we simply cannot afford.
