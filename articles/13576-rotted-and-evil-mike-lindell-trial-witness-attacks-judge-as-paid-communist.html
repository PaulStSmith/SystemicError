'Rotted and evil': Mike Lindell trial witness attacks judge as 'paid communist'

A witness in MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell's Colorado defamation trial accused U.S. District Court Judge Nina Wang of being "a paid communist operative."

Colorado activist Joe Oltmann made the remarks Monday on X.

He testified in Lindell's trial after claiming that former voting machine executive Eric Coomer said President Donald Trump would not be allowed to win the 2020 election. Coomer's legal team alleged that Lindell learned the plaintiff's name indirectly through Oltmann's podcast.

"The judge in the Mike Lindell lawsuit is a paid communist operative and what she has done is nothing short of despicable," Oltmann wrote. "She prevented the information about Eric Coomer from being presented while him to lie with impunity, interfered with the evidence presented and even denied witnesses that were right outside the courtroom to testify."

"It was obvious… and yet, this crooked judge was allowed to bully and do what judges are forbidden to do," he continued. "In the jury instructions Nina Wong told the jury defamation did happen. She again stacked the deck and her instructions were designed to make the case predetermined."

Oltmann predicted that Lindell would win the case on appeal.

"The malpractice and impeachable behavior by this judge is obvious," he insisted. "It easily shows the collusion between them all. This is what rotted and evil looks like."

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Lindell's Legal Saga

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a political satire, the defamation trial involving MyPillow CEO Mike Lindell has devolved into an arena where the absurd meets the judicial. At the heart of this spectacle is Joe Oltmann, a Colorado activist whose accusations against U.S. District Court Judge Nina Wang read more like the ramblings of a conspiracy theorist than anything resembling a coherent legal argument.

Allegations of Communism in the Courtroom

Oltmann's claim that Judge Wang is "a paid communist operative" is not just a baseless accusation; it's a reckless and dangerous assertion that undermines the very fabric of the judicial system. The term "communist" has been historically weaponized in political discourse, often devoid of factual basis and employed as a catch-all to discredit and diminish the opposition. Oltmann's use of this tactic is not just an attack on Judge Wang's integrity but an attempt to erode trust in the judicial process itself.

The Misdirection Play

The central narrative Oltmann attempts to weave—that Judge Wang prevented evidence from being presented and interfered with witness testimony—is a classic misdirection. By focusing on these allegations, attention is diverted away from the substantive matters at hand, namely the defamation claims centered around Lindell's propagation of unfounded election fraud narratives.

Oltmann's commentary on the judge's jury instructions further illustrates this misdirection. He criticizes the instructions as biased and predetermined, yet fails to provide substantive evidence to support his claims. This tactic is indicative of a broader strategy to cast doubt on the legitimacy of the trial's outcome, regardless of the evidence presented.

Predictions of Victory on Appeal: A Hail Mary

Oltmann's prediction that Lindell will win the case on appeal is wishful thinking at best. Appeals are based on questions of law, not fact, and the notion that appellate courts will overturn a trial court's decision based on unsubstantiated claims of judicial malpractice is a long shot. This assertion seems less about confidence in the legal process and more about maintaining a narrative of persecution and victimhood.

Conclusion: The Rotten Core of Conspiracy

Oltmann's statement that the judge's behavior is "what rotted and evil looks like" is ironically more reflective of the tactics employed by Lindell's defenders. The strategy of attacking the integrity of the judiciary, spreading baseless accusations, and distorting the legal process reveals a desperate attempt to manipulate public perception in the absence of factual evidence.

The defamation trial of Mike Lindell has become a microcosm of the broader issues plaguing our political discourse: the erosion of trust in institutions, the weaponization of unfounded accusations, and the relentless pursuit of an agenda at the expense of truth. As this legal drama unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the challenges facing our democracy and the urgent need for a return to a discourse grounded in reality, not conspiracy.
