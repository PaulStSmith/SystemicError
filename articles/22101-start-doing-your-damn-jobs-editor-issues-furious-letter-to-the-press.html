'Start doing your damn jobs': Editor issues furious letter to the press

Based on an article by D. Earl Stephens

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Normalization Is the Collaboration

The source piece is a furious indictment of a press corps that keeps treating an authoritarian project like routine politics. Its core claim is simple: the people with institutional power are doing damage openly, and the media keeps laundering that damage through process stories, horse-race trivia, and false balance.

The Center of Gravity Is Power

This is not a story about pundit boredom or bad phrasing. The institutional power sits with the White House, the Republican apparatus shielding it, and the broader state machinery that can be bent to service it. The article names the obvious: when the people controlling federal force, courts, and executive authority act, that is not theater. That is governing.

The source is strongest when it refuses the usual evasions. It does not pretend harm is accidental. It does not hide the fact that an administration can damage public health, civil rights, voting access, and historical memory by design. That matters, because authoritarian politics depends on getting its violence recast as confusion, excess, or mere controversy.

The Media’s Favorite Trick

The press is not being asked to take sides. It is being asked to stop lying by omission. The problem is not that coverage exists. The problem is that coverage keeps shrinking deliberate political destruction into one item among many, then handing the frame over to the people causing it.

That is misdirection. When the media spends its energy on insider drama while the government is being used as a wrecking tool, it is not informing the public. It is helping power launder itself. The source is right to call out the habit of turning the most consequential attacks into just another news cycle.

Republicans Are Not Bystanders

The article’s sharpest political point is that Republican officials are not confused witnesses. They are cover. They may not all write the checks, but they normalize the conduct, defend the conduct, and keep the machinery running. That is not neutrality. It is participation.

This is where the usual civility theater fails. There is no symmetry here. One party is functioning as the operating base for a destructive executive project; the other is the imperfect but necessary institutional barrier against it. Pretending otherwise is not sophistication. It is surrender dressed up as balance.

Democrats Are Flawed, Not Equivalent

The source is honest enough to admit that Democrats have not done enough. That critique is correct and useful. Democratic leaders have been too cautious, too procedural, and too willing to act as though constitutional restraint will defeat itself if they keep speaking in parliamentary tones.

But flaw is not equivalence. Democrats have not openly set out to break the country, strip rights, or turn state power into a personal weapon. They are the only major institutional vehicle still capable of defending democratic government at scale. That is the actual strategic fact, even if it offends pundits who prefer contempt to responsibility.

What This Pattern Reveals

The larger pattern is authoritarian normalization: brutal policy, media fatigue, elite accommodation, and civic numbness all feeding each other. The method is not subtle. Break institutions, flood the zone, exhaust the public, and rely on journalists to describe the collapse as a messy day in politics.

That is the real scandal here. Not just the damage itself, but the machinery that keeps translating obvious abuse into conventional governance. Once that translation succeeds, the country starts adapting to its own erosion. That is how democratic decay becomes administratively respectable.
