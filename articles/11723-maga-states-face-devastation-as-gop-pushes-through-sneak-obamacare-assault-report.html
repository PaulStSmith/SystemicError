MAGA states face devastation as GOP pushes through sneak Obamacare assault: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 13, 20255 min read

Donald Trump and congressional Republicans have found a sneaky new way to gut Obamacare without the messy optics of an outright repeal—and experts say it could be even more damaging than their failed 2017 effort.

Unlike the dramatic showdown that saw John McCain's famous thumbs-down save the Affordable Care Act, Republicans barely mentioned healthcare while ramming through Trump's massive domestic agenda package this year. But they're quietly pushing through changes that cause severe damage to former President Barack Obama's signature act, CNN reported.

"The net effect of the changes they are making is a partial repeal of the ACA," warned Larry Levitt, executive vice president for health policy at KFF.

“Many of the changes are so technical, it may be hard for the public to grasp what’s happening."

The so-called "big, beautiful bill," combined with new rules from the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, is projected to leave millions more Americans without health coverage while jacking up costs for those who manage to keep their plans. The Congressional Budget Office forecasts that 2.1 million more people will be uninsured by 2034.

But even more immediately, up to 1.8 million people could lose their Obamacare coverage next year, with losses concentrated in GOP-led states including Florida, Georgia, South Carolina, and Texas.

The Republican assault includes brutal new verification requirements that effectively kill automatic reenrollment—a system that covered nearly 11 million people in 2025. Now, enrollees must verify their income upfront and face the nightmare of paying back entire subsidy amounts if they underestimate their earnings, CNN reported.

"It's a radical weakening of what the marketplaces will be able to deliver in the next few years," said Jennifer Sullivan from the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities.

The carnage extends beyond coverage losses. The rule forces low-income enrollees to pay $5 monthly for previously free plans, shortens enrollment periods, and bars certain legal immigrants—including refugees and trafficking victims—from receiving federal assistance.

Insurers are already fleeing. Aetna announced it won't offer Obamacare coverage next year, while remaining companies have proposed a median 18% premium hike for 2026—more than double last year's increase.

Republicans defend the changes as fraud prevention, with former Trump adviser Brian Blase claiming the bill "restores the ACA, rather than repeals it." But healthcare advocates aren't buying the spin.

The timing couldn't be worse. Enhanced federal premium subsidies that helped drive record Obamacare enrollment expire at the end of 2025, threatening to send premiums soaring even higher.

"It will be a tumultuous few years," Sullivan predicted, as millions of Americans brace for the healthcare chaos ahead.

Our Analysis:

The Covert Assault on Obamacare: A Closer Look

Donald Trump and his congressional cronies have embarked on a stealth mission to dismantle the Affordable Care Act (ACA), otherwise known as Obamacare, without resorting to the theatrical attempts of outright repeal we've seen in the past. This time, their strategy is insidious, leveraging the legislative process to inflict death by a thousand cuts on Obama's hallmark healthcare legislation. But make no mistake, the damage they're poised to cause is not only substantial—it's potentially far more devastating than their ham-fisted 2017 efforts.

The Trojan Horse of Healthcare Policy

While the Republicans' previous, high-profile attempts to axe the ACA were thwarted, notably by John McCain's dramatic thumbs-down, their current tactics are shrouded in the shadows of broader legislative maneuvers. Their approach? Embedding "technical" changes within a sprawling domestic agenda package, as reported by CNN. According to Larry Levitt, executive vice president for health policy at KFF, this is tantamount to a "partial repeal of the ACA." The cunning part of this strategy lies in its complexity; the changes are so convoluted and arcane that the average American may struggle to grasp the full extent of the impending damage.

The Devastating Consequences

The repercussions of these under-the-radar adjustments are nothing short of catastrophic. The Congressional Budget Office ominously forecasts that an additional 2.1 million people will be uninsured by 2034. But the immediate future looks even bleaker, with up to 1.8 million potentially losing coverage as early as next year—victims predominantly in GOP-dominated states.

One of the most egregious maneuvers is the obliteration of automatic reenrollment, a lifeline that previously covered nearly 11 million people. This, coupled with new verification hurdles and the imposition of fees on low-income enrollees for plans that were once free, not only undermines the ACA's foundational principles but actively punishes the most vulnerable segments of the population.

The Exodus of Insurers and Skyrocketing Premiums

As if these measures weren't punitive enough, insurers are responding predictably by fleeing the ACA marketplace. Aetna's departure is just the tip of the iceberg, as those remaining are poised to hike premiums by a median of 18% for 2026—more than double last year's increase. This exodus and subsequent price surge effectively cripple the ACA's goal of making healthcare accessible and affordable.

The Republican Justification: A Masterclass in Spin

Republicans, in a display of breathtaking cynicism, cloak these changes under the guise of fraud prevention. Brian Blase, a former Trump adviser, even had the audacity to claim that this legislative sabotage "restores the ACA, rather than repeals it." This is, of course, a masterclass in Orwellian spin. The reality is that these measures gut the very essence of the ACA, betraying a blatant disregard for the millions of Americans who rely on it for healthcare coverage.

The Road Ahead: Turbulence and Uncertainty

As the enhanced federal premium subsidies that buoyed Obamacare enrollment to record highs are set to expire at the end of 2025, the timing of these changes could not be worse. The result? A healthcare landscape marked by turmoil and skyrocketing costs, leaving millions of Americans in a precarious position.

Conclusion: Unmasking the Assault

This latest Republican strategy to undermine the ACA is not just an attack on a piece of legislation; it's an attack on the health and well-being of millions of Americans. It's a calculated, cold-hearted attempt to unravel a safety net that, while imperfect, has provided a lifeline to countless individuals and families. The lack of transparency and reliance on technicalities to achieve their goals is emblematic of a party that has lost any semblance of moral compass when it comes to healthcare policy. As advocates, policymakers, and citizens, we must remain vigilant and resist this covert assault on our healthcare system with every tool at our disposal. The health of our nation depends on it.
