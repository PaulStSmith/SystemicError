True taxpayer cost of Trump's golf obsession may be even worse than reported $71 million

During former President Barack Obama’s eight years in the White House, Donald Trump repeatedly attacked him for spending too much time playing golf. Trump, in 2016, claimed that if he won the presidential election and defeated Democratic nominee Hillary Clinton, he would be too busy helping the American people to play golf.

But according to a 2020 fact-check from CNN, Trump spent a lot more time playing golf than Obama — 266 days playing golf by May 2020 compared to Obama playing 98 rounds by May 2012.

Now, ten months into Trump’s second presidency, HuffPost and the Daily Beast are reporting that his passion for golf is costing taxpayers a fortune.

HuffPost’s S.V. Date, in a late November article, reports, “Taxpayers have now shelled out nearly $71 million for President Donald Trump’s golf hobby since he retook office in January, with his second-term total on pace to break $300 million, according to a HuffPost analysis.

His visit to his course adjacent to the Palm Beach County jail Wednesday was on his 16th trip to his Mar-a-Lago country club home four miles away. Each of those trips cost $3.4 million in travel and security expenses.”

The Daily Beast’s Jack Revell says of HuffPost’s reporting, “If that figure turns out to be accurate, it would be nearly double the $151.5 million Trump spent on similar sporting outings while in office between 2017 and 2021.”

The Lincoln Project’s Rick Wilson, a Never Trump conservative and former GOP strategist, doesn’t find HuffPost’s figures surprising.

Wilson, one of Trump’s most scathing critics on the right, told HuffPost, “I really wish I could tell you that it would make anyone in America change their mind about him, but the corruption is so baked in, so endemic, and so ludicrous that it feels like the collective reaction will be a shrug. It’s one more example of Trump defining the presidency down. Way, way down.”

According to Revell, the cost of Trump’s golf obsession may be even worse than what HuffPost is reporting.

The Daily Beast reporter notes, “HuffPost’s analysis is based on a Government Accountability Office report on the cost of four of Trump’s golf trips in 2019 and has not been updated for inflation, indicating the true expense is likely far higher…. Most of Trump’s golf trips have been made to the president’s own private course in Palm Beach, Florida, near his Mar-a-Lago residence, and have also included visits to his courses in Bedminster, New Jersey, and Aberdeen, Scotland.”

Revell adds, “Travel and security are the major expenses, with the challenges of securing Mar-a-Lago raising costs in particular. Trump’s use of Air Force One for trips between Joint Base Andrews in Prince George’s County, Maryland, near the White House, to Palm Beach International Airport in Florida cost $273,063 per hour. In total, a four-hour round trip will cost taxpayers $1.1 million.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Trump’s Golf Expenses: A Testament to Hypocrisy

The saga of Donald Trump’s golfing escapades, as reported by HuffPost and the Daily Beast, is a glaring example of political hypocrisy, a theme that seems to recur with the subtlety of a sledgehammer in the narrative of Trump’s presidency. Despite his pre-election promise that he’d be too busy “helping the American people” to play golf, Trump has managed to outdo his predecessor, Barack Obama, in both the frequency and cost of his golf outings, turning his pledge into nothing more than a punchline.

Trump’s Golfing by the Numbers: A Costly Hobby

The numbers are staggering and deserve to be highlighted with the incredulity they warrant. By May 2020, Trump had spent 266 days playing golf, dwarfing the 98 rounds played by Obama by May 2012. Fast forward to his second term, and the financial toll on taxpayers is nearing the grotesque. Nearly $71 million has been spent on Trump’s golf hobby since January, with projections suggesting a potential expenditure of $300 million before the end of his term. To put this into perspective, this amount is nearly double the $151.5 million spent during his first term from 2017 to 2021. The sheer scale of these figures not only underscores a blatant contradiction of Trump’s own criticisms of Obama but also highlights a reckless disregard for taxpayer money.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection of Trump’s Presidency

Rick Wilson’s comments to HuffPost encapsulate the broader sentiment of resignation and frustration that many Americans feel towards Trump’s presidency. The corruption and self-indulgence have become so “baked in” and normalized that new revelations of misuse of funds or breaches of ethical conduct no longer shock or surprise; they merely add to the towering pile of what many see as the defining characteristics of Trump’s time in office.

The True Cost: Beyond the Dollar Amount

While the financial figures are indeed jaw-dropping, the true cost of Trump’s golfing obsession cannot be fully quantified by dollar amounts alone. The implications stretch into the realms of national security, with each trip requiring extensive and costly security measures, especially given the locations often include Trump’s own private courses. The use of Air Force One for these personal excursions, at a cost of $273,063 per hour, symbolizes a misuse of national resources on an epic scale.

Conclusion: A Presidency Defined by Discrepancy

In the end, the story of Trump’s golf expenditures is more than a tale of financial extravagance; it’s a microcosm of a presidency marked by a chasm between promise and practice, between the rhetoric of public service and the reality of self-service. The stark contrast between Trump’s pre-election promises and his actions in office offers a cautionary tale about the dangers of political leaders who place personal indulgence above public service.

The reports from HuffPost and the Daily Beast are not just an indictment of Trump’s hypocrisy but a call to scrutinize the actions of our leaders more closely. As taxpayers foot the bill for these golf outings, one must wonder what true costs lie hidden beneath the surface, masked by the guise of presidential privilege.
