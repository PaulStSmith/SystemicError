In a column published on Sunday, Smith said that "political gravity" has contributed to Trump's loss of support compounded by policies that have dismayed non-MAGA voters who chose him over Vice President Kamala Harris looking for change.

As he wrote, "After a hundred days ... a majority of Americans regard him as both a failure and a would-be dictator. From the courts to the streets, from law offices to college campuses, revolt is swelling. Republicans are eyeing next year’s midterm elections with nervousness."

According to pollster John Zogby, "The honeymoon is over."

“He actually squandered his hundred days, perhaps you can argue, by doing too much, not succeeding with much of it and overplaying his hand. At the end of the 100 days his polling numbers reflect an unsuccessful quarter," he continued before pointing out, "Every poll that I know of, including mine, has him upside down.”

According to Smith, Trump's "Liberation Day" announcement of sweeping tariffs has accelerated his decline as consumers and investors have been swamped the chaos he created.

"The chaos has shaken the faith of Trump voters who felt that he would at least deliver economic competence and guarantee the bottom line. Food prices are rising and tariffs are expected to disrupt supply chains soon, leading to empty shelves reminiscent of the Covid-19 pandemic," he wrote.

Pointing to Republican lawmakers who are eyeing the 2026 midterms already with an eye on how Trump is faring with voters, former Obama official Patrick Gaspard predicted, "I would not judge this presidency to be a success. More likely than not we’ll begin to see Republicans whose names are on the ballot in 2026 slowly but clearly moving away from this agenda. It’s very clear that many Trump voters already have buyer’s remorse.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Downward Spiral of Trump's Presidency: A Precipitous Decline

In an article that slices through the euphemistic fog often surrounding political discourse, David Smith of the Guardian presents a scathing autopsy of Donald Trump's presidency, particularly focusing on the aftermath of his re-election. The narrative is not just a recounting of events but a deep dive into the why and how Trump's support has eroded at such a historic rate. Let's dissect the key points and add some much-needed context to this political unraveling.

The Illusion of Political Gravity

Smith pins down "political gravity" as a significant factor in Trump's decline. This term, while poetic, barely scratches the surface of the self-inflicted wounds Trump has suffered. It's not just gravity pulling him down; it's a maelstrom of incompetence, divisiveness, and policy failures that have disillusioned a broad swath of voters. To ascribe his fall to a natural re-balancing is to ignore the active role his administration has played in its own demise.

Policies of Discontent

The article points to a dissonance between Trump's promises and his delivery, particularly highlighting policies that have alienated non-MAGA voters. This isn't just about failing to fulfill promises; it's about actively pursuing an agenda that undermines the very fabric of American society. From the "Liberation Day" tariff fiasco to the mishandling of economic policy leading to rising food prices and disrupted supply chains, Trump's actions have not just dismayed; they've directly harmed the American people. The comparison to Covid-19 pandemic shortages is particularly damning, equating Trump's economic competence to a once-in-a-century crisis.

The Honeymoon is Over

John Zogby's commentary that "the honeymoon is over" could be the understatement of the century. It's not just that the honeymoon phase has ended; it's that the marriage between Trump and American voters is on the brink of divorce. Zogby's note that Trump "overplayed his hand" is key. This isn't about doing too much; it's about understanding too little. Trump's tenure has been marked by a fundamental misunderstanding of governance, diplomacy, and economics, turning even potential victories into self-sabotage.

Republican Nervousness and Buyer's Remorse

The nervous glances towards the 2026 midterms from Republican lawmakers signal a deeper recognition of Trump's toxicity. Patrick Gaspard's prediction of GOP distancing is not just a strategic maneuver; it's a desperate act of self-preservation. The mention of "buyer's remorse" among Trump voters is particularly poignant, suggesting not just disappointment but a profound sense of betrayal.

Conclusion: A Presidency in Peril

David Smith's analysis, while sharp, might still be too kind. Trump's presidency isn't just experiencing a "historic collapse"; it's undergoing a full-blown implosion. The chaotic policies, the economic incompetence, and the divisive rhetoric have not just eroded his support; they've endangered the very tenets of American democracy. As we look to the future, the question isn't just about Trump's political survival but about the resilience of the American system in the face of such systemic error.
