5 Republicans defect to kill GOP measure to censure indicted Dem

A Republican measure to censure a Democratic lawmaker targeted with criminal charges by the Trump administration went down in flames after a handful of GOP members of Congress crossed over to vote against it, reported NBC News on Wednesday.

The resolution was targeting Rep. LaMonica McIver (D-NJ), who was indicted earlier this year by President Donald Trump's acting New Jersey U.S. Attorney and former personal lawyer Alina Habba, over her protest alongside other New Jersey lawmakers at a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention facility. Habba alleges that McIver assaulted officers trying to arrest New Jersey Mayor Ras Baraka, which McIver denies.

"Five Republican members defected and joined all Democrats to kill the resolution. Two other Republicans voted present. That was enough to table the measure," said the report. The resolution was introduced by Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) on Tuesday night, "as a privileged matter, the fast-track process that forces a vote within two legislative days. This was his second attempt, as he had also introduced the resolution before the August recess."

Some of the lawmakers who voted no or present, Axios noted, are members of the House Ethics Committee, who are running their own parallel investigation of McIver. "I think best to let Ethics Committee finish its report," Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE), who sits on the committee, told Axios after he voted against the resolution.

More recently, Habba's entire appointment to serve as a federal prosecutor has been thrown into legal doubt.

A group of judges decided to remove her after the Senate did not move forward with a permanent confirmation — a highly unusual move reserved for uniquely unqualified candidates. The Justice Department responded by firing the judicially-appointed replacement and putting Habba in that role to effectively reinstate her, but a federal judge last month ruled she has not been legally serving for weeks.

Habba has refused to acknowledge the legitimacy of that decision, because "I am the pick of the president."

Our Analysis:

The Farcical Censure Attempt and the Judicial Jugglery: A Closer Look

In what reads like a script torn straight from the pages of a political satire, a recent Republican measure to censure Democratic lawmaker Rep. LaMonica McIver fizzled out in an amusing spectacle of party defection and legal quandaries. The report from NBC News unveils a narrative so rich in irony and political maneuvering that it almost begs disbelief. Let's dissect the layers of this absurdity with the precision of a surgeon and the cynicism of a seasoned political observer.

The Censure Circus

At the heart of this debacle is the Republican-led initiative to censure Rep. McIver, a maneuver spearheaded by Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) with a zeal that might have been commendable had it not been so blatantly misguided. The charges against McIver, stemming from her participation in a protest at a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention facility, were levied by Alina Habba, an acting New Jersey U.S. Attorney and, notably, a former personal lawyer for Donald Trump.

The resolution's failure, thanks to the dissent of five Republican members and the abstention of two others, is a telling sign of the cracks within the GOP facade. The fact that some of these dissenters are members of the House Ethics Committee, currently investigating McIver, adds a delicious layer of complexity to the plot. Rep. Don Bacon's (R-NE) comment, "I think best to let Ethics Committee finish its report," sounds less like a call for procedural integrity and more like a desperate attempt to distance himself from a sinking ship.

The Legal Labyrinth

The narrative takes a turn for the Kafkaesque with the legal drama surrounding Habba's appointment as a federal prosecutor. Described as a move of dubious legality and questionable judgment, Habba's installation and subsequent removal by a group of judges — only to be reinstated in a defiance of legal norms — reads like a farce. The Justice Department's decision to fire the judicially-appointed replacement and put Habba back in the saddle, despite a federal judge ruling she hasn't been legally serving for weeks, is a brazen display of political muscle over legal propriety.

Habba's refusal to acknowledge the legitimacy of the decision against her, with the assertion "I am the pick of the president," is perhaps the most audacious (and laughable) aspect of this entire affair. It encapsulates the current era's disregard for legal standards and processes in favor of loyalty to political figures.

Conclusion: A Symptom of a Larger Malaise

This episode is more than just a failed censure attempt or a legal curiosity; it's symptomatic of the systemic rot plaguing American politics. The brazenness with which legal appointments are manipulated, the cavalier attitude towards the rule of law, and the internal divisions within parties expose a system in dire need of reform.

In the end, the failed resolution against Rep. McIver is a minor footnote in the broader narrative of political decay. Yet, it serves as a poignant reminder of the need for vigilance, accountability, and, above all, a return to principled governance. As observers, critics, and participants in the democratic process, we must not let these instances pass without scrutiny, lest the farce turns into tragedy.
