'This is not good': CNN host astonished by 'crazy' updates on GOP health talks

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 11, 20255 min read

Jasmine Wright, Audie Cornish and Charlie Dent/CNN

CNN’s Audie Cornish was astonished by updates from her panelists on the state of Republican health care talks.

Senate Republicans have put forth at least five proposals to address the Affordable Care Act subsidies expiring at the end of the year, with a possible vote Thursday, which is putting pressure on House Republicans to come up with their own plan, but Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) appears paralyzed by a lack of guidance from the White House.

“Yeah, well, look, on health care there is no Republican consensus on what to do about health care,” former GOP congressman Charlie Dent told “CNN This Morning.”

“Say that again,” Cornish interrupted, and Dent repeated his assertion. “So even as we’re about to have a vote, you’re saying that doesn’t even have the full support?”

“Well, okay, let’s assume for a second that the [health savings accounts] being discussed become law,” Dent said. “It would take a while to stand them up, and so in the meantime –”

“You’re still going to die,” interjected Democratic strategist Antjuan Seawright, paraphrasing an infamous quote on Medicaid by Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA).

“In the meantime, you’re still going to probably have to extend the current law,” Dent continued. “I don’t know if it’s going to be for one year or two years, and you have Republicans in the House, Brian Fitzpatrick, Jen Kiggans and others, who have bills to extend the subsidies. That’s the bottom line. The ACA, you know, it has expanded coverage, but it has not bent the cost curve down.”

Cornish then turned to NOTUS correspondent Jasmine Wright and asked what was the point of the proposed health savings accounts if those funds couldn’t be used to pay premiums.

“If you just, like, take a step back, there are five Republican plans percolating in the Senate right now,” Wright said. “None of them, to your point, have any consensus. The Senate, the Cassidy-Crapo bill is going to be voted on on Thursday. It’s widely acknowledged when you talk to folks on the Hill. We were reporting it on our NORUS newsletter this morning that that won’t pass, Democrats have already said no. It’s unclear whether or not Republicans even support that bill.”

“There are bills going from one-year extensions to three-year extensions of those subsidies, and so I just think that that there is a fundamentally no real idea idea from Republicans about what they want to do going forward on health care, and I think that that extends to the White House,” Wright added, “because you’re not seeing the White House weigh in in a real way that we saw them perhaps do it two weeks ago when they let that trial balloon go of what they were going to do, which is extend those subsidies, and so I think that you’re going to see a real kind of flurry of action over these next two days. But what comes out of it is a huge question.”

Cornish could hardly believe what she heard.

“That’s crazy for you to tell me,” Cornish said. “There are five different bills. The one that is closest you’re throwing water on. This is not good.”

Our Analysis:

An Analysis of the Republican Health Care Conundrum

In a recent CNN segment, anchor Audie Cornish and her panelists dissected the chaotic state of Republican health care proposals with a level of astonishment that perfectly encapsulates the current state of affairs. This discussion reveals not just the paralysis within the Republican ranks but also a glaring lack of direction from the White House. Let’s dive into the madness.

The Sheer Lack of Consensus

Former GOP congressman Charlie Dent’s comments to CNN highlight a fundamental rift within the Republican Party: a complete absence of consensus on health care. As Dent points out, even as a vote looms, there’s no unified front on what the plan should entail. This isn’t just a minor hiccup; it’s a glaring spotlight on the party’s inability to coalesce around a central health care strategy, a critical issue impacting millions of Americans.

The HSA Red Herring

The discussion around Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) is particularly telling. Dent’s admission that HSAs, even if approved, would require a significant ramp-up time, during which the current law might need to be extended, highlights the impracticality of the proposed solutions. Democratic strategist Antjuan Seawright’s interjection, paraphrasing Senator Joni Ernst’s infamous Medicaid quote, underscores the life-and-death stakes of these policy discussions. HSAs are a band-aid solution to a hemorrhaging problem; they fail to address the immediate needs of those who will lose coverage or face skyrocketing costs without ACA subsidies.

A Flurry of Inaction

Jasmine Wright’s commentary from the NOTUS newsletter paints a picture of a party at odds with itself. With five different plans “percolating” in the Senate and no clear preference or direction, the Republicans are engaging in what can only be described as legislative chaos. The Cassidy-Crapo bill, set for a vote, is already expected to fail, demonstrating a lack of coordination and support that is frankly bewildering.

The White House’s Deafening Silence

Perhaps most alarming is the apparent absence of guidance from the White House. Wright’s observation that there has been no significant input from the administration in the way that there was, even just two weeks prior, speaks volumes. This silence is indicative of a leadership vacuum at a time when decisive action and clear policy direction are desperately needed.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

What we’re witnessing is a systemic failure of governance. The inability of the Republican Party to unify around a health care plan is not just a political embarrassment; it’s a failure to fulfill their duties to the American people. The discussion highlighted by CNN’s Audie Cornish is a microcosm of a larger problem: a government struggling to address critical issues, with real lives hanging in the balance.

In the end, the floundering around health care reform is a stark reminder of the consequences of political inaction and indecision. As the clock ticks down on ACA subsidies, the urgency for a coherent strategy has never been more acute. The Republican Party, together with the White House, needs to find its way out of this quagmire. The health of millions of Americans depends on it.
