Donald Trump over the weekend posted a graphic that alarmed numerous political observers.

Trump took to his own social media site, Truth Social, to post the graphic, which is called, “President Trump is reshaping the media.” The image celebrates that NPR, Stephen Colbert, Jim Acosta, and others in the media are “gone,” while noting that CNN and the FCC have both been “reformed” by the president.

That graphic sent shivers down the spines of some onlookers, who took to X to express their concern.

Republicans against Trump noted, “Trump posted an image claiming he is ‘reshaping the media,’” to which political science professor Michael McFaul replied, “Wow. Scary. Reminds me of Putin’s early years of reshaping Russian media.”

WaPo media reporter Scott Nover chimed in, “latest Truth Social posts includes a series of boasts about how he’s ‘reshaping the media.’”

Garry Kasparov, founder of Renew Democracy, also added, “Fascism doesn’t sneak up on you. It boasts in your face about war, attacking ‘internal enemies’ and the free press, taking total power, and tells you that you must embrace it, not fight it. Consolidating and purging media into loyal hands is Putin’s playbook.”

One popular news curator, @SkylineReport, also noted that “Trump just posted a graphic bragging that he’s ‘reshaping the media.’”

They added, “It literally lists journalists pushed out, public broadcasters ‘defunded,’ layoffs at major outlets, and regulatory pressure as ‘wins.’ Read that again. A president openly celebrating the use of political power to punish critics and pressure the press. That’s not media criticism. That’s media capture — the playbook authoritarian leaders use to bend the information system toward themselves. And the most dangerous part? He’s not hiding it anymore.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Trump’s Media Claims

In a recent move that has sparked widespread concern and debate, Donald Trump posted a graphic on his social media site, Truth Social, claiming he is “reshaping the media.” This claim, laid out in a stark graphic, lists the departure or downfall of various media figures and outlets as achievements. It’s crucial to dissect this narrative, not only for its immediate implications but for the broader context it attempts to construct around the power dynamics between political figures and the media.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

Before diving into the claims, it’s essential to clarify who actually holds institutional power in this context. The graphic attributes a significant level of control and influence over the media directly to Trump. However, the presidency, while influential, does not possess unilateral power to reform or reshape media institutions such as CNN or the FCC directly. These institutions operate independently of the executive’s whims, guided by their internal policies, regulatory frameworks, and, in the case of the FCC, statutory mandates from Congress.

Decision Makers

Attributing the “reform” of CNN or the FCC to a single individual wildly oversimplifies the complex processes behind organizational or regulatory changes. Such changes typically involve decisions by multiple stakeholders, including management teams, regulatory bodies, and, in some cases, legislative action. The graphic’s implication that Trump singularly enacted these changes not only misrepresents how decisions are made within these entities but also inflates the power a president has over independent media and regulatory agencies.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article’s framing suggests a direct causality where there might be none, or at least not one that can be simplistically traced back to Trump. While a president can certainly influence public discourse and indirectly impact media outlets through rhetoric and policy preferences, the assertion that Trump has “reformed” media institutions or directly caused the departure of media figures ventures into misleading territory. This misdirection serves to amplify Trump’s perceived power over the media landscape, potentially overshadowing the actual, more nuanced dynamics at play.

Furthermore, celebrating the defunding of public broadcasters, layoffs at major outlets, and regulatory pressure as victories crosses from media criticism into what the article accurately describes as “media capture.” This is a serious accusation, implying an authoritarian tilt towards controlling the media landscape, a strategy observed in various regimes where press freedom is suppressed.

Analysis

The narrative Trump is promoting, and the article’s examination of it, touches on the dangerous territory of undermining press freedom and democratic norms. It’s imperative to challenge and scrutinize such claims, not only for their factual inaccuracies but for the underlying intent to reshape the narrative around press freedom and the role of a democratic leader.

However, it’s equally important to recognize that the institutional power to enact the kind of sweeping media reform implied does not rest with Trump alone, or indeed with any single individual. The governance of media institutions and regulatory bodies involves a complex interplay of laws, policies, and independent governance structures designed to resist undue influence or control by any one branch of government.

Conclusion

While Trump’s post may signal an intent or desire to reshape the media landscape to his favor, the portrayal of his success in doing so is both exaggerated and misleading. It’s critical for observers and analysts to distinguish between the aspirational declarations of individual political figures and the actual institutional capabilities and constraints within which they operate. Misplaced attribution of power not only distorts the public’s understanding of how media and regulatory bodies function but also risks amplifying authoritarian narratives that threaten the foundational principles of press freedom and democracy.
