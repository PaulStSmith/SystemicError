'He can Bigfoot anybody': Biographer reveals the Trump administration's 'hidden hand'

The Daily Beast screenshot

A Trump biographer revealed on Tuesday that there is someone behind the scenes pulling the strings of the president's administration.

Journalist Michael Wolff, who wrote "Fire and Fury: Inside the Trump White House," discussed it on the premiere of "Inside Trump’s Head," a twice-weekly podcast from The Daily Beast. Wolff noted that while Trump's son-in-law, Jared Kushner, has been active in the administration despite his physical absence.

"Jared is still there," Wolff said. "Jared can, and that's what everyone in the White House will tell you, can at any moment, Bigfoot anybody."

"It's the hidden hand of Jared Kushner," he added.

For instance, Wolff noted that Susie Wiles, Trump's chief of staff, was picked by Kushner.

The Trump administration also has close ties with countries where Kushner is doing business. For example, Kushner secured a $2 billion investment from Saudi Arabia after the first Trump administration. Trump also secured a lucrative real estate deal in the country after he won the 2024 election.

Jared's father, Charles, is also Trump's ambassador to France. Charles Kushner was convicted of 18 crimes in 2005, including tax evasion, federal witness tampering, and making false claims to the Federal Election Commission

Wolff added that Jared Kushner's influence in the White House appears to be substantial.

Our Analysis:

The Hidden Hand of Jared Kushner: A Closer Look

The revelation by Trump biographer Michael Wolff about Jared Kushner's behind-the-scenes influence within the Trump administration exposes a concerning dynamic of power and privilege. Wolff's insights, shared on the premiere of "Inside Trump’s Head," unveil a shadowy figure maneuvering within the highest echelons of American political power. Let's dissect the implications and the unsettling undercurrents it reveals about the Trump administration.

Kushner's Omnipresence: More Than Meets the Eye

Jared Kushner, despite a physical absence, has been depicted as a puppet master of sorts, capable of 'Bigfooting' anyone within the administration. This term, colloquially used here, suggests Kushner has the power to overshadow, override, and influence decisions and personnel at will. The specific mention of Susie Wiles, Trump's chief of staff, being picked by Kushner, not only highlights his influence over crucial appointments but also raises questions about the criteria and motivations behind such decisions.

Business Entanglements and Political Power: A Dangerous Cocktail

The intertwining of Kushner's business dealings with the diplomatic relations of the United States is particularly alarming. The $2 billion investment from Saudi Arabia shortly after Kushner's intensive involvement in the first Trump administration, coupled with Trump's lucrative real estate deal in the country post the 2024 election, paints a stark picture of quid pro quo. It's a scenario where personal profit and political power blur dangerously, challenging the very foundations of ethical governance.

Furthermore, the appointment of Charles Kushner, a convicted felon, as Trump's ambassador to France, is emblematic of the nepotism and cronyism that seem to pervade this administration. His convictions, including tax evasion, federal witness tampering, and making false claims to the Federal Election Commission, should disqualify him from any public office, let alone an ambassadorship. Yet, here we are, witnessing a blatant disregard for ethical standards and legal precedents.

The Kushner Conundrum: A Reflection of Systemic Flaws

Michael Wolff's exposé is not just about Jared Kushner's shadow governance; it's a mirror to the systemic errors within the Trump administration and, by extension, the American political system. The unchecked influence of a single individual, especially one with extensive business interests that could conflict with national interests, poses a significant threat to democratic principles and governance.

The "hidden hand of Jared Kushner" is a symptom of a larger disease - a political environment where power can be wielded without accountability, and personal gain often trumps public good. Wolff's revelations call for a critical examination of not just the individuals in power but the structures that allow such unchecked influence to flourish.

In conclusion, while Kushner's role as the power behind the throne is troubling, it is the environment that enables such dynamics that should be the focus of our collective ire and action. The intertwining of personal business interests with national policy is a recipe for corruption and must be addressed with urgency. The case of Jared Kushner is a wake-up call for tighter regulations, transparent governance, and a reevaluation of the ethical foundations upon which political power is exercised.
