GOP pundits fret MAGA's gone woke: 'Darker, dumber — and ultimately self-defeating'

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20264 min read

The Republican Right Is Not Being “Woke.” It Is Being Honest About Its Cruelty.

A useful panic from the wrong people

A Princeton professor and a Washington Free Beacon writer used the New York Times to argue that the Republican Party’s anti-woke crusade is curdling into something self-defeating. Their complaint is not that the right has embraced racism, sexism, and antisemitism. Their complaint is that it is losing discipline, saying the quiet part too loudly, and risking electoral damage by looking too much like the extremists it has spent years empowering.

Power is not the problem for them

The first thing to name is who actually holds power here. Not the podcast hacks. Not the fringe trolls. The institutional power belongs to the Republican coalition that has spent years making race and gender central to its political project, then acting shocked when the bigots it courted stopped staying in the basement. The source article tries to turn this into a story about messaging failure. That is a dodge. Messaging does not create a “subculture of racism, sexism and antisemitism” out of nowhere. Power creates the conditions, rewards the signalers, and then calls the result an unfortunate drift.

“Antiwhite discrimination” as cover

The op-ed the source describes is built on a grotesque inversion: anti-trans attacks, panic over “antiwhite discrimination,” and culture-war posturing are presented as if they have made America “safer” and “fairer.” That is not analysis. It is justification dressed in concern. The article notes what the pair left out: the ICE agents who killed Renée Good and Alex Pretti, and the wave of hate speech aimed at the WNBA after anti-trans rhetoric escalated. That omission matters. They are not describing the real consequences of the right’s politics because acknowledging them would puncture the fantasy that this is a debate among equals over abstract values.

The real function of “gatekeeping”

Their worry about the GOP “losing its immune response to genuine cranks” is almost funny, except it is a confession. The Republican Party is not losing an immune system. It is revealing that the immune system was always selective. It can absorb open bigotry when that bigotry helps consolidate power, and it panics only when the most vulgar actors threaten to embarrass the brand or shrink the coalition. That is not moderation. It is brand management for a movement that has already chosen its poison.

The line that “the party is becoming more diverse” while allegedly losing its will to gatekeep open bigotry is especially revealing. Diversity, in this framing, is not a democratic achievement. It is a problem to be managed. The complaint is not that racism persists. It is that the party can no longer preserve a culturally sealed version of itself while keeping the same politics intact.

The scapegoats are convenient

Blaming “podcast bros and online streamers” is convenient because it moves responsibility away from elected leaders, party strategists, donors, and media institutions that normalized the terrain in the first place. It also lets the respectable right pose as a victim of its own fringe. That is backwards. The fringe is not a foreign body. It is a laboratory for what the broader movement tolerates, tests, and eventually launders into respectability.

The source notes that the authors want the GOP to sound more moderate while still carrying the same anti-left, anti-trans, anti-equality agenda. That is the old trick: keep the policy direction, soften the vocabulary, and hope the violence becomes less visible. The problem is not that the party is being misread by the public. The problem is that it is being read correctly.

Abolition, not adjustment

Any story that puts ICE in the background while elites fret about conservative branding is already telling you what kind of power structure this is. A federal immigration agency whose agents can kill people should not be treated as a normal policy instrument that merely needs better optics. It should be abolished. Not reworked, not humanized, not retrained into decency. Abolished. The same logic applies more broadly: institutions that enforce hierarchy through violence will keep producing cruelty even when their defenders learn to speak in cleaner sentences.

The pattern underneath

This is the larger political pattern the source accidentally exposes: American right-wing politics keeps manufacturing harm, then pretending the real crisis is that the harm looks ugly. That is how authoritarian movements survive in polite company. They normalize the agenda, outsource the filth to influencers, and ask the public to worry about tone instead of consequences. The editorial complaint here is not that Republicans have become too extreme. It is that they have become less useful at hiding what they are.
