'Desist': Dept run by DeSantis orders reporter to stop probing wife's charity

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Dubious Dance of Power: A Closer Look at Florida's Hope Florida Foundation ScandalIn Conclusion

Casey and Ron DeSantis (Photo via Reuters)

The Florida Department of Children and Families sent a cease-and-desist letter to Orlando Sentinel capital bureau reporter Jeff Schweers over his efforts to investigate the operations of the Hope Florida Foundation, the embattled private welfare substitute charity founded by First Lady of Florida Casey DeSantis.

"It has been reported to the State of Florida Department of Children and Families that Orlando Sentinel reporter Jeff Schweers has been contacting Florida foster families and both falsely and with malicious intent asserting that the families are implicated in fraudulent activity by accepting financial assistance from Hope Florida Foundation, Inc., a charitable Direct Support Organization affiliated with this Department," said the letter, noting that two families who received home restoration services after Hurricanes Helene and Milton were contacted.

"It is believed that Mr. Schweers' threats and accusations were used as coercion to get the families to make negative statements about Hope Florida for his reporting, and/or to dissuade them from accepting future assistance," the letter continued. "To harass and intentionally cause distress to foster families by threats and coercion is abhorrent. Cease and desist the above-described intimidation of these families."

Some reporters, however, did not buy the agency's account of what had happened, and suspect a more sinister intent behind the letter.

"The DeSantis administration is refusing to turn over public records about Hope Florida and now threatening reporters who are trying to know more about it," wrote Lawrence Mower of the Tampa Bay Times.

Hope Florida, a pet project of Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis and his wife to connect low-income families to private and faith-based sources of assistance and reduce government dependency, has been increasingly mired in scandal since it was revealed the DeSantis administration diverted $10 million from a Medicaid settlement into the charity. That money then went to a pair of "dark money" groups that later gave $8.5 million to a political committee run by DeSantis' chief of staff.

Republicans in the legislature have condemned this arrangement as possibly illegal, and state prosecutors have opened an investigation. The governor, for his part, has blasted the scrutiny into Hope Florida, saying he continues to stand behind the program and calling the scandal "manufactured."

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Dance of Power: A Closer Look at Florida's Hope Florida Foundation Scandal

The recent actions of the Florida Department of Children and Families (DCF) against Orlando Sentinel reporter Jeff Schweers unveil not just a troubling instance of governmental overreach but a stark illustration of how power dynamics are manipulated to shield questionable activities from public scrutiny. Schweers, in his pursuit of the truth behind the operations of the Hope Florida Foundation, has been hit with a cease-and-desist letter, an aggressive move that raises more questions than it answers.

The Alleged Harassment: The letter accuses Schweers of contacting Florida foster families under false pretenses, alleging their involvement in fraudulent activities linked to the Hope Florida Foundation. It asserts, without presenting evidence, that Schweers sought to coerce negative statements about the foundation or dissuade families from accepting future assistance. However, this narrative conveniently ignores the core duty of journalists: to investigate and question, especially when public funds and welfare are at stake.

The Real Issue at Hand: Behind the smokescreen of protecting families lies a much larger, more concerning picture. The Hope Florida Foundation, a darling project of Governor Ron DeSantis and First Lady Casey DeSantis, is embroiled in scandal. The diversion of $10 million from a Medicaid settlement into the charity, only for it to funnel into dark money groups linked to DeSantis' chief of staff, is alarming. This is not just a minor mishap but a significant redirection of funds meant for the public good into the murky waters of political financing.

Manufactured Scandal? Hardly.: Governor DeSantis' dismissal of the scrutiny as "manufactured" is both dismissive and disingenuous. When $10 million intended for Medicaid ends up partly in political coffers, the scandal is not manufactured; it is self-inflicted. It's a betrayal of public trust at a fundamental level, especially when those funds could have supported countless individuals in need.

The Sinister Silencing of the Press: What's particularly sinister here is the attempt to silence Schweers and, by extension, any journalistic inquiry into Hope Florida. Lawrence Mower of the Tampa Bay Times rightly points out the refusal to turn over public records and the threatening of reporters as a deeply troubling trend. This is not about protecting foster families from harassment; it's about shielding Hope Florida from accountability.

A Troubling Pattern: This incident is emblematic of a larger pattern of behavior where those in power seek to undermine the press, a vital institution for democracy, to evade scrutiny. The cease-and-desist letter to Schweers is not just an attack on him but an assault on the very principles of transparency and accountability.

In Conclusion

The Hope Florida Foundation scandal, with its web of diverted funds and political entanglements, is a glaring example of how the interplay of power, money, and politics can lead to outcomes that serve the few at the expense of the many. The attempt to muzzle Jeff Schweers and, by proxy, the free press, is a dangerous precedent. It's imperative that the public demand transparency and hold those in power accountable. After all, in a democracy, the truth should not be a casualty of political ambition.
