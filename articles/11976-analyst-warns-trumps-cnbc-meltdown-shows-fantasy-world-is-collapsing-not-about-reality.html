President Donald Trump made a stunning admission during his meltdown on CNBC Tuesday morning, according to one analyst.

CNBC host Joe Kernen corrected Trump after he claimed to have more than 70% of Americans supporting his presidency. Kernen gave him the benefit of the doubt and said Trump might be referring to his support among Republicans. The comment caused Trump to lash out at Kernen and become defensive when the two talked about other topics.

Trump's meltdown happened just days after he fired BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer. Trump accused McEntarfer of being politically biased after she released a report showing hiring had flatlined during his first 90 days in office. Several experts have suggested that Trump fired McEntarfer to change the story about his economy.

Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen discussed the episode on a recent episode of his show, "No Lies."

"For Trump, it's not about working to move public opinion," Cohen said. "He doesn't care about reality, he just wants to be told he's the best."

"He doesn't care that his approval rating is at 37% or about how to get it up," Cohen added. "His only concern is shoveling horses--- down everyone's throats that he's at 70% approval and that's that. Just like he doesn't care about what the actual job numbers say. He just wants everyone to think that they're high."

"And if that reality isn't playing out, then he'll just fire the statistician at the Bureau of Labor Statistics and replace her with someone who will offer stats that are a bit more palatable for Donald Trump," he continued. "A little more praising for Dear Leader."

"It's not about reality. It's what best reflects for Trump," Cohen added.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's CNBC Meltdown and the Dismissal of BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer

President Donald Trump's recent appearance on CNBC was nothing short of a theatrical display of defensiveness and denial. His stunning admission, as one analyst put it, was not just a footnote in a larger story of political theatrics but a glaring spotlight on his administration's approach to governance and truth.

The Facts Behind the Meltdown

During the interview, Trump claimed he had the support of more than 70% of Americans. CNBC host Joe Kernen, in a moment of journalistic integrity, corrected Trump, suggesting he might be referring to his support among Republicans. This correction, seemingly innocuous, was enough to send Trump into a defensive spiral, lashing out at Kernen as they discussed other topics.

The Firing of BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer

Days before this interview, Trump fired BLS Commissioner Erika McEntarfer after a report showed hiring had flatlined during his first 90 days in office. Trump accused McEntarfer of being politically biased, a claim that seems to be more about shifting narratives than addressing the underlying economic issues. Several experts suggest that Trump's dismissal of McEntarfer was an attempt to change the story about his economy, a tactic that reeks of manipulation and deceit.

The Reality Distortion Field

What's particularly alarming is Trump's blatant disregard for reality. Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen hit the nail on the head: "For Trump, it's not about working to move public opinion... He doesn't care about reality, he just wants to be told he's the best." This sentiment was vividly displayed during the CNBC meltdown. Trump's insistence on a 70% approval rating, despite being corrected, and his dismissal of the BLS Commissioner for reporting unfavorable job numbers, is a stark reminder of his administration's approach to truth and governance.

It's not about the truth; it's about what Trump wants the truth to be. Firing a statistician for presenting factual economic data because it doesn't align with Trump's narrative is not just an assault on the Bureau of Labor Statistics; it's an assault on the very notion of truth and accountability in governance.

Reflections

Trump's actions and reactions are not just individual moments of controversy but a systemic pattern of behavior that undermines the integrity of public offices and the factual basis of public discourse. His meltdown on CNBC and the firing of Erika McEntarfer are not isolated incidents but part of a broader, more disturbing trend of reality distortion for political gain.

This episode, as dissected by Brian Tyler Cohen, is a glaring example of Trump's governance strategy: if reality doesn't align with Trump's desired narrative, then reality must be altered, regardless of the consequences. This approach is not only fundamentally undemocratic, but it also erodes the public's trust in institutions designed to serve and inform them, not the whims of any single leader.

In sum, Trump's recent actions and public meltdown serve as a stark reminder of the urgent need for accountability, transparency, and a commitment to truth from our leaders. Anything less is a disservice to the American public and a threat to the democratic principles upon which this country was founded.
