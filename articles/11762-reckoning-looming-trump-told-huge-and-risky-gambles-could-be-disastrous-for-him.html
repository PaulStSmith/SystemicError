'Reckoning looming': Trump told 'huge and risky' gambles could be disastrous for him

But he's making gambles that are "huge and risky," and are threatening to leave him and his MAGA movement in extremely "perilous" territory, a CNN analyst warned Monday.

The twice-impeached ex-president has unleashed a torrent of extreme policies that experts warn could devastate the economy and shatter America's global standing. His obsessive tariff war — dismissed by many experts as economically illiterate — has already cost manufacturing jobs, with the sector hemorrhaging 14,000 positions in May and June alone.

"We're taking in trillions of dollars," Trump boasted to CNBC about his tariffs while economists warned American families will foot the bill through sky-high prices on everyday goods.

And CNN's Stephen Collinson wrote that Trump's deportation machine has transformed into a fascistic nightmare, with masked federal agents conducting neighborhood sweeps while the administration constructs what critics call "armed camps" for migrants. Border-state Democrat Mark Kelly is sounding alarms about Trump's extreme overreach.

"We have now swung drastically in another direction. And I don't think this is what the American people want either," Kelly warned on CBS.

"There’s a common thread running through most of Trump’s actions — whether it’s browbeating political enemies and weaponizing the legal system or forcing trade concessions out of small and vulnerable nations," Collinson wrote.

"He’s always assessing his leverage over an adversary and seeking to impose his personal will."

The numbers prove Kelly right — 55% of Americans now say Trump has gone too far on deportations, a 10-point jump since February, Collinson wrote. His legislative "triumph"—a massive giveaway to the wealthy—is opposed by 6 in 10 Americans who expect it will crater the economy.

"A perilous national moment could arrive if public opinion turns sharply against Trump and Republicans seem about to pay the price in next year’s midterm elections," wrote the CNN columnist. "This, after all, is a president who believes he has almost unlimited power and tried to steal an election he lost in 2020."

Perhaps most terrifyingly, Trump is preparing to meet Vladimir Putin in Alaska, potentially abandoning Ukraine to Moscow's imperial ambitions, Collinson wrote.

Meanwhile, his handpicked Health Secretary Robert Kennedy Jr. is sabotaging public health by slashing vaccination funding, setting the stage for preventable disease outbreaks that could kill thousands.

All of this together poses a real threat to his administration, Collinson warned.

"Trump is a singular politician, and he’s been helped by a Republican Party that marches to his tune and a Democratic Party that doesn’t have one," he wrote.

"But unless Trump shattered political logic while he was breaking the mold of the presidency, a reckoning could be looming."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Second Term: A Masterclass in Political Recklessness

Seven months into his encore in the Oval Office, Donald Trump is demonstrating what can only be described as a brazen disregard for the foundational pillars of American democracy and global stability. The CNN analysis paints a portrait of a president not merely content with pushing the envelope but seemingly determined to shred it entirely, engaging in what are described as "huge and risky" gambles. Let's dissect the layers of this unfolding chaos.

The Economic Kamikaze: Tariffs and Job Losses

Trump's tariff war, a move lambasted by a chorus of economic experts as akin to shooting the American economy in the foot, has led to the hemorrhaging of 14,000 manufacturing jobs in just two months. Trump's boast of "taking in trillions" from tariffs is a gross misrepresentation, as the reality hits American families hard, with soaring prices on everyday goods. This policy isn't just economically illiterate; it's economic malpractice.

Fascistic Nightmares and Deportation Drives

The transformation of Trump's deportation strategy into what can only be described as a "fascistic nightmare" is alarming. With masked federal agents sweeping neighborhoods and the construction of what are effectively armed camps for migrants, America's moral compass is spinning out of control. The fact that 55% of Americans believe Trump has gone too far on deportations speaks volumes. It's not just a policy shift; it's a humanitarian crisis in the making.

Weaponizing Power and Political Bullying

Trump's approach to governance, described succinctly by CNN's Stephen Collinson, hinges on bullying political foes, weaponizing the legal system, and exploiting small nations. This is not the art of negotiation; it's the art of coercion and intimidation. His massive tax cuts for the wealthy, opposed by 6 in 10 Americans, showcase a leader more concerned with enriching allies than fostering economic equity or stability.

A Troubling Foreign Policy and Public Health Hazard

On the international stage, Trump's preparation to meet with Vladimir Putin, potentially sacrificing Ukraine's sovereignty, is a stark reminder of his first-term flirtations with autocrats. Domestically, appointing Robert Kennedy Jr. as Health Secretary and slashing vaccination funding is a public health disaster waiting to happen, risking thousands of American lives.

The GOP's Complicity and the Democrats' Disarray

What amplifies Trump's ability to pursue these extreme policies is the Republican Party's unwavering loyalty and a Democratic Party struggling to find its voice. This dynamic has allowed Trump to operate with a sense of impunity, pushing the boundaries of his office's power to dangerous new lows.

The Path Ahead: A Looming Reckoning?

Despite Trump's efforts to defy political gravity, the CNN analysis suggests a reckoning could be on the horizon. Public opinion is turning, and the midterm elections could spell a significant shift. However, this assumes that the mechanisms of democracy can withstand the assault they're currently under. Trump's belief in his "almost unlimited power" and his attempts to subvert the electoral process are red flags that cannot be ignored.

In sum, Trump's second term is shaping up to be a masterclass in how not to lead a nation. Unless there's a significant course correction, the United States stands on the precipice of economic, social, and political crises that could take generations to repair. The question now is not just about Trump's legacy but about the resilience of American democracy itself.
