PIX, De-Dollarization, and the Crack in America’s Financial Empire

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Washington’s worst nightmare wasn’t born in Beijing, Moscow, or even Riyadh. It was born in Brasília. And it didn’t come in the form of a nuclear weapon, a new alliance, or some grand oil deal—it came as a humble app. Pix, Brazil’s instant payment system, launched in 2020, has become both a tool of domestic empowerment and a geopolitical tremor that threatens to unravel America’s financial chokehold on the world.

PIX: Financial Democracy in Practice

Let’s start with what Pix is. At its core, Pix is a government-run, free, instant payment system. It’s so simple and so effective that in just four years it has reached over 76% of Brazil’s population. It has become the default method for Brazilians to pay rent, buy groceries, send money to relatives, or settle debts—all without ever touching the U.S. dollar, Visa, or Mastercard.

For Brazil, this is more than convenience—it’s financial liberation. Brazil’s banking sector has long been dominated by oligopolistic institutions that charge outrageous fees for the most basic services. Ordinary citizens, especially the poor and unbanked, were either gouged or excluded. Pix shattered that. For the first time, street vendors, gig workers, pensioners, and small businesses have access to real-time, free digital transactions. This isn’t Silicon Valley hype about “financial inclusion.” It’s the real deal, and it’s happening in the Global South, led by a government agency, not venture capitalists.

In our previous writing, we’ve called Pix a weapon of financial democracy. It levels the playing field domestically. But what the video transcript makes explicit—and what Washington is panicking over—is that Pix also levels the playing field internationally.

De-Dollarization in Action

The dollar’s global dominance is not about efficiency or trust anymore—it’s about control. Through SWIFT, sanctions, and the institutional inertia of using the dollar as a reserve currency, Washington has been able to flip a switch and cripple entire economies. Syria, Russia, Venezuela, Cuba—the list goes on. America has long relied on the financial nuclear option: deny your adversary access to dollars, and watch their economy wither.

But Pix doesn’t run on dollars. It doesn’t run on SWIFT. It doesn’t even ask permission. And now, it’s being integrated with BRICS Pay, a system designed to allow direct currency exchange among Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa. Together, these platforms represent the embryonic stages of a parallel financial world where Washington’s chokehold is irrelevant.

The video puts it bluntly: Pix is a foundational step toward de-dollarization. Each time a transaction happens outside the dollar’s orbit, the symbolic power of the greenback erodes. The dollar remains dominant because nations consent to use it—even when it makes them vulnerable. That consent is wearing thin. China already settles more trade with key partners in yuan than in dollars. India and Russia experiment with ruble-rupee deals. And Brazil? Brazil is building the pipes for a world where the dollar is optional.

Washington’s Heavy-Handed Panic

And how does the U.S. respond? With bluster, tariffs, and coercion. According to the video, the Trump administration slapped 50% tariffs on Brazilian exports and even banned visas for Supreme Court judges involved in Bolsonaro’s coup trial. Washington also launched a trade probe, whining that Pix “unfairly” sidelines Visa and Mastercard. Imagine the absurdity: the U.S. government crying foul because Brazil built a free system that people actually prefer over fee-gouging U.S. credit card companies.

This is classic imperial overreach. By trying to punish Brazil for choosing sovereignty, Washington accelerates the very process it fears. Sanctions and tariffs don’t frighten Brazil into compliance—they push it deeper into BRICS integration. The transcript itself highlights the irony: the U.S. is hastening the end of dollar hegemony by weaponizing its dominance so brazenly.

PIX as a Geopolitical Statement

Make no mistake: Pix wasn’t designed as an anti-American tool. Brazil didn’t roll it out to stick a thumb in Uncle Sam’s eye. The Central Bank built Pix to solve a domestic problem: the exclusion of millions from the financial system. And in that sense, Pix is about national strength, not foreign policy.

But intent doesn’t matter when impact is this significant. Whether Brasília wanted it or not, Pix has become a political statement. It says that a country doesn’t need to wait for Wall Street to bless its financial system. It says that alternatives are possible, scalable, and attractive. And once alternatives exist, Washington can no longer take consent for granted.

The Cracks in the Dollar Empire

Let’s not exaggerate: the dollar is not dead. Brazil still depends on it for trade, foreign investment, and inflation control. Brazilian elites still keep their money in Miami condos. The U.S. remains deeply embedded in Brazil’s economy. But what’s changed is the trajectory. Dollar dominance is no longer unquestioned, and Pix is proof.

The transcript hits the nail on the head: systems like Pix aren’t just technical innovations; they’re political statements. They chip away at the foundation of U.S. financial power. And when those foundations crack, empires tremble.

The Manufactured Narrative of “Anti-Americanism”

Western pundits love to paint de-dollarization as inherently anti-American—a battle of good vs. evil, freedom vs. authoritarianism. But this framing is a lie. As we’ve argued before, currency multipolarity isn’t anti-American, it’s pro-sovereignty. It allows nations to chart their own financial destinies without being held hostage by Washington’s whims.

Brazil isn’t trying to burn down the dollar. Brazil is trying to build itself up. And yet Washington insists on treating every assertion of independence as an act of rebellion. That paranoid worldview is exactly why the U.S. keeps overplaying its hand and driving countries away.

The PIX Precedent

Here’s the truth: Pix has set a precedent. It proves that a state-led financial innovation can scale, can empower the poor, and can terrify Washington without firing a single shot. If Brazil can do it, why not Argentina? Why not South Africa? Why not Indonesia? Each new Pix-like system is another crack in the façade of inevitability around the dollar.

The U.S. dollar empire was built on consent, inertia, and coercion. Pix undermines all three:

  • Consent: Brazilians now see they can transact without the dollar, and so can their government.

  • Inertia: Old excuses about “there’s no alternative” no longer hold. Pix is an alternative.

  • Coercion: The U.S. can’t freeze or sanction Pix transactions the way it can with SWIFT.

This is why Washington is terrified of a “humble app.” Because once the illusion of inevitability breaks, the empire crumbles faster than anyone expects.

Conclusion: From Tremor to Earthquake

The transcript ends with a warning: the U.S. may one day look back and realize that its overreaction to a Brazilian payment app turned a tremor into an earthquake. That’s not hyperbole. That’s history repeating itself. Empires always fall not just because of external enemies, but because of their own arrogance, their own insistence on domination at all costs.

Pix is not anti-American. Pix is not revolutionary by design. But Pix shows the world that another way is possible. And when the world sees that, the dollar’s days of unchallenged supremacy are numbered.

So yes, de-dollarization is real. Brazil just proved it. And Washington’s frantic, ham-fisted response shows just how fragile the empire truly is.
