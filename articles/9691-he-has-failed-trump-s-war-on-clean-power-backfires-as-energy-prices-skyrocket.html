'He has failed': Trump's war on clean power backfires as energy prices skyrocket

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 10, 20254 min read

President Donald Trump’s pledge to slash energy prices “by half within 12 months” has “failed” spectacularly, argued The New York Times’ editorial board in an op-ed Thursday, which blamed skyrocketing energy prices squarely on the president’s “war on solar and wind power.”

“Mr. Trump ran for president promising to reduce the cost of living and of energy prices in particular,” the op-ed reads. “He has failed so far.”

During Trump’s second term, electricity prices have surged at an unprecedented rate, increasing by 10% in the first half of 2025. The culprit, The New York Times argued, was largely supply constraints caused by the war in Ukraine and the astronomical energy demands of artificial intelligence data centers, the cost of the latter often subsidized by nearby communities.

Yet, instead of embracing more forms of energy production, Trump has waged a “war” on renewable energy sources, making “matters worse” The New York Times argued, and setting the stage for the prices of non-renewable energy sources like gas to increase as well.

“Gas prices will also increase over the next decade, according to Rhodium Group, a think tank, as consumers who would otherwise have driven electric cars continue using vehicles that burn fossil fuels,” the op-ed reads.

Trump has frequently targeted renewable energy sources as “horrible,” having raged against windmills for “causing whales to die in numbers never seen before,” “killing your birds,” “ruining your oceans,” and destroying property values. As such, he repealed a Biden-era tax credit program for clean energy in July, axing billions of dollars’ worth of investments in renewable energy sources.

Trump’s top energy official, Energy Secretary Chris Wright, former oil tycoon, appeared to know back in August that energy prices would continue to soar,admittingit to be a political liability for Republicans, but blamed “Obama-Biden policies” for the price spikes regardless.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Energy Policy: A Spectacular Failure

In the realm of promises made, promises unkept, President Donald Trump’s pledge to slash energy prices by half within 12 months stands as a glaring testament to a presidency marred by policy misfires and a fundamental misunderstanding of the energy sector. The New York Times editorial board’s recent op-ed does more than just highlight this failure; it paints a vivid picture of a policy approach that is not just flawed but spectacularly misguided.

The War on Renewables

Trump’s so-called “war on solar and wind power” is not just a simple policy disagreement; it is a full-blown assault on the very solutions that could mitigate some of the energy crises we face today. While Trump promised to reduce the cost of living and particularly energy prices, his administration has taken a path that ensures the opposite. Skyrocketing electricity prices, with a 10% increase in the first half of 2025 alone, are a clear indicator of a policy gone awry. But to blame these increases solely on Trump would be an oversimplification. Yes, the supply constraints caused by the war in Ukraine and the energy demands of artificial intelligence data centers play a significant role. However, Trump’s aggressive stance against renewable energy has exacerbated an already dire situation.

Ignoring the Inevitable

The administration’s failure to embrace a diversified energy production strategy, particularly one that includes renewable sources, is short-sighted. The New York Times points out the folly in this approach, noting how it sets the stage for the prices of non-renewable energy sources like gas to increase as well. The Rhodium Group’s prediction of rising gas prices over the next decade, due in part to consumers sticking with fossil fuel vehicles amidst a lack of electric options, is a foreseeable outcome of Trump’s policies.

The Rhetoric vs. The Reality

Trump’s rhetoric on renewable energy sources, describing them as “horrible” and blaming windmills for a slew of environmental and economic ills, is not just misleading; it is dangerously false. His claims about windmills “causing whales to die in numbers never seen before,” “killing your birds,” “ruining your oceans,” and “destroying property values” are not grounded in reality. This kind of rhetoric only serves to deflect from the tangible benefits of renewable energy sources and highlights a profound ignorance of their potential.

Furthermore, the repeal of a Biden-era tax credit program for clean energy, which axed billions of dollars’ worth of investments in renewable energy sources, is a move that can only be described as antithetical to progress. It represents a step backwards in the fight against climate change and a blow to the economic opportunities that the clean energy sector presents.

The Blame Game

The Trump administration’s attempt to blame the “Obama-Biden policies” for the energy price spikes is a transparent effort to deflect responsibility. Energy Secretary Chris Wright’s admission that energy prices would continue to soar, while simultaneously blaming previous administrations, underscores a refusal to acknowledge the consequences of their own policy decisions. It is a clear example of political expediency taking precedence over effective governance.

Conclusion

In the final analysis, the Trump administration’s energy policies, particularly its adversarial stance on renewable energy, represent a spectacular failure. Not only have these policies failed to deliver on the promise of reduced energy prices, but they have also set the stage for future economic and environmental turmoil. The New York Times’ op-ed is more than just a critique; it is a call to action for a reassessment of our energy policies and priorities. The future of our planet and our economy depends on it.
