Trump triggered economists' worst fears — and Nobel laureate warns it may be 'permanent'

Based on an article by Robert Davis

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

Trump’s Economy Is Not a Mistake. It Is the Policy.

The Decision Was the Point

The source is straightforward on the facts: Trump’s administration is trying to deport millions of people it says are here illegally, and the latest labor data showed employment fell in July after earlier months were revised down. Nobel laureate Paul Krugman’s warning is not that the economy got unlucky. It is that a policy choice made by the most powerful office in the country is producing a weaker labor market and may leave a lasting scar.

That is the key fact the political class keeps sanding down. This is not an external shock. It is not a weather event. It is the governing program of the White House.

Power, Not Confusion

Trump holds the institutional power. His administration chose deportation as a central instrument of rule. That means the consequences belong to the administration, not to the workers being targeted, not to economists pointing at the damage, and not to some vague market mood. When a government removes workers from the economy, it is choosing to shrink the labor supply and push costs onto everyone else.

Krugman’s point about “fewer workers, paying less taxes” is not abstract bookkeeping. It is an indictment of the administration’s priorities. The same government that claims responsibility for national security, retirees, infrastructure, and health care is actively making those obligations harder to finance and staff.

Scapegoating as Economic Policy

The Trump line is familiar: blame immigrants, promise strength, deliver contraction. That is the structure. Immigrants are cast as a problem to justify state violence, while the real damage comes from the state itself. The administration gets to posture as tough while stripping the economy of labor, skill, and tax base.

Krugman notes that foreign-born workers do jobs native-born workers often avoid, including emptying bedpans and picking fruit. That is not sentimental language. It is a description of how the economy actually functions. The people being targeted are not decorative extras. They are part of the machinery that keeps hospitals running and food on the table.

The Weakness Is Deliberate

The July jobs report, the 23,000-job loss between June and July, and the revisions downward all point in one direction: the slowdown is not a blip. The source says economists increasingly see a “possibly permanent” era of stagnant employment. That is what policy looks like when it is designed to punish, exclude, and narrow the labor force while pretending the damage is incidental.

The framing is weak whenever it treats this as an unfortunate side effect of Trump’s politics. The harm is not collateral. It is built into the project.

What This Pattern Reveals

This is how authoritarian politics works in practice: concentrate power, manufacture a target, and then act surprised when the machinery of punishment breaks other systems. Deportation is sold as order. In reality, it is economic self-harm aimed downward at workers, public services, and the people who depend on them.

The larger lesson is simple. A government that sees human beings as removable inputs will eventually start treating the economy itself the same way. Trump is not rescuing the country from decline. He is governing through it, and calling the wreckage strength.
