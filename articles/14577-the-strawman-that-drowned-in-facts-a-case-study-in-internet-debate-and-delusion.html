The Strawman That Drowned in Facts: A Case Study in Internet Debate and Delusion

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 22, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

1. Projection as Policy: The Delusion of “Real Work”2. Welfare Math for the Willfully Ignorant3. The Despair Spiral: Cynicism Disguised as Wisdom4. The One Thing He Got Right — and Still Got WrongConclusion: Truth Isn’t a Matter of Opinion

It started with a simple, damning observation: the wealthiest blue states fund the poorest red states. A fact so uncomfortable to the American right-wing narrative that it functions like garlic to a vampire. Within minutes, someone charged into the comments, rage-first, logic last, to tell us how wrong we were.

Thus began a saga of denial, projection, and increasingly unhinged deflection. What followed wasn’t a debate. It was a dissection of what happens when ideology slams headfirst into reality and leaves nothing behind but a puddle of resentment.

1. Projection as Policy: The Delusion of “Real Work”

The first punch came fast: “Blue states contribute nothing of value.” Apparently, tech, biotech, entertainment, research, and finance are worthless because they don’t come wrapped in denim and diesel. Never mind that Silicon Valley, Wall Street, and Boston hospitals fund, power, and literally heal the country. To this man, it wasn’t real unless it looked like 1953.

This is the heart of the red-state mythos: that “real work” involves calluses and carbon emissions, and anything else is coastal elitism. Never mind that the very smartphone he used to post that take was designed in California, runs on cloud infrastructure managed from Seattle, and connects through software invented in blue cities.

2. Welfare Math for the Willfully Ignorant

Next came the claim that our welfare data was false, because it didn’t account for the mythical drain of undocumented immigrants. Never mind that multiple peer-reviewed studies (including from the Cato Institute and National Academies of Sciences) show that undocumented immigrants pay billions in taxes and receive almost nothing in return.

And the original point still stood, untouched: Mississippi, Kentucky, and West Virginia take in more than double what they pay in federal taxes. California, New York, and New Jersey are net contributors. Red states survive off the productivity of blue states while calling them lazy. That’s not just hypocrisy. That’s parasitism with a superiority complex.

3. The Despair Spiral: Cynicism Disguised as Wisdom

When the facts refused to yield, the tone shifted: everything is fake, everything is rigged. The economy is a grift, innovation is dead, Wall Street is a scam, and Silicon Valley is just people eating each other’s lunch.

This wasn’t argument. It was nihilism in a trucker hat. A man so consumed by the idea that he’s been cheated that he now sees value itself as a lie. But this fatalism isn’t revolutionary. It’s just lazy. It offers no solution, no alternative, just a bitter sneer from the bleachers while others do the work of building, creating, and fixing.

4. The One Thing He Got Right — and Still Got Wrong

“Criminals face no consequences.”

Yes. That part, sadly, is true. But the face of that injustice isn’t some faceless banker or imaginary welfare queen. It’s Donald J. Trump. A man with 88 indictments, multiple civil judgments, and a history of fraud, sexual abuse, and inciting insurrection. Still free. And, worse, reelected president.

And yet, this guy didn’t bring up Trump. Instead, he attacked Wall Street, tech firms, and blue-state governance — ignoring the fact that the Republican Party he likely supports is the one actively protecting the elite from consequence. The GOP didn’t drain the swamp. They opened luxury condos in it.

Conclusion: Truth Isn’t a Matter of Opinion

This wasn’t a discussion. It was a postmortem. A man walked into a thread swinging ideology like a hammer, only to find the facts were made of vibranium. Every rebuttal was met with deflection. Every receipt was ignored. And in the end, he didn’t refute anything. He just got tired, grumbled about cleaning his shop, and rage-quit with a passive-aggressive thumbs-up emoji.

He didn’t lose because he was outnumbered. He lost because he brought feeling to a fact fight.

This is where we are in public discourse: half the country arguing that 2 + 2 = tyranny. And when shown the math, they don’t recalculate. They accuse the calculator of bias.

So yes, there is a lesson here: not every argument is worth having. But every time we do have it, the truth stands taller.

Let the strawmen burn.
