Lawyer suggests governor could be in handcuffs in wild tirade over jailed MAGA clerk

The lawyer representing jailed former Colorado elections clerk turned MAGA conspiracy activist Tina Peters suggested that Gov. Jared Polis (D-CO), as well as Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold, could find themselves in prison soon.

The attorney, Peter Ticktin, made this claim as part of a furious statement responding to Polis’ refusal to release Peters from her sentence for unlawfully breaching election equipment, as part of her plot to prove President Donald Trump was the rightful winner of the 2020 presidential election.

Trump issued a pardon for Peters this week, but that pardon is considered purely symbolic because she was convicted on state charges, which are not subject to federal pardons. But Ticktin insists that this is an unsettled legal question and Peters should, at the very least, be released until courts decide one way or the other.

“The Governor states that he will abide by the judgment of the courts,” said Ticktin in a message posted to X by local reporter Kyle Clark. “However, the process of litigation takes time, and Tina Peters needs to be released while the issues are being resolved. The governor has no way to know which way the courts will decide the issue of whether President Trump has the right to pardon prisoners who were convicted of state offenses.”

“As such, he has no right to hold her, until the issue is determined, as he does not know if he is rightfully holding a free person in prison or one who should be in prison,” Ticktin continued. “He has no right to keep a person in prison because she may belong there. If he does, he is, at the very least, imprisoning a pardoned lady, which has serious legal ramifications in their own right.”

“The fact of the matter is that those in power, such as Governor Polis, become lulled in their echo chambers to be blind to the fact that their situation is changing,” he said. “Today, these folk are in charge, but there is a good chance that tomorrow, they, especially Jena Griswold, will be in handcuffs, herself. After all, she violated federal law by installing the Trusted Build, being fully aware that they would delete the evidence of the election. It is so ironic when the one who destroyed evidence is taking a harsh position that the one who was preserving the evidence remain in prison. If they have any smarts, at all, they would stop their silly game of wrongfully imprisoning an innocent woman who is in the prayers of half of this country.”

This is not Ticktin’s only eyebrow-raising statement about the case. Earlier in the day, he agreed with longtime Trump ally Steve Bannon that Trump should deploy the 101st Airborne Division to spring Peters from prison.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Ticktin’s Legal Fantasies

In an episode that blurs the line between legal advocacy and political theater, Peter Ticktin, the attorney for Tina Peters—a former Colorado elections clerk turned MAGA conspiracy activist—has unleashed a tirade that could be mistaken for a script from a political satire, were it not so earnestly presented. This narrative, featuring threats of imprisonment for Colorado Governor Jared Polis and Secretary of State Jena Griswold, is a fascinating study in the desperation and distortion that seems to plague certain fringes of American political discourse.

The Core of Ticktin’s Argument: A Misunderstood Pardon

Ticktin’s argument hinges on a fundamental misunderstanding—or perhaps a willful misrepresentation—of the legal system. His claim that President Donald Trump’s pardon of Peters, which is undeniably symbolic given its impotence against state charges, should somehow prompt her immediate release, showcases either a baffling ignorance of the law or a deliberate attempt to sow confusion.

The insistence that this scenario presents an “unsettled legal question” is, at best, a legal Hail Mary devoid of precedent or, at worst, a farcical misunderstanding of federalism. The clarity with which the U.S. Constitution delineates the separation of federal and state powers leaves little room for ambiguity; a presidential pardon does not extend to state convictions. Ticktin’s assertion that Governor Polis has no right to hold Peters “until the issue is determined” is a bewildering misinterpretation of legal authority and process.

A Threat to Polis and Griswold: Baseless and Reckless

Ticktin’s veiled threats against Governor Polis and Secretary of State Griswold are as baseless as they are reckless. Suggesting that these officials could soon find themselves “in handcuffs” for adhering to their duties and the rule of law is not only an affront to legal and political norms but also a dangerous escalation of rhetoric. The accusation levied against Griswold, alleging that she violated federal law by installing the Trusted Build—a system update for Colorado’s election equipment—is a gross mischaracterization of election security measures.

The irony Ticktin attempts to highlight, positioning Peters as a preserver of evidence and Griswold as its destroyer, is a distorted reflection of reality. In truth, Peters is convicted of unlawfully breaching election equipment, an act that undermines the integrity of the electoral process, while Griswold’s actions are in defense of that very integrity.

The Theatrical Finale: A Call to Deploy the 101st Airborne

Perhaps the most startling aspect of Ticktin’s rhetoric is his agreement with Steve Bannon’s call for Trump to deploy the 101st Airborne Division to liberate Peters from prison—a suggestion so outlandish that it transcends legal advocacy and enters the realm of political fantasy. This proposal is not only impractical but also highlights a dangerous undercurrent of extremism that seeks to undermine the judiciary and glorify vigilantism.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Diversion

Ticktin’s statements, rife with legal inaccuracies and inflammatory threats, contribute to a dangerous diversion from democratic principles and the rule of law. The narrative constructed around Tina Peters’ case is emblematic of a broader trend in which facts are contorted to fit political narratives, and legal proceedings are misrepresented to rally support. This episode should serve as a cautionary tale of the lengths to which some will go to subvert the legal system for political ends, and the imperative to remain vigilant against such distortions.
