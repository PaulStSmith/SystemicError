The Education Secretary Who Came to Burn the House Down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There are moments in American politics that are so grotesquely absurd they don’t simply strain credulity—they vaporize it. The clip in question is one of them. A polished, camera-ready government official sits before the Capitol dome and announces, with breathtaking confidence, that California—the state whose university system is a global academic engine—should model its education standards after “powerhouses” like Alabama. It’s a line so laughable, so inverted, so brazenly detached from empirical reality, it feels ripped straight from a glitchy simulation.

But this wasn’t a pundit trying to juice engagement. This wasn’t a random talking head straining to impress their audience. No—this was the Secretary of Education of the United States, appointed by the Orange Baboon to dismantle the very institution she was sworn to lead. And she is doing that job with ruthless efficiency.

The Trojan Horse in Designer Clothing

The Secretary’s appointment was never a mystery. She wasn’t chosen for her expertise in pedagogy, for her commitment to public education, or for any demonstrated willingness to strengthen America’s intellectual infrastructure. No, she was chosen for one reason: she wanted to kill the Department of Education and hand its husk to private interests.

Her mission was that of a wrecking ball disguised as a public servant.

It was a perfect ideological alignment with an administration allergic to competence, hostile to knowledge, and committed to the corrosive fantasy that the private sector can solve everything if you simply drown the government in a bathtub first.

The sheer audacity of appointing a Secretary of Education who fundamentally opposes public education would be hilarious if it weren’t a national catastrophe. It’s the exact same reason the EPA was handed to men who thought clean air regulations were a personal insult, and why ICE became a militarized extraction force rather than an immigration agency. You don’t pick stewards—you pick saboteurs.

And the Education Secretary rose to that task with zeal.

Weaponized Mediocrity as Policy

Her statement about Alabama isn’t ignorance. It’s a roadmap.

By praising one of the consistently lowest-performing states in nationwide education rankings, she’s not making a mistake. She’s revealing her ideology: that the public system should be gutted, hollowed out, and dragged down to a point where privatization looks like the only escape.

When a far-right education secretary calls Alabama an “academic powerhouse,” it’s a quiet part said at full volume. Because in her worldview, power isn’t measured by excellence. It’s measured by control—who gets it, who keeps it, and who is cut out entirely.

Alabama is a dream model for this ideology: underfunded public schools, high dependence on religious institutions, and a state government that treats education like a nuisance rather than an investment. Of course she calls it a powerhouse. It’s the blueprint.

The goal is regression. The goal is shrinkage. The goal is a generation intellectually kneecapped enough to accept the next wave of regressive policies without protest.

This isn’t incompetence. It’s strategy.

Dismantling from Within: A Masterclass in Institutional Sabotage

What makes this so galling—so infuriating—is not that she misunderstands education. It’s that she understands it perfectly well.

She knows that public education is one of the last remaining equalizers in a society spiraling toward plutocratic rule. She knows that dismantling it cements generational inequality. She knows that starving schools creates a vacuum easily filled by for-profit institutions, religious indoctrination centers, and unregulated private voucher mills.

When she praises Alabama, she’s not admiring scholastic excellence. She’s admiring ideological compliance.

To her, the ideal education system isn’t one that uplifts students. It’s one that sorts them, contains them, and ensures that only the privileged can access real opportunity.

And California—the state that dared to build world-class institutions accessible to ordinary people—becomes the villain in this world. It is too successful, too effective, too committed to the radical notion that education should be a public good.

Of course she wants it dragged down.

Of course she wants it modeled after the basement instead of the ceiling.

Wrecking balls don’t aspire upward.

The Smile of Someone Paid to Pull the Pin

Look closely at the clip. The calm smile. The unwavering tone. The serene confidence.

That is not the face of someone misspeaking. That is the face of someone carrying out orders.

When she says California should learn from Alabama, she’s not offering advice—she’s delivering a threat. A warning shot from an administration that views intellect as an enemy combatant.

The Orange Baboon did not staff his Cabinet with experts. He staffed it with demolitions teams. Men and women selected specifically because they were hostile to the institutions they now commanded.

And in education, the damage will echo the longest.

Millions of students won’t get these years back. Generations will feel the aftershocks of policies crafted by ideologues who see educated citizens not as assets but as obstacles.

This wasn’t mismanagement.

It was demolition.

A Nation That Treats Education as Optional Is a Nation on Fire

The Secretary’s performance on Fox Business wasn’t a gaffe. It was a confession of faith.

The faith is simple:

  • Public education should fail.

  • Private profiteers should rise.

  • States with broken systems should set the national standard.

  • Excellence in blue states should be punished, undermined, or mocked.

It’s a worldview that treats knowledge as dangerous and critical thought as a threat. A worldview that weaponizes mediocrity, wraps it in patriotic rhetoric, and sells it to voters as “common sense.”

Of course she wants California to imitate Alabama. It’s the easiest way to sabotage what works.

It’s the simplest way to drag the nation down to a level where educated citizens can’t fight back.

The Endgame: A Permanently Undereducated Public

The real goal was never improvement. It was indoctrination through deprivation.

Destroy the schools.
Delegitimize the teachers.
Starve the budgets.
Privatize the futures.

And then stand on television and call it reform.

This is how democracies fall—not with tanks in the streets but with education secretaries praising the basement as the penthouse and convincing millions to climb down the staircase.

The tragedy isn’t that she said it. The tragedy is that she meant it.

And the greater tragedy is that she was allowed to do it.

America didn’t get an education secretary.
America got an arsonist with a federal salary.

And the house she was hired to burn is still smoldering.
