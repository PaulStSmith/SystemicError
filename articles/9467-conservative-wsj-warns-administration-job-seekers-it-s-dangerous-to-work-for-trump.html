Using the indictment of former Trump national security advisor John Bolton as a leaping-off point, the editors of the conservative Wall Street Journal lambasted the continuing efforts of the president and his Department of Justice to prosecute former advisors and warned it should be a red flag to stay out of the president’s orbit.

In Journal editors’ op-ed late Thursday, they got right to the point in the first line by declaring, “Opposing Donald Trump is a perilous business, but working for him can be equally as dangerous. That’s one lesson from Thursday’s indictment of Mr. Trump’s former national security adviser John Bolton for mishandling classified documents.”

Taking Bolton’s side by writing, “The indictment makes much of the fact that the information Mr. Bolton is accused of mishandling didn’t appear in his book after his draft was reviewed by the White House. But the review process exists to safeguard national secrets, and Mr. Bolton subjected himself to it,” they added that the motivation for the targeted prosecution is no secret.

“There’s little doubt that the underlying motivation for this prosecution is retribution. The President has targeted Mr. Bolton at least since 2020 when Mr. Trump called for his prosecution after Mr. Bolton wrote his book,” the editors wrote.

According to the editors, the treatment of Bolton should alarm anyone in Trump’s employ.

“Mr. Trump recruited Mr. Bolton to work for him and cast him aside over policy disagreements. The lesson is that if you work for the President, he then sours on you and you criticize him, you are not safe,” they wrote before adding, “Mr. Bolton will get his day in court, and we look forward to his defense. In our experience he is a patriot who would do nothing to compromise national security. He never leaked classified information to us. If Mr. Bolton had praised Mr. Trump in his book, it’s safe to say he wouldn’t have been indicted.”

Our Analysis:

The Perils of Orbiting Trump: A Scathing Analysis of Bolton’s Indictment

The recent indictment of John Bolton, Donald Trump’s former national security advisor, serves as a stark warning to current and prospective White House aides: align with Trump at your own peril. The Wall Street Journal, a bastion of conservative thought, has lambasted Trump’s seemingly vindictive use of the Department of Justice to prosecute former advisors, using Bolton’s indictment as a prime example. This isn’t just a legal issue; it’s a glaring spotlight on the toxic environment fostered by Trump’s administration.

The Dangerous Double-Edged Sword

In a blistering op-ed, the Journal editors mince no words: “Opposing Donald Trump is a perilous business, but working for him can be equally as dangerous.“ This opening salvo sets the tone for a critique that is both a warning to those circling Trump and a condemnation of his retaliatory tactics. The indictment of Bolton for mishandling classified documents—after his book draft, which presumably irked Trump, was cleared by the White House—smells less of justice and more of retribution.

The Misuse of Power for Personal Vendettas

The editors argue, quite convincingly, that the move against Bolton is less about safeguarding national secrets and more about settling scores. Trump’s pattern of discarding and then discrediting those who fall out of his favor is not new, but the use of legal prosecution as a weapon marks a disturbing escalation. It’s clear: the real crime in Trump’s eyes was not mishandling classified information but criticizing him publicly.

A Patriot or a Pawn?

The Journal stands by Bolton, describing him as a patriot who has always acted with the nation’s security in mind. This defense is notable, coming from a publication that has been both critical and supportive of Trump at different times. The assertion that Bolton would not have been indicted had he sung praises of Trump in his book is a damning indictment of Trump’s pettiness and his administration’s willingness to misuse its powers for personal vendettas.

Conclusion: A Warning Ignored at One’s Own Risk

The Wall Street Journal‘s op-ed is more than just a defense of John Bolton; it’s a clarion call to anyone considering aligning themselves with Trump. The message is stark: Working with Trump exposes individuals to the whims of a leader who views loyalty as a one-way street, and who is quick to use the machinery of government to punish dissent and protect his own ego. As the Journal aptly warns, this should be a red flag for anyone in Trump’s orbit. The indictment of Bolton is not just about the mishandling of classified documents; it’s a glimpse into the corrosive culture of revenge that permeates Trump’s presidency.

In this saga, the Wall Street Journal has positioned itself as a voice of reason, warning against the perils of political loyalty that borders on fealty. As we watch Bolton’s case unfold, it’s worth remembering the broader implications of this episode: In Trump’s world, loyalty is demanded, but never guaranteed.
