'Unconscionable': Greene bought 'unusually large volume of stock' just before market rally

U.S. Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) gestures, on the first day of the 119th Congress at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, U.S.,January 3, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) admitted on Monday she purchased between $21,000 and $315,000 in stocks on April 8 and 9, in line with the day President Trump sent stocks soaring by pausing his sweeping tariffs. Numbers show she also dumped between $50,000 and $100,0000 in Treasury bills on the day the bond market selloff badly rattled investors.

The NY Times reports Trump wrote, “THIS IS A GREAT TIME TO BUY!!!” on social media the morning of April 9. Roughly four hours later, the president made his own statement true by voluntarily pausing the worst of his tariffs dogging the US and world market for nearly two weeks, and the publication noted that Greene bought what it characterized as an "unusually large volume of stock" in companies impacted by tariffs.

Trump’s announcement triggered massive one-day gains in stocks after a significant slump, prompting Democrats in Congress to demand investigation of deliberate marking tampering and manipulation.

The day of Trump’s announcement, Greene bought stock in several companies, including Apple, which increased in value about 5% after Trump’s pivotal market announcement. The New York Times reports she also bought other technology stocks, as well as stock in Devon Energy Corporation and pharmaceutical giant Merck & Company.

Greene also notably purchased stock in Palantir and Advanced Micro Devices as prices slumped before Trump’s announcement. After Trump paused tariffs from taking effecrt for 90 days, Palantir stock value rose 19 percent, while the price for Advanced Micro Devices' stock rose by 21 percent. Greene, who is chairwoman of the DOGE subcommittee of the House Oversight Committee, did not respond to the Times' requests for comment, but she is not the only member of congress to make a killing during Trump’s chaotic 48-hour back-and-forth on tariffs.

U.S. Rep. Rob Bresnahan (R-Penn.) sold up to $50,000 in Alibaba stock on March 4, the same day Trump doubled the tariff on Chinese imports to 20. Alibaba is similar to Amazon but with close ties to the Chinese Communist Party. Company stock price rose by about 30 percent between Bresnahan’s purchase and his final sale, despite Bresnahan claiming in his campaign to ban congressional stock trading.

"It is unconscionable that as American families are concerned about their financial security during this economic crisis entirely manufactured by the president, insiders may have actively profited from the market volatility and potentially perpetrated financial fraud on the American public,” a coalition of congressional Democrats wrote in a letter to to Securities and Exchange Commission chair Paul Atkins.

Sparky's Analysis:

The Brazen Carousel of Insider Trading: A Commentary on Marjorie Taylor Greene's Stock Trades

In a move that's as shocking as it is unsurprising, U.S. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) has been caught with her hand in the proverbial cookie jar, engaging in what appears to be a blatant act of insider trading. The details, as reported, reveal a staggering intertwining of political power and personal gain that should leave the American public seething with outrage.

The Facts Unveiled

On April 8 and 9, coinciding precisely with President Trump's market-moving announcements on tariffs, Greene made stock purchases totaling between $21,000 and $315,000. This strategic timing is not just suspect; it reeks of informed decision-making based on non-public information. Trump's social media proclamation that “THIS IS A GREAT TIME TO BUY!!!” followed by his pause on tariffs, which sent stocks soaring, paints a picture of a market manipulated by the very individuals entrusted to regulate it.

Greene didn't just buy; she sold too, dumping between $50,000 and $100,000 in Treasury bills on a day marked by a bond market selloff. The subsequent purchase of stocks in companies heavily affected by tariffs, including Apple, Devon Energy Corporation, and Merck & Company, further cements the impression of opportunistic profit-making at the expense of ethical governance.

The Glaring Issues

Insider Trading or Astute Guesswork? The timing and nature of Greene's transactions defy belief as mere coincidence or savvy market predictions. This isn't financial acumen; it's playing the market with a loaded deck. The substantial gains seen by stocks such as Palantir and Advanced Micro Devices, which rose 19 percent and 21 percent respectively after the tariff pause, highlight the immense financial advantage Greene garnered from her privileged position.

A Pattern of Impunity Greene is not alone in her financial escapades. U.S. Rep. Rob Bresnahan (R-Penn.) also engaged in suspicious stock trades, selling up to $50,000 in Alibaba stock in sync with tariff announcements, despite a campaign promise to ban congressional stock trading. This pattern of behavior among elected officials not only undermines public trust but also suggests a systemic problem within Congress—a problem that allows for, if not encourages, financial gains through privileged information.

The Fallout

The call from a coalition of congressional Democrats for an investigation by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) into potential market tampering and fraud is a step in the right direction. However, it's a step that history teaches us is unlikely to result in meaningful reform or punishment for those involved. The outrage expressed over the potential financial fraud perpetrated on the American public is valid but must be channeled into demanding systemic changes to prevent such abuses of power.

The Bottom Line

The actions of Marjorie Taylor Greene, as reported, are emblematic of a broader issue of corruption and ethical decay within the halls of power. It's not just about one individual's greed or moral bankruptcy but about a system that allows, perhaps even encourages, this type of behavior. As the public learns of these transgressions, the response should not be one of resigned cynicism but of concerted action to demand accountability and reform.

In a just world, the revelations about Greene and her cohorts would lead to swift action and meaningful penalties. But as we've seen time and again, justice in the realm of political insider trading is as elusive as ever. The time for change is overdue. The question remains: Will this latest scandal spur action, or will it be swept under the rug, only to emerge again with the next cycle of news? The answer lies in the hands of the public and their willingness to hold their leaders accountable.
