Hypocrisy in High Places: The Unsurprising Swampiness of Mike Brown’s Treasury Appointment

The Power Broker’s Reward

Mike Brown, a former Kansas Republican Party Chairman known for his combative and conspiratorial tenure, has landed a plush job as the director of the Bureau of Engraving and Printing at the U.S. Treasury. This position, which oversees the design and distribution of U.S. currency, comes with a tidy $200,000 annual paycheck. Brown’s appointment is a classic example of political patronage, repaying years of loyalty and fundraising for the Trump administration with a high-profile government role.

From Critic to Crony

Brown has spent considerable time criticizing what he calls the “Deep State,” positioning himself as an outsider intent on draining the swamp. Yet, his recent appointment places him deep within the bureaucratic apparatus he once claimed to oppose. This pivot from critic to government insider highlights a typical maneuver in political careers where rhetoric often contradicts action, especially under the auspices of patronage in the Trump era.

The Mechanisms of Misdirection

The original reporting by Dion Lefler in the Wichita Eagle adeptly points out the hypocrisy in Brown’s new role compared to his past public statements. However, it’s crucial to underscore not just the individual hypocrisy but the systemic nature of such appointments. Brown’s career trajectory from a GOP chair, known for his divisive and exclusionary tactics, to a significant bureaucratic position underlines how political loyalty often trumps public service or expertise in such roles.

Consequences of Patronage

Brown’s appointment is not just a personal victory but a symptom of a broader issue within American politics where critical administrative roles are filled based on loyalty rather than qualifications. This practice not only undermines the efficiency and integrity of public institutions but also erodes public trust in government operations. When figures like Brown, with histories of controversial and exclusionary politics, are placed in roles of significant bureaucratic power, it raises concerns about the motivations and operations of those institutions.

Broader Patterns and Political Insights

The story of Mike Brown is a microcosm of a larger political pattern where the spoils system overrides the meritocratic ideals that public institutions ideally represent. His transition from a party chair embroiled in internal conflicts and conspiracy theories to a federal position with substantial influence over national currency epitomizes the transactional nature of political appointments under the Trump administration. This practice not only contradicts the proclaimed aims of reducing government corruption and inefficiency but also perpetuates a cycle where political allegiance is the currency of power, rather than competence or integrity.

In Conclusion

Mike Brown’s appointment should be viewed not just as an individual instance of political payback but as a revealing insight into the transactional dynamics at play within the Trump administration. It exemplifies how political patronage continues to shape the operations of American governmental institutions, often at the expense of governance quality and public trust. Such appointments are less about draining the swamp and more about redistributing its waters to loyalists, a practice that is neither new nor exclusive to any single administration, but one that is starkly transparent in its current execution.
