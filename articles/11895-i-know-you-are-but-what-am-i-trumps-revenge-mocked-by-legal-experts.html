'I know you are, but what am I?' Trump’s revenge mocked by legal experts

Legal analysts are collectively rolling their eyes and responding with ridicule after President Donald Trump's administration assigned longtime ally Ed Martin to serve as a special counsel to investigate Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and New York Attorney General Letitia James.

During the 2024 campaign, Trump alleged that the DOJ was being "weaponized" to go after him after he stole classified documents and refused to return them, and he attempted to overturn the 2020 election. In this case, Trump's pledge for "retribution" and "revenge" has come to target those he believes are his foes.

The Atlantic's legal commentator Quinta Jurecic cited the CNN report on the matter, which says, "A grand jury investigation into James also convened in Albany, New York, according to a source familiar. The grand jury probe into James is said to be looking into deprivation of rights, which means violating someone's constitutional rights, against Trump."

"My instinct is that they're going with deprivation of rights under section 242 because Trump was prosecuted under section 241, and this administration operates according to the venerable legal principle of 'I know you are, but what am I,'" wrote Jurecic on Bluesky.

She went on to cite the New York Times report, which said, "It is a highly unusual use of civil rights law more typically used to investigate potential racial, religious or sex discrimination, among other categories."

As Jurecic characterized, "This is not really how the statute works."

Former Justice Department appointee Eric Columbus said he wondered "what’s the status of their mortgage fraud investigation against [James]. Unless I missed something, we haven’t heard about that in a while."

Jurecic replied that it was an example of "Spaghetti, wall, etc."

Constitutional scholar Robert Black commented, "I had this thought the other day when they were talking about going after Obama for 'election interference' or whatever, he latched onto that phrase as a Bad Thing after 2016 but he doesn't actually understand it or why it's bad."

National security analyst Marcy Wheeler pointed out that under Judge Aileen Cannon's ruling, appointing a special prosecutor to investigate James and Schiff "is patently illegal."

"All the better given that he was pointedly NOT confirmed," she added about Martin.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump’s Latest Legal Gambit

In a move that can only be described as a brazen affront to the principles of justice and a mockery of legal standards, President Donald Trump's administration has once again descended into the realm of political vendettas, this time by appointing Ed Martin—a long-standing ally with questionable qualifications—as a special counsel to investigate Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and New York Attorney General Letitia James. This act isn't just a mere overreach; it's a full-blown circus act designed to distract, distort, and ultimately undermine the very fabric of legal integrity.

The Dubious Foundations

The pretext for this investigation is as flimsy as it is transparently vindictive. Trump, in his 2024 campaign, cried foul over the Department of Justice being "weaponized" against him for allegations ranging from mishandling classified documents to attempting to subvert the 2020 election results. His solution? To weaponize the DOJ in return, against perceived adversaries, under the guise of seeking "retribution" and "revenge." It's a classic Trump playbook maneuver: accuse others of your own sins to muddle the waters.

A Legal Farce

What's particularly galling is the choice of Ed Martin for this charade. Martin's appointment as a special counsel, especially given his close ties to Trump, makes a mockery of the position. It's akin to appointing a fox to investigate who's been raiding the henhouse. The legal community's collective eye-roll is well-justified, as the move reeks of nepotism and cronyism, eroding trust in impartial justice.

The legal basis for the investigation into James and Schiff is equally ludicrous. The use of a grand jury to probe James for "deprivation of rights" under civil rights law is a gross misapplication of statutes typically reserved for cases of discrimination. As Quinta Jurecic insightfully points out, this is not how the statute works. It's a desperate clutch at straws, attempting to mirror the charges against Trump with a retaliatory strike that holds little water in the sea of legal legitimacy.

The Expert Take: A Unanimous Verdict

Legal commentators and scholars have been quick to denounce this travesty. From Quinta Jurecic's biting analysis of the administration's "I know you are, but what am I" strategy, to Eric Columbus's wry observation about the conveniently forgotten mortgage fraud investigation against James, the consensus is clear: this is nothing more than political theater, designed to obfuscate and retaliate, not to uphold the law.

Moreover, Robert Black's reflections on Trump's misuse of legal terms and Marcy Wheeler's outright declaration of the move as "patently illegal" under Judge Aileen Cannon's ruling only add layers to the farce. The cherry on top? Martin wasn't even confirmed, spotlighting the administration's disregard for legal norms and procedures.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

Trump's latest escapade is not just a systemic error; it's a calculated attack on the judiciary's independence and the rule of law. By appointing a loyalist to conduct a baseless investigation into his political adversaries, Trump has shown a flagrant disregard for legal ethics and democratic principles. This isn't just about Trump's vendetta against Schiff and James; it's a glaring example of how far he's willing to go to undermine justice for personal gain.

In the end, this isn't a strategy destined for legal success—it's a tactic aimed at sowing discord, distracting from his own legal troubles, and rallying his base. The real casualty here is the public's faith in an impartial and fair legal system, a loss that will have ramifications far beyond the immediate political cycle. It's a dangerous game, and one that we, as a society, cannot afford to ignore.
