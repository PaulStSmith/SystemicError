Brett Ratner, the director of “Melania,” the new documentary about First Lady Melania Trump, was spotted in the Justice Department’s Friday release of thousands of files on Jeffrey Epstein, Vanity Fair reported late Friday.

Among the thousands of files released by the DOJ Friday, internet sleuths were quick to identify Ratner in an undated photograph with a shirtless man that appears to be Jean-Luc Brunel, a French model scout and owner of the international modeling agency MC2 Model Management, which has considerable financial backing from Epstein.

Brunel died by apparent suicide in 2022 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, as did Epstein in 2019.

Ratner made his directorial debut in the 1990s with the release of “Money Talks,” and went on to direct the popular “Rush Hour” films before being ousted from Hollywood in 2018 amid sexual misconduct allegations. While he moved to Israel in 2023, Ratner is now set to direct “Rush Hour 4” after the project was greenlit by Paramount, and after President Donald Trump “lobbied” for the studio to take up Ratner’s project.

Ratner was also selected to direct “Melania,” set for release in select-theaters on Jan. 30, 2026. The film was announced in January of 2025, an indication that Ratner was likely still held in high regard among the Trumps, further supported by Donald Trump’s push for Paramount to fund “Rush Hour 4” with Ratner at the helm.

Warner Bros. cut ties with Ratner in 2018 and decided not to renew a $450 million financing deal after several women accused him of sexual misconduct, including one woman whoallegedRatner had “forced her to perform oral sex.”

Our Analysis:

Brett Ratner’s Dubious Resurgence: A Deep Dive into Hollywood’s Morally Bankrupt Redemption

In a recent revelation that should surprise absolutely no one familiar with the incestuous relationship between power, privilege, and the entertainment industry, Brett Ratner, a director marred by sexual misconduct allegations, has been spotted in the Justice Department’s latest file dump related to Jeffrey Epstein. This discovery was made amidst thousands of files, with Ratner seen in a photograph alongside a shirtless man believed to be Jean-Luc Brunel, a name synonymous with sex-trafficking charges, as reported by Vanity Fair.

The Unholy Alliance: Ratner, Epstein, and Brunel

Ratner’s association with Brunel—a man who, before his apparent suicide in 2022, was awaiting trial for sex-trafficking charges, mirroring the fate of his financial backer, Epstein, in 2019—casts a long, dark shadow over Ratner’s already controversial career. This connection is not a mere footnote; it’s a glaring spotlight on the systemic rot within the circles of power where men like Ratner and Epstein thrive.

Hollywood’s Selective Amnesia

Despite being ousted from Hollywood in 2018 amid sexual misconduct allegations, including a harrowing account where a woman accused Ratner of forcing her to perform oral sex, Ratner’s career is far from over. In a move that reeks of hypocrisy and a blatant disregard for the victims of sexual misconduct, Ratner has been welcomed back into the fold, with open arms, by none other than Paramount. The studio has greenlit Rush Hour 4, a project that reportedly received a push from former President Donald Trump.

Trump’s Influence: A Red Flag Ignored

The involvement of Donald Trump in lobbying for Ratner’s return is particularly troubling, yet unsurprising. Trump’s support for Ratner not only highlights the former president’s disregard for the serious allegations against the director but also underscores the deeply entrenched culture of enabling and protecting powerful men. The announcement of Ratner directing “Melania,” a documentary about Trump’s wife, scheduled for release in January of 2026, is a testament to the unbreakable bond between Trump and Ratner, forged in the fires of mutual benefit over moral integrity.

The Price of Redemption in Hollywood

Warner Bros.’ decision in 2018 to cut ties with Ratner and not renew a $450 million financing deal seemed, at the time, to be a step in the right direction. However, Ratner’s resurgence, with significant projects in the pipeline, illustrates a grim reality: in Hollywood, redemption is not about accountability or reparations to those harmed; it’s about who you know, how much power you wield, and how quickly the public forgets.

The Reckoning That Never Came

As Ratner prepares to step back into the limelight, one must ponder the message Hollywood is sending. By allowing men like Ratner to return to positions of power and influence, the industry is effectively saying that allegations of sexual misconduct, no matter how severe, can be overlooked if you’re valuable enough. This not only diminishes the experiences of the survivors but also emboldens potential predators, perpetuating a cycle of abuse and silence.

In the end, the case of Brett Ratner is not an anomaly but a symptom of a much larger, systemic problem—a culture that prioritizes profit and prestige over the safety and dignity of individuals, especially women. As long as this culture persists, Hollywood’s so-called reckonings will remain nothing more than performative gestures, empty of any real substance or change.
