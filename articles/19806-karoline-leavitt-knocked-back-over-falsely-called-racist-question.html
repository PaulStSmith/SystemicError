“Despite the fact that I am falsely and consistently called a Racist by the Scoundrels and Lunatics on the Radical Left, Democrats ALL, it was always my pleasure to help Jesse along the way,” he wrote.

On Wednesday, a reporter asked, “Where or when does the president believe he’s been falsely called racist?”

Leavitt replied, “You’re kidding, right?”

“I will pull you plethora of examples,” she said, vowing to get her team “going through the internet of radical Democrats throughout the years ... who have accused this president falsely of being a racist, and I’m sure there’s many people in this room and on network television, across the country, who have accused him of the same.”

“In fact, I know that because I’ve seen it with my own eyes,” she said, before noting that Trump is hosting a Black History Month celebration later on Wednesday.

Trump, she said, will “talk about how his policies are advancing opportunity and prosperity for all Americans through record tax cuts, through the Trump accounts that all Americans can access regardless of race.”

“These are a great thing,” she continued, before noting that the president “has also awarded hundreds of millions of dollars in additional funding to strengthen educational outcomes at historically Black colleges and universities, across the country.”

She also said that Trump is “protecting the hard-earned benefits of the 2.4 million Black veterans who honorably served in our nation’s armed forces by reducing the Black backlog of veterans waiting for their VA benefits, and for their home loans through the Department of Veterans Affairs.”

“So, there is a lot this president has done for all Americans, regardless of race, and he has, absolutely, been falsely called and smeared as a racist, and I’m happy to provide you those receipts,” she added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by Karoline Leavitt

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the hallowed halls of the White House, Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt delivered what can only be described as a masterclass in deflection, obfuscation, and the fine art of not answering the question. When pressed for examples of President Donald Trump being falsely accused of racism, Leavitt’s response was nothing short of a performance worthy of an award for Best Dramatic Pause in a Political Theater.

“You’re kidding, right?” This was Leavitt’s initial comeback, a line so dripping with incredulity that one might momentarily forget the seriousness of the inquiry. It’s a classic tactic—feign surprise, act as though the question is beneath you, and buy time. But let’s not be fooled; this is not about the shock of the question but about the audacity of accountability.

Leavitt vows to pull a “plethora of examples” from the digital abyss to vindicate Trump from the accusations of racism. Yet, the promise feels hollow, a verbal sleight of hand meant to dazzle with the promise of evidence without the immediate burden of producing any. She speaks of going through “the internet of radical Democrats,” painting a picture of a vast conspiracy so vast and intricate that only those with the most ardent dedication to Trump could navigate it.

The press secretary then swiftly pivots to listing Trump’s policies, claiming they advance “opportunity and prosperity for all Americans.” She cites record tax cuts, the creation of “Trump accounts,” additional funding for historically Black colleges and universities, and efforts to reduce the backlog of veterans waiting for VA benefits. These points are strategically deployed to build a narrative of Trump as a champion of racial equality, conveniently ignoring any policies, statements, or actions that might paint a different picture.

The Deflection Dance

What we witness here is the Deflection Dance, a carefully choreographed routine designed to shift focus from the troubling question of racism to a highlight reel of supposed achievements. It’s a tactic as transparent as it is tired, yet it continues to be employed with unyielding enthusiasm.

Leavitt’s confidence in providing “receipts” for Trump’s innocence in the face of racism accusations is bold, yet as of the moment she spoke, unsubstantiated. The promise of evidence remains just that—a promise, hanging in the air like a note in a song yet to be played.

The Bottom Line

Karoline Leavitt’s performance is a stark reminder of the lengths to which the current administration will go to rewrite narratives and deflect criticism. The question of racism is not one to be brushed aside with feigned shock or buried under a list of policy points. It demands a direct answer, a level of accountability that the American public deserves but, based on this exchange, is unlikely to receive.

In the end, the spectacle at the White House was less about proving Trump’s innocence and more about showcasing a tried and true strategy of evasion. It’s a reminder that in the political arena, the dance of deflection is often more about preserving image than confronting uncomfortable truths. And in this instance, Leavitt proved to be a lead dancer in an administration that has long mastered the choreography of avoidance.
