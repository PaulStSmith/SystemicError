Shockingly close race now has Trump and GOP on edge: 'This Republican is a little nervous'

Republicans are admittedly “nervous” about Tuesday’s special election in Tennessee’s seventh congressional district, and that’s reason enough to put them on edge about next year’s midterms.

President Donald Trump won that district by more than 20 percentage points just over a year ago, but polling shows Republican Matt Van Epps with a single-digit lead over Democrat Aftyn Behn, who has centered her campaign around affordability as Republicans bash her as too far left and called her “the AOC of Tennessee,” reported the Washington Post.

“It’s interesting that Van Epps isn’t in a strong enough position just to ignore her,” said Vanderbilt University political scientist John G. Geer. “That tells me they’re worried.”

The district stretches from the Alabama border in the south to the Kentucky border in the north and was redistricted in 2022, when state Republicans dispersed voters from a Democratic district centered on Nashville into three nearby GOP districts, and Behn has worked to activate Black voters in parts of the city added to the seventh district.

“I’m not a moderate white guy, but I am an organizer and I’m a Tennessean,” Behn told the Post, saying she hoped to “rewrite the Democratic playbook in the South.”

Trump and his allies have poured considerable resources into the campaign to replace Republican Rep. Mark Green — who also won by more than 20 points last year but resigned from Congress in July – and while most would be surprised if Behn won, the fact that the race is close indicates the GOP’s narrow 219-213 House majority is fragile.

“Remember, the world is watching this one,” Trump said, acknowledging the result is an early referendum on his second term. “I need somebody like Matt Van Epps. He is going to be one of our best congressmen.”

Republicans have been scrambling in recent weeks, the Post reported, to salvage down a race they believed had been locked down with Trump’s endorsement of Van Epps, but the president’s approval rating has dipped deeply into negative territory over the course of this year.

“It’s fair to say this Republican is a little nervous,” said Rep. Tim Burchett (R-TN). “It’s an off-year. It’s a special election. It’s around the holiday, and there’s just a lot of things that could play into the Democrats’ favor.”

Our Analysis:

The Underdog Battle in Tennessee: A Sign of GOP Jitters?

In the heart of Tennessee’s seventh congressional district, a political tremor is sending shockwaves through the Republican ranks, hinting at a seismic shift that could forecast trouble for the GOP in the looming midterms. Let’s dissect the unfolding drama where Republican Matt Van Epps’s unexpectedly precarious lead over Democrat Aftyn Behn has set the stage for a nail-biting spectacle.

Trump’s Bastion Wavers

The district, which President Donald Trump clinched by a staggering 20 percentage points just a year ago, is now the battleground for a contest that’s too close for the GOP’s comfort. The fact that Van Epps is hanging by a single-digit lead is a glaring red flag for Republicans. It’s not just a battle for a seat; it’s a litmus test for the GOP’s stronghold and Trump’s lingering influence.

Behn: “The AOC of Tennessee”?

The GOP’s strategy to paint Aftyn Behn as “too far left” and dub her “the AOC of Tennessee” is a playbook move, but it’s one that reeks of desperation. Behn’s focus on affordability and her efforts to galvanize Black voters in newly added parts of the district are not radical; they’re resonating. Her ambition to “rewrite the Democratic playbook in the South” is not just bold—it’s a direct challenge to the GOP’s narrative and a testament to the shifting sands of Tennessee politics.

Redistricting and Racial Politics

The GOP’s redistricting gambit in 2022, designed to dilute Democratic influence by dispersing voters from a Nashville-centered district, has backfired spectacularly. Behn’s strategic mobilization of Black voters in these areas is not just smart politics; it’s a powerful countermove against the GOP’s attempts to engineer a safer electoral landscape for themselves.

Trump’s Fading Midas Touch?

The considerable resources Trump and his allies have poured into Van Epps’s campaign signal more than just support; they betray a deep-seated anxiety about the former president’s waning influence. Trump’s acknowledgment of the election as a referendum on his second term is particularly telling. It’s an admission of the stakes involved, wrapped in the bravado typical of Trump’s endorsements.

GOP Nerves and Election Dynamics

The GOP’s scramble to secure what seemed like a sure win is emblematic of a broader pattern of anxiety within the party. Representative Tim Burchett’s candid admission of nervousness speaks volumes about the uncertainties clouding the Republican outlook. The special election’s timing—during an off-year and around the holiday season—adds layers of unpredictability to an already tense race.

Conclusion: The Fragility of GOP Dominance

The tight race in Tennessee’s seventh district is more than just a local election; it’s a microcosm of the GOP’s fragile grip on power. The Republicans’ nervousness is a tacit acknowledgment of the shifting political tides and the potential for upheaval in the midterms. As the world watches, the outcome of this special election may well prefigure the challenges lying in wait for the GOP in the broader electoral battleground.

In essence, the Republicans’ struggle to maintain a once-assured seat lays bare the vulnerabilities and internal tremors threatening the party’s unity and future prospects. The special election in Tennessee is not just a test for Matt Van Epps or a referendum on Trump—it’s a harrowing glimpse into the GOP’s anxious soul as it navigates the choppy waters of American politics.
