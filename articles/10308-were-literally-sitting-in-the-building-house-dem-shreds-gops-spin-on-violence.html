‘We’re literally sitting in the building’: House Dem shreds GOP’s spin on violence

FILE PHOTO: Charlie Kirk, Turning Point USA founder, puts on a MAGA hat during the AmericaFest 2024 conference sponsored by conservative group Turning Point in Phoenix, Arizona, U.S. December 19, 2024. REUTERS/Cheney Orr/File Photo

A House Democratic lawmaker blasted the right’s narrative that extremist violence comes largely — or exclusively — from the left, a claim contradicted by evidence, including a Justice Department study recently scrubbed from its website. The motive and political ideology of the shooter remain largely unknown.

President Donald Trump has been leading the charge on the right in suggesting that left-wing violence is prevalent.

“For years,” Trump said in an address from the Oval Office the night of Charlie Kirk’s killing, “those on the radical left have compared wonderful Americans, like Charlie, to Nazis and the world’s worst mass murderers and criminals. This kind of rhetoric is directly responsible for the terrorism that we’re seeing in our country today, and it must stop right now.”

White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy Stephen Miller on Monday declared there is “a vast domestic terror movement,” CNBC reported, noting that he was specifically “speaking of left-wing political organizations.”

“With God as my witness, we are going to use every resource we have at the Department of Justice, Homeland Security and throughout this government to identify, disrupt, dismantle and destroy these networks and make America safe again for the American people,” Miller also said. “It will happen, and we will do it in Charlie’s name.”

Attorney General Pam Bondi on Monday declared, “Yes, who killed Charlie [Kirk]? Left-wing radicals. And they will be held accountable.”

U.S. Rep. Jared Moskowitz on Wednesday tore into House Republicans.

“I want to caution my colleagues on, you know, one-sided violence in this country,” the Florida Democrat began.

“We just heard that there were no riots on the right. We’re literally sitting in the building where there was a giant riot by supporters of the president, the right,” he said, referring to the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the U.S. Capitol, which many have deemed an insurrection.

“We also heard that only leftists have said that Trump is a ‘Nazi.’ In 2016, the current Vice President of the United States said, ‘Trump is either a cynical a– or he’s America’s Hitler’.”

READ MORE: Navarro Demands Left Face ‘Accountability’ for MAGA’s Own Misdeeds—and Kirk Assassination

Moskowitz lamented having to “say this garbage. But you want me to sit here and get painted with one brush—the whole left. No, no, there are extremes on the left, and there are extremes on the right. And we collectively should tackle that.”

“But to come here and just look at us and say, ‘we all support what’s going on in the extremes,’ is why we can’t solve anything in this building.”

On Wednesday, NBC News reported that Miller’s “suggestion that a secret network of violent left-wing extremists was behind the killing” of Charlie Kirk, “stands in contrast to the evidence that law enforcement officials presented on Tuesday in Utah, where Kirk was fatally shot. There was no indication presented Tuesday that the suspect, Tyler Robinson, was a member of a group or that he fell under the sway of a particular leader. The investigation is ongoing.”

On Monday, as The New York Times reported, President Trump, “who has downplayed violence from right-wing or other supporters, said that he would like to designate a range of groups, including the loosely affiliated group of far-left anti-fascism activists, known as ‘antifa,’ as domestic terrorists and bring racketeering cases against people funding protests.”

The Times added that “other officials, from Vice President JD Vance on down, made it clear on Monday that they believed that political violence was a liberal problem and not a conservative one,” and announced “that they would be cracking down on what they called leftist nongovernmental organizations, and that they would use every available lever of the federal government to do so.”

Our Analysis:

The Twisted Narrative of Violent Extremism: A Critical Analysis

In an era where the truth is more elusive than ever, the recent comments from various officials and lawmakers epitomize the ongoing distortion of reality concerning violent extremism in the United States. The article under scrutiny reveals a deeply troubling attempt by certain political figures to skew the narrative towards an unfounded theory that extremist violence is predominantly a left-wing issue. This claim is not only dangerously inaccurate but also a stark diversion from the evidence at hand.

The Disregard for Facts

It's both ironic and alarming that despite a Justice Department study—which was inconveniently scrubbed from its website—highlighting the contrary, figures like President Donald Trump and White House Deputy Chief of Staff for Policy Stephen Miller continue to propagate the myth of left-wing violence being the most significant threat to national security. This narrative is not just misleading; it's a calculated manipulation of public perception. Trump's assertion that rhetoric comparing certain Americans to Nazis is fueling terrorism is a blatant attempt to shift blame and distract from the more pressing issue of right-wing extremism.

The Misuse of Charlie Kirk's Tragedy

The assassination of Charlie Kirk has been weaponized by officials like Attorney General Pam Bondi and Stephen Miller to further this distorted narrative. Bondi's premature accusation of left-wing radicals being behind Kirk's killing, without concrete evidence, is not just irresponsible; it's an affront to the principles of justice and due process. This rhetoric, under the guise of making "America safe again," dangerously oversimplifies the complex nature of political violence and extremism.

The Reality of Right-Wing Extremism

U.S. Rep. Jared Moskowitz's criticism of the one-sided portrayal of violence is a much-needed voice of reason in this skewed discourse. The reference to the January 6, 2021, attack on the U.S. Capitol is a stark reminder of the real and present danger of right-wing extremism. This event, which can be aptly described as an insurrection, lays bare the hypocrisy of claims that the left is the primary source of political violence. It's telling that those who are quick to label antifa and other left-leaning groups as terrorists remain conspicuously silent on right-wing violence.

The Ongoing Investigation into Kirk's Death

Further undermining the baseless claims of a left-wing conspiracy behind Kirk's assassination, NBC News reported that there was no evidence linking the suspect to any left-wing groups or ideology. This revelation not only calls into question the integrity of those who were quick to point fingers but also highlights the danger of politicizing criminal investigations without waiting for the facts.

The Broader Implications

The attempt to designate antifa and similar groups as domestic terrorists, as reported by The New York Times, is a troubling indication of the lengths to which certain officials will go to vilify their ideological opponents. This move is not about ensuring public safety; it's about leveraging federal power to suppress dissent. The focus on leftist nongovernmental organizations, while ignoring the clear and present danger posed by right-wing extremism, is a clear sign of a political agenda superseding the welfare of the nation.

Conclusion

The narrative that extremist violence in the U.S. is largely or exclusively a product of the left is not just a fallacy; it's a dangerous diversion from the truth. The manipulation of tragedies and the selective outrage over political violence serve only to deepen the divisions within the country. It's imperative that the discourse surrounding this issue is grounded in fact, not political convenience. As the investigation into Charlie Kirk's death continues, one can only hope for a return to a fact-based approach to addressing the real threats to our nation's security, rather than succumbing to the convenience of scapegoating and misinformation.
