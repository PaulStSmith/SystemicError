'Treasonous': Naomi Klein reveals how billionaires are 'provoking' societal collapse

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Sparky's Analysis:The Return of Corporate Feudalism: A Scathing Analysis

Author Naomi Klein on Sky News on June 11, 2024 (Image: Screengrab via Sky News / YouTube)

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor tell The Guardian that corporate city-states are back in fashion thanks to Trump politics indulging tax-hating millionaires and billionaires to start their own high-tech fiefdoms.

A “glorified gated communit[ies]” like Próspera on a Honduran island and Elon Musk’s distant proposal of “a handful of people and robots eke[ing] out survival on two barren orbs (a radically depleted Earth and a terraformed Mars) appear to be catching on with ultra-wealthy members of the Trump circle.

End-time communities were on the rise in 2023, when a campaigning Donald Trump promised to hold a contest that would lead to the creation of 10 “freedom cities” on federal lands. And since election, “would-be country starters have been on a lobbying blitz, determined to turn Trump’s pledge into reality.” Próspera Chief of Staff Trey Goff called the new energy in DC “absolutely electric, and legislation “paving the way for a bevy of corporate city-states” complete by the end of the year, Klein writes.

It’s a fantasy word evolved from the “doom-prepping” movement of the first Trump administration, only now fueled by ultra-wealthy “religious end-timers.” It also hastens the degradation of the only world humanity realistically has, Klein and Taylor say.

“Indeed, in a strange twist on the Old Testament tale, Musk and his fellow tech billionaires, having arrogated god-like powers to themselves, aren’t content to just build the arks. They appear to be doing their best to cause the flood. Today’s right-wing leaders and their rich allies are not just taking advantage of catastrophes, shock-doctrine and disaster-capitalism style, but simultaneously provoking and planning for them,” says Klein, a Canadian author.

There is no second planet to occupy, however, and reducing humanity to a tiny dome on a distant island or mountain peak requires a commitment to letting a huge swathe of humanity die. So, how do you break the apocalyptic fever?

“… We help each other face the depth of the depravity that has gripped the hard right in all of our countries,” Klein writes. “We must first understand … [that] we are up against an ideology that has given up not only on the premise and promise of liberal democracy but on the livability of our shared world – on its beauty, on its people, on our children, on other species. The forces we are up against have made peace with mass death. They are treasonous to this world and its human and non-human inhabitants.”

Second, Klein advises countering “their apocalyptic narratives with a far better story about how to survive the hard times ahead without leaving anyone behind. A story capable of draining end times fascism of its gothic power and galvanizing a movement ready to put it all on the line for our collective survival. A story not of end times, but of better times; not of separation and supremacy, but of interdependence and belonging; not of escaping, but staying put and staying faithful to the troubled earthly reality in which we are enmeshed and bound.”

Sparky's Analysis:

The Return of Corporate Feudalism: A Scathing Analysis

In a revelatory piece by Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor for The Guardian, the dystopian revival of corporate city-states is dissected with a critical eye, revealing a disturbing trend among the ultra-wealthy, particularly those orbiting the sphere of Trumpian politics. This movement, characterized by its disdain for taxes and an unquenchable thirst for autonomy, is pushing us toward a new age of high-tech feudalism, with projects like Próspera on a Honduran island and Elon Musk's fantastical visions of a colonized Mars leading the charge.

The Allure of Gated Apocalypse

The concept, as described, is nothing short of a glorified gated community—or, to put it more bluntly, a retreat for the mega-rich to escape the consequences of their actions on the planet. These end-time communities, gaining traction amidst the backdrop of Trump's 2023 campaign promises of creating 10 "freedom cities," signify not just a withdrawal from society, but a reckless gamble with humanity's future.

Trey Goff's comment about the "absolutely electric" energy in DC surrounding these endeavors, alongside legislation aimed at birthing these corporate city-states, is a chilling reminder of the power wielded by those with wealth to burn. Yet, what's more alarming is the underlying ideology fueling this movement—a cocktail of doom-prepping and disaster-capitalism, with a twist of religious end-timer fervor.

A Planet in Peril

Klein's assertion that these tech billionaires, having assumed "god-like powers," are not content with merely building arks but are seemingly intent on causing the flood themselves, hits the nail on the head. It's a stark illustration of how today's right-wing leaders and their affluent allies are not just exploiting catastrophes but are actively engineering them.

The stark reality is that there is no "Planet B." The pursuit of establishing human colonies on desolate islands or distant planets isn't just a flight of fancy; it's a death sentence for the vast majority of humanity and a betrayal of our collective home.

Breaking the Fever

How do we combat this apocalyptic ideology? Klein suggests a two-pronged approach. First, by acknowledging the depth of depravity that has ensnared the right, recognizing that we're up against forces content with mass death and betrayal of our world and its inhabitants. This is not merely political opposition; it's an ideological war against the very premise of a livable, shared world.

Second, Klein advocates for countering their nihilistic narratives with a vision of hope—a story of survival that doesn't leave anyone behind. This isn't about espousing utopian fantasies but about fostering a movement grounded in interdependence, belonging, and a steadfast commitment to our earthly reality.

In Conclusion

The Guardian piece serves as a grim reminder of the lengths to which the ultra-wealthy will go to insulate themselves from the consequences of their actions. In their pursuit of autonomy and survival, they're willing to forsake the planet and the bulk of its inhabitants. This is not just a failure of empathy but a systemic error in our global priorities.

As we face these daunting challenges, the need for collective action and a recommitment to our shared humanity has never been more urgent. It's time to reject the lure of end times fascism and craft a narrative not of separation and supremacy, but of unity, resilience, and hope. The future of our planet—and of humanity itself—depends on it.
