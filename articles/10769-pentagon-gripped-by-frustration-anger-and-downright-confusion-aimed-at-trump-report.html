Pentagon gripped by 'frustration, anger and downright confusion' aimed at Trump: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Outside of Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, there is little excitement within the Pentagon and among former defense officials to see the Department of Defense (DOD) renamed as the Department of War (DOW) with worries about cost, confusion and also how other nations will use the change for propaganda purposes.

According to a report from Politico, the long-anticipated rebranding landed with a thud on Friday as the president and the controversial Hegseth discussed it in an Oval Office press availability.

Trump told reporters, “We won World War I, we won World War II, we won everything before that and in between, and then we decided to go woke, and we changed the name to DOD. So, we’re going Department of War.”

That was greeted with “frustration, anger and downright confusion at the effort, which could cost billions of dollars for a cosmetic change that would do little to tackle the military’s most pressing challenges — such as countering a more aggressive alliance of authoritarian nations,” wrote Politico’s Jack Detsch, Paul McLeary and Joe Gould.

One former defense official scoffed, “This is purely for domestic political audiences. Not only will this cost millions of dollars, it will have absolutely zero impact on Chinese or Russian calculations. Worse, it will be used by our enemies to portray the United States as warmongering and a threat to international stability.”

“I see there being a million small headaches and annoyances if this actually happens. It’ll eat up time and effort,” a current Pentagon official added.

Going on the record, former Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell (R-KY) cautioned on X, “If we call it the Dept. of War, we’d better equip the military to actually prevent and win wars. Can’t preserve American primacy if we’re unwilling to spend substantially more on our military than Carter or Biden. ‘Peace through strength’ requires investment, not just rebranding.”

Politico is reporting, “The seemingly ad hoc rollout of the name change has caused confusion within the building. One Pentagon official, who independently decided to squat on the Department of War LinkedIn page to prevent a foreign adversary or Trump administration critic from taking it over, openly asked on the social network to whom he should hand the page.”

Another official pointed out that the Pentagon, during Trump’s second term, has already been dragged into making major changes at Hegseth’s request, when they were forced to scrub any mention of DEI initiatives, explaining, "That was just taking down photos. The seal will have to change and thus anything with it.”

The Department of War changes would be significantly more labor-intensive.“On a tactical level, it would mean having to rebrand a mountain of contracting, marketing, business development materials, you name it, both digital and otherwise, that specifically cite the Department of Defense or DOD,” a defense analyst warned.

Our Analysis:

The Ill-Conceived Rebranding of the Department of Defense

In a move that can only be described as both perplexing and recklessly nostalgic, the Trump administration, with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth by its side, has proposed to change the Department of Defense (DOD) back to its archaic predecessor, the Department of War (DOW). This proposal, as reported by Politico, has landed not with the triumphant blast of trumpets it perhaps expected, but with the dull thud of widespread disapproval and concern.

Trump's Justification: A Misguided Nostalgia

Trump's rationale for this change is rooted in a misguided nostalgia for a bygone era of perceived unambiguous military triumph. “We won World War I, we won World War II...and then we decided to go woke, and we changed the name to DOD. So, we’re going Department of War,” Trump declared. This statement, apart from its glaring oversimplification of history, underscores a disturbing inclination to glorify war. It conflates past military successes with a name that evokes an era when war was more overtly valorized and less understood in terms of its profound human, economic, and geopolitical costs.

The Real Costs: Beyond the Financial

Politico's report outlines the multifaceted concerns that this rebranding raises, from the practical to the philosophical. The financial cost alone, potentially running into billions, is a staggering waste, considering the myriad pressing challenges facing the military, such as countering the rising alliance of authoritarian nations. However, the implications extend far beyond mere dollars.

A former defense official rightly critiqued the move as catering to "domestic political audiences," with no tangible impact on the strategic calculations of adversaries like China or Russia. Worse yet, it provides ammunition for these nations to paint the U.S. as a warmongering state, thereby destabilizing international peace efforts. The concern is not just about image; it's about the dangerous misallocation of priorities and resources.

Internal Confusion and Opposition

The proposal has evidently sown confusion and frustration within the Pentagon itself. From officials squatting on the Department of War LinkedIn page to prevent misuse, to concerns about the monumental task of rebranding a vast array of materials, the internal opposition is palpable. This opposition is not merely a matter of bureaucratic resistance to change but a profound disagreement with the proposal's underlying philosophy and practical implications.

Mitch McConnell's Cautionary Note

Even former Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell, hardly a critic of military spending, has expressed caution. His statement, "‘Peace through strength’ requires investment, not just rebranding,” underscores a fundamental truth. The strength of the U.S. military—and, by extension, the nation's security—cannot be enhanced by superficial changes. It requires thoughtful investment in capabilities and strategies that address the present realities of global conflict.

Conclusion: A Misguided Venture

The Trump administration's proposal to rename the DOD to the Department of War is a misguided venture that demonstrates a profound misunderstanding of both history and the current geopolitical landscape. It is a move that prioritizes political posturing over the substantive advancement of national security and international stability. As the critique within the Pentagon underscores, this is not merely a cosmetic change but a potentially harmful shift in how the United States projects its military ethos and engages with the world.

In an era where the nuances of power, diplomacy, and defense are increasingly complex, reverting to an archaic nomenclature with its inherent glorification of war is not just retrograde; it's dangerous. The focus should be on strengthening the military through strategic investments and fostering international peace, not on rebranding exercises that distract from the real issues and potentially exacerbate them.
