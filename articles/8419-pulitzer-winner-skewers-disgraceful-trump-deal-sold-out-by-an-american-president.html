Pulitzer winner skewers 'disgraceful' Trump deal: 'Sold out by an American president'

Donald Trump’s plan for Ukraine is a “disgrace,” according to a foreign affairs expert who has won three Pulitzer Prizes.

Thomas L. Friedman, the foreign affairs Opinion columnist for New York Times, skewered the president for his effort to achieve the Nobel Peace Prize.

“Finally, finally, President Trump just might get a peace prize that would secure his place in history. Unfortunately, though, it is not that Nobel peace prize he so covets. It is the ‘Neville Chamberlain Peace Prize’ — awarded by history to the leader of the country that most flagrantly sells out its allies and its values to an aggressive dictator,” he wrote. “This prize richly deserves to be shared by Trump’s many ‘secretaries of state’ — Steve Witkoff, Marco Rubio and Dan Driscoll — who together negotiated the surrender of Ukraine to Vladimir Putin’s demands without consulting Ukraine or our European allies in advance — and then told Ukraine it had to accept the plan by Thanksgiving.” He added, “That is this coming Thursday.”

Friedman then says the deal threatens the holiday itself.

“If Ukraine is, indeed, forced to surrender to the specific terms of this ‘deal’ by then, Thanksgiving will no longer be an American holiday. It will become a Russian holiday,” he wrote. “It will become a day of thanks that victory in Putin’s savage and misbegotten war against Ukraine’s people, which has been an utter failure — morally, militarily, diplomatically and economically — was delivered to Russia not by the superiority of its arms or the virtue of its claims, but by an American administration.”

The expert went on to say that, “By rewarding Putin’s unprovoked invasion of Ukraine based on his obsession with making it part of Mother Russia, the U.S. will be putting the whole European Union under Putin’s thumb.”

“Trump’s message to our allies will be clear: Don’t provoke Putin, because as long as I am commander in chief, the United States will pay no price and we will bear no burden in the defense of your freedom. Which is why, if this plan is forced on Ukraine as is, we will need to add a new verb to the diplomatic lexicon: ‘Trumped’ — to be sold out by an American president, for reasons none of his citizens understand (but surely there are reasons),” he added. “And history will never forget the men who did it — Donald Trump, Steve Witkoff, Marco Rubio, Dan Driscoll — for their shame will be everlasting.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Ukraine Plan: A Disgrace Unveiled

In an era where the absurd has become the norm, the latest development in Donald Trump’s foreign policy playbook marks not just a deviation but a catastrophic nosedive into the realm of the disgraceful. Thomas L. Friedman, a towering figure in foreign affairs commentary with three Pulitzer Prizes under his belt, has delivered a scathing critique of Trump’s Ukraine strategy, likening it to the historical blunder of Neville Chamberlain’s appeasement policy. This comparison is not made lightly; it is a dire warning from the past, screaming into the present.

The “Neville Chamberlain Peace Prize” that Friedman sarcastically awards Trump is a stark symbol of failure. It is presented to a leader who, in a bid for a legacy, betrays not just allies but the very essence of democratic values, by capitulating to an authoritarian aggressor. Trump’s quest for a Nobel Peace Prize, as Friedman highlights, has transformed into a grotesque irony where the prize sought is for peace, but the means achieve nothing but surrender and moral defeat.

The Surrender of Ukraine: Trump, alongside his unofficial ‘secretaries of state’ – Steve Witkoff, Marco Rubio, and Dan Driscoll – stands accused of negotiating Ukraine’s fate without the courtesy of involving either Ukraine or the European allies. This unilateral decision-making process reeks of arrogance and a dangerous ignorance of international diplomacy. Friedman’s revelation that Ukraine was given a deadline by Thanksgiving to accept this humiliating ‘deal’ adds insult to injury, turning a day of gratitude into a potential monument of shame.

Thanksgiving or Thanks-taking? The audacity of the Trump administration to potentially transform Thanksgiving from an American to a Russian holiday – metaphorically speaking – is both appalling and revealing. It speaks volumes about the administration’s disregard for the sacrifices of Ukraine in resisting Putin’s imperial ambitions. Friedman’s assertion that victory would be handed to Russia “not by the superiority of its arms or the virtue of its claims, but by an American administration” is a chilling indictment of Trump’s foreign policy.

Under Putin’s Thumb: The broader implications of Trump’s plan, as Friedman argues, extend beyond Ukraine to the entire European Union. By rewarding Putin’s unprovoked invasion, Trump signals a willingness to abandon the principles of freedom and sovereignty that underpin the transatlantic alliance. The message to allies is clear and dangerous: provoke Putin at your peril, for the U.S. will not stand by you.

The Birth of a New Verb: “Trumped” Friedman’s proposal to add “Trumped” to the diplomatic lexicon captures the essence of betrayal by an American president. It signifies a forsaking of allies for opaque reasons, leaving a legacy of shame and dishonor. The names Trump, Witkoff, Rubio, and Driscoll, as per Friedman, will be eternally linked to this disgrace.

In conclusion, Friedman’s article is not just a critique but a clarion call to recognize the gravity of Trump’s actions concerning Ukraine. The comparison to Neville Chamberlain’s appeasement is a powerful reminder of the costs of capitulation to dictatorial aggression. This plan, if pursued, could not only redefine Thanksgiving but also realign global power structures to the detriment of democratic values and international stability. The disgrace, as Friedman articulates, will indeed be everlasting, marking a pivotal moment of failure in American foreign policy.
