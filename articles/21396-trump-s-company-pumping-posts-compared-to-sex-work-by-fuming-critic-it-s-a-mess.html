Trump's company-pumping posts compared to sex work by fuming critic: 'It's a mess'

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paywalling the Presidency

Context

The Washington Post reports that Trump Media plans to sell milliseconds-faster access to Donald Trump’s Truth Social posts through a product called Truth API, charging hedge funds and high-frequency traders up to $100,000 a month. Trump owns about 41 percent of Trump Media, so the arrangement does not merely surround his presidency with commercial noise. It routes money from traders seeking advantage straight back to him while his posts move markets.

The Real Power Is Not the Platform

The central fact is not that Truth Social is “innovating.” The central fact is that the president controls the signal. Trump is not a celebrity offering content; he is the holder of institutional power whose posts can move stocks, shift expectations, and announce policy shifts on war, tariffs, and central bank leadership. When a president’s social media feed becomes a premium financial product, the line between governance and enrichment is gone. The state is being converted into a trading edge.

This Is Self-Dealing With a Badge

The source quotes an ethics expert calling it a “real mess,” and another market participant saying that if this were a corporate CEO, “this would be jail time.” That is the right instinct, but even that framing is too narrow. A CEO abusing inside access is bad. A president monetizing proximity to his own official power is worse, because the office itself is what gives the posts their value. Trump is not accidentally adjacent to the market reaction. He is the cause of it. The subscription is a toll on foreknowledge, and the tollbooth is his public office.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The soft framing here is that Truth Social is just selling speed, the way other platforms sell speed. That comparison is useful only if you ignore the entire political structure around it. Other platforms are not run by the president. Other platforms are not vehicles for converting official behavior into private revenue. Other platforms do not allow a leader to reward a class of traders with earlier access to announcements that can trigger buying frenzies. Calling this a social-media business model is misdirection. It is a patronage system for financial insiders.

Market Manipulation as Governance Style

The Post says Trump has been posting more about publicly traded companies and that approving posts about Palantir Technologies and Intel helped send stock prices higher. That matters because it shows the mechanism at work: Trump does not just comment on power, he manufactures market movement, then sells proximity to that movement. This is governance as pump-and-paywall. The political pattern is not confusion or chaos. It is deliberate blurring of public authority and private extraction, with enough plausible deniability to keep the machinery running.

The Larger Pattern

What this story reveals is a presidency organized like a racket: policy, publicity, and personal profit fused into one channel, then sold to the highest bidder in fractions of a second. The scandal is not only that Trump benefits from it. The scandal is that the system still has to be argued into naming it plainly. When the office of the president becomes an asset class, democratic accountability is no longer merely weakened. It is turned into a market opportunity for the people already closest to power.
