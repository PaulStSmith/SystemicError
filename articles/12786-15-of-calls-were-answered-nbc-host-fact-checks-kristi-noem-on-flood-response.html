'15% of calls were answered': NBC host fact-checks Kristi Noem on flood response

NBC host Kristen Welker called out Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Secretary Kristi Noem because the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) reportedly responded to fewer than 16% of calls in response to the recent deadly flooding in Texas.

"The New York Times is reporting that thousands of calls from flood victims to FEMA call centers went unanswered in the middle of this ongoing disaster because you didn't renew contracts to keep call center staff in place until nearly one week after the floods," Welker told Noem on Sunday. "Why did it take so long to extend those contracts?"

"It's just false. Those contracts were in place," the DHS secretary replied. "So false reporting, fake news, and it's discouraging."

"Just to be very clear, on July 7th, 15.9% of calls were answered," Welker pressed. "I mean, does that concern you that only 15% of calls were answered? These are people in a desperate state."

Noem, however, again insisted that the "contracts were in place and those people were in those call centers and they were picking up the phone and answering these calls."

"So that report needs to be valid if — I'd — I'm not certain it's accurate and I'm not sure where it came from," she said. "And the individuals who are giving you information out of FEMA, I'd love to have them put their names behind it because the anonymous attacks to politicize this situation is completely wrong."

"I care," she added. "These emergencies need to be conducted exactly how President Trump handled this one."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing DHS Secretary Noem's Response to FEMA's Alleged Inadequacies

In a recent NBC interview, Kristen Welker brought to light a concerning report from The New York Times regarding the Federal Emergency Management Agency's (FEMA) response to the deadly flooding in Texas. The claim was stark: FEMA call centers, a critical lifeline for victims in the throes of disaster, responded to less than 16% of calls. The reason? Allegedly, DHS Secretary Kristi Noem failed to renew necessary contracts to keep these call centers adequately staffed.

Facts vs. Deflections: The Heart of the Matter

Secretary Noem's response to these serious allegations was, to put it mildly, deflective. When pressed by Welker about the contract renewals, Noem retorted, claiming the report to be "just false" and branding it as "false reporting" and "fake news." Yet, her response was notably bereft of any concrete evidence or data to refute the claims directly. Instead of addressing the heart of the issue—the alleged failure to respond to citizens in dire need—Noem chose to attack the report's credibility without substantiation.

The Numbers Don't Lie

Welker, armed with data, pointed out that on July 7th, a mere 15.9% of calls were answered. This statistic isn't just a number; it represents thousands of individuals in desperate situations, seeking help that, reportedly, was not there. Noem's response was again to question the report's accuracy and to criticize the anonymity of the sources. Yet, this line of defense misses the mark. The focus on the anonymity of criticism rather than the substance of the criticism itself is a classic diversion tactic. It does nothing to address the very real possibility that FEMA was unprepared to fulfill its mission of aiding citizens during a disaster.

Echoing the Past or Learning from It?

Noem's assertion that "these emergencies need to be conducted exactly how President Trump handled this one" is perplexing, given the context. It's an attempt to pivot the conversation away from FEMA's recent performance to a broader, more politicized discussion. However, the assertion dodges the immediate issue: the alleged inadequacy of FEMA's response under her watch. It's an evasion, lacking in accountability or insight into how these critical emergency responses can be improved.

The Verdict

Secretary Noem's responses to the allegations against FEMA are a textbook example of political deflection. Instead of tackling the issue head-on, providing data or evidence to counter the claims, or even acknowledging the potential for improvement, Noem chose to attack the report's credibility and lean on political allegiance as a shield.

The real issue at hand—the alleged failure of FEMA to effectively respond to a disaster and the potential consequences of such failures—deserves serious scrutiny. Transparency, accountability, and a willingness to improve are essential qualities in managing crises. Deflection, denial, and the invocation of "fake news" do nothing to aid those affected by disasters nor do they contribute to the betterment of emergency response strategies in the future.

In the end, the victims of the Texas flooding—and indeed, all citizens—deserve more than political sidestepping. They deserve a FEMA that is prepared, responsive, and adequately resourced to meet the challenges of natural disasters head-on. Anything less is a systemic failure that must be addressed, not dismissed.
