Jack Smith says he had 'tons of evidence' in now-dismissed criminal case against Trump

In a rare interview, former Justice Department attorney Jack Smith claimed he had “tons of evidence” against President Donald Trump in the criminal case against him for allegedly mishandling classified documents, a case that was ultimately dismissed in the lead up to the 2024 election.

Smith was tasked with investigating Trump for his alleged mishandling of classified documents in 2023, an investigation that led to Trump being indicted by a federal grand jury on multiple charges in June of that year. Trump pleaded not guilty to all charges, and both he and his supporters spouted claims of being politically targeted given former President Joe Biden was also discovered to have retained classified documents from his time as vice president.

Smith, who was speaking at the University College London in an interview published on Tuesday, was asked about the case, and explained that the key difference between the investigations into Trump and Biden was that with Trump, the evidence of obstruction and other wrongdoing was clear.

“Both cases involved classified documents; the difference is the facts. In my particular case, we had tons of evidence of willfulness and obstructive evidence: publicly saying ‘these are my documents’ or things like that, and ‘I can keep them,’” Smith said.

“The government even tried to get them back before there was a criminal investigation, and then after the investigation started, [Trump] still [refused] to give them back, and then [tried] to obstruct the investigation. That sort of evidence didn’t exist in the other case.”

Smith did not investigate Biden’s mishandling of classified documents, but was familiar enough with the investigation to proclaim that there was no “willfulness” on Biden’s part to obstruct the federal government’s attempts to retrieve said documents, a key component of the crime of “willful retention of national defense information,” for which Trump was indicted.

“Both cases involved classified documents; the difference is the facts,” Smith said. “The rule of law allows for different outcomes when the facts are different. One of the major differences between the two cases is the obstructive conduct in the case that I investigated.”

Smith has become a target for Trump, who last week called Smith “deranged” and “a real sleazebag” following reports that Smith had sought call log info of some Republican lawmakers as part of his separate investigation into the Jan. 6 Capitol riot.

Our Analysis:

The Dichotomy of Justice: Unpacking the Trump vs. Biden Classified Documents Saga

In a revelation that should surprise precisely no one, the legal labyrinth surrounding former Presidents Donald Trump and Joe Biden’s handling of classified documents has once again highlighted the stark disparities in how justice is seemingly meted out at the highest echelons of American politics. Former Justice Department attorney Jack Smith’s candid commentary in a recent interview sheds light on the glaring differences between the two cases, with Trump’s actions being painted in a far more nefarious hue than Biden’s. But let’s dissect Smith’s claims with the skepticism they duly warrant.

The Allegations and the Evidence

Smith, embroiled in the investigation into Trump’s alleged mishandling of classified documents, boldly asserts he had “tons of evidence” against Trump, a claim that ignites curiosity given the eventual dismissal of the case as the 2024 election loomed closer. The charges against Trump, stemming from an indictment by a federal grand jury in June 2023, were met with vehement denials and cries of political witch hunts. Yet, Smith contrasts Trump’s case with Biden’s, attributing the difference to the evidence of “willfulness and obstructive evidence” in Trump’s scenario. Trump’s public declarations claiming ownership and the subsequent refusal to return the documents even in the face of a criminal investigation are highlighted as clear markers of his guilt.

However, one can’t help but marvel at the convenient timing and the selective outrage. The contrast drawn between Trump’s and Biden’s mishandling of classified documents, with the latter apparently not demonstrating the same level of “willfulness,” smacks of a double standard that has become all too familiar in the political arena. The key takeaway from Smith’s interview is not the evidence itself, but the interpretive lens through which it is viewed and presented to the public.

The Political Theater

The narrative swiftly moves from legal analysis to political vilification, with Trump lashing out at Smith, labeling him “deranged” and “a real sleazebag.” This exchange underscores the deeply personal and vitriolic nature of political discourse, where legal proceedings are often overshadowed by character assassinations and sensational rhetoric. Smith’s involvement in the investigation into the Jan. 6 Capitol riot, and his subsequent targeting of Republican lawmakers, adds another layer of complexity and bias to the saga.

The Rule of Law or the Rule of Convenience?

Smith’s assertion that “the rule of law allows for different outcomes when the facts are different” is a principle that, while true in theory, seems to falter in its application. The discrepancy in the treatment of Trump versus Biden raises uncomfortable questions about the impartiality of justice. Is the rule of law being upheld, or is it being manipulated to serve political ends?

Conclusion: A Systemic Error?

The crux of the matter lies not in the mishandling of classified documents per se but in the systemic disparities in how justice is pursued and perceived. Smith’s interview, while shedding light on the procedural aspects of the investigation, inadvertently highlights the broader issue of trust in the justice system. When political affiliations dictate the severity of accusations and the fervor of investigations, the very foundation of justice is under threat.

In essence, the saga of Trump versus Biden’s handling of classified documents is a microcosm of the larger, more troubling narrative of selective justice. It’s a narrative that deserves not just scrutiny but a call to action for a return to impartiality and fairness, away from the theatrics of political bias and toward the true essence of the rule of law. As the 2024 election approaches, one must wonder whether justice will continue to be a pawn in the game of political chess, or if it will finally reclaim its place as the cornerstone of a functioning democracy.
