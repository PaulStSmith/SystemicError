'Oh my god': New report on Trump-Epstein FBI 'cover-up' has experts saying 'wowza'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the press at Trump Tower in New York City, U.S., September 26, 2024. REUTERS/David Dee Delgado

A new report published on Sunday has some FBI experts' heads spinning.

Legal analyst Allison Gill, who authors the popular newsletter Mueller She Wrote, published a report detailing that about 1,000 FBI personnel were assigned to review 300,000 pages of the Jeffrey Epstein files and flag any mentions of President Donald Trump. Any mentions of the president were logged in an Excel spreadsheet, the report adds.

The report included some searing details as well. For instance, it says FBI agents received video training on an unsecured network about how to review the files. The files were also kept on a shared drive for the entire department to see. Under more normal circumstances, permission to view the files would have been limited to people with the appropriate security clearances, the report says.

"There was one confirmed mention of Donald Trump in the files reviewed by an analyst who again spoke on the condition of anonymity," Gill reported. "Beyond that, there were other instances of Trump appearing in the files, but the number of times and to what extent is unknown."

Social media lit up with reactions to the story.

"Oh my god," conservative and activist Rick Wilson, co-founder of The Lincoln Project, posted on X.

"Wowza," Republican lawyer George Conway wrote on X.

"This needs to be treated as a true blockbuster. So many layers of WTF," journalist Ed Moltzen wrote on Bluesky.

"1,000 FBI agents reviewed 300,000 documents for mentions of Trump," another Bluesky user posted. "That's considerably more effort than has been taken to protect the identities of the VICTIMS."

"So let me get this straight—there are training videos on how to flag Trump in the Epstein files… and the spreadsheet does exist? But sure, let’s keep pretending this is all some leftist fever dream while the GOP turns into a full-blown gaslight cult," reads another post on Bluesky.

One popular account, called KT 'Special MI6 Operation', said, "Donald Trump is in the Epstein files. You don't instruct 1,000 FBI agents to look for references to Donald J Trump unless you know you're in the files."

Our Analysis:

The Absurd Theatre of the FBI's Epstein-Trump Fiasco

The latest bombshell to hit the waves, courtesy of legal analyst Allison Gill and her newsletter Mueller She Wrote, details an operation so ludicrous it would be comedic if it weren't so deeply troubling. According to Gill, the FBI has apparently allocated a staggering 1,000 personnel to sift through 300,000 pages of the Jeffrey Epstein files, with a laser focus on any mentions of Donald Trump. This isn't just a story; it's a glaring spotlight on the theater of the absurd that our national security has become.

Priorities Skewed? Let's address the elephant in the room: the allocation of resources. The FBI deploying a small army to flag Trump mentions in a case as serious and vile as Jeffrey Epstein's is a shocking revelation of priorities, or the lack thereof. The sheer volume of manpower dedicated to this task screams of an obsession not with justice but with political implications. Contrast this with the reported negligence towards the protection of victims' identities, and the picture becomes grimmer. It begs the question: where do the FBI's true priorities lie?

Security Circus Adding insult to injury is the searing detail that agents received video training on an unsecured network and stored sensitive files on a shared drive accessible to the entire department. This is Security 101 failure. In what universe does it make sense to handle such sensitive material with the carelessness of a high school IT project? Under "more normal circumstances," as Gill points out, such files would be safeguarded under lock, key, and clearance level. The breach of protocol here is not just alarming; it's a farce.

The Spreadsheet of Absurdity At the heart of this spectacle lies an Excel spreadsheet, a digital ledger of Trump mentions, which somehow manages to encapsulate the absurdity of the entire operation. The existence of such a document, and the resources poured into its creation, is a testament to the bizarre juncture at which political vendetta meets bureaucratic inefficiency.

Social Media Erupts The reactions on social media platforms, from X (formerly Twitter) to Bluesky, range from incredulous to outraged. Figures like Rick Wilson and George Conway express shock and awe, while others highlight the disproportionate effort dedicated to Trump mentions over victim protection. A particularly poignant critique comes from a Bluesky user pointing out the transformation of the GOP into a "full-blown gaslight cult," a sentiment echoed across the digital sphere.

The Uncomfortable Truth The involvement, or mention, of Donald Trump in the Epstein files is a matter of public interest, undoubtedly. However, the FBI's handling of the investigation, as detailed by Gill, raises more questions than it answers. It paints a picture of an agency possibly more concerned with political implications than the pursuit of justice for Epstein's victims.

In the end, this report does not just shed light on the FBI's questionable allocation of resources; it exposes a deeper systemic rot within our institutions of power. The prioritization of political over procedural, the glaring security lapses, and the circus that unfolds in the public eye point to a systemic error that needs addressing.

As the public digests this blockbuster story, it's essential to remember the victims of Epstein's crimes, who deserve justice and protection, not to be overshadowed by the political theater. Our critique must not end with a shake of the head or a wag of the finger but with a demand for accountability, transparency, and a reevaluation of our national priorities.
