Immigration expert taken aback as 'unprepared' MAGA senator flails during 'interrogation'

An explosive Senate committee meeting last week made headlines after Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) lashed out at immigration expert David Bier over what he called “hyperbolic” social media posts critical of the Trump administration, but on Monday, Bier revealed that he was taken aback by the GOP senator’s ill-prepared attacks.

“I am a little surprised that he seemed unprepared for his interrogation of me,” Bier told The Bulwark in a video interview. “He didn’t really come with any arguments.”

The director of immigration studies for the CATO Institute, Bier testified before the Senate Budget Committee last week and was targeted by Kennedy over social media posts he’d made criticizing the Trump administration. Kennedy first quoted a social media post from Bier that championed a video published by several Democratic lawmakers last year, urging service members to refuse “illegal orders.”

“You posted: ‘The Democrats’ video telling service members to ignore illegal orders didn’t go far enough, they should have also urged them to refuse unethical orders, whether illegal or not,’” Kennedy said, reading one of Bier’s social media posts. “Did I read that correctly?”

Bier confirmed to Kennedy that he had accurately read his social media post, and asked him whether he disagreed with the statement. Kennedy ignored Bier’s question and moved to read another of Bier’s social media posts, one that criticized a statement from the Department of Homeland Security that advocated for deporting 100 million immigrants from “the Third World,” a statement Bier labeled as an open call for “ethnic cleansing.”

Recalling the exchange to The Bulwark Monday, Bier said he was genuinely shocked at what he said was Kennedy’s unpreparedness, particularly in regards to the lawmaker’s lack of responses.

“I asked him ‘do you disagree with that, do you think they should follow unethical orders that could be technically deemed illegal?’ And he didn’t respond!” Bier said. “He immediately transitioned on to the next question.”

Our Analysis:

The Clash of Perspectives: Kennedy vs. Bier

In a recent Senate committee meeting that has grabbed public attention, Senator John Kennedy (R-LA) confronted immigration expert David Bier over his critical social media posts about the Trump administration. This interaction, primarily characterized by Kennedy’s alleged unpreparedness and Bier’s pointed criticism, offers a fertile ground for dissecting the dynamics of power, responsibility, and the framing of political narratives.

Identifying the Power Dynamics

At the heart of this confrontation are two figures: Sen. John Kennedy, wielding the institutional power inherent to a United States Senator, and David Bier, an expert from the CATO Institute with a platform but without direct political power. The institutional power imbalance here is clear. Kennedy, as part of the legislative body, holds sway over national policy and public discourse in a way Bier does not. Bier, meanwhile, influences through expertise and public commentary.

Decision Makers and Outcomes

The immediate decision maker in this scenario is Kennedy, who chose to confront Bier over his public statements. The outcome, a public spat that drew media attention, was the result of Kennedy’s approach during the committee meeting. Bier’s role was reactive; his decisions were confined to the responses he gave to Kennedy’s provocations.

Framing and Responsibility

The article suggests a frame where Kennedy’s unpreparedness is pitted against Bier’s criticisms of the Trump administration, with a focus on the former. This framing potentially misdirects responsibility by emphasizing Kennedy’s preparation for the meeting over the substantive issues at play—namely, the ethical considerations around military orders and immigration policies Bier highlighted. While Kennedy’s approach and preparation (or lack thereof) are indeed newsworthy, there’s a risk of overshadowing the broader, more critical discussion about policy and ethics that Bier’s comments invite.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating

There’s no false equivalence here; the article doesn’t attempt to equate the actions or positions of Kennedy and Bier directly. However, it does risk turning Kennedy into a convenient scapegoat for those who might wish to dismiss the substantive issues under discussion. Critiquing Kennedy’s preparation is low-hanging fruit; the harder, more important task is engaging with the ethical and policy questions Bier raises.

Conclusion

In dissecting this political narrative, it’s essential to recognize the different types of power and platforms at play. While Kennedy’s approach to the confrontation can and should be critiqued, it’s vital not to lose sight of the broader and more critical issues at stake. The real story isn’t just about a senator’s preparation for a committee meeting; it’s about the significant, often contentious debates surrounding immigration policy and military ethics. As observers and analysts, our focus should remain on these substantive issues, ensuring they are not overshadowed by the spectacle of political theater.
