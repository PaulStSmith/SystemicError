'Impeach this idiot': Trump battered after 'pathetic' MAGA display at West Point

While more than a few critics of Donald Trump were appalled that he decided to talk about "trophy wives" at Saturday's West Point graduation, another faction couldn't get over his decision to wear a "Make America Great Again" (MAGA) hat for the occasion.

With CNN reporting that the commencement speech was half-traditional exhortations to go forward and half-MAGA rally ranting, the bright red hat fashion choice was on the receiving end of a wave of disgust and derision.

Journalist David Bernstein took to Bluesky to observe, "Our President is currently delivering the commencement address at West Point while wearing a MAGA hat and talking about his great campaign victory and various fantastic accomplishments wtaf."

"The only difference between a MAGA hat and KKK hood is that the KKK were ashamed to show their faces," economist Patrick Chovanec suggested.

Popular podcaster and veteran Fred Wellman wrote on Bluesky, "This is deeply disgusting. Wearing his pathetic campaign hat to the West Point graduation."

On X, minor league baseball franchise owner Lou DiBella suggested the commander-in-chief should have been wearing an "Army" hat, writing: "Ladies and Gentlemen, the President of the United States! A lesson on trophy wives; how inspiring for our future Army officers at their commencement. Nice MAGA hat ... how appropriate! Consider this one instead on next graduation day."

"Trump wearing a MAGA hat while talking to graduates at West Point is pretty terrible and hardly anyone will make a fuss of it," predicted BYU political scientist Darin Self.

Elon Musk's own Grok on X has called upon to verify if the photo of the president in the hat was photoshopped and responded, "The photo of Donald Trump wearing a MAGA hat at the West Point commencement on May 24, 2025, appears authentic based on available evidence. Multiple news outlets, including The Mirror US, CBS News, and The New York Times, confirm he wore the hat during the event. No credible claims suggest the image was photoshopped, despite speculation due to the unusual context of a political hat at a military ceremony. The controversy stems from the perceived inappropriateness, not the photo's veracity."

Veteran Daniel Larsen chipped in, "Orange blob menace psychopath convicted felon maga cult leader rambled and dissembled. Wearing that hat to a West Point commencement is high crimes and misdemeanors. Impeach this idiot."

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Politics: Trump at West Point

In an act that blurs the already thin line between political spectacle and solemn ceremony, Donald Trump's appearance at the West Point graduation has ignited a firestorm of controversy, critiqued by many as a brazen display of political theater at an event that should have transcended partisan divides. His decision to don a "Make America Great Again" (MAGA) hat, a symbol deeply polarizing to the American public, during a commencement speech to future military leaders, is a distillation of Trump's presidency: divisive, unapologetic, and always, unerringly, Trump.

The MAGA Hat: More Than Just Fabric

The bright red MAGA hat has come to symbolize much more than Trump's campaign; it's a beacon of his presidency, representing, depending on who you ask, either the revitalization of American pride or the embodiment of its deepest prejudices. Wearing this hat at West Point wasn't just a fashion choice; it was a political statement, loud and clear. It's a declaration that even in spaces that should be apolitical, Trump's brand—and indeed, Trumpism—cannot and will not be muted.

Public Reaction: Outrage and Disgust

The response to Trump's sartorial choice was swift and scathing. Critics lambasted the move as "deeply disgusting" and an insult to the occasion. The comparison by economist Patrick Chovanec between the MAGA hat and a KKK hood is particularly jarring, highlighting the perceived toxicity of Trump's political brand. Such comparisons, while extreme, underscore the depth of division Trump evokes in the American psyche.

Minor league baseball franchise owner Lou DiBella's suggestion that Trump should have worn an "Army" hat instead speaks volumes. It's a call for the President to embody the role of Commander-in-Chief in a manner that respects and honors the institution and its traditions, rather than using it as a backdrop for political grandstanding.

The Defense: Authenticity or Audacity?

In the face of speculation, the confirmation by Elon Musk's Grok on X that the photo of Trump in the MAGA hat was authentic did little to quell the controversy. Instead, it reinforced the reality of the moment: the President of the United States chose, with full intent, to politicize a military graduation. The authenticity of the photo isn't the issue; the audacity of the act is.

Political Scientist's Prediction: A Fleeting Fuss?

BYU political scientist Darin Self's prediction that "hardly anyone will make a fuss of it" is perhaps the most disheartening take. If true, it suggests a normalization of the extraordinary, a sign that the public may have become desensitized to what once would have been considered egregious breaches of decorum and respect. Yet, the fervent reactions across social media platforms indicate that for many, this is a bridge too far.

Conclusion: A Divisive Legacy

Trump's decision to wear a MAGA hat at West Point encapsulates his presidency's divisive legacy. It's a reminder that for Trump, every opportunity is an opportunity for branding, regardless of the appropriateness of the occasion. The fallout from this event is a microcosm of a nation grappling with the consequences of a presidency that has relentlessly tested the boundaries of tradition, decorum, and unity.

In the end, the spectacle at West Point is not just about a hat. It's about what that hat represents: a deeply divided America, a presidency that thrives on division, and a political culture that struggles to find common ground even in spaces that should unite us.
