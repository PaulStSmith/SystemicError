Donald Trump could be planning a major, MAGA-themed blowout for the final year of his presidency, a political analyst has claimed.

Whether the president makes the most of his last year in office in 2028 is yet to be seen, but Slate columnist Justin Peters thinks Trump could pull out all the stops with a garish display. With both the 2026 World Cup and 2028 Olympics set to take place in the US, the president could be testing the waters of how MAGA will look on the world stage.

The 2028 Olympic Games, which will be hosted in Los Angeles, will be a chance for Trump to throw his weight around one last time, according to Peters. He wrote, “What I’m saying is that Donald Trump probably can’t ruin the Olympics—or at least he can’t ruin these Olympics.

“Once the 2026 Winter Games conclude, the global sporteaucracy will set its sights on the 2028 Los Angeles Games, which will be held in the final year of Trump’s second presidential term. There is every reason to think that Trump will go out of his way to ensure that those Olympics are a tacky MAGA horror show made in his own image.”

Financial strains could be a part of that power play too, with the United Nations announcing it is on the brink of insolvency due to unpaid US debts. $2.2billion in debt is yet to be repaid and will likely remain unpaid, with the International Olympic Committee potentially collapsing as part of the UN’s announcement.

Peters added, “But that dark day is still a couple of years away, and there’s little point in worrying about the prospect of Olympic medals minted with Charlie Kirk’s face on them.

“For now, there are plenty of more pressing problems for us to freak out about all day, every day. But I hope you’ll forgive me if I’m not thinking about any of that while I’m watching the luge.

“The Olympics are quaint in the best way, insofar as they are the sort of well-meaning project that people like Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin would never, ever come up with on their own. They are the least terrible, still objectively bad thing we’ve got going right now. That’s something, at least.”

Our Analysis:

The Trumpian Spectacle: Olympics Edition

In what can only be described as a dystopian preview of what 2028 might hold, Slate columnist Justin Peters has floated the notion that Donald Trump is gearing up for a MAGA-themed blowout to mark the end of his presidency. This spectacle, set against the backdrop of the 2028 Olympics in Los Angeles, promises to be a garish display of Trump’s brand. But let’s dissect the layers of absurdity and potential reality in this scenario.

The MAGA Olympics: A Horror Show or Hot Air?

Firstly, the notion of Trump planning his swan song around the Olympics reeks of the grandiosity and spectacle that has defined his public life and presidency. Peters’ vision of an Olympic Games “made in [Trump’s] own image” is a chilling forecast for an event that, at its core, is supposed to champion global unity and the pinnacle of athletic achievement, not serve as a stage for political pageantry.

Financial Strains and Global Repercussions

The article touches on a far more alarming issue: the United States’ $2.2 billion debt to the United Nations, which threatens the very fabric of the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and, by extension, the Olympics themselves. The potential financial collapse of the IOC would be catastrophic, far beyond the tarnishing of the Games by any individual’s ego. Yet, the article breezes past this like it’s a minor subplot in the Trumpian drama, when in fact, it should be a blaring alarm for immediate action.

The Real Villains: Indifference and Spectacle

What’s most telling in Peters’ piece is the resigned tone to the “more pressing problems” we face, suggesting a collective desensitization to the absurd. The mention of Olympic medals possibly featuring the face of Charlie Kirk—while likely hyperbolic—underscores the farcical turn our reality has taken, where the once-unthinkable becomes fodder for casual speculation.

The Olympic Spirit: Lost or Preserved?

Peters asserts that the Olympics represent the “least terrible, still objectively bad thing we’ve got going right now,” a backhanded compliment that speaks volumes. It’s a recognition that, despite its flaws, the Olympic Games embody a spirit and idealism worth preserving. The suggestion that figures like Trump and Putin could never conceive of something as inherently well-meaning as the Olympics is a sharp reminder of the values at stake.

Conclusion: Beyond the Spectacle

While the article paints a vivid picture of a Trump-dominated Olympic Games, it’s crucial to look beyond the spectacle. The real story here is about the potential erosion of international institutions, financial irresponsibility on a global scale, and the need to safeguard the values that the Olympics strive to represent. As 2028 approaches, let’s hope the focus shifts from fearing a “tacky MAGA horror show” to addressing the underlying issues that threaten the Games and our global community. After all, the Olympics should transcend politics, serving as a beacon of hope, unity, and human achievement.
