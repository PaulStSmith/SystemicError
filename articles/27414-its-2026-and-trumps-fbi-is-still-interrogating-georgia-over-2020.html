It’s 2026 —and Trump’s FBI is still interrogating Georgia over 2020

Based on an article by Roxanne Cooper

Paulo SantosNovember 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An election official questioned over rejected theoriesTrump’s grievance now has federal staffingGeorgia already counted the votes three times

Six years after Donald Trump lost Georgia, his administration is still using federal law enforcement to pursue the fraud narrative that failed to survive the state’s audits, recounts, court proceedings and Republican-led investigations. On Friday, two FBI agents and two federal prosecutors questioned former Fulton County elections director Richard Barron for more than two hours in Atlanta, according to The New York Times. No criminal charges have emerged from the investigation.

This is not merely Trump refusing to move on. The federal government has placed personnel, investigative authority and public money behind another attempt to validate his grievance. One of the officials questioning Barron was Dan Bishop, a former Republican congressman who voted against certifying Trump’s 2020 defeat and whom the Justice Department appointed its top election-fraud prosecutor in April.

An election official questioned over rejected theories

Barron oversaw Fulton County’s vote count in 2020 and left the job in 2022. He told The Atlanta Journal-Constitution that agents approached him last week and that he agreed to be interviewed at his lawyer’s office without a subpoena. Prosecutors have indicated that they want another meeting.

Their questions returned to allegations promoted by conservative activists Garland Favorito and Kevin Moncla. Barron said Bishop specifically mentioned Favorito. Moncla, cited in an affidavit supporting an FBI ballot raid, had submitted a 263-page report alleging “fictitious ballots” and fabricated tabulator poll tapes in Fulton County.

Asked by the AJC whether the activists’ theories were reasonable, Barron rejected them. He said investigators concentrated on the 2020 machine recount and minor administrative matters unrelated to the result. Barron maintained that neither he nor his staff committed crimes.

That distinction matters. Administrative irregularities are not proof of fraudulent votes, much less evidence that an election result was stolen. Treating routine details as possible fragments of a vast crime allows investigators to keep the premise alive even when the conclusion has repeatedly failed.

Trump’s grievance now has federal staffing

The inquiry extends well beyond one voluntary interview. Federal authorities have contacted other current and former Fulton County election workers, The New York Times reported. In January, agents raided a county election warehouse for ballots in an operation attended by Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard. FBI special agent Hugh Raymond Evans, who signed the affidavit supporting that raid, also participated in Barron’s questioning.

FBI Director Kash Patel separately assigned hundreds of intelligence analysts to examine records from the 2020 election, according to an internal bureau directive described in the reporting. The scale reveals the power imbalance at the center of the story: former local election employees must retain lawyers and answer federal investigators, while the president’s disproven account of his own defeat receives the resources of the Justice Department, the FBI and the intelligence apparatus.

Bishop’s role makes the institutional choice especially stark. He did not approach the matter as a detached figure with no recorded position on the election. As a member of Congress, he voted to reject certification of the defeat that his office is now helping investigate. That history does not establish misconduct in the present inquiry, but it does make claims of neutral fact-finding difficult to separate from the political project that preceded his appointment.

Georgia already counted the votes three times

Georgia’s result was confirmed through three tallies, including a hand audit of the paper ballots. State and federal courts addressed challenges, while investigations led by Republican state officials found no basis to overturn the outcome, according to the Georgia secretary of state’s office.

The renewed federal effort therefore does not begin with an unresolved count. It begins after the count was checked repeatedly and after the proposed alternatives failed. The government is not filling an obvious evidentiary void; it is reopening the search under officials selected by the man who benefits politically from portraying his loss as theft.

Fulton County Commission Chairman Robb Pitts told the AJC that he remains confident the county conducted a secure and fair election. He said the current and former officials approached by agents would continue to reinforce that conclusion.

Perhaps they will. But the investigation already imposes a consequence even without charges: it subjects election workers to recurring federal scrutiny while laundering a defeated political claim through the prestige and coercive capacity of law enforcement. After three counts and years of failed challenges, the unanswered question is no longer whether Georgia counted its ballots. It is how long Trump’s administration intends to make public servants defend that count against the president’s refusal to accept it.
