Ryan Goodman, editor-in-chief of “Just Security,” uncovered a document in which the New York Police Department was told by the federal government to “stand down” on its investigation into Jeffrey Epstein.

In a video posted Monday, Goodman said that he found documents in which the FBI sent the instructions the NYPD just five days after Epstein’s arrest. Goodman said he found it surprising that the order also applied to the SVU (Special Victims Unit). Goodman explained that it’s the key part of law enforcement “that is specially trained and equipped to investigate crimes against minors and crimes of such abuse.”

The documents show that the existing district attorney of New York was in an ongoing investigation into Epstein and was communicating with the victims. Some of those victims then began to speak out, including a 2019 interview with Savannah Guthrie on NBC.

Goodman said that they considered the DA reaching out to victims a “fire” that needed to be put out. All of this was during President Donald Trump’s first administration.

“It’s quite stunning because a large part of the allegations against Epstein, Ghislaine Maxwell and alleged co-conspirators happened in New York. That would be the epicenter of a lot of the crimes, of the trafficking of the young girls and women, the sexual abuse of the young girls and women happened in the townhouse of the Upper East Side in Manhattan,” Goodman explained.

So, to close off such an investigation from those on the ground, he said, is “astonishing” as it would be the most productive avenue of getting at the other men involved in the crimes.

While the federal government may have assumed that the NYPD and DA stopped, they actually continued the probe through a “robust investigation,” including speaking to the survivors through their attorneys. One part of that investigation focuses specifically on Leon Black, a private equity investor, whom Business Insider reported on last week. Black has never been charged.

The Office of the New Mexico Attorney General, Raúl Torrez, said last week that in 2019, it was also told to stand down.

The “investigation was closed in 2019 at the request of the U.S Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York,” Torrez said. He wants them to be reopened. The U.S. attorney in 2019 was Geoffrey Berman, who was also actively investigating potential financial abuses related to Donald Trump’s 2017 Presidential Inaugural Committee and pursuing investigations involving Michael Cohen from 2018 to 2019.

The documents show that on Dec. 6, 2018, the NYPD, SDNY and the FBI Child Exploitation Human Trafficking Task Force opened a case on Epstein.

On July 10, 2019 an Epstein survivor, Jennifer Araoz, was interviewed by Guthrie for the TODAY Show. She said the recruiter knew she was 14. After the interview aired, Araoz submitted a New York State court filing seeking information from Epstein about a recruiter who allegedly worked with him to “recruit” her. Her lawsuit against Maxwell and an assistant was filed on Aug 14, 2019, just four days after Epstein was found dead. So, she filed the suit against his estate.

The “stand down” directive came in 2019 on July 10 and 11. One month later, Epstein was dead.

By Jan. 2020, emails showed that the FBI was discussing the NYPD’s probe of Epstein.

Our Analysis:

The “Stand Down” Order: A Stark Revelation in the Epstein Saga

In a revelation that cuts through the murky waters of high-profile criminal investigations, Ryan Goodman, editor-in-chief of “Just Security,” has unearthed a document that throws a glaring spotlight on the federal government’s interference in the New York Police Department’s (NYPD) probe into Jeffrey Epstein. This directive, instructing the NYPD to “stand down,” particularly targeting the Special Victims Unit (SVU), is not just surprising; it’s a brazen affront to justice.

The Untouchable Epstein?

The documents Goodman discovered show that the FBI sent these instructions merely five days after Epstein’s arrest. This directive included the SVU, the very unit specialized in investigating crimes against minors and abuse. This is not just interference; it’s a calculated obstruction, an attempt to smother a “fire” that the District Attorney of New York was fervently fighting to spread - the fire of truth and justice for the victims.

It’s all in the timing: The stand down order came right as victims began to speak out, including a poignant 2019 interview with Savannah Guthrie on NBC. The existing district attorney was in active communication with victims, indicating an ongoing, rigorous investigation. Yet, the federal government deemed it necessary to douse these flames of investigative rigor.

The Epicenter of Epstein’s Alleged Crimes

Goodman underscores a critical point: New York was the epicenter of Epstein’s alleged crimes. It was within the luxurious bounds of Epstein’s Upper East Side townhouse that many of the trafficking and sexual abuse of young girls and women allegedly took place. To cut off the investigation at its most productive avenue is not just “astonishing,” as Goodman puts it; it’s a blatant miscarriage of justice.

A Continued Probe Despite Federal Orders

The NYPD and the District Attorney’s defiance in continuing the probe is commendable. Their “robust investigation” persisted, focusing on survivors and, interestingly, Leon Black, a figure yet to face charges. This continued pursuit of justice, despite federal orders, is a testament to the commitment of local law enforcement to their duty.

A Pattern of Interference?

This isn’t an isolated incident. The Office of the New Mexico Attorney General, Raúl Torrez, revealed a similar directive to stand down in 2019 by the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Southern District of New York. This pattern of interference, particularly under the banner of the U.S. attorney in 2019, Geoffrey Berman, who had his hands in several high-profile investigations, raises serious questions about the motives and impacts of such directives.

The Aftermath and the Ongoing Quest for Justice

Epstein’s death, occurring a short month after the “stand down” directive, adds a sinister note to an already dark melody. Jennifer Araoz’s lawsuit against Epstein’s estate, filed just days after his death, underscores the tragic persistence of his victims in seeking justice.

By January 2020, the FBI was still discussing the NYPD’s probe of Epstein, indicating that the investigation’s echoes continued to reverberate. This ongoing dialogue between federal and local law enforcement, despite previous orders to halt, sheds a sliver of hope that justice might still be served.

Conclusion

The federal government’s directive for the NYPD to “stand down” in its investigation into Jeffrey Epstein is more than an obstruction; it’s a stark reminder of the lengths to which power structures will go to protect their own. This saga is not just about Epstein; it’s about every victim silenced by the weight of influence and corruption. As we peel back the layers of this investigation, let us not forget the core of the matter: the quest for justice for those who suffered at the hands of the powerful.
