Frantic Trump calls top editor to try to kill 'explosive' WSJ story: report

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump attends the Congressional Picnic at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

White House officials called the Wall Street Journal to try to kill a possible coming story about President Donald Trump's ties to deceased wealth manager and child sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, Status News reported on Thursday.

"The article, said to be in the works for days, had still not appeared as of Thursday afternoon — raising eyebrows and fueling speculation across Washington and New York," said the report. "According to people familiar with the matter, The Journal is facing pressure from the White House over the story."

The report claimed Trump was "said to have personally called Emma Tucker, The Journal's editor-in-chief, to voice his objections."

Further details on that reported call weren't immediately known. Status' author Oliver Darcy, a former CNN correspondent, has reported that the story relies on "documentation" and is "potentially explosive."

This follows days of turmoil for the White House and the GOP after the Trump Justice Department closed the book on a review of the investigation into Epstein, concluding his death was a suicide as initially reported and that there is no long-fabled "client list" of wealthy celebrities, executives, and politicians who participated in his trafficking ring.

It was a massive 180 for many of Trump's law enforcement officials who had spent years fanning the flames of conspiracy theory about the case, and it led to many of Trump's supporters revolting against him and accusing him of hiding the truth.

Trump and his inner circle have scrambled to contain the fallout, with the president defending the work of the Justice Department and even criticizing his own supporters, demanding they move on from the issue.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Alleged White House Interference with the Wall Street Journal

The recent report by Status News throws a spotlight on a deeply troubling episode of potential White House interference in the journalistic process, centered around President Donald Trump's purported efforts to quash a Wall Street Journal story detailing his connections to the deceased wealth manager and convicted child sex trafficker, Jeffrey Epstein. This situation, if accurate, underscores not just an egregious attempt to manipulate the free press, but also reflects the larger, systemic issues within Trump's administration concerning transparency and accountability.

The Pressure on The Wall Street Journal

The Wall Street Journal, a publication with a storied history and a reputation for investigative rigor, reportedly faced direct pressure from the highest echelons of power. The claim that Trump personally reached out to Emma Tucker, the Journal's editor-in-chief, to express his objections to the story's publication is alarming. The President of the United States attempting to influence editorial decisions, particularly regarding stories that may cast him in an unfavorable light, is a stark assault on the principle of journalistic independence.

The Role of Documentation and the Potential Fallout

Oliver Darcy's assertion that the story is based on "documentation" and is "potentially explosive" indicates the gravity of what might have been uncovered. Documentation in such contexts often means hard evidence, which could have provided critical insight into Trump's connections with Epstein. The subsequent silence from The Wall Street Journal, as of the report's release, is not just troubling—it's a glaring red flag about the power dynamics at play and the possible capitulation to external pressures by a leading journalistic institution.

The Bigger Picture: Transparency and Accountability

This episode is emblematic of a broader pattern of behavior from Trump's administration. The closure of the investigation into Epstein by the Trump Justice Department, with a conclusion that Epstein's death was a suicide and the dismissal of the much-speculated "client list," has already stirred controversy. The administration's pivot, particularly among law enforcement officials who had previously entertained conspiracy theories about the case, smacks of political convenience rather than a pursuit of truth.

Moreover, Trump's defense of the Justice Department's conclusions and his admonishment of his own supporters to "move on" from the issue reveal a disturbing inclination to suppress inconvenient narratives. This is not the mark of a leadership committed to transparency or accountability. Instead, it suggests a leadership style that prioritizes image and personal loyalty over factual accuracy and ethical governance.

Conclusion

The alleged attempt by the White House to suppress a potentially damaging story about Donald Trump's ties to Jeffrey Epstein is a serious indictment of Trump's presidency. It raises significant questions about the integrity of his administration and its commitment to democratic principles. The freedom of the press is a cornerstone of democracy, ensuring that power is held to account. When that freedom is threatened, especially by those in the highest offices, it is not just an attack on the press, but an attack on the very foundation of democratic governance.

In sum, this episode should not be viewed in isolation but as part of a disturbing pattern of behavior that undermines public trust and erodes the foundations of our democratic institutions. It's a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance and resistance in the face of attempts to manipulate or control the free flow of information.
