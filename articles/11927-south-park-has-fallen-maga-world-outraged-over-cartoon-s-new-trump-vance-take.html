The latest episode of South Park that took aim at a number of top Trump administration officials had MAGA world up in arms late Wednesday for its portrayal of the president, many of whom decried the show as having gone “woke.”

“South Park is not MAGA and does not support America,” wrote X user RealCherokeeOwl, a pro-Trump account with more than 57,000 followers.

Premiering late on Wednesday, the second episode of the long-running show’s 27th season lampooned President Donald Trump, depicted in the episode alongside Vice President JD Vance, at the president’s Mar-a-Lago Club in Florida.

It also pokes fun at Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, and includes a parody of her real-life story of having killed her own dog, which she admitted to in her memoir and defended due to the dog being “untrainable.”

The episode marks the second dig at Trump since the president’s second term in office, with the first triggering a response from the White House, with White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers decrying the program as a “fourth-rate show” that “hasn’t been relevant for over 20 years.”

“Didn’t think I’d ever see the day that South Park went woke, shame,” wrote X user Memestonker with his 1,000-plus followers.

Alamo Taylor called the new episode “too extreme,” while X user Squarehead44 wrote that the show’s creators had been claimed by the “woke mind virus,” and X user Florida Sooner wrote that the show “had fallen.”

Self-described “proud patriot” Victoria Bestolarides railed against the episode, decrying the show in a social media post on X as being partisan, and being “just another extension of the liberal agenda.”

“What made us love South Park was the fact that they were willing to make fun of everyone,” Bestolarides wrote.

“Nobody was off limits. Currently, I can see where the puck is going and they have zero intentions of going after the Russia hoax or the auto pen debacle. If we don’t see an episode making fun of Jasmine Crockett or AOC (because there’s plenty of material, that I can tell you) we will know that South Park is just another extension of the liberal agenda.”

Our Analysis:

South Park and the MAGA Meltdown: A Lesson in Irony

In the latest cultural skirmish that has the MAGA world clutching its pearls, South Park, the satirical animated series known for sparing no one from its scathing humor, has apparently crossed an unforgivable line. The show's recent episode, which dared to lampoon Trump and his administration, has been met with cries of betrayal from those who once championed the series for its fearless mockery of political correctness and liberal sacred cows. The irony, thick enough to cut with a knife, seems lost on the show’s conservative critics.

"South Park is not MAGA and does not support America," wrote Twitter user RealCherokeeOwl, echoing a sentiment that seems to confuse satire with political allegiance. The episode in question, a part of the show's 27th season, took aim at Trump, JD Vance, Kristi Noem, and even included a darkly comedic nod to Noem’s real-life admission of killing her "untrainable" dog. This satirical broadside was enough to trigger accusations of the show "going woke," a term so overused in the current discourse that it has all but lost any specific meaning.

White House spokesperson Taylor Rogers's dismissal of South Park as a "fourth-rate show" that "hasn’t been relevant for over 20 years" is a marvel of irony, given the administration's seemingly urgent need to respond to it. If irrelevance is indeed measured by the White House's need to comment, South Park has never been more pertinent.

The outcry over South Park’s alleged political bias, particularly the lamentation that the show has become "just another extension of the liberal agenda," is a study in selective outrage and a misunderstanding of what satire is. Satire does not play favorites; it aims its barbs at the powerful, the hypocritical, and the absurd, regardless of party lines. To be shocked that South Park would target Trump and his administration is to have fundamentally misunderstood the show’s ethos from the start.

Self-described “proud patriot” Victoria Bestolarides's complaint that the show is not targeting "the Russia hoax or the auto pen debacle" is particularly telling. It suggests that for some, satire is only palatable when it aligns with their own biases and targets their perceived enemies. The expectation that South Park should serve as a mouthpiece for right-wing grievances underlines a broader issue in our polarized political landscape: the increasing inability to tolerate dissent or mockery, especially from sources that one considers to be ideologically aligned.

South Park has, from its inception, been an equal-opportunity offender. Its willingness to offend is precisely what has made it a lasting cultural touchstone. The notion that it has suddenly become partisan because it chooses to satirize Trump and his allies reveals not a shift in the show’s philosophy, but a shift in the audience's tolerance for satire that hits too close to home.

In the end, the backlash against South Park from MAGA supporters is a testament to the show’s continued relevance and its ability to provoke thought and, yes, outrage. It's a reminder that in satire, as in life, the truth often hurts. The fact that it's the MAGA world’s turn to feel the sting should not be surprising. After all, in the grand tradition of satire, nobody is off-limits—not even Donald Trump.
