'Once again like never before!' Trump contradicts himself in nonsensical pledge

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks at U.S. Steel Corporation–Irvin Works in West Mifflin, Pennsylvania, U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

President Donald Trump highlighted his announcement that he's doubling the tariff on steel imports to 50 percent with a seeming contradiction.

The president spoke last week at U.S. Steel’s Mon Valley Works–Irvin Plant in suburban Pittsburgh, where he told steelworkers he was boosting the tariffs starting Wednesday to protect their industry, and he reposted video of that speech – along with a vapid sales pitch – on Truth Social.

"With the help of Patriots like you, we're going to produce our own metal, unleash our own energy, secure our own future, build our Country, control our destiny and we are once again going to put Pennsylvania steel into the backbone of America like never before!" Trump posted Monday afternoon and left pinned to the top of his social media page.

Copy editors and pedants may debate whether it's possible to "once again" make Pennsylvania the backbone of the U.S. steel industry "like never before," but economists agree the dramatically higher tariffs would further drive up prices for housing, autos and other goods.

“Twenty-five percent tariffs were already high,” Kyle Handley, a professor of economics at the University of California, San Diego, told ABC News. “Fifty percent is incredibly high.”

The president also announced that aluminum tariffs would also be doubled to 50 percent, also starting Wednesday, which could drive up prices for food and beverages by about 1 percent for each can of soup or soda, Hauk said.

“It isn’t a huge amount on its own," Hauk said, "but if you think about the number of sodas or beers people have out of aluminum cans in a given year, it adds up over time."

Steel prices have risen 16 percent since Trump became president, according to the government's Producer Price Index.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tango: A Dance of Disarray

President Donald Trump's recent announcement to double the tariff on steel imports to a whopping 50 percent is a classic case of economic policy that seems to confuse more than clarify. At an event at U.S. Steel’s Mon Valley Works–Irvin Plant in suburban Pittsburgh, Trump painted a picture of revival and strength, invoking imagery of Pennsylvania steel as the "backbone of America." Yet, this move is less about strengthening backbones and more about placing burdens on the shoulders of American consumers and industries.

The Contradiction of "Making America Great Again"

Trump's rhetoric on social media, promising to "produce our own metal" and "control our destiny," is a hollow echo of his broader "America First" economic policies. Yet, the economics of his decision tell a different story. The idea that one can simultaneously revive an industry "like never before" while imposing tariffs that have historically led to higher prices for consumers and strained international relations is, to put it mildly, fantastical.

The Economic Fallout

The sharp increase from the already high 25% tariff to 50% is an economic gamble with high stakes. Economists like Kyle Handley warn of the significant impacts these tariffs will have on the cost of goods, from housing to autos. The notion that this will somehow bolster the American economy is at best, misguided, and at worst, dangerously naive.

Furthermore, the decision to also double aluminum tariffs to 50% is a slap in the face to any notion of affordability. The increase in prices for everyday items, such as cans of soup or soda, may seem minimal individually. However, as economist Hauk points out, these costs add up, creating an additional financial burden on American families.

The Reality of Steel Price Increases

Since Trump's presidency, steel prices have already seen a 16 percent increase. This rise is a testament to the flawed logic at the heart of these tariff policies. Rather than fostering growth and prosperity, these policies have contributed to an environment of uncertainty and increased costs for manufacturers and consumers alike.

Conclusion: A Misguided Policy

In essence, Trump's doubling of steel and aluminum tariffs under the guise of national revival is a misguided economic policy that ignores the complexity of global trade and the interconnectedness of modern economies. The promise of reviving the American steel industry "like never before" is not only a contradiction in terms but a misjudgment of the economic realities facing the United States and its trading partners.

It's time for a reality check. Economic policies should be grounded in rationality and a clear understanding of their impacts, not in the nostalgic rhetoric of a bygone era. The consequences of these tariffs will be felt not on the balance sheets of steel magnates but in the grocery bills and housing costs of everyday Americans. In attempting to bolster the steel industry, Trump risks undermining the very economy he seeks to protect.
