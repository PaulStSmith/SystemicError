'Knives will be out': Conservatives predict big 'firestorm' around new Trump admin scandal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 22, 20255 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump disembarks Air Force One as he arrives at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., June 21, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

President Donald Trump's border czar Tom Homan is facing backlash following reports released Saturday that revealed he was investigated by the FBI for allegedly accepting a $50,000 cash payment from undercover agents posing as business executives. The investigation, initiated in 2024, was ultimately closed by Trump administration officials earlier this year due to a lack of "credible evidence" of criminal wrongdoing.

Conservative commentator Sarah Longwell sharply reacted to the news, calling the lack of accountability “insane” and saying the scandal is emblematic of broader corruption in the Trump orbit.

During a podcast on The Bulwark Saturday, she argued that the entire “ecosystem around Trump sells influence,” noting that people close to Trump often profit simply by being close, promising to deliver favors or contracts if he wins, regardless of whether they hold formal office. She said it mirrors what she sees as “downstream corruption” from Trump — from countries offering bribes, to premium‑clubs run by his children — and that in Homan’s case, it shows how normalized these kinds of allegations have become.

Longwell noted that Homan has been a controversial figure in the administration.

"If this were just some unnamed member of the Trump administration, people would probably be like, yep, they’re all corrupt. But in this case, this guy is... like one of the biggest villains in the Trump administration."

She continued: "He is the architect of going and yanking people off the streets, of sending people to torture prisons, even when they’re innocent. Like that’s this guy. And so knives will be out forever for this guy. And rightly so. He has been terrible from the beginning. He has never made even an ounce of an attempt to act like he is going to work through the immigration process in sort of a lawful, humane way. He has been clear from the jump he was going to be as inhumane and destructive as possible as he did this. And so it doesn’t strike me one bit that this is not a good person. And that it is the kind of person who was taking old school bribes," she added.

The Bulwark's Tim Miller added that what distinguishes this scandal is its relative simplicity. He said the Homan case, essentially accepting a bag of cash in exchange for promising favors, is the sort of corruption most people can immediately grasp.

Miller contrasted it with more abstract or technical issues like crypto, stablecoins, or AI regulation, which tend to lose people’s attention.

"I do wonder if that the simplicity of it is going to mean that there is a greater firestorm around this than there’s been around the crypto stuff," he added.

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth About Tom Homan's Alleged Bribery Scandal

In the sprawling opera of corruption that has become synonymous with the Trump administration, a new aria has reached its crescendo. Reports have emerged, setting the stage for a scathing examination of Tom Homan, President Donald Trump's border czar, who allegedly found himself ensnared by the FBI for accepting a $50,000 cash payment from undercover agents masquerading as business executives. This episode, though it reads like a script ripped straight from a political thriller, underscores a disturbing reality about the Trump orbit's relationship with integrity and accountability—or, more accurately, the lack thereof.

The Allegations: A Simplicity Shrouded in Complexity

At the heart of this scandal lies a transaction as old as the concept of power itself: a bag of cash exchanged for promises of influence. This form of corruption, in its stark simplicity, cuts through the noise of more convoluted controversies, such as those surrounding crypto or AI regulation, which often leave the public disengaged due to their complexity. Yet, despite its straightforwardness, the implications are anything but simple.

Tom Homan, a figure who has long courted controversy for his hardline stance on immigration, now stands accused of engaging in the very essence of corruption. The FBI's investigation into this matter, initiated in 2024 but prematurely closed by Trump administration officials citing a lack of "credible evidence," raises serious questions about the mechanisms of accountability within the administration. The closure of this investigation, devoid of any substantive exoneration, does little to dispel the cloud of suspicion that hangs over Homan. Instead, it further entrenches the narrative of a system rigged to protect its own, at the expense of ethical governance.

The Reaction: Righteous Indignation and a Call for Accountability

Sarah Longwell's fierce condemnation of the lack of accountability is not just a voice in the wilderness; it is a clarion call for those who stand against the normalization of corruption. Longwell's assertion that the "ecosystem around Trump sells influence" is a damning indictment of a culture where proximity to power is leveraged for personal gain, with little regard for the principles of democracy and justice.

Her remarks on Homan's role within the administration paint a picture of a man who not only embraced but celebrated policies of cruelty and inhumanity. This alleged willingness to accept bribes only adds another layer to the portrait of a figure driven not by a commitment to public service, but by self-interest and a disregard for the rule of law.

The Implications: A Reflection of Systemic Rot

What this scandal—and the reaction to it—highlights is not just the actions of one man, but the systemic rot that appears to have taken hold within the Trump administration. The normalization of such allegations, where they are merely seen as par for the course, is a bleak testament to the erosion of ethical standards at the highest levels of government.

The outcry from commentators like Longwell and Tim Miller is not just about the specifics of this case, but about what it represents: a broader culture of corruption, where influence is sold to the highest bidder, and accountability is an afterthought. This incident starkly illustrates the urgent need for a renewed commitment to integrity and transparency in governance.

Conclusion: The Road Ahead

As this saga unfolds, it serves as a critical reminder of the importance of vigilance in holding those in power accountable for their actions. The alleged bribery scandal involving Tom Homan is more than just a story of personal failings; it is a symptom of a deeper malaise that threatens the very foundations of our democratic institutions. It is incumbent upon us, as a society, to demand better, to refuse to become desensitized to corruption, and to insist on a governance that serves the people, rather than the vested interests of those at the top. Only through sustained scrutiny and a refusal to accept the unacceptable can we hope to stem the tide of corruption that threatens to engulf our political landscape.
