Jack Smith fought to have Aileen Cannon tossed from Trump case — but got silenced: report

Former special counsel Jack Smith wanted to file a motion to have far-right Trump appointed U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon disqualified from overseeing the Mar-a-Lago classified documents case against President Donald Trump, according to a new book by Carol D. Leonnig and Aaron C. Davis and profiled by The Washington Post.

But he hit a brick wall and felt unable to escalate the situation.

The book, “Injustice,” details the criminal cases against Trump.

“Smith said U.S. District Judge Aileen M. Cannon, a Trump appointee with limited experience, had ignored decades of precedent in dismissing the case outright and urged an appeals court to reverse her decision. He had also wanted to take an even more dramatic step ... by trying to get Cannon tossed off the case entirely,” said the report.

However, “But when a senior Justice Department official in the Biden administration rebuffed Smith’s desire to have Cannon pulled, the special counsel chose not to press his case with Attorney General Merrick Garland.”

According to the authors, Smith felt constrained because if Garland agreed with the lower official and refused to allow a motion to disqualify Cannon, “it would have to be reported to Congress” — presumably kicking off a political firestorm.

Ultimately, the appellate court never got a chance to rule on Cannon’s dismissal, which was based on a fringe Supreme Court concurrence by Justice Clarence Thomas, because with Trump retaking office following the election, the Justice Department followed its longstanding guidelines that a sitting president cannot be prosecuted and the case was dropped.

More recently, Smith has sought to testify on the details of the case before Congress, reportedly triggering fears among Republicans that Trump’s years of publicly attacking and smearing the prosecutors on the case could backfire.

Our Analysis:

The Cannon Conundrum: A Judicial Jigsaw in Trump’s Mar-a-Lago Case

In a narrative that sounds like it was ripped from the pages of a political thriller, the recent revelations from Carol D. Leonnig and Aaron C. Davis’ book, “Injustice,” lay bare the complex and controversial dynamics at play in the Mar-a-Lago classified documents case against President Donald Trump. The spotlight shines brightly on former special counsel Jack Smith and his thwarted efforts to navigate the judicial maze, with U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon, a figure emblematic of the Trump era, at the center.

The Smith Struggle: A Tale of Judicial Frustration

Jack Smith, a name synonymous with rigorous legal scrutiny, found himself at loggerheads with an unexpected adversary: the system itself. Appointed to oversee one of the most contentious and high-profile cases in recent American history, Smith’s path was anything but straightforward. His desire to file a motion to disqualify Judge Aileen Cannon — a Trump appointee with a scant judicial record — from the Mar-a-Lago documents case underscores the depth of the procedural and ethical quagmires he faced.

Smith’s rationale was clear: Cannon’s decision to dismiss the case, flouting decades of legal precedent, was a judicial aberration that demanded correction. Yet, his pursuit of justice hit a brick wall, not due to lack of merit, but because of internal resistance within the Justice Department and the potential political fallout that challenging a sitting judge’s impartiality might incite.

The Biden Administration’s Role: A Cautionary Stance or a Missed Opportunity?

The refusal of a senior Justice Department official in the Biden administration to support Smith’s bold move to disqualify Cannon is a pivotal moment in this saga. This decision — and Smith’s subsequent reluctance to escalate the matter to Attorney General Merrick Garland — speaks volumes about the intricate dance of legal strategy, political considerations, and the overarching goal of maintaining public trust in the judiciary.

One cannot help but ponder: Was this a case of prudent restraint or a missed opportunity to assert the integrity of the judicial process? The implications of this decision were far-reaching, essentially tying Smith’s hands and leaving Cannon’s initial ruling unchallenged at a critical juncture.

The Trump Factor: Political Dynamics and Judicial Decisions

The narrative takes an even more dramatic turn with the election of Donald Trump, leading to the Justice Department dropping the case based on longstanding guidelines against prosecuting a sitting president. This decision, while procedurally consistent, raises uncomfortable questions about the intersection of politics and the administration of justice.

Moreover, Smith’s recent push to testify before Congress about the case details — and the reported anxiety this has caused among Republicans — highlights the enduring impact of Trump’s relentless attacks on the judicial process and those who serve it. It underscores a worrying trend where political considerations may unduly influence the pursuit of justice.

Conclusion: The Unresolved Symphony of Justice

The saga of the Mar-a-Lago documents case, as detailed in “Injustice,” is a testament to the complex interplay between the judiciary, the executive, and the ever-looming specter of politics. Jack Smith’s thwarted efforts to disqualify Judge Aileen Cannon and the subsequent dropping of the case post-Trump’s election victory leave many questions unanswered.

This episode serves as a stark reminder of the challenges facing the American legal system in times of deep political division. It underscores the need for a judiciary that remains impervious to political pressures and committed to the principles of justice and impartiality. As this story unfolds, one thing is clear: The quest for justice in the shadow of politics is a treacherous path, fraught with obstacles, but essential for the health of democracy.
