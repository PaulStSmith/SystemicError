GOP is force-feeding Americans 'zombie food': NYT reporter

Dan Bongino, Kash Patel and Pam Bondi created their own trap, the New York Times reported. By promoting conspiracy theories and promising to fix them, they've backed themselves into a corner that they can't "fix" something that doesn't exist.

"There is a basic recognition among the president’s advisers and allies, at least in private, that expectations have been allowed to get too far out in front of the facts," said the report. "But bridging the gap between campaign messaging and reality is never easy, particularly in an administration that espoused maximalism, bravado and bending the truth to fit its tactical objectives."

Co-author Glenn Thrush told MSNBC on Monday that GOP officials and leaders make fun of MAGA fans for being easily distracted from issues like the economy by manufactured fights with the governor of California.

"Look, I'll put it in the bluntest possible terms: White House officials and congressional Republican staffers have an expression for it. It's called 'zombie food. Feeding the zombies,'" he said.

"The sense is that they have stoked up — Trump and company — have stoked up the far right with the [Jeffrey] Epstein conspiracy. The issue of the cocaine that was found outside of the Situation Room during the Biden administration, which has spawned a zillion conspiracies. And then something far more important, the Justice Department and FBI investigation into the discovery of pipe bombs on the eve of the January 6th Capitol attack at the RNC and the DNC."

Thrush said normal people likely see those reports as straightforward, but "for the extreme far right," it's a different matter. "Because it seems to implicate this unproven and frankly, somewhat delusional theory that there was a large government conspiracy involving whoever put the pipe bombs in, you know, that will somehow mitigate the fact that Trump supporters stormed the Capitol. There's zero evidence for that so far."

For people like Bongino, who is "terminally online" and "tweets constantly," the goal has been to go after all of these conspiracy theories.

"He is on Fox News perpetually," said Thrush. "He is tweeting out everything that he does, and one of the things that he seems to be taking on as his own responsibility is defending the administration on its record of sort of pursuing investigations in these conspiracy theories. The problem is, [there's] not a lot of evidence to support any of this stuff."

Thrush said that in the abstract, Trump would be held accountable for the MAGA anger with people like Patel, Bongino, and Bondi.

"In reality, this is the way the whole system is geared," he explained. "These guys have adopted an employee model. They are not really appointees. Senate confirmed appointees, at least Bondi and Patel, in the conventional sense, they're behaving as employees, which is what Trump wants. And that entails basically doing what Trump wants to do, absorbing all the incoming and shutting up about it. This is the deal that these guys struck."

Our Analysis:

The Great MAGA Mirage: Trump's Team and the Conspiracy Cult

The latest dispatch from the Trump administration's inner sanctum offers a grim view of a political machine that thrives on the creation and perpetuation of conspiracy theories. The New York Times report, dissected with razor-sharp insight by Glenn Thrush, lays bare the mechanics of a strategy that could be aptly termed as feeding the zombies. This isn't just a tale of political maneuvering gone awry; it's a cautionary narrative about the dangers of blurring the lines between reality and fabrication for political gain.

The Architects of Illusion

Dan Bongino, Kash Patel, and Pam Bondi have emerged not just as Trump's lieutenants but as the chief architects of a self-imposed quagmire. By hitching their wagon to conspiracy theories, they have ventured past the point of no return. The notion that they can "fix" the inexistent problems they've promulgated is as ludicrous as it is impossible. Here lies the crux of the issue: You cannot solve a problem that doesn't exist.

The MAGA Expectation Gap

The report highlights a fundamental disconnect between the administration's campaign bravado and the stark terrain of reality—a chasm that has evidently become too vast to bridge. This isn't merely a matter of overenthusiastic campaign rhetoric; it's a deliberate strategy of maximalism and distortion, designed to reshape reality to fit tactical objectives. The administration's reliance on bending the truth has not only set unrealistic expectations but has also fostered a toxic political environment where facts are malleable.

Zombie Food: Distract and Devour

The term "zombie food," as Thrush reports, encapsulates the administration's strategy of distraction and manipulation. By igniting the flames of the far right with conspiracy theories—be it the Epstein saga, the cocaine discovery saga, or the pipe bomb investigation—Trump and his cohorts have diverted attention from substantive issues like the economy. The targeting of these manufactured controversies is a calculated move to feed the base's insatiable appetite for conspiracy, keeping them engaged and enraged.

The Delusion of Accountability

Perhaps one of the most damning insights from Thrush's analysis is the acknowledgment that, in theory, Trump should be held accountable for the MAGA anger fueled by figures like Bongino, Patel, and Bondi. However, the reality is starkly different. The system, as it stands, rewards compliance and silence over truth and accountability. These individuals, operating more as Trump's employees rather than independent appointees, embody the transactional nature of the Trump administration—a quid pro quo of loyalty for protection, regardless of the cost to democratic norms.

Conclusion: The Mirage of Truth

This report is not just an indictment of a few individuals but a scathing critique of an administration that has weaponized conspiracy theories for political gain. The Trump team's strategy of creating and perpetuating baseless claims has not only trapped them in their own web of deceit but has also eroded the foundational pillars of truth and accountability. As the administration continues to feed the zombies of the far right, one must wonder at what point the diet of distractions and distortions will no longer satiate the appetite for reality. The great MAGA mirage, built on the sands of falsehoods, is bound to collapse under the weight of truth—leaving its architects and adherents stranded in a desert of delusion.
