Trump's 'verbal vomit' to deployed troops leaves veterans disgusted

Based on an article by Frank Yemi

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A holiday message about replaceabilityWar reduced to presidential whimThe market as applause meter

Donald Trump turned a Thanksgiving call with deployed U.S. service members into a celebration of stock prices, a warning that a Marine could be readily replaced, and a claim that war would end whenever he chose. The remarks drew disgust from veterans who heard the president treating troops as an audience for his branding rather than people spending the holiday away from home.

Calling Thursday night from Mar-a-Lago with first lady Melania Trump beside him, Trump was asked what made him most thankful. According to The Independent’s reporting, he began with his campaign slogan and then pointed to the market.

“As of the election, we have created a value in terms of stock markets — we have the highest stock markets in history,” Trump said.

The answer established the call’s priorities. Deployed personnel and their families were present, but Trump’s gratitude centered on a financial benchmark he could claim as a political achievement.

A holiday message about replaceability

Coast Guard veteran Evaristus Odinikaeze identified the omission: “Not the troops on the call. Not their families keeping an empty seat at the table. Not the wife sitting right next to him.”

Odinikaeze, who said he had spent holidays on duty, described what deployed personnel needed to hear instead: “that someone back home sees you.”

Trump offered Marine Lt. Col. Matthew S. Andrews a different message. After boasting about military recruitment while Andrews was deployed at Al Dhafra Air Base in the United Arab Emirates, the president converted those numbers into a joke about the officer’s disposability.

“In other words, what I’m saying to you, Matthew, is that if you ever leave, we’re going to replace you very easily, so don’t leave, Matthew, OK? Don’t leave,” Trump said.

“Thank you, Mr. President,” Andrews replied. “I appreciate it.”

That exchange was structurally unequal: a commander in chief speaking publicly to a deployed officer, who answered with the deference his position demanded. Treating Andrews’ response as evidence that the remark landed harmlessly would ignore who possessed institutional power in the conversation.

Army veteran Chad Scott was less constrained. He called Trump’s performance “an endless stream of verbal vomit,” adding that the president had been given the elementary task of saying something kind about troops on Thanksgiving and failed to do it.

War reduced to presidential whim

Trump’s most consequential remark concerned the Iran war. After asking a fighter pilot whether he would trade lives with him, Trump said the deployment would continue because “we have victory at hand.”

“It’s going to be — whenever sort of — whenever I want it to be, to be honest,” he added.

The phrasing presented the duration of war as an extension of presidential preference. Whatever authority a president exercises as commander in chief, troops absorb the consequences through deployments, separation and physical danger. Trump’s formulation erased every constraint and every person between his will and their exposure.

Even a service member’s question about Thanksgiving dessert became an opportunity to attack Joe Biden. Trump dismissed it as “a Joe Biden question,” complained that Biden had supposedly received easy questions about ice cream, and only then answered: pumpkin pie with vanilla ice cream.

The reflex was revealing. A ceremonial call designed to recognize military service repeatedly became a vehicle for campaign slogans, market claims, personnel boasts and complaints about a political rival.

The market as applause meter

Trump’s invocation of record stock prices also followed a sharply different market message the previous day. He had complained on social media that stocks were “down sharply because of the November Third Election results,” arguing that markets and retirement accounts would have been “BOOMING” had he been on the ballot.

Taken together, the statements show Trump using the market as a personal political scoreboard: credit belongs to him when prices rise, while voters and election results take the blame when they fall. On Thanksgiving, deployed troops became the captive audience for that self-assessment.

Democratic influencer Harry Sisson accused Trump of spending the holiday “rambling on social media” at Mar-a-Lago before telling service members that the stock market was what made him most thankful. But the veterans’ criticism carried a more specific indictment. They were not objecting merely to an awkward joke or poor holiday manners. They were responding to a commander in chief who spoke to deployed personnel about their replaceability, described war’s timetable as his personal choice, and reserved his clearest gratitude for an asset market.
