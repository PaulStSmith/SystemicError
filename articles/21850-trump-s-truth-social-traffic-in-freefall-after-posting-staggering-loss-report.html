Trump's Truth Social traffic in freefall after posting staggering loss: report

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

Truth Social Is Not a Platform. It Is a Grift With a Login Screen.

The Collapse Is the Story

Trump Media is now staring at the basic math it spent years trying to outrun: traffic is falling, revenue is tiny, losses are enormous, and the stock has been shredded. Monthly visitors to Truth Social are down about 36 percent from a year ago, the company reported a $406 million first-quarter loss on just $871,000 in revenue, and the stock has fallen roughly 80 percent since the company went public in March 2024.

That is not a temporary wobble. It is the market finally meeting the product.

Power Runs Through Trump

The real institutional power here does not sit with the platform’s users, its tiny staff, or even its executives. It sits with Donald Trump, whose posts are the company’s core asset and whose political brand is the only reason the company still exists as a public story at all. Truth Social is built to monetize proximity to presidential power, not to build a durable social network.

That matters because the company is not being punished for obscurity. It is failing despite constant attention. Trump’s loudest megaphone could not create a real audience, because outrage is not the same thing as retention, and personality cults are not business models.

The Real Product Is Access

The most revealing detail in the reporting is not the traffic decline. It is the new premium service selling Wall Street traders instantaneous access to Trump’s posts, priced at up to $100,000 a month for companies that want the edge.

That is the company telling the truth about itself. It is not selling communication. It is selling asymmetric access to political noise as a financial instrument. The pitch is naked: pay for first look at Trump’s messages so you can trade before everyone else reacts. This is not innovation. It is influence laundering itself through a subscription plan.

Only ten firms have signed up so far. That should tell you the market understands exactly what this is.

The Executives Are Not Victims

The executives at Trump Media now “have some explaining to do,” but the blame should not be distributed upward into vague corporate fog. The decisions were made. The company was launched, the brand was attached to a sitting president, the platform was marketed as a political weapon, and a premium trading product was introduced anyway.

Devin Nunes getting forced out as CEO does not solve anything. It just marks one more rotation in a structure where loyalty is rewarded until the numbers become impossible to ignore. The problem is not one bad manager. The problem is a company built around a single man’s political mythology, then floated in public markets as if that mythology were a viable business foundation.

The Misdirection Is the Point

Stories like this often get framed as a curiosity about Trump’s media habits or the volatility of a social platform. That framing is too small. The issue is not whether Truth Social can compete with X or Threads. The issue is that a former president turned a communications channel into a speculative asset, then tried to sell investors and traders a direct line into his own political output.

That is a power arrangement, not a tech story. The platform gives Trump leverage, the company tries to cash in on that leverage, and outside investors are left holding the risk while the brand owner keeps the influence. When the traffic collapses, the article’s numbers show the obvious result: the aura was never the asset. It was the cover.

The Pattern Is Familiar

This is what authoritarian-adjacent political business looks like in practice: private enrichment wrapped in public grievance, institutional failure sold as populist strength, and personal power converted into financial speculation. The company’s losses, the stock collapse, and the weak subscriber response are not side notes. They are the evidence that the model depends on hype more than substance.

Trump Media is not dying because the internet moved on. It is struggling because it was always organized around one unstable premise: that loyalty to Trump could be monetized indefinitely. The numbers say otherwise. The system works for a while, until the bill arrives.
