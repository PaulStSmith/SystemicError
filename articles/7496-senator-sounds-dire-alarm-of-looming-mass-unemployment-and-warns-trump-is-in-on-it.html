Senator sounds dire alarm of looming 'mass unemployment' — and warns Trump is in on it

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 19, 20254 min read

WASHINGTON — Outspoken Vermont Sen. Bernie Sanders raised dire concerns Wednesday about the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence and robotics, warning that the United States is unprepared for the economic disaster that such technologies will bring.

In comments to Raw Story, Sanders cited major tech figures such as Elon Musk in noting that industry leaders openly predict an ominous future in which traditional work becomes obsolete. According to Sanders, the U.S. faces the prospect of widespread unemployment, particularly among young people already grappling with a dearth of entry‑level jobs.

“He tells us that the concept of work itself, your job, may be obsolete. That means mass unemployment,” Sanders warned. “Is Congress dealing with that issue?”

Sanders emphasized that while AI offers potential benefits, the nation must ensure that tech serves the broader public rather than a tiny group of billionaires. To that end, Sanders demanded a temporary “moratorium” on new data centers until lawmakers can figure out how to integrate AI responsibly and protect workers from economic ruin.

The senator also cast doubt on the motivations of tech elites, including Musk, Larry Ellison, Jeff Bezos, and Mark Zuckerberg, suggesting that their priorities don’t align with the needs of the working class.

He called President Donald Trump an “oligarch” who is “working with other oligarchs.”

“Do you think he’s staying up nights worrying about the working class of this country? I don’t think so,” said Sanders.

Our Analysis:

Bernie Sanders: The Canary in the Technological Coal Mine

In a recent alarm-sounding declaration, Vermont Senator Bernie Sanders has positioned himself as a veritable Paul Revere of the impending artificial intelligence (AI) and robotics revolution. Unlike Revere’s midnight ride, Sanders’ warning gallops through the digital age, cautioning against a future where the concept of work is as outdated as the fax machine. His comments to Raw Story underscore a grim forecast for the American workforce, invoking the specter of an economic apocalypse heralded by the rapid expansion of AI.

Sanders’ Dire Warning: A Future of Mass Unemployment

The senator pulls no punches in describing a dystopian future where machines usurp human roles, rendering traditional employment a relic of the past. Citing tech luminaries like Elon Musk, Sanders paints a picture of an America unprepared for the seismic shifts AI and robotics promise to deliver. The specter of “mass unemployment” looms large, especially for the youth already navigating a scarcity of entry-level positions. Sanders’ question, “Is Congress dealing with that issue?” slices through the legislative inertia with the sharpness of Occam’s razor. The implication? A resounding, deafening silence.

AI: A Double-Edged Sword

Sanders acknowledges the Janus-faced nature of AI, conceding its potential to usher in unparalleled benefits. Yet, his call for a “moratorium” on new data centers until a responsible integration strategy is devised speaks volumes of his concerns over AI’s trajectory. It’s a clarion call for a pause, a moment of reflection to ensure that technology serves the many, not the few. Sanders’ demand is not rooted in Luddite fearmongering but in a pragmatic understanding of technology’s potential to exacerbate economic inequality.

The Oligarchs vs. The Working Class

The senator’s critique extends beyond the disembodied realm of AI, casting a wary eye on the human architects of our digital future. His skepticism of tech elites’ motivations and priorities is scathing. Sanders’ portrayal of figures like Musk, Ellison, Bezos, and Zuckerberg as out of touch with the needs of the working class is not just a critique; it’s an indictment of a system that privileges billionaire whims over worker well-being. The mention of President Trump as an “oligarch” working in concert with others adds a layer of political intrigue and underscores Sanders’ narrative of a government more beholden to wealth than to the welfare of its people.

Conclusion: A Looming Economic Precipice

In essence, Sanders’ comments to Raw Story are a stark reminder of the challenges and choices facing America in the age of AI. His warning is not just about technology; it’s about the societal and economic fabric of the nation. The senator’s call for action, for a pause, and for reflection, is a plea to redirect the course of progress so that it benefits all, not just an elite few. As the technological tide continues to rise, Sanders stands on the shore, urging a reevaluation of our priorities before we are swept away by the current. His message is clear: the future of work, and the very concept of employment, hangs in the balance. Will Congress heed the warning, or will it be drowned out by the relentless march of innovation? Only time will tell, but for now, Sanders’ voice is a lone, urgent cry in the wilderness of technological advancement.
