Pete Hegseth menaces CNN over its Iran reporting during Pentagon press bash-fest

Pete Hegseth (Photo Via Reuters)

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth interrupted his Pentagon press briefing on Friday morning to bash the press, taking particular aim at CNN and urging an ownership change.

After opening by complaining the press was not willing to “admit” the success of his Iran war planning, he later added, “They see headlines. I used to be in that business, and I know that everything is written intentionally. For example, a banner or a headline ‘Mideast War Intensifies,’ splashing on the screen the last couple of days, alongside visuals of civilian or energy targets that Iran has hit. Because that’s what they do.”

“What should the banner read instead? How about Iran ‘increasingly desperate’ because they are,” he lectured. “They know it, and so do you ... more fake news from CNN reports that the Trump administration underestimated the Iran war’s impact on the Strait of Hormuz — patently ridiculous, of course. For decades, Iran has threatened shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. This is always what they do hold —the strait hostage.”

“CNN doesn’t think we thought of that,” he accused. “It’s a fundamentally unserious report. The sooner [Paramount’s] David Ellison takes over that network, the better. Another example of a fake headline that I saw yesterday: ‘War widening.’ Here’s a real headline for you, for an actual patriotic press. How about ‘Iran shrinking, going underground?’”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s Critique of the Press

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s recent outburst against the press, particularly CNN, during a Pentagon press briefing raises several critical points of analysis regarding institutional power, decision-making, and the framing of responsibility.

Institutional Power

Firstly, it’s essential to identify who holds institutional power in this scenario. As the Defense Secretary, Pete Hegseth wields significant influence over military policy, strategy, and operations. His role places him within the upper echelons of the U.S. government’s power structure, particularly in matters of national defense and military engagement. On the other side, the press, including CNN, holds a different kind of power: the power to inform the public and hold those in authority accountable through reporting and analysis.

Decision-Making

The decision to criticize the press publicly, especially in an official briefing intended to inform about military matters, was made by Hegseth himself. This choice to deviate from the briefing’s primary purpose to launch an attack on the press’s credibility and suggest an ownership change for CNN highlights a strategic decision to challenge and discredit media narratives directly.

Framing of Responsibility

Hegseth’s criticism implies that the press, and CNN in particular, is responsible for misleading the public about the Iran war’s dynamics and impacts. By suggesting alternative headlines, he argues that the media’s framing misdirects responsibility and fails to acknowledge the supposed success of his Iran war planning and the strategic considerations of the U.S. government regarding the Strait of Hormuz.

However, this critique seems to misdirect responsibility in two key ways:

  1. Misdirection Towards Actors with Limited Control: Hegseth’s attack on the press suggests that the media possesses more influence over public perception and policy outcomes than it actually does. While the press plays a crucial role in shaping public discourse, it does not make policy decisions or engage in military strategy. Thus, blaming the press for perceived inaccuracies or biases in war reporting overlooks the primary responsibility of government officials and military leaders to conduct and communicate their strategies effectively.

  2. False Equivalence and Scapegoating: By focusing on the press’s role and suggesting ownership changes as a solution, Hegseth creates a false equivalence between media reporting and government accountability. This approach serves as a convenient scapegoat, diverting attention from the administration’s actions and decisions regarding the Iran conflict.

Conclusion

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s remarks represent an attempt to control the narrative surrounding the Iran conflict by discrediting the press. While criticism of media reporting can be valid, it is crucial to distinguish between constructive critique and efforts to undermine the press’s role in a democratic society. In this instance, Hegseth’s comments reflect a misdirection of responsibility and a misunderstanding of the press’s role vis-à-vis government power and decision-making. The primary focus should remain on the decisions and actions of those in positions of power, such as Hegseth himself, rather than on those reporting on these actions.
