Backfiring meme attack sees Republican demonize own GOP allies: 'Who do they represent?'

Arizona Sen. Vince Leach, a Tucson Republican, shared a meme on Facebook decrying foreign-born politicians — even though members of his own caucus were born outside the country.

“There are 32 foreign-born members of Congress!” the meme says, alongside an image of Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar, long a target of Islamophobic attacks. “Who do they really represent?”

Leach posted the image to both his personal and candidate Facebook pages along with the caption “Question of the day.” Leach did not respond to repeated requests for comment.

He’s correct that 32 members of Congress were born outside of the United States — nine Republicans and 23 Democrats. One of them is Arizona Republican Rep. Juan Ciscomani who was born in Mexico, and became a citizen through the naturalization process.

Ciscomani did not respond to a request for comment from the Arizona Mirror.

“As someone born abroad while my father proudly served in the U.S. Air Force, I find Senator Leach’s post surprising and disappointing,” Arizona Senate Minority Whip Rosanna Gabaldón told the Mirror. “Implying that foreign-born Americans are somehow less capable of representing their communities undermines the very principles of service, equality and democracy that our country is built on.”

Gabaldón, who was born in Bermuda, is one of six Arizona state lawmakers who are foreign born. Four of the six are Republicans.

“Arizona is made stronger by its diversity, and our leaders should focus on bringing people together, not questioning the patriotism or legitimacy of their colleagues based on birthplace,” Gabaldón said. “I stand proudly with all who serve honorably, regardless of where they were born.”

Speaker of the House Steve Montenegro, who was born in El Salvador, did not respond to the Mirror’s request for comment about the meme that Leach shared.

Other notable politicians born outside the country include Texas Republican Ted Cruz, Illinois Democrat Tammy Duckworth, Texas Republican Dan Crenshaw and Nevada Gov. Joe Lombardo.

The image elicited a number of xenophobic and Islamophobic comments.

“ALL FOLLOWING 9-11,” one commenter claimed, wrongly alleging that all the foreign-born members of Congress were elected after the tragic events of Sept. 11, 2001.

The countries where the largest number of current members of Congress were born are India, Mexico and West Germany.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Vince Leach's Facebook Fiasco

In a display that can only be described as a masterclass in hypocrisy and xenophobia, Arizona Senator Vince Leach, a Tucson Republican, decided to stir the pot of division with a meme that questions the loyalty of foreign-born members of Congress. With his target locked on Minnesota Democrat Ilhan Omar, Leach not only indulged in Islamophobic undertones but also blatantly ignored the diversity within his own party ranks. 32 members of Congress were born outside of the United States, including nine Republicans, making Leach's insinuation not just offensive but laughably ignorant.

The Irony of the "Question of the Day"

Leach's attempt to cast doubt on the loyalty of foreign-born politicians is not just a low blow in political discourse; it's an ironic misfire. Among those he indirectly questions is Arizona Republican Rep. Juan Ciscomani, born in Mexico and a beacon of the American dream through his naturalization story. Ciscomani's silence on the matter speaks volumes, underscoring the discomfort and division such rhetoric fosters within the Republican ranks.

The Voices of Reason

Arizona Senate Minority Whip Rosanna Gabaldón's response to Leach's post was both dignified and damning. Born in Bermuda to a family serving the U.S., Gabaldón highlighted the fundamental flaw in Leach’s logic: questioning the patriotism of foreign-born Americans undermines the principles of service, equality, and democracy. Gabaldón, alongside other foreign-born state lawmakers, stands as a testament to the strength diversity brings to the American political landscape. Her condemnation of Leach's insinuations is a timely reminder of what true service to the country looks like.

The Broader Picture of Diversity

Foreign-born politicians are not a new or unusual phenomenon in American politics. Notable figures include Texas Republican Ted Cruz, Illinois Democrat Tammy Duckworth, and Nevada Governor Joe Lombardo, all of whom bring unique perspectives and strengths to their roles. The xenophobic and Islamophobic comments that Leach's post elicited, including baseless claims linking the election of foreign-born members to 9/11, only serve to illustrate the dangerous undercurrents of bigotry and misinformation that leaders like Leach, wittingly or unwittingly, feed into.

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity for Unity

In a country as diverse as the United States, the representation of foreign-born citizens in Congress should be celebrated as a strength, not maligned as a weakness. Senator Vince Leach's decision to question the loyalty and representation of these members is not only a disservice to his constituents but a glaring example of the systemic issues plaguing our political discourse. Arizona, and indeed America, deserves better from its leaders. Instead of sowing division, our focus should be on bridging differences to foster a more inclusive and united society. Leach's post was a missed opportunity to do just that, and it is incumbent upon us all to demand more from those who represent us.
