Trump turns on Federalist Society and 'sleazebag' Leonard Leo in anti-judge rant

President Donald Trump finally issued a public statement on Truth Social about the lawsuits seeking to strike down his "reciprocal tariffs" on Thursday evening — not just attacking the judiciary but specifically going after the Federalist Society, blaming it for influencing the judges who ruled against him.

Trump's tariffs were ruled unlawful by the U.S. Court of International Trade on Wednesday, and while that ruling is currently on hold due to an appellate court stepping in, some legal experts believe conservatives on the judiciary will ultimately not bail Trump out.

"Where do these initial three Judges come from?" wrote Trump. "How is it possible for them to have potentially done such damage to the United States of America? Is it purely a hatred of 'TRUMP?' What other reason could it be? I was new to Washington, and it was suggested that I use The Federalist Society as a recommending source on Judges. I did so, openly and freely, but then realized that they were under the thumb of a real 'sleazebag' named Leonard Leo, a bad person who, in his own way, probably hates America, and obviously has his own separate ambitions. He openly brags how he controls Judges, and even Justices of the United States Supreme Court — I hope that is not so, and don’t believe it is! In any event, Leo left The Federalist Society to do his own 'thing.'"

The Federalist Society is a notorious right-wing legal group that has established a "pipeline" for conservative lawyers to the federal bench. Trump relied extensively on advice from the group, and Leo, in his prolific judicial appointments in his first term, but in his second term he has focused more on nominating personal loyalists to judgeships, like his former attorney Emil Bove.

"I am so disappointed in The Federalist Society because of the bad advice they gave me on numerous Judicial Nominations," Trump continued. "This is something that cannot be forgotten! With all of that being said, I am very proud of many of our picks, but very disappointed in others. They always must do what’s right for the Country!"

He then went on to rage about how unfair it was that he might have tariff powers taken away from him.

"Backroom 'hustlers' must not be allowed to destroy our Nation!" wrote Trump. "The horrific decision stated that I would have to get the approval of Congress for these Tariffs. In other words, hundreds of politicians would sit around D.C. for weeks, and even months, trying to come to a conclusion as to what to charge other Countries that are treating us unfairly. If allowed to stand, this would completely destroy Presidential Power — The Presidency would never be the same! This decision is being hailed all over the World by every Country, other than the United States of America. Radical Left Judges, together with some very bad people, are destroying America."

"Under this decision, Trillions of Dollars would be lost by our Country, money that will, MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN," he added. "It would be the harshest financial ruling ever leveled on us as a Sovereign Nation. The President of the United States must be allowed to protect America against those that are doing it Economic and Financial harm. Thank you for your attention to this matter!"

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Tirade on Tariffs and the Judiciary

In a recent outburst on Truth Social, former President Donald Trump launched a scathing attack against the Federalist Society, the judiciary, and what he perceives as an assault on his presidential powers. His diatribe, sparked by legal challenges to his "reciprocal tariffs," reveals not only his disdain for dissent but also his fundamental misunderstanding of the American legal and political system.

Trump's Misguided Missile at the Judiciary

Trump's rhetorical question, "Where do these initial three Judges come from?" underscores a profound ignorance or deliberate obfuscation of the judicial appointment process—a process he once exploited with glee. His insinuation that the judges' ruling against his tariffs is driven by personal animus rather than legal reasoning is not just baseless but dangerous, undermining public trust in the judiciary's independence.

The Federalist Society Fallacy

Trump's vilification of the Federalist Society and Leonard Leo, a figure instrumental in shaping the judiciary during Trump's tenure, is laced with irony and ingratitude. After heavily relying on the Society's recommendations to appoint conservative judges, Trump now bemoans their influence because it no longer serves his interests. His ad hominem attacks on Leo as a "sleazebag" who "probably hates America" are not just unpresidential; they reflect a petulant refusal to accept any opposition.

The Tariff Tantrum

Trump's rage over the potential loss of unilateral tariff powers is emblematic of his broader disregard for constitutional checks and balances. His claim that needing Congress's approval for tariffs would "destroy Presidential Power" reveals a desire for autocratic authority, not democratic governance. Furthermore, his assertion that the ruling would be "the harshest financial ruling ever leveled on us as a Sovereign Nation" is hyperbolic and unfounded. The U.S. economy is not, despite Trump's claims, at the mercy of his tariff whims.

Misconstruing Economic Patriotism

Trump's final plea, that the President must be allowed to "protect America against those that are doing it Economic and Financial harm," conflates his personal policy preferences with national security. His use of the phrase "MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN" in this context is not just a slogan but a dog whistle, implying that any opposition to his policies is unpatriotic.

Conclusion: Trump's Dangerous Demagoguery

Trump's statement is a potent mix of misinformation, narcissism, and an alarming disdain for the democratic process. His attacks on the judiciary and the Federalist Society are not just the ramblings of a disgruntled former president; they are indicative of a broader trend of undermining institutions and norms that hold the fabric of American democracy together. This latest tirade is a reminder of the dangers posed by Trump's brand of politics—a blend of personal vendettas and populist rhetoric that threatens the very foundations of the republic.
