Donald Trump on Saturday ignored his feud with Jimmy Kimmel and focused in on another late-night TV host, deeming coverage critical of the president as “probably illegal,” without evidence.

Trump over the weekend took to his own social media site, Truth Social, to attack Seth Meyers.

“Seth Meyers of NBC may be the least talented person to ‘perform’ live in the history of television,” the president said. “In fact, he may be the WORST to perform, live or otherwise.”

Trump continued:

“I watched his show the other night for the first time in years. In it he talked endlessly about electric catapults on aircraft carriers which I complain about as not being as good as much less expensive steam catapults. On and on he went, a truly deranged lunatic.”

Trump then asked, “Why does NBC waste its time and money on a guy like this???” before making a claim about unlawful conduct.

“NO TALENT, NO RATINGS, 100% ANTI TRUMP, WHICH IS PROBABLY ILLEGAL!!!” according to the president.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Attack on Seth Meyers: The Merging of Media Critique and Legal Fantasy

In an era where political discourse frequently descends into the realm of the absurd, former President Donald Trump’s recent tirade against late-night host Seth Meyers stands as a prime example of this unsettling trend. Trump, taking a break from his ongoing feud with Jimmy Kimmel, directed his ire towards Meyers in a post on his social media site, Truth Social. What emerges from Trump’s statements is not just a personal vendetta, but a disturbing conflation of criticism with illegality, devoid of any factual basis.

Trump’s Claims: A Blend of Personal Disdain and Legal Gibberish

Trump’s attack begins with a subjective assessment, declaring Meyers as possibly “the least talented person to ‘perform’ live in the history of television.” This hyperbolic statement sets the tone for what follows: a blend of personal animosity and unfounded legal postulations. Trump criticizes Meyers for discussing electric catapults on aircraft carriers, a seemingly random topic that Trump himself has opinions on. He labels Meyers a “truly deranged lunatic” for his coverage, a personal attack that underscores Trump’s inability to distinguish between critique and insanity.

The climax of Trump’s tirade is his assertion that Meyers’ anti-Trump stance is “probably illegal.” This claim is not only baseless but also reveals a profound misunderstanding (or deliberate misrepresentation) of the law and the principles of freedom of speech. Trump’s assertion that negative coverage could be considered illegal is a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism, suggesting that critique of his policies or persona should potentially be met with legal repercussions.

The Misuse of ‘Illegal’

Trump’s casual use of “probably illegal” to describe Meyers’ content is emblematic of a broader issue: the weaponization of legal language to intimidate and silence critics. This tactic, familiar in autocratic regimes, has no place in democratic discourse. The implication that the legal system could be manipulated to punish dissenting voices is not only misleading but also undermines the foundational principles of free expression.

NBC’s Role and Trump’s Obsession with Ratings

Trump’s questioning of NBC’s decision to retain Meyers, citing “NO TALENT, NO RATINGS,” highlights another dimension of Trump’s critique: an obsession with television ratings as a measure of worth. This perspective is indicative of Trump’s broader view of media - not as a vehicle for information or diverse viewpoints, but as a popularity contest where the highest numbers justify existence. This reductionist view ignores the critical role of media in challenging power structures and fostering public discourse.

Conclusion: A Disturbing Pattern

Trump’s attack on Seth Meyers is not an isolated incident but part of a disturbing pattern of attempting to delegitimize any form of criticism or opposition. By labeling negative coverage as “probably illegal,” Trump not only misunderstands the law but also seeks to chill free speech. It’s a tactic that should concern anyone who values democratic principles and the role of the media in holding power to account.

In the end, Trump’s statements offer a case study in the dangers of conflating personal vendettas with public discourse, especially when those grievances are aired by individuals in positions of significant power. The critical task is to discern between legitimate legal issues and the misuse of legal terminology for intimidation, a distinction that Trump’s recent comments blur with alarming casualness.
