‘Frightening escalation:’ Experts ‘unnerved’ by leaked memo on military use in US

A leaked Department of Homeland Security memo reveals advanced plans to expand military involvement within the U.S., which has stunned a number of policy experts, The New Republic reported on Saturday.

“The memo is alarming, because it speaks to the intent to use the military within the United States at a level not seen since Japanese internment,” said Carrie Lee, a senior fellow at the policy think tank German Marshall Fund, speaking with TNR. “The military is the most powerful, coercive tool our country has. We don’t want the military doing law enforcement. It absolutely undermines the rule of law.”

Obtained by TNR, the leaked memo details a high-level meeting between leadership at DHS and the Department of Defense focused on expanding coordination between the two agencies on immigration enforcement. It was attended by DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, and Defense Secretary Secretary Pete Hegseth, along with other top leaders at the two agencies.

According to TNR the memo "has unnerved experts who believe it portends a frightening escalation."

Agency leaders cited recent military immigration enforcement operations in Los Angeles, California and the California-Mexico border that involved the deployment of thousands of troops as models for future cooperation, suggesting an increase in future military deployments to urban centers may be on the horizon.

“It’s disturbing to see DHS officials pressuring the U.S. military to turn its focus inward even further,” said Joseph Nunn, counsel in the Brennan Center's Liberty and National Security Program, speaking with TNR.

Specifically, agency leaders named the recent operation in Los Angeles as “a good indicator of the type of operations (and resistance) we’re going to be working through for years to come.” That operation saw around 5,000 members of the National Guard deployed to downtown Los Angeles to quell protests sparked by immigration raids, and to protect immigration enforcement officers.

Agency leaders also assessed the threat of illegal immigration to be “on the same plain” as groups like Al Qaeda and ISIS according to the memo, naming immigration crackdown efforts as “a priority” for President Donald Trump.

“The conflation of a low-level threat like transnational criminal organizations with Al Qaeda, which was actually attempting to topple the United States government, is a clear attempt to use excessive force for a purpose normally handled by civil authorities,” said Lindsay Cohn, associate professor at the U.S. Naval War College, speaking with TNR.

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Unthinkable: A Deep Dive into the DHS Memo Leak

The recent leak from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) detailing advanced plans to escalate military involvement within the United States borders on the dystopian. As reported by The New Republic, this memo not only shocks but serves as a dire warning of the lengths to which the current administration might go to enforce its hardline immigration policies. Let's dissect this alarming development with the precision it demands.

A Historical Echo Too Loud to Ignore

Carrie Lee from the German Marshall Fund draws a chilling parallel between the proposed military involvement and a dark chapter in American history - Japanese internment during World War II. This comparison is not made lightly. The use of the military as a domestic law enforcement tool is an affront to the foundational principles of civilian oversight and the rule of law. The memo, as Lee rightly points out, signals a potential shift towards a form of governance that the United States has long criticized in other nations.

The Unsettling Details

The specifics of the memo, as outlined in the article, are nothing short of disturbing. High-level discussions between DHS and the Department of Defense, attended by notable figures such as DHS Secretary Kristi Noem and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, centered on "expanding coordination" for immigration enforcement. This isn't just about bureaucratic collaboration; it's a blueprint for militarizing responses to what are essentially civil issues.

Furthermore, the article mentions previous military immigration enforcement operations in Los Angeles and at the California-Mexico border as models for future actions. The deployment of 5,000 National Guard members to Los Angeles to suppress protests sparked by immigration raids is a stark illustration of this strategy in action. It's a scenario that reads more like a military coup in a fragile state than a policy decision in one of the world's oldest democracies.

A Conflation of Threats

Perhaps one of the most egregious aspects of this memo is the comparison of illegal immigration to the threats posed by Al Qaeda and ISIS. This conflation, as highlighted by Lindsay Cohn of the U.S. Naval War College, represents a dangerous and misguided escalation of rhetoric. Transnational criminal organizations, while a concern, do not equate to the global and existential threats posed by terrorist organizations intent on the destruction of the United States. This rhetoric is not just fearmongering; it's a deliberate tactic to justify the use of excessive force against a vulnerable population.

The Bigger Picture

The implications of this memo extend far beyond immigration policy. It signals a potential erosion of civil liberties, an undermining of the rule of law, and a shift towards a more authoritarian approach to governance. Joseph Nunn of the Brennan Center's Liberty and National Security Program voices a concern many share: the inward turn of the U.S. military’s focus under pressure from DHS officials. This is not merely a policy shift; it's a fundamental transformation of the role of the military within American society.

In Conclusion

The leaked DHS memo is a harbinger of a deeply troubling future, one in which military force is normalized as a tool for domestic policy enforcement. This is not the first time a government has attempted to wield its military against its own people, but it is certainly a moment for the United States to reflect on the path it is choosing. The memo is not just alarming; it's a wake-up call for all who value democracy, civil liberties, and the rule of law. The question now is not only how did we get here, but more importantly, how do we navigate our way back?
