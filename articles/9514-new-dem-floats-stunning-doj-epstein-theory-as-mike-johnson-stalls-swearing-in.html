New Dem floats stunning DOJ-Epstein theory as Mike Johnson stalls swearing in

Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ) questioned what the Justice Department might be doing with the Jeffrey Epstein files while Speaker Mike Johnson refuses to swear her into the post.

Johnson told Fox News that Grijalva has keys to her office and can be doing her job and taking calls, prefacing it with the southern phrase, “bless her heart,” and saying “she doesn’t know how it works” in Congress.

Grijalva’s father was the previous holder of the seat.

MSNBC host Alex Witt asked Grijalva, “You know what you’re doing, right? ... The bless your heart part. I mean, it could be taken seriously or kind of condescendingly. What do you say?”

“No, it’s totally condescending, patronizing, misogynistic, and really telling of where he is. I mean, I think that people can see what’s happening here, and his push to obstruct me getting sworn in and why he’s doing that is because I will be the 218th signer to the Epstein files,” said Grijalva.

There is a discharge petition, a measure that requires 218 signatures to mandate that the Justice Department release all investigatory documents from the probe and indictment of child sex offenderJeffrey Epstein, accused of trafficking minors and more.

“And this is a delay tactic,” said Grijalva. “I believe he’s trying to get one of the other four Republicans who signed on to the petition to remove their name. So, my swearing in is not that big of an issue.”

She went on to ask what the Justice Department was doing with the Epstein files while they were waiting.

“I do feel like they’re going to do everything they possibly can to have things permanently redacted. The longer these files are, sort of, sitting, you wonder who has access to them, how much of any of that is going to be complete when the American people finally see it,” she noted.

Witt then asked whether Grijalva had “thought about personally talking to the Speaker.”

Grijalva wanted to know how the other newly sworn-in lawmakers reached Johnson to schedule their swearing-in.

“I have called, we sent a letter, and now our attorney general sent a demand letter two days ago, basically saying, ‘Swear her in’. You are depriving 812,000 Arizonans of their voice in Congress,” she explained.

When Witt played the clip of Johnson saying she could be working, Grijalva invited him to her office to explain why there’s no access to email or phones.

“Speaker Johnson, why don’t you come over and check it out? I got keys. We have no phones, no computers. We brought in a printer because we don’t have government email in order to connect to any of the services here. How am I supposed to take constituent calls and information when I don’t have a government email? We don’t have the capacity to tap into resources. I mean, does he know his job? Because we can’t do that without being sworn in,” she schooled.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Congressional Standoff: Grijalva vs. Johnson

In an episode that seems ripped from the pages of a political thriller, Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva’s (D-AZ) fight to be sworn into Congress has revealed a tangled web of condescension, obstruction, and a potentially explosive quest for transparency concerning the Jeffrey Epstein investigation files. The dynamics at play shed light on the complexities and, frankly, the absurdities within the halls of power in Washington, D.C.

The Condescending Obstruction

Speaker Mike Johnson’s refusal to swear Grijalva into her post, under the guise of procedural pedantry coupled with a patronizing “bless her heart,” is not just a personal slight but a stark illustration of how power can be wielded to silence and belittle. Johnson’s assertion that Grijalva “doesn’t know how it works” in Congress, despite her having keys to her office and theoretically being able to perform her duties, misses a critical point: without being sworn in, Grijalva lacks access to essential resources needed to serve her constituents effectively. This isn’t just about office logistics; it’s a deliberate act to disenfranchise the 812,000 Arizonans Grijalva represents.

The Epstein Files: A Catalyst for Controversy

At the heart of this standoff is the discharge petition concerning the Jeffrey Epstein files. Grijalva’s assertion that her swearing-in is being obstructed to prevent her from becoming the 218th signer, thereby triggering the release of all investigatory documents from the Epstein probe, paints a picture of political maneuvering designed to keep potentially damaging or embarrassing information under wraps. This move by Johnson, as Grijalva suggests, is not merely procedural but a calculated delay tactic. The implications are far-reaching, suggesting that transparency and accountability are being sacrificed at the altar of political expediency.

Systemic Dysfunction Exposed

Grijalva’s ordeal highlights a systemic dysfunction within Congress, where procedural roadblocks and political gamesmanship can override the democratic imperative of representation. The fact that Grijalva, despite her best efforts to officially begin her tenure, is hamstrung by a lack of access to government email, phones, and computers is a damning indictment of a system that seemingly prioritizes political infighting over governance and service.

A Call for Transparency and Accountability

The saga of Grijalva’s delayed swearing-in is more than an individual grievance; it is symptomatic of a broader problem of opacity and obstruction within government. The fight over the Epstein files is emblematic of the public’s growing demand for transparency and the lengths to which some will go to prevent it. It’s a stark reminder that, in the quest for accountability, there are forces at work determined to keep the curtains closed.

In conclusion, the standoff between Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva and Speaker Mike Johnson is not just a procedural hiccup but a microcosm of the larger battles being fought within the corridors of power in Washington, D.C. It’s a reminder that the machinery of government can be used to empower or to obstruct, and that vigilance is necessary to ensure it serves the people, not just the political elite. The outcome of this particular saga will not only determine Grijalva’s ability to serve her constituents but also signal the extent to which transparency and accountability can be expected in the handling of sensitive and potentially scandalous investigations.
