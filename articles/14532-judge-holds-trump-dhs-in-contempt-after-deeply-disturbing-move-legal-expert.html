Judge holds Trump DHS 'in contempt' after 'deeply disturbing' move: legal expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 23, 20255 min read

A judge leveled a decision that was tantamount to finding Donald Trump's administration in contempt of its order, following the government's "deeply disturbing" move, a former prosecutor said.

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance weighed in via a Substack post dated Thursday. In the write-up, the legal analyst flagged "preliminary reports" from this week showing "that a small group of people who were not legally in this country were being deported to Sudan."

"But none of them came from that country. Early reports suggested a Burmese man and a Vietnamese man were included in the group that was being summarily deported. The facts were confused. Lawyers went to court quickly," she wrote, adding that "this wasn’t the government’s first outing involving these individuals in the case in front of" U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy in Boston.

According to Vance, the judge "had previously entered an order that the government appeared to be in clear violation of."

"The case is a class action filed on behalf of people who are deportable—people who the government has obtained final orders to remove—but whose home countries either won’t accept them or are places they have credible or reasonable fear of being persecuted, tortured, or killed if they are returned to them," she wrote. "After the case was filed, Judge Murphy entered a temporary restraining order preventing deportations to third-party countries without notice. Although the government asked the First Circuit to countermand his order, they declined to."

What happened next, according to Vance, "is deeply disturbing."

"Despite the court order, DHS removed four people in the class to Guantanamo, where the Department of Defense supposedly took over, flying them to a third country," according to the ex-prosecutor. "The government argued it hadn’t violated the court’s order, since the Defense Department wasn’t a defendant in the case and the court’s order didn’t apply to them. In other words, a level of sophistry the government—the non-Trump government at least—doesn’t use in its dealings with the courts. There was an utter absence of good faith."

That takes us to Wednesday night, when the judge "entered his order on the plaintiffs’ requests for injunctions to keep the government from deporting them while the litigation is underway."

"The Judge styled his order as a 'Remedy For Violation Of Preliminary Injunctions.' That is tantamount to saying the government was in contempt of his order," Vance wrote. "It is perhaps nicer and softer and less confrontational, but that is, essentially, what it is."

Noting the good reasons for the "softer approach," Vance said, "the judge is giving the government the opportunity to comply and avoid an outright confrontation."

"That should be everyone’s first choice. But ultimately, you can’t avoid a collision with someone who wants one," she added. "This may be the case the government has chosen for the head-on collision, and if so, the Judge will have done everything he can to make that clear when the case goes on appeal to the Supreme Court, as it inevitably will if that happens."

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Indictment of Trump's Administration's Legal Sophistry

In a move that can only be described as brazen contempt for judicial orders, the Trump administration has once again found itself under the harsh spotlight of legal scrutiny. According to a former federal prosecutor, Joyce Vance, the administration's actions regarding the deportation of individuals to third-party countries—countries from which they did not originate and where they may face persecution, torture, or death—represent a "deeply disturbing" maneuver that flouts the sanctity of legal injunctions.

The Case at Hand

The heart of the matter revolves around a class action filed on behalf of individuals who, while deportable, cannot safely return to their home countries due to credible fears of persecution. U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy, presiding over the case in Boston, issued a temporary restraining order to halt deportations to third-party countries without notice. This order was not just a suggestion—it was a mandate meant to protect the vulnerable.

Yet, in what Vance describes as a display of "utter absence of good faith," the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) proceeded to deport four members of the class to Guantanamo, subsequently transferring them to a third country. Their justification? A loophole they claimed exempted them from compliance since the Department of Defense, which carried out the transfers, was not a defendant in the case. This is not just legal gymnastics; it's a dangerous precedent of governmental evasion.

The Judge's Response

Judge Murphy's subsequent order, framed as a "Remedy For Violation Of Preliminary Injunctions," is a masterclass in judicial restraint and diplomacy. Yet, make no mistake—it is a clear accusation of the government's contempt for his preliminary injunction. Vance rightly points out that, while the judge's approach may appear "softer," it unequivocally signals the grave nature of the administration's defiance.

This isn't about the semantics of legal discourse; it's about the integrity of the judicial system and the protection of human rights. When the government engages in what Vance terms "sophistry" to circumvent legal obligations, it erodes trust in its commitment to justice and international human rights standards.

Looking Forward

Vance ominously notes that this confrontation may very well be the battleground the government has chosen, anticipating a collision course with the judiciary that could ascend to the Supreme Court. If so, this case represents not just a challenge to the Trump administration but to the very principles of checks and balances that underpin the American legal system.

In an era where the actions of the Trump administration are continually scrutinized, this episode stands out for its sheer audacity and the potential implications for the rule of law. It's a stark reminder of the lengths to which this administration will go to pursue its immigration agenda, regardless of legal barriers or human costs.

In conclusion, the Trump administration's actions, as dissected by Joyce Vance, reveal a disturbing willingness to exploit legal loopholes for expedient ends. This case is not merely a legal skirmish; it is a litmus test for the soul of the American judiciary and its resilience against executive overreach. The outcome of this legal battle will undoubtedly resonate far beyond the courtroom, challenging the nation to reflect on its commitment to justice, human rights, and the rule of law.
