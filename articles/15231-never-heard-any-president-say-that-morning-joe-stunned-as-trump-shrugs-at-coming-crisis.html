'Never heard any president say that': Morning Joe stunned as Trump shrugs at coming crisis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 6, 20255 min read

Mika Brzezinski and Joe Scarborough. (MSNBC)

MSNBC's Joe Scarborough expressed shock at President Donald Trump's latest comments about the impact of his tariffs.

The president dismissed concerns about the economic consequences of his sweeping tariffs, saying Americans would simply have to make do with less and brushing aside fears of a recession. The "Morning Joe" host was stunned by his lack of concern.

"What's so fascinating is when we had [The Atlantic's editor in chief] Jeff Goldberg in the other day, he said, you know, we always talk about how Donald Trump will move anywhere and he doesn't really believe in anything," Scarborough said.

"The one thing Jeffrey Goldberg said while he's in the White House, he said, you can see it in his eyes. You can read it, like, he believes in tariffs. He's believed in tariffs since 1987, he's moving forward with tariffs. I think what is remarkable and what we have not heard other presidents say is, yeah, maybe we'll get go into, maybe we'll go into recession. Maybe your kids need to get by with less at Christmas, maybe you need to get by with less."

"Never heard any president say that ever, which may also lead to what you've heard from inside the White House, what I've heard from inside the White House and what he said yesterday," Scarborough added. "He's not running again, he's not running in 2028, so he's willing to play this out, and if there's some short-term pain, he's fine with it."

Trump has openly admitted that his tariffs would cause economic pain, just like his billionaire adviser Elon Musk has conceded that his government cuts would hurt.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Tumult: A Callous Gamble or Strategic Play?

In a recent episode that sounds more like a script from a political drama than reality, MSNBC's Joe Scarborough unveiled President Donald Trump's brazen indifference toward the economic fallout of his tariff crusade. Trump's statements, dismissing concerns over potential recessions and advising Americans to "get by with less," have sparked a mixture of shock and indignation. Yet, beneath the surface of these remarks lies a complex interplay of ideology, strategy, and a gamble with the economic future of the country.

The Unwavering Belief in Tariffs

Firstly, Trump's affinity for tariffs isn't a sudden whim; it's a conviction he's held since 1987. This unwavering belief in the power of tariffs to renegotiate the country's economic standing on the global stage is both his armor and Achilles' heel. While consistency in a politician's views is a rare commodity these days, the nature of these views and their impact on the broader populace is where the concern peaks. Trump's readiness to accept a recession as a potential outcome of his policies is not merely unorthodox; it's unprecedented in modern presidential rhetoric.

The Economic Sacrifice

Scarborough's astonishment at Trump’s candor about the economic consequences of his tariffs reflects a broader disbelief. The idea that a president would so openly admit to the possibility of economic hardship for his people is startling. Comparing Trump's remarks with those of his billionaire adviser, Elon Musk, and Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessent, underscores a disturbing trend: a detachment from the economic realities faced by average Americans. Bessent's comment about cheap consumer goods not being a "God-given right" encapsulates this disconnect, revealing a blithe unawareness (or perhaps, disregard) for the value many Americans place on affordable living.

Strategic Misfire or Calculated Risk?

Trump's acknowledgment of "short-term pain" as a consequence of his policies could be seen as an attempt at candidness, yet it dangerously skirts the line of negligence. The rationale that enduring economic hardship now will yield greater benefits later is a high-stakes gamble, one that assumes the resilience and capacity of the American economy and its citizens to weather the storm. However, this gamble fails to account for the immediate, tangible impact on families and businesses across the nation.

The Verdict

Trump’s approach to tariffs and his broader economic strategy is a divisive issue, one that elicits strong opinions on both sides of the debate. However, the president's nonchalance about the potential for recession and his willingness to accept economic hardship for American citizens as part of his broader economic vision is not only a departure from presidential precedent but a deeply concerning gamble with the nation's economic future.

In conclusion, while Trump’s belief in tariffs and his unfiltered honesty about their consequences might be seen by some as a breath of fresh air in the political landscape, the potential cost of this strategy — both politically and economically — cannot be overlooked. The real test will come not just from the shelves of American stores but from the living rooms and bank accounts of its citizens. As the tariff saga unfolds, the question remains: will the gamble pay off, or will it be remembered as a strategic misfire with long-lasting repercussions?
