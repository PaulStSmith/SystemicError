Farmers bemoan crushing Trump tariffs facing higher prices during holiday season

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Anatomy of a Policy Blunder: Trump’s Tariffs

A farm worker in a field. (Shutterstock)

US farmers warned on Tuesday that they are under increasing strain thanks to President Donald Trump’s tariffs, and they predicted more price increases were coming for American consumers during the holiday season.

As reported by The Packer, representatives from the Kansas Farmers Union, supermarket chain supplier Royal Food, and North Carolina-based Red Scout Farm detailed during a conference call how Trump’s tariffs on nearly all imported goods were raising prices on vegetables, fruits, grains, and meats.

Mary Carol Dodd, owner of Red Scout Farm, said during the call that her farm depends on products imported from other countries, including greenhouse materials, insect netting, and produce bags. With no low-cost domestic substitutes for these products available, said Dodd, she will have no choice but to raise prices.

“When the price of everything it takes to grow vegetables goes up, from soil to tools to fertilizer, packaging, transportation, then the vegetables on the holiday table go up as well,” Dodd explained. “For a small, diversified farm like us, those costs add up quickly. Our profit margins are already very thin, so every increase means tough choices.”

For Dodd, those tough choices have taken the form of a 50% price hike on collard greens and kale, and a 50-cent price increase on mixed-lettuce bags.

Nick Levendofsky, executive director of the Kansas Farmers Union, said during the call that price increases were inevitable given that most farms already operate on razor-thin profit margins.

“Every added cost in the supply chain eventually shows up at the checkout line,” he said. “Tariffs stack up on top of already high input costs, and families end up paying more for the same ingredients they bought last year.”

Colin Tuthill, president of Royal Food, expressed bewilderment that the president would enact policies that raised Americans’ food prices, especially after he won an election last year on the promise to reduce grocery prices starting on his first day in office.

“Placing a tariff or a tax on any kind of food item makes absolutely no sense to me,” he said. “We’re raising the price of food for the most in need.”

The American Federation of Teachers, Century Foundation, and Groundwork Collaborative last week issued a report estimating that Thanksgiving costs for US consumers have gone up by roughly 10% over the last year, with staples such as onions, spiral hams, and cranberry sauce all recording increases of 22% or higher.

The groups also found that Trump’s policies were squarely to blame for the price increases, and not just the tariffs. Specifically, they pointed to chaos at agencies such as the US Department of Agriculture that have weakened efforts to contain bird flu on US farms, which has in turn hurt the supply of poultry heading into the holiday season.

Although Trump has walked back some of his tariffs on staples such as coffee, bananas, and chocolate, the groups noted that this rollback likely came too late to offer relief to US families this year.

“Trump campaigned on bringing down the price of groceries on day one,” they wrote. “Yet in the biggest grocery week of the year, families across the country aren’t seeing any savings. Instead, their budgets are being carved up alongside the Thanksgiving turkey.”

Our Analysis:

Anatomy of a Policy Blunder: Trump’s Tariffs

In what can only be described as an economic self-own, the Trump administration’s imposition of tariffs has placed American farmers in an increasingly precarious position. This isn’t just about the abstract notions of trade imbalance or geopolitical muscle-flexing; it’s about the tangible, immediate, and severe impact on the American dinner table, especially as the holiday season looms.

The Tariff Trap

The narrative that tariffs would protect American industries has crumbled under the reality that they are, in fact, a tax on American consumers and a stranglehold on American farmers. Representatives from the Kansas Farmers Union, Royal Food, and Red Scout Farm have laid bare the consequences of these policies. With an emphasis on the agricultural sector, it’s clear that the tariffs on nearly all imported goods have not only escalated the prices of vegetables, fruits, grains, and meats but have also put an undue strain on the resources needed to produce these items.

Mary Carol Dodd’s account from Red Scout Farm is particularly telling. The necessity of imported goods like greenhouse materials and produce bags, for which there are no low-cost domestic substitutes, underscores a critical oversight in the tariff policy. The assertion that these tariffs would somehow bolster domestic production falls flat when confronted with the reality of interconnected global supply chains. Dodd’s decision to raise prices is not a choice but a forced hand, highlighting how thin profit margins in farming leave no room for absorbing increased costs.

The Irony of Election Promises

Colin Tuthill’s bewilderment at policies that directly contradict election promises to reduce grocery prices is shared by many. It’s a stark reminder of the gap between campaign rhetoric and policy implementation. The notion that placing a tariff on food items, as Tuthill puts it, makes “absolutely no sense” is not just a matter of economic principle but of basic human empathy. Raising the price of food affects everyone, but disproportionately so the most vulnerable in society.

The Unseen Costs: Beyond the Price Tag

The American Federation of Teachers, Century Foundation, and Groundwork Collaborative’s report sheds light on another dimension of this issue: the indirect consequences of administrative chaos. The mishandling of the bird flu containment efforts is a sobering example of how policy missteps in one area can exacerbate the fallout from another. The resulting shortage in poultry supply, heading into the holiday season, is a direct hit to both consumers and producers.

A Missed Opportunity for Relief

The rollback on tariffs for certain staples is a tacit acknowledgment of their detrimental effect, but as the groups rightly note, this gesture comes too little, too late for American families this year. The damage has been done, and the administration’s attempts at damage control cannot undo the immediate hardships faced by many.

Conclusion: A Policy Feast of Follies

The landscape painted by these accounts is one of a policy in direct contradiction with its purported goals. Far from protecting American interests, the tariffs have inscribed a narrative of struggle across the heartland of America. The disconnect between campaign promises and policy outcomes is a stark reminder of the need for policies grounded in economic realities rather than political bravado.

As families gather around their holiday tables this year, many will feel the pinch of these tariffs. It’s a lesson in economic interdependence, the folly of isolationist policies, and the tangible impact of political decisions on everyday lives. The administration would do well to heed these warnings, for the sake of American farmers, consumers, and the broader economy.
