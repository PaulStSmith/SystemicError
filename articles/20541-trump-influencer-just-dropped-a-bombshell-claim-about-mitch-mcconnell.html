Trump influencer just dropped a bombshell claim about Mitch McConnell

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

McConnell’s Health Is Not the Story. His Power Was.

The gossip is the bait

The source opens with Laura Loomer’s claim that Mitch McConnell is “officially brain dead,” then spends much of its space on rumors, freezes, falls, and hospital reports. That is the wrong center of gravity. McConnell’s body is a side issue. The political fact is that he spent years using Senate power to choke off democratic governance.

What actually mattered

McConnell held real institutional power as Senate Republican leader and used it relentlessly. The source names the record plainly: he blocked hearings on judicial nominees, limited debate on major legislation, and in 2016 successfully blocked Merrick Garland’s Supreme Court confirmation. That was not confusion, temperament, or accident. It was deliberate obstruction with lasting consequences.

The party of procedure

McConnell’s great talent was to turn procedural control into political veto power. He was not a loud demagogue in the Trump mold, but he helped create the conditions for Trumpism by normalizing rule manipulation, minority obstruction, and the idea that sabotage is a legitimate governing strategy. He could back Trump’s agenda when it suited him, denounce Trump when it was useful, and still keep the machinery of the Republican Party pointed at Democratic defeat.

The health fixation misses the point

The article lingers on McConnell’s age, hospital stay, falls, and public freezes. Those facts may explain why he is now politically diminished. They do not explain the damage he already did. Framing this as a question of physical decline invites a softer reading of a man whose career was defined by hard institutional cruelty. The Senate did not become anti-democratic because McConnell looked unwell. It became more anti-democratic because he knew exactly how to use its rules.

What this story really shows

McConnell is the face of a conservative project that treats democratic legitimacy as optional so long as it preserves Republican power. Loomer’s rumor mill is trash, but it also serves a purpose: it turns a story about elite power into a spectacle about an individual’s body. That is a familiar dodge. It replaces responsibility with gossip. The actual lesson is harsher: the Republican Party relied on a disciplined operator who used the Senate to entrench minority rule, and the country is still living with the consequences.
