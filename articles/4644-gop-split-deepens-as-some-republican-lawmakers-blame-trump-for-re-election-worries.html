GOP split deepens as some Republican lawmakers blame Trump for re-election worries

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 3, 20265 min read

Republican Party representatives must now decide whether they are anti-Trump or anti-losing, according to members fighting for districts they never thought they would lose.

Republican state Sen. Jon Bramnick failed to secure the Republican primary spot for Governor of New Jersey last year on the back of an anti-Trump campaign. The New Jersey Senate member still believes the president is a concern within the GOP and will be a deciding factor at the upcoming midterm elections.

Speaking to Politico, Bramnick said, “It’s so obvious when you look at the poll of Trump, you look at the Jack Ciattarelli catastrophe, you look at losses in my district that we haven’t lost in decades. I’ve got Republicans who tell me, ‘You’re just anti-Trump.’ No, no, no. I am anti-losing.”

Tom Kean Jr. may also find himself at risk ahead of the November elections, with the Trump issue set to loom over his chances for re-election in New Jersey. Kean has represented New Jersey’s 7th District in the House since 2023, and was succeeded by Bramnick in the New Jersey Senate seat in 2022.

Political advisors have since suggested Kean would be wise to distance himself from the president should he wish to hold onto his position.

Mike DuHaime, a former Republican campaign strategist, said, “It was already going to be tough because midterms are tough for the party in power. And I think on issues like this, Tom would be safe to carve a little space between him and the president.”

Kean’s opponents suggested now is the time to strike against the long-serving New Jersey GOP rep, with Rebecca Bennett saying, “Tom Kean Jr. has a tougher voting record than he did last cycle. This is the first time he’s running with a Republican president in office.”

Critics of Kean believe the GOP rep is not willing to take a hard stance against Trump, and that this may cost him ahead of the midterms.

In a statement made by the New Jersey House rep, Kean said the “overwhelming majority of residents” support the Trump administration’s policy on “getting criminal illegal migrants off our streets”.

Brian Varela, another Democrat running to challenge Kean, said the statement is “trying to soften the blow and improve his image” despite “not even coming out against the detention center.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Trump-sized Dilemma: A Tale of Political Survival

In the grand theater of American politics, the Republican Party finds itself at a crossroads, torn between allegiance to former President Donald Trump and the stark reality of electoral viability. The case of New Jersey’s political landscape, as dissected in a recent Politico article, serves as a microcosm of the broader GOP dilemma. Here, we see a party grappling with its identity in the shadow of Trump’s polarizing influence.

Jon Bramnick’s Anti-Trump Gambit: A Failed Experiment?

Republican state Sen. Jon Bramnick’s bold move to distance himself from Trump in a bid for the gubernatorial nomination not only ended in failure but also underscored a poignant question: Is the GOP ready to pivot away from Trump, or does fear of electoral loss bind them to his legacy? Bramnick’s assertion that Trump remains a “concern” within the party and a decisive factor in upcoming elections is not without merit. His analysis, based on the “poll of Trump,” the “Jack Ciattarelli catastrophe,” and unprecedented losses in traditionally Republican districts, paints a vivid picture of a party at war with itself. Bramnick’s lament, “I’ve got Republicans who tell me, ‘You’re just anti-Trump.’ No, no, no. I am anti-losing,” encapsulates the existential crisis facing the party. Yet, one cannot help but question whether his stance is too little, too late, or if it’s the harbinger of a much-needed seismic shift within the GOP.

Tom Kean Jr.’s Precarious Balancing Act

The spotlight then shifts to Tom Kean Jr., another Republican wrestling with the Trump conundrum. Kean, who has navigated the treacherous waters of New Jersey politics to secure a seat in the House since 2023, now faces the Herculean task of reelection amidst the Trump maelstrom. Political advisors, cognizant of the “midterms curse” that often plagues the party in power, advise distancing from Trump as a survival strategy. However, Kean’s tepid stance, particularly his support for Trump administration policies on immigration, highlights a reluctant, if not outright refusal, to sever ties with Trumpian ideologies. His critics, notably Democrat Brian Varela, perceive Kean’s maneuvering as a feeble attempt to “soften the blow and improve his image,” without taking a definitive stand against controversial policies.

The GOP’s Quandary: To Trump or Not to Trump

What emerges from the New Jersey saga is a vivid illustration of the GOP’s Trump dilemma. On one hand, there’s a palpable fear of alienating the Trump base, a formidable force capable of swinging primaries. On the other, there’s the daunting prospect of general electoral losses, exacerbated by a distancing from moderate and independent voters repelled by Trump’s brand of politics.

The question that looms large is whether the Republican Party can navigate this dichotomy without splintering. Can the GOP recalibrate its moral compass and redefine its identity beyond Trump’s shadow, or will it remain ensnared in his divisive legacy, risking not just electoral defeat but a profound identity crisis?

In the end, the GOP’s struggle with the Trump dilemma is not just about political survival; it’s a test of its soul. Will it choose the path of principle, potentially at the cost of short-term losses, or will it continue to cling to the coattails of a figure whose influence has both galvanized its base and alienated vast swathes of the electorate? Only time will tell, but the stakes have never been higher, both for the party and for the fabric of American democracy itself.
