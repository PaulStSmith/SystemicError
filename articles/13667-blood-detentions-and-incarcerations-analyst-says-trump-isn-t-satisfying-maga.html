'Blood, detentions and incarcerations': Analyst says Trump isn't satisfying MAGA

Supporters of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. president Donald Trump raise MAGA hats, on the day Trump returns for a rally at the site of the July assassination attempt against him, in Butler, Pennsylvania, U.S., October 5, 2024. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Despite President Donald Trump sanctioning ongoing immigration raids nationwide and deploying U.S. troops to the second-largest city in the United States, it still might not be enough to please his base, according to a recent analysis.

In a Tuesday article for anti-Trump conservative news outlet The Bulwark, journalist Will Sommer argued that there was a "vicious MAGA feedback loop" that is dictating the administration's latest actions, and that it's showing no sign of slowing down. In fact, Sommer suggested that even Trump circumventing California Governor Gavin Newsom (D) in calling up the California National Guard (the first time a president went around a governor to call National Guardsmen since 1965) and sending roughly 700 U.S. Marines to Los Angeles may still not be enough to give his MAGA supporters what they want.

"It is a thrilling way to show the Trump faithful that the administration is really doing something," Sommer wrote. "It also underscores one of the most underappreciated elements of what is currently happening in Los Angeles and elsewhere: Much of it is being driven by a feedback loop between the Trump administration and online MAGA-world, each urging the other on to more cruelty."

Sommer pointed to a recent report showing that MAGA was turning on even the most vociferously pro-Trump officials within the administration. One recent example is FBI deputy director Dan Bongino, who faced MAGA's wrath after he conceded that convicted child predator Jeffrey Epstein did indeed take his own life in prison — shooting down a conspiracy theory he himself promoted in the recent past.

"In a similar vein, Trump world figures like Noem and White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller have spent years telling followers that the world around them was a horrifying hellhole — with millions of illegal immigrants terrorizing the country and threatening the very fabric of our civilization," Sommer wrote.

"But while Bongino can’t really cook up a fake Jeffrey Epstein murder plot to win back his fans, Noem and Miller definitely can ramp up deportations," he added. "And they can do so while bringing the MAGA media ecosystem along for the ride."

The Bulwark writer went on to observe that many on the far right were calling for the administration to follow through on its threat to arrest Gov. Newsom after Trump floated the idea earlier this week (Newsom has also dared Trump to arrest him). While Trump administration immigration advisor Tom Homan has said that Newsom hasn't done anything to merit arrest, Sommer posited that "law and due process seem to matter little to MAGA die-hards who want the blood, detentions, and incarcerations they were promised."

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Feedback Loop: A Dive into the Depths of Political Extremism

In a recent piece for The Bulwark, journalist Will Sommer takes a deep dive into the dark heart of the MAGA movement and its influence on the Trump administration's policies, particularly regarding immigration and military deployment within the United States. Sommer's analysis paints a chilling picture of a "vicious MAGA feedback loop" that not only drives but also dictates the administration's increasingly extreme actions. This analysis, grounded in disturbing developments and a keen understanding of the MAGA base's demands, requires a closer examination.

The Administration's Escalation: Beyond the Bounds of Reason

The most alarming aspect of Sommer's report is President Trump's unprecedented actions in Los Angeles and his administration's ruthless immigration raids across the nation. Deploying 700 U.S. Marines to Los Angeles and sanctioning ongoing immigration raids without showing signs of restraint is not only a testament to the administration's commitment to its hardline policies but also an indication of how far it's willing to go to appease its base. This escalation in domestic military involvement, particularly the circumvention of California Governor Gavin Newsom to call up the National Guard, marks a dangerous departure from traditional presidential respect for state sovereignty and democratic norms.

The MAGA Feedback Loop: Fueling Extremism

The core of Sommer's argument lies in the existence of a "vicious MAGA feedback loop", where the Trump administration and its most fervent supporters online egg each other on towards increasingly extreme measures. This symbiotic relationship between the administration and the MAGA base is not just a mutual admiration society; it's a mechanism through which extremism is not only perpetuated but celebrated. Sommer smartly underscores how this loop is less about policy effectiveness and more about the spectacle of action — a performance designed to thrill rather than to govern.

The Casualties of Political Theater

The real victims of this political theater, as Sommer points out, are the principles of law and due process. The discussion around potentially arresting Governor Newsom, despite the lack of any legal basis for such an action, showcases a terrifying departure from the foundational principles of American governance. The willingness of MAGA die-hards to forego these principles in exchange for the "blood, detentions, and incarcerations they were promised" reveals a frightening descent into a world where justice is not only blind but irrelevant.

MAGA Turning on Its Own

Sommer's observation that the MAGA world is turning on its own — exemplified by the backlash against FBI deputy director Dan Bongino for dismissing a conspiracy theory he once promoted — highlights a movement eating itself alive. This self-cannibalization is indicative of a broader problem within extreme political movements: the never-ending pursuit of purity tests that ultimately leave them fractured.

Conclusion

Will Sommer's article in The Bulwark is a scathing indictment of the current state of political discourse in the United States, driven by a MAGA base that demands ever more extreme measures from an administration all too willing to comply. This feedback loop between the Trump administration and its most extreme supporters represents a clear and present danger to the democratic norms and legal principles that have long underpinned American governance. As Sommer rightly points out, the spectacle of action has overtaken the substance of policy, leaving the nation in a precarious state where governance is secondary to performance. This analysis serves as a critical reminder of the consequences when political extremism is allowed to dictate policy, urging a return to principle over performance.
