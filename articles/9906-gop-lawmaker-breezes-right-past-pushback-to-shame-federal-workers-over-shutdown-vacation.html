Rep. Mike Haridopolos (R-FL) appeared Wednesday on “CNN This Morning,” hours into a federal government shutdown after Congress was unable to reach an agreement on a funding bill to carry into the the start of this fiscal year. The lawmaker said federal workers could blunt the impact by continuing to show up for work.

“I think where we’re at right now is, you know, the rules currently, folks who we furloughed will still get paid when they come back,” Haridopolos said. “That is the current rule.

“So they’re literally, some people crassly would say they’re getting a paid vacation. They’re going home knowing full well when this government opens back up, they’re getting paid. I think that, as a military person, like, my son is in the Air Force, that the common-sense thing is, if you’re in public service, you should know that, in these political times, you should show up to work, because that’s the goal. We’re all paying for this one way or the other. Let’s have people at work and not play these political games. It’s what we did in Florida.”

Hill was dubious of the congressman’s explanation and challenged his point.

“It’s not the workers playing the political games,” she said, “to your point, right?”

Haridopolos brushed right past her challenge.

“That’s why I think we should change the rules,” he said. “I think if you’re a federal worker, you should show up to work knowing full well when the government opens back up you’re going to get paid. That’s the give-and-take. They’re very generous federal benefits versus a lot of people in the private sector.

“They should recognize when you’re a federal worker that when they have these political events happening, that they know they’re going to be paid on the back side, and that’s what’s happening. You know the rules.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distraction and Distortion: Rep. Haridopolos’s Commentary on Federal Workers

In a recent appearance on CNN, Rep. Mike Haridopolos (R-FL) delivered a masterclass in the age-old political strategy of blame shifting. As the federal government grinds to a halt due to Congress’s inability to pass a funding bill, Haridopolos pointed fingers at federal workers, suggesting they play “political games” with their jobs during shutdowns. This assertion not only deflects responsibility from where it squarely lies—on Congress—but also grossly misrepresents the reality of government shutdowns.

Federal Workers: Pawns in Political Games

Haridopolos’s comments insinuate that federal workers, aware they will eventually receive back pay, treat shutdowns as “paid vacations.” This crass oversimplification ignores the stress and financial strain that many workers face when their income is abruptly paused. Unlike the congressman suggests, these are not employees gleefully skipping to a vacation; they are hardworking individuals suddenly unsure of when their next paycheck will come, how to cover bills, or afford groceries.

The Irony of Calling for “Common Sense”

The congressman’s call for “common sense” for public servants to continue working amidst political turmoil is ironic, to say the least. The real lack of common sense lies in the failure of Congress to fulfill one of its most basic responsibilities: funding the government. Haridopolos’s attempt to shift the burden of responsibility to individual workers is not only misguided but also dismissive of the fundamental issues at play.

The Challenge That Wasn’t

Erica Hill’s challenge to Haridopolos’s point—that it’s not the workers but rather the policymakers playing political games—was met with a typical political sidestep. Rather than addressing the critique, Haridopolos doubled down on his stance, suggesting a change in rules that would essentially force federal workers to show up without any assurance of when they might be paid. This proposal not only skirts the issue but further entrenches the dangerous precedent of exploiting federal workers as leverage in political standoffs.

Generous Federal Benefits? A Misleading Comparison

Haridopolos’s comment on the “very generous federal benefits” compared to the private sector is a misleading diversion. The benefits in question are not handouts but are part of the compensation for the work federal employees perform. Moreover, this argument fails to recognize that many private sector jobs offer competitive, if not superior, benefits packages. The real issue isn’t the generosity of federal benefits but rather the repeated willingness of politicians to jeopardize the livelihoods of federal workers for political gain.

Conclusion

Rep. Mike Haridopolos’s remarks on CNN are emblematic of a broader issue: the willingness of some politicians to distort the truth and shift blame to avoid accountability. Federal workers are not pawns in a game; they are essential to the nation’s functioning. The suggestion that they should bear the brunt of Congress’s failures is not only unfair but deeply disrespectful. It’s time for lawmakers to stop playing political games with people’s lives and do their job. The real “common sense” solution is clear: pass a funding bill and keep the government—and its workers—running.
