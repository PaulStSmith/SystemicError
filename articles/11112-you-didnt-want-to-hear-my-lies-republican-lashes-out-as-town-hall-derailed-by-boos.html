by Tyler Kirwan and Emily Skidmore, Missouri Independent
August 28, 2025

HARRISBURG — U.S. Rep. Mark Alford defended actions taken by President Donald Trump at a contentious town hall in Harrisburg on Wednesday night.

More than 100 constituents packed the Lions Club event hall. The small venue — full of shouting and passionate pleas — drew in people from all over the county who voiced their questions and complaints.

Hot topics during the session included Medicaid cuts, Trump’s deployment of the National Guard to Washington, D.C., and complaints about the priorities of the Republican Party.

Alford began the event with a prayer and the Pledge of Allegiance, then opened the floor to questions. Questions continued for more than two hours.

As soon as the microphone opened, over 20 members of the crowd queued up for their chance to speak to the congressman.

The crowd spoke on a variety of issues, including the need for gun reform and their disapproval of Trump’s “Big Beautiful Bill.”

One man who voiced his concern about Medicaid cuts said he was “living proof” of the importance of Medicaid after surviving being on a ventilator in a recent hospitalization.

To concerns about Medicaid cuts, Alford cited legislation proposed by Sen. Josh Hawley, who voted for cuts in Trump’s bill, to invest in rural hospitals and stop future Medicaid cuts.

“I wish I had thought of it,” Alford said, drawing mocking laughter. “We need more money to shore up to our hospitals.”

Alford is a Republican in his second term in Congress, following a long career as a TV news anchor in Kansas City.

Many people pleaded with Alford to address his commitments to Trump, with one attendee saying that Alford wasn’t in the Republican Party but the Trump party.

In response, Alford said, “Trump isn’t a perfect person but has the best interest at heart.” His response was met overwhelmingly with boos and shouts from the audience.

Throughout the meeting, Alford heard complaints from people who said they were unable to make contact with him or his team to voice their concerns.

“We are not doing the job we need to be doing,” Alford said. Throughout the meeting, he directed people with questions to reach out to his team for answers.

As some in the audience chanted “liar” at him, Alford replied sarcastically, “If you didn’t want to hear my lies why did you come here tonight?”

One speaker noted that Alford was not visiting Columbia despite stopping in 15 counties this week.

“We are in Boone County, yes, but we are just a few feet away from the county line,” said Mark Haim, director of Mid-Missouri Peaceworks. “Basically we have been denied even though Columbia represents a significant portion of your district in terms of population.”

“We will come to Columbia next year,” Alford said.

Alford experienced a contentious town hall in February when federal workers were experiencing layoffs and got national attention for his comment that “God has a plan” for those affected. He is one of the few Republicans in Congress who has met publicly with constituents.

Two people voiced support for Alford, thanking him for his work.

“I want to say that I am proud of the work you’ve done, especially as a student,” said Brenden Poteet, club president of Mizzou Republicans.

Trump last week endorsed Alford’s reelection on his social media platform Truth Social.

Our Analysis:

The Charade of Public Accountability: Unveiling the Farce at Harrisburg

In an age where political theater often masquerades as genuine public engagement, the recent town hall hosted by U.S. Rep. Mark Alford in Harrisburg, Missouri, serves as a textbook example of this disturbing trend. The event, as reported by Tyler Kirwan and Emily Skidmore of the Missouri Independent, was nothing short of a cacophony of discontent, where the voices of the constituents clashed against the unyielding defenses of a congressman more loyal to the former president than to the people he's sworn to serve.

The Spectacle of Deflection

Alford's stance on various hot-button issues, including the controversial Medicaid cuts championed by President Donald Trump, speaks volumes about the priorities of those in his political alignment. The congressman's attempt to mollify concerns with references to legislation proposed by Sen. Josh Hawley — himself a proponent of the cuts — is a masterclass in political deflection. The mockery that followed his regret of not having thought of Hawley's proposal first was not just a moment of levity but a piercing insight into the disconnection between Alford's political maneuverings and the dire realities faced by his constituents.

The Trump Party Allegiance

The contention that Alford belongs not to the Republican Party, but to the "Trump party," underscores a broader issue plaguing American politics: the cult of personality over policy and principle. Alford's rebuttal, asserting Trump's flawed character but good intentions, was met with the expected disdain it deserved. It's a telling moment that illustrates the chasm between elected officials and the electorate, where the former's fealty to Trumpism overshadows their commitment to the people's welfare.

The Mockery of Engagement

Throughout the town hall, Alford's responses to the grievances aired by his constituents ranged from dismissive to outright sarcastic. His retort to accusations of lying — questioning why attendees bothered showing up if they did not care for his "lies" — is a blatant mockery of democratic engagement. It reveals a disturbing trend among certain politicians who, when faced with accountability, resort to belittlement rather than substantive dialogue.

The Illusion of Accessibility

Complaints about the lack of access to Alford and his team were met with a hollow promise of directing queries to his office — a gesture that, without follow-through, amounts to little more than lip service. The decision to exclude Columbia, a significant population center within his district, from his visits until "next year" is a strategic choice to avoid confrontation. It's a clear indication that Alford, like many of his contemporaries, prefers echo chambers to the challenging arenas of public scrutiny.

Conclusion: The Farce Unveiled

The Harrisburg town hall was not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger, more troubling trend in American politics: the erosion of genuine public engagement. In an era where political allegiance often supersedes accountability to the electorate, events like these serve as stark reminders of the widening gap between representatives and those they represent.

The support Alford received from figures like Brenden Poteet, the club president of Mizzou Republicans, and the endorsement from Trump himself, further cement his position within a faction of politics that values loyalty over leadership. As constituents continue to voice their concerns, one wonders whether their cries fall on deaf ears, or if they are simply lost in the cacophony of a political system that has strayed far from its foundational principles of representation and public service.
