Bombsell: Only 2 percent of Epstein files have been released by DOJ

Survivors of Jeffrey Epstein stand as U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi is questioned during a House Judiciary Committee hearing on oversight of the Justice Department, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 11, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

The UK’s Channel 4 News reported this week that the Justice Department isn’t being honest about what they have on trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, his accomplices and the years of investigation done by the federal government.

According to its analysis of emails in the latest batch of 3.5 million pages, the Epstein files suggest that what has been released is only a small fraction of what there is. “Potentially only 2 percent of the information the FBI retrieved from Epstein’s homes,” is what has been shared with the public.

Survivors of Epstein have told members of Congress and the media that the Justice Department has consistently refused to follow the law passed by Congress mandating the release.

Speaking to the House Judiciary Committee last week, Attorney General Pam Bondi refused to meet with survivors. In fact, she wouldn’t even turn around to look at them when they were acknowledged. Instead, she demanded that Congress spend its time praising Dow Jones stock numbers rather than asking about the Epstein files.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Saga: A Masterclass in Governmental Obfuscation

In a revelation that surprises absolutely no one who has been paying even the slightest bit of attention, Channel 4 News has uncovered what amounts to a mere tip of the iceberg in the sordid Jeffrey Epstein scandal. The Justice Department, in what can only be described as a flagrant display of obfuscation, has apparently been sitting on a mountain of evidence, dribbling out only about 2 percent of the information it has retrieved from Epstein’s dens of iniquity. This isn’t just a failure; it’s a masterclass in governmental evasion.

A Fraction of a Fraction

The figure Channel 4 News brings to light, that the public has seen “potentially only 2 percent of the information the FBI retrieved from Epstein’s homes,” is not just shocking; it’s an insult to every principle of transparency and justice. This isn’t merely holding back; it’s a veritable chokehold on the truth. The fact that there are 3.5 million pages of emails in the latest batch alone suggests a trove of evidence so vast, it’s mind-boggling that so little has made its way into the public domain.

The Department of Injustice

The behavior of Attorney General Pam Bondi, as reported, is nothing short of abhorrent. Her refusal to even acknowledge the presence of Epstein’s survivors is a stark symbol of the Justice Department’s broader stance on this issue: a steadfast refusal to confront the ugly truths within its walls. Bondi’s demand that Congress spend its time lauding Dow Jones stock numbers instead of addressing the Epstein files is not just tone-deaf; it’s a perverse inversion of priorities.

Survivors in the Cold

The survivors of Epstein’s crimes, who have bravely come forward to tell their stories, are being met with a wall of silence from those in power. Their calls for transparency, for justice, are not just being ignored; they’re being actively stifled. The law passed by Congress, mandating the release of these files, is being treated not as a directive to be followed but as a mere suggestion to be sidestepped. This isn’t just a failure of justice; it’s a systemic mockery of it.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

What the Channel 4 News report highlights is not just a problem with the Justice Department; it’s indicative of a systemic rot, a deep-seated aversion to transparency and accountability that plagues our institutions. This isn’t just about Epstein or his high-profile accomplices; it’s about a culture of secrecy and protectionism that allows such figures to thrive in the shadows.

The refusal to release the full extent of the Epstein files is a stark reminder of the monumental gap between the government’s proclaimed values and its actions. This isn’t just a call for transparency; it’s a demand for a fundamental reevaluation of our institutions’ commitment to justice. The survivors deserve not just our attention but our action. It’s high time the full scope of Epstein’s depravities and his enablers within the corridors of power are dragged into the harsh light of day. Enough is enough.
