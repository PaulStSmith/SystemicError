“There’s a video that he posted over at Truth Social that apparently contains an image of the Obamas as apes,” the MAGA star noted. “Do I think that President Trump even watched the whole video? “Now, does that justify it? Our Analysis:

The Thin Veil of Excuses: Ben Shapiro’s Dubious Defense of Trump’s Racist Post

In a recent spectacle of verbal gymnastics, right-wing influencer Ben Shapiro attempted to shield former President Donald Trump from the backlash of sharing a blatantly racist video on his Truth Social account. The video in question, a deplorable piece that depicted Barack and Michelle Obama as apes, unsurprisingly ignited a firestorm of criticism. However, Shapiro’s defense, as reported by Media Matters, is as flimsy as it is revealing of the lengths to which some will go to excuse the inexcusable.

Shapiro’s Argument: A Cloak of Ignorance

Shapiro argued that Trump likely did not watch the entire video before sharing it, suggesting a lack of attention span rather than an endorsement of its racist content. He painted a picture of Trump as an insomniac social media user, mindlessly sharing pro-Trump content without full awareness of its contents. “I don’t think he has the attention span to get through any of the videos that he has tweeted,” Shapiro stated, attempting to diffuse the situation by attributing it to negligence rather than malice.

The Flaws in Shapiro’s Defense

Shapiro’s defense is riddled with problems, not least of which is its implicit assumption that ignorance absolves one of responsibility. Even if Trump’s attention span were as limited as Shapiro claims, it does not excuse the sharing of deeply offensive and racist content by a former president. The act of dissemination itself, especially by someone with Trump’s platform, endorses and amplifies the hateful message, irrespective of whether the sharer watched the video in its entirety.

Furthermore, Shapiro’s argument inadvertently highlights a disturbing reality: that the individuals defending Trump are willing to portray him as comically inept—a man who cannot be bothered to fully review the content he shares with millions—rather than confront the possibility of intentional racism. This portrayal not only insults the intelligence of the public but also sets a dangerous precedent where ignorance is seen as an adequate shield for racism.

The Underlying Issue: A Pattern of Behavior

Shapiro’s attempt to brush off Trump’s action as a product of late-night impulsivity ignores a broader pattern of behavior. Trump’s tenure and public life have been marked by numerous instances of racist remarks and actions. This latest incident is not an isolated mistake made in the wee hours of the morning; it is part of a consistent pattern that reflects Trump’s disregard for the dignity and humanity of people of color.

Conclusion: Unacceptable, Regardless of Attention Span

The suggestion that Trump’s possible lack of attention is a valid excuse for sharing a racist video is a testament to the moral gymnastics some are willing to perform to defend the indefensible. It underscores a willingness to overlook racism, provided it can be framed as an accident. However, when one occupies a position of immense power and influence, ignorance is not an excuse—it’s a dereliction of duty.

Shapiro’s defense of Trump serves not to exonerate the former president but to expose the lengths to which his supporters will go to rationalize his actions, no matter how reprehensible. In the end, the responsibility for what one shares on social media, especially as a public figure, is inescapable. Trump’s apparent insensitivity or ignorance does not diminish the harm caused by his actions; it only amplifies the urgency of holding him—and those who defend such actions—accountable.
