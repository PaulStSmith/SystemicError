'Get over it': Republicans order Trump to surrender as brawl threatens future of MAGA

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump has been told to make amends with Marjorie Taylor Greene as the president’s ex-ally takes center stage in a schism that’s tearing apart the GOP.

Republican representatives have challenged Trump in recent weeks on the cost-of-living crisis, health and food benefits, as well as the release of Jeffrey Epstein’s files. The president had a change of heart and went on to support the release of the Epstein files, despite previously calling it a “Democratic hoax”.

Trump also faced pushback from GOP representatives on SNAP benefits, with some Republican states committing to paying out millions of dollars in benefits, despite being ordered not to. The president has also posted several times about once-ardent supporter Marjorie Taylor Greene, who he has since dubbed a “traitor”.

Greene, who is the U.S. representative for Georgia’s 14th congressional district, has soured on Trump over the last few months. The Republican representative had supported the release of the Epstein files. This seemed to spark a rift between the two, with Trump turning on Greene with a series of posts to Truth Social making his feelings clear.

Trump posted on November 15, “Marjorie “Traitor” Green is a disgrace to our GREAT REPUBLICAN PARTY!” In response, Greene said Trump had betrayed her loyalty and that she would continue to back the release of the Epstein files. She said, “I was called a traitor by a man that I fought for five, no, actually, six years for, and I gave him my loyalty for free.

“I won my first election without his endorsement, beating eight men in a primary. And I’ve never owed him anything, but I fought for him, for the policies and for America First, and he called me a traitor for standing with these women and refusing to take my name off the discharge petition.”

Those represented by Greene have called on the president to get over himself and fix the rift. Tammy Carter, who lives in Rome, Georgia, where Greene also resides, told The Washington Post that Trump needs to “get over” the spat with his former ally. Epstein was the key decider too, with Carter suggesting that she is “totally on her [Greene’s] side.”

Jim Tully, chairman of the Georgia Republican Party for the 14th Congressional District, has since made his support for Greene public amid the split with Trump. He wrote on X, “The 14th Congressional District Republican Party issues the following statement in full and unwavering support of our duly elected Representative, Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Internal Meltdown: Trump vs. Greene

The latest episode in the GOP’s ongoing soap opera features none other than Donald Trump and Marjorie Taylor Greene, two figures who’ve previously stood shoulder to shoulder in their America First crusade, now at each other’s throats. The fallout, as reported, centers around a series of policy disagreements and personal betrayals that highlight the deep fractures within the Republican Party. Let’s dissect the madness and the implications of this schism.

Trump’s Abrupt Policy Shifts: A Study in Political Expediency

Donald Trump’s pivot on the release of Jeffrey Epstein’s files, from dismissing it as a “Democratic hoax” to supporting its release, is emblematic of his mercurial stance on policy issues. This flip-flop, while shocking to none, underscores a pattern of erratic leadership that prioritizes personal vendettas and public perception over consistent policy positions. The fact that GOP representatives have begun to challenge Trump on significant issues like the cost-of-living crisis and SNAP benefits speaks volumes about the growing discontent within his own ranks.

The SNAP Benefits Saga: A Clash of Ideologies

The confrontation over SNAP benefits, with Republican states defying orders and committing to paying out millions in benefits, further illustrates the ideological divides plaguing the GOP. It’s a rare instance where certain Republican factions have opted to prioritize the well-being of their constituents over the party line. This rebellion against Trump’s directives reveals a party at war with itself, torn between rigid conservatism and a more compassionate, pragmatic approach to governance.

Marjorie Taylor Greene: From Loyalist to “Traitor”

The transformation of Marjorie Taylor Greene in Trump’s eyes from a staunch ally to a “traitor” is particularly noteworthy. Greene, known for her controversial statements and unwavering support for Trump, has found herself in the crosshairs for her advocacy for the release of the Epstein files. Trump’s public denouncement of Greene on Truth Social, branding her a “disgrace to our GREAT REPUBLICAN PARTY,” is not just a personal attack but a strategic move to isolate and discredit one of his most vocal supporters turned critic.

Greene’s response, emphasizing her loyalty to Trump and her victory in her first election without his endorsement, points to a growing sense of independence among some GOP members. They are willing to challenge the party’s figurehead for what they perceive as the greater good. This bold stance, however, has not left her isolated; local and state-level support for Greene, as highlighted by the backing from Jim Tully and constituents like Tammy Carter, suggests that Trump’s influence may be waning, or at the very least, contested.

Conclusion: The GOP’s Existential Crisis

This public spat between Trump and Greene is more than just a petty squabble; it’s symptomatic of the broader existential crisis facing the GOP. As the party grapples with its identity in the post-Trump era, these internal conflicts will likely become more pronounced. The challenge for the GOP will be to find a way to bridge these divides and present a united front, or risk further alienation of its base and potential electoral fallout.

The call for Trump to “get over” his spat with Greene underscores a desire among some Republicans for a more cohesive, less divisive leadership style. Whether this will lead to any significant change in Trump’s approach or the GOP’s direction remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the Republican Party is at a crossroads, and how it navigates these internal conflicts could shape its future for years to come.
