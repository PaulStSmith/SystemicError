Trump quietly asking GOP lawmakers if he should fire Kristi Noem: White House insiders

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem attends a House Judiciary Committee hearing in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 4, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

During the 2000s, Donald Trump’s hit real show “The Apprentice” made him famous for the words, “You’re fired.” Trump went on to do a lot of firing during his first presidency and clashed with quite a few traditional conservatives who served in his administration. But Trump hasn’t fired nearly as many appointees during his second presidency, as he has surrounded himself with MAGA loyalists who are unlikely to question him.

According to Punchbowl News’ Jake Sherman, however, one Trump loyalist is in danger of being fired: Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

In a March 5 column, Sherman reports, “President Donald Trump has quietly asked Hill Republicans if he should fire Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, the latest sign of her tenuous standing inside the West Wing, according to multiple Republicans who have spoken with the president. Even Speaker Mike Johnson speculated about the potential for a change at the top of DHS during a recent House Republican elected leadership retreat in Fort Lauderdale, Fla.”

Trump, according to Sherman, called some GOP senators following Noem’s early March testimony in two hearings: a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing on Tuesday, March 3, followed by a House Judiciary Committee hearing the next day.

“Those appearances were marked by extraordinarily bitter exchanges between Noem and Democratic lawmakers, especially over Trump’s harsh immigration crackdown,” Sherman explains. “But some of the most notable exchanges, especially in the Senate hearing, were with Republicans. Trump was said to be especially upset about Noem’s response when Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) pressed her Tuesday about a government-funded ad campaign that Kennedy said only served to boost her own personal name recognition nationally. ... Under questioning from Kennedy, Noem said repeatedly that Trump personally approved the controversial ad blitz featuring her in the lead role. This has so angered Trump that Noem’s future at DHS may be at risk, we’re told.”

Our Analysis:

The Perilous Position of Kristi Noem: Between Loyalty and the Line of Fire

In the labyrinth of Trump’s second presidency, where loyalty is the currency of survival, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem finds herself on the thin ice of the president’s favor. The saga of Noem’s potential firing is a stark illustration of the fragile nature of tenure under Trump’s administration, especially when it strays from the unwavering allegiance expected by MAGA loyalists.

The MAGA Loyalty Test

Trump’s administration, known for its revolving door, seems to have found some stability by stacking the deck with MAGA faithfuls. Yet, this stability is illusory, as even the staunchest loyalists are not immune to Trump’s impulsive dismissals. Noem’s precarious situation, as reported by Jake Sherman of Punchbowl News, underscores a critical flaw in Trump’s leadership style: the prioritization of personal loyalty over professional competence and integrity.

The Noem Controversy

The root of Noem’s potential downfall lies in her heated exchanges with both Democratic and Republican lawmakers during recent Judiciary Committee hearings. Particularly damning was her confrontation with Senator John Kennedy over a government-funded ad campaign that seemed more like a vehicle for her personal promotion rather than public service. Noem’s defense—that Trump had personally approved the ad campaign—highlights a troubling deference to Trump’s judgement, even when it might contravene the norms and ethics expected of public officials.

Trump’s Reaction: A Leadership Style in Question

Trump’s response to Noem’s testimony and her subsequent backlash from both sides of the aisle is telling. By querying GOP senators about Noem’s fate, Trump demonstrates a leadership style that is both reactive and insecure, prioritizing personal vendettas and perceived slights over constructive feedback or critical analysis. This approach not only undermines the stability and efficacy of his administration but also erodes the foundational principles of democratic governance and accountability.

The Larger Implication

The potential firing of Kristi Noem is not just about the fallout from a controversial ad campaign or her failure to navigate the treacherous waters of Capitol Hill’s politics. It’s a symptom of a deeper malaise within Trump’s presidency: a culture where loyalty is prized above all else, and where the whims of the leader dictate the course of public policy and national security strategies.

In sum, Noem’s situation serves as a cautionary tale for current and future members of Trump’s administration. In an environment where loyalty trumps competence, no one is safe, and the very institutions designed to protect and serve the American public are rendered unstable and unpredictable. The question then becomes not just about the future of Kristi Noem, but the future of governance under a presidency that seems perpetually mired in controversy, conflict, and contradiction.
