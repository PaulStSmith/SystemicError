Boebert dips into campaign money to attend Kid Rock concert as dating rumors swirl: report

Rep. Lauren Boebert (R-CO) dipped into her campaign funds to attend a concert hosted by MAGA artist Kid Rock, amid ongoing rumors that the two are dating, The Daily Beast reported Wednesday.

“The Colorado congresswoman attended Rock N Rodeo — a May 16 event hosted by the 54-year-old singer — during the Professional Bull Riding Championship World Finals at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas,” reported Adam Downer. “Boebert, 38, even reposted a photo of herself in a cowboy hat cozying up to the rocker at the rodeo-meets-concert experience.”

Her post was accompanied by the message “Making America GREAT Again.”

According to the report, “Federal Election Committee data shows that three days after the event, the Republican representative’s campaign paid $2,455.83 to ‘Live by Loews,’ a hotel in Arlington, Texas, as well as $925 worth of ‘Event Tickets’ to AT&T Stadium.”

This would appear to run afoul of federal laws that bar candidates for office from personal use of campaign funds — something for which many members of Congress have been criminally prosecuted in recent years, although Trump issued pardons for a number of them.

This comes amid swirling rumors that Boebert and Kid Rock, real name Robert James Ritchie, are dating, which began with the two of them being seen getting in a cab together at a Feb. 11 post-inauguration party thrown by the right-wing group Turning Point USA.

Neither Boebert nor Ritchie has confirmed the rumors, and in response to questioning from TMZ, Boebert said, “Y’all with all your stories, Mom won’t stop asking for concert tickets, so, that’s the problem I’m facing now,” per the report.

Boebert divorced her husband, Jayson, in 2023, and came under controversy later that year after she was ejected from a “Beetlejuice” musical production in Denver for fondling her then-boyfriend in public.

The divorce appeared to have significantly impacted her finances, as a federal disclosure earlier this year had her disclosing zero assets. The disclosure indicated she was one of the poorest members of Congress.

Our Analysis:

The Concert Cash Conundrum: Boebert’s Campaign Funds Saga

In a recent revelation that reads more like a tabloid drama than a political report, Rep. Lauren Boebert (R-CO) has found herself in the hot seat for utilizing campaign funds to attend a concert by MAGA artist Kid Rock. The incident, as reported by The Daily Beast, not only fuels the gossip mill with rumors of a romantic liaison between Boebert and Kid Rock but also raises serious questions about the legality of her actions concerning campaign finances.

The Allegations: A Financial Faux Pas

According to the report, Boebert’s campaign shelled out $2,455.83 for a stay at “Live by Loews,” a hotel in Arlington, Texas, and an additional $925 on “Event Tickets” for the Rock N Rodeo event during the Professional Bull Riding Championship World Finals. The expenditures came amidst whispers of a budding romance between the Colorado congresswoman and the 54-year-old singer, Robert James Ritchie, better known as Kid Rock. This rendezvous wasn’t just a night out but seemingly a breach of federal laws that strictly prohibit the personal use of campaign funds.

The Federal Election Committee (FEC) has been clear: Campaign funds are for campaigning, not for personal entertainment. This isn’t just an administrative guideline; it’s a legal boundary that, when crossed, has seen members of Congress face criminal prosecution. While President Trump may have pardoned several of these violators, the law remains unchanged, and Boebert’s financial frolic raises both eyebrows and questions about accountability.

The Response: Dodging and Weaving

In light of these allegations, Boebert’s response has been less than satisfactory. Brushing off inquiries with quips about her mother asking for concert tickets does little to address the core issue at hand: the potential misuse of campaign funds. The congresswoman’s personal life, including her recent divorce and reported financial struggles, might tug at the heartstrings, but they do not excuse or justify any misuse of funds entrusted to her by supporters.

The Bigger Picture: Accountability in Politics

What stands out in this saga is not just the alleged misuse of funds, but what it signifies about the state of political accountability today. If the allegations are true, Boebert’s actions not only flout federal law but also betray the trust of those who donated to her campaign under the assumption that their contributions would be used to further political, not personal, objectives.

Moreover, the timing of these expenditures, juxtaposed with Boebert’s personal controversies and financial disclosures indicating she is among the poorest members of Congress, paints a troubling picture of mismanagement and desperation.

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency

As this story unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the importance of transparency and accountability in political campaign financing. The American public deserves assurance that their contributions are being used appropriately and legally, not for VIP tickets to a concert or cozy hotel stays under the guise of campaigning.

Rep. Lauren Boebert’s situation is more than a gossip column’s dream; it’s a potential breach of trust and law that demands thorough investigation. For the sake of her constituents and the integrity of the political system, one hopes that clarity and accountability will prevail over evasion and excuses.
