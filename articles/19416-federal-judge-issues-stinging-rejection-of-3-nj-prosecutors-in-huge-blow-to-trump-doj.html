Pam Bondi keeps illegally appointing attorney generals for New Jersey and local judges keep throwing them out.

A chief U.S. District Judge for Pennsylvania rejected three new leaders of the New Jersey U.S. attorney’s office on the argument that the Trump administration is illegally trying to circumvent Senate confirmation of U.S. attorneys.

“One year into this administration, it is plain that President [Donald] Trump and his top aides have chafed at the limits on their power set forth by law and the Constitution,” wrote Chief District Judge Matthew W. Brann on Monday. “To avoid these roadblocks, this administration frequently purports to have discovered enormous grants of executive power hidden in the vagaries and silences of the code.”

After Brann determined that Trump administration appointee Alina Habba was unlawfully serving as the acting U.S. Attorney for New Jersey and booted her, AG Pam Bondi appointed and delegated a “Byzantine” triumvirate of lesser leaders, all unconfirmed by the U.S. Senate: Senior Counsel Philip Lamparello became Senior Counsel, Special Jordan Fox and Executive Assistant US Attorney Ari Fontecchio to conduct prosecutions.

But because the triumvirate are all equally unconfirmed by the Senate, all are illegally conducting prosecutions for New Jersey, ruled Brann — who then ejected the three.

Congress has a “clear and unambiguous requirement of Presidential nomination and Senate confirmation before a person may exercise the powers of a United States Attorney, … which has been the standard for more than 236 years,” argued Brann. But Bondi argues that she “can appoint anyone to any subordinate position in the Department of Justice and delegate them the authority to act in any other subordinate role, no matter how significant.”

Brann isn’t buying that, according to his Monday ruling, which characterized the claim as “an enormous assertion of Presidential power.”

“The current leadership structure for the office … constitutes a unilateral appointment in violation of the Appointments Clause. This finding requires disqualification of the Attorney General’s appointees,” said Bran.

Because the triumvirate were not authorized to prosecute, Brann could have made the additional decision to toss the cases they lodged at defendants during their short time in office. Brann stopped there, however — but warned the government that further prosecutions lodged by unauthorized people could get dismissed.

Rep. Frank Pallone (D-N.J.) said he was happy with the decision on X.

“I’m glad to see the rule of law prevail once again,” Pallone posted. “The chaos New Jersey suffered under Alina Habba was too great — we can’t afford to have our justice system torn limb from limb by Trump loyalists who only prioritize his political agenda.”

Our Analysis:

The Unchecked Ambition: A Deep Dive into the Trump Administration’s Legal Maneuverings

In what can only be described as a flagrant disregard for the constitutional and legal framework that has governed the United States for over two centuries, the Trump administration, spearheaded by AG Pam Bondi, has once again demonstrated an audacious attempt to sidestep the established process for appointing U.S. attorneys. This maneuver, as adjudicated by Chief U.S. District Judge Matthew W. Brann, is not just a breach of legal protocol but a glaring testament to an administration that operates with a brazen sense of entitlement to power.

The Illegitimate Appointments

Pam Bondi’s repeated attempts to appoint attorney generals for New Jersey without Senate confirmation have been met with resistance from the judiciary, and rightfully so. The latest saga involves a Byzantine configuration of leadership within the New Jersey U.S. attorney’s office, comprising Senior Counsel Philip Lamparello, Special Jordan Fox, and Executive Assistant US Attorney Ari Fontecchio. This trio, none of whom have been confirmed by the Senate, were deemed to be unlawfully conducting prosecutions, a clear violation of the constitutional mandate that requires Presidential nomination and Senate confirmation.

The Judicial Backlash

Judge Matthew W. Brann’s ruling on this matter is both a scathing indictment of the Trump administration’s cavalier approach to the rule of law and a robust defense of constitutional governance. Brann’s assertion that the administration has frequently sought to uncover “enormous grants of executive power hidden in the vagaries and silences of the code“ is a damning commentary on the lengths to which Trump and his aides have gone to consolidate power, bypassing legal and procedural checks.

The Defense of the Constitution

The administration’s defense, articulated by Bondi, posits that the Attorney General possesses the authority to appoint anyone to any subordinate position within the Department of Justice and delegate them significant roles, regardless of Senate confirmation. This claim, characterized by Brann as an “enormous assertion of Presidential power“, flies in the face of the Appointments Clause and over 236 years of constitutional practice. It’s a stark reminder of the Trump administration’s penchant for challenging the very foundations of the country’s legal and constitutional order.

The Consequences and the Future

While Judge Brann has stopped short of dismissing cases brought forth by the unlawfully appointed officials, his warning to the government is clear: further prosecutions by unauthorized individuals could face dismissal. This ruling is not just a rebuke of the Trump administration’s tactics in New Jersey but a signal to future administrations about the sanctity of constitutional norms and the rule of law.

Rep. Frank Pallone (D-N.J.)’s response to the ruling encapsulates the relief and vindication felt by many who have watched with dismay as the Trump administration has attempted to erode the integrity of the justice system. The “chaos” under Alina Habba, as Pallone describes it, is emblematic of a broader trend of governance by whim, rather than by law, under Trump.

Conclusion

The saga of the Trump administration’s attempts to unilaterally appoint U.S. attorneys in New Jersey is not just a story of legal overreach; it is a cautionary tale about the fragility of democratic institutions in the face of unchecked ambition. The judiciary, in this case, has proven itself a bulwark against such ambitions, but the continued vigilance of all branches of government, as well as the public, is essential to safeguard the nation’s constitutional and democratic principles. The rule of law must prevail over the rule of convenience and power.
