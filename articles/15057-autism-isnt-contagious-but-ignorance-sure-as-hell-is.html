Autism Isn’t Contagious — But Ignorance Sure As Hell Is

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In the age of endless online discourse, it's hard to be surprised anymore. But every now and then, a post surfaces that smacks you in the face with such a raw cocktail of ignorance and arrogance that you have to read it twice — not because it’s profound, but because it’s profoundly stupid.

"My son hangs out with an autistic kid sometimes and I don't want him to start taking after him and becoming autistic. How can I nip this problem in the bud?"

Let’s be clear: Autism is not contagious. You can’t “catch” it like a cold, a rash, or a bad opinion — though the person who posted this clearly caught something, and it wasn’t empathy. Autism is a neurodevelopmental condition. It's not a virus. It’s not a disease. It’s not something you absorb through osmosis from your friends like a secondhand bad haircut.

But the real disease on display here is ignorance, and that one is contagious. Passed down from parents to children, from social circles to group chats, from Facebook rants to dinner table diatribes. It's fed by misinformation, isolation, and a profound unwillingness to understand anyone outside your neurotypical little bubble.

What’s even more disturbing is the mindset lurking beneath the post — the assumption that being autistic is something bad, something to avoid, something that makes a kid less worthy of friendship. That’s the kind of thinking that breeds bullying, ostracism, and a world where neurodivergent kids grow up feeling like something’s wrong with them.

And yet, in one of the most glorious replies in the history of internet takedowns, someone cuts through the nonsense with surgical precision:

"Autism is not contagious, but, I’m afraid stupidity is, so please stop spreading yours."

Boom. That’s not just a burn — it’s a public service announcement.

So here’s the truth: If your kid is hanging out with an autistic classmate, you should be grateful. He’s learning empathy. He’s learning acceptance. He’s growing into a human being who doesn’t judge others based on differences. Meanwhile, if you’re panicking because you think compassion is contagious, maybe it’s time to look in the mirror — because the real problem isn’t the kid your son’s befriending. It’s you.

Let’s stop teaching children to fear what they don’t understand. Let’s stop pathologizing kindness. And for the love of all that’s decent, let’s stop assuming that autism is a flaw, rather than just another way of being human.

Because while autism isn’t contagious, your toxic attitude absolutely is — and the world could use a whole lot less of that.
