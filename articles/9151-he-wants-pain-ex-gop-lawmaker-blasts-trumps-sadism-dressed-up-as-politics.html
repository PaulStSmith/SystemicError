‘He wants pain’: Ex-GOP lawmaker blasts Trump’s ‘sadism dressed up as politics’

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 5, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

Republican former U.S. Rep. Adam Kinzinger sharply condemned President Donald Trump and his onetime GOP colleagues, accusing the president of “sadism” for refusing to release what experts say are legally mandated funds to sustain food stamp payments once they’re cut off on Nov. 1.

Warning that “millions of Americans will stop receiving food stamps” if Congress does not act, Kinzinger explained that “mothers won’t be able to buy groceries. Veterans won’t be able to feed their families. Children will go hungry — not because of some natural disaster or accident of bureaucracy, but because our leaders made a deliberate choice.”

“I’ve been in Congress,” he wrote. “I’ve sat in those rooms where politicians calculate pain — where they decide that hurting ordinary Americans might be good for their ‘message.’”

READ MORE: Johnson Says Trump ‘Trolling’ on Major Constitutional Question

Indeed, on Tuesday afternoon, Politico reported that Speaker of the House Mike Johnson told his GOP conference to sit tight as the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) food stamps shutoff hits.

“‘Things are getting real’ this week,” Johnson said, as Politico noted, adding that “he braced his members for some of the worst real-world fallout of the shutdown so far.”

Johnson “urged Republicans to stay in lockstep as ‘pressure mounts on Democrats’ — including key deadlines that will impact millions of low-income Americans.”

“’The pain register is about to hit level 10,’ Johnson said, adding that ‘sadly’ 42 million Americans will be hit this weekend when Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits run out of money.”

Kinzinger pointed out that Trump “has a choice” in this.

“He can use emergency funds to keep food assistance flowing while negotiations continue. He could say, ‘Not on my watch will people go hungry.’ But he isn’t. He is choosing not to — because he wants the pain. He wants the headlines. He wants to point to struggling families and say, ‘Blame the Democrats.’”

“That’s not leadership,” the former lawmaker lamented. “That’s sadism dressed up as politics.”

Expressing anger over Trump’s $300 million ballroom, and possible $250 million Department of Justice settlement for being investigated, Kinzinger said, “I’ve seen what shutdowns do.”

“I’ve seen families line up at food pantries because their pay got delayed. I’ve heard from single parents who rely on SNAP to get through the week. These are not ‘lazy’ people. They’re workers — often working full-time — who still fall below the poverty line because the system rewards wealth, not work.”

He added that “what government is supposed to do” is “protect its citizens in hard times. Not turn hunger into a political weapon.”

Denouncing the “moral bankruptcy” of congressional Republicans and President Trump, Kinzinger wrote, “When you see a president who intentionally withholds help to make a point, you’re seeing the moral rot that now defines the GOP. The cruelty isn’t a side effect — it’s the strategy.”

Our Analysis:

The Strategy of Cruelty: A Scathing Indictment of GOP Tactics

In a damning critique, former U.S. Representative Adam Kinzinger lays bare the calculated cruelty at the heart of recent Republican maneuvers concerning the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), better known as food stamps. This isn’t mere political jockeying; it’s a deliberate infliction of suffering, with President Donald Trump and GOP leaders weaponizing hunger to score ideological points. Kinzinger’s scorching rebuke sheds light on the moral decay festering within the Republican Party, a decay that prioritizes political gain over the well-being of millions.

Calculated Sadism

The term “sadism” is not used lightly by Kinzinger, but it aptly describes the refusal to release legally mandated funds for SNAP. This decision is set to plunge millions into food insecurity, not as a result of bureaucratic oversight or natural disaster, but through a choice—a choice made by those elected to protect and serve their constituents. The imagery of mothers unable to buy groceries, veterans struggling to feed their families, and children going hungry paints a stark picture of the consequences of these political games.

The Politics of Pain

The GOP’s strategy, as outlined by Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, is to remain unified as the SNAP cutoff looms, using the ensuing hardship to leverage concessions from Democrats. This approach, where politicians “calculate pain” as a bargaining chip, is grotesquely cynical. It’s a betrayal of the fundamental duty of governance: to safeguard the welfare of the populace. Johnson’s admission that the “pain register is about to hit level 10” is a chilling acknowledgment of the impending suffering, worn as a badge of strategic foresight rather than the grim forecast it truly is.

Trump’s Role: A Choice for Cruelty

Kinzinger points a direct finger at Trump, accusing him of choosing to let Americans go hungry to extract political advantage. This isn’t just an abdication of leadership; it’s a perverse embrace of suffering as political theater. Trump’s refusal to utilize emergency funds to bridge the gap during negotiations is presented not as a logistical or fiscal decision, but as a deliberate tactic to inflict pain and blame the opposition. It’s a maneuver Kinzinger rightly identifies as “sadism dressed up as politics.”

The Moral Bankruptcy of the GOP

This episode is symptomatic of a deeper, more insidious problem within the Republican Party: a moral bankruptcy that views the hardship of the vulnerable not as a crisis to be alleviated, but as an opportunity to be exploited. Kinzinger’s outrage over Trump’s lavish expenditures juxtaposed with his indifference to the plight of SNAP recipients underscores a system that “rewards wealth, not work,” and a party that has strayed far from any principled ideological moorings it once claimed.

Conclusion: The Real Cost of Political Gamesmanship

Adam Kinzinger’s condemnation of his former colleagues and the sitting president is a powerful testament to the ethical corrosion that threatens the foundational principles of American democracy. The deliberate infliction of suffering for political gain is not just morally reprehensible; it’s a betrayal of the social contract that underpins democratic governance. As Kinzinger poignantly observes, the cruelty isn’t a side effect of GOP strategy—it’s the core of it.

In a political landscape marred by division and partisanship, it’s essential to remember the human cost of these political games. The strategy of cruelty laid bare by Kinzinger isn’t just a failure of policy or leadership; it’s a failure of humanity. And it’s a stark reminder of the urgent need for a politics that places people over power, compassion over cruelty, and moral integrity over strategic calculation.
