'It will get worse': Experts alarmed as Putin again thumbs nose at US

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 13, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Two former top Trump officials from his first administration sounded the alarm after Russia attacked Poland Tuesday night, as several critics assailed the President for years of insisting Putin would never have dared attack Ukraine if he were in office.

Russia launched more than a dozen drones into Poland, which “prompted NATO forces to launch warplanes overnight,” according to The New York Times.

But NATO reportedly did so without help from the U.S.

“It’s notable that the NATO response to Russia’s drone incursion across the border into Poland makes no mention of US military assistance to defend Poland,” reported Fox News chief national security correspondent Jennifer Griffin. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte “mentions Polish F16s, Dutch F35s, Italian AWACS, and German Patriots. Russia’s testing of NATO airspace to test NATO’s response comes just days after the Pentagon informed NATO Allies bordering Russia that the US will halt training and security funding to them.”

The attack occurred as Trump dined at Joe’s Seafood, Prime Steak & Stone Crab in D.C., with his Secretary of State and his Secretary of Defense. He was besieged by protestors caught on camera (video below).

Former Trump National Security Advisor John Bolton slammed President Putin and invoked Trump’s Alaska Summit as well.

“Putin has acted like he has a free hand since the Alaska summit,” Bolton wrote on Wednesday. “He’s ignored any further conversation on a ceasefire, the Russian military is expanding its operations, and he’s received the political support of his friends Xi Jinping, Narendra Modi, and Kim Jong Un.”

Political science professor Maria Popova responded: “American weakness always fuels russian aggression.”

Former Trump Vice President Mike Pence urged U.S. action.

“As Russian drones violate Polish and NATO airspace and Russia continues to escalate it’s brutal attacks on civilian populations across Ukraine, it’s time for renewed military support for Ukraine and allies in Eastern Europe and harsh new sanctions on Russia. Peace comes through strength,” Pence wrote.

His remarks also came days after the Pentagon announced it will no longer be supporting programs that help America’s European allies stave off attacks from Russia, as The New York Times reported last week.

Meanwhile, critics blasted Trump for what they see as weakness and a lack of action.

Alexander Vindman, a former Director of European Affairs for the United States National Security Council (NSC), wrote: “Trump right now getting ready with: if I were president Russia never would have attacked Poland/NATO. @realDonaldTrump‘s weakness has been encouraging Russian aggression.”

Attorney George Conway, a well-known Trump critic, wrote: “None of this would have happened on any day if virtually anyone other than Donald Trump were president.”

Russian expert Julia Davis, a Daily Beast columnist, responded: “It will get worse,” to which Conway agreed.

So far, Trump has said nothing on his Truth Social account about Russia attacking Poland, but the U.S. Ambassador to NATO, Matthew Whitaker, wrote: “We stand by our @NATO Allies in the face of these airspace violations and will defend every inch of NATO territory.”

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of the "Alarm" Sounded by Former Trump Officials

In a world where the echoes of political rhetoric reverberate louder than the truth, it's essential to dissect the cacophony with a scalpel of facts and a lens of critical analysis. The recent article detailing Russia's attack on Poland and the subsequent reactions from former Trump officials, pundits, and critics offers a fertile ground for such an examination.

The Incident and NATO's Response

Russia's bold move to launch drones into Poland, a NATO member, is not only a significant escalation in the region but also a litmus test for the alliance's unity and response capabilities. The detail that NATO forces launched warplanes overnight to address this incursion, notably without the aid of U.S. military forces, speaks volumes about the current state of international military cooperation and the United States' role within it. The U.S. absence in the immediate military response, as highlighted by Fox News' Jennifer Griffin, raises questions about the strategic decisions at play within the Pentagon and beyond.

The Echo Chamber of Former Trump Officials

John Bolton and Mike Pence, both high-ranking officials in the Trump administration, were quick to frame the incident within the narrative of American strength—or perceived lack thereof—on the global stage. Bolton's reference to the "Alaska summit" and the subsequent emboldening of Putin, alongside Pence's call for "renewed military support for Ukraine and allies in Eastern Europe," serve to not only critique the current administration's foreign policy but also subtly absolve the previous one of any geopolitical missteps.

However, it's crucial to scrutinize these assertions with a critical eye. The insinuation that American weakness is the catalyst for Russian aggression is a simplification that ignores the complex web of historical, political, and military factors that drive international relations. Furthermore, the timing of these statements—amidst revelations that the Pentagon will cease support for certain programs aimed at bolstering European allies against Russian attacks—seems conveniently orchestrated to deflect from the broader systemic issues at play.

Critics' Chorus and the Silence of Trump

The critics' voices, notably Alexander Vindman, George Conway, and Julia Davis, weave a narrative of Trump's presidency as an era of emboldening adversaries through perceived weakness. Their arguments, while politically charged, point to a broader concern about the impact of domestic leadership styles on international aggression. Trump's silence on the matter, especially on platforms like Truth Social, contrasts sharply with the vocal stance of his former officials and critics alike, leaving a void filled with speculation about his position on these pressing international issues.

The Bigger Picture

This incident, and the flurry of reactions it has provoked, underscores the intricate dance of power, perception, and politics on the world stage. The portrayal of Trump as a leader whose absence would have prevented such an attack is as much about political rivalry as it is about analyzing the nuances of international diplomacy and military strategy.

In this dance, the truth often becomes a casualty of war, sacrificed at the altar of political expediency and narrative shaping. As observers and analysts, it is our duty to cut through the fog of political posturing and examine the roots of these systemic issues. Only then can we hope to understand the complex dynamics that govern our world and, perhaps, inch closer to solutions that transcend the simplistic binaries of strength and weakness, aggression and appeasement.

In conclusion, this article presents a microcosm of the broader debates surrounding U.S. foreign policy, NATO's role in contemporary geopolitics, and the enduring shadow of the Trump administration's decisions. As the situation unfolds, it will be crucial to remain vigilant, critical, and informed—lest we allow the narrative to be dictated solely by those with the loudest voices, rather than the strongest facts.
