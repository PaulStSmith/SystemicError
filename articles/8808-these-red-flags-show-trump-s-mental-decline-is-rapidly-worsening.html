These red flags show Trump's mental decline is rapidly worsening

Over the last month, Trump’s mental decline has appeared to worsen. Consider:

  • On Sunday, he posted an image claiming that Barack Obama had been collecting millions in taxpayer dollars from “royalties linked to Obamacare.” (The bogus item was from a satirical website called the “Dunning-Kruger Times,” a reference to the Dunning-Kruger effect — the well-observed tendency of stupid people to vastly overestimate their abilities or intelligence.)

  • After last Tuesday’s elections, he called “affordability” a “new word” and said Republicans had not talked enough about it, but then blasted it as a Democratic “con job” and declared “I don’t want to hear about the affordability.”

  • Seemingly oblivious to the economic struggles Americans find themselves in, he has posted incessantly about the new Lincoln bathroom, remodeled in black and white marble with gold faucets and light fixtures; his new White House ballroom, to be built in marble and gilded in gold; and renovations at the Kennedy Center, which he said would be outfitted in marble and “magnificent high end carpeting.”

  • When responding to a question about his mental acuity, he confused a dementia screening test for an IQ test.

  • When he addressed America’s top military brass, he veered abruptly from discussing Marine morale to “Biden’s autopen” and said, “I have to sign for a general because we have beautiful paper, the gorgeous paper. I said, ‘Throw a little more gold on it, they deserve it.’ Give me — I want the A paper, not the D paper. We used to sign a piece of garbage.”

  • He claimed that he had halted a “nuclear” war between Iran and Pakistan, repeatedly confusing Iran and India without noticing his mistake.

  • He insisted he had “solved” an imaginary conflict between Cambodia and Armenia — two nations 4,000 miles apart. Days before, he bragged he’d stopped a showdown between Azerbaijan and Albania, apparently meaning Armenia.

  • Speaking to reporters one day after meeting with Chuck Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries to avert a shutdown, Trump discussed his talks with “Chuck Schumer, who was here yesterday, along with ... uhh, the, a very nice gentleman who I didn’t really know. You know who I’m talking about.”

  • On October 18, when more than 7 million Americans protested against him, he posted an AI video depicting him bombing the protesters with feces.

I could go on, but you get the point. Trump appears to be rapidly losing his mind.

Yet the media isn’t covering his mental deterioration. Why not? When I ask in the media, I usually get one of the following responses:

1. There’s been no noticeable decline in Trump’s mental faculties. They tell me that Trump has always been incoherent. He’s always veered off script into bizarre tangents. He’s always made bonkers factual mistakes (along with his ubiquitous lies). So, they say, there’s been no noticeable decline in recent months.

2. His decline can’t be substantiated. A second response I get is that Trump’s declining mental condition can’t be substantiated. Yes, there are abundant anecdotes, such as those I listed above, but no hard evidence. Unless or until he says or does something totally and dangerously bonkers or his decline is substantiated, this isn’t news.

3. Media owners are blocking stories about his mental decline. A third response I get is that yes, his mental faculties have been in free fall, but no media owner wants to cover this for fear of retribution. The owners of all major media are either being sued by Trump for defamation or have recently settled with him, or are dependent on Trump’s FCC or fear Trump’s FTC and don’t want to risk his wrath.

4. Journalists don’t want to cover this. A final response is that, while the evidence of his decline is overwhelming, reporters themselves are self-censoring. There are so many other important Trump stories to cover, and his mental condition is such a controversial topic, that they’ve put any investigation into his mental deterioration on the back burner.

Hence, today’s Office Hours question: Why in your view is the media not reporting on Trump’s mental decline?

Our Analysis:

The Unspoken Truth: Trump’s Alleged Mental Decline and Media’s Muted Response

In an era where the absurd has become the norm, the latest installment in the Trump saga reads like a narrative spiraling out of control, yet the chorus of media coverage sings a surprisingly subdued tune. Robert Reich’s article pierces through the cacophony with a scathing analysis of former President Donald Trump’s purported mental decline and the media’s hesitance to spotlight this alarming trend. Let’s dissect the farcical odyssey and the deafening silence that surrounds it.

The Parade of Absurdities

Trump’s recent exploits, as detailed by Reich, paint a picture of a man increasingly detached from reality. From boasting about non-existent geopolitical achievements to confusing a dementia screening test with an IQ test, the former president seems to be lost in a self-created alternate universe. The claims range from the ludicrous (stopping a nuclear war between Iran and Pakistan, a sheer geographical and political impossibility) to the bizarre (AI videos of bombing protesters with feces). These anecdotes, while they might provide fodder for late-night comedy, underscore a potentially grave issue: the mental fitness of a man who once held, and may seek again, the highest office in the land.

Media’s Muted Response: A Four-Pronged Analysis

Reich’s quest to understand the media’s silence on Trump’s mental state yields four primary explanations, each revealing a systemic flaw in our current media landscape:

  1. Denial of Decline: The argument that Trump has always communicated incoherently and that his behavior is not a departure from the norm is a dangerous benchmark. Accepting erratic behavior as a baseline sets a low bar for future leaders.

  2. Lack of “Hard Evidence”: The reluctance to report on Trump’s mental decline due to a lack of substantiated evidence reflects a paralysis in journalistic responsibility. When the abundance of anecdotes paints a consistent pattern, the media owes it to the public to investigate further, rather than waiting for an unignorable catastrophe.

  3. Fear of Retribution: The suggestion that media owners are silencing stories for fear of Trump’s wrath or legal retaliation exposes a chilling reality of self-censorship under the threat of power. This is antithetical to the role of the press in a democratic society.

  4. Journalistic Self-Censorship: The notion that journalists are sidelining stories of Trump’s mental decline due to the controversial nature of the subject or the abundance of other Trump-related stories is perhaps the most disheartening revelation. It hints at a normalization of the extraordinary and a failure to prioritize the public’s right to know over narrative convenience.

The Deeper Malaise

The heart of the issue transcends Trump’s individual case, touching on the broader problems of media accountability, journalistic integrity, and the societal implications of normalizing deviant behavior in public figures. Reich’s analysis, while focused on the microcosm of Trump’s mental state, opens up a macrocosm of questions about the media’s role in shaping public perception and the thresholds for newsworthiness.

In Conclusion: A Call for Media Courage and Clarity

Reich’s article, though speculative in parts, is a necessary provocation in a media environment that seems all too comfortable skating on the surface of deeper issues. The reluctance to delve into the mental fitness of a former president, potentially eyeing a return to power, is not just a failure of nerve; it’s a systemic error that undermines the very foundation of informed democracy.

As observers, analysts, and citizens, it is incumbent upon us to demand more from our media institutions—to challenge the narratives being fed to us and to question the silence that envelops issues of monumental importance. Only then can we hope to navigate the murky waters of modern political discourse with any semblance of clarity and purpose.
