This lazy — and stupid — admission dooms a GOP senator to an eternity of contempt

Based on an article by John Casey

The Senate Didn’t Vet These Nominees. It Licensed Them.

A crisis with a signature line

The immediate story is simple: Pete Hegseth was shoved into the Pentagon on a 51-50 vote, RFK Jr. was handed the nation’s health machinery despite no public health credentials, and Todd Blanche is now being elevated into the Justice Department as if Trump’s personal lawyer were a neutral public servant. The deeper story is not confusion. It is consent. These outcomes required Republican votes, Republican discipline, and Republican senators willing to fake concern in public and surrender in private.

The chamber that still pretends to deliberate

The article is right to name the Senate as the site of the rot, but the real point is harsher: “advice and consent” has been reduced to ceremonial cover for executive capture. When a vice president has to break a tie to install a nominee with a sexual assault settlement and a paper-thin résumé, the institution is not exercising restraint. It is laundering contempt into procedure.

The same goes for Blanche. The near-historic narrowness of the vote does not signal caution. It signals a body that knows the choice is toxic and makes it anyway.

Cassidy’s record is the evidence

Bill Cassidy is the useful example because he already gave the game away. He is a doctor who sat through RFK Jr.’s confirmation, heard the anti-vaccine nonsense confronted on the record, and still voted yes after taking White House assurances that collapsed the moment the roll was called. He did it again for Blanche.

That is not “balancing concerns.” That is not statesmanship. It is an elected official deciding that his own stated doubts matter less than party obedience and presidential demand. Cassidy did not get fooled once. He chose the damage, twice, after seeing the evidence.

The damage is not abstract

Hegseth’s record, as described here, is not a set of unfortunate mistakes. It is a model of institutional degradation: a reckless war with Iran that burned through munitions, contracts flowing to Trump-linked investment interests, 80,000 civilian jobs cut, and qualified Black and female officers denied promotions. That is not managerial error. That is the point of the appointment.

RFK Jr. at HHS and Blanche at Justice fit the same pattern. One brings conspiracy thinking into public health. The other turns law enforcement into protection for Trump. Different departments, same function: weaken the institution, politicize the chain of command, and replace public purpose with personal loyalty.

The pattern is capture, not chaos

The source text correctly identifies the individual senators who enabled these confirmations, but the larger pattern matters more than any single name. This is what authoritarian politics looks like in a constitutional costume: not tanks in the street, but senior officials selected for obedience; not outright abolition of institutions, but their conversion into instruments for one man’s protection and one faction’s power.

Republican senators keep invoking process because process is the disguise. The substance is surrender. The Senate is not merely failing to block bad nominees. It is helping build the machinery that makes bad nominations the norm.

What this really means

The lesson is not that a few nominees are unusually unfit. It is that institutional gatekeepers have become so disciplined by Trump that their job is no longer scrutiny, but ratification. Hegseth, RFK Jr., and Blanche are not anomalies at the edge of the system. They are what happens when the system stops defending its own standards.

That is the political fact underneath the spectacle: the rot is not accidental, and the blame does not belong to the weakest officials in the room. It belongs to the senators who knew better, voted anyway, and handed the state to men who treat public power as private property.
