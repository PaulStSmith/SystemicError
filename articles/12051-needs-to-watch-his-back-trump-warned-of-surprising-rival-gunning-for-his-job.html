'Needs to watch his back': Trump warned of surprising rival gunning for his job

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 5, 20255 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks in the Roosevelt Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 31, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Vice President JD Vance stands to benefit tremendously from President Donald Trump's botched response to the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, and he might have cause for concern.

The vice president was an odd choice as Trump's running mate from the start, and a Trump critic from way back, and USA Today columnist Rex Huppke said there was no one who had more to gain by the president's inability to move past questions surrounding his longtime relationship with the disgraced financier.

"Vance is an ideological contortionist backed by dodgy tech billionaires like Peter Thiel, Elon Musk and David Sacks, whose money helped elevate him to become Trump’s unlikely vice presidential pick," Huppke wrote.

"So is it that hard to believe Vance is watching Trump digging his Epstein hole deeper and deeper and thanking his lucky stars for the president’s incompetence and history of loutish behavior?" Huppke added. "The vice president was already on a magical political ride buoyed by his own lack of principles. Now all he needs to do is sit back and let Trump self-immolate while occasionally furrowing his brow at liberals and feigning support for the president."

Vance behaves publicly like a Trump loyalist, saying the president has "nothing to hide," but he hasn't deleted old social media posts challenging journalists to keep tugging at threads related to Epstein's sex trafficking case, and those reporters who have been pursuing the story keep finding evidence linking Trump.

"That surely puts a sparkle in Vance’s eye. And in the event more damaging details about Trump’s connection to Epstein come out, the vice president can always say, 'Gosh, he told me he had nothing to hide. Who could’ve imagined?'" Huppke wrote. "Present-day Vance seems to be saying: 'Shhhhhhhh. Let’s hang back and see how this plays out.'"

Huppke stopped short of accusing Vance of engaging in a conspiracy to take down his boss, but the columnist noted that Elon Musk, the tech billionaire who helped convince Trump to select him as running mate, helped revive the Epstein scandal after his acrimonious departure from his perch in the White House.

"Trump built his political career on conspiracies, and now he’s knee-deep in one," Huppke wrote. "So I’m comfortable throwing a not-unreasonable conspiracy onto the fire."

"Vance would never make a move on Trump or publicly root against him," the columnist added. "But if you don’t think he’d gladly slide into the big boy chair, you haven’t been paying attention. Trump needs to focus on the massive Epstein scandal burning around him. But if you ask me, he also needs to watch his back."

Our Analysis:

The Opportunist's Playbook: JD Vance's Calculated Silence

In the swirling vortex of scandal and controversy that has come to define the Trump administration, Vice President JD Vance emerges as a figure of particular intrigue. Not for actions loudly taken, but for the strategic silence and calculated inaction that could very well position him as the ultimate beneficiary of President Donald Trump's latest debacle: the Jeffrey Epstein scandal.

Rex Huppke of USA Today paints a vivid picture of Vance as an "ideological contortionist," a descriptor that might seem harsh at first glance but is undeniably grounded in the murky waters of political maneuvering and opportunism. Vance, propped up by the financial might of "dodgy tech billionaires" like Peter Thiel, Elon Musk, and David Sacks, finds himself in the unenviable position of being Trump's vice presidential pick—an alliance that seems more a marriage of convenience than a meeting of the minds.

The Spectacle of Silence

Vance's public persona as a Trump loyalist belies a more complex, perhaps more cunning strategy. His claim that Trump has "nothing to hide" in the face of mounting evidence to the contrary regarding the Epstein scandal does not reflect naivety or blind loyalty. Instead, it signals a man playing a long game, one where silence is complicit and inaction is a form of action.

His refusal to delete old social media posts challenging journalists to dig deeper into Epstein's sex trafficking case is a masterstroke of passive aggression. It allows Vance to maintain an aura of plausible deniability, positioning himself as a seeker of truth, all while benefiting from the increasing scrutiny on Trump's connections to Epstein.

A Conspiratorial Undertone

The article stops short of outright accusing Vance of plotting Trump's downfall, but the implications are clear. With allies like Elon Musk—who himself has played a role in reviving the Epstein scandal—Vance is not the solitary figure he might seem. Instead, he represents a faction within the Trump administration that sees the Epstein scandal not as a disaster to be mitigated but as an opportunity to be exploited.

"Trump built his political career on conspiracies, and now he’s knee-deep in one," Huppke aptly notes. This statement is not just an observation but a warning. The political landscape is shifting, and Vance, with his calculated silence and strategic inaction, seems poised to ride the wave rather than be crushed by it.

Conclusion: The Opportunistic Silence

Vance's behavior is emblematic of a broader trend in politics where loyalty is often performative, principles are malleable, and power is the ultimate prize. While Trump battles the flames of the Epstein scandal, Vance's silence speaks volumes. He need not voice opposition or plot in the shadows; he merely needs to wait, watch, and, when the time is right, step forward from the chaos unscathed and more powerful than before.

In the end, Vance's magical political ride, as Huppke describes it, is not just a journey marked by a lack of principles but a calculated exercise in opportunism. As Trump's administration continues to be embroiled in scandal, one cannot help but wonder if Vance's silence is not only golden but strategically so.
