Judge slams Trump's DOJ for making a rookie error in Minnesota protester probe

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi attends a House Judiciary Committee hearing on oversight of the Justice Department to testify, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 11, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A federal judge slammed President Donald Trump’s Department of Justice during a recent court order after the agency admitted to making a rookie mistake in the probe of protesters who attended a church service in St. Paul, Minnesota, according to a new report.

In January, protesters attended a service at City Church in St. Paul, where the pastor, Dave Easterwood, also serves as a top Immigration and Customs Enforcement official in the state. Following the protest, the Department of Justice charged 39 people, including former CNN anchor Don Lemon, with conspiring to violate religious freedoms and interfering with the exercise of religious freedoms.

On Saturday, the New York Times reported that Trump’s DOJ dropped charges against one of the protesters “in an apparent case of mistaken identity,” a move that Judge Douglas L. Micko in the District of Minnesota argued could have been avoided if the DOJ adhered to some rudimentary steps.

“Here we are, months into a case that the government had an intense appetite to initiate, but cannot seem to keep up the pace when it comes to discovery obligations,” Micko wrote. “This is unacceptable.”

The defendant’s lawyer, Brock Hunter, told the NYT that the government believed his client was one of the protesters because they cross-referenced her cell phone location data with video footage and his client’s driver’s license.

“Mr. Hunter said the government could have avoided the blunder if it had sent Ms. Lewis a letter informing her that she was under criminal investigation,” the report reads. “That routine step in federal cases can clear up misconceptions before charges are filed or pave the way for plea deals early on.”

Our Analysis:

Missteps in Justice: Unraveling the DOJ’s Blunder

In a recent judicial rebuke that should alarm anyone concerned with the integrity of the U.S. legal system, a federal judge castigated President Donald Trump’s Department of Justice (DOJ) for what can only be described as a glaring oversight in the prosecution of protesters in St. Paul, Minnesota. This incident, involving the wrongful charging of an individual attending a church service-turned-protest, not only exposes the pitfalls of hasty legal pursuits but also raises crucial questions about accountability and oversight within the DOJ. Let’s dissect the situation to understand who truly holds the power, who made the critical decisions, and whether the framing of the narrative misdirects responsibility.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Who Holds the Power?

The Department of Justice, under the Trump administration, is the central player wielding institutional power in this scenario. As a federal agency, the DOJ has broad authority to enforce the law and administer justice in the United States. Its decisions, especially regarding whom to charge with crimes, carry significant weight and consequences.

Who Made the Decision?

The decision to charge 39 people, including high-profile individuals like former CNN anchor Don Lemon, with conspiring to violate religious freedoms was made by officials within the DOJ. However, the subsequent dropping of charges against one protester due to mistaken identity underscores a critical lapse in judgment and due diligence on the part of these officials.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article implies that a singular “rookie mistake” led to the wrongful charging of an individual. However, this framing potentially obfuscates a larger systemic issue within the DOJ regarding the rush to prosecute without adequate evidence or investigation. Judge Douglas L. Micko’s comments highlight a deeper problem of failing to adhere to fundamental procedural steps, such as verifying the identity of suspects thoroughly before pressing charges.

Is the Criticism Misplaced?

To an extent, yes. While the article focuses on this specific incident under Trump’s DOJ, it is essential to recognize that such procedural failures reflect broader institutional shortcomings that transcend individual administrations. Criticism should not only target the operatives who made the mistake but also question the systemic practices that allowed such an error to occur.

Conclusion: Beyond a “Rookie Mistake”

Labeling the DOJ’s failure as a mere “rookie mistake” dangerously minimizes the gravity of the situation. This incident reveals a concerning eagerness within the DOJ to initiate prosecutions without sufficient groundwork, risking the rights and freedoms of individuals. The real story here is not just about a procedural blunder; it’s about the need for systemic reforms within the Department of Justice to prevent such egregious errors in the future.

It’s crucial that the DOJ takes this critique seriously, not only to rectify this particular case but to ensure that its procedures and checks are robust enough to prevent similar mistakes. Accountability must be demanded at all levels of the agency to restore faith in the justice system’s fairness and precision. In this instance, while the DOJ holds the institutional power and made the decision leading to the wrongful charge, the narrative should focus more on systemic reform than on casting aspersions on specific actors within the system.
