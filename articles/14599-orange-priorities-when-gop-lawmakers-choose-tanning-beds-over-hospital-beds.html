Orange Priorities: When GOP Lawmakers Choose Tanning Beds Over Hospital Beds

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 21, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There are moments in American politics so absurd, so revealing, so jaw-droppingly tone-deaf that they act as a spotlight on the decaying conscience of those entrusted with power. One such moment unfolded recently in a congressional committee hearing when a Republican lawmaker, presumably the author or sponsor of a massive piece of legislation, was asked to read a single line from his own bill. A simple request. But it was met not with confidence or transparency, but with visible discomfort, evasion, and a tacit admission of either ignorance or shame.

The line in question? Buried deep in the legislative morass on page 901, line 20, was this gem: “Repeal of excise tax on indoor tanning services.”

Yes. In a nation where rural hospitals are shuttering, where life expectancy is slipping, where healthcare access is a daily struggle for millions of Americans—this bill saw fit to offer a tax break to tanning salons. While you gasp for air waiting for a hospital bed in Appalachia or the Midwest, rest assured that somewhere, someone is enjoying a cheaper tan thanks to the Republican Party.

When asked directly to read the text, the congressman deflected. He attempted to pass the buck to other members. He stammered. He waffled. Ultimately, he refused. It was left to a Democratic member, Representative Teresa Leger Fernandez, to read the offending line aloud. And in that moment, the farce was exposed for what it was: a party so unwilling to defend the indefensible that they couldn’t even summon the courage to say it out loud.

Let that sink in: the GOP couldn’t defend their own bill on record. Not with facts. Not with spin. Not even with the laughable excuse that it was somehow in the public interest. Instead, they choked on their own legislation like a child caught sneaking cookies before dinner.

And then came the jab that landed harder than anything else: “We do know that there are certain elected officials who appear to have a certain orange hue about them…”

The implication was unmistakable. It was a not-so-subtle reference to the former president, a man who made spray tanning a political aesthetic. A party that deifies his image has now enshrined his glow into the tax code.

But this isn’t just a funny clip to laugh at online. It’s not just late-night comedy fodder. It’s a damning portrait of legislative priorities in the modern Republican Party. When public health is pitted against private vanity, they chose vanity. When life-saving infrastructure in rural communities is up for debate, they chose to champion bronzing booths.

This isn’t just political theater. It is a betrayal. A grotesque abandonment of duty. Because there is absolutely no universe—parallel or otherwise—in which a tax break for indoor tanning should take precedence over funding emergency services, community clinics, and rural hospitals gasping for support.

And let’s be perfectly clear: this isn’t an isolated incident. It’s a pattern. It’s a consistent throughline in GOP policymaking. From opposing Medicaid expansion to slashing pandemic preparedness budgets, to now... subsidizing tanning? The message is clear: image matters more than integrity. Surface over substance. Politics as performance, while the nation bleeds out in the wings.

This bill, and the spinelessness surrounding its defense, is a fluorescent sign flashing “we don’t care about you.” And the people should remember that. The next time a Republican lawmaker campaigns on the promise of serving "real Americans," ask them how many emergency rooms they helped fund. Ask them how many nurses they supported. And then ask them how much cheaper it is now to get a spray tan.

The modern GOP isn’t just out of touch. It’s been bronzed, lacquered, and shrink-wrapped in its own narcissism. And while they bask under UV lights, the rest of the country is left in the dark.
