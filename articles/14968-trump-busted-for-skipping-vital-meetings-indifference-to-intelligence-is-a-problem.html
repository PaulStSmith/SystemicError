The president infamously skipped nearly all of his intelligence briefings during his 2016 transition process and had little patience for them during his first term, saying he didn't need them because he was "like, a smart person." MSNBC's Steve Benen wrote that he's even less interested the second time around in hearing the information he needs to guide his decision making.

"It’s worth emphasizing that different presidents have approached these briefings in different ways," Benen wrote. "George W. Bush received intelligence briefings on a nearly daily basis. Barack Obama received briefings roughly every other day, but he was known to be a voracious reader of the written President’s Daily Brief (often referred to as the PDB). Joe Biden received an in-person briefing once or twice a week, but like Obama, he was also known to read the PDB briefing book."

Trump reportedly doesn't read the PDB and gets an in-person briefing about once every 10 days.

"Broadly speaking, a couple of angles are worth keeping in mind in response to reporting like this," Benen wrote. "The first is probably obvious: Trump is dealing with serious national security challenges — war in Ukraine, a crisis in the Middle East, China expanding its global influence, domestic security threats, et al. — and the United States is being led by an incurious former television personality who desperately needs — but apparently isn’t getting — valuable information that would lead to better decision-making."

"Less obvious, however, is the pattern: The problem isn’t just that Trump is avoiding intelligence he needs; the problem is made worse by the fact that Trump has always avoided intelligence he needs," the columnist added.

That's what caused him to miss the early alarms on the Covid-19 pandemic, because he rarely read the PDB and had "little patience" for in-person summaries of the intelligence, and he instead reportedly interrupted those meetings with gossip or off-topic tangents while relying on conservative media and his own friends for information.

"It was an extraordinary revelation to consider: A sitting American president, in a time of multiple and dangerous crises, was so resistant to learning about security threats that his own country’s intelligence officials have sought outside help to figure out how to get him to listen and focus," Benen wrote.

"Or, put another way, Trump’s indifference to intelligence is a problem, but it’s not a new problem," he added.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Disregard for Intelligence Briefings: A Dangerous Precedent

In an analysis that is as alarming as it is unsurprising, President Donald Trump's approach to intelligence briefings in his second term has been characterized by a level of apathy that borders on willful ignorance. MSNBC's Steve Benen offers a stark portrayal of a president who, despite holding the most powerful office on the planet, exhibits a disturbing disinterest in the very information designed to inform and guide crucial decisions affecting not only the United States but the global community at large.

The Indifference of a "Smart Person"

Trump's claim of not needing intelligence briefings because he is "like, a smart person" is a statement so fraught with arrogance and folly that it almost defies belief. Intelligence briefings, a staple in the toolkit of any leader worth their salt, are not just another bureaucratic hoop to jump through; they are critical updates on the state of the world, offering insights into threats, opportunities, and challenges. To dismiss them is to fly not just blind, but backwards into the face of crises.

A Comparative Look

Benen's analysis does well to contextualize Trump's apathy by comparing it with his predecessors. George W. Bush and Barack Obama, with their near-daily briefings and voracious reading habits, respectively, underscore what it means to actively engage with intelligence. Joe Biden's approach, combining in-person briefings with reading the President’s Daily Brief (PDB), further highlights the norm from which Trump deviates. The stark contrast between Trump's once every 10 days briefing and his predecessors' more engaged practices could not be more telling.

The Consequences of Ignorance

Trump's disinterest in intelligence briefings is not merely a personal failing; it is a dereliction of duty that has real and dire consequences. The article points to Trump's mishandling of the COVID-19 pandemic as a case in point. His preference for gossip and conservative media over in-depth intelligence allowed the early alarms of the pandemic to go unheeded, with catastrophic results. This pattern of avoidance is not just troubling; it is downright dangerous.

The Unsurprising Pattern

What Benen describes as an "extraordinary revelation" is, for those who have followed Trump's presidency, not surprising. Trump's indifference to intelligence, characterized as a "new problem" by Benen, is anything but. It is a continuation of a well-documented pattern of behavior that prioritizes personal belief and convenience over hard facts and expert advice.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The implications of Trump's disregard for intelligence briefings cannot be overstated. In a world fraught with "serious national security challenges," the need for a leader who is informed, curious, and willing to engage with complex information is paramount. Trump's failure to fulfill this basic requirement of his office is not just a personal failing; it is a systemic failure that puts the entire nation, and indeed the world, at risk.

In closing, Benen's analysis, while insightful, barely scratches the surface of the profound recklessness embodied by Trump's approach to intelligence. It is a symptom of a much larger problem: a disregard for the mechanisms of governance and a blatant disrespect for the office he holds. This behavior should not be normalized or excused but seen for what it is — a dangerous abdication of responsibility.
