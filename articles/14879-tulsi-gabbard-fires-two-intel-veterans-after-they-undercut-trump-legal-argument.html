Tulsi Gabbard fires two intel veterans after they undercut Trump legal argument

Tulsi Gabbard is sworn in as Director of National Intelligence at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Intelligence chief Tulsi Gabbard fired a pair of veteran analysts whose work undercut a key legal argument the Trump administration had used in court.

The director of national intelligence dismissed the top two career officials leading the National Intelligence Council, the in the intelligence community's senior most analytical group that determines the biggest threats to U.S. security, after Gabbard's office released a declassified assessment that undercut administration claims about the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua, reported CNN.

"Gabbard fired Mike Collins, the acting chair, and his deputy, Maria Langan-Riekhof, on Tuesday," the network reported, citing a spokesman's confirmation of their firings.

The NIC's declassified memo found no coordination between Tren de Aragua and the Venezuelan government, which contradicted statements by Trump administration officials used to justify the invocation of the Alien Enemies Act and deportation of Venezuelan migrants.

Some portions of that memo had already been leaked to the media, which Gabbard said was already under review, and she blamed reporters for “twisting and manipulating intelligence assessments to undermine the president’s agenda to keep the American people safe.”

Former intelligence official Jonathan Panikoff, who served on the NIC and worked with both newly fired officials, said Collins is “an unbelievable professional who’s served selflessly for 30 years and is a real China expert,” and Langan-Riekhof “is not just a strategic thinker but an unbelievably gifted analyst."

Gabbard recently told conservative podcaster Megyn Kelly there were 11 internal investigations currently under way into unauthorized disclosures to the media and said she has referred three of those cases to the Department of Justice for potential prosecution.

“Really what is happening when they do that is they’re undermining our democracy because what they’re doing… is saying, ‘I’m doing what’s best for the country and I know what’s better for the country than the majority of the American people who chose this duly elected president,” Gabbard told Kelly. “The only way we bring about accountability is by doing the work of conducting these investigations.”

Our Analysis:

Intelligence Community Shakeup: A Closer Look

In a recent move that has sent shockwaves through the corridors of power, Intelligence Chief Tulsi Gabbard has ousted two seasoned analysts from the National Intelligence Council (NIC). This decision does not merely reflect internal personnel changes but underscores a deeper, more troubling manipulation of intelligence to serve political ends. The ousted analysts, Mike Collins and Maria Langan-Riekhof, were not just any employees; they were leading figures in the intelligence community's premier analytical group. Their sin? Producing a declassified assessment that dared to challenge the Trump administration's narrative on the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua.

Facts Over Fiction? Not in This Administration

According to reports from CNN, the NIC's assessment found no evidence of coordination between the Venezuelan gang Tren de Aragua and the Venezuelan government. This revelation directly contradicts the administration's claims, which were used to justify harsh measures against Venezuelan migrants under the Alien Enemies Act. The administration's eagerness to deport Venezuelan migrants on shaky grounds is not just a policy issue; it's a moral failing that uses human lives as pawns in political gamesmanship.

The Irony of Undermining Democracy

Gabbard's criticism of the leaked memo and her accusation against reporters for "twisting and manipulating intelligence assessments" is rich with irony. Undermining the president’s agenda is not the same as undermining democracy, despite Gabbard’s claims. If anything, the attempt to suppress or alter intelligence findings to fit a political narrative is a genuine threat to the democratic process. It's crucial to question who is really "doing what’s best for the country" here. Is it the analysts, striving for honesty in their assessments, or those who seek to dismiss inconvenient truths?

The Professional Caliber of the Ousted Analysts

The credentials and professionalism of Collins and Langan-Riekhof speak volumes. Described by former intelligence official Jonathan Panikoff as an "unbelievable professional" and an "unbelievably gifted analyst," their dismissals are not just a loss for the NIC but for the integrity of U.S. intelligence. By sidelining experienced and principled officials, the administration risks echoing in an echo chamber of its own making, devoid of critical analysis and dissenting voices.

The So-Called "Accountability"

Gabbard’s call for accountability through investigations into unauthorized disclosures is a smokescreen. It’s a tactic to redirect attention from the administration's accountability for misusing intelligence. Referring cases to the Department of Justice for potential prosecution over leaks is a clear message: fall in line, or face consequences. This is not accountability; it's intimidation.

Conclusion

The firing of Mike Collins and Maria Langan-Riekhof is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend. It highlights a willingness to sacrifice truth at the altar of political expediency. As these events unfold, one must ponder the cost to the nation's security and democratic principles. When intelligence serves politics, the truth becomes the first casualty.
