These 4 cowards are living in luxury — and throwing the nation to Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 29, 20263 min read

The Real Story Behind the Scandal

In the swirl of accusations and expectations surrounding former Presidents Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, Barack Obama, and Joe Biden, a crucial question emerges: what can they realistically do against Donald Trump’s alleged dismantling of government and the economy? The article suggests these ex-presidents should mobilize the citizenry to impeach Trump, but this call overlooks the structural limits of their power and misdirects responsibility.

Assessing the Power and Responsibility

Institutional Power: The Reality

The former presidents, despite their influential legacies, do not hold any formal power in government once they leave office. Their ability to enact legal or executive action against a sitting president is non-existent. This fundamental misunderstanding of institutional power skews expectations of what they can realistically achieve.

Decision-Making Authority

The decision to impeach a president rests with the current Congress, not with individuals who no longer hold office. While these former leaders can influence public opinion, they cannot directly initiate any legal actions against Trump. The article’s call for them to form impeachment groups shows a significant misreading of their capabilities and roles.

Misdirected Responsibility

The framing of the article suggests that the responsibility for stopping Trump rests solely on the shoulders of these ex-presidents. This not only misdirects responsibility but also minimizes the roles of currently elected officials and the judicial system, which are the proper channels for addressing any legal transgressions by a sitting president.

The Role of Former Presidents

While it’s true that former presidents can play a significant role in shaping public discourse, their main power lies in their ability to speak out and influence. However, the expectation for them to lead a grassroots movement to impeach Trump ignores the practical limitations of their post-presidential roles and the existing political structures.

Public Influence vs. Political Power

Former presidents often engage in advocacy and public speaking, raising awareness about issues. However, there is a stark difference between influencing public opinion and wielding political power. The article conflates the two, leading to unrealistic expectations of direct political action from private citizens, albeit highly influential ones.

Activities of the Ex-Presidents

The critique of their current activities — such as Bush’s painting and Obama’s involvement with March Madness — is misplaced. These activities do not negate their ability to speak out on issues, nor do they obligate them to lead political movements. Their engagements in various causes and speeches, however limited, do not equate to abdication of public responsibility as private citizens.

Media and Public Engagement

The role of the media in covering the actions, or inactions, of these former presidents is also crucial. However, the expectation that media should coerce them into action misinterprets the role of journalism, which is to report, not to mobilize former leaders to undertake political crusades.

Conclusion: The Misallocation of Blame

The narrative that former presidents are responsible for countering Trump personally and politically is a misallocation of blame that overlooks the actual mechanisms of power in the U.S. political system. While their voices are powerful, expecting them to act as de facto political leaders in office is both unrealistic and institutionally ignorant.

Rather than looking to past presidents for salvation from current political woes, the focus should be on the legally designated bodies and officials currently in power. It is they who can and should be held accountable for addressing the issues raised by Trump’s presidency, not those who have already served their terms.
