President Donald Trump sat down for a lengthy interview with Time Magazine on the occasion of his 100th day in office, and the reporter who spoke with him revealed what he believes was one the most "noteworthy" comments.

The magazine's Eric Cortellessa asked the president whether he had asked El Salvador president Nayib Bukele to return Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a legal U.S. resident mistakenly deported out of the country, and Trump said no one had asked him to request that – although the U.S. Supreme Court had ruled unanimously that the administration should facilitate his return.

"I thought that was very noteworthy in the interview," Cortellessa said. "You know, I started you know, we started that sequence basically asking him about, you know, his commitment to complying with court orders. When I interviewed president Trump a year ago, in April of 2024, he said he would always comply with Supreme Court orders, and then I said, well, look, the Supreme Court just ruled 9-0 that you have to bring this guy back – why haven't you? – and his answer was that his lawyers read the ruling differently, that it did not order him to bring back Kilmar Abrego Garcia."

"I then read, you know, from the ruling saying that the order required him to facilitate his release, and when I asked, you know, have you have you asked president of El Salvador whether you would bring him back?" Cortellessa added. "He said that he hadn't, that his lawyers didn't tell him he had to, and that nobody had even asked him that except for me. I did get the sense that president Trump was following the advice of lawyers on this, on his answers to this question."

Host John Berman was skeptical of the president's claims.

"Yeah, I have a hard time believing you're the first person to raise the possibility with him, even though I do agree," Berman said. "It's a great question on your part, but I have to believe other people have suggested it was at least a possibility."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Defiance of the Supreme Court: A Dangerous Precedent?

In a recent Time Magazine interview marking President Donald Trump's 100th day in office, a startling revelation was made that not only challenges the very fabric of the United States' constitutional framework but also highlights a concerning disregard for judicial authority at the highest level of government.

Eric Cortellessa, the interviewer, unearthed a notable admission from President Trump concerning the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a legal U.S. resident who was mistakenly deported. Despite the U.S. Supreme Court's unanimous ruling that the administration should facilitate his return, Trump disclosed that he had made no effort to comply with this order, claiming that his lawyers interpreted the ruling differently.

The Supreme Court's Unanimous Decision: A Mere Suggestion?

The Supreme Court's 9-0 ruling is not a light recommendation that can be brushed aside based on alternative legal interpretations. It is a binding decision that the executive branch is constitutionally obliged to execute. Trump's admission that he disregarded this ruling because his lawyers "read it differently" sets a dangerous precedent where executive interpretation can effectively veto the judiciary's decisions.

Legal Advice or Legal Abdication?

Trump's reliance on his legal team's interpretation raises questions about the integrity and independence of legal advice given to the president. It's concerning that these advisors seem to support, or at least enable, a selective adherence to the rule of law. This behavior is not just a red flag; it's a siren call, signaling a potential erosion of democratic norms and principles.

Accountability and the Rule of Law

The incident is emblematic of a broader issue within Trump's administration: a systematic disregard for judicial authority and an alarming inclination towards autocracy. When a president can ignore the Supreme Court without consequence, it undermines the checks and balances that are foundational to American democracy.

The Role of the Press and Public Inquiry

Cortellessa's pursuit of this line of questioning, and Trump's subsequent admission, underscores the vital role of the press in holding power to account. However, the skepticism voiced by John Berman, host of the interview, reflects a broader disbelief and concern. It's implausible that Cortellessa was the first to address this issue with Trump, suggesting a lack of serious engagement from the administration on matters of legal and ethical importance.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call for Democratic Vigilance

This episode is not just a footnote in Trump's presidency; it's a glaring illustration of a leader who believes himself above the law. The executive branch's failure to comply with a direct Supreme Court order is an affront to the judiciary and a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic institutions. It's a wake-up call for vigilant defense of the rule of law, transparency, and accountability in governance. Without these, the very pillars of democracy are at risk of crumbling under the weight of autocratic tendencies.
