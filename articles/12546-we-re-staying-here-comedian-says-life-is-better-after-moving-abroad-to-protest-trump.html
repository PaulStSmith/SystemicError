'We're staying here': Comedian says 'life is better' after moving abroad to protest Trump

By Paulo Santos

Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump gestures to the crowd as sons Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr. look on near the exit, during a campaign rally at J.S. Dorton Arena in Raleigh, North Carolina, U.S., November 4, 2024. REUTERS/Brian Snyder TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

A famous American comedian confirmed reports that she left the country because of President Donald Trump's election last November, according to a new report.

Ellen DeGeneres and her wife, Portia de Rossi, moved to the United Kingdom before Trump was elected. DeGeneres told a crowd at Cheltenham, Gloucestershire that "life is just better here" because America can be "scary for people to be who they are," the BBC reported.

"We got here the day before the election and woke up to lots of texts from our friends with crying emojis, and I was like, 'He got in'," DeGeneres told the outlet. "And we're like, 'We're staying here'."

It's been almost six months since Trump was sworn in for his second term, but he's already made a major impact on American life. For instance, Trump has initiated one of the most wide-scale deportation efforts in American history. He has also passed legislation that makes sweeping changes to the health care system and social benefits such as Medicaid and food stamps.

To DeGeneres, who was one of America's biggest television stars for more than three decades, Trump's election inspired her and de Rossi to pursue a new life together. They are considering getting married in the U.K. after the dust from their move settles.

"It's absolutely beautiful," DeGeneres told the BBC. "We're just not used to seeing this kind of beauty. The villages and the towns and the architecture - everything you see is charming, and it's just a simpler way of life."

"It's clean. Everything here is just better - the way animals are treated, and people are polite. I just love it here," she continued.

DeGeneres isn't the only comedian to move abroad after Trump's election. Rosie O'Donnell moved to Ireland in March 2025.

O'Donnell slammed Trump's attacks against comedians in a recent Substack post.

"You don’t want free speech — you want blind loyalty," O'Donnell wrote. "But America doesn’t work like that. We question power here. We laugh at bullies."

Our Analysis:

The Great American Exodus: A Tale of Ellen, Portia, and the Trump Era

In a move that's less about the scenic beauty of the United Kingdom and more a scathing indictment of the current American socio-political climate under President Donald Trump, Ellen DeGeneres' relocation story unfolds. It's a narrative that intertwines personal liberation with political protest, a theme that's becoming all too familiar in today's America.

Ellen DeGeneres and Portia de Rossi's departure to the UK isn't just a whimsical quest for quaint villages and polite society. It's a bold statement against what America has morphed into—a place where, as DeGeneres puts it, it's "scary for people to be who they are." This sentiment, shared by many, underscores a broader concern: the erosion of a safe and inclusive environment under Trump's administration.

Trump's America: A Landscape of Polarization and Fear

Let's dissect the meat of the matter. Trump's reign has been marked by policies that seem to systematically dismantle the very fabric of inclusivity and human rights. The "wide-scale deportation efforts" reflect not just a hardline immigration stance but a broader, more disturbing nativist sentiment. Sweeping changes to the health care system and social benefits, such as Medicaid and food stamps, don't just alter policy—they threaten the well-being of millions of vulnerable Americans.

Ellen and Portia's decision to consider marriage in the UK isn't a mere change of venue; it's a poignant critique of the shrinking space for personal freedoms and rights in their homeland. Their story is a microcosm of the larger exodus of those who find the current American socio-political landscape untenable.

The Charm of the UK vs. The Chaos of Trump's America

The allure of the UK, with its "simpler way of life," "cleanliness," and the overall treatment of animals and people, speaks volumes. It's not just the physical beauty that captivates but the stark contrast in societal values and governance. Ellen's admiration for the UK's charm is interlaced with a subtle yet biting critique of what America has lost or perhaps never fully had.

The Echo of Rosie O'Donnell's Relocation

Rosie O'Donnell's move to Ireland and her subsequent slam against Trump's disdain for free speech and comedians further amplify the narrative of discontent. O'Donnell's assertion—"But America doesn’t work like that. We question power here. We laugh at bullies."—is a powerful reminder of the core American values that seem to be under siege.

Conclusion: The Bitter Irony and a Call to Action

The tale of Ellen DeGeneres and Portia de Rossi's relocation is more than a celebrity gossip column. It's a wake-up call—a bitter irony that those who once symbolized the American dream now seek refuge elsewhere. It underscores a critical question: When the champions of free speech, inclusivity, and human rights abandon ship, what does that say about the state of the nation?

As we ponder Ellen and Portia's new life in the UK, let's not miss the forest for the trees. This isn't just about escaping Trump's America; it's a clarion call for introspection and action. If America is to reclaim its soul, it must start by questioning power, laughing at bullies, and, most importantly, protecting the rights and freedoms of all its people.
