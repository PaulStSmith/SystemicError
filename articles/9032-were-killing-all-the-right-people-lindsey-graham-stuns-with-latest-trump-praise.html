'We’re killing all the right people!' Lindsey Graham stuns with latest Trump praise

U.S. Senator Lindsey Graham (R-SC) speaks to the media, after a Senate GOP lunch, as Republican lawmakers struggle to pass U.S. President Donald Trump’s sweeping spending and tax bill, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 28, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) heaped praise onto President Donald Trump over the weekend while speaking at the the Republican Jewish Coalition Leadership Summit in Las Vegas, Nevada, — plaudits that quickly derailed into celebrating the administration’s recent wave of state-sanctioned killings.

“I feel good about the Republican Party and where we’re going as a nation,” Graham said to an energetic crowd. “We’re killing all the right people and we’re cutting your taxes! Trump is my favorite president. We’ve run out of bombs. We didn’t run out of bombs in World War II!”

The Trump administration has carried out several operations abroad that have resulted in fatalities, perhaps most notably the ongoing strikes on suspected drug cartels in the Caribbean, which to date have killed at least 64 people in what critics – including a prominent Republican senator – have labeled as illegal “extrajudicial killings.”

Back in March, the Trump administration also killed at least 31 people after the president authorized large-scale strikes on Yemen to combat the Houthis, and in defense of Israel which the Houthis had launched strikes against in response to its siege on Gaza.

At the summit, Graham also heaped praise onto the Republican Jewish Coalition, a nonprofit political advocacy group and political action committee that advocates for a strong U.S.-Israel alliance, among other things.

“As a coalition of Republicans – some Baptist, some Jewish – we organize ourselves around principles that we love and we cherish; we love individual freedom, we love entrepreneurship, we love capitalism… we love Israel,” Graham said. “We love the idea of Israel, we love the struggle they’ve been involved in, we love helping them because you’re helping yourself.”

A clip of the video was shared by the X account Republicans Against Trump, with a flood of shocked responses.

Graham has been among the most hawkish lawmakers in Congress, having regularly advocated for U.S. military intervention abroad. Specifically, Graham has publicly voiced support for military action againstIran,North Korea,Venezuela,Iraq,Syria,Libya.andMexico, and many on repeated occasions.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Praise of State-Sanctioned Killings

In a world where political discourse should ideally be governed by principles of humanity, justice, and international law, comments like those made by Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) at the Republican Jewish Coalition Leadership Summit in Las Vegas, Nevada, strike a dissonant chord that is as alarming as it is revealing. Graham’s effusive praise for President Donald Trump, culminating in a celebration of “state-sanctioned killings,” not only underscores a troubling disregard for human life but also provides a stark insight into the current ethos dominating segments of the Republican Party.

“We’re killing all the right people and we’re cutting your taxes!” This statement, seemingly made with pride, encapsulates a profound moral bankruptcy at the heart of certain political circles. To publicly declare that the measure of a political party’s success is its efficiency in killing and fiscal benefits to its constituents is to ignore the foundational principles of governance and human rights.

The Troubling Context of Graham’s Remarks

The operations cited by Graham, including the ongoing strikes on suspected drug cartels in the Caribbean and large-scale strikes in Yemen, have resulted in significant loss of life. The fact that these operations have been criticized, including by prominent figures within his own party, as “illegal ‘extrajudicial killings,’” seems to be of little concern to Graham. Rather than engaging with the legal and ethical implications of these actions, Graham appears to revel in their supposed effectiveness, devoid of any critical reflection on their consequences.

A Misguided Celebration of Military Intervention

Graham’s record as one of the most hawkish lawmakers, advocating for U.S. military intervention across a swath of countries, is well-documented. However, the glee with which he speaks of running out of bombs—a distressing echo of wartime scarcity—reveals a disturbing enthusiasm for military action as an end in itself. The comparison to World War II, a conflict marked by unprecedented human suffering and destruction, as a benchmark for sufficient armament, is both inappropriate and insensitive.

The Republican Jewish Coalition and the Ideology of Support

Graham’s comments at the summit also touched on the coalition’s support for Israel, framing it within a context of shared values like individual freedom, entrepreneurship, and capitalism. While support for an ally is a legitimate aspect of foreign policy, Graham’s framing—especially within the same speech glorifying military action and state-sanctioned killings—risks conflating these values with an unconditional endorsement of conflict. It’s a dangerous rhetorical move that simplifies complex international relations into a binary of us-vs-them, ignoring the nuanced realities on the ground.

The Reaction and the Reality

The shock expressed by viewers of the clip shared by Republicans Against Trump on X (formerly Twitter) indicates a widespread recognition of the inappropriateness of Graham’s remarks. Yet, the fact that such statements can be made openly at a political summit suggests that they may reflect a broader acceptance, or at least a tolerance, of such views within certain segments of the party and its support base.

In Conclusion:

Sen. Lindsey Graham’s comments, while shockingly blunt, are not merely the ramblings of a single politician. They are symptomatic of a deeper, more systemic issue within certain political factions: a cavalier attitude towards violence and a troubling disregard for the sanctity of human life. This mindset, when left unchecked, poses a significant threat not only to the United States’ moral standing in the world but to the very fabric of international peace and security. It is a stark reminder of the need for a return to principled leadership and a recommitment to the values of humanity, justice, and the rule of law.
