At the same time that Donald Trump was addressing cadets at West Point's graduation ceremonies, the Washington Post is reporting that some midshipmen at the U.S. Naval Academy are sneaking around to share complaints about the President's policies.

On Saturday Trump gave a half-commencement speech, half-MAGA rally rant as he spoke to the West Point graduates and their families.

During that speech –– when he wasn't talking about "trophy wives" –– he bragged about ridding the nation's military academies of "DEI policies" before ranting, "We have liberated our troops from divisive and demeaning political trainings. There will be no more critical race theory or transgender for everybody forced onto the brave men and women in uniform, or on anybody else for that matter, in this country. We will not have men playing in women’s sports.”

With former scandal-plagued Fox News personality Pete Hegseth acting as Trump's defense secretary and immediately purging the military of the policies that offend MAGA conservatives, the Post is reporting that there is a growing resistance at the Naval Academy.

"At the U.S. Naval Academy, some midshipmen have started using nongovernment email addresses to run underground versions of the same affinity clubs disbanded by their administration. Others have used the new email addresses to communicate their concerns about banned books or shuttered clubs to professors, according to three faculty members interviewed by The Washington Post," the report states.

According to one of those educators who wished to remain anonymous admitted he told his students, "There is a point where compliance turns into complicity.”

“We at the Naval Academy are here to prepare young officers to command. They need to know what we have learned from our study of politics and history and literature and languages,” they explained. “We are failing them and we are failing in our jobs if we suppress some things we know are true and we parrot other things we know are false.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Divisive Rhetoric at West Point: An Analysis

In a move that surprises precisely no one, Donald Trump managed to transform a West Point graduation ceremony into a platform for his characteristic blend of ego, controversy, and political provocation. According to a recent exposé by the Washington Post, while Trump was busy delivering what can only be described as a "half-commencement speech, half-MAGA rally rant," a quiet rebellion was brewing at the U.S. Naval Academy against his administration's policies.

The Speech: A Conduit for Controversy

Trump's speech at West Point was nothing short of a spectacle—a blend of self-aggrandizement and political diatribe. His remarks about "trophy wives" and the eradication of "DEI policies" from military academies were emblematic of his administration's broader stance against diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives. The former president proudly proclaimed the end of "divisive and demeaning political trainings," specifically targeting critical race theory and transgender inclusion. Trump's assertion that "We will not have men playing in women’s sports" is a clear nod to the ongoing culture wars that continue to polarize the nation.

The Underground Resistance at the Naval Academy

Meanwhile, at the U.S. Naval Academy, a story of quiet resistance unfolds. Midshipmen, in defiance of the administration's crackdown on diversity and inclusion programs, have resorted to using non-government email addresses to sustain their affinity clubs and communicate concerns over banned books and the shuttering of clubs. This move, as reported by the Washington Post, underscores the deep divisions and dissatisfaction within the ranks of future military leaders regarding the Trump administration's policies.

One educator's comment to their students that "there is a point where compliance turns into complicity" resonates as a stark reminder of the moral quagmires faced by those in service when confronted with policies they find ethically or morally objectionable. This educator, along with others, emphasizes the importance of a broad education in preparing young officers to lead, suggesting that the suppression of diverse voices and viewpoints does a disservice not only to the individuals at the academy but to the very essence of what it means to command.

The Bigger Picture: A Nation Divided

This episode at West Point and the Naval Academy is symptomatic of a larger issue plaguing the United States—a deep, persistent divide over the role of diversity, equity, and inclusion in public life. Trump's speech and the subsequent revelations from the Naval Academy underscore a troubling reality: the politicization of military education and leadership training. The insistence on purging so-called "divisive" elements from the curriculum not only undermines the academy's mission to foster well-rounded, critically thinking leaders but also mirrors the broader societal tensions at play.

Pete Hegseth's role as Trump's defense secretary and his immediate purging of policies deemed offensive by MAGA conservatives is particularly telling. It highlights a willingness to prioritize political ideology over the nuanced realities of leadership in a diverse and complex world. The underground efforts by midshipmen to preserve their affinity clubs and communicate their concerns are a defiant stand against this ideological encroachment, emphasizing the importance of diversity and inclusion in developing effective, empathetic leaders.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Resistance

The juxtaposition of Trump’s speech at West Point with the quiet resistance at the Naval Academy serves as a microcosm of the broader battle for the soul of America. It is a stark reminder that the fight for diversity, equity, and inclusion extends far beyond the hallowed halls of academia and into the very institutions tasked with defending the nation.

As the country continues to grapple with these issues, the courage of those midshipmen who choose to stand up against the suppression of diversity and inclusion should not be overlooked. Their actions, though small in the grand scheme, represent a beacon of hope and a call to action for those who believe in the value of diverse perspectives and inclusive policies. In the end, it is not just about resisting Trump's policies but about affirming the principles of equity and inclusion as essential to the fabric of American society and its future leaders.
