GOP lawmaker immediately fact-checked on CNN after 'celebrating' Trump's honesty

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theatre of Transparency

CNN’s Brianna Keilar and Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL), (CNN screenshot)

Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL) praised Donald Trump for his "transparency" after the White House disclosed the president has been diagnosed with chronic venous insufficiency, a condition that causes blood to pool in the veins.

"I think he's been the most transparent president we've ever had," Fine said Thursday on CNN. "You talked about the health conditions -- we had to have Joe Biden's doctor take the Fifth Amendment last week over their lack of transparency."

Host Brianna Keilar asked if Fine was concerned about the president's diagnosis.

"No, I think the president is, you know, a man in his 70s. He's going to have the kinds of conditions that people of that age do. But what I appreciate is the fact that, again, they are being transparent about them."

Fine continued, "I mean, Joe Biden tried to tell us for years there was nothing wrong with his brain when it was obvious there was something to the point that his doctor had to take the Fifth Amendment against self-incrimination of a crime last week. He's basically admitting they committed crimes and covering up Joe Biden's health conditions. So, I think we should all celebrate the fact that Donald Trump is willing to tell us what's going on."

Keilar interjected, "I do just want to note that this disclosure came after his ankles were visibly swollen, and we could see makeup on his hand covering up a bruise, which they say is because of an aspirin regimen and a lot of hand shaking," Keilar said.

Our Analysis:

The Theatre of Transparency

In the latest act of political theater, Rep. Randy Fine (R-FL) has taken to the stage to laud Donald Trump for his "transparency" regarding his health, specifically his diagnosis with chronic venous insufficiency. This condition, which causes blood to pool in the veins, is indeed common among older adults. However, the framing of this revelation as a triumph of transparency is nothing short of a farcical attempt to rewrite the narrative of an administration that has been anything but open.

Fine's comments, made on CNN, are a masterclass in the art of deflection and distortion. Let's dissect the layers of irony and misinformation here.

Firstly, the commendation of Trump's transparency is laughable when juxtaposed with his administration's track record. This is, after all, the same administration that fought tooth and nail to conceal everything from visitor logs to tax returns, setting a new low for presidential opacity.

Secondly, Fine's attempt to contrast Trump's supposed transparency with Joe Biden's alleged lack of the same is a textbook example of whataboutism. The claim that Biden's doctor "took the Fifth Amendment" is not only misleading but a gross misrepresentation of the facts, designed to divert attention from the issue at hand.

Moreover, Fine's assertion that this disclosure is somehow praiseworthy is undermined by the timing of the revelation. As pointed out by the host, Brianna Keilar, the condition was only disclosed after visible signs (swollen ankles and a bruised hand) made it impossible to conceal. This is not transparency; this is forced acknowledgement.

The irony of Fine celebrating Trump's transparency over a health condition, while simultaneously claiming Joe Biden's doctor admitted to crimes by invoking the Fifth Amendment, is a bold-faced attempt at rewriting reality. It's important to note that invoking the Fifth Amendment is a legal right designed to protect individuals from self-incrimination, not an admission of guilt or wrongdoing. The mischaracterization of such a fundamental legal principle is not just misleading; it's dangerous.

Fine's narrative is a clear indication of the lengths to which some will go to spin facts in favor of their preferred narrative. The framing of Trump's disclosure, necessitated by visible physical symptoms, as a virtue of transparency, is a manipulative ploy. It's an effort to distract from the many instances of obfuscation and outright deception that characterized his administration.

In the world of politics, where the battle for public perception is relentless, the truth often becomes the first casualty. Fine's comments are a stark reminder of how facts are twisted, and transparency is weaponized for political gain.

In conclusion, while chronic venous insufficiency is indeed a common condition that warrants sympathy, the framing of its disclosure by Trump's camp—and its champions like Rep. Randy Fine—as an act of unparalleled openness is a cynical manipulation of the truth. The real story here is not about the president's health, but about the ongoing campaign to redefine transparency to suit political ends. It's a campaign that insults the intelligence of the public and undermines the very foundation of democratic accountability.
