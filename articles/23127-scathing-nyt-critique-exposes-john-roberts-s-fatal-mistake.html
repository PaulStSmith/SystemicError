Scathing NYT critique exposes John Roberts's fatal mistake

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20263 min read

The Outlaw Works Because the Gatekeepers Keep Moving

Source in Brief

The source describes Donald Trump as treating law and institutions like an obstacle course for personal gain, with Maureen Dowd arguing that he has bulldozed the East Wing, torn up the White House gardens, and pushed ahead with projects and power grabs while the Supreme Court and Republicans in Congress keep yielding. That is the surface story. The real story is who keeps letting him do it.

The Office Is the Weapon

Trump is not operating from the margins. He is using the presidency, the courts, and a compliant congressional minority as force multipliers. The source understands this more clearly than it sometimes knows: the issue is not that he is unusually disruptive in the abstract, but that he occupies the one office built to turn private appetite into public consequence.

That is the institutional fact this piece keeps circling. A president can break things quickly because the system assumes a president will not behave like a grifter with a wrecking crew. Trump is exploiting that assumption exactly as described: not by hiding his intent, but by making the machinery normalize it after the fact.

Roberts Helped Clear the Path

The most revealing detail is not the ballroom, the arch, or the gardens. It is the delayed outrage. Chief Justice John Roberts, after helping shape a Court that expands executive latitude, now sounds alarmed that Trump has the latitude he wanted. That is not restraint. That is damage control after the damage has already been authorized.

The source is right to treat the “standing” obsession as a symptom of institutional cowardice. The Court is often more eager to police who may object than what is being done. That is a political choice, not a neutral legal posture. If “any American” should have standing, the problem is not obscure doctrine. The problem is that elite legalism keeps finding elegant ways to avoid confronting raw abuse of power.

Congress Is Not Being Defeated, It Is Cooperating

The article lists a parade of Republican capitulations: the war in Iran, disenfranchisement schemes, Postal Service interference, a Yosemite land grab, attacks on wolves and wild horses, vanity branding, and the rest. The pattern is not confusion. It is permission.

Congress is not some innocent institution stunned by events. Republican members are actively laundering Trump’s agenda by treating each abuse as a discrete controversy instead of a governing method. That framing is useful to them because it hides a simple truth: they are not failing to stop him. They are helping him clear the field.

Misdirection as Political Strategy

The source spends a lot of energy on Trump’s vulgarity, his appetites, his absurdity, his orange dust, his “small potatoes” war language, his vanity projects. Those details are not irrelevant, but they can become a trap. Trump’s grotesquerie draws attention away from the institutional collaboration that turns his impulses into durable outcomes.

That is how authoritarian-style politics often works in plain sight. The scandal becomes the personality. The personality becomes the explanation. The explanation then substitutes for accountability. Meanwhile, judges, lawmakers, and senior officials get to pose as reluctant observers while they keep the system open for business.

The Pattern Is Bigger Than One Man

The deeper pattern here is not simply that Trump exploits loopholes. It is that American institutions are structured to rely on decency from people who have no incentive to provide it, and then to reward the people who exploit that gap fastest. The source quotes that logic directly, and it is the most important line in the piece.

Trump is the beneficiary, but not the originator, of this system. The originators are the legal, legislative, and judicial actors who keep converting obvious abuse into procedural debate. That is the real scandal: not that he is a “biggest shark,” but that so many supposed guardians keep stocking the water.
