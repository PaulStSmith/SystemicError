Trump was lured into war with Iran by tantalizing tidbit in secret phone call: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Chilling Coordination Behind a Controversial Strike

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump reaches for his phone as he makes an announcement about a trade deal with the U.K., in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 8, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis/File Photo

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called President Donald Trump last week with a tantalizing tidbit that has since blown up into a major war operation.

Three sources tipped off Axios to the Feb. 23 call, which Trump took in the White House Situation Room and had not been previously reported, and said that Netanyahu told the president that Iran’s supreme leader and his top advisers were all set to meet Saturday morning at one location in Tehran – giving U.S. and Israeli forces an opportunity to kill them all in a single strike.

“It answers the question that lawmakers, MAGA skeptics and world leaders have all been asking since Saturday: why now?” Axios reported. “The answer: Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and his inner circle were irresistible targets of opportunity that neither Trump nor Netanyahu wanted to pass up.”

Trump was already leaning toward a military strike, but he decided on the timing after Netanyahu’s call, which came after months of close coordination – including 15 phone calls in two months – between the two leaders.

“The U.S. and Israel had considered striking a week earlier than Saturday, but postponed for intelligence and operational reasons, including bad weather,” Axios reported. “An initial CIA check, conducted at Trump’s direction, confirmed the information about Khamenei gathered by Israeli military intelligence.”

Trump told Netanyahu he’d consider a strike, but he and U.S. officials deliberately downplayed talk about Iran in his State of the Union address the following night, saying they did not want the Iranian leaders to change their plans, and the CIA confirmed by Thursday the Tehran gathering for Saturday morning.

“That same day, Trump’s envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff called from Geneva after hours of talks with Iranian officials and delivered a blunt verdict: negotiations were going nowhere,” Axios reported. “Trump was now convinced of two things: the intelligence was solid, and diplomacy was dead. On Friday at 3:38 p.m. EST, he gave the final order.”

Our Analysis:

The Chilling Coordination Behind a Controversial Strike

In a revelation that cuts through the fog of geopolitical speculation, we’ve learned from Axios about a phone call that might as well have been a prelude to a dramatic escalation in Middle Eastern tensions. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, in a move as bold as it is controversial, tipped off President Donald Trump to a “tantalizing tidbit” that could have changed the course of history, had it not morphed into the blueprint for a major military operation.

The Setup: A Call That Sparked a War Operation

On February 23, Netanyahu’s call to Trump wasn’t just another diplomatic check-in. It was a calculated move, infused with the promise of a decisive blow against Iran - by targeting none other than Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and his closest advisors. The allure of decimating Iran’s top leadership in one fell swoop was presented as an “irresistible target of opportunity.” This wasn’t just an operation; it was an assassination plot on an unprecedented scale, dressed in the guise of strategic precision.

Months of Coordination: A Prelude to Aggression

The backstory here is not just about a singular phone call; it’s about a prolonged conspiracy between the U.S. and Israel, marked by “15 phone calls in two months.” Such a level of engagement suggests that the decision to strike was not a spur-of-the-moment choice but the culmination of extensive planning and deliberation. The narrative that unfolds is one of two leaders, Trump and Netanyahu, inching closer to a decision that could have easily spiraled into a full-blown regional, if not global, conflict.

The Decision: Intelligence Over Diplomacy

The Axios report underscores a critical junction where Trump, already “leaning toward a military strike,” finds his resolve hardened not just by Netanyahu’s intel but by the failure of diplomacy. Reports from Trump’s envoys in Geneva painted a stark picture: negotiations with Iran were “going nowhere.” This blunt assessment, coupled with CIA confirmation of the Tehran gathering, tipped the scales. Trump’s decision to consider a strike after the State of the Union address, while intentionally downplaying tensions with Iran, speaks volumes about the clandestine nature of this operation.

The Final Order: A Leap into the Abyss

The final decision, made on a Friday at 3:38 p.m. EST, was not just a military order; it was a leap into the abyss. The ramifications of such a strike - had it gone through as planned - are difficult to overstate. Beyond the immediate loss of life, the strategic fallout could have included a severe escalation in hostilities between Iran and a U.S.-Israel coalition, with unpredictable consequences for global stability.

In Conclusion: A Stark Reminder of the Razor’s Edge

This episode serves as a stark reminder of the razor’s edge on which global peace often balances. The actions described in the Axios report highlight not just the audacity of Netanyahu’s proposal or Trump’s willingness to entertain it, but the profound risks inherent in geopolitical gambles. Diplomacy, it seems, was sidelined in favor of a more direct, and potentially catastrophic, approach.

As we dissect the implications of this operation, one thing becomes clear: the line between strategic precision and reckless endangerment is perilously thin. The audacity to target a nation’s leadership in one fell swoop is not a display of strength; it is a testament to the perilous lengths to which leaders are willing to go, often at the expense of stability, peace, and human lives. This episode is a somber reminder of the high stakes game that is international politics, where decisions made in secret can have profound implications for millions.
