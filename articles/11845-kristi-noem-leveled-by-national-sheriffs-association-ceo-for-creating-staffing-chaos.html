Kristi Noem leveled by National Sheriff’s Association CEO for creating staffing chaos

U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security Kristi Noem holds a press conference, in Los Angeles, California, U.S., June 12, 2025. REUTERS/Aude Guerrucci

Local law enforcement leaders are growing increasingly frustrated with DHS s Secretary Kristi Noem's recruitment efforts, which they say have aggressively targeted members of their own ranks amid nationwide police shortages.

“It's just one of those things where you would not try to steal your brother's girlfriend just to have a date, so we're a little bit frustrated to say the least,” said Jonathan Thompson, executive director and CEO of the National Sheriff’s Association, speaking with CNN Saturday.

The Trump administration has launched an aggressive recruitment campaign for ICE as it seeks to reach its self-imposed quota of arresting 3,000 migrants a day, and has gone as far as sending emails to local law enforcement agencies looking to poach their officers.

“Your experience in state or local law enforcement brings invaluable insight and skills to this mission – qualities we need now more than ever,” read one email targeting local law enforcement officers written by ICE Deputy Director Madison Sheahan, obtained by CNN.

With local law enforcement agencies suffering from staffing shortages nationwide, however, Thompson said the recruitment campaign was causing growing frustration among police officers and sheriff deputies.

“We're frustrated, I think it's hard to fathom how difficult the recruiting and retention problems facing law enforcement across this country are,” he said. “With many agencies looking at anywhere from 25% to 40% vacancy rates, this came to us as a bit of a surprise that you'd go to your partners to start recruiting for them.”

Trump has bolstered ICE with a record-high budget of $37.5 billion, making the agency’s budget higher than the military budgets of all but 15 countries, with the agency offering sign-on bonuses as high as $50,000.

And yet, the agency, led by Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, has struggled to meet hiring expectations, going as far as forcibly reassigning Federal Emergency Management Agency staff to ICE under threat of termination.

Our Analysis:

The Recruitment Rumble: DHS vs. Local Law Enforcement

In a brazen display of inter-agency rivalry gone awry, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), under Secretary Kristi Noem, has embarked on a controversial recruitment spree. This isn't just any hiring push; it's an aggressive poaching expedition aimed squarely at the ranks of local law enforcement agencies. The situation has sparked considerable ire, drawing criticism for exacerbating an already dire nationwide police shortage. Let's dissect the elements of this unfolding drama.

The Unbrotherly Heist

Jonathan Thompson, the executive director and CEO of the National Sheriff’s Association, delivers a metaphor that's as poignant as it is colorful. Comparing DHS's recruitment tactics to stealing your brother's girlfriend, Thompson encapsulates the betrayal felt by local law enforcement bodies. This isn't just recruitment; it's an act of poaching that threatens the very fabric of local public safety nets.

ICE's Quest for 3,000

At the heart of this controversy is the Trump administration's ambitious, perhaps overly ambitious, goal for ICE: arresting 3,000 migrants a day. To achieve this, DHS has not only launched an aggressive recruitment campaign but has also dangled carrots such as sign-on bonuses as high as $50,000. Yet, despite these incentives and a whopping $37.5 billion budget that puts ICE's funding above the military budgets of all but 15 countries, DHS has struggled to meet its hiring expectations. This desperation led to the controversial strategy of targeting local law enforcement officers.

The Frustration of Local Forces

Local law enforcement agencies are already in the grips of a recruitment and retention crisis, with vacancy rates hovering between 25% to 40%. These agencies are on the front lines, maintaining public safety and community order. Yet, here comes DHS, siphoning off much-needed talent with the promise of higher pay and federal benefits. The frustration expressed by Thompson and his colleagues is not just understandable; it's a glaring indictment of the federal government's lack of coordination and respect for its local counterparts.

The Unsettling Reassignment of FEMA Staff

Perhaps the most disturbing aspect of this saga is the forcible reassignment of Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) staff to ICE, under threat of termination. This move not only undermines the critical work FEMA does but also highlights a troubling willingness to compromise disaster preparedness for immigration enforcement goals. It's a stark reminder of the administration's prioritization and the lengths it's willing to go to achieve its objectives, even at the expense of its own people and the American public's safety.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Judgment

The DHS's recruitment strategy, as led by Secretary Kristi Noem, is not just a case of inter-agency rivalry; it's a systemic error in judgment that undermines the foundational principles of public safety and inter-agency cooperation. At a time when unity and coordination are paramount, the DHS's actions are a divisive force, pitting federal ambitions against local necessities. The repercussions of this approach—both for public safety and for the morale of law enforcement officers across the nation—cannot be overstated. It's a strategy that might fill the ranks of ICE but at what cost? The answer, it seems, is too high a price for our communities to bear.
