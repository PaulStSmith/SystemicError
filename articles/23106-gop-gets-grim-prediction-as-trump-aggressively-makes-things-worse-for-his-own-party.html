GOP gets grim prediction as Trump 'aggressively makes things worse' for his own party

Based on an article by Matthew Chapman

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The ReportingWhere The Power IsWho Enabled The OutcomeThe MisdirectionThe PatternThe Larger Lesson

Trump Is the Damage

The Reporting

Rick Wilson, now an anti-Trump commentator, told MS NOW that Republicans are heading into the fall with a problem they did not have in 2006: not just bad conditions, but a president “aggressively trying to make things worse.” He pointed to growing regret among Trump voters and argued that this erosion could cost Republicans their majorities.

Where The Power Is

The article pretends the story is about voter mood. It is actually about power sitting in one place: Trump. Gas prices, war, and Katrina were external shocks in 2006. This time, the central political actor is not merely inheriting chaos. He is actively producing it. That distinction matters because it identifies responsibility instead of laundering it into atmosphere.

Who Enabled The Outcome

The Republican Party did not get trapped by some abstract “environment.” It built and sustained a politics in which Trump remains the reference point for the whole coalition. If Republicans are now sweating a 15 to 20 percent slice of Trump voters turning sour, that is not a sign of randomness. It is the predictable cost of tethering a party to one man’s sabotage and calling it strategy.

The Misdirection

The useful lie in coverage like this is that Republicans are suffering from bad luck, bad optics, or voter fatigue. Wilson partially cuts through that by naming Trump’s daily sabotage. But the frame still risks hiding the deeper truth: this is not an accidental backlash. It is consequence. The party empowered a figure whose political method is permanent self-destruction as long as it harms everyone else first.

The Pattern

This is what authoritarian-adjacent politics looks like inside an electoral party: damage is normalized, then rebranded as turbulence, then blamed on the people forced to live through it. Republicans are not being undone by a stray headline cycle. They are being consumed by the leader they kept choosing because cruelty, chaos, and grievance were useful until they became expensive.

The Larger Lesson

The story is not that Trump is becoming a liability in the abstract. The story is that Republican institutional dependence on him has made the party structurally incapable of separating itself from the harm he causes. That is the real political pattern here: a party that mistakes submission for strength, and then acts surprised when the damage comes due.
