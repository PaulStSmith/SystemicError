'No idea what her job was': Karoline Leavitt hammered for remarks on fired 'American hero'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 11, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Firing of Carla Hayden: A Case Study in Misdirection and MisunderstandingConclusion: A Disgraceful Misstep

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt speaks to members of the media in the briefing room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 9, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

Responding to a reporter's question about President DonaldTrump firing Librarian of Congress Carla Hayden, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt accused her of "putting inappropriate books in the library for children."

"She was doing concerning things in the pursuit of Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI)," Leavitt said during a White House briefing on Friday, defending Hayden's termination.

The Librarian of Congress is the chief officer of the Library of Congress, selected by the president with the approval of the Senate, and serves a ten-year term. Hayden's term was set to expire next year.

Political commentators reacted to the press secretary's remarks and noted that the Library of Congress is not frequented by minors. According to the library's website, anyone aged 16 or older can become a member.

Author Jack Pitney wrote on X: "Inappropriate books for children? Ms. Leavitt does not know that you have to be at least 16 to use the Library of Congress. And you can't wander the shelves or check books out."

Journalist Lydia Polgreenmockingly said: "At least she admits some members of Congress are children."

Writer Joe Floodwrote: "Kids can’t check books out of the LOC. No one can! It’s a research library. There’s not much for kids unless they want to read the Federal Register."

Podcaster Hemant Mehtasaid: "They have no idea what her job was, do they."

User @GreekMediaHub wrote: "This is how fascism works. Slow to start, and then the walls start tumbling, one by one. Education. Books. Speech. Press. That sound is your rights crumbling away."

Hayden was the first woman and the first African American to be librarian of Congress.

Rep. Joe Morelle (D-NY), the top Democrat on the House panel that oversees the Library of Congress, called Hayden "an American hero" who "has spent her entire career serving people—from helping kids learn to read to protecting some of our nation's most precious treasures."

On Thursday, the White House informed Hayden that she had been dismissed from her role, CNN reported, citing a spokesperson for the Library of Congress.

The exact cause of Hayden’s dismissal was not immediately known. Congressional Democrats quickly condemned the president's decision, saying he was aiming to censor books.

House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) said Hayden's removal was “a disgrace and the latest in his ongoing effort to ban books, whitewash American history and turn back the clock.”

"There will be accountability for this unprecedented assault on the American way of life sooner rather than later," hesaid in a statement posted to the social platform XThursday.

Our Analysis:

The Firing of Carla Hayden: A Case Study in Misdirection and Misunderstanding

In a move that reeks of political maneuvering and a profound misunderstanding of the role of the Librarian of Congress, President Donald Trump's administration has fired Carla Hayden, the first woman and the first African American to hold the position. According to White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, Hayden was accused of "putting inappropriate books in the library for children" and engaging in "concerning" activities in the name of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI). This reasoning is not just flawed; it's a glaring testament to the administration's failure to grasp the basic functions and rules governing the Library of Congress.

The Absurdity of the "Inappropriate Books" Argument

Firstly, the assertion that Hayden was dismissed for making the Library of Congress a haven for "inappropriate books for children" is laughably misguided. As pointed out by political commentators and experts in the wake of this controversy, the Library of Congress is not a children’s library. In fact, it's not a typical library in any sense that Leavitt implies. One must be at least 16 years old to become a member, and even then, the library does not function as a lending library where one can simply "check out" books.

Author Jack Pitney and journalist Lydia Polgreen highlight the absurdity of Leavitt's remarks, with Polgreen's quip about Congress members being children serving as a poignant (and humorous) critique of the misunderstanding at play. Joe Flood and podcaster Hemant Mehta further dismantle the administration's rationale by pointing out the research-focused nature of the Library of Congress and the impossibility of checking out books, showcasing a glaring lack of knowledge about Hayden's job and the library's operations.

The Real Issue at Hand: A Misguided Attack on DEI

Leavitt's comments on Hayden's efforts towards Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion reveal the true colors of this dismissal. It's a thinly veiled attack on DEI under the guise of protecting children from non-existent threats. This move aligns with a broader, more alarming trend of censoring educational resources and rewriting history to suit a particular narrative. It's an attempt to stifle diversity and inclusion, repackaged as concern for the youth.

The Response: A Nation Divided

The response from various quarters has been swift and divided. Rep. Joe Morelle (D-NY) hailed Hayden as "an American hero," emphasizing her lifelong dedication to serving people and preserving the nation's treasures. In contrast, Congressional Democrats view this firing as part of Trump's larger agenda to ban books and whitewash American history, with House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries decrying it as “a disgrace” and an “unprecedented assault on the American way of life.”

The Larger Implication: Echoes of Fascism

Perhaps the most chilling reaction comes from @GreekMediaHub, which succinctly ties the dismissal of Hayden to the gradual erosion of democratic freedoms, equating it to the onset of fascism. This comparison may sound extreme to some, but it underscores the gravity of the situation and the potential slippery slope we find ourselves on when education, books, speech, and the press are under attack.

Conclusion: A Disgraceful Misstep

The firing of Carla Hayden is not just about the removal of the first woman and the first African American from a prestigious position. It's a symptom of a larger, more concerning trend of ignorance, censorship, and a deliberate assault on diversity and inclusion. The administration's justification for this dismissal is as baseless as it is revealing, showcasing a dangerous mix of misunderstanding and malintent. As we navigate these troubled waters, it’s crucial to remain vigilant against such attempts to rewrite our narratives and redefine our rights.
