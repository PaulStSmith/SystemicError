What Trump’s escalating behavior actually reveals about him behind closed doors

Defenders of President Donald Trump often accuse his opponents of having “Trump derangement syndrome,” but Salon’s Chauncey DeVega has a much different viewpoint. DeVega believes that some of Trump’s critics aren’t critical enough and make the mistake of underestimating what he is capable of — especially when he is feeling threatened.

In an article published by Salon on February 5, DeVega stresses that Trump’s weak approval ratings in countless polls and large demonstrations against his policies don’t make him less “dangerous” — they make him even more dangerous.

“As 2025 came to a close,” DeVega explains, “mainstream liberal and centrist commentators were boldly declaring, often with visible relief, that Donald Trump’s authoritarian power grab had exhausted itself. The tide, they insisted, had finally turned in favor of American democracy and its resilient institutions. That relief was premature. One month into 2026, Trump is striking back with the strength of a tsunami. The authoritarian tide is rising again — faster than before.”

The Salon journalist continues, “It began in earnest on January 3 when the U.S. military attacked Venezuela and abducted President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores…. Trump’s lawlessness abroad reflects a profound contempt for the rule of law and democratic institutions at home. Despite Wednesday’s announcement that around 700 federal immigration officers would be withdrawn from Minneapolis and St. Paul, the Twin Cities remain effectively under occupation by thousands of agents from Immigration and Customs Enforcement and Border Patrol in an effort to enforce the Trump Administration’s mass deportation campaign.”

DeVega notes a long list of troubling developments in 2026.

“DHS (the U.S. Department of Homeland Security) is building dozens of detention centers around the country, with a combined capacity of approximately 80,000 people,” the reporter warns. “On January 28, the FBI raided Fulton County’s election office ostensibly to ‘prove’ the Big Lie conspiracy that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen from Trump. Many legal scholars suspect the action is part of the president’s plan to nationalize elections ahead of the 2026 midterms and the 2028 presidential election.”

According to DeVega, the fact that “resistance” to Trump is “growing” makes him increasingly desperate.

“Like other autocrats and aspiring dictators,” DeVega observes, “Trump’s escalating behavior reflects not absolute strength and power, but a deep-seated fear that he may instead lose it. This makes the president and the MAGA movement all more dangerous.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Trump Conundrum: A Critical Dissection

In the realm of political commentary, Chauncey DeVega’s latest piece for Salon serves as a chilling reminder of the perils of complacency in the face of authoritarianism. DeVega posits a theory that is as compelling as it is terrifying: the idea that Donald Trump’s critics—and by extension, American society—may not be alarmed enough about the potential depths of his authoritarian ambitions, especially as he appears cornered by low approval ratings and public dissent.

The Danger of Underestimation

DeVega’s argument hinges on a critical mistake made by mainstream liberal and centrist commentators: the assumption that the observable decline in Trump’s popularity and the vocal opposition against his policies somehow translate to a diminished threat. This is a fallacy of logic and a misreading of historical precedents concerning authoritarian figures. The assumption that Trump’s weakened state makes him less dangerous ignores the basic tenet that a cornered animal is often at its most dangerous. DeVega rightly points out that rather than receding, Trump’s authoritarian tendencies have intensified, a fact underscored by the audacious military action in Venezuela and the domestic overreach in Minneapolis and St. Paul.

The Authoritarian Playbook

DeVega’s analysis extends to specific actions undertaken by the Trump administration that scream authoritarianism. The abduction of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, the occupation of American cities by federal immigration officers, the construction of detention centers, and the FBI raid on Fulton County’s election office are not isolated incidents but components of a coherent strategy aimed at consolidating power. DeVega warns of Trump’s plan to nationalize elections—a move that could have disastrous implications for American democracy.

The Resistance Paradox

One of the most insightful aspects of DeVega’s piece is the exploration of the dynamic between Trump’s authoritarianism and the resistance it has engendered. He suggests that as resistance grows, so does Trump’s desperation, leading to an escalation in authoritarian behavior. This is a critical observation because it highlights a paradox at the heart of political resistance: the stronger the opposition, the more dangerous the authoritarian figure becomes. It’s a precarious balance between pushing back against authoritarianism and not fueling its fire.

The Critical Takeaway

DeVega’s article is a stark warning against complacency and a call to action for the American public and its political leaders. The danger posed by Trump’s authoritarian tendencies is not just present but is escalating. The assumption that traditional American institutions and values will automatically repel authoritarianism is not just naive; it’s dangerous.

In conclusion, DeVega’s analysis is a potent reminder of the fragility of democracy and the continuous effort required to sustain it. It challenges readers to reassess their perceptions of Trump’s threat level and to recognize the nuanced dynamics at play. Rather than a simple narrative of resistance leading to victory, DeVega presents a more complex reality where the very act of resistance must be carefully calibrated to avoid exacerbating the threat. In essence, DeVega doesn’t just critique the underestimation of Trump’s potential for damage; he lays bare the intricate dance between authoritarianism and the fight to preserve democracy.
