MAGA insiders suddenly embrace 'indispensable' energy they long derided as a 'parasite'

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump spent much of his first year in office in an all-out war against solar power, even going so far as to change regulations so that renewable energy faces vastly more permitting. But now, many prominent MAGA voices are beginning to enthusiastically promote solar development.

The reason for the shift is simple, The Washington Post reported on Monday: artificial intelligence.

One of the most notable figures in Trump’s orbit to be touting the benefits of solar power is Katie Miller, the wife of Trump’s infamous anti-immigrant strategist Stephen Miller.

“’Solar energy is the energy of the future,’ Katie Miller posted recently. ‘Giant fusion reactor up there in the sky — we must rapidly expand solar to compete with China.’ Another of her posts suggested solar is more vital to the U.S. than coal power, contradicting White House messaging and policy.”

Nor is Miller alone, noted the report, as “a growing number of prominent Trump allies — including former House speaker Newt Gingrich, veteran strategist Kellyanne Conway and GOP pollster Tony Fabrizio — are promoting solar as electricity demand surges and energy affordability climbs the list of voter concerns.”

Perhaps nowhere is this starker than Energy Secretary Chris Wright, who previously called solar power a “parasite” on the power grid, but last week, while speaking with reporters, said, “Is there a commercial role for solar power that can add to the grid affordable, reliable energy? Certainly there is.”

It isn’t a newfound attention to climate change driving the shift, the report said — rather, the big change that has happened over the last year is “a realization taking hold more broadly among Republicans that solar power — long embraced by liberals — is increasingly indispensable to America’s bid to dominate AI, close a yawning ‘electron gap’ with China and contain runaway residential electricity costs. These conservatives describe it as crucial to U.S. competitiveness, the grid’s reliability and their own movement’s political survival.”

It also comes as communities all over America that voted for Trump begin to rise up against the construction of AI data centers near them, driven by a fear that these facilities will consume all the local energy and drive up people’s power bills. Trump himself responded to these concerns in his State of the Union Address, promising he would push technology companies to generate all their power onsite — a plan experts have said is just not that simple.

Our Analysis:

The Chameleonic Nature of Political Expediency: Trump Allies Embrace Solar Energy

In a stunning pivot that would leave even the most seasoned political observers with whiplash, former President Donald Trump’s inner circle has taken a sharp turn from their entrenched war against solar power to becoming its enthusiastic proponents. This dramatic shift underscores a fundamental truth about political discourse in America: principles are often as malleable as Play-Doh, especially when faced with the immutable forces of technological advancement and economic pressure.

The Solar Reversal

At the heart of this volte-face is, ostensibly, the burgeoning significance of artificial intelligence (AI) and the increasing realization that renewable energy sources like solar power are not merely environmental boons but strategic necessities in the global race for technological supremacy. Katie Miller, juxtaposed against her husband Stephen Miller’s notorious anti-immigrant rhetoric, boldly champions solar energy as the future, emphasizing the need to outpace China. This sentiment is echoed by other high-profile Trump affiliates, including Newt Gingrich, Kellyanne Conway, and Tony Fabrizio, who are now vocal advocates for solar power.

Contradictions Abound

Yet, this newfound enthusiasm for solar energy is not born out of a late-blooming concern for the climate crisis. Instead, it’s framed as a strategic maneuver to bolster America’s AI capabilities, bridge the “electron gap” with China, and mitigate the soaring costs of residential electricity. Energy Secretary Chris Wright, who previously derided solar power as a “parasite” on the power grid, now acknowledges its potential to contribute affordably and reliably to the grid. The irony is rich and the hypocrisy, thicker than molasses.

Political Survival Over Principle

The report suggests that the Republican rebranding of solar energy is less an epiphany on the merits of renewable resources and more a calculated response to the imperatives of U.S. competitiveness and political survival. This pivot also addresses local opposition to AI data centers, feared to be voracious energy consumers that could inflate electricity costs for nearby communities. Trump’s response, advocating for technology companies to generate their power onsite, is dismissed by experts as oversimplified and impractical.

Conclusion: The Expediency of the Hour

This saga of political transformation among Trump’s allies from solar skeptics to proponents is a textbook example of how political narratives evolve not necessarily on the altar of truth but at the altar of expediency. The embrace of solar energy by these figures is not a tale of environmental enlightenment but a story about the relentless pursuit of power, influence, and strategic advantage. It casts a glaring light on the elasticity of political positions, which can stretch dramatically in the face of emerging technological and economic landscapes.

In this narrative, solar power has been transmuted from a liberal fancy into a conservative strategy, not through a change of heart but through a change in circumstances. The lesson here is clear: in politics, today’s adversary can be tomorrow’s ally, provided the winds shift in a favorable direction. As the global race for AI dominance intensifies and the demand for sustainable energy solutions grows, even the most ardent critics of renewable energy find themselves realigning their stances, not out of conviction, but out of convenience.
