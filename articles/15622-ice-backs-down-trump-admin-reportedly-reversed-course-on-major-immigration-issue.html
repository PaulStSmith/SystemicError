'ICE backs down': Trump admin reportedly 'reversed course' on major immigration issue

The Trump administration suddenly backed down on its effort to revoke the visas of thousands of foreign students, according to reports.

The administration announced Friday in court that it would return the students to active status after terminating their records in a federal database earlier this month, reported WUSA-TV.

As Politico's Kyle Cheney wrote, "After canceling thousands of foreign students' immigration records — threatening their ability to study and live in the US — the Trump administration has reversed course and restored them all."

"It follows intense pushback from courts across the country," the reporter added.

“ICE is developing a policy that will provide a framework for SEVIS record terminations," said assistant U.S. Attorney Joseph Carilli. "Until such a policy is issued, the SEVIS records for plaintiff(s) in this case (and other similarly situated plaintiffs) will remain Active or shall be re-activated if not currently active and ICE will not modify the record solely based on the NCIC finding that resulted in the recent SEVIS record termination."

Federal judges have issued more than 40 emergency orders blocking Immigration and Customs Enforcement from unilaterally terminating records in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS), which the Department of Homeland Security uses to monitor foreign students while they're in the U.S.

They must maintain active status in the database to complete their educational programs, obtain authorization to work and apply for the H-1B visa lottery.

Hundreds of students have sued over the ICE action, saying they had never been charged with any crimes.

Carilli said the Department of Justice would file a similar statement in other cases around the country, saying ICE had the authority to terminate SEVIS records for students who did not maintain their nonimmigrant status or engage in other unlawful activity.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's Abrupt Reversal on Foreign Student Visas: A Closer Look

The Trump administration's recent flip-flop on the status of thousands of foreign students' visas is a stark reminder of the chaotic and often inhumane policy approaches that characterized much of its tenure. In what reads like a script for a bureaucratic thriller, the administration, after having unceremoniously terminated the records of countless foreign students in a federal database, was forced to backtrack amidst a formidable legal onslaught. This decision, as reported by WUSA-TV and Politico's Kyle Cheney, underscores not just the administration's capricious policy-making but also the power of judicial pushback in safeguarding the rights of individuals against governmental overreach.

The Initial Assault on Foreign Students

The move to revoke the visas of thousands of foreign students was nothing short of a policy debacle. By threatening the ability of these students to study and live in the U.S., the administration not only jeopardized the futures of these individuals but also the standing of the U.S. as a beacon of higher education. The decision to terminate their records in the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System (SEVIS) was met with the kind of intense pushback it deserved, from courts across the country no less.

Judicial Pushback and Policy Reversal

It's telling that it took "more than 40 emergency orders" from federal judges to prevent Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) from unilaterally ending these students' SEVIS records. This powerful judicial response highlights a critical check on the arbitrary use of executive power, particularly when it impacts the lives and futures of young people coming to the U.S. with aspirations of studying, working, and contributing to society.

Assistant U.S. Attorney Joseph Carilli's statement that ICE is "developing a policy that will provide a framework for SEVIS record terminations" is a belated recognition of the need for clear, fair, and transparent policies governing the status of foreign students. The chaos unleashed by the initial decision could have been avoided with a more thoughtful and systematic approach to policy formulation and implementation.

The Broader Implications

This incident is emblematic of a broader pattern of policy-making under the Trump administration, marked by a disregard for the well-being of immigrants and a penchant for decisions that seem more rooted in xenophobia than in any coherent policy rationale. Hundreds of students felt compelled to sue over the ICE action, a clear indication of the distress and uncertainty caused by these abrupt policy shifts. Moreover, these students had never been charged with any crimes, highlighting the indiscriminate nature of the policy.

The fact that foreign students must maintain active status in SEVIS to complete their educational programs, work, and apply for the H-1B visa lottery underscores the importance of this database not just for the students themselves but for academic institutions and employers across the country. The administration's attempt to tamper with this system without a clear policy rationale was not just misguided but potentially damaging to the U.S.'s reputation and economy.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's sudden reversal on revoking the visas of thousands of foreign students is a welcome outcome to an otherwise alarming display of policy ineptitude. It serves as a reminder of the importance of judicial oversight, the need for clear and fair immigration policies, and the value of foreign students to the fabric of American society. This incident should prompt a broader reflection on how immigration policies are formulated and the need to prioritize humanity and fairness over fear and exclusion.
