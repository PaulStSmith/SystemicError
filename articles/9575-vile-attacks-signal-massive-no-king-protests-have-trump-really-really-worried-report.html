'Vile' attacks signal massive No King protests have 'Trump really, really worried': report

U.S. President Donald Trump arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 30, 2025. (REUTERS/Ken Cedeno)

The impending No Kings protests scheduled for this weekend have President Donald Trump “really, really worried,” argued journalist Greg Sargent on Tuesday, adding that Trump’s panic was made evident by what he described as a “coordinated” effort from Republicans to dismiss the demonstrators as “terrorists.”

The No Kings protests are scheduled for this Saturday, Oct. 18, and are a follow up to the previous No Kings protests that took place last summer, attended by an estimated four-to-six million people.

“It has become very clear that President Donald Trump is really, really worried about the No Kings protests that are scheduled for October 18,” Sargent said on his podcast “The Daily Blast,” published on The New Republic Tuesday.

“Suddenly, multiple Republicans are all using the same disgusting talking point about the protests, referring to [protesters] as agitators and terrorists. It sure looks coordinated. They’re all using the same vile language.”

A growing number of Republican lawmakers have condemned the impending protests, and have used similar language. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) called the demonstration a “hate America rally,” as did Rep. Tom Emmer (R-MN); Sen. Roger Marshall (R-KS) called it a “Soros paid-for protest,” referencing the billionaire liberal philanthropist George Soros, a frequent “bogeyman” on the right.

The pushback, Sargent argued, would only backfire by prompting protests to “get larger.”

“There’s nothing Trump hates more than seeing big crowds arrayed against him,” Sargent said.

Sargent was joined on the podcast by columnist Jill Lawrence, who said that while she wasn’t surprised by the GOP backlash to the impending protests, the coordinated attacks revealed the party’s growing fear of dissent among Americans.

“This is traditional, this is constitutional, this is a right, and they’re trying to turn it into something really evil. But that’s because they’re scared of it, and they know that it turns public opinion,” Lawrence said.

“The problem here is that people like Johnson and the Republicans in Congress aren’t doing their jobs. They’re not putting any checks and balances on the president, and they’re not upholding American values or even American constitutional rights. So this is where we are.”

Our Analysis:

The Fearful Reaction to the No Kings Protests: A Display of Desperation

As the No Kings protests loom on the horizon, a palpable wave of panic has seemingly overtaken President Donald Trump and his Republican cohorts. This isn’t just a case of political disagreement; it’s a full-blown exhibition of fear. Journalist Greg Sargent’s recent insights on “The Daily Blast” podcast paint a stark picture of a presidency rattled to its core by the prospect of widespread dissent.

The Coordinated Campaign of Disinformation

Let’s dissect the “coordinated” effort to malign the No Kings protesters as “terrorists,” as Sargent aptly noted. This isn’t a simple slip of the tongue or an isolated case of political mudslinging. This is a calculated campaign aimed at delegitimizing a constitutional right to protest. When figures like House Speaker Mike Johnson label these demonstrations as a “hate America rally,” or when Senator Roger Marshall derides them as a “Soros paid-for protest,” they’re not just attacking the protesters. They’re attacking the very fabric of American democratic expression.

The Desperation Behind the Disparagement

What’s truly disgusting is the transparent desperation behind these attacks. The use of George Soros as a bogeyman is a tired, old tactic that reeks of anti-Semitism and conspiracy theories. It’s a clear attempt to distract from the issues at hand and to demonize the opposition without engaging in substantive debate. The fact that multiple Republicans are parroting this language is no coincidence; it’s a desperate bid to undermine the protests before they can even happen.

The Fear of Public Dissent

Why the fear? As Sargent and columnist Jill Lawrence suggest, it’s the sight of “big crowds arrayed against him” that Trump dreads the most. But it goes deeper than that. This fear isn’t just about optics or ego; it’s about the power of public opinion. The GOP’s coordinated attacks reveal a fundamental insecurity about their standing with the American public. They’re aware that these protests could further sway public opinion against them, exposing not just their policy failures but their moral bankruptcy.

The Abdication of Responsibility

At the heart of this issue is a glaring abdication of responsibility by the GOP. Instead of engaging with the protesters’ grievances, they resort to name-calling and fear-mongering. Instead of upholding American values and constitutional rights, they attempt to vilify those who do. As Lawrence puts it, they’re not doing their jobs. They’re not providing the checks and balances that are the cornerstone of American democracy. They’re not protecting the rights of citizens to peacefully assemble and express dissent.

Conclusion: A Call to Stand Firm

The GOP’s reaction to the No Kings protests is not just reprehensible; it’s revealing. It shows a party so afraid of losing power that they’ll stoop to any level to discredit and undermine their opposition. But if history has taught us anything, it’s that attempts to suppress dissent only make the voices of dissent louder. The No Kings protests, and the millions of Americans they represent, will not be silenced by baseless accusations or political fear-mongering. This isn’t just a protest; it’s a testament to the enduring strength of American democracy. And to President Trump and his allies, it’s a stark reminder that no amount of coordinated attacks can quell the spirit of dissent that lies at the heart of our nation.
