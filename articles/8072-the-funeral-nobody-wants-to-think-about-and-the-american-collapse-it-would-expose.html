The Funeral Nobody Wants to Think About — and the American Collapse It Would Expose

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20255 min read

There are moments when political commentary stops being about prediction and instead becomes autopsy. Not because the subject is dead, but because the republic that enabled him is. A recent video by the Godless Gardener goes straight for the jugular: he looks into the camera and says, effectively, that the United States may be on the verge of witnessing something not seen since Dallas, 1963 — a sitting president dying in office.

No euphemisms. No soft gloves. Just the blunt assessment that the man looks, in his words, “two weeks dead,” and the nation is watching him decay in real time.

The point isn’t to ghoulishly anticipate death. The point is to confront the political detonation such a moment would trigger.

A Funeral as National Rorschach Test

The United States has never been more fractured. Layer the death of the most polarizing figure in modern American history onto that, and what should be a straightforward civic ritual becomes a national Rorschach test.

A presidential funeral is ordinarily scripted to the millimeter — flags, caissons, the Washington Cathedral, the quiet drumline of ceremonial grief. But this would be nothing like that. This would be a political and moral earthquake.

Who shows up?

Who stays away?

Who pretends it’s solemnity, and who turns it into spectacle?

Even the basic optics become radioactive. Attend, and the right praises your “class.” Don’t attend, and Fox News will treat it as treason. The video lays this out with cutting clarity: attendance becomes ideology. No one gets to be neutral.

When a Cult Loses Its Idol

Most democracies don’t have a political movement built around a single brittle personality. The United States does. And the death of the idol doesn’t dissolve the cult — it accelerates it.

In the video, the Godless Gardener imagines the funeral hijacked not just by mourners but by conspiracy pilgrims. He isn’t wrong. Trump has followers who believe he’s immortal, resurrected, undercover, or divinely shielded from earthly harm. If he dies, the conspiracy ecosystem wouldn’t shut down — it would metastasize.

Some would allege assassination. Others would insist he’s still alive and merely “went dark.” Still others would claim the funeral is fake, staged by the deep state to demoralize the faithful.

A country struggling to combat basic misinformation would suddenly be drowning in a funeral-themed apocalypse of it.

The Political Class Would Show Its Cowardice Instantly

There’s also the matter of America’s elected officials, many of whom have spent the past decade trying to have it both ways — enabling Trump while claiming they aren’t responsible for the consequences.

A funeral would strip away that camouflage.

To attend is to tacitly endorse the man; to skip is to invite a primary challenge, a right-wing media feeding frenzy, or even targeted harassment. There is no safe harbor. The video calls it out plainly: the funeral becomes a scandal before it even happens.

And the cowards in Congress, who have spent years pretending that “thoughts and prayers” can sanitize cruelty, would be forced to pick a side with no hiding place.

The Gold-Plated Grief Machine

It’s impossible to ignore the grotesque theatricality that would accompany the event. Trump commodified everything — the office, the flag, the rhetoric of patriotism, even the basic architecture of truth. Why would death be different?

The Godless Gardener imagines the funeral draped in hideous gold trim. He’s being sarcastic, but he’s also right. There is no universe in which Trump’s final rites escape the gravitational pull of kitsch. His brand is gilded excess, and the opportunists circling him would ensure that his departure reflects the same aesthetic vulgarity as his tenure.

A funeral that should symbolize national unity would instead become a Swarovski-encrusted tribute to self-worship.

The Left’s Complicated Humanity

One of the most striking points in the video comes when the creator confesses he doesn’t want to feel relief at someone’s death. It’s one of those rare moments where political exhaustion intersects with moral honesty.

No democratic citizen wants to celebrate a death. But millions would feel it — not out of sadism, but out of cumulative trauma. Trump’s rise is a decade-long case study in what happens when a democracy’s immune system fails: institutions buckle, courts stall, accountability evaporates, and a single man turns the whole country into his personal casino.

If the legal system can’t hold him to account, then mortality becomes the last remaining check — the only thing he cannot sue, bribe, threaten, or lie his way through.

It’s bleak, but it’s true.

What Happens After the Coffin?

This is the angle nobody wants to discuss: if Trump dies in office or even shortly after leaving it, the political crisis doesn’t end. It ignites.

A nation this polarized cannot absorb such a shock without fracturing further. The questions become existential:

  • Who inherits the movement — the grifters, the ideologues, or the opportunists?

  • Does MAGA become a martyrdom cult?

  • Do conspiracy theorists claim he was killed by the deep state?

  • Do extremists lash out believing they must “avenge” him?

  • Does the GOP finally break free, or does it double down, canonizing him as a fallen prophet?

The video’s creator is blunt: this wouldn’t be closure. It wouldn’t be healing. It wouldn’t be a reset. It would be the beginning of another, possibly more chaotic, chapter.

The Real Indictment Is of America, Not the Man

Trump didn’t get away with everything because he was brilliant. He got away with it because the system — a system supposedly built on checks and balances — allowed him to.

He bulldozed norms because norms are only as strong as the people who enforce them. He dodged consequences because wealth, whiteness, and celebrity have always operated as a kind of American Kevlar. He turned public office into a personal escape room, and the country kept handing him more keys.

When institutions fail this completely, the final arbiter isn’t justice — it’s biology.

That’s the rot the video exposes. And that’s the part too many pundits are afraid to say out loud.

Conclusion: A Nation Unprepared for Its Own Reflection

If Trump dies in office, or even shortly after exiting it, the country won’t just confront the loss of a political figure. It will confront its own moral collapse.

A funeral shouldn’t be a referendum on the soul of a nation. But in this case, it will be. The United States has tied its identity to a man whose existence has tested every boundary of ethics, truth, governance, and sanity.

His death wouldn’t close a wound. It would expose how deep it already goes.

The video isn’t ghoulish. It’s honest. It forces America to imagine the chapter that comes after impunity — and to face the reality that the nation is woefully unprepared for it.

And maybe that’s the point: you can’t fix a country until you admit what broke it.
