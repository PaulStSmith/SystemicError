'We take our direction from the judge': Florida sheriff vows to ignore DeSantis directive

On February 13, Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis signed into law a bill making it a first-degree misdemeanor to illegally enter the Sunshine State as an “unauthorized alien” and imposing additional penalties for reentry. But on April 4 in Miami, U.S. District Court Judge Kathleen Williams issued an order temporarily blocking the law.

Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier, on Wednesday, April 23, sent Florida law enforcement a letter saying that he won't stop them from enforcing the law — which has already led to arrests. However, Pinellas County, Florida Sheriff Bob Gualtieri, according to Florida Phoenix reporter Jackie Llanos, is saying he plans to abide by Williams' order and is telling his deputies not to arrest anyone under the law.

Gualtieri told the Phoenix, "I don't think it changes anything. We take our direction on something like that from the judge, not from anybody else."

In his April 23 letter, Uthmeier — a DeSantis ally — wrote, "I cannot prevent you from enforcing §§811.102 and 811.103, where there remains no judicial order that properly restrains you from doing so."

Regardless, Gualtieri, according to Llanos, said he is abiding by Williams' order.

DeSantis, meanwhile, is openly critical of Williams, who he described as an "activist judge" during an April 7 press conference. And in an April 23 post on X, formerly Twitter, the far-right Florida governor wrote, "The mission continues. Immigration law must be enforced and FL is leading on working with the Trump administration to get it done."

The Florida ACLU, the Community Justice Project and Americans for Immigrant Justice are among the groups and organizations fighting the law in court.

Community Justice Project Director Alana Greer, criticized Uthmeier's April 23 letter, saying, in a written statement, "A federal judge entered not one, but two clear and direct orders stopping enforcement of this law. The Attorney General's letter makes us gravely concerned that Floridians will continue to be arrested under this unconstitutional statute. We will be back in court to ensure their rights are protected."

Our Analysis:

Florida's Controversial Immigration Law: A Tug-of-War Between State and Federal Authorities

In a move that has sparked considerable controversy and legal pushback, Florida Governor Ron DeSantis enacted a law on February 13, ostensibly aimed at curbing illegal immigration by making unauthorized entry into the state a first-degree misdemeanor. This law also introduces stiffer penalties for those caught re-entering the state illegally. However, this piece of legislation quickly hit a judicial roadblock when U.S. District Court Judge Kathleen Williams issued an order on April 4, temporarily halting its enforcement.

The standoff between state and federal authority escalated when Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier, a noted DeSantis ally, issued a letter on April 23, essentially green-lighting law enforcement to continue enforcing the law, despite the judicial order to the contrary. This defiance against the federal court's decision didn't sit well with everyone in the law enforcement community. Sheriff Bob Gualtieri of Pinellas County has chosen to adhere to Judge Williams' order, highlighting a rift within the state's own ranks about how to proceed.

DeSantis' Disregard for Judicial Oversight

Governor DeSantis's reaction to the judicial block of his immigration law is telling. By calling Judge Williams an "activist judge" and vowing to continue with the law's enforcement, DeSantis is blatantly disregarding the checks and balances that are foundational to the U.S. legal system. His statement on X (formerly Twitter), emphasizing Florida's role in leading immigration enforcement in collaboration with the Trump administration, further underscores a political agenda that prioritizes harsh immigration policies over legal and human rights considerations.

Legal and Human Rights Concerns

The legal battle against this law is being spearheaded by organizations such as the Florida ACLU, the Community Justice Project, and Americans for Immigrant Justice. The criticism from Alana Greer, Director of the Community Justice Project, is particularly poignant. She points out the Attorney General's audacity in encouraging law enforcement to ignore not one, but two direct federal orders halting the law's enforcement. Such actions not only undermine the federal judiciary but also pose a significant threat to the constitutional rights of individuals within Florida.

The Role of Law Enforcement

Sheriff Gualtieri's decision to abide by the federal court's order, rather than the Attorney General's letter, shines a beacon of rationality amidst this legal chaos. It underscores the predicament law enforcement officials are placed in — having to choose between state directives and federal court orders. Gualtieri's stance is a stark reminder that adherence to the rule of law, especially orders issued by the judiciary, is paramount in a democratic society.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The unfolding situation in Florida is more than a mere legal skirmish over an immigration law; it's a litmus test for the state's commitment to the rule of law and judicial authority. Governor DeSantis's and Attorney General Uthmeier's actions set a dangerous precedent, signaling a willingness to flout judicial checks when they conflict with political objectives. This not only endangers the rights of Floridians but also erodes the foundational principles of checks and balances that safeguard American democracy. As this legal battle continues, one can only hope that judicial oversight and the rule of law prevail over political expediency and disregard for human rights.
