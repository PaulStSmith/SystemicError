“It Wasn’t Me!” – The International Blame Game as Political Theater

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 12, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In the digital age, sometimes a one-minute skit does more to explain international relations than a semester of geopolitics. A recent comedic short, "Who Started It?", perfectly captures the pathetic theater that passes for diplomacy today: nations bickering like children, each desperately trying to deflect blame with a “they started it!” tantrum, as if World War III were just another schoolyard scrap.

The video anthropomorphizes countries as characters in a group chat of dysfunction. Germany, weary of its historic baggage, breathes a sigh of relief that—for once—the spark of global conflict might not ignite in Europe. Pakistan, always on the defensive, insists it’s innocent while the U.S., Afghanistan, and Iran raise a collective eyebrow so hard it could crack a satellite.

Then there’s India, cloaked in Bollywood glitter, projecting peace while conveniently ignoring the authoritarian elephant in the room. When Europe tries to remind them of their problematic leadership, India shrugs—“nobody's perfect”—and throws the blame back at Pakistan. Of course, Pakistan is more than willing to mirror that sentiment, like a game of nuclear hot potato.

And Europe? Oh, sweet, self-important Europe, trying to mediate while forgetting its own colonial fingerprints are smudged all over these tensions. Meanwhile, Germany pleads, “I am not involved,” the geopolitical equivalent of whistling and walking away from a burning building you helped construct. Finally, the UK strolls in late, ready to offer commentary, as if it hasn’t ghostwritten half the post-colonial chaos we’re dealing with.

The skit is short, but its brilliance lies in exposing how global politics has devolved into a farce where no one takes responsibility, yet everyone wants to control the narrative. Instead of diplomacy, we get denial. Instead of accountability, we get accusation. And the result? A world perpetually on the edge, held hostage by the egos of nations that behave more like toddlers than tacticians.

When war looms on the horizon, leaders ought to be sober strategists. But what we have instead is an international sandbox full of bullies, gaslighters, and attention-seekers, all pretending they’re victims while arming themselves to the teeth.

So the next time you hear a politician talk about "peacekeeping" or "stability," remember this skit. Because often, the ones who shout the loudest about keeping peace are the same ones playing with matches behind their back.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
