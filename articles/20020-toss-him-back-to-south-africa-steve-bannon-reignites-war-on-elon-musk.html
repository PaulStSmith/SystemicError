‘Toss him back to South Africa!’ Steve Bannon reignites war on Elon Musk

Former Trump official and conservative commentator Steve Bannon reignited his feud with Elon Musk Saturday after urging the South African native to be deported to his home country on his podcast “War Room.”

Bannon’s comments came shortly after President Donald Trump signed an executive order requiring all companies to pay a $100,000 annual fee for every employee hired through an H-1B visa, an announcement that sent shockwaves through the corporate world, much of which has become reliant on cheap foreign labor.

Bannon has historically feuded with Musk over H-1B visas, with Bannon calling them a form of “indentured servitude,” and Musk, championing them and pledging to “go to war on this issue.”

“We started this drama in Mar-a-Lago I guess in late December, early January where I got into it with Vivek Ramaswamy and the great 'Elmo' Musk,” Bannon said. “By the way, he's totally illegal, he came in on an H-1B scam and then scammed the whole thing about his record.”

Bannon also mentioned recent calls from MAGA lawmakers to deport Rep. Ilhan Omar (D-MN), a sitting member of Congress and United States citizen, for her criticism of Charlie Kirk’s past controversial comments in the wake of his killing.

“If you want to go after Omar – and I do, to toss her out of the country and send her back to the horn of Africa – I want to toss out Elon 'Elmo' and send him back to South Africa, because he's here on a different version of the scam,” Bannon continued. “...Screw you, dude! You're a foreigner that is taking advantage of foreign labor at the detriment of American citizens.”

Musk’s company Tesla is heavily reliant on H-1B visas, having brought in 724 employees from the program in 2023 alone. Musk himself entered the United States on a similar visa.

The clash between the two over H-1B visas stems back to late last year, and exploded into the public in June after Bannon first urged the Trump administration to launch a probe into Musk’s immigration status, and his alleged use of illegal drugs.

With Trump’s latest announcement, however, an announcement expected to significantly reduce the use of H-1B visas in the United States, Bannon also took a moment to gloat for having gained ground on the debate.

“Given where we were at the beginning of this fight in January, and this was going to be the hill that Elon Musk died on... he's bleeding out a little bit, isn't he, from where President Trump has come?” Bannon said.

Our Analysis:

Bannon vs. Musk: A Battle of Ideologies and Insults

Once again, Steve Bannon, the former Trump official and conservative commentator, is courting controversy with his caustic rhetoric, this time setting his sights on Elon Musk, the tech mogul behind Tesla and SpaceX. On his podcast "War Room," Bannon has not only reignited his longstanding feud with Musk but has also called for the deportation of the South African-born entrepreneur. This call to action comes in the wake of President Donald Trump's executive order imposing a hefty annual fee on companies employing workers through H-1B visas—a move that has sent tremors through the corporate landscape.

The Clash Over H-1B Visas

At the heart of Bannon and Musk's feud lies the divisive issue of H-1B visas. Bannon has labeled the program a form of "indentured servitude," while Musk has been a vocal champion, even pledging to "go to war on this issue." Musk's company, Tesla, heavily relies on these visas, with 724 employees brought in through the program in 2023 alone. Musk himself is a product of a similar immigration pathway, making this debate deeply personal.

Bannon's Deportation Crusade

Bannon's comments on deportation aren't limited to Musk. He also targets Rep. Ilhan Omar (D-MN), a sitting member of Congress and U.S. citizen, for her criticism of Charlie Kirk. Bannon's willingness to lump together Musk's immigration status with Omar's citizenship status in his argument for deportation is not only a conflation of distinct legal scenarios but also a dangerous nod to the more xenophobic undercurrents of political discourse. His use of derogatory nicknames and the casual suggestion of deporting dissenting voices are indicative of a broader, more troubling trend of political polarization and dehumanization.

The Bigger Picture: Nationalism vs. Globalism

This feud illuminates the ongoing battle between nationalist and globalist viewpoints. Bannon, with his nationalist stance, views H-1B visas and similar programs as detrimental to American workers, advocating for a more isolationist, America-first approach. Musk, on the other hand, represents a globalist perspective, seeing value in the cross-pollination of talent and ideas across borders. This clash is emblematic of a larger ideological struggle within the United States, one that affects immigration policy, labor markets, and the tech industry's future.

The Fallout of Trump's Executive Order

Trump's executive order, no doubt celebrated by Bannon, poses significant challenges for companies reliant on H-1B visas. For an industry that has thrived on global talent, this move could stifle innovation and economic growth. It's a clear victory for Bannon's camp, at least in the short term, but the long-term consequences could be far-reaching, affecting America's competitiveness on the world stage.

Conclusion: A Divisive Debate with No Clear Winners

Bannon's latest comments, while shocking, are not surprising. They reflect a deep-seated animosity towards globalism and a simplistic solution to complex issues. Deportation as a punishment for political and business adversaries sets a dangerous precedent. While Bannon may relish in this momentary "victory," the broader implications of such rhetoric and policy moves could harm the very fabric of American society and its standing in the global economy. The debate over H-1B visas and immigration is far from over, but one thing is clear: the discourse needs a return to civility and a focus on crafting policies that balance the needs of American workers with the realities of a global economy.
