Pam Bondi on ‘shaky ground’ with Trump after ‘disqualifying’ gaffe: Political expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 15, 20264 min read

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi attends a House Judiciary Committee hearing on oversight of the Justice Department to testify, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 11, 2026. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Pam Bondi has faced a “MAGA media backlash” after deflecting from a question about Jeffrey Epstein this week by pivoting to the strong performance of the economy, a backlash so fierce that one political expert suspects she might get the boot from President Donald Trump.

On Wednesday, Bondi testified before the House Judiciary Committee, fielding a barrage of questions about her agency’s failure to adequately investigate or prosecute potential co-conspirators of Jeffrey Epstein.

On multiple occasions, Bondi pivoted from questions about Epstein to brag about the performance of the Dow Jones, a pivot that has gone on to be ridiculed in both liberal and conservative media spaces. And, while Bondi may have been sufficiently loyal to Trump during the hearing, the widespread mockery has put her on “shaky ground” with the president, political strategist and expert Mohamad Aly Elleithee said on CNN Saturday.

“She was performing for an audience of one, but that audience of one does pay attention to Fox, does pay attention to internet memes, and if he feels like it’s leading to he and his administration being mocked by their own people, that’s shaky ground to be on,” Elleithee said during an appearance on CNN’s “The Arena: Saturday.”

“It was a disqualifying performance by someone who is increasingly becoming disqualified by the American people; her approval ratings, the Department of Justice’s approval ratings under her have plummeted.”

David Chalian, host of “The Arena: Saturday,” asked the show’s panel, bluntly, what the Dow Jones and its performance had to do with the House Judiciary Committee hearing. His question was met with a sarcastic quip from CNN contributor Lulu Garcia-Navarro, who sarcastically berated Chalian for asking such a question.

“I mean, oh my lord! This was a disaster. They’re making fun of her on Fox News, which that’s when it really hurts because that’s what the president watches,” Garcia-Navarro said.

“If she was trying to curry favor with him, I don’t think that she did a very good job. That pivot to the DOW... I think you can pivot to the DOW, maybe, but not when you’re answering an Epstein question! It’s a little bit too much of a leap.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by Pam Bondi

In what can only be described as a tour de force in the realm of political evasion, Pam Bondi’s recent testimony before the House Judiciary Committee has become a textbook example of how not to handle serious inquiries. The subject at hand was her agency’s glaring incompetence—or perhaps willful negligence—in investigating the nefarious activities of Jeffrey Epstein. Yet, Bondi’s response was to gloat about the Dow Jones’ performance, a move so bafflingly irrelevant that it has sparked a “MAGA media backlash” fierce enough to potentially jeopardize her standing with none other than President Donald Trump himself.

The Pivot to Nowhere

The pivot from discussing Jeffrey Epstein—a convicted sex offender whose connections to high-profile figures have made his case a matter of public interest—to the Dow Jones is not just a leap; it’s a quantum jump into the absurd. The Dow Jones, while an indicator of economic health, has about as much relevance to the Epstein case as the latest scores in the Premier League have to solving climate change.

The Mockery Ensues

The backlash was not confined to the predictable quarters of liberal media but spilled over into conservative spaces, including the hallowed halls of Fox News. This universal mockery highlights a critical misstep by Bondi: underestimating her audience’s intelligence and overestimating their tolerance for deflection. Mohamad Aly Elleithee’s commentary on CNN underscores this point, suggesting that Bondi’s performance was not just inadequate but potentially “disqualifying,” as it did little to shield Trump from ridicule, even among his base.

It’s worth noting, the notion of performing for an “audience of one” reflects a broader issue within the Trump administration: the tendency of officials to prioritize the president’s approval over accountability or transparency. Yet, this strategy has its pitfalls, as Bondi’s case demonstrates. When even that audience of one tunes into Fox News only to find his administration being lampooned, it’s clear the tactic has backfired spectacularly.

Approval Ratings in Freefall

The consequences of Bondi’s disastrous pivot are not abstract. As Elleithee pointed out, both her approval ratings and those of the Department of Justice under her watch have plummeted. This is no small matter. In the context of the Trump administration’s often tumultuous relationship with public opinion and media, any significant dip in approval ratings can spell disaster for an official’s career longevity.

The Bottom Line

The lesson here is stark and unmistakable. Attempting to deflect a question about a serious scandal by bragging about unrelated economic indicators does not merely fail to deceive the public; it insults their intelligence. Bondi’s performance was not just a personal failure but a symptom of a larger, systemic issue within the Trump administration: a disregard for accountability, a preference for spectacle over substance, and a dangerous game of prioritizing the whims of one man over the concerns of the many.

In the end, the saga of Pam Bondi’s testimony is more than just a cautionary tale of political hubris; it’s a damning indictment of an administration’s systemic error in judgment.
