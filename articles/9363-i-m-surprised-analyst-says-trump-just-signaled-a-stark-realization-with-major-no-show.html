The White House announced on Tuesday that President Donald Trump will not attend a meeting in Hungary with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy.

Speaking about the report on MSNBC, international affairs reporter Elise Labott is hopeful that this will influence Trump’s decision about sending more weapons to Ukraine. It hasn’t in the past, however.

Trump spent over two hours on the phone with Putin last week and announced a trip to Hungary with the world leaders as part of the ongoing efforts to end the war after the Russian invasion.

“Kind of an abrupt postponement. But maybe the question is, should we be surprised?” asked MSNBC host Chris Jansing.

“I’m surprised that he’s not going,” said Labott. “I think Donald Trump can’t quit ... Vladimir Putin. He wants to go there. He wants to see that — he — obviously, we just talked about the Middle East. He fancies himself a dealmaker. He really wants to get Putin to agree, and he thinks that he can. I think the fact that he’s not going is a recognition by President Trump himself that Vladimir Putin has no interest in ending the war.”

She recalled that U.S. officials were also set to meet with Russian officials, but now those meetings are canceled.

Labott said that the Russian position hasn’t changed, but she’ll be curious to see if Trump will give Tomahawk missiles to Ukraine after all. During his meeting with Zelenskyy, Trump said he wasn’t prepared to provide them with anything.

“Now that President Trump has made the conclusion, you know, [Putin] is continuing to play us, he’s not ready to end the war. I’m going to look to see whether President Trump is ready to, you know, put the squeeze on Putin. And at least in rhetoric, I think you’ll hear more about these tomahawks. Now, whether President Trump is actually willing to give them to the Ukrainians is an open question because that would be an escalation.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Putin Tango: A Dance of Duplicity and Delay

The latest episode in the ongoing political drama between the United States, Russia, and Ukraine unfolds with a plot twist: President Donald Trump has abruptly decided to skip a crucial meeting in Hungary with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. This decision, as reported, sheds light on the complex and often contradictory foreign policy stance of the Trump administration, particularly in relation to the Russian aggression in Ukraine.

Trump’s Absence: A Strategic Retreat or a Tacit Admission?

The White House’s announcement, as dissected by international affairs reporter Elise Labott on MSNBC, hints at a deeper narrative underpinning the Trump administration’s approach to Russia and Ukraine. The optimism that Trump’s no-show might influence his stance on sending more weapons to Ukraine is both hopeful and naive, given his historical reluctance to confront Putin directly. Trump’s over two hours long telephonic engagement with Putin last week, followed by a now-canceled trip, reeks of the ongoing flirtation with diplomacy that has yielded little in terms of concrete outcomes.

Why the sudden change of heart? Labott speculates, and perhaps rightly so, that Trump’s decision stems from a realization or admission that Putin has no real interest in ending the war in Ukraine. This realization, however, is not a newfound epiphany but a reflection of the consistent Russian stance. The cancellation of meetings between U.S. and Russian officials further cements the stagnation in diplomatic progress.

The Illusion of Leverage

Trump’s rhetoric on potentially providing Ukraine with Tomahawk missiles appears to be more bluster than substance. During his meeting with Zelenskyy, Trump’s reluctance to arm Ukraine was evident, portraying a president who, despite recognizing the futility of appeasing Putin, remains hesitant to escalate the conflict. Labott’s curiosity about whether Trump will indeed “put the squeeze on Putin” resonates with a broader skepticism about the Trump administration’s willingness to take decisive action.

Conclusion: A Symphony of Inconsistencies

The unfolding scenario is a testament to the Trump administration’s symphony of inconsistencies in dealing with Russia and Ukraine. Trump’s portrayal as a dealmaker clashes with the reality of his administration’s diplomatic dance around Putin’s aggression. The abrupt cancellation of the Hungary meeting is but a chapter in this ongoing saga, highlighting a foreign policy approach that is reactive, rather than strategic.

Trump’s ambivalence towards arming Ukraine not only questions the United States’ commitment to supporting its allies but also underscores the precarious nature of its stance against Russian expansionism. As the situation evolves, one thing remains clear: Trump’s foreign policy legacy will be marked by missed opportunities, mixed messages, and the perpetual shadow of Vladimir Putin.
