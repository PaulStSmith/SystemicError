Trump's 'odd weekend' amid global turmoil rattles expert: 'We’re seeing him temperamental'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 17, 20265 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump looks from the stage after delivering remarks to members of the Republican Party, at Trump National Doral Miami in Miami, Florida, U.S., March 9, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

As tensions with Iran escalate, President Donald Trump’s behavior last weekend appeared “chaotic” and “odd,” The Guardian’s Adam Gabbatt noted on Sunday, citing an expert who offered a possible explanation for the president’s “rash behaviors.”

While the Trump administration’s ongoing and widening war against Iran continues to rattle global stability and send “tremors” through the world economy, Trump’s conduct last weekend, Gabbatt argued, appeared strikingly out of step with the gravity of the moment, and to such an extent that it “defied norms and raised eyebrows over his state of mind.”

“Take last Sunday, for example: the Pentagon solemnly announced that a seventh US service member had been killed in the Iran conflict,” Gabbatt wrote. “Trump spent the day playing golf in Florida, where he appeared to be wearing the same baseball cap he wore during a dignified transfer ritual of dead military members on Saturday.”

That same day, Trump hosted a summit in Florida where he spoke at length about the Spanish language, praising himself on his ability to gauge the quality of English-Spanish interpreters despite not speaking the language, and telling Latin American leaders “I’m not learning your damn language.”

“Trump’s odd weekend came after he had posted eight separate times on Friday about how he had fallen out with Bill Maher, the American TV host and comedian,” Gabbatt wrote. “Maher has no connection to the war in Iran.”

The possible explanation for Trump’s behavior, Emmitt Riley III, a political professor at the University of the South, theorized, was a simple one.

“The president has always exemplified these rash behaviors, but I think he sometimes uses that strategically,” Riley told The Guardian in its analysis Sunday.

“But the other element of this is the president is older, and all of the critiques that he leveled against Joe Biden’s age appear to be impacting him now: we’re seeing him falling asleep in cabinet meetings, we’re seeing him be temperamental.”

It was also Trump’s enormous personal wealth, Riley argued, that may explain his “odd” behaviors amid global chaos.

“How do we expect a person who is a billionaire to actually be concerned about the wellbeing of people who are not of wealth, who are not of that particular economic class?” she told The Guardian. “And I think Trump’s behavior shows either he’s a narcissist, or he just simply doesn’t care.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Narrative: Trump’s Behavior Amid Escalating Tensions

The recent article from The Guardian, focusing on President Donald Trump’s behavior amid escalating tensions with Iran, threads a narrative that paints the president’s actions as not only incongruent with the expectations of his office but potentially symptomatic of deeper personal or strategic flaws. However, a closer examination of the institutional power dynamics, decision-making processes, and the framing of the narrative itself reveals a more complex picture that requires dissection beyond surface-level observations.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

1. Institutional Power: In the context of U.S. foreign policy and military engagement, the President holds significant institutional power. This includes the authority to direct military actions, shape foreign policy, and, to a large extent, influence the global narrative around these engagements. Therefore, President Trump, during his tenure, wielded considerable control over the actions leading to and the management of tensions with Iran.

2. Decision-Making: The decisions leading to the “widening war against Iran,” as mentioned in the article, would have been influenced by a broad spectrum of actors within the Trump administration, including defense and foreign policy advisers. However, as President, Trump’s decisions—ranging from military engagement to public communications—were pivotal. Thus, his personal behavior and public statements, especially in times of crisis, are reflective of the administration’s stance and are consequential in their impact on both domestic and international perceptions.

Framing and Responsibility

The article’s framing focuses heavily on President Trump’s personal behavior—his golfing during a critical time, his comments at a summit, and his social media engagements with figures unrelated to the Iran conflict. This narrative choice raises questions about the allocation of responsibility and the potential misdirection away from systemic issues.

Misdirected Responsibility? Critiquing the President’s behavior during times of crisis is valid, particularly when such behavior seems to diverge from the expected norms of leadership. However, focusing solely on Trump’s “chaotic” and “odd” actions without a deeper analysis of the systemic and institutional frameworks that enable such behavior risks attributing complex geopolitical dynamics to the personal whims of one individual. While Trump’s actions and statements are undoubtedly influential, they are part of a larger matrix of decision-making processes within the U.S. government.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating: The article’s emphasis on Trump’s personal wealth and alleged narcissism as explanations for his perceived indifference to the “wellbeing of people” simplifies the multifaceted motivations behind political decisions. This framing can inadvertently create a false equivalence between personal characteristics and institutional actions. Moreover, it risks scapegoating Trump’s personality for broader systemic behaviors and policies that persist beyond any single administration’s tenure.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Critique

While President Trump’s behavior, as outlined in The Guardian article, certainly warrants scrutiny, a nuanced critique must look beyond personal idiosyncrasies. It is essential to consider the institutional powers at play, the collective decision-making process, and the broader systemic issues that influence, and are reflected by, the actions of any U.S. President.

By focusing too narrowly on Trump’s personal behavior, the narrative risks overshadowing the systemic and institutional critiques necessary for a comprehensive understanding of U.S. foreign policy actions. In the spirit of holding power to account, it is crucial to dissect these layers with the same rigor applied to analyzing individual behaviors, ensuring that critiques are not only sharp and scathing but grounded in a holistic view of systemic dynamics.
