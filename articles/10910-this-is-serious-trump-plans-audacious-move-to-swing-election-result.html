'This is serious': Trump plans 'audacious' move to swing election result

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In a sharp move to give former Gov. Andrew Cuomo (D-NY) a push to defeat Democrat and front-runner Zohran Mamdani in the New York City mayor's race this November, advisors to President Donald Trump say he's considered giving Mayor Eric Adams an administration position.

Adams was offered a position at the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, an anonymous person with direct knowledge told Politico. Adams denied the reports, saying he is not dropping out of the race.

Polls show Adams trails in fourth place, and the possible move could aim to potentially damage Mamdani's chances in the general election and give Cuomo a path to victory, The New York Times reports. Insiders also shared that the administration is looking for a role for Republican candidate Curtis Sliwa.

The Times called it a "potentially audacious intervention."

Billionaire grocery and oil magnate John Catsimatidis told the Times that after speaking with Trump on Sunday about the election, he thought the race may change within days.

“He’s very concerned,” Catsimatidis said of Trump. “How do they say it, this is for all the tea in China. This is serious.”

Mamdani wrote on X that the Times report confirmed his opponents were backed by Trump.

"Today’s news confirms it: Cuomo is Trump’s choice for Mayor," he wrote. "The White House is considering jobs for Adams and Sliwa to clear the field. New Yorkers are sick of corrupt politics and backroom deals. No matter who’s running, we will deliver a better future on November 4."

It's unclear if the discussions have led to any decisions from Adams, who is a Democrat running as an independent. Sources indicated to The Times that nothing has been confirmed, and an anonymous source said talks were heading in "several different directions."

“Mayor Adams is a lifelong New Yorker who has dedicated his entire career to this city, and he intends to continue that work for another four years as mayor,” the spokeswoman, Kayla Mamelak Altus, said in a statement.

Cuomo has known Trump for more than 40 years. The former New York governor, who resigned in 2021 after multiple reports of sexual harassment, conceded to Mamdani after he was defeated by the 33-year-old self-professed socialist in the primary elections.

Sliwa has not confirmed if any Trump advisors have contacted him, stating he is "committed to carrying this fight through to Election Day."

Our Analysis:

The Chessboard of Political Maneuvering: Trump, Cuomo, and the NYC Mayor's Race

In a political saga that reads more like a strategic play in a high-stakes game of chess than a democratic election, the recent moves surrounding the New York City mayor's race reveal the depth of political maneuvering and alliances that often remain obscured from the public eye. At the heart of this unfolding drama is none other than former President Donald Trump, whose advisors have floated the idea of offering Mayor Eric Adams an administration position in what appears to be a calculated attempt to reshape the mayoral race in favor of former Governor Andrew Cuomo (D-NY).

The Pawns and the Kings: Trump's Alleged Strategy

The proposition to relocate Mayor Adams to a role within the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development—courtesy of an anonymous source as reported by Politico—signals a strategic maneuver designed to weaken Democrat front-runner Zohran Mamdani's position in the upcoming election. By potentially removing Adams from the race, the move ostensibly opens a pathway for Cuomo, a political figure whose career seemed all but over following his resignation amid multiple sexual harassment allegations in 2021.

The Reaction from the Camps

Adams has publicly denied these reports, asserting his intention to continue his mayoral campaign, despite trailing in fourth place in recent polls. This denial, however, does little to quell speculations about the nature and intent behind these political machinations. Meanwhile, Mamdani's response on X (formerly Twitter) encapsulates the sentiment of many New Yorkers: a profound distaste for corrupt politics and backroom deals, underscoring his commitment to a more transparent and equitable future for the city.

Trump's Inexplicable Interest in the NYC Mayor's Race

The involvement of Trump and his advisors in the local politics of New York City is a telling indication of the former president's continued influence and interest in political affairs, post-presidency. His discussions with billionaire John Catsimatidis about the election's importance further emphasize the high stakes involved, with Catsimatidis paraphrasing Trump's concern as being "for all the tea in China."

Cuomo: A Controversial Choice

The support for Andrew Cuomo, a figure mired in controversy following his resignation, is a contentious strategy. It raises questions about the values and objectives guiding the political endorsements of influential figures like Trump. Cuomo's concession to Mamdani in the primary elections seemed to mark the end of his political aspirations, yet this new development suggests a possible, albeit controversial, path back to political relevance.

The Uncertain Path Forward

As the election approaches, the potential for last-minute changes and surprises looms large. With Adams denying plans to exit the race and the discussions around potential roles for Adams and Republican candidate Curtis Sliwa still unconfirmed, the political landscape remains volatile. Sliwa's commitment to continue his campaign further complicates the scenario, adding another layer of unpredictability to an already tumultuous race.

Conclusion: The Spectacle of Political Chess

The New York City mayor's race has transformed into a spectacle of political strategy, alliances, and interventions. The rumored involvement of Trump and his advisors in attempting to reshape the race in favor of Cuomo is a vivid demonstration of the complex interplay of power, ambition, and influence that defines the American political system. As New Yorkers prepare to cast their votes, the underlying question remains: Will the city's future be decided by the electorate's will, or by the strategic maneuvers of political power players operating behind the scenes?
