One of the first reporters to extensively interview the survivors of Jeffrey Epstein’s sexual abuse was amazed by an admission by one of the late sex offender’s closest associates.

Les Wexner, the 88-year-old former Victoria’s Secret chief executive, testified Wednesday at his Ohio home to Democrats from the House Oversight Committee, and The Atlantic’s Sarah Fitzpatrick reacted on “CNN This Morning” to a viral moment from that deposition that was caught on video.

“I want to play for you one thing,” said host Audie Cornish, “because what we’re seeing here in the U.S. is more conversation, more depositions. So for example, you have Victoria’s Secret owner Les Wexner. He’s been questioned for his relationship or ties to Epstein by the House Oversight Committee, specifically, okay, and video is starting to come out about the deposition, and I want to show you an example of a moment in the conversation he had just told lawmakers that the FBI had never spoken to him about Epstein, and as he’s talking, he gets a note. So here’s an example of that.”

The video shows the elderly retail baron providing lawmakers with loquacious answers to their questions about his relationship with Epstein, which he insisted was professional and finance-related only, when his attorney leaned over and whispered, “I’ll f------ kill you if you answer another question with more than five words.”

“It’s incredible,” Fitzpatrick said. “I mean, it’s amazing moment caught on tape. But to me, an even more important moment that you see there, the fact that he’s never been questioned by the FBI, never been questioned by the FBI in over almost two decades of federal investigations into Jeffrey Epstein, a man who had if not so much material information, and we have had complaints.”

“I mean there are reports to the FBI, to [New York Police Department] in 1996 stemming from Wexner,” she added, “from a victim that had spent time at Wexner’s home. So it just like it it defies logic and it really makes me question our confidence in the FBI and in our justice system.”

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth Behind the Wexner-Epstein Nexus

In a recent spectacle that could only be described as both astonishing and disturbing, we’ve seen Les Wexner, the octogenarian former chief executive of Victoria’s Secret, entangled in the sordid web of Jeffrey Epstein’s legacy. The deposition, as covered by The Atlantic’s Sarah Fitzpatrick on “CNN This Morning,” sheds light on a saga that continues to unravel, revealing the depths of systemic failure and the aloofness of those in power.

Wexner’s Testimony: A Dance Around the Truth

Wexner’s testimony to Democrats from the House Oversight Committee, held at his Ohio residence, was a masterclass in evasion. The moment his lawyer threatened him, albeit in a whisper not meant for public ears, “I’ll f------ kill you if you answer another question with more than five words,” we witnessed not just a viral spectacle but a damning indictment of the culture of silence and intimidation that seems to pervade the upper echelons of power.

This moment, caught on video, is emblematic of a larger issue at hand: the reluctance of those closely associated with Epstein to fully disclose their connections and the degree to which those connections influenced their personal and professional lives. Wexner’s insistence that his relationship with Epstein was purely professional and finance-related rings hollow when juxtaposed against the backdrop of allegations and evidence that suggest otherwise.

The FBI’s Mysterious Silence

Perhaps even more egregious than the individual failings of Wexner is the systemic failure of federal law enforcement, specifically the FBI, to adequately investigate these connections. Fitzpatrick’s astonishment at the revelation that Wexner had never been questioned by the FBI in almost two decades of investigations into Epstein is not just justified; it’s a clarion call for accountability.

The fact that there were reports to the FBI and NYPD as early as 1996, stemming from a victim who had spent time at Wexner’s home, only to see this avenue of enquiry seemingly ignored, is nothing short of scandalous. It defies logic, as Fitzpatrick rightly points out, and fundamentally undermines our confidence in the institutions that are supposed to protect us from such predators.

A Systemic Error

This debacle is not just a story of individual malfeasance; it’s a narrative about systemic error. It’s about a system that allows the rich and powerful to operate with impunity, where connections can shield one from scrutiny, and where silence can be bought or enforced through intimidation.

The Oversight Committee’s investigation into Wexner’s ties to Epstein is a step in the right direction, but it’s just the tip of the iceberg. What’s needed is a comprehensive overhaul of how these cases are handled, ensuring that no one is above the law, and that justice is not just a concept but a reality for the victims of Epstein’s crimes.

In a world that often feels like it’s spiraling out of control, the Wexner-Epstein saga is a stark reminder of the work that remains to be done. It’s a call to action for those in power to dismantle the structures that allow such egregious breaches of trust and morality to occur. Only then can we begin to restore confidence in our institutions and in the very fabric of our justice system.
