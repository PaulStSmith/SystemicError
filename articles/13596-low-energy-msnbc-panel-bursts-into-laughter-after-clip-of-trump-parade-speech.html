'Low energy': MSNBC panel bursts into laughter after clip of Trump parade speech

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 16, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Dissected Display of Diminishing CharismaThe Underlying Narrative

Donald Trump's speech at his much-maligned U.S. Army 250th anniversary Saturday celebration on his own birthday did not escape withering scrutiny on Sunday morning.

For those who stuck around to hear the president's speech after the Army parade through the streets of D.C. they were able to witness a downbeat address compared to normal Trump events, according to the panel on MSNBC's "The Weekend."

After sharing a clip from Saturday of the president speaking in near monotone, co-host Jonathan Capehart offered, "What's notable to me in in the president's remarks is the lack of, at least in that clip we showed and at least in the reporting that I've seen, it wasn't his usual partisan nasty speech. '"

"He also seemed a little –– it was short and he seemed a little, oh, I don't know, somber might be too strong a word, low energy might be too mean a word," he pointed out which led to laughter.

"What word would you think?" co-host Eugene Daniels asked Jeff Mason of Reuters who was in attendance.

"I think it contrasted with some of his remarks previously in military settings. I was with him when he went to West Point and spoke to that group of cadets, and that was a very political speech," he recalled.

"So that yesterday's wasn't at that same level," he later added. "What is interesting, though, just to give a little bit of color from being out there, is when the president came to speak, a lot of people in the crowd had left him. So it was it was a long parade, and I think some people ended up feeling like they'd seen what they came to see after, you know, multiple tanks and multiple sets of troops and when he came up to talk, people could still watch it on the Jumbotrons. but the crowds had begun to thin out."

Our Analysis:

A Dissected Display of Diminishing Charisma

Donald Trump's speech at the 250th anniversary of the U.S. Army, held on his own birthday, has once again stirred the pot, but not in the usual manner we've come to expect from the former reality TV star turned President. The event, which was criticized even before it took place, has provided a rich tableau for analysis, not for the bombastic or controversial statements that have been Trump’s hallmark, but rather for the absence of them.

Firstly, let's address the elephant in the room—the "downbeat address". Trump, a figure synonymous with combative rhetoric and high energy presentations, delivered what was described as a near monotone speech. This is not merely a departure from his standard operating procedure; it is an aberration that screams volumes. The analysis provided by the panel on MSNBC's "The Weekend" slices through the facade to question, why the lack of fire and brimstone? Was it strategic, or has the once unflappable Donald Trump finally encountered a foe he can't outmaneuver: waning influence?

Jonathan Capehart's observations point out the "lack of... usual partisan nasty speech," raising eyebrows. In the political theater, where Trump has played both the hero and the villain (depending on whom you ask), a performance lacking in vitriol is as noteworthy as a Shakespeare play devoid of tragedy. The panel's laughter at the search for a word to describe Trump's demeanor—settling somewhere between somber and low energy—is telling. It's not just the speech that was lacking; it was the energy, the life force that usually fuels Trump's engagements. This wasn't Trump being strategic; this was Trump deflated.

Jeff Mason of Reuters, who attended the event, provided a critical piece of context: the thinning crowds. This is not merely an incidental observation; it is a barometer for Trump's current political climate. The masses that once hung on his every word, that weathered storms and controversies to support him, seemed to have dwindled. Was it the long parade, or is it a sign of a deeper issue—an erosion of the unwavering support Trump once commanded?

The Contrast with Past Military Speeches

Jeff Mason's comparison with Trump's previous addresses in military settings, such as his speech to West Point cadets, underscores a significant shift. Those speeches were charged, political, and delivered to rapt audiences. The contrast couldn't be starker. It raises the question: what has changed? Is it Trump, the audience, or the political landscape at large?

The Underlying Narrative

This event, and more specifically Trump's role in it, signals more than just a bad day or an off speech. It's a mirror reflecting the changing dynamics of Trump's base, his influence, and perhaps his recognition of both. The thinning crowd, the subdued delivery, and the absence of the usual fervor paint a picture of a leader struggling to connect in the ways he once did.

In the grand scheme, this may be seen as a minor footnote in Trump's tumultuous narrative. However, it's these small cracks that often precede the crumbling of foundations. For a figure who has built an empire on the strength of personality and the power of speech, a somber, low-energy address in front of a diminishing audience cannot be dismissed as an anomaly. It's a signal, loud and clear, that the tides may be turning.

As we dissect this event, it's crucial to keep an eye on the horizon. Political fortunes can change as quickly as the weather, but the signs are there, for those willing to read them. This may very well be the beginning of a new chapter in the saga of Donald Trump, one marked not by what is said, but by what is left unsaid, and by those who are no longer there to listen.
