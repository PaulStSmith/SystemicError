Republican threatens to kill DC transit funding unless it names trains for Trump

A Florida Republican is demanding that Washington, D.C., name subway trains in its transit system after President Donald Trump and, If it doesn't, he says Congress will cut funding to the public system shared by the district as well as the states of Maryland and Virginia.

Fox5DC reported Thursday that Rep. Greg Steube (R-FL) will stop funding the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA) unless it changes the whole agency's name to the Washington Metropolitan Authority for Greater Access (WMAGA) — and renames the metro system the "Trump Train."

"WMATA has received billions in federal assistance over the years and continues to face operational, safety, and fiscal challenges," Steube said in a statement on his congressional website. "In the spirit of DOGE, this bill demands accountability by conditioning federal funding on reforms that signal a cultural shift away from bureaucratic stagnation toward public-facing excellence and patriotism."

Washington, D.C. is not an official state and, as such, the way the government is funded is radically different than its local counterparts. One example is that the budget of D.C. must be approved not by the mayor or the city council, as in other large cities, but rather by Congress.

Steube believes that rebranding the DC metro would "promote efficiency, service quality, and public trust," according to the report.

However, D.C. is known for its quality and efficiency in public transportation, according to one firm. The financial analytics firm SmartAsset used Census Bureau data to find the nation’s best public transit systems, putting Washington, D.C. at number one, the Washington Post reported.

It's also known as a city with a strong anti-Trump sentiment. Washington, D.C., voted against Trump in the 2024 election to such a degree that he earned only 6.47%. Vice President Kamala Harris earned more than 90% of the vote.

Steube cites events like the NFL Draft in D.C. and the 2026 FIFA World Cup games as the reason for the rebranding. The World Cup will take place in 16 cities across the U.S., Canada and Mexico. One of the host cities is Washington, D.C.

However, none of the other host cities have been threatened with funding cuts if they don't change the names of their public transit systems.

Our Analysis:

The "Trump Train" Fiasco: A Deep Dive into Political Absurdity

In a maneuver that could only emerge from the depths of what can generously be described as a bizarre political strategy, Rep. Greg Steube (R-FL) has thrown down the gauntlet, demanding the Washington, D.C. metro be renamed after former President Donald Trump. His ultimatum? Rename or face financial ruin. This isn't just a proposal; it's a theatrical display of political absurdity that demands scrutiny.

The Proposition: Steube's demand, as reported by Fox5DC, goes beyond mere renaming. He proposes transforming the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA) into the Washington Metropolitan Authority for Greater Access (WMAGA), a not-so-subtle nod to Trump's campaign slogan. But the cherry on top? The metro system shall henceforth be known as the "Trump Train."

The Justification? Steube claims, with a straight face, that this rebranding is about accountability, efficiency, and—wait for it—patriotism. He cites WMATA's operational challenges and fiscal woes as reasons for his proposal, wrapping it in a veneer of concern for public-facing excellence. The irony of demanding a cultural shift towards patriotism by renaming a public service after a polarizing figure is palpable.

Let's not forget, Washington, D.C. is a stronghold of anti-Trump sentiment, where Trump secured a measly 6.47% of the vote against Vice President Kamala Harris's overwhelming majority in the 2024 election. This proposal isn't just tone-deaf; it's an outright provocation.

The Threat: Steube's weapon of choice is federal funding. It's a stark reminder of the unique vulnerability of Washington, D.C., which, not being a state, has its budget subject to Congress's approval. By threatening to cut funding, Steube is not just attacking the city's transit system; he's holding hostage the very livelihood of its residents and the functionality of a city critical to the nation's operations.

The Absurdity: Steube attempts to rationalize his demand by citing major events such as the NFL Draft and the 2026 FIFA World Cup, somehow suggesting that a transit system named in honor of Trump would enhance these events. This leap of logic is not just a stretch; it's an Olympic-level vault over the realms of rational thought.

The Reality Check

Washington, D.C., has been recognized for its efficient and quality public transportation. A report by the financial analytics firm SmartAsset even ranked it number one in the nation. This fact makes Steube's argument for renaming as a path to promoting efficiency and service quality not just weak, but laughably irrelevant.

Moreover, the singularity of Steube's threat—targeting only D.C. among the World Cup host cities—exposes this move for what it truly is: a politically motivated stunt aimed more at garnering attention and currying favor with Trump loyalists than at addressing any real issues of public transportation.

The Bottom Line

Rep. Steube's proposal is a stark illustration of the depths to which political discourse in the United States has sunk. It's a maneuver that prioritizes spectacle over substance, divisiveness over unity, and personal political gain over the public good. The "Trump Train" proposal is not a serious policy suggestion; it's a political sideshow, unworthy of the serious consideration of Congress but deserving of our scrutiny and, ultimately, our condemnation.

In the end, the proposal to rename Washington, D.C.'s metro system after Donald Trump is less about improving public transit and more about making a mockery of it. It's a reminder of the importance of separating genuine policy debates from political theatre. And in this case, the theatre is particularly poor.
