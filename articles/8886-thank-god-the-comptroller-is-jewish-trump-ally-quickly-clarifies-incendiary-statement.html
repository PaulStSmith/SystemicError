'Thank God the comptroller is Jewish': Trump ally quickly clarifies incendiary statement

By Paulo Santos

Former White House Senior Advisor Nayyera Haq (left) and Brookyln attorney Arthur Aidala (right) appear on CNN’s “Table for Five,” Nov. 8, 2025. (Screengrab/CNN)

CNN’s “Table for Five” crew erupted in surprise after Brooklyn attorney Arthur Aidala let slip a potentially racist quip.

The Saturday Morning “Table for Five” panel was discussing the anti-Muslim movement that opponents of mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani faced in the run up to his successful election last on Tuesday.

“I think a lot of the antagonism against Mamdani is really not about policies or past statements. It’s the idea of a scary Muslim person suddenly leading New York City,” said former White House senior advisor Nayyera Haq. “Luckily, I mean, I was on the capitol property at my first job on 9/11. I think in 20 years we’ve evolved and moved past these scary images of halal meat coming for you.”

“But this is a personal point for me,” Haq continued. “Someone like Mamdani being able to be his full, truthful self, be authentic about what it means to be a New Yorker right now, who is struggling. That works. I wish it was integrity… but we’ve seen a two-time, impeached president with multiple corruption scandals get reelected. I think it’s the authenticity.”

“That’s what Trump has,” argued “Cari Champion Show” host Cari Champion. “So why can Trump be as authentic and divisive as he is while what Mamdani does is completely inappropriate?”

“He’s already said we’re not going to invest in any of our funds — thank God the [New York] comptroller who controls the funds is Jewish,” blurted the “Arthur Aidala Power Hour” host, causing confused looks and sidelong stares around the table.

“What does the comptroller being Jewish have to do with it,” asked Haq.

“He’s already said we’re not going to invest any more funds with anyone who works with Israel,” said Aidala, who Trump was temporarily considering for the position of U.S. Attorney of Brooklyn. “He decides where the pension funds go, and if you invest in Israel, then, then, according to Mamdani, he doesn’t want [that].”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Unspoken: A Critical Analysis of Arthur Aidala’s Commentary

In the midst of a seemingly progressive conversation on CNN’s “Table for Five”, Brooklyn attorney Arthur Aidala’s interjection serves as a stark reminder of the subtle yet pervasive racial prejudices that continue to permeate political discourse. The panel’s discussion, centered around the anti-Muslim sentiments faced by mayor-elect Zohran Mamdani, took an unsettling turn with Aidala’s comment, revealing not just an individual’s bias but a broader societal issue that merits a scathing examination.

The Context of Controversy

While former White House senior advisor Nayyera Haq highlighted the evolution of societal perspectives since 9/11, emphasizing the importance of authenticity in political leadership, Aidala’s remark about the New York comptroller being Jewish abruptly shifted the focus. The implication that the comptroller’s Jewish identity somehow ensures a prudent management of funds, particularly in the context of investments related to Israel, is not just deeply problematic but blatantly insinuative of age-old stereotypes.

The Underlying Assumptions

Aidala’s comment is a textbook example of racial stereotyping, suggesting that the comptroller’s religious identity directly influences his professional decisions, especially regarding investments in Israel. This insinuation is not only irrelevant to the conversation but also perpetuates harmful biases against Jewish people, echoing stereotypes of Jewish individuals having monolithic views or allegiances, particularly concerning Israel.

Moreover, the juxtaposition of this remark within a discussion on anti-Muslim sentiments faced by Mamdani underscores a broader issue: the normalization of casually racist comments under the guise of political commentary. It is indicative of a systemic problem where certain identities are scrutinized and stereotyped, while others are questioned for their authenticity and integrity.

The Double Standards of Authenticity

The panel’s debate on authenticity in politics, contrasting Mamdani’s authenticity with Trump’s divisive yet authentic persona, further illuminates the double standards applied to politicians of color versus their white counterparts. While Mamdani’s authenticity is seen as a positive yet scrutinized trait, Trump’s authenticity, despite being fraught with divisiveness and corruption, is often removed from the same level of critical analysis. This disparity not only highlights the racial biases in political discourse but also the challenges faced by politicians of color in navigating their authentic identities within the political landscape.

Conclusion: The Need for a Racially Conscious Discourse

Aidala’s remark, and the subsequent reaction it garnered, should not be dismissed as a mere slip. It is a symptomatic representation of the deeper racial prejudices embedded within political discourse. The incident calls for a critical reflection on how racial stereotypes are perpetuated by public figures and the impact of such comments on broader societal attitudes towards race and religion.

As we dissect incidents like these, it becomes imperative to advocate for a more racially conscious and inclusive political discourse, one that recognizes and challenges the subtle biases and stereotypes that continue to undermine the principles of equality and justice. In the quest for authenticity in politics, let us not overlook the authenticity of our societal prejudices, for it is only through acknowledging and confronting these truths that we can aspire to a more equitable political landscape.
