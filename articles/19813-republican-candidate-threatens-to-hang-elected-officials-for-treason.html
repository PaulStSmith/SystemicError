Republican candidate threatens to hang elected officials for 'treason'

Republican gubernatorial candidate Anthony Hudson speaking to the Mason City Council. Feb. 2, 2026 | Screenshot

Anthony Hudson, a Genesee County truck driver running for the Republican nomination for governor, has made a habit of threatening elected officials with hanging for “treason,” making such threats at least twice since the start of 2026.

On Jan. 4, in a video posted to Hudson’s social media, he says, “It’s time to get the rope” — while holding a large loop of rope around his arm — as a call to action to “Michiganians and patriots” against politicians who he alleges received kickbacks from former Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro, including Michigan’s U.S. Sens. Elissa Slotkin and Gary Peters, both Democrats.

“My hope is that every one of them will be prosecuted to the fullest extent of the law and may their names forever live in shame for their treason,” he says in the video.

Less than a month later, on Feb. 1, in a Mason City Council meeting, speaking in opposition to data center development, Hudson again threatened elected officials — this time local government officials — with hanging.

“You guys never listen, and that’s the problem. These people voted you into office so that you could be their voice in government,” he said. “When you go against that voice, you commit what we call treason. The punishment for treason is public hanging. So, I suggest you start listening.”

Hudson’s statements were strongly condemned by the state Democratic party, who also urged the Michigan Republican Party and the other Republican candidates for governor to similarly condemn the threats.

“Political violence is completely unacceptable, and this kind of dangerous, hateful extremism has no place in our state and country. Anthony Hudson must immediately retract these comments and issue an apology,” Michigan Democratic Party spokesperson Derrick Honeyman wrote in a statement.

The Michigan Republican Party did not respond to a request for a response.

But Hudson doubled down when asked to comment on his statements.

“Know the law and the consequences! I didn’t write the law, as governor I will enforce the law. Educating someone about the law and what the consequences are is not threatening anyone,” he wrote in an email to the Michigan Advance. “Don’t come at me for holding them accountable and saying the worst part out loud.”

Hudson’s comments are similar to those of President Donald Trump, who has accused six elected Democrats, including Slotkin, of committing treason after they recorded a video reminding military members that they did not have to follow illegal orders.

In reaction to the video, Trump called it “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR FROM TRAITORS” later saying it was “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH!”

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Rhetoric of Anthony Hudson: A Critical Examination

In an era where political discourse has increasingly veered into the realm of the extreme, Anthony Hudson, a Genesee County truck driver turned gubernatorial hopeful, has taken a disturbing leap, intertwining political ambition with overt threats of violence. Hudson’s explicit calls for the hanging of elected officials under the guise of combating “treason” represent not just a vile departure from democratic norms but also a chilling echo of the darkest chapters of political intimidation and violence.

The Incendiary Statements

Hudson’s rhetoric, marked by calls to “get the rope” in a social media video, directly targets Michigan’s U.S. Senators Elissa Slotkin and Gary Peters, accusing them of receiving kickbacks from former Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro. This baseless accusation, coupled with a visual display of a loop of rope, is not just metaphorically violent; it is a literal call to arms against perceived political adversaries.

Moreover, Hudson’s comments at a Mason City Council meeting, where he threatens local government officials with hanging for what he perceives as acts of “treason,” underscore a profound misunderstanding of the term and its legal application. His statement, “The punishment for treason is public hanging. So, I suggest you start listening,” is as ignorant of the law as it is barbaric in its implications.

The Response and the Lack Thereof

The condemnation from the state Democratic party is a necessary denouncement of Hudson’s dangerous rhetoric. Their call for the Michigan Republican Party and fellow Republican candidates to similarly condemn these threats highlights a pivotal moment for the party to take a clear stand against political violence and extremism. The silence from the Michigan Republican Party, however, is deafening and disturbing. It serves as a tacit endorsement of Hudson’s rhetoric, or at the very least, a concerning reluctance to confront the extremist elements within their ranks.

Hudson’s defense of his comments, insisting he is merely “educating someone about the law and what the consequences are,” is a thinly veiled attempt to legitimize his threats. His refusal to acknowledge the violent implications of his statements, coupled with a failure to retract or apologize, only compounds the irresponsibility and danger of his actions.

The Echoes of Trumpian Rhetoric

Hudson’s comments do not exist in a vacuum. They are reflective of a broader, more dangerous trend in American politics, epitomized by former President Donald Trump’s own incendiary language. Trump’s accusations of treason against six elected Democrats, including Slotkin, for reminding military members of their right to disobey illegal orders, and his characterization of such reminders as “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR,” pave the way for figures like Hudson to escalate their rhetoric with seeming impunity.

The Implications

The implications of Hudson’s rhetoric are far-reaching. Beyond the immediate threat it poses to the individuals he targets, it represents a broader assault on democratic principles and the rule of law. When political disagreements are framed not as differences in policy or vision but as acts of treason worthy of death, the very foundation of democratic discourse is undermined.

Moreover, Hudson’s statements, and the lack of a robust response from his party, signal a worrying emboldenment of extremist rhetoric within mainstream political conversations. It is a development that not only endangers the lives of public officials but also threatens the fabric of American democracy itself.

Conclusion

Anthony Hudson’s threats against elected officials are reprehensible and indefensible. They are an affront to democratic norms and a clear and present danger to the individuals he targets and to the political community at large. The silence from the Michigan Republican Party is unacceptable. It is incumbent upon all political leaders, regardless of party, to condemn such rhetoric unequivocally and to reaffirm their commitment to the principles of democracy and the rule of law. Anything less is a tacit endorsement of political violence and a betrayal of the democratic ideals upon which this country was founded.
