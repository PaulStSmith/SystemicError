New emails suggest Trump made 'insulting' bid to fund Epstein-linked modeling 'project'

A new batch of more than 20,000 emails reveals that President Donald Trump may have offered to help fund a proposed modeling company spearheaded by Jeffrey Epstein in 2006, a proposal that was described in an email as “ridiculous and insulting.”

The new batch of emails was obtained by Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets), a nonprofit whistleblower group that shared them selectively with news outlets, including Raw Story. They differ from the document dump of 20,000 pages of files from Epstein’s estate, released by the House Oversight Committee last month.

In early 2006, an individual by the name of Cecilia Steen – who Bloomberg has described as an “assistant” of Epstein’s – sent Epstein an email regarding a new “project,” the creation of a new company, with proposed names being “e models,” “e=mc²,” and “e management.”

According to the email, the proposed company would “take over the assets” of MC2 Model Management, an international modeling agency that operated out of New York, Florida and Tel Aviv, Israel, and was founded by Jean-Luc Brunel, a French model scout who launched MC2 with considerable financial backing from Epstein.

In 2020, Brunel was accused of being involved in sex trafficking in association with Epstein, and died by apparent suicide while awaiting sex-trafficking charges in 2022, and in a manner similar to how Epstein died while also awaiting sex-trafficking charges in 2019.

While the email was sent to Epstein by someone named “Steen,” it was signed with the name “Alain Kittler,” the co-founder of Elite Model Management, a French modeling agency. Kittler co-founded Elite Model Management alongside John Casablancas, a French modeling agent who was friends with Trump and whose modeling agency represented Trump’s daughter, Ivanka, when she was 15 years old.

On the creation of the new company, the email sent by Steen – but signed by Kittler – describes an apparent funding offer from a “Mr Trump.”

“Last week, we also received a last written proposal from Mr Trump; as expected it was ridiculous and insulting,” the email reads. “The problem for us remains in the payment of our loans and commissions for 2005. We will seek some advice on how to handle it.”

Epstein, responding to the email on Jan. 17, 2006, wrote that the details of the potential startup sounded “like a good begining,” but asked for “more details.”

“can you te%ll me how much is currently owed by trump to you, and what is his position.. will he try to attach any ownership in e=mc2 , maybe you should only have an irrevocable option on the shares to avoid his litigation,” Epstein wrote

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of a Disturbing Alliance

In a revelation that should disturb, but sadly not surprise, the latest cache of over 20,000 emails has unearthed potential ties between Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein that extend beyond mere acquaintanceship into the murky waters of business dealings. This time, the association hints at an attempt by Trump to finance a modeling company that Epstein had in his sights back in 2006.

The nonprofit whistleblower group, Distributed Denial of Secrets (DDoSecrets), has brought to light this correspondence, which not only deepens the intrigue surrounding Trump’s connections but also casts a long, dark shadow over his judgment and moral compass. Let’s dissect the layers of this damning revelation.

The Proposal: A Modeling Company with Epstein’s Touch

An email from Cecilia Steen, described as an assistant of Epstein’s, brings to the forefront a proposal for a new venture—an ominously named modeling company, with suggestions like “e models,” “e=mc²,” and “e management.” The plan was to absorb the assets of MC2 Model Management, a company already tainted by its financial and operational ties to Epstein and accusations against its founder, Jean-Luc Brunel, of sex trafficking.

The involvement of Alain Kittler, co-founder of Elite Model Management, in this correspondence, signing an email that discusses a funding proposal from “Mr. Trump,” is not just a footnote. It’s a glaring indicator of the interconnected web of influential figures in the modeling industry, some of whom have had questionable relationships and dealings.

Trump’s Role: The Offer That Was Both “Ridiculous and Insulting”

The email exchange candidly mentions a funding proposal from Trump, labeled as “ridiculous and insulting.” Yet, what stands out is not just the dismissal of Trump’s offer but the underlying implication that Trump was willing to engage financially with a venture linked to Epstein—a man whose criminal activities and predatory behavior were already circulating within certain circles by 2006.

Epstein’s response, seeking more details on Trump’s financial involvement and suggesting a strategic avoidance of Trump’s litigation tendencies, speaks volumes. It underscores a familiarity with Trump’s operational methods and a cautious approach toward entangling the proposed venture with Trump’s financial claims.

The Broader Implications

This email exchange is not just a peek into a failed business venture. It’s a window into the sordid underbelly of power, money, and exploitation. The connection between Trump and Epstein, in this context, illustrates how the wealthy and powerful can attempt to extend their influence into industries ripe for exploitation. The modeling industry, already criticized for its vulnerabilities and exploitation of young women, becomes even more sinister with the involvement of figures like Epstein and, potentially, Trump.

Furthermore, the mention of Trump’s daughter, Ivanka, being represented by an agency led by a close Trump associate, John Casablancas, adds another layer of discomfort, given the industry’s notorious reputation and the close circles in which these men operated.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Systemic Failure

The revelations from these emails serve as a stark reminder of the systemic failures that allow such figures to exploit their power unchecked. It’s not just about the moral failings of individuals like Trump or Epstein but about a system that enables, and sometimes protects, their actions. The association between Trump and a proposed venture with Epstein is a damning testament to the depths of their willingness to engage in potentially exploitative enterprises.

As the public digests these revelations, the focus should not only be on the individuals involved but on the broader mechanisms of power and exploitation that allowed these connections to flourish. It’s a call to action for accountability, transparency, and a reevaluation of the systems that enable the rich and powerful to operate with impunity.
