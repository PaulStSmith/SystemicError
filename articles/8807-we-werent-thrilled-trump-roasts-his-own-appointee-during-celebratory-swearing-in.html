'We weren’t thrilled': Trump roasts his own appointee during celebratory swearing-in

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 12, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hands Sergio Gor, during the swearing-in ceremony for Gor as U.S. Ambassador to India, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 10, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump took a shot at Sergio Gor during the swearing-in ceremony for Gor as U.S. Ambassador to India on Monday.

The ceremony was held at the White House and featured what The Daily Beast flagged as an “awkward” moment — when the president publicly threw Gor under the bus.

“We have had a great team, a couple of mistakes there Sergio, but we won’t mention that,” Trump said to laughs. “Thank god, we got rid of them pretty quick. We found a couple we weren’t thrilled with, we got them out of here.”

The Beast noted Trump, 79, appeared to be referencing Gor’s performance overseeing the appointments of thousands of executive branch staff while serving as director of the White House Presidential Personnel Office. The Beast noted he saw a record number of failed nominations.

Even so, Trump said he expects Gor will do “an outstanding job” in his new gig — but couldn’t resist giving Gor another jab.

“I’ve known him for a long time and he’s a great guy and most people love him. Some don’t like him so much. I’ll be honest with you, Sergio,” Trump said. “Some people, when they don’t like Sergio, they don’t like him, but when they like him, they like him more than anybody.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Undermining: Trump’s “Praise” for Sergio Gor

In a scene rife with the kind of irony that only real life can afford, President Donald Trump’s recent remarks at Sergio Gor’s swearing-in ceremony as U.S. Ambassador to India encapsulated the peculiar blend of backhanded compliments and outright criticism that has become a hallmark of his public speaking. Trump’s comments, as reported, not only highlight an awkward moment but serve as a masterclass in the art of undermining.

The Awkward “Compliment”

At face value, Trump’s remarks might seem benign, a mere ribbing among colleagues. However, a closer examination reveals a more insidious pattern of behavior. “We have had a great team, a couple of mistakes there Sergio, but we won’t mention that,” Trump said, igniting laughter at Gor’s expense. This statement, ostensibly a joke, functions as a public reprimand, a peculiar choice of sentiment at what should be a celebratory occasion for Gor.

The Veiled Reference

The Daily Beast’s interpretation of Trump’s comments as a nod to Gor’s tenure overseeing executive branch appointments is particularly telling. With Gor at the helm, the administration saw a “record number of failed nominations.” This fact, though lightly touched upon by Trump, is a damning indictment of Gor’s effectiveness in a crucial role. Yet, in the same breath, Trump predicts Gor will do an “outstanding job” in his new position. This juxtaposition of criticism with confidence is a classic Trump move, leaving the audience to wonder where the truth lies.

A Backhanded Endorsement

Perhaps the most perplexing element of Trump’s speech is his concluding remarks about Gor’s likability. “Some people, when they don’t like Sergio, they don’t like him, but when they like him, they like him more than anybody,” Trump stated. This observation, while perhaps true of any individual, coming from Trump, feels less like praise and more like a backhanded endorsement. It’s as if Trump is saying, “He’s not for everyone, but hey, he’s got some fans.”

The Underlying Message

What emerges from Trump’s comments is a layered message, one that ostensibly supports Gor while simultaneously highlighting his past failures and divisive nature. It’s a classic Trump strategy: keep the subject off balance, ensuring they remain grateful for the support, however qualified it may be. This approach not only undermines Gor’s achievements but also serves to remind him and everyone else of Trump’s power to make or break careers with a mere word.

In the end, the ceremony, meant to mark the beginning of a prestigious chapter in Gor’s career, was overshadowed by Trump’s peculiar brand of “support.” As observers of this political theater, we must ask ourselves: is this the standard we’ve come to accept from our leaders? The incident is less about Gor’s capabilities and more about Trump’s continued use of public platforms to assert dominance, a tactic that is as transparent as it is tiresome.

The real story here isn’t Gor’s appointment or even Trump’s backhanded compliments; it’s about the continued degradation of political discourse, where achievements are undercut by pettiness, and loyalty is a currency traded in public humiliations. It’s high time we demand more from our public officials, starting with a basic level of respect for one another.
