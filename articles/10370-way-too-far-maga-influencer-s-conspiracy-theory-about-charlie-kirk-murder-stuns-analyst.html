Owens floated a theory about Kirk's murder on a new episode of her podcast on Tuesday, claiming that Kirk may have been killed by Israel because he spoke out about some of the country's actions. Owens also implied that hedge fund owner Bill Ackman staged an "intervention" designed to save Kirk's life.

"Threats were made," Owens said of the alleged intervention. "That is what I am told, and I will tell you that I am very happy for Bill Ackman to dispute this narrative."

Emma Vigeland, co-host of the liberal podcast "The Majority Report," responded to Owens' theory on a new episode Tuesday night.

"I find this reporting fascinating, I really do!" Vigeland said. "But what she's implying is way too far. She's using this language that is really heavily influenced by her emotions about the situation, which I imagine is quite rational. But she's talking about this in terms that are histrionic."

Vigeland mentioned reporting from The Jerusalem Post and other outlets that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has met with multiple right-wing podcasters because he is concerned about Israel's image in America.

"I don't doubt that meeting was impactful, but I am a little surprised by the conclusion that she is drawing here," Vigeland said.

Our Analysis:

The Perilous Conjectures of Candace Owens

In the latest episode of her podcast, Candace Owens introduces a theory so absurd, it's almost a caricature of conspiracy theorizing itself. The subject? The tragic murder of conservative activist Charlie Kirk at a Utah university campus. Owens' explanation? An alleged assassination by Israel, due to Kirk's criticisms of the country, paired with claims of a dramatic "intervention" by hedge fund owner Bill Ackman.

A Dive into Baseless Speculation

Owens, a MAGA influencer, has never been one to shy away from controversy or conspiracy. Yet, her latest claim not only leaps over the bounds of reasonable speculation but plunges into a realm of baseless accusation. The assertion that Kirk's murder could be an international assassination orchestrated by Israel is not only unfounded but dangerously irresponsible. It feeds into the worst kinds of conspiracy theories that have historically fueled antisemitism and geopolitical animosity.

The Ackman "Intervention"

Adding another layer to her speculative narrative, Owens implies that Bill Ackman, a well-known hedge fund owner, somehow staged an "intervention" to save Kirk's life. "Threats were made," Owens cryptically notes, inviting Ackman to dispute her narrative. This nebulous claim adds nothing but confusion and unfounded drama to an already convoluted theory. It's a typical Owens move: throw in high-profile names, imply shadowy doings, and sit back as the internet erupts.

Rational Reactions in an Irrational Discourse

Emma Vigeland, co-host of the liberal podcast "The Majority Report," offers a much-needed counterpoint to Owens' wild theorizing. Vigeland rightly criticizes Owens for her "histrionic" language and emotionally charged implications. Yet, even in her critique, Vigeland acknowledges the strategic meetings between Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and right-wing podcasters, including the potential influence on Israel's image in America. This concession to Owens' underlying concerns about political narratives and image management does little to lend credibility to Owens' overarching conspiracy theory but does highlight the complexities of international relations and media influence.

The Bottom Line

Candace Owens' theory about Charlie Kirk's tragic murder is not just a leap; it's a pole vault over the chasm of credibility. It's a concoction that serves only to distract from the real issues at hand, muddying the waters of political discourse and fueling the fires of conspiracy. In an era where facts are more important than ever, Owens' approach is not just flawed; it's dangerous.

Owens' narrative is a stark reminder of the power of platform and the responsibility that comes with it. Influencers like Owens wield significant influence over public opinion and discourse. When they choose to promote unfounded theories and baseless accusations, the consequences can be far-reaching, affecting not just public perception but potentially international relations.

In the end, the tragic murder of Charlie Kirk deserves a serious, fact-based investigation, not baseless speculation. It's high time for public figures like Owens to remember the weight of their words and the impact of their platforms. Let's return to a discourse grounded in reality, not rife with conspiracy.
