'So lame': MAGA turns on Republican candidate for New York mayor after debate flop

Supporters of Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. president Donald Trump raise MAGA hats, on the day Trump returns for a rally at the site of the July assassination attempt against him, in Butler, Pennsylvania, U.S., October 5, 2024. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Several pro-Trump and MAGA voices expressed frustration at Curtis Silwa, the Republican candidate for New York mayor, on Thursday after Silwa’s debate performance.

Silwa is the only Republican in the New York mayoral race. Front-runner Zohran Mamdani, a socialist, and independent candidate Andrew Cuomo are also vying for the seat.

President Donald Trump has tried to put his thumb on the scale of the race, threatening to revoke federal funding from New York City if they elect Mamdani. His administration has also reportedly struck a deal to support Cuomo’s campaign, although Cuomo has denied such a deal exists.

Several right-wing figures expressed their frustration with Silwa on social media.

MAGA influencer Laura Loomer chided Silwa for not appropriately answering the question “Who is the best president in history?”

“None of the candidates said President Trump. Not even the Republican @CurtisSliwa,” Loomer posted on X. “And this is why @CurtisSliwa should drop out. He just doesn’t get it. So lame.”

“After watching this 2 hour long debate, I only have one major takeaway… Curtis Silwa very much so endorses Zohran Mamdani for Mayor of NYC,” comedian Zach Sage Fox, who leads Cuomo’s social media team, posted on X.

“He comes across as a good man who cares and knows a lot about the City,” hedge fund manager Bill Ackman posted on X. “But if he really cared as much about NYC as he seems to, he needs to drop out of the race tomorrow. A vote for Sliwa is a vote for Mamdani. Sliwa will hand NYC to Mamdani if he stays in. And I am concerned he will do so as he comes across as having a big ego, and he is out of touch with his place in the polls.”

“Hey @CurtisSliwa drop out NOW if you actually care about NY!!!” Ellie Cohanim, former deputy envoy to combat antisemitism in the first Trump administration, posted on X.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Dilemma: Curtis Silwa’s Debacle and the New York Mayoral Race

In a turn of events that can only be described as a political circus, the New York mayoral race has become the latest battleground for the soul of the Republican Party, with Curtis Silwa’s recent debate performance sending shockwaves through the pro-Trump and MAGA camp. Their frustration is palpable, but the underlying issues reveal a deeper crisis within the party, one that speaks volumes about the current state of American politics.

Silwa’s Sin: Failing the Trump Litmus Test

At the heart of the controversy is Silwa’s apparent failure to unequivocally declare Donald Trump as the best president in history during the debate. This omission has drawn the ire of prominent MAGA figures, including Laura Loomer, who lamented on X (formerly Twitter), “None of the candidates said President Trump. Not even the Republican @CurtisSliwa.” Her call for Silwa to drop out encapsulates the MAGA movement’s litmus test, which often places loyalty to Trump above all else, including, it seems, practical political strategy and the broader interests of New York City.

A Fragmented Right Wing: Trump’s Shadow

The reactions from right-wing figures, ranging from comedians to hedge fund managers, underscore a fragmented landscape that Trump continues to dominate, even out of office. The demand for ideological purity—essentially, the requirement to bow at the altar of Trumpism—has left little room for nuanced debate or divergent strategies within the party. Bill Ackman’s remark that “A vote for Sliwa is a vote for Mamdani” highlights the perceived zero-sum game of New York politics, where any deviation from the MAGA playbook is seen as a direct aid to the opposition.

The Trump Administration’s Controversial Involvement

Adding another layer of intrigue is the Trump administration’s alleged attempt to manipulate the race by threatening to revoke federal funding if Zohran Mamdani, the socialist frontrunner, is elected. This, coupled with reports of a deal to support independent candidate Andrew Cuomo (which Cuomo denies), paints a picture of a party willing to engage in Machiavellian tactics rather than rally behind their sole Republican candidate. The desperation to control the outcome of the New York mayoral race reveals a fear of socialist policies gaining traction, as well as an acknowledgment of the diminishing power of the Republican brand in urban centers.

The Bottom Line: A Loss for New York

In their zeal to enforce ideological purity, MAGA influencers and Trump loyalists risk alienating moderate Republicans and independents, essential constituents in a city as diverse and politically complex as New York. Their scathing criticism of Silwa not only undermines the Republican campaign but also diverts attention from substantive policy debates crucial for the city’s future.

Ellie Cohanim’s plea for Silwa to drop out “if you actually care about NY” inadvertently touches on the crux of the matter: Does the current Republican strategy truly serve the best interests of New York, or is it merely a power play, a show of loyalty to Trump at the expense of governance and practical politics?

As the mayoral race heats up, one thing is clear: the Republican Party’s internal struggle—between allegiance to Trump and the pragmatic needs of a diverse electorate—continues to cast a long shadow, not just over New York, but over the future of American politics. In this circus, it’s the citizens, the would-be audience, who stand to lose the most.
