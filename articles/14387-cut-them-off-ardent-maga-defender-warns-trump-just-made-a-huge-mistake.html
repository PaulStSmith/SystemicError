'Cut them off': Ardent MAGA defender warns Trump just made a huge 'mistake'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A staunch defender of President Donald Trump delivered a rare rebuke of the MAGA leader over Trump's crypto dinner that rewarded the top 220 investors of his meme coin.

Trump held an hour-long dinner Thursday night at his golf club in Virginia with investors who collectively spent about $394 million on the coin. The top 25 were also promised a private cocktail reception and a tour of the White House.

Critics have warned the dinner enriched the president's pockets while selling access to the White House. And they found themselves an unlikely ally late Thursday in Pete Seat, a former White House spokesman who served as deputy assistant press secretary to President George W. Bush.

Seat told panelists on CNN's "NewsNight" that Trump's dinner was a bad idea.

"Politically, it had value. Right? He leaned into the crypto bros, he strung them along, got their votes. Now he's got to cut them off," said Seat.

He quickly clarified that his issue is less about Trump than it is about the country.

"Crypto is a scam," he said, to agreement from multiple panelists. "These investors desperately need the government to legitimize their so-called investment. Otherwise, they'll lose everything. And if they get what they want, which is displacing fiat currency, the U.S. dollar, we’re no longer the reserve currency. We no longer have the power to sanction. We lose everything."

Seat backed off his criticism slightly, adding that there are other ways to "buy access" to the president as well, such as by donating to his campaign.

The problem, he said, is what this could do to America.

Host Abby Phillip found his response notable given he's a supporter of Trump, and echoed Seat's remarks.

"That’s very interesting. It’s more of a national security question more than anything, which I think is colliding honestly with Trump’s pocketbook. He’s in that meeting not because they necessarily — that he wants to hear what they have to say. He’s in that meeting because they paid money to see him. Which is in direct conflict to what you're saying, which is what’s in the best interest of the country, from a fiscal perspective."

Seat agreed with the host's criticism.

"And they still get access. I agree with what you said earlier. The most precious commodity is the president's time. And the calendar is policy. And by him going to this and spending the time, those precious minutes — it’s a mistake," he concluded.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Crypto Dinner: A Rare Rebuke from Within

In a political narrative that often reads like a circus, the recent episode of President Donald Trump's crypto dinner for top investors of his meme coin marks a significant, albeit bizarre, milestone. Trump, a figure synonymous with controversy and the MAGA movement, hosted an hour-long dinner at his Virginia golf club for investors who collectively poured approximately $394 million into the meme coin. The event, which promised the top 25 investors a private cocktail reception and a tour of the White House, has sparked outrage and criticism, not just from the usual quarters, but from within his own camp.

A Staunch Defender's Critique

Pete Seat, a former White House spokesman for President George W. Bush and a noted Trump defender, delivered a scathing critique of the dinner on CNN’s "NewsNight". His condemnation is not just rare; it’s a glaring spotlight on the ethical and political tightrope Trump is walking. Seat's argument that "Crypto is a scam" and his fear of cryptocurrency displacing fiat currency, thereby endangering the U.S. dollar's status as the world reserve currency, strikes at the heart of national security. It's a bold claim, suggesting that the dinner wasn't just a questionable fundraising effort but a potential threat to America's financial dominance.

The Intersection of Politics and Pocketbook

What's particularly notable is Seat’s insight into the transactional nature of the dinner, emphasizing the direct conflict between Trump’s financial gains and the best interest of the country. The notion that the president's time, a "precious commodity", was sold to the highest bidder for an evening is a stark illustration of how the lines between personal profit and political power are increasingly blurred in Trump's America.

Critics Find an Unlikely Ally

Critics of Trump's administration and his unorthodox methods of mingling business with politics found an unlikely ally in Pete Seat. His critique aligns with longstanding concerns about Trump's conflicts of interest, yet coming from a supporter, it carries a weight that cannot be easily dismissed.

A National Security Question

The discussion surrounding the dinner transcends mere political critique and ventures into the realm of national security, as Seat points out. The potential for cryptocurrencies to undermine the U.S. dollar is a debate that has been simmering for years. However, when such debates are ignited not by theoretical discussions but by the actions of a former president, the stakes are significantly higher. Seat’s remarks, echoed by host Abby Phillip, underscore the gravity of the situation, framing it as a national security issue rather than mere political fodder.

The Verdict

Trump's crypto dinner is emblematic of a broader issue plaguing American politics: the commodification of political power. While Pete Seat's critique may not sway the staunchest MAGA supporters, it represents a crack in the facade, a moment where political allegiance is momentarily set aside for a glimpse at the bigger picture. The event, and the criticism it has garnered, serves as a cautionary tale of what happens when the lines between political leadership and personal enrichment are not just crossed but obliterated.

In a political era where loyalty often trumps reason, Seat’s rebuke is a reminder that principles should have a place at the table, even if that table is set for a $394 million dinner.
