'False things over and over': CNN smacks down 10 Trump lies from just this week

During Donald Trump's first presidency, CNN's Daniel Dale aggressively fact-checked him and found numerous inaccuracies or flat-out lies. Dale fact-checked former President Joe Biden as well, describing, at times, some of his statements as misleading. But Trump gave Dale a lot more material to work with.

In a fact-check published by CNN early Friday morning, Aug. 29, Dale debunks 10 claims Trump made in the course of only one week.

"President Donald Trump keeps saying the same false things over and over," Dale explains. "Trump's lying has always been notable for its audacity — his willingness to make obviously untrue claims that can be very quickly debunked. But it has also been distinguished by its repetitiveness — his unwillingness to stop deploying an exaggerated statistic, baseless accusation or fictional tale after months or even years of debunkings…. The president's recent public remarks have featured many old lies."

Dale adds, "In the interest of not letting them go uncorrected, here is a look at 10 debunked claims he repeated over the past week alone."

Those debunked claims, according to Dale, include: (1) "imaginary sub-$2 gasoline," (2) "an impossible '1500 percent' reduction in prescription drug prices," (3) "no inflation amid continued inflation," (4) "The (non-)uniqueness of U.S. mail-in voting," and (5) "The water Trump says he sent to Los Angeles (he didn't) by turning a 'valve' (that doesn't exist)."

The other false claims Dale cites are: (6) "the 2020 election, again," (7) "that nonexistent monument law," (8) "a phony Ukraine aid total," (9) "a fictional story about Biden and South Korea," and (10) "a disproven tale Trump claimed had been proven."

The "disproven tale" in #10 is Trump's false description of a late 2024 post-election conversation with Democratic Maryland Gov. Wes Moore.

"Trump told a story on Monday, (August 25) in which he claimed a Democratic governor with whom he has been trading public barbs this month, Gov. Wes Moore of Maryland, told him during a behind-the-scenes conversation last year, 'Sir, you're the greatest president of my lifetime,'" Dale observes. "The story was disproven by Trump-friendly Fox News the very same day: it turned out that a Fox documentary show had recorded the conversation, in which Moore didn't say anything close to what Trump claimed."

Dale continues, "Trump was undeterred by the footage. He told a similar story on Tuesday, this time falsely saying Moore had told him, 'Sir, you're the greatest president' — and adding, 'They caught him on camera.' In reality, it was Trump who the camera had caught in a lie."

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Lie: Trump's Fact-Checked Fantasies

In a week where the truth seems more malleable than Play-Doh in the summer sun, Daniel Dale of CNN has once again donned his fact-checking cape, diving headfirst into the murky waters of Donald Trump's claims. With the precision of a surgeon and the tenacity of a bulldog, Dale dissects a plethora of Trump's statements, revealing a concoction so potent with falsehoods it could almost be admirable—if it weren't so dangerously misleading.

The Audacity of Untruth

Trump's relationship with the truth has always been, to put it mildly, estranged. However, Dale's recent fact-checking expedition unveils a level of audacity in Trump's falsehoods that is nothing short of breathtaking. From imaginary sub-$2 gasoline to an impossible '1500 percent' reduction in prescription drug prices, Trump's fabrications are not just garden-variety political puffery; they are bold-faced lies, so easily debunked they beggar belief.

The Repetition Ruse

One of Trump's most notable tactics—his "repetitiveness"—is on full display here. Despite repeated debunkings, he clings to his exaggerated statistics, baseless accusations, and fictional tales with the desperation of a sinking man to a lifeline. This tactic, as Dale rightly points out, is not just about spreading misinformation; it's about cementing lies in the public consciousness through sheer force of repetition.

The Top Ten Trumpian Tall Tales

Dale's breakdown of Trump's top ten fabrications from just one week is a tour de force of fact-checking. The list reads like a greatest hits album of political deception, featuring classics like no inflation amid continued inflation and new tracks like the water Trump says he sent to Los Angeles (he didn't). Each claim is deftly debunked by Dale, yet the sheer volume of falsehoods is overwhelming—a deliberate strategy, one might surmise, to exhaust the truth until it can no longer stand.

The Disproven Tale: A Case Study in Chutzpah

Perhaps the most telling of all Trump's falsehoods is the disproven tale regarding his post-election conversation with Democratic Maryland Gov. Wes Moore. Trump's claim, so bold as to border on the surreal, was swiftly debunked by none other than Fox News—a network typically friendly to Trump. Yet, undeterred, Trump simply altered his story and doubled down. This incident serves as a microcosm of Trump's broader strategy: when caught in a lie, lie again, louder.

The Role of Fact-Checking in the Post-Truth Era

Dale's work, while commendable, underscores a bleak reality: in the era of post-truth politics, fact-checking is both more crucial and more Sisyphean than ever. As Trump's falsehoods proliferate, fact-checkers like Dale are the dike holding back a flood of misinformation. But as each lie is debunked, two more spring up in its place, raising the question: in a world where lies can spread with the click of a button, can the truth ever truly catch up?

In conclusion, Dale's fact-checking is a vital public service, shining a light on the dark corners of political discourse. Yet, as long as figures like Trump continue to spew falsehoods with impunity, the battle for the truth will rage on. It's a battle we cannot afford to lose, for in the war against misinformation, reality itself is at stake.
