'Arrogant' Trump sinks US popularity in new global survey

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks at the annual National Memorial Day Observance in the Memorial Amphitheater at Arlington National Cemetery in Arlington, Virginia, U.S., May 26, 2025. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

President Donald Trump's approval ratings have been underwater since February — but it's not just within America that his image is struggling. Around the globe, his behavior has caused a rapid deterioration of the entire country's global image, Politico reported on Wednesday.

"Pew Research Center surveyed people in 24 countries, including 10 in Europe, from late February through early April. It found that the share of respondents who view the U.S. favorably has dropped in most of them compared to the spring of 2024," said the report. Additionally, "Respondents have little confidence in Trump as a world leader or in his ability to address global challenges, from wars in Ukraine and Gaza to climate change and U.S. immigration policies."

Just about the only good news for Trump in the poll is that a majority of respondents worldwide also see him as a "strong leader" — they just don't agree with his policies on virtually anything, and also view him as "dangerous" and "arrogant."

Trump beat out China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin for confidence, at 34 percent vs 25 percent and 16 percent, respectively, for the authoritarians. But Emmanuel Macron, the French president and the leader of a fellow democracy, vastly outperformed him with 46 percent.

The numbers echo what happened when Trump took office in 2017, when the United States also saw a global erosion of trust and confidence.

For his part, the president has raged about polls that show him underwater, publicly attacking the surveys recently done by The New York Times and The Washington Post.

"The Failing New York Times and the Washington ComPost, two 'papers' that have ruthlessly fought me for years, have each done Polls where a vast majority of those sampled are Democrats," he wrote last week on his Truth Social platform. "In other words, the Polls, just like their writings, are RIGGED - It is not possible for a Republican to do well in such a Poll! They are 'corrupt as hell.'"

Our Analysis:

The Global Peril of Trump's Presidency: A Scathing Analysis

In an era where the image of the United States on the world stage is of paramount concern, recent findings from Politico shed a stark and unflattering light on President Donald Trump's contribution to America's global standing. The report, grounded in research by the Pew Research Center, reveals a precipitous decline in global favorability towards the United States, a trend that correlates worryingly with Trump's tenure in the oval office. Here's a breakdown of the salient points, and why this should alarm anyone with a vested interest in America's place in the world.

The Stark Numbers: A Dive into Global Disfavor

The Pew Research Center's survey, encompassing 24 countries, including 10 in Europe, paints a grim picture of the U.S.'s global image under Trump's stewardship. The survey's timeline, from late February through early April, suggests a continuation, if not an exacerbation, of a troubling trend—a majority of respondents view the U.S. less favorably now compared to the spring of 2024. This is not just a dip; it's a nosedive.

The reasons behind this plummeting perception are manifold but centralize on Trump's policies and demeanor on the global stage. The survey highlights a global consensus viewing Trump as a "strong leader"—a backhanded compliment when considering the subsequent descriptors of "dangerous" and "arrogant." This duality in perception underscores a critical issue: strength, devoid of wisdom and tact, is a recipe for global apprehension.

Trump vs. The World: A Confidence Crisis

Trump's confidence ratings when compared to other global leaders tell a tale of isolation and mistrust. Scoring a meager 34 percent in confidence, Trump marginally outperforms authoritarian figures like Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin. However, this is hardly a victory lap-worthy statistic. When juxtaposed with Emmanuel Macron's 46 percent—a leader of a fellow democracy—it becomes glaringly apparent that Trump's brand of leadership is not what the world wants or respects.

A Historical Echo of Distrust

This isn't the first rodeo of distrust and disfavor for Trump. The echoes of 2017, when Trump first took office, are loud and clear. The initial erosion of trust and confidence that marked the onset of his presidency has, according to this report, only deepened. This continuity of global disdain is not just a blip but a sustained verdict on his governance.

Trump's War on Polls: A Desperate Diversion

In a characteristic move, Trump has lashed out against negative polling, directing his ire at The New York Times and The Washington Post, lambasting their surveys as "RIGGED" and corrupt. This tirade against unfavorable numbers is a transparent attempt to discredit the messenger rather than address the substantive criticisms reflected in these polls. It's a ploy as old as politics itself, yet in Trump's case, it reeks of desperation and denial.

Conclusion: A Leadership Crisis

The Politico report, supported by the Pew Research Center's findings, is a damning indictment of Trump's global leadership—or the lack thereof. The implications of such a widespread disfavor and distrust are profound, affecting not just international relations but the very fabric of America's global image and influence.

In essence, Trump's presidency, marked by arrogance and a dangerous unilateral approach to global challenges, has not just eroded America's standing in the world; it has fundamentally questioned the nation's ability to lead on the global stage. This is a systemic error of monumental proportions, one that requires immediate rectification lest the United States find itself isolated and impotent in the face of global challenges that demand collaborative solutions and genuine leadership.
