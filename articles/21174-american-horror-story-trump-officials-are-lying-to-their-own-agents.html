American horror story: Trump officials are lying to their own agents

Based on an article by Sabrina Haake

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

ICE Kills, Then Lies About It

ICE shot and killed two men who were on their way to work, both legally authorized to work in the United States, both not even the agents’ intended targets. One died in Texas on July 7, 2026. Another died in Maine on July 13, 2026. In both cases, the same machinery moved first to violence and then to excuse-making.

This Was a Decision, Not an Accident

The key fact is not that agents were “confused.” The key fact is that federal officers in unmarked vehicles chased the wrong men, with guns drawn, and lethal force followed. That is not a misunderstanding in any meaningful political sense. It is an operational choice made inside a system that rewards escalation, treats targets as disposable, and assumes the public will absorb the lie afterward.

The victims were not abstract collateral. Lorenzo Salgado Araujo had spent more than 30 years building a life and a construction business in Texas. Johan Sebastian Guerrero was also driving to work when he was killed in Maine. The state killed workers on their morning commute and then tried to narrate them into threats.

The Real Power Is in the Office Above the Trigger

The person pulling the trigger matters, but the larger authority sits higher up. DHS, ICE leadership, Trump officials, and Stephen Miller’s political project create the conditions for this violence. Congress helped by giving ICE another $70 billion on June 10, 2026, after an already grotesque $170 billion the year before. That is not border policy. That is a federally funded enforcement regime designed to normalize pursuit, fear, and death.

The article is right to name Miller’s role, but the deeper point is institutional: when a deportation machine gets this much money, its incentives stop being accuracy and start being throughput. The victims are what a quota-driven system calls acceptable error.

The Immunity Story Is a Lie

The administration’s legal theater is not a side issue. It is part of the violence. ICE and DHS have been telling agents they have “complete federal immunity,” and that state prosecutors cannot touch them. That claim is false, according to the legal history cited in the source: federal officers are not automatically shielded, and state prosecution remains available when officers act outside the scope of their duties or unreasonably.

That matters because propaganda inside law enforcement is not harmless chatter. If leadership tells armed agents they are untouchable, it functions as a green light. It lowers the cost of killing. It turns the law from a constraint into a story told after the body is on the ground.

Fear Is the Point

The source makes clear that the victims saw masked men in unmarked vans and thought they were being robbed. That reaction was not irrational. It was the only sane response to armed strangers in covert vehicles barreling at working people before dawn. The state then weaponized that fear by claiming the men “tried to use [their] vehicle as a weapon,” even while witnesses described something much closer to panic and self-protection.

This is the ugliness of modern enforcement politics: it depends on confusion it can later weaponize. First come the masks, the unmarked vans, the chase. Then comes the accusation that the target was “dangerous” for reacting like a human being under armed pursuit. The state creates the terror and then prosecutes the terrorized.

Abolish ICE, Don’t Sand It Down

This is not a bad training problem. It is not an oversight gap. It is not one rogue officer, two bad stops, or a communications failure. ICE is operating exactly as a coercive deportation force is designed to operate when handed blank checks, political cover, and ideological encouragement from above.

If an agency repeatedly shoots the wrong people, lies about immunity, hides behind secrecy, and is then rewarded with more money, the democratic answer is not reform theater. It is abolition. An institution built to make terror routine does not become humane because a press release says so.

The Pattern Is the Point

The larger pattern here is simple: when the state wants to produce cruelty at scale, it first strips the target of protection, then strips the agents of accountability, then strips the public of a truthful account. That is how authoritarian systems work while still wearing democratic language. The uniforms change. The bureaucracy expands. The dead are told to have been the problem.

These killings should not be read as tragic mishaps inside a functioning system. They are evidence that the system itself has been made more dangerous, more secretive, and more politically insulated. The horror is not that ICE lost control. The horror is that this is what control looks like when a state decides some lives are acceptable losses on the way to mass deportation.
