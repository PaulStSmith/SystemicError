'Not a good sign' for FBI as report on infighting 'shows up in Trump’s favorite newspaper'

When Christopher A. Wray, a traditional Republican conservative who got along with the Biden Administration, announced his resignation as FBI director in late 2024, Donald Trump was determined to appoint an ultra-MAGA replacement. And he found it in now-FBI Director Kash Patel, a far-right conspiracy theorist and Trump loyalist

But Patel is far from universally loved among FBI alumni, and a 115-page report — according to the Trump-friendly, Rupert Murdoch-owned New York Post — offers a biting critique of his months leading the agency. The report describes the FBI as a “rudderless ship” under Patel’s direction.

Patel, in the report, is described as “in over his head,” and Deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino is described as “something of a clown.”

According to the Post’s Miranda Devine, “Patel is described by multiple internal sources as inexperienced, with one source saying he ‘has neither the breadth of experience nor the bearing an FBI director needs to be successful.’ Another source, a self-professed Trump supporter, said Patel is ‘not very good,’ ‘may be insecure,’ and ‘lacks the requisite experience’ or the ‘measured self-confidence’ to be FBI director. However, an additional source described him as ‘very personable and likable’ while noting he ‘created a culture of mistrust and uncertainty among the ranks.’”

The report is drawing a lot of reactions on X, formerly Twitter.

Chuck Todd, former host of NBC News’ “Meet the Press” — which goes back to 1947 — tweeted, “Not a good sign for the FBI bros that this story showed up in Trump’s favorite newspaper.”

Mach Realty’s David Mach told Todd, “Or Trump fed the post that story so he can then fuher…I mean further justify the firing of both.”

KrassenCast’s Brian Krassenstein posted, “A massive 155-page report on the FBI, leaked to the NY Post finds that the FBI Under Kash Patel is a ‘rudderless ship’ and ‘ll f––d up.’ It also covers this embarrassing story about @Kash_Patel: The day after Kirk was assassinated, Patel flew into Provo, Utah, on the FBI jet but ‘would not disembark from the plane without an FBI raid jacket,’ according to ALPHA 99, a ‘highly respected’ source who has served in the FBI for multiple decades.”

X user Henry Hamblin wrote, “This is really intense, Miranda. The inclusion of the ‘medium’ raid vest that he demanded (and it was a woman agents!) before getting off the plane in Utah, I’m guessing h’’s been targeted for dismissal.”

Another X user, Mike B., commented, “Dan Bongino is ‘something of a clown.’ This statement is definitely true.”

Business owner Ashley Kenyon posted, “And somehow they don’t say same about Pam Bondi? I think Bongino isn’t the issue at Deputy director he doesn’t choose how to run the FBI. Patel is the issue. He talked big on all the podcasts for years but isn’t doing anything he said he would. I think also there is significant leftovers of known bad actor FBI agents who they can’t seem to fire.”

Our Analysis:

Patel at the Helm: A Dire Diagnosis for the FBI

The recent resignation of Christopher A. Wray and the subsequent appointment of Kash Patel as FBI Director have set the stage for what can only be described as a dramatic shift in the agency’s leadership and operational ethos. The narrative surrounding Patel’s tenure, as detailed in a 115-page report and further amplified by media coverage, paints a picture of an agency spiraling into chaos under the stewardship of a figure whose qualifications and leadership style are, to put it mildly, controversial.

The Alleged “Rudderless Ship”

The characterization of the FBI as a “rudderless ship” under Patel’s direction is a damning indictment of his ability to steer one of the nation’s most critical security institutions. Descriptions of Patel as “in over his head” and his deputy, Dan Bongino, as “something of a clown” are not just embarrassing epithets; they’re symptomatic of a broader issue of incompetence and mismanagement at the highest levels of the FBI. Such portrayals suggest a profound disconnect between the leadership’s capabilities and the agency’s mission-critical requirements.

The Critique from Within and Without

The critique detailed in the Rupert Murdoch-owned New York Post—a publication generally favorable to Trump’s circle—lends a certain gravitas to the allegations. This isn’t the liberal media’s usual suspects taking potshots at Trump’s appointees; this is criticism emanating from a corner of the media landscape that one would expect to be more sympathetic to Patel and his ilk.

The internal sources cited in the report describe Patel as inexperienced, lacking in both the breadth of experience and the measured self-confidence necessary for successful leadership of the FBI. This isn’t merely a matter of partisan politics; it’s a question of basic competency. When even self-professed Trump supporters express doubts about Patel’s efficacy in his role, it’s clear that the issue transcends ideological divides.

A Culture of Mistrust and Uncertainty

Perhaps the most troubling aspect of Patel’s tenure, as described in the report, is the “culture of mistrust and uncertainty” he’s purported to have fostered among the FBI’s ranks. An effective leader inspires confidence and loyalty; Patel, by contrast, seems to have engendered the opposite. His insistence on a medium raid vest for a disembarkation in Utah—highlighted as an instance of vanity or insecurity—is emblematic of a leadership style that prioritizes appearances over substance.

The Reaction on X

The discourse on X, formerly Twitter, further underscores the widespread concern and ridicule that Patel’s leadership has invited. From Chuck Todd’s observation that the story’s appearance in a Trump-friendly outlet is a “not a good sign” to individual users’ critiques and jests, it’s evident that the FBI’s current predicament has captured the public’s attention—and not in a good way.

Concluding Thoughts

The leadership of the FBI, under Kash Patel, appears to be an exercise in how not to manage a crucial national security institution. The reports of incompetence, vanity, and a failure to command respect or instill confidence paint a bleak picture of the agency’s current state. This isn’t just a problem for the FBI; it’s a challenge to the nation’s security and a stark illustration of the consequences of placing loyalty over competence in critical roles. The FBI, and by extension, the country, deserves better.
