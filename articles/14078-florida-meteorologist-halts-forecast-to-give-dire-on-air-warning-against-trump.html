Florida meteorologist halts forecast to give dire on-air warning against Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20255 min read

Miami channel 6 meteorologist John Morales‬ (Photo: screen capture from WTVJ video)

Miami meteorologist John Morales‬ issued a dire warning Monday about hurricane season as it begins this month.

He began with a clip of Hurricane Dorian slowly moving due west directly towards Florida in 2019. Morales promised it would turn north and dodge Florida landfall — and that's exactly what it did.

But he said that kind of forecasting would be impossible this year.

"As you've grown accustomed to my presentations over my 34 years in South Florida newscast, confidently, I went on TV and told you, 'It's going to turn. You don't need to worry,'" Morales said on the air.

Then he dropped the bombshell: "I'm here to tell you I'm not sure I can do that this year. Because of the cuts — the gutting, the sledgehammer attack on science, in general, and I could talk about that for a long, long time and how that's affecting the leadership and science over the years and how we're losing that leadership, and that is a multi-generation impact on science in this country."

ALSO READ: 'People will die because of this': Meteorologists warn of consequences of latest cuts

On the screen behind him appeared bullet points reading: "Central and South Florida National Weather Service is 19-39% understaffed." There has been a "17% reduction in weather balloon launches across the United States." He said it has "degraded forecast accuracy."

"But, specifically, let's talk about the federal government cuts to the National Weather Service and to NOAA," he continued, reading the bullet point. "This type of staffing shortage is having impacts across the country," he said, noting the weather balloon loss.

"What we're starting to see is that the quality of the forecasts is becoming degraded," he continued. "There's also a chance because of some of these cuts that NOAA hurricane hunter aircraft will not be able to fly this year and with less reconnaissance missions may be flying blind. And we may not exactly know how strong a hurricane is before it reaches the coastline, like what happened a couple of years ago with Hurricane Otis in Acapulco, Mexico."

He then made a plea to the audience to call their members of Congress to fight for this funding.

NOAA and NWS were cut as part of President Donald Trump's 2024 campaign promise to cut $2 trillion in what he called "waste, fraud and abuse" in government.

Our Analysis:

The Meteorological Meltdown: A Forecast of Disaster

In an era where science should be leading the charge against the escalating threats of climate change, the U.S. is witnessing a systematic dismantling of its most critical scientific infrastructure. Miami meteorologist John Morales, with a career spanning over three decades, recently highlighted a dire situation that paints a grim picture not just for Florida but for the entire country. His warning is not just a whisper in the wind; it's a howling gale of distress signals about the future of weather forecasting in the U.S.

The Demolition of Science

Morales, a respected figure in meteorology, has always been the beacon of hope and accuracy when hurricanes barrel towards the U.S. coastline. His confidence in forecasting, particularly his accurate prediction regarding Hurricane Dorian in 2019, has been a source of comfort for many. However, the meteorologist's recent admission on air that he might not be able to provide the same level of assurance this year due to "the gutting, the sledgehammer attack on science" is alarming. This isn't just about Morales losing confidence; it's about the entire nation being plunged into a state of uncertainty due to the reprehensible neglect of science and scientific infrastructure.

The Cold, Hard Facts

Behind Morales on screen were bullet points that should send chills down the spine of anyone who understands the importance of advanced and accurate weather forecasting: - The Central and South Florida National Weather Service is 19-39% understaffed. - There has been a 17% reduction in weather balloon launches across the United States, a critical component in forecast accuracy. - The potential for NOAA hurricane hunter aircraft being grounded this year, a vital tool in understanding hurricane strength before landfall.

These are not mere inconveniences; they are catastrophic failures in our ability to protect lives and property. The staffing shortages and reduction in essential data collection tools like weather balloons directly degrade the quality of forecasts. This degradation isn't an abstract concept—it's a tangible, immediate threat to safety.

The Political Storm

The root of this scientific sabotage? Look no further than the budgetary priorities of the current administration. The cuts to the National Weather Service (NWS) and NOAA are a direct consequence of a campaign promise by President Donald Trump to cut $2 trillion in what he labeled "waste, fraud, and abuse" in government. This decision is not just shortsighted; it's a willful ignorance of the critical role these services play in disaster preparedness and response. The claim of cutting "waste" becomes laughably ironic when the true waste is the potential loss of lives and livelihoods due to compromised forecasting abilities.

A Call to Action

Morales's plea to his audience to contact their members of Congress to fight for funding is more than a request; it's a clarion call for the public to take a stand for science. The consequences of these cuts are not abstract future scenarios; they are imminent, and they demand immediate action. The degradation of the U.S.'s weather forecasting capabilities is a self-inflicted wound, one that puts millions at risk.

Cuts have consequences, as Morales aptly illustrated. But beyond the cuts, it's the blatant disregard for the welfare of the public and the planet that underpins these decisions, which should be the rallying cry for anyone who values the role of science in society. The storm of political shortsightedness is upon us, and it's time to weather it with the fortitude of informed action and unwavering support for scientific integrity.
