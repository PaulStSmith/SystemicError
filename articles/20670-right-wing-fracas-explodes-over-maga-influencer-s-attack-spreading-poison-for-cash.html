Right-wing fracas explodes over MAGA influencer's attack: 'Spreading poison for cash'

Murder as Merchandise, Truth as a Liability

A Fight Over Ownership

The source is simple enough: Candace Owens attacked Ben Shapiro and Turning Point USA spokesperson Andrew Kolvet while the trial over the killing of Charlie Kirk was underway, and Shapiro, Kolvet, and Laura Loomer responded by accusing Owens of slander, narcissism, and helping the defense of the accused killer. That is the surface-level event. The political meaning is uglier: a dead man’s name has become contested property, and the fight is over who gets to cash in, control the narrative, and define loyalty.

Who Holds Power

The actual power in this story is not in Owens’ feed, Shapiro’s reply, or Loomer’s demand that YouTube intervene. The power sits with the ecosystem that rewards all of them: platform distribution, donor attention, influencer monetization, and movement institutions like Turning Point USA. These figures are not outside the machine. They are the machine’s mouthpieces, competing for status inside a politics that turns grievance into revenue and loyalty into a litmus test.

Conspiracy as Revenue Model

Owens’ “alternative theories” are not treated here as an honest search for facts; Shapiro explicitly calls them “conspiratorial poison for cash.” That phrase matters because it names the incentive structure more clearly than the usual fake concern over “drama.” The controversy is not a misunderstanding between personalities. It is a business model built on suspicion, selective outrage, and the promise that every tragedy can be repackaged into content.

The Real Misdirection

The easiest move in this kind of coverage is to frame the story as a clash of egos. That framing is too small. Yes, Owens is being attacked for slandering Kirk’s wife, friends, and organization, and Kolvet says she has “hijacked” the story. But the deeper problem is that the movement itself creates the conditions for this rot: it elevates conspiracists, then acts shocked when conspiracists monetize grief and weaponize martyrdom. The outrage is performative because the incentives are intact.

What the Infighting Reveals

This is what authoritarian politics looks like when it cannot fully control the story it has created. Instead of accountability, there is factional warfare. Instead of truth, there is brand maintenance. Instead of mourning, there is a scramble to police the approved version of events. Loomer’s call for YouTube to investigate Owens only deepens the logic: the movement that worships censorship myths is perfectly happy to demand platform discipline when one of its own becomes inconvenient.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is not chaos; it is degradation. A politics built on conspiracy, personal loyalty, and perpetual outrage eventually eats its own evidence. It turns every death into a branding contest and every accusation into a monetization strategy. That is not a glitch. It is what happens when a movement trains people to treat reality as optional and power as something you keep by controlling the story, not by telling the truth.
