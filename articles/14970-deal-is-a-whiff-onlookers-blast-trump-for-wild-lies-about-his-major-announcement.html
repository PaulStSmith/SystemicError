'Deal is a whiff': Onlookers blast Trump for 'wild lies' about his major announcement

Donald Trump on Sunday made an announcement on prescription drug price controls, resulting in mockery and backlash online.

Trump on Sunday took to his own social media site, Truth Social, and announced what he called "one of the most consequential orders in our country's history." It was a price control plan for prescriptions.

"For many years the World has wondered why Prescription Drugs and Pharmaceuticals in the United States States of America were SO MUCH HIGHER IN PRICE THAN THEY WERE IN ANY OTHER NATION, SOMETIMES BEING FIVE TO TEN TIMES MORE EXPENSIVE THAN THE SAME DRUG, MANUFACTURED IN THE EXACT SAME LABORATORY OR PLANT, BY THE SAME COMPANY??? It was always difficult to explain and very embarrassing because, in fact, there was no correct or rightful answer," Trump wrote. "The Pharmaceutical/Drug Companies would say, for years, that it was Research and Development Costs, and that all of these costs were, and would be, for no reason whatsoever, borne by the 'suckers' of America, ALONE."

The feedback from political observers was swift.

Former Republican lawmaker Barbara Comstock asked, "Can Trump sign an Executive Order lowering the exorbitant prices charged for membership and rooms at Mar-Lago, Doral and Bedminster that are so much more expensive than similar clubs and rooms and 'suckers' are getting ripped off paying these high prices to get access?"

Democratic insider Aaron Fritschner also chimed in, saying, "Some of the wild lies in this are wild even for Trump, which is really saying something."

"As always, the smart ones will wait for the paper which will confirm that this order does not do what he claims," Fritschner added.

Democratic lawmaker Ro Khanna said he supports Trump's "effort to ensure Americans do not pay more for drugs than those in other countries," but noted, "instead of an EO that will get challenged again by Big Pharma, why not work with [Bernie Sanders] and me to make this law!"

Fox reporter Charles Gasparino said on Sunday, "This makes me think the China deal is a whiff. Price controls seems like a big compensation."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Prescription Price Control: A Masterclass in Irony and Mockery

Donald Trump's recent announcement on prescription drug price controls has erupted into a spectacle of mockery and backlash, and rightly so. His post on Truth Social, touting what he called "one of the most consequential orders in our country's history," was nothing short of a melodramatic monologue that could rival the best of daytime soap operas. Trump's lament about the exorbitant prices of prescription drugs in the United States compared to other countries is both laughable and hypocritical, considering his administration had four years to address this issue but chose to prioritize other agendas.

"For many years the World has wondered why Prescription Drugs and Pharmaceuticals in the United States of America were SO MUCH HIGHER IN PRICE THAN THEY WERE IN ANY OTHER NATION," Trump wrote, employing his trademark caps lock to emphasize his belated epiphany. The irony here is thicker than the plot of a telenovela. The pharmaceutical companies' excuse of research and development costs being borne by the "suckers" of America alone was something Trump had four years in office to challenge, yet here we are.

The Backlash is Swift and Merciless

The feedback from political observers was as swift as it was scathing. Former Republican lawmaker Barbara Comstock's quip about Trump lowering the prices at his own luxury properties to save the "suckers" from getting ripped off is as pointed as it is humorous. It highlights the gross hypocrisy in Trump's sudden concern for the financial wellbeing of Americans, given his history of profiteering from his presidency.

Democratic insider Aaron Fritschner and Democratic lawmaker Ro Khanna's comments underscore a critical flaw in Trump's grand announcement: the lack of substance and feasibility behind the bombastic claims. Fritschner's anticipation of the paperwork not matching Trump's lofty promises is a reminder of the former president's penchant for overpromise and underdeliver. Khanna's suggestion to work with Bernie Sanders on making this a law, instead of relying on an executive order prone to legal challenges, points to a more constructive path that Trump is unlikely to take.

The Underlying Irony and What It Means

The real kicker here is Fox reporter Charles Gasparino's interpretation of Trump's announcement as a compensation for the lackluster China deal. This insight suggests that Trump's sudden pivot to prescription drug price controls might be nothing more than a smoke screen designed to distract from other failures. It's a classic Trump move: when faced with criticism or failure, throw something shiny (or controversial) in the opposite direction and hope everyone forgets about the mess you've made.

Conclusion: The Emperor Has No Clothes

Donald Trump's announcement on prescription drug price controls is a textbook example of too little, too late. It reeks of desperation and disingenuity, wrapped up in a performative concern for the American people. The swift backlash from both sides of the political aisle not only mocks Trump's attempt at policy-making but also highlights the broader issue of his credibility and the feasibility of his proposals. In the end, this announcement may be remembered more for the mockery it inspired than any significant impact on prescription drug prices. Once again, Trump has proven that when it comes to substantive policy changes, the emperor has no clothes.
