'Vulgar display of weakness': Newsom delivers scathing rebuke of Trump

California Gov. Gavin Newsom unleashed a blistering rebuke of Donald Trump on Thursday as he slammed the president’s deployment of the California National Guard as "an abomination," and called his planned military parade on Saturday a “vulgar display of weakness.”

The fiery comments from the Democratic governor, viewed as a possible 2028 White House contender, came moments after a federal judge on Thursday night temporarily blocked Trump from deploying the National Guard to Los Angeles, a move the judge ripped as “illegal.”

Newsom, speaking to reporters in San Francisco, where U.S. District Judge Charles Breyer held an emergency hearing over the legality of Trump’s troop deployment, warned that the president’s defiance of the order would sink the country into “a constitutional crisis, the likes of which we haven't seen in our lifetime.”

“He must work under the constraints as a constitutional officer of the Constitution of the United States of America,” Newsom insisted Thursday. “That's what this court order demands of the president of the United States. He is not a monarch. He is not a king. And he should stop acting like one.”

But Newsom also tore into Trump over other hot topics of the day. In response to a question about Trump’s multimillion-dollar military parade scheduled for Saturday, Newsom didn’t mince words.

“It’s a vulgar display of weakness,” Newsom said. “It’s the kind of thing you see, Kim Jong Un, you see Putin, you see it with dictators around the world that are weak and just want to demonstrate strength, weakness, masquerading as strength.”

“What an embarrassment,” Newsom added. “Honestly, it's that's about as small as it gets. How weak. How weak do you have to be to commandeer the military to fete you on your birthday in a vulgar display of weakness? That’s Donald Trump.”

Newsom continued his criticism of the Trump administration over the treatment of Sen. Alex Padilla (D-CA), who was forcibly removed from a news conference earlier in the day by federal agents, before being shoved to the ground by three officers and handcuffed.

“It was a disgrace,” Newsom said of the incident Thursday, adding that the California senator was “one of the most mild-mannered, decent people you'll meet.”

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Newsom vs. Trump

In a political landscape that often mirrors a Shakespearean tragedy (or farce, depending on the day), California Governor Gavin Newsom's latest verbal barrage against President Donald Trump reads like a script from an alternate reality where common sense and constitutional adherence are the plot twists. Newsom, in his blistering rebuke of Trump, not only showcased the widening chasm between Democratic ideals and Trumpian governance but also highlighted a disturbing trend of authoritarian whims masquerading as national pride.

Constitutional Crisis or Political Stunt?

First, let's dissect the elephant in the room: the deployment of the California National Guard. Described by Newsom as "an abomination," this move by Trump, subsequently blocked by U.S. District Judge Charles Breyer, was a glaring testament to the President's disregard for legal boundaries and constitutional mandates. Newsom's language, painting the scenario as one that could plunge the nation into "a constitutional crisis, the likes of which we haven't seen in our lifetime," is not an overstatement. It's a chilling reminder that the lines separating democratic governance from dictatorial overreach are being blurred, not with a pen, but with a sledgehammer.

Trump is not a monarch, nor a king. Yet, his actions, as Newsom aptly pointed out, echo those of a ruler who believes the Constitution is a mere suggestion rather than the law of the land. The federal judge's emergency block is a temporary band-aid on a bleeding wound. The real cure lies in adherence to the Constitution, something Trump seems to view as optional.

The Vulgarity of Military Parades

Moving onto the military parade - a multimillion-dollar spectacle that Newsom rightly criticized as a “vulgar display of weakness.” Here, Trump's penchant for grandiosity over subtlety comes to the fore, echoing the actions of dictators who use military might as a crutch for their fragile egos. Comparing Trump to the likes of Kim Jong Un and Putin, Newsom didn't pull any punches, categorizing the parade as an embarrassment to the nation. It's a sentiment hard to argue against; democratic leaders should inspire through action and integrity, not through wasteful displays of power.

The Treatment of Sen. Alex Padilla

The incident involving Sen. Alex Padilla, forcibly removed and manhandled by federal agents, is a case study in the Trump administration's approach to dissent. Newsom's defense of Padilla as one of the "most mild-mannered, decent people you'll meet" underscores a broader issue: the systematic suppression of opposition. In Trump's America, disagreeing with the President can literally land you on the ground, handcuffed and humiliated.

Conclusion

Governor Newsom's criticisms are not just political rhetoric; they are a clarion call for accountability, respect for the Constitution, and a return to a governance that values dignity over dominance. While his words may be sharp, they are necessary. In an era where the absurd has become the norm, voices like Newsom's remind us of the values we're in danger of forsaking under the guise of national pride and security.

Trump's actions, from the attempted National Guard deployment to the planning of a military parade, are not signs of strength but of profound weakness. They reflect a leadership style that is antithetical to the principles upon which the United States was founded. In this ongoing saga of governance gone awry, Newsom's rebuke is not just warranted; it's essential. As the political theater unfolds, one can only hope that the final act is one of redemption and a return to sanity, not a further descent into the theater of the absurd.
