‘Ugly and dangerous’: Ex-GOP strategist lays out what happens to MAGA after Trump dies

Rick Wilson, political strategist and co-founder of the anti-Trump group The Lincoln Project, described the harsh reality for MAGA after President Donald Trump dies.

In a Substack essay published Tuesday, Wilson identified how “what happens next will be ugly and dangerous” as Trump ages and ultimately meets his demise.

“We don’t spend enough time on how much chaos and blood comes after that tap in the Generallismo’s shoulder, on the inevitable collapse of systems built around a single man entail,” he wrote.

The end is inescapable for Trump. And the question of who comes next has yet to be answered.

“The leader dies, weakens, is defeated politically or militarily, or even loses a step, and the entire structure that pretended to be a unified movement reveals itself as what it really was all along: a feeding frenzy for sycophants who think they were born to inherit the throne. The pressure cooker of autocratic systems rewards fealty, loyalty, public and private obeisance,” Wilson wrote.

“If you want to understand what is coming for MAGA as Donald Trump ages and declines, you start there,” the former Republican strategist wrote.

While MAGA loyalists have an eye on the future, it might not bode well for Trump’s most faithful followers. One main issue has persisted.

“Not with his tweets, not with daily outrages and excesses, but with the iron law of personality cults: succession is their unsolved problem,” he explained.

What will come next for MAGA is still unknown.

“Autocrats are very good at seizing power and holding it. They are very bad at leaving it behind without blowing something up on the way out,” Wilson wrote. “Political scientists have long argued that personality cult regimes are especially fragile at succession because the leader spends his life eliminating rivals rather than training successors.”

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Aftermath: Dissecting Wilson’s Prognosis for MAGA Post-Trump

Rick Wilson, once a Republican strategist turned vehement anti-Trump voice, has recently unfurled a chilling prophecy regarding the post-Trump era for the MAGA movement. In a Substack essay that reads like a cautionary tale, Wilson paints a bleak picture of what lies beyond the reign of Donald Trump. His analysis, while steeped in the language of political doom, merits a closer, critical examination.

The Cult of Personality and Its Inevitable Demise

Wilson’s central thesis pivots on the idea that MAGA, much like other autocratic movements, is intrinsically tied to the whims and vitality of its leader, Donald Trump. He eloquently describes the MAGA structure as a facade of unity, poised to crumble into chaos once its linchpin is removed. This is neither an original observation nor an unfounded one. History is littered with examples of personality cults that imploded spectacularly with the decline or departure of their figurehead.

However, where Wilson’s analysis might veer into the territory of sensationalism is in his portrayal of the aftermath as “ugly and dangerous.” While the potential for disorder cannot be understated, the assumption that MAGA will devolve into a “feeding frenzy for sycophants” seems to overlook the resilience and adaptability of political movements, even those as controversial as MAGA.

The Unsolved Problem of Succession

Wilson hits a nerve when discussing the issue of succession in personality cults. Indeed, the iron law of personality cults he mentions — that succession is their Achilles’ heel — is a critical point. Autocrats, by nature, do not mentor successors; they eliminate rivals. This creates a vacuum of power when they depart, which, as Wilson correctly asserts, often leads to internal conflict and instability.

Yet, what Wilson outlines as a MAGA-specific dilemma is, in reality, a universal challenge for any movement or organization built around a singular, charismatic leader. The focus on MAGA’s future post-Trump perhaps misses a broader question: How does any political movement transition beyond its founding personalities without losing its essence or disintegrating entirely?

Autocrats, Succession, and the MAGA Movement

Wilson’s assertion that “Autocrats are very good at seizing power and holding it. They are very bad at leaving it behind without blowing something up on the way out,” is a poignant observation. However, applying this blanket statement to Trump and the MAGA movement simplifies and overlooks the nuances of American political systems and culture. While Trump’s leadership style may exhibit autocratic tendencies, the institutional checks and balances, alongside a vibrant civil society, act as counterweights to potential excesses.

Moreover, equating MAGA’s future challenges solely to the pitfalls of autocratic regimes ignores the movement’s deeply entrenched ideological and cultural roots within a significant segment of the American populace. The issues at hand are not merely about succession but how the movement’s core ideas will evolve or be reinterpreted in the post-Trump era.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Understanding

Rick Wilson’s essay serves as a stark reminder of the perils of personality cults in politics. His insights into the potential chaos that could ensue in a MAGA movement post-Trump are both valuable and necessary. However, the narrative could benefit from a more nuanced understanding of the complex interplay between leader, movement, and the broader socio-political context.

In the end, predicting the future of MAGA post-Trump is as much about understanding the currents of American political culture as it is about the man himself. While Wilson presents a compelling scenario of what might come next, the reality will likely be shaped by a multitude of factors, both predictable and unforeseen. In dissecting the future of political movements, the devil is always in the details, and broad strokes, while illustrative, often miss the subtleties of the canvas.
