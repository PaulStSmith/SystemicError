'You should be embarrassed': Trump shames reporter for asking about $400M Qatar plane

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

As President Donald Trump signed an executive order to lower drug prices, an ABC reporter questioned the gift of a $400 million plane from Qatar which he is set to receive.

“What do you say to people who view that luxury jet as a personal gift to you?” The reporter asked. “Why not leave it behind?”

Trump cut the reporter off. “You are ABC fake news, right? Only ABC would — well, a few of you would ask that question.”

He added, “Let me tell you. You should be embarrassed asking that question. They're giving us a free jet. I can say no, no, no, don't give us — I want to pay you a bill again, or 400 million or whatever it is, or I can say thank you very much.”

Trump then went on to give a golf analogy as to why he accepted the plane. “You know, there was an old golfer named Sam Snead. Did you ever hear of him? He won 82 tournaments, he was a great golfer.

"He had a motto, ‘When they give you a putt, you say thank you very much. You pick it up and walk to the next hole.’ A lot of people are stupid. They say no, no, I insist on putting it. Then they putt it, they miss it. Then their partner gets angry at them.”

“You know what? Remember that, Sam Snead, when he gives you a putt, you pick it up and say thank you very much.”

The reporter, and several others, then attempted to ask follow-up questions, which were inaudible.

Trump cut the reporter off again, “It’s not, is not a gift to me. It's a gift to the Department of Defense, and you should know better. Cause you have embarrassed enough, and so has your network. Your network is a disaster, ABC is a disaster.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump's Mastery Over Questionable Gifts and Media Manipulation

In an almost theatrical display of evasion and deflection, President Donald Trump recently showcased yet again his uncanny ability to sidestep pressing inquiries with a blend of personal attack, redirection, and questionable justifications. As he signed an executive order aimed at lowering drug prices—a move that, on its surface, seems commendable—Trump was faced with a probing question from an ABC reporter regarding a luxuriously ostentatious $400 million plane gifted by Qatar. Instead of addressing the ethical implications head-on, Trump reverted to his well-worn playbook: attack, belittle, and deflect.

The Attack on ABC News: A Classic Trump Maneuver

In a response that is as predictable as it is troubling, Trump immediately labeled the ABC reporter as a purveyor of "fake news," a term he has consistently used to undermine confidence in the media whenever its narrative diverges from his own. By doing so, he not only attempts to discredit the reporter's question but also to rally his base against a perceived common enemy—the mainstream media. This tactic, while effective in certain circles, is inherently dangerous as it seeks to erode the foundational pillar of democracy that is a free and critical press.

A Gift or a Gilded Cage?

Trump's justification for accepting the $400 million jet from Qatar is, at best, a masterclass in false equivalency and, at worst, a gross misinterpretation of ethical standards. Drawing a parallel between a luxury jet and a short putt in golf is not only a dramatic oversimplification of the issue but also an insult to the intelligence of the American public. The ethical dilemmas surrounding the acceptance of such an extravagant gift from a foreign nation cannot be brushed aside with folksy anecdotes. This is not a matter of polite etiquette; it's a question of ethical governance and potential conflicts of interest.

A Distraction from the Real Issues

What is particularly egregious about Trump's response is not just his refusal to engage with the ethical implications of the gift but his characterization of the plane as a "gift to the Department of Defense." This claim is a transparent attempt to reframe the narrative and deflect personal responsibility. It raises more questions than it answers: Why would Qatar gift a luxury jet to the Department of Defense? What strings are attached to such a gift? And how does this align with America's foreign policy and national security interests?

In Conclusion: A Pattern of Deflection

Trump's handling of the question regarding the $400 million plane from Qatar is emblematic of a broader pattern of behavior. Time and again, he has demonstrated a willingness to sidestep difficult questions, attack the press, and manipulate facts to suit his narrative. While his supporters might find this approach endearing or even effective, it poses a serious threat to democratic discourse and accountability. The role of the president is not to belittle, deflect, and degrade but to lead with integrity, transparency, and respect for the principles of governance. On all these counts, Trump's response was not only inadequate but deeply troubling.
