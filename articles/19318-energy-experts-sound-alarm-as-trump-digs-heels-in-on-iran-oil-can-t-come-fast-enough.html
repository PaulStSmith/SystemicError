Energy experts sound alarm as Trump digs heels in on Iran: 'Oil can't come fast enough'

By Paulo Santos

The U.S. economic outlook is growing bleaker two weeks into the Trump administration’s military operation against Iran, two energy experts warned Saturday, and has already cemented a “higher floor on pricing” for various goods for the indefinite future.

“Compared to a week ago, the situation looks more challenging and longer lasting,” said Dan Pickering, an oil forecaster and founder of Pickering Energy Partners, speaking with Fortune in its report Saturday.

“An easy solution to the straits does not appear on the horizon. With that, there’s fear of inflation, fear that stocks might be overvalued, and you’re hearing ‘stagflation’ a lot. It’s rippling through sentiment, and it’s putting a higher floor on pricing whenever this conflict ends.”

President Donald Trump authorized unprecedented attacks on Feb. 28, and in the weeks since, oil prices have surged to levels not seen since Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine. At the center of rising oil prices is Iran’s closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a major shipping route through which 20% of the world’s oil trade passes.

The skyrocketing energy prices have reportedly sparked panic within the Trump administration, with one of Trump’s top aides now urging Trump to seek an “off-ramp” to “get out” of the conflict.

For energy expert Bob McNally, however, who previously served as President George W. Bush’s energy adviser, Trump had already passed the point of no return to halt rising costs.

“The world can’t grow without 20% of its energy – not in the short term,” McNally told Fortune in its report Saturday. “People are just unwilling to come to grips with the idea that we’re not going to get 20% of our energy back really fast.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Economic Impact of the Trump Administration’s Military Operation Against Iran

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Who holds institutional power? The Trump administration, specifically President Donald Trump, holds the institutional power in this situation. The decision to authorize military operations against Iran is a clear exercise of executive power, particularly in matters of foreign policy and military action. This decision directly impacts international relations, global markets, and, as highlighted, the U.S. economic outlook.

Who made the decision? The article explicitly states that President Donald Trump authorized unprecedented attacks on Iran, making him the primary decision-maker in this context. The ramifications of this decision, such as the closure of the Strait of Hormuz by Iran and the subsequent surge in oil prices, can be directly traced back to the administration’s actions.

Framing and Responsibility

Does the article’s framing misdirect responsibility? The article does a commendable job of pinpointing the origin of the current economic anxiety and potential for stagflation to the Trump administration’s military operation against Iran. It correctly identifies the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a direct consequence of the conflict, as a central factor in the rising oil prices and economic uncertainty. Therefore, there’s no misdirection of responsibility; the Trump administration’s decisions are presented as the catalyst for these economic developments.

Analysis of Economic Consequences

Impact of Military Operations on the Economy The military operation against Iran has led to significant economic repercussions, primarily through the surge in oil prices following Iran’s closure of the Strait of Hormuz. This strategic move by Iran, in response to the US’s military actions, underscores the interconnectedness of global politics, energy markets, and the economy. The experts cited, Dan Pickering and Bob McNally, both emphasize the long-term and challenging nature of the situation, with a particular focus on the fears of inflation and stagflation due to the higher floor on pricing for goods.

Misplaced Criticism or Valid Concerns? Criticism of the Trump administration’s decision in this context is valid and grounded in the direct consequences of its actions. The decision to engage militarily with Iran has set off a chain reaction affecting global oil supplies, energy prices, and the broader economic outlook. This situation exemplifies the critical importance of understanding the economic implications of foreign policy decisions. The concerns raised by energy experts about inflation, overvalued stocks, and the potential for stagflation are directly related to the administration’s actions and are not the result of misplaced criticism.

Conclusion

The article under review effectively attributes the bleaker U.S. economic outlook to the Trump administration’s decision to initiate military operations against Iran. By focusing on the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the subsequent rise in oil prices as central issues, it avoids misdirecting blame and highlights the significant impact of executive decisions on the economy. The analysis provided by energy experts reinforces the view that these developments have deep and potentially lasting effects on global markets and the U.S. economy, making the criticism of the Trump administration’s actions both relevant and accurately placed.
