Elon Musk was right — there is a 'really big bomb' controlling Trump: ex-aide

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Incessant Tug-of-War of Allegations and Denials

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he arrives at the White House, in Washington, U.S., June 9, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

An Ex-aide of Donald Trump revealed Tuesday that he believes there is a big secret compromising the president — but it isn’t the one Elon Musk claimed to have revealed.

Amid a brutal public row Thursday, Musk claimed it was “time to drop the really big bomb.”

“@realDonaldTrump is in the Epstein files,” he went on. “That is the real reason they have not been made public. Have a nice day, DJT!"

Trump and his associates immediately denied the allegation, and Musk later deleted the claim, made on X. But the president’s ex-spokesman, Anthony Scaramucci, believes there’s much more damaging information in his former boss's closet.

Scaramucci told The Daily Beast Podcast that he believes Russia’s President Vladimir Putin holds damning information about Trump.

“Did he go over the line with Jeff Epstein and do something super-malevolent and super-nefarious? I don’t believe that’s the case because I think that would have come out,” he told the podcast.

“But the flip side is, while I’m saying that to you, I believe there is kompromat on Donald Trump. I don’t know what it is. We all used to say, ‘OK, what is it? What is the hold that Vladimir Putin has on this guy?’

Scaramucci — whose term in office with Trump lasted just 10 days, said the sway Putin holds over him makes no sense otherwise.

“He’s in a trainwreck situation at home, and yet he owns the president of the United States,” he said. “How does he own the president of the United States? You tell me. He has to have something on him, OK, that we don’t understand.”

He added, “Something that would be too much for Trump, even Trump himself, to deal with.

“ … Trump doesn’t love authoritarians. He loves himself, OK? And he would not be kowtowing to Putin, sitting in the biggest economy with the biggest military.”

“He would be shoving Putin, but he doesn’t shove Putin because they got something on him.”

Our Analysis:

The Incessant Tug-of-War of Allegations and Denials

In what feels like a never-ending circus of accusations, retractions, and denials, the political arena once again finds itself embroiled in controversy. This time, the spotlight turns towards former President Donald Trump and a tangled web of allegations involving none other than Elon Musk and Anthony Scaramucci. The narrative, as convoluted as ever, dances around secrets, potentially explosive revelations, and the ever-looming figure of Vladimir Putin.

Musk's Bombshell and Its Swift Disappearance

Elon Musk, in a move characteristic of his impulsive engagement on social media, dropped what he termed the "really big bomb" on X, implicating Trump in the Epstein files. Such an assertion, given the gravity and implications of being connected to Jeffrey Epstein, would indeed be monumental—if it held any substance. Yet, the subsequent deletion of Musk's claim does little to solidify its credibility, instead adding another layer to the spectacle without substance that political discourse has degenerated into.

Scaramucci's Speculations: Kompromat and Putin's Puppeteer Act

Enter Anthony Scaramucci, the ephemeral White House Communications Director whose tenure lasted a mere 10 days. Scaramucci posits another angle—that the real dirt on Trump is not his alleged associations with Epstein but rather kompromat held by Russia's Putin. This isn't a novel narrative. The specter of Russian influence has haunted Trump's presidency, with "Russian collusion" being a chant among his detractors. Yet, Scaramucci's assertions, though lacking concrete evidence, align with a broader concern regarding Trump's seemingly deferential stance towards Putin.

Analyzing the Allegations: Between Fact and Fiction

Let's cut through the fog of insinuation and speculation. The allegations, as they stand, are grounded in beliefs rather than hard evidence. Scaramucci admits to not knowing the specifics of the kompromat, which begs the question: Why air such claims? The answer, unfortunately, may lie in the realm of political maneuvering rather than a genuine concern for transparency or national security.

The Putin Enigma and the Art of Deflection

The narrative of Putin possessing kompromat on Trump has been a convenient tool for explaining away Trump's foreign policy approach towards Russia. It's an explanation that requires no proof, only the willingness to believe in the worst. Yet, it overlooks a simpler explanation that perhaps Trump's foreign policy was just that—policy, misguided or not, derived from his administration's objectives.

Conclusion: The Muddied Waters of Political Discourse

What we're witnessing is the continued muddying of political discourse, where allegations fly with little regard for evidence, and retractions do little to undo the damage. In the absence of concrete proof, these claims and counterclaims serve only to deepen the divides, distract from substantive issues, and erode trust in the political process.

In a world where the truth is already hard to come by, these spectacles do not enlighten but further obfuscate. One must ask: In the pursuit of political advantage, have we lost sight of the importance of truth and evidence? As readers, as voters, as participants in democracy, we must demand more than just sensational claims. The stakes are too high, the issues too important, for us to settle for anything less.
