'We've set up the enemy': Trump biographer claims there's a sinister midterm plan in place

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump has already built an excuse if the Republican Party gets a midterm election drubbing in November, according to his biographer.

The president has seemingly cleared a path for a case against the Democratic Party performing well by trying to push the SAVE Act through Congress — a bill many have said he knows has no chance of passing.

Michael Wolff told The Daily Beast, “Let’s assume this [bill] is not going to pass. So why is he doing this?

“The reason he is doing this is to set up and to continue the narrative when he loses the midterms. This then becomes the reason he lost the midterms, and he lost the midterms illegitimately…we’ve set up the enemy here.”

Should the SAVE Act pass, it would mean citizens are hit with major restrictions on how they vote, including requiring voter ID and tougher measures taken against those who vote by mail.

It should be no surprise, according to Wolff, that Trump has pushed forward with this as a potential excuse if the GOP underperforms at the November elections.

“He’s pushing this forward because he has pushed this story forward from day one of his political career,” he said. “That’s what, essentially, his politics is founded on, this... narrative.”

Podcast host Joanna Coles asked, “So if he wins, the system is perfect. And if he loses, the system is rigged.” Wolff replied, “Yes, but he’s not going to win. So the system is going to be rigged.”

Trump has tried to pressure Republican reps into making the SAVE Act law ahead of the elections, though the GOP appears to be split on whether to give in to the president.

He took to Truth Social earlier this week and wrote, “Democrats are desperate to keep illegals, no matter how bad or dangerous they may be, in the Country. They want them to VOTE! That’s why they are fighting so hard to neutralize ICE. We will fight them all the way, and WIN.”

Our Analysis:

The Misdirection Play: Trump’s SAVE Act Gambit

The Power Behind the Throne

At first glance, the article paints a picture of former President Donald Trump maneuvering within the political landscape to preemptively excuse a potential Republican loss in the midterm elections by pushing the SAVE Act—a piece of legislation designed to impose significant restrictions on voting. The key to understanding this situation lies in dissecting the layers of power and decision-making at play.

Donald Trump, despite being a former president, wields substantial influence over the Republican Party’s base and, by extension, its elected officials. This influence is the primary lever of power in this scenario. However, it’s crucial to differentiate between influence and formal institutional power. Trump can pressure, persuade, and mobilize public opinion, but he does not directly control the legislative process.

Congress, specifically the Republican members who might either support or oppose the SAVE Act, holds the institutional power here. Their decision to push forward, stall, or reject the bill is the direct action that will lead to the legislation’s success or failure.

The Decision Makers and Their Choices

The article implies that Trump’s advocacy for the SAVE Act is a strategic move to frame any potential electoral loss as illegitimate. Michael Wolff, Trump’s biographer, suggests that Trump is fully aware the bill is unlikely to pass but is leveraging it to perpetuate his narrative of a ‘rigged’ electoral system.

The decision-makers in this narrative are Republican legislators who must choose whether to align with Trump’s push for the SAVE Act or resist it due to its controversial nature and potential unpopularity with the broader electorate. Their decision is the pivot on which this situation turns.

Framing and Misdirection

The article, while focusing on Trump’s motivations and strategies, may inadvertently misdirect responsibility toward him for the GOP’s potential midterm performance. While Trump’s influence is undeniable, it’s the GOP legislators who will decide the fate of the SAVE Act, and by extension, influence the narrative around the midterms.

The framing suggests a binary outcome tied directly to Trump’s actions: if the GOP loses, it’s because of his failed gambit with the SAVE Act. This oversimplification glosses over the myriad factors that influence electoral outcomes, including candidate quality, campaign strategies, and voter sentiment, not to mention the actual impact of legislation on voter turnout and behavior.

Conclusion: Beyond the Scapegoat

Labeling Trump as the sole architect of potential GOP midterm losses due to his promotion of the SAVE Act is a convenient narrative that overlooks the complex web of decisions, power dynamics, and institutional actions at play. While Trump’s efforts to shape the narrative are evident, the responsibility for the GOP’s legislative agenda and electoral strategy is collectively shared among its members.

It’s essential to scrutinize the roles of all actors involved, resisting the temptation to attribute broad outcomes to single individuals, even those as influential as Trump. The real story is often more nuanced, requiring a critical examination of where power resides, who makes the decisions, and how narratives are constructed and wielded in political discourse.
