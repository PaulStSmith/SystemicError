'Wow, thank you!' Dramatic flip sees Trump gushing over bitter nemesis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 13, 20254 min read

Donald Trump gestures at Arlington National Cemetery. REUTERS/Ken Cedeno

President Donald Trump heaped praise onto longtime NBC journalist Chuck Todd Tuesday following remarks comparing Trump to former President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

The gushing on Truth Social bucked years of bitter animosity between the two figures.

“Wow, thank you Chuck,” Trump wrote in the online post Tuesday.

“I always said that you were far better than the scum that runs the fake news at NBC and, especially, Brian Roberts and ‘concast.’ You are a free man now.”

Speaking on the Christian Broadcast Network Monday Todd, who left NBC in January, said that he believed Trump had been the most impactful president “since FDR, particularly when it came to his cultural impact.

“I think culturally, in particular, certainly on our political identity, I think he's had – not since FDR we had this kind of, a president with – look, he wants to involve himself in everything,” Todd said Monday. “FDR involved himself in almost everything."

Trump’s praise reverses a longstanding feud between the two, with Trump regularly lashing out at Todd over the past several years. In 2018, Trump labeled the then-NBC reporter as “sleepy eyes Chuck Todd,” later calling him a “sleeping son of a b—-.” In 2020, Trump called for Todd to “be fired” after NBC ran a clip of comments made by then-Attorney General William Barr allegedly out of context.

Trump’s targeting of Todd was so fierce that the senior journalist said he began to receive real-world harassment from apparent Trump supporters, including receiving “weird death threats” and having his tires slashed.

While Todd’s recent comments comparing Trump to FDR appear largely neutral, speaking only to the level of impact Trump has had on American society, they were apparently enough to appease the president, who went on to congratulate Todd on leaving NBC — and telling him to “use (his) time well!”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Praise for Chuck Todd: A Study in Opportunism

In an almost Shakespearean twist of irony, President Donald Trump's recent outpouring of affection towards Chuck Todd, a journalist he has previously lambasted with vitriol, encapsulates the essence of political opportunism. Trump's sudden pivot, from hurling insults to heaping praise, is a spectacle that warrants a closer examination, not just for its stark contrast but for the underlying motives and implications.

A Brief History of Animosity

For years, the relationship between Trump and Todd resembled less a professional disagreement and more an outright feud. Trump's descriptors for Todd ranged from “sleepy eyes” to “sleeping son of a b—-,” not to mention calls for his firing over perceived slights. This barrage of insults was not without consequence; Todd reported real-world harassment and threats, a testament to the tangible impact of Trump's words. Yet, in a sudden reversal, Trump's tune changes to one of gratitude and admiration following Todd's comparison of Trump to Franklin D. Roosevelt on the Christian Broadcast Network.

The FDR Comparison: Neutral or Loaded?

Todd's comments, painting Trump as the most impactful president since FDR, especially in terms of cultural influence, are the linchpin of this dramatic reversal. While Todd's assessment might appear neutral or purely analytical, the context within which it is made—and subsequently seized upon by Trump—bears scrutiny. To liken Trump's cultural impact to that of FDR is to place him in a lineage of profound societal change. However, the nature and quality of their respective impacts are vastly different, a nuance lost or conveniently ignored in Trump's gleeful reception of the comparison.

Opportunism Knocks

Trump's response, laden with newfound affection for Todd, is not just a personal reconciliation; it's a strategic move. By embracing Todd's comparison, Trump signals to his base and beyond that even his critics cannot deny his significance. It's an attempt to rewrite the narrative of his presidency, from one mired in controversy, to a tenure of historic consequence. However, this opportunistic pivot does not erase years of antagonism; it merely underscores Trump's readiness to exploit any narrative, however fleeting, that casts him in a favorable light.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is emblematic of a broader trend within political discourse, where figures are willing to overlook years of conflict in favor of momentary alignment. Trump's sudden endorsement of Todd, contingent solely on a perceived compliment, is a stark reminder of the transactional nature of political relationships. It also highlights a worrying dynamic: the potential for historical revisionism, where the contentious aspects of a presidency can be glossed over with strategic praise.

Conclusion: Beyond the Praise

Trump's lauding of Todd following the latter's FDR comparison is more than a simple change of heart; it's a calculated maneuver. It underscores the fluidity of political alliances and the power of narrative control. While Todd's comments might have been intended as a neutral observation, their appropriation by Trump serves a clear agenda: to recast his presidency in the mold of historical significance. As observers, the onus is on us to sift through this opportunism, to challenge the rewriting of narratives, and to remember that the impact of a presidency cannot be distilled into a single comparison, however flattering it may seem.
