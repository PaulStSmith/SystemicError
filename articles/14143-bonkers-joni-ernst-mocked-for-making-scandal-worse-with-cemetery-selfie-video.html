'Bonkers': Joni Ernst mocked for making scandal worse with cemetery selfie video

Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) was brutally ridiculed by political observers on Saturday after she released an awkward selfie video at a cemetery in response to a recent town hall scandal.

The Iowa Republican came under fire at her town hall when she defended rolling back benefits for those she said were not supposed to be eligible for assistance. "People will die," one woman shouted out.

Ernst horrified social media users with her sarcastic response to the woman. "Well, we are all going to die," Ernst said, which elicited more angst in the auditorium and online.

Over the weekend, Ernst released what purported to be an apology video but was ultimately mocking those she offended.

"I made an incorrect assumption that everyone in the auditorium understood that yes, we are all going to perish from this Earth," she said. "So I apologize. And I’m really, really glad that I did not have to bring up the subject of the tooth fairy as well."

"But for those that would like to see eternal and everlasting life, I encourage you to embrace my lord and savior, Jesus Christ," she added.

The backlash was instant.

Not only was Ernst ridiculed on MSNBC, but onlookers also took to social media.

Popular YouTuber Keith Edwards wrote, "Against all odds, Joni Ernst has made it worse."

Edwards also asked, "Is she at a cemetery?"

Conservative commentator Bill Kristol wrote, "For a pro-life party, they’re kind of pro-death."

Journalist Aaron Rupar called it "absolutely bonkers."

"Joni Ernst doubles down on her 'we are all gonna die' defense of Medicaid cuts while walking through a cemetery," Rupar wrote Saturday.

Strategist Aaron Parnas said on Substack Saturday, "Senator Joni Ernst doubled down on the idea that everyone is going to die, in response to constituents being upset that Medicaid cuts will literally lead to some earlier deaths, by filming a fake 'apology' video in a cemetery and trolling those who’s healthcare she is about to cut."

Our Analysis:

The Macabre Misstep of Senator Joni Ernst

In a move that can only be described as a blend of political insensitivity and surreal satire, Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) has managed to deepen the chasm between herself and constituents concerned about healthcare cuts. Her response to the backlash from a town hall meeting – a video filmed in a cemetery – is not only tone-deaf but borders on the grotesque.

The Town Hall Tumult

The controversy began at a town hall meeting where Ernst defended her stance on rolling back benefits for individuals deemed ineligible for assistance. When confronted with the dire proclamation that "People will die" due to these cuts, Ernst's retort, "Well, we are all going to die," was not only sarcasm at its most bitter but a shocking dismissal of genuine concerns over life-and-death policies.

The Cemetery Apology: A Mockery in Disguise

Taking to a cemetery to issue what was purportedly an apology, Ernst instead produced a video that can only be characterized as a mockery. Claiming an "incorrect assumption" about her audience's grasp of mortality, she proceeded to condescend further by stating her relief at not having to discuss the tooth fairy, followed by an evangelical nudge towards her religious beliefs. This so-called apology was not just ill-conceived; it was a blatant insult to those expressing legitimate fears about healthcare accessibility.

The Backlash: A Chorus of Disbelief and Disdain

The backlash was swift and scathing. Figures from across the political spectrum, from MSNBC to conservative commentator Bill Kristol, voiced their disbelief and criticism. Kristol's remark, "For a pro-life party, they’re kind of pro-death," sharply underscores the hypocrisy apparent in Ernst's stance and her flippant response to concerns about life-preserving healthcare services.

A Deeper Analysis: The Political and the Personal

What Ernst's video betrays is a profound detachment from the lived realities of her constituents. Her attempt to cloak policy defense in morbid humor and religious evangelism does not obscure the stark truth: policies have real, tangible effects on human lives. The decision to film in a cemetery, a place symbolizing both respect for the departed and the solemnity of death, only amplifies the callousness of her message.

Conclusion: A Grave Error

Senator Joni Ernst's video is not merely a faux pas; it is a glaring testament to the systemic insensitivity entrenched within certain political echelons. It's a reminder that some elected officials are woefully out of touch with the precariousness of their constituents' lives, preferring instead to retreat into mockery rather than engage with critical issues of life, death, and dignity. Ernst's response is a misstep of macabre proportions, underscoring a need for more empathetic and thoughtful leadership in the face of human hardship.
