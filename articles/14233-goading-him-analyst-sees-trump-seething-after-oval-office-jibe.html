'Goading him': Analyst sees Trump seething after Oval Office jibe

President Donald Trump has been "goaded" by recent mockery of his tariff agenda into harshening it, said Bharat Ramamurti, former director of the U.S. National Economic Council, in a panel discussion on MSNBC Thursday evening.

In particular, Trump was enraged by the TACO ("Trump Always Chickens Out") meme circulating to make fun of how he keeps walking back his tariff threats. He blew up at a reporter who asked him about it this week.

And this comes at the same time federal courts are moving to pump the brakes on his tariffs altogether, although orders doing so are currently on hold as an appeals court considers the issue.

"I want to underscore all of the lies that are happening here," said anchor Alicia Menendez. "There was a lie that this administration was going to bring manufacturing back to the United States. There was a lie that these tariffs were going to bring down prices for American consumers. And there was a lie that somehow this tariff war was going to make America stronger in the world. Prices have not come down. Manufacturing is not back in this country. And our allies are beginning to create new allyships and new trading partnerships because they're not certain they can rely on us. So he has to now own all of that."

"That's exactly right," agreed Ramamurti.

"You know, there is a way of doing this where you pair some targeted tariffs with some real strategic investment in manufacturing, and then you work with our allies to go after what you think are unfair trade practices in China," Ramamurti said. "We should be working with Japan and South Korea and Vietnam in a partnership against China. Instead, because of the way that they've gone about this, those countries are actually seeking out agreements with China because in some ways, they feel like it's a more stable trading relationship than the United States right now. He's actually turned away our allies in what should be a joint effort to take on Chinese manufacturing and bring some of that back to our shores. And so it has backfired across the board. It's a political loser."

"But I think that, you know, there's been a lot of discussion lately of TACO, right? Trump Always Chickens Out," Ramamurti continued. "And I think that that is honestly goading him into escalation on this issue. And I wouldn't be surprised if, regardless of what the court says in this case, they start to pursue those other legal avenues to impose tariffs using other legal authorities and to just keep the train moving, because that's going to generate more court cases. And but all throughout that time, those, those tariffs will be in place."

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Bluff: Trump's Tariff Turmoil

In an era where political discourse is more akin to a circus act than a solemn duty, President Donald Trump’s tariff agenda has become the latest spectacle. Bharat Ramamurti, the former director of the U.S. National Economic Council, recently shed light on this debacle during a panel discussion on MSNBC, where the conversation was as illuminating as it was concerning.

The TACO Mockery: A Catalyst for Escalation

Trump, ever sensitive to mockery, has apparently been "goaded" into intensifying his tariff agenda, thanks to the viral TACO ("Trump Always Chickens Out") meme. This meme, a sharp jab at his tendency to backtrack on his tariff threats, has not only infuriated him but also led to an embarrassing outburst at a reporter. This is a classic example of Trump's impulsiveness potentially dictating policy—a dangerous precedent.

The Lies Unveiled

Alicia Menendez, the anchor, didn't mince words when she spotlighted the trifecta of lies propagated by the Trump administration regarding the tariffs. These were: - The myth of revitalizing manufacturing in the U.S. - The false promise of tariffs reducing consumer prices. - The delusion of strengthening America's position globally through a tariff war.

The reality, as starkly laid out by Menendez, is that none of these promises have come to fruition. If anything, the situation has deteriorated, with manufacturing not making its grand return, prices remaining high, and U.S. allies seeking new partnerships out of distrust for America's reliability.

A Missed Opportunity for Strategic Diplomacy

Ramamurti's insights further dismantle the Trump administration's approach, highlighting a missed opportunity for strategic diplomacy. Instead of leveraging tariffs as a tool within a broader strategy of investment in manufacturing and collaboration with allies to address unfair trade practices, particularly those of China, Trump’s administration has isolated the U.S. This isolation has driven countries like Japan, South Korea, and Vietnam towards seeking more stable agreements with China, ironically making China the beneficiary of Trump's erratic policies.

The Political Loser

Ramamurti doesn't hesitate to label Trump's tariff agenda a "political loser," and it's hard to disagree. The administration's approach has not only failed to achieve its stated goals but has also weakened the U.S.'s strategic position globally. The TACO meme, while humorous, underscores a more serious issue of policy being influenced by ego and the desire for retaliation rather than rational decision-making.

The Unending Cycle of Legal Battles

The final twist in this saga is Trump's determination to pursue tariffs through other legal avenues, regardless of federal court pushbacks. This stubbornness to "keep the train moving" ensures a continuous cycle of court cases, all the while keeping ineffective tariffs in place.

Conclusion

The narrative surrounding Trump's tariff agenda is a cautionary tale of governance by ego, where policy is driven by personal vendettas and public mockery rather than strategic planning and diplomatic finesse. The repercussions of this approach are already evident—strained international relationships, unfulfilled promises, and a tarnished global image. As the TACO meme humorously but accurately captures, when policy is dictated by pride rather than pragmatism, everyone loses.
