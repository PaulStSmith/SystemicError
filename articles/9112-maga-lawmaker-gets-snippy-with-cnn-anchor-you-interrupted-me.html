Assistant Whip Rep. Nicole Malliotakis (R-NY) joined CNN’s Brianna Keilar to discuss the ground stop at New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport due to staffing shortages as the government shutdown drags on, and how FAA staffing problems have ended previous stalemates and forced the government to reopen.

Keilar moved the conversation to the subject of food stamp benefits, which two federal judges ruled Friday must be continued to be funded by the Trump administration and the U.S.Department of Agriculture, as the deadline for funding the program was slated to run out Saturday.

“The question I’m asking you, because you’re a Republican and that is your party — and that is your party in the White House making that decision — do you think it’s fair to just to the country’s most needy as leverage?” Keilar asked.

“I was just about to answer when you interrupted me, because the blame is not just on this administration, because this administration is not the one that voted to shut down the government. It was the Senate Democrats that chose to,” Malliotakis shot back.

“But what I’m simply saying is, if the Democrats are not going to vote to reopen the government, they want to continue using this leverage and inflict this type of pain in the American people unnecessarily. Then what I think the president and the administration should do is what they’ve done with the Women, Infants, and Children program,” she said. “They were able to find some money to shift funds to continue that program, very critical for young families that I represent. They found funds from Pentagon research and development to be able to pay our military. And as I said earlier, they should do the same here, if given, which they seem to have received green lights from the judges to allow them to use it.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A MAGA Lawmaker’s Sidestep on CNN

In a display that could only be described as a masterclass in political evasion, Rep. Nicole Malliotakis (R-NY), an Assistant Whip no less, took to the airwaves on CNN, engaging in a verbal tango with anchor Brianna Keilar. The topic? The potential catastrophic impact of the government shutdown on food stamp benefits, a lifeline for America’s most vulnerable. But instead of addressing the heart of the issue, Malliotakis opted for a maneuver as old as politics itself: deflection.

Keilar’s question was straightforward, pointed, and undeniably crucial: “Do you think it’s fair to use the country’s most needy as leverage?” Yet, Malliotakis, perhaps finding the question too hot to handle, chose to redirect the blame towards Senate Democrats, entirely bypassing the core concern. This pivot is a classic political strategy, but let’s not mistake familiarity for acceptability. It’s the rhetorical equivalent of smoke and mirrors, designed to obscure rather than illuminate.

The Blame Game

Malliotakis’s retort, “because this administration is not the one that voted to shut down the government. It was the Senate Democrats that chose to,” is an exercise in selective memory. It blatantly ignores the intricate dance of negotiation and compromise (or lack thereof) that leads to a government shutdown. To suggest that the responsibility for such a complex situation rests solely on one party’s shoulders is not just misleading; it’s intellectually dishonest.

The Painful Irony

What’s particularly jarring here is the underlying issue at hand: the potential cessation of food stamp benefits. This isn’t a mere political football; it’s a matter of survival for millions. Yet, Malliotakis’s comments do little to reassure those in need or to propose any tangible solutions. Instead, she points to the administration’s ability to reallocate funds for other programs as a precedent. This is a mere Band-Aid solution to a gaping wound, and it sidesteps the question of why such financial gymnastics are necessary in the first place.

Conclusion: Politics Over People

Malliotakis’s appearance on CNN serves as a stark reminder of a troubling trend in contemporary politics: the willingness to prioritize political victories over the well-being of the populace. The conversation wasn’t just about food stamps; it was a litmus test for empathy, accountability, and leadership. Unfortunately, but not surprisingly, the response was found wanting.

The real question we should be asking, beyond the immediate issue of food stamp funding, is why our political discourse has become so characterized by evasion and deflection. When did the art of conversation become the art of war? Malliotakis’s interview is not just a single instance of political maneuvering; it’s symptomatic of a deeper systemic malaise that values partisanship over people.

In a world where the truth is often a casualty of political expediency, let’s demand more from our leaders. We deserve answers, not deflections.
