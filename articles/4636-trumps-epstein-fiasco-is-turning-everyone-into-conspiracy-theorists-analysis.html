Trump’s Epstein fiasco is turning everyone into conspiracy theorists: analysis

The Trump administration’s botched rollout of the Epstein files, as well as the revelations contained within them, have produced a world where even “normal” people have become conspiracy theorists, according to the new analysis from Salon.

Published on Monday, the breakdown from Salon senior writer Andi Zeisler examined how the fallout of the files has impacted the wider culture. She noted that the revelations about Jeffrey Epstein, the deceased sex trafficker with close connections to Donald Trump and numerous other elite public figures, have unsurprisingly provided a wealth of new material for conspiracy theorists, but additionally, they also seem to have spread conspiratorial thinking beyond the typical boundaries.

“The Epstein files were always going to be a gift to conspiracy theorists, and have indeed resulted in a raft of brand new theories, including one that has Epstein alive and well and living in Israel,” Zeisler wrote. “But parsing the tranches of documents — the most recent release puts the total at roughly 3.5 million pages — turns out to be making [normal people] feel suspicious too. All over social media, people with no ties to conspiracy communities are unsettled by what they reveal: The sheer breadth of Epstein’s network, the casual references to abuse, the confirmation of everything that hid in plain sight.”

According to Zeisler, the chaotic state in which the files were released to the public by the Justice Department has also contributed to this phenomenon, as it forced the public to make their own sense of things.

“There’s no order to the files themselves: No indexing, no differentiation between material evidence and uninvestigated complaints, missing files and overenthusiastic redactions — a build-your-own-conspiracy board minus the red twine,” Zeisler explained.

Others have also argued that the Trump administration’s handling of the disclosure has sown doubt amongst the public in a way that the president might never be able to shake. Despite pledging to release the files on the campaign trail in 2024, once in office, Trump became highly resistant to the idea, with his officials in the DOJ and FBI claiming at one point that there were no files. Once an act of Congress forced their disclosure, the DOJ missed several deadlines for the full release and released numerous pages with heavy redactions. The sum of it all has been a situation in which a plurality of the public believes that the government is covering something up.

Conspiracy theory expert Anna Merlan told Zeisler that the sheer magnitude of Epstein’s connections to powerful figures also made it difficult for people to escape conspiratorial thinking, as the revelations about his connections contained in the files painted a picture of someone with vast influence.

“[Epstein] had so much to do with so many famous and powerful people, and was involved in so many things,” Merlan said. “So part of the reason we’re seeing [the Epstein files] and going, ‘Wow, he’s everywhere, he’s the grand unified answer to every bit of corruption that is plaguing us,’ is because, by design, he tried to be everywhere. He worked very, very hard at it.”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Masterclass in Mishandling and Mistrust

The recent Salon article shines a torchlight on a glaring debacle that could only be concocted in the underbelly of the Trump administration: the botched rollout of the Epstein files. Authored by Andi Zeisler, the piece not only dissects the chaos unleashed by this mishandling but also spotlights the deeper, more insidious spread of conspiracy theories among the general populace. Here’s where the analysis cuts deep and where it could have sliced even deeper.

The Gift to Conspiracy Theorists

Zeisler is spot on in her assessment that the Epstein saga was a ticking time bomb for conspiracy theorists. The files, teeming with about 3.5 million pages of documents, were a feast for those predisposed to connect dots where lines may not even exist. The claim that Epstein is alive and kicking in Israel is just the tip of the iceberg. But where Zeisler’s narrative excels is in highlighting the transition of this conspiracy mindset from the fringes to the mainstream.

The real horror is not the emergence of new conspiracy theories but the legitimization of conspiratorial thinking among “normal” people. Social media has become a cesspool of suspicion, with even the most rational minds drawn into the whirlpool of skepticism spawned by the Epstein revelations. The breadth of Epstein’s network and the nonchalance towards abuse uncovered in the files didn’t just confirm suspicions; they shattered the glass of naivety that perhaps protected our collective psyche.

The Chaos of Disclosure

The Trump administration’s handling (or mishandling) of the Epstein files is a textbook example of how not to foster public trust. The Salon piece astutely points out the lack of order and coherence in the file release, effectively turning public understanding into a DIY conspiracy kit. This disarray, as Zeisler highlights, was not accidental but a strategic blunder, if not outright sabotage, by an administration that once promised transparency.

However, Zeisler could have sharpened her critique by focusing more on the implications of such chaos. The administration’s resistance to transparency, the missed deadlines, and the overzealous redactions didn’t just fuel public suspicion; they fundamentally eroded trust in government institutions. When officials from the DOJ and FBI claim “there are no files,” only to be contradicted by an act of Congress, the message is clear: the government is at best incompetent, at worst, complicit.

The Unavoidable Descent into Conspiratorial Thinking

The insights from conspiracy theory expert Anna Merlan underscore a critical point: Epstein’s omnipresence in the circles of the rich and powerful makes it almost impossible for the public not to fall into the trap of conspiratorial thinking. Epstein’s deliberate cultivation of influence and connections paints a picture of a man who was everywhere because he wanted to be the center of everything.

Herein lies a missed opportunity for a deeper dive: the systemic failure that allowed Epstein to thrive. The Salon article touches upon this but stops short of exploring the structural deficiencies—a cocktail of wealth, power, and impunity—that made Epstein’s sprawling network possible.

Conclusion

The Salon analysis by Andi Zeisler provides a compelling overview of how the Epstein files have transformed from a conspiracy theorist’s dream into a societal nightmare. The article captures the essence of the fallout but could have gone further, delving into the systemic corruption and institutional failures that Epstein’s saga lays bare.

In a world where trust in public institutions is at an all-time low, the Epstein files saga is not just a cautionary tale about the spread of conspiracy theories; it’s a stark reminder of the consequences of governmental opacity and mishandling. As the lines between fact and fiction blur, the real casualty is public trust—an asset far too precious to be squandered.
