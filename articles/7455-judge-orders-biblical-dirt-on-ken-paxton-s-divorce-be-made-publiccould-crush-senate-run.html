Judge orders 'biblical' dirt on Ken Paxton's divorce be made public—could crush Senate run

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton speaks during a news conference in Washington, U.S., April 26, 2022. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz/File Photo

Far-right Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton’s divorce records are about to become public — with potentially destructive consequences for his Senate run.

According to the Dallas Morning News, Paxton and his ex-wife, Angela, ended their weekslong fight to keep the records under seal after media outlets sued for access. “The sudden shift in the case came late Thursday ... after one of Ken Paxton’s attorneys reached out to inform him the couple was ending its fight to keep the files closed. The move came just hours before the two sides were set to argue before a judge in Collin County District Court as to whether the records should remain sealed.”

With the Paxtons consenting to the release, the judge has signed off on an order to proceed.

“It’s a victory, I think, for the American public and for the Texas voters,” said attorney Tyler Bexley, who represents the media outlets pursuing access to the records. “If there’s new information that hasn’t been released publicly yet, we certainly think the voters have the right to know that.”

Paxton, an ally of President Donald Trump with a long history of legal problems including a now-settled securities fraud case and an impeachment for bribery and abuse of power, has been mounting a bid to dethrone longtime Texas Sen. John Cornyn. It’s one of the very few federal races in Texas where Trump has not yet made an endorsement.

But the race was shaken up in July when Paxton’s then-wife announced she was divorcing him on “biblical grounds.” She did not elaborate further on the details, but many observers noted that one of the only grounds for divorce explicitly named in the Bible is adultery — an allegation that has followed Paxton for years.

Our Analysis:

Paxton’s Divorce Drama: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

The unfolding saga of Far-right Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton’s divorce records becoming public is more than just tabloid fodder; it’s a glaring spotlight on the hypocrisy and moral bankruptcy often found within the ranks of those who tout themselves as paragons of virtue and family values. The Dallas Morning News reveals a narrative that, while personal in nature, has significant political ramifications, especially considering Paxton’s ambitions to replace longtime Texas Sen. John Cornyn.

Firstly, the very fact that Paxton and his ex-wife, Angela, attempted to keep these records under wraps speaks volumes. The sudden capitulation to media pressure, as noted by the Dallas Morning News, underscores a desperate attempt to control a narrative that could potentially derail Paxton’s Senate run. It’s ironic that those who often champion transparency and accountability are the first to shroud their own lives in secrecy when it suits them.

Moreover, the involvement of media outlets in a legal battle to unveil these records is a testament to the critical role of journalism in holding public figures accountable. Attorney Tyler Bexley’s remark about it being a “victory for the American public and for Texas voters” is not hyperbole. In an era where misinformation abounds, the truth, especially about those in or aspiring to high office, is more precious than ever.

Paxton’s track record of legal and ethical controversies only adds layers to this unfolding drama. With a history that includes a “now-settled securities fraud case and an impeachment for bribery and abuse of power,” one has to wonder about the judgement of those who continue to support his political ambitions. The mention of his alliance with President Donald Trump and the lack of endorsement (so far) in this race by Trump paints a complex picture of political alliances and perhaps, emerging fissures within the far-right faction.

The “biblical grounds” for divorce, as announced by Paxton’s then-wife, and the subsequent speculation about adultery, is a grotesque irony for a figure often associated with the so-called moral high ground. The public’s right to scrutinize the personal ethics of those who legislate on matters of morality and family cannot be overstated. It’s a potent reminder that those who wield power and influence are often the most flawed, and their personal failings can have public consequences.

In summary, the saga of Ken Paxton’s divorce records is more than just a salacious glimpse into a public figure’s private life. It’s a microcosm of the larger issues of accountability, transparency, and the often yawning chasm between public persona and private reality. As this story unfolds, it will undoubtedly continue to provoke debate about the qualifications and character of those who seek to represent us. Paxton’s political future may hang in the balance, but the broader implications for political integrity and trust in our leaders remain the larger concern.
