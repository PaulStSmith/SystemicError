'I will destroy you': Trump fixer accused of threatening DOJ official — then got her fired

By Paulo Santos

New banners depicting U.S. President Donald Trump are hung on the Department of Justice building in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 19, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump’s influential MAGA ally Mike Davis is facing new scrutiny after allegedly threatening to stop a Justice Department official during negotiations over a major tech merger, according to a Wall Street Journal investigation.

Gail Slater, then head of the DOJ’s antitrust division, was reviewing Hewlett-Packard Enterprise’s $14 billion bid to acquire rival Juniper Networks when Davis – serving as outside counsel for HPE – reportedly warned her, “If you don’t approve this settlement, I will destroy you. I will destroy your job at the DOJ,” according to sworn testimony from her former deputy, Roger Alford, reviewed by the Journal.

Slater later told colleagues the call from Davis – who had not only been her friend for years but also recommended her to Trump for the top job – had left her shaken.

“The fallout was swift,” the Journal reported Friday. “Within months, two of Slater’s deputies – including Alford – were pushed out of the Justice Department. By February, Slater was gone, too. A senior White House official said Davis played a role in her ouster.”

Davis denied making any threats. The MAGA lawyer called the allegation “utter bull” in an interview published Friday by the Journal and insisted personnel changes stemmed from what he described as “bogus” corruption claims against him.

But a Journal investigation found that Davis “pushed antitrust officials at the Justice Department to approve his deals – and he went over their heads when they wouldn’t comply.”

“Davis, despite having little experience practicing antitrust law, is one of the most visible practitioners of a change playing out across the division,” the Journal reported Friday.

HPE ultimately pursued settlement talks after the DOJ moved to block the deal, arguing the merger would reduce competition in corporate wireless networking.

Our Analysis:

The Real Power Play in the DOJ-HPE Merger Drama

In a scenario that reads like a political thriller, the Wall Street Journal has shed light on a contentious power struggle involving high-stakes tech mergers, alleged threats, and the ouster of key Justice Department officials. At the center of this storm is Mike Davis, a MAGA ally and outside counsel for Hewlett-Packard Enterprise (HPE), and Gail Slater, the former head of the DOJ’s antitrust division.

Identifying the Power Brokers

First, let’s dissect the institutional power dynamics at play. The Justice Department, specifically its antitrust division, holds significant institutional power over the approval of mergers and acquisitions. This ensures that corporate consolidations do not harm consumer interests by stifling competition. However, this case introduces an external influencer, Mike Davis, who, despite his lack of antitrust law experience, appears to wield considerable influence both within and outside the DOJ.

Decision-Makers and Their Impact

The decision to approve or block the $14 billion merger between HPE and Juniper Networks rested with the DOJ’s antitrust division, making Gail Slater and her team the primary decision-makers. However, the alleged intervention by Mike Davis, through threats and political pressure, represents an attempt to circumvent this institutional mechanism.

Misdirection and Misplaced Responsibility?

The narrative could easily mislead readers to focus solely on the interpersonal dynamics between Davis and Slater, overshadowing the broader issue of political influence on regulatory decisions. While the personal relationship and the alleged threat are indeed newsworthy, they are part of a larger, more concerning pattern of influence and retaliation.

Analysis

The Wall Street Journal article effectively highlights the tension between political power and regulatory integrity. However, it is crucial to pinpoint where the responsibility for this breakdown lies. The real story is not just about a threat; it’s about how political allies of the administration seemingly leverage their connections to influence decisions that should be made based on legal and economic analysis.

Misplaced Criticism? While Davis’s alleged threats and the subsequent fallout for Slater and her deputies are the focal points, the narrative might shift undue blame onto individual actors within the DOJ. This perspective risks obscuring the systemic issue: the potential for political interference in what should be impartial regulatory processes.

The Bigger Picture: Beyond the immediate drama, this situation raises red flags about the integrity of antitrust enforcement under political pressure. Davis’s role as a MAGA ally and his apparent ability to influence DOJ personnel decisions underscore a concerning overlap between political allegiances and regulatory oversight.

Conclusion

In unraveling the intricate dynamics of this case, it becomes clear that the problem is not merely about personal misconduct or individual threats. It’s a symptom of a larger, more systemic issue where political influence can potentially override institutional integrity and expertise. The focus should not just be on punishing or exonerating individual actors like Davis or Slater but on ensuring that regulatory institutions like the DOJ’s antitrust division can operate free from undue political pressure. This scenario serves as a stark reminder of the importance of maintaining clear boundaries between political influence and the impartial administration of justice.
