Donald Trump is the biggest failure in recent US history

Based on an article by Peter F. Crowley, Common Dreams

Paulo SantosJuly 7, 20263 min read

Trump’s Failure Is Not a Footnote. It Is the System Showing Its Teeth.

The Ledger

The source article uses the 250th anniversary of the United States to compare presidents and lands on a blunt conclusion: Donald Trump’s second term is not merely bad governance, but a concentrated assault on prices, public institutions, speech, and war powers. That framing matters because it puts the story where it belongs: on institutional damage, not presidential theater.

Power, Not Confusion

Trump is not a bystander to inflation, war, or administrative collapse. The article attributes the tariff-driven price spike, the assault on scientific and medical research, the expansion of ICE, the redirection of public money toward billionaire tax cuts, and the renewed war on Iran to his own decisions. That is the actual center of gravity here. When a president moves money out of nutrition, aid, and research and into repression and military spending, the proper label is not “controversy.” It is state power being repurposed for class rule and coercion.

The Misdirection Machine

The weakest move in political coverage is pretending deliberate harm is just bad luck or messy management. This source does not entirely fall into that trap, but it still carries the old habit of measuring democratic injury against presidential “success” in narrow policy terms. That is too small. A president can pass a bill, stabilize a market, or win a war objective and still deepen the architecture of domination. Lincoln Memorial aesthetics, unemployment optics, and headline inflation figures are distractions unless they are tied back to who gets punished and who gets protected.

Democrats Are Not The Villains Here, But They Are Not Off The Hook

The article is right to say establishment Democrats have been tepid. That is not a rhetorical flourish; it is a real strategic failure. When Trump is openly escalating authoritarian practice, Democrats cannot answer with procedural caution and moral discomfort. They have to organize like the institutional opposition to a regime that is using the state against the public. The source notes more aggressive language from figures like Chris Van Hollen and the rise of progressives in primaries. That is the direction that matters: not apology, not triangulation, not a search for “reasonable” language while the machinery of repression expands.

ICE Is The Point

The expansion of ICE by a factor of nine is not a policy detail. It is the state building a larger deportation and intimidation apparatus. The article names the brutality plainly, and it should be named more plainly still: ICE should be abolished. Not reformed. Not retrained. Not given better oversight while it continues to terrorize immigrants and normalize domestic policing as racialized punishment. An agency that expands under authoritarian conditions is not a service problem. It is an instrument of coercion.

What This Moment Reveals

The larger pattern is simple: U.S. politics keeps treating democratic erosion as a side effect of bigger stories about inflation, war, and personality. It is the opposite. The same political order that tolerates endless war, billionaire subsidy, prison logic, and the criminalization of dissent also produces presidents like Trump, who stop pretending to hide the cruelty. His failure is real, but it is not accidental. It is what happens when the state is built to absorb abuse until someone comes along willing to use it without shame.
