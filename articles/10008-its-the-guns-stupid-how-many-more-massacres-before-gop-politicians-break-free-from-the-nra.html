It’s the Guns, Stupid: How Many More Massacres Before GOP Politicians Break Free from the NRA?

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Another Sunday morning in America, another slaughter. This time, worshipers at a Mormon church in Grand Blanc Township, Michigan, were targeted. A man rammed his car through the front doors, stormed in with an assault-style rifle, unleashed gunfire, and then set the building ablaze. At least one person is dead, nine more are wounded, and officials warn the toll could rise as investigators sift through the charred wreckage.

If you’re thinking, “Surely, this will be the moment politicians act,” don’t hold your breath. We’ve been here too many times before—Sandy Hook, Uvalde, Parkland, Las Vegas, Buffalo, Pittsburgh, Sutherland Springs, Charleston. The names change, the settings vary, but the blood-soaked script stays the same. Thoughts. Prayers. Empty platitudes. And silence when it comes to legislation that would actually keep weapons of war out of the hands of domestic terrorists.

The NRA’s Chokehold on the GOP

Republican politicians never tire of regurgitating the same bankrupt talking points: “Now is not the time to politicize tragedy.” Translation: Now is not the time to jeopardize their NRA checks. The National Rifle Association, weakened though it may be by corruption scandals and bankruptcy filings, still wields enormous influence in Republican primaries. Candidates know that crossing the NRA means opening themselves up to a barrage of attack ads painting them as “anti-Second Amendment.”

This blood money ensures that AR-15s and other assault-style weapons remain as accessible as candy bars. The result? Our country has become a grotesque outlier: a so-called “civilized” nation where children practice active shooter drills and families fear attending church, grocery stores, or concerts.

We Did This Once Before

What makes the GOP’s inaction especially galling is that we already know the solution. In 1994, Congress passed the Federal Assault Weapons Ban, which restricted the manufacture and sale of certain semiautomatic firearms and large-capacity magazines. The results were clear: mass shooting fatalities declined during the ban. When Republicans and their NRA patrons let the law expire in 2004, mass shootings surged again.

The data is unambiguous. The cause is obvious. The only obstacle is political cowardice.

It’s Not Mental Health. It’s Not Doors. It’s Not Video Games.

Every time the country is forced to endure another massacre, Republicans scramble for scapegoats. Mental health. Video games. Fatherless homes. Even the number of doors in a building. Anything but the glaring truth staring them in the face: it’s the damn guns.

Mental illness exists in every country. Video games are played worldwide. What sets the U.S. apart is our near-unrestricted access to battlefield weapons. Other nations have figured this out. Australia enacted strict gun laws after the Port Arthur massacre in 1996. New Zealand did the same after Christchurch in 2019. Both saw immediate declines in gun violence. Meanwhile, America clings to the fantasy that more guns somehow equal more safety. Tell that to the parishioners hiding under pews in Michigan.

The Political Calculus of Bloodshed

Republicans know exactly what they’re doing. They’ve decided that clinging to NRA loyalty and the fantasy of “gun rights” is more important than protecting their constituents’ lives. It’s not ignorance—it’s cold, cynical calculation. They look at the polling, they look at the NRA’s campaign coffers, and they make a choice: the deaths of innocent Americans are acceptable collateral damage.

And let’s be crystal clear: the overwhelming majority of Americans support stronger gun laws. Poll after poll shows bipartisan majorities favor universal background checks, red-flag laws, and yes, an assault weapons ban. The public is ahead of the politicians. It’s Congress that is stuck in the past—bought, paid for, and shackled by the gun lobby.

The Hypocrisy of the “Pro-Life” Party

This is the same party that will gleefully legislate women’s bodies under the banner of being “pro-life.” Yet when it comes to protecting actual lives from bullets ripping through classrooms, churches, and shopping malls, suddenly life is negotiable. Suddenly freedom means the “right” of a would-be mass murderer to carry an AR-15 supersedes the right of a child to survive second grade. It’s not pro-life. It’s pro-gun. Pro-NRA. Pro-slaughter.

A Cycle We Refuse to Break

Here’s what happens next. Investigators will piece together the Michigan shooter’s motives. We’ll hear stories of heroism—parishioners who shielded loved ones, first responders who rushed in. Vigils will be held, candles lit, prayers murmured. Politicians will issue carefully manicured condolences, followed by the usual gaslighting. Then, within a week, the cameras will move on. Until the next massacre forces us to repeat the cycle all over again.

Unless we break it.

Breaking the NRA’s Grip

What will it take for Republicans to finally pry themselves from the NRA’s bloody pocket? How many more congregants need to be mowed down mid-prayer? How many more children executed at their desks? How many more supermarket shoppers gunned down on a Saturday morning?

The lesson of the 1994 ban is that progress is possible. The lesson of every shooting since is that cowardice kills. The GOP could act tomorrow if it wanted to. But it won’t—not until voters force them to. Not until the price of NRA loyalty becomes steeper than the cost of defection.

Enough is Enough

The victims in Michigan deserved better. The victims in Uvalde, Buffalo, and Charleston deserved better. The victims in Sandy Hook—tiny children, barely old enough to spell their names—deserved better. Instead, they were sacrificed at the altar of an AR-15 and the political cowardice of men and women in Washington who decided their careers mattered more than lives.

When historians look back on this era, they will see a nation held hostage by a minority death cult of gun fetishists, cheered on by their political enablers. They will wonder how a democracy could tolerate such carnage in plain sight. And they will conclude, rightly, that our failure was not due to a lack of solutions but a lack of will.

It’s the guns, stupid. It has always been the guns. Until we reckon with that truth and elect leaders who will act on it, the pews will keep emptying, the classrooms will keep bleeding, and the cycle of slaughter will grind on.
