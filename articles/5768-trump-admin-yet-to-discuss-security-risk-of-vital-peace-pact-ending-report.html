Trump admin yet to discuss security risk of vital peace pact ending: report

By Paulo Santos

A vital peace pact which prevents escalations in creating nuclear arms is set to end tomorrow (February 5).

Donald Trump’s administration has not reportedly worked on finding a solution to the treaty, with negotiations stalling last month and not picked up from there. According to those familiar with the peace treaty developments, the president and his advisers are yet to even hold a conversation about what to do about the impending deadline, let alone how to resolve it.

Writing in Slate, Fred Kaplan claimed those who knew of the New SMART expiry date were not in a position to bend to Trump’s demand that China be included in the next treaty arrangement. Such a suggestion could take years, according to Kaplan.

He wrote, “If past is precedent, a new treaty would take at least a year to negotiate; if China takes part, something that has never happened before, it would take many years.

“In the meantime, we may well see the renewal of a nuclear arms race, reversing a trend of the past half-century. The stunning thing is that, by all accounts, Trump and his advisers haven’t so much as held a conversation about the possibility or its implications for U.S. policy or the safety of the world.”

Trump was flippant when asked about the treaty last month, saying, “If it expires, it expires. We’ll do a better agreement.

“It’s worth recalling that when Trump scuttled the Iran nuclear deal back during his first term as president, he said that he—master of the “art of the deal”—would goad Tehran into accepting a ‘better’ deal.

“This never happened. There is no reason to believe, especially given Washington’s tense relations with both Moscow and Beijing, that he’ll bring about a superior substitute for New START either.”

An ex-Pentagon official had previously warned the expiration of the treaty may bolster Russia and its allies. Kingston Reiff warned the new START deal had offered valuable insight into what Russia had been doing with its military.

He wrote, “So, my net assessment is the treaty reduced uncertainty about Russian strategic nuclear forces and provided us with greater confidence in our own nuclear plans and capabilities.

“Since New START’s entry into force, there has been no real progress on further arms control measures. Moscow and Beijing deserve most of the blame for this. Charting a course to the next chapter will not be easy, but remains a necessary pursuit.”

Our Analysis:

The Precipice of Nuclear Proliferation: Trump’s Disregard for New START

In the shadow of looming nuclear uncertainty, the Trump administration’s apathy toward the imminent expiration of the New Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty (New START) is a chilling reminder of the fragile state of global security. The treaty, a cornerstone of nuclear arms control, faces oblivion on February 5, with the current U.S. administration seemingly indifferent to its fate. This lackadaisical approach not only undermines decades of nuclear disarmament efforts but also teeters on the brink of reigniting an arms race with unforeseeable consequences.

The Art of Forgetting the Deal

Donald Trump’s cavalier attitude towards vital international security measures is not a new spectacle. His administration’s failure to engage in meaningful dialogue to extend New START is emblematic of a broader disregard for established arms control frameworks. Trump’s flippant response, “If it expires, it expires. We’ll do a better agreement,” echoes his unfounded confidence when dismantling the Iran nuclear deal. Yet, the stark absence of a “better” deal with Tehran should have been a harrowing lesson in the complexities of nuclear negotiations. The insistence on including China in a future treaty—a demand that complicates negotiations to a Sisyphean task—reveals either a gross underestimation of diplomatic intricacies or a deliberate sabotage of disarmament efforts.

Misplaced Blame and Lost Opportunities

The article attributes the stalling of further arms control measures partially to Moscow and Beijing, yet this overlooks the elephant in the room: the U.S.’s own faltering commitment to diplomacy. While it is convenient to cast blame on traditional adversaries, it is the U.S. that stands at the precipice, ready to dismantle a treaty that has, as former Pentagon official Kingston Reiff notes, provided “greater confidence in our own nuclear plans and capabilities” and reduced uncertainties about Russian strategic forces.

The potential expiration of New START without a successor not only emboldens adversaries but also signals to the world a dangerous pivot away from collective security. The breakdown of such treaties erodes the very architecture of nuclear disarmament and non-proliferation, setting a perilous precedent for international relations and stability.

The Silence of Strategy

The Trump administration’s silence on the matter is deafening. The absence of discussions, as highlighted by Fred Kaplan’s observations, underscores a startling detachment from the gravity of the situation. The world stands to witness a revival of the nuclear arms race, a specter thought to be relegated to the annals of history, yet now a looming threat due to strategic apathy.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The expiration of New START without a clear path forward is a systemic failure of the Trump administration’s foreign policy and a testament to its flawed approach to international security. As the clock ticks down, the need for urgent, pragmatic dialogue has never been more critical. The administration must abandon its cavalier and isolationist tendencies in favor of genuine diplomatic engagement. Failure to do so not only jeopardizes decades of arms control progress but also imperils global security in an age where the specter of nuclear conflict should belong to a bygone era. The world cannot afford to backslide into the shadows of nuclear proliferation; the stakes are simply too high.
