'Time may not be on his side': Dictator expert claims age could shatter Trump's plans

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Paradox: A Dictatorship Derailed by Incompetence and Decline

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

With multiple experts agreeing that Donald Trump is using every page out of the authoritarian playbook to seize more and more power, a professor who specializes in the rise and rule of dictators suggested there is every reason to believe he may not live long enough to finish the job.

In a column for The Bulwark, Lee Morgenbesser of the School of Government and International Relations, Griffith University in Australia, claimed the 79-year-old American president, who increasingly shows signs of mental and physical decline, wavers back and forth from fully being aware of what he’s doing and occasional signs of dementia.

More to the point, he wrote: “He is alternately a conniving destroyer of Republican institutions or a mentally and emotionally debilitated, increasingly senile wackjob.”

According to Morgenbesser, Trump has not been able to succeed at getting a stranglehold on power because, while he may be using all the tools previous dictators deploy, he’s simply not good at it due to his limitations.

"Trump is merely implementing the authoritarian playbook poorly,” he observed. “Like a high school squad trying to run the same plays as an NFL team, the play sheet can be identical but the results won’t look the same. Early in Trump’s first term, Benjamin Wittes dubbed this synthesis ‘malevolence tempered by incompetence.’”

Saying Trump lacks the skill of a “top-tier destroyer of democracy,” he claimed his impetuousness is making it harder in his second term to keep a substantial number of people by his side.

Then there is his health, which raised alarms on Labor Day weekend when Trump uncharacteristically went silent and was only photographed from a distance.

“Time may not be on Trump’s side,” he suggested. “He is already 79, showing signs of deteriorating health, and liable to be under considerable stress for the foreseeable future. And he does not seem mentally robust, which is too bad for him and too bad for the United States.”

He then warned, “While elected autocrats can numb citizens to their actions by generating economic growth, stoking nationalist fervor, and providing political stability, mad kings wreck just about everything they touch.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Paradox: A Dictatorship Derailed by Incompetence and Decline

In a recent column for The Bulwark, Lee Morgenbesser, a professor from the School of Government and International Relations at Griffith University in Australia, draws a harrowing portrait of Donald Trump's presidency. He posits that while Trump has seemingly leafed through every page of the authoritarian playbook, his quest for absolute power is thwarted not by a resilient democracy, but by his own glaring incompetencies and possible health issues. Let's dissect this intriguing premise.

Trump's Authoritarian Ambitions vs. Reality

Morgenbesser's assertion that Trump is utilizing the "authoritarian playbook" is hardly groundbreaking but remains profoundly disturbing. The idea that a former American president has designs on power that mirror those of history's most notorious dictators is a chilling commentary on the current state of American politics. However, the twist in Morgenbesser's narrative is Trump's ineffectiveness at this dark craft. Describing him as "alternately a conniving destroyer of Republican institutions or a mentally and emotionally debilitated, increasingly senile wackjob," Morgenbesser doesn't mince words. This duality presents a unique danger: unpredictability rooted in incompetence.

Incompetence: The Unexpected Safeguard?

The concept of "malevolence tempered by incompetence," originally coined by Benjamin Wittes, encapsulates Trump's tenure. It's a peculiar form of reassurance, suggesting that Trump's lack of a "top-tier destroyer of democracy" skill set has inadvertently protected the nation from his more destructive tendencies. This is not to diminish the damage done but to highlight an ironic twist of fate. Trump's inability to effectively marshal the levers of power and maintain alliances due to impulsiveness and erratic behavior has blunted his authoritarian aspirations.

The Health Factor

Morgenbesser's mention of Trump's health adds another layer to this complex narrative. At 79, with visible signs of both mental and physical decline, Trump's capacity to wreak havoc is naturally diminishing. This is not a celebration of an individual's ill health but a critical factor in assessing the threat level of his political machinations. The silence over Labor Day weekend and the limited public appearances signal a potential decline that could influence his political future and, by extension, the American political landscape.

The Danger of "Mad Kings"

The final warning from Morgenbesser about "mad kings" is perhaps the most ominous. The ability of elected autocrats to "numb citizens to their actions" through various strategies is well-documented. Yet, the instability brought by leaders who are both mentally and physically compromised can lead to unpredictable and often destructive outcomes. This unpredictability could be as damaging to the fabric of American democracy as any concerted effort to undermine it.

Conclusion

Morgenbesser's analysis offers a stark yet oddly reassuring perspective on Trump's authoritarian tendencies. The reassurance comes not from faith in the system's checks and balances but rather from Trump's own deficiencies as a would-be autocrat. However, this should not lead to complacency. The very notion that a leader's incompetence is a safeguard against tyranny underlines the precarious state of American democracy. As we move forward, the focus must be on strengthening democratic institutions to ensure they can withstand threats from leaders more competent and less hindered by personal failings than Trump. Let's not rely on the next would-be dictator's incompetence to save us.
