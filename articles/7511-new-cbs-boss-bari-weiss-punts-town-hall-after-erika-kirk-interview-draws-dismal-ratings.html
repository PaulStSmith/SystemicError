New CBS boss Bari Weiss punts town hall after Erika Kirk interview draws dismal ratings

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Arizona, USA - September 25 2025: Erika Kirk’s Love Story With Charlie and What’s Next for Turning Point. (Photo credit: Saku_rata160520 / Shutterstock)

CBS News staffers were spared an end-of-the-year all-hands meeting with the head of the news division after a much-hyped town hall with Erika Kirk, widow of TPUSA founder Charlie Kirk, produced poor ratings and criticism from a conservative news outlet.

Bari Weiss, the former New York Times opinion writer appointed by Donald Trump-friendly Paramount Skydance CEO David Ellison to oversee CBS News editorial direction, personally hosted the town hall with Kirk on Saturday night. Her decision to conduct the interview herself, rather than utilize existing CBS on-air journalists, initially drew skepticism.

Despite extensive promotional efforts, the broadcast failed to attract substantial viewership. According to Nielsen ratings data reported by Justin Baragona, the Kirk interview drew approximately 1.5 million viewers, representing an 11 percent decline in total viewership compared to CBS’s standard programming for that time slot throughout 2025. The key demographic age 25-54 saw a 41 percent drop.

Saturday nights at 8 p.m. ET typically represent one of broadcast television’s lowest-watched prime time hours. However, CBS has generally averaged at least 2 million viewers during that slot over the past year, with 2.109 million total viewers and 449,000 in the target demographic throughout 2025.

Following the disappointing ratings, Weiss canceled a planned all-hands town hall previously scheduled for this month, according to media watchdog Status reporter Natalie Korach.

“According to multiple people familiar with the matter, Weiss decided not to hold the previously planned internal town hall this month, opting instead to push it to the new year. The decision came just as the network was grappling with underwhelming ratings for a broadcast it had heavily promoted as a signal of its new editorial direction.”

The New York Post amplified the embarrassment with coverage stating, “Bari Weiss’ town hall with Erika Kirk saw ratings plummet as CBS News editor-in-chief debuts on-screen.” The negative coverage proved particularly damaging, especially given that CBS’s sub-2 million prime-time audience followed the Army-Navy football game, which had averaged 7.3 million viewers in the preceding hours.

Our Analysis:

A Misguided Editorial Gamble: The CBS News Conundrum

The recent debacle surrounding CBS News under the guidance of Bari Weiss, the former New York Times opinion writer turned editorial overseer, is a glaring testament to the perils of misaligned leadership and a profound misunderstanding of audience engagement. The decision to host a much-hyped town hall with Erika Kirk, widow of TPUSA founder Charlie Kirk, which resulted in dismal ratings and widespread criticism, serves as a cautionary tale for media entities everywhere.

The Weiss Factor: A Questionable Decision

Bari Weiss, appointed by the Trump-friendly Paramount Skydance CEO David Ellison, took it upon herself to host the town hall with Kirk. This decision was puzzling, not least because of the available pool of seasoned CBS on-air journalists who were overlooked. It smacks of a vanity project, a stark deviation from journalistic norms where the story, not the storyteller, should command attention. This self-indulgent approach did little more than raise eyebrows and skepticism from the outset.

Ratings Plummet: A Stark Reality Check

Despite extensive promotional efforts, the broadcast’s performance was underwhelming, to say the least. Drawing approximately 1.5 million viewers, the town hall saw an 11 percent decline in total viewership compared to CBS’s standard programming for that time slot throughout 2025. Even more damning was the 41 percent drop in the key demographic age 25-54. For a network that has managed to maintain at least 2 million viewers during its Saturday 8 p.m. ET slot over the past year, these figures are not just disappointing; they’re indicative of a profound disconnect between CBS News’s new editorial direction and its audience’s expectations.

The Aftermath: Cancellation and Embarrassment

In the wake of this ratings fiasco, Weiss’s decision to cancel a planned all-hands town hall is telling. It suggests a retreat, a reluctance to face the music internally just as much as externally. Natalie Korach of the media watchdog Status reports this move as a direct response to grappling with the underwhelming broadcast performance. Moreover, the negative coverage from the New York Post, highlighting the ratings plummet, further cements the narrative of a failed experiment.

Analysis: Missteps and Mismanagement

CBS News’s flirtation with a new editorial direction under Weiss has, thus far, been a lesson in what not to do. A town hall that was heavily promoted as a signal of its new editorial direction failed not merely because of its content but because of the hubris in its execution. Opting to use this platform as a stage for Weiss rather than leveraging the seasoned talent within CBS News was a misstep that speaks to a larger issue of mismanagement.

The Path Forward: Reevaluation and Realignment

The path forward for CBS News requires a thorough reevaluation of its editorial direction and leadership approach. Engagement with the audience, understanding their preferences, and respecting the journalistic integrity and expertise of its staff should be paramount. Leadership in news organizations must be grounded in journalistic experience and a keen understanding of audience needs, not personal branding exercises.

As CBS News charts its course, let this episode serve as a stark reminder of the responsibilities media outlets have to their audiences and the dangers of straying too far from the principles of journalism in pursuit of ratings or rebranding. The lesson here is clear: leadership and content decisions should be made with the audience’s interests at heart, not the egos at the helm.
