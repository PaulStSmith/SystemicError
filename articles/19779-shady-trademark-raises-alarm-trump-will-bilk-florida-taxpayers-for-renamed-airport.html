Shady trademark raises alarm Trump will bilk Florida taxpayers for renamed airport

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 21, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trumpian Branding Crusade: A Palm Beach Saga

President Donald Trump looks on as he exits Air Force One on his arrival at Palm Beach International Airport in West Palm Beach, Florida, January 31, 2026. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

The way is clear for Palm Beach County International Airport to be renamed after Donald Trump, but despite his claims, suspicions remain that taxpayers will be footing the bill for the latest example of his name-changing crusade.

The Republican-controlled Florida State House recently passed a bill that will rename the airport closest to the president’s Mar-a-Lago residence to “Donald J. Trump International Airport.” According to a Friday report from USA Today, Trump and the Trump Organization have insisted that the county will be allowed to use his name for the airport “at no charge,” further citing the recently renamed “President Donald J. Trump Boulevard” as an example of his willingness to offer up his name to his home county.

Despite that assurance, a registration at the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office around the same time as the Florida State House vote has given observers reason to doubt those claims. In the filing, the Trump Organization trademarked the title, “President Donald J. Trump International Airport.” It also trademarked the president’s initials, “DJT,” which would be the most likely new code for the airport.

Opponents of Trump and the name change argue that, with these trademarks in play, Palm Beach County would be required to pay a licensing fee to use its impending new name. The Trump Organization has further insisted that it would “not receive any royalty, licensing fee or financial consideration whatsoever from the proposed airport renaming,” according to USA Today, and the State House bill contains language to that effect. Some critics, however, remain unconvinced.

“I don’t understand why this continues to be an issue,” state Rep. Meg Weinberger, a Palm Beach Gardens Republican who co-sponsored the airport bill. The language, she stressed, grants the county “the perpetual and unrestricted right to use the name ‘President Donald J. Trump International Airport,’” at no cost for things like “signage, advertising, marketing, merchandising, and promotions and for the branding of the airport and its operations,” USA Today noted.

State Rep. Kelly Skidmore, a Boca Raton Democrat, argued that despite the bill’s wording, the county could still potentially be charged licensing fees. She also questioned why the Trump organization trademarked an airport name in the first place if this was not their intention. The organization claimed that the filing was merely meant to protect Trump’s name from being infringed upon.

Other critics of the bill have decried the name change being pushed through without a suitable period for public comment. Some also say that the matter should not have been raised in the first place until Trump leaves office.

Our Analysis:

The Trumpian Branding Crusade: A Palm Beach Saga

In what could only be described as a masterstroke of ego-driven branding, the saga of renaming Palm Beach County International Airport to “Donald J. Trump International Airport” unfolds with all the subtlety of a sledgehammer at a glassware convention. Let’s dissect this latest episode in the ongoing series of Trumpian exploits with the clinical precision it deserves.

The Claim of Cost-Free Renaming

First, we’re fed the narrative that this grand gesture of renaming the airport closest to Mar-a-Lago will come at no charge to the taxpayers. A magnanimous offer, indeed, if it weren’t so shrouded in a fog of suspicion and trademark registrations. The Trump Organization, in a move that could outmaneuver Machiavelli himself, insists that Palm Beach County can use the Trump name gratis, reminiscent of the “President Donald J. Trump Boulevard.” At face value, it’s a deal too good to refuse—except for the pesky detail of a trademark filing for the very same name, casting a long shadow over the veracity of this “free” offer.

Trademark Troubles

The plot thickens with the revelation that the Trump Organization, in a timing that could only be described as conveniently coincidental, has trademarked “President Donald J. Trump International Airport” and even the president’s initials, “DJT.” This maneuver sends a clear signal: while the name might be “free,” the strings attached come in the form of legal trademarks. Critics argue this could potentially lock Palm Beach County into paying licensing fees, despite the Trump Organization’s claims to the contrary. The situation reeks of a bait-and-switch, leaving us to wonder: how free is free?

Legislative Language and Loopholes

Enter the Florida State House, which, with a Republican majority, has pushed through the bill for the airport’s renaming with language that supposedly grants the county “perpetual and unrestricted right” to the Trump name. Yet, skepticism remains, fueled by the trademark filing and the Trump Organization’s history of fiercely protecting its brand. State Rep. Kelly Skidmore raises a valid point: if the intention was truly benign, why the need to trademark the airport name? This discrepancy between public assurances and legal maneuvers paints a picture of a strategy designed to maintain leverage over the county’s use of the Trump name.

Public Sentiment and Process

Amidst the legal and political machinations, the voice of the public seems to have been sidelined. Critics of the bill decry the lack of a suitable period for public comment, a crucial step in democratic processes, especially for a decision with such symbolic weight. The rush to rename the airport, coupled with the absence of public discourse, only adds to the controversy, suggesting a top-down decision-making process that prioritizes branding over community consent.

Conclusion: A Masterclass in Branding or a Cautionary Tale?

The renaming of Palm Beach County International Airport to “Donald J. Trump International Airport” is more than a mere change of signage; it’s a case study in the complex interplay of politics, branding, and public policy. While the Trump Organization and its allies may tout the renaming as a cost-free tribute, the underlying trademark strategies and bypassing of public discourse reveal a different agenda. It’s a stark reminder of the power of branding and the lengths to which individuals and organizations will go to protect and proliferate their namesake, often at the expense of transparent and democratic processes.

As this saga unfolds, one must ask: is the public witnessing a masterclass in strategic branding or a cautionary tale of overreach and transparency in governance? Only time—and perhaps, the fine print of trademark laws—will tell.
