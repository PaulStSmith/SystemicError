The electric vehicle maker will release its first quarter financial reports Tuesday in a conference call after the markets close, but the company's stock has already plummeted 50 percent from its record high as Musk aligns himself with Trump. "Morning Joe" co-host Jonathan Lemire said the Tesla CEO may make some news on that call.

"Speaking of Musk, we should note today there's a Tesla earnings call," Lemire said. "Some people believe that Musk in that call might reveal how much longer he's going to stay working for the administration."

Host Joe Scarborough said the damage might be hard to undo.

"Their problem is, part of the problem is a political problem," Scarborough said. "Obviously, the very areas where he used to get the most, you know, whether you look at California or, let's just say, bluer states where he'd find most of his [electric vehicle] customers, those cells are drying up.

"You look at France, you look at Germany, you look at the EV sales in Europe getting absolutely pounded because, again, he got involved unnecessarily in far-right politics to little to no effect. In fact, God, have you looked at the numbers in Canada lately? I mean, [Prime Minister Mark] Carney is going to probably win an outright majority there. You look at what's happened across Europe? It's not working."

"All he's doing is damaging his brand, and then China, I mean, if this were just politics, that would be one problem with Tesla," Scarborough added. "The problem is he's getting lapped in China on technology.

"I'm not giving Elon Musk advice, but ... I think it is time to go back to your day job. I mean, he's getting pounded, Tesla is getting routed."

Our Analysis:

The Fall of a Titan? Elon Musk's Political Ventures and Their Impact on Tesla

Joe Scarborough's recent critique of Elon Musk on MSNBC casts a glaring spotlight on the dangers of intertwining personal political affiliations with business operations, especially when the business in question, Tesla, sits at the pinnacle of innovation but on the precipice of potential decline. Musk's apparent alignment with controversial political figures and ideologies seems not only to have muddied his personal brand but, by extension, Tesla's market valuation and global appeal.

The Political Quagmire: Scarborough points out a fundamental issue that's hard to ignore: Musk's political maneuvering is alienating a significant portion of his customer base. It's an elementary business principle that alienating your core market is akin to corporate sabotage. In regions like California and various "bluer states," where environmental consciousness and progressive politics often go hand in hand, Musk's political affiliations are particularly jarring. The backlash is evident in declining sales, a clear signal that consumer loyalty can swiftly pivot when a brand's values no longer align with its customers'.

The European Exodus: Scarborough's observation extends beyond American borders, highlighting a stark downturn in European electric vehicle (EV) sales. This isn't merely a reflection of Musk's political dalliances but a potent example of global consumer responsiveness to corporate ethics and affiliations. Europe's stringent stance on environmental issues and its typically progressive political landscape make it a critical battleground for any EV maker, especially Tesla. Musk's political posturing, therefore, doesn't just risk American sales but threatens Tesla's global market share.

The Chinese Conundrum: The mention of China brings a different dimension to Tesla's troubles. Here, the issue transcends politics, touching on technological competition and innovation. China's rapid advancement in the EV sector poses a significant threat to Tesla, not because of politics, but due to sheer technological evolution and market saturation. Musk's political distractions could be diverting essential focus from this critical front, where innovation and adaptation are key to survival.

Scarborough's Unheeded Advice: The conclusion drawn by Scarborough, suggesting Musk should "go back to your day job," is not just a flippant remark but a dire warning. It underscores a pivotal moment for Musk and Tesla: the need to recalibrate focus away from the political arena and back to the core of Tesla's success — innovation, sustainability, and technological advancement.

In essence, the intertwining of Musk's political ventures with Tesla's operational ethos serves as a cautionary tale of how personal politics, when allowed to permeate professional realms, can have far-reaching consequences. The plummeting stock value, the drying up of traditionally loyal customer bases, and the intensifying global competition underscore a critical juncture for Tesla. Will Musk heed the warning signs and steer his company away from the political storm, or will Tesla's trajectory continue to be clouded by the controversies of its CEO? Only time will tell, but the current narrative suggests a need for a significant course correction if Tesla aims to maintain its leadership and innovative edge in the global EV market.
