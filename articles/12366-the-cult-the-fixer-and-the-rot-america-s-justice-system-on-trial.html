The Cult, the Fixer, and the Rot: America's Justice System on Trial

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

It would be comforting to believe that the United States justice system is blind, that the scales of justice are balanced no matter how much wealth, power, or political influence one carries. But the theater playing out in real time before the world is not a courtroom drama. It is a grotesque farce, one in which accountability is a prop and impunity the star of the show.

A recent image captures the state of decay with haunting clarity. As Ghislaine Maxwell's attorney, David Markus, addresses the press, a woman stands in the background holding a handmade sign. Its message is blistering:

Help fuel the work. Independent analysis doesn’t fund itself:
☕ Buy us a coffee

"Trump is a sexual predator/pedophile. Todd Blanche is here as Trump's atty/fixer. Are y'all ok with that?"

This isn't hyperbole. It's not hysteria. It’s an act of civil courage in a nation where truth has become radical, and moral clarity a threat to power.

Let’s dissect the sign’s message and what it reveals.

Trump, Epstein, and the Convenient Amnesia

Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein were not strangers. They were, by Trump’s own words, close. In a 2002 quote dug up repeatedly, Trump boasted:

“I’ve known Jeff for fifteen years. Terrific guy. He’s a lot of fun to be with. It is even said that he likes beautiful women as much as I do, and many of them are on the younger side.”

Yet as Epstein's crimes came to light and Ghislaine Maxwell was convicted for her role in the trafficking of minors, Trump skated away untouched. Not questioned, not subpoenaed, not forced to answer for his proximity to one of the most notorious sex traffickers in modern history.

How? Because America’s legal system doesn’t prosecute the powerful. It launders them.

Enter Todd Blanche: The Legal Laundromat Specialist

Blanche is not just any lawyer. He is a fixer in the vein of Roy Cohn and Michael Cohen, the men who made Trump's career possible by bullying the system into compliance.

Blanche represented Paul Manafort, Trump's former campaign chair, who was convicted for tax and bank fraud and later pardoned by Trump himself. Now, he serves as Trump’s lead attorney in a multitude of criminal and civil proceedings. The revolving door of corruption keeps spinning, and Blanche is pushing the rotation.

His presence at Maxwell's legal proceedings is not just coincidental—it is symbolic. It shows the extent of Trumpworld's infiltration into every crevice of legal accountability. Blanche's mere proximity to these characters isn't damning; it's expected. The legal profession is saturated with mercenaries, and Blanche is one of the elite.

A Cult, Not a Movement

Let’s stop pretending that Trumpism is a political ideology. It is a cult of personality, built on grievance, ignorance, and the worship of unchecked power. When nearly half the country can look at a man credibly accused by dozens of women, who has paid off porn stars, lied under oath, and openly bragged about assault, and still cry witch hunt, you know the body politic has gangrene.

There is no line Trump can cross that will make his followers flinch. There is no atrocity too egregious, no scandal too damning. His base will follow him into moral oblivion, dragging what remains of America's institutions behind them.

The Judicial Rot Is the Point

What’s worse than the crimes are the systemic enablers. The judges who look the other way. The prosecutors who slow-walk investigations. The media outlets that chase ratings instead of truth. The defense attorneys who make six figures weaponizing process over principle.

In the shadow of Epstein and Maxwell, one would expect a reckoning. Instead, we get selective memory and deliberate apathy. We get headlines about Hunter Biden's laptop and Hillary Clinton's emails, but silence when it comes to the real network of elite abusers.

The woman in the photo is an aberration. She is the exception, not the rule. Because she still believes that saying the truth out loud matters.

Impunity Is a National Pastime

Let’s speak plainly: the American justice system exists to punish the poor and protect the rich. It hands out life sentences for shoplifting under three-strikes laws, but delays and dismisses trials for the powerful under the guise of "complexity" and "fairness."

Trump isn’t merely exploiting the system. He is the system. He is the distilled essence of American exceptionalism: a rich white man who fails upward, assaults downward, and escapes consequences every time.

Every fixer, every enabler, every deferential judge and dithering prosecutor is part of the machinery that keeps him untouchable.

History Will Ask: What Did You Do?

The image of that woman holding her sign in silence is a moment of moral clarity. While the cameras are turned toward a man paid to lie, a woman in sunglasses dares to tell the truth.

And that’s the real tragedy. It takes anonymous protestors with cardboard signs to say what the media won’t. What prosecutors won’t. What judges refuse to.

The system is broken, yes. But more damningly, it is designed to be. And those who know better, and do nothing, are just as culpable as the monsters they defend.
