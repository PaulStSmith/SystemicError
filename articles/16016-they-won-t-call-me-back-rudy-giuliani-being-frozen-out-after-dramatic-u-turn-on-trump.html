Donald Trump confidante Rudy Giuliani says he can't get a call back from the White House after he started criticizing the President's top Cabinet officials.

Giuliani "made a dramatic U-turn over President Donald Trump's 'crooked' choices after years of working as his fixer," according to the Daily Mail. It cites Giuliani's recent interview with conservative pundit Eric Bolling.

"The 80-year-old, who is currently facing mounting legal woes, told Bolling that although he's pleased with changes at the Justice Department, he still has problems with 'crooked' choices made by FBI director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi," the outlet reported Saturday.

ALSO READ: 'Frustrated' Trump confused why 'emotional' Cabinet member always in White House: report

According to the report, "He specifically said he doesn't agree with the way Patel and Bondi have been 'going after Catholics in the Justice Department' and the January 6 participants."

The interview was also flagged by The Daily Beast, which published an article entitled, "Rudy Giuliani Whines About FBI Trump BFF Kash Patel is Running."

"No one at the Department of Justice (DOJ) or the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) will return Rudy Giuliani’s calls, and he’s really not happy about it," the outlet reported.

Both outlets quote the attorney as saying, "I tried to reach them today, they didn’t call me back."

"They say there’s an explanation for it. It better be damn good," he reportedly added.

"President Donald Trump’s former lawyer claims he was calling up FBI Director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi to complain about the DOJ and FBI supposedly appointing employees who investigated the Capitol Riots to top posts at the bureau’s Washington, D.C., office," according to the report. "It is a dramatic change of tone for a man who had praised Patel as one of Trump’s 'most consequential picks' and hugged and kissed Bondi on stage in 2018."

Our Analysis:

Giuliani's Grievances: A Tale of Disloyalty and Desperation

Rudy Giuliani, once the unshakeable consigliere to Donald Trump, finds himself adrift in a sea of ignominy, his calls to the White House echoing unanswered. The Daily Mail and The Daily Beast have chronicled Giuliani's fall from grace, painting a picture of a man who, after years of unwavering support for Trump and his administration, has suddenly found fault with the machinery he once helped grease.

Giuliani's critique centers on what he perceives as "crooked" choices within Trump's cabinet, specifically targeting FBI Director Kash Patel and Attorney General Pam Bondi. His complaints? A bizarre accusation that Patel and Bondi are unjustly targeting Catholics within the Justice Department and persecuting participants of the January 6 Capitol riots. This narrative, as Giuliani would have us believe, marks a stark departure from his previous adulations, notably his once glowing endorsement of Patel as one of Trump's "most consequential picks."

The Irony of Loyalty and Betrayal

The irony of Giuliani's sudden disaffection cannot be overstated. This is a man who, as Trump's "fixer," has been at the forefront of defending, justifying, and, by many accounts, enabling the President's most controversial actions. His transformation from Trump's staunchest defender to a critic of the administration's appointments reeks not of principled dissent but of a personal vendetta fueled by access denied.

Giuliani's legal woes—a convenient footnote in his complaints—loom large over this entire saga. As he faces mounting legal challenges, his estrangement from the White House and its power brokers suggests a man frantically looking for allies in a battle he knows he cannot win alone. His inability to reach Patel and Bondi, as reported, smacks of desperation—a Hail Mary from someone who once walked the corridors of power with impunity.

The Misguided Crusade Against "Crooked" Appointments

Giuliani's accusations against Patel and Bondi, particularly the alleged persecution of Catholics and January 6 defendants, are not only baseless but dangerously misleading. They represent a twisted view of justice, one where political allies are immune to scrutiny and the rule of law bends to the will of those in power. This perspective is not only flawed but perilous, undermining the very foundations of our justice system.

Furthermore, Giuliani's lament over the unreturned calls is emblematic of a broader issue within the Trump administration: a culture of loyalty over integrity. The fact that Giuliani expected his grievances to be addressed simply because of his close ties to Trump speaks volumes about the transactional nature of their relationship. It's a stark reminder of how access and influence have been commodified in Trump's Washington, with loyalty prized above all else—until, of course, it's no longer convenient.

Conclusion: The Fall of a "Fixer"

As Giuliani's saga unfolds, so too does the narrative of a man consumed by his own hubris. His fall from grace is not just a personal tragedy but a cautionary tale of what happens when loyalty to an individual supersedes commitment to principles. Giuliani's grievances, while cloaked in the language of righteousness, reveal a deeper truth about his—and by extension, Trump's—vision of governance: one where loyalty is the currency, and dissent is the greatest sin.

In the end, Giuliani's plight is a stark reminder of the transient nature of power and the perilous path of those who seek to wield it without regard for the consequences. His unanswered calls to the White House are not just a personal rebuke but a symbol of a broader systemic failure—one that prioritizes allegiance over accountability and personal gain over public good.
