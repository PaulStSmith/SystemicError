'MAGA hysteria': Death threat scandal said to reveal Trump allies' 'staggering hypocrisy'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

James Comey set off a firestorm of criticism from president Donald Trump's allies after posting a photo featuring the numbers "8647," but others called out their hypocrisy on social media.

The former FBI director shared a photo of an anti-Trump protest he spotted on a beach – "86" means to throw out or nix something, and "47" refers to Trump as the 47th president – and the U.S. Secret Service is investigating after Trump allies claimed the post was calling for his assassination.

But some of those same critics used similar language against former president Joe Biden or shared posts explicitly calling for violence against him

"There’s no hypocrisy in history like MAGA hypocrisy," said psychologist and author Dennis Lustig, sharing a photo of the right-wing influencer Jo's post featuring a decal of Biden gagged and bound in the back of a pickup, which was posted by Trump. "There’s no more worthless, despicable, and evil troglodytes than MAGAts. Our country will never recover from the damage they’ve inflicted. There’s no hell awful enough for every one of them."

"A story of staggering hypocrisy, in two tweets," posted Democratic strategist Matt McDermott, showing screenshots of two tweets by right-wing influencer Jack Posobiec accusing Comey of violent threats and an older one of his reading "86 46."

"Will @MarshaBlackburn ask for @amazon to be investigated for selling '8646' shirts and merch?" added the Tennessee Holler account. "Or is it only worthy of FBI investigations when it’s '8647'? We’ll hang up and listen."

"The MAGA hysteria over Comey's '8647' post is a test to see how gullible you are. Comey's post was a play on '8646,' a MAGA anti-Biden slogan," said the Capitol Hunters account. "If you watch MAGA rallies, you've seen '8646' - might even find Kash [Patel] wearing it on a podcast. Treat this op as the joke that it is."

"So MAGA freaks out when the former FBI director posts something that says '86 47,'" posted X user Harry Sisson. "But when Trump posts a video that includes an image of Biden being tied up and kidnapped, that’s acceptable? The hypocrisy is astounding."

"Apparently this has been a thing for quite a while, judging by Amazon," conceded conservative columnist Byron York, who found a way to remain mad about it several hours later in a subsequent post: "Comey, Trump and '86 47.' The resistance has used '86 45,’ now '86 47,’ for a while. Maybe it just means dump Trump. But there's no doubt it has a sinister, post-Butler feel to it since would-be assassins actually tried to kill him."

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of MAGA Outrage: A Case Study in Selective Offense

James Comey, the former FBI director whose very name seems to send shockwaves through the corridors of political discourse, has once again found himself at the epicenter of controversy. But this time, the uproar is not about leaked emails or investigations into presidential candidates. Instead, it's about a photo, a beach, and a set of numbers: "8647."

To the uninitiated, these numbers might seem innocuous, even random. However, in the charged atmosphere of American politics, they're anything but. "86" is widely understood to mean to get rid of something, while "47" represents Donald Trump, ostensibly the 47th president in this context. Comey's photo of an anti-Trump protest has ignited a firestorm among Trump's allies, who claim, with a straight face, that this is tantamount to a call for assassination. The U.S. Secret Service is even investigating these claims.

Let's pause for a moment to appreciate the irony here. These same individuals, who are now clutching their pearls over a beach photo, have previously engaged in or endorsed rhetoric and imagery that is explicitly violent towards former president Joe Biden. It's a stunning display of hypocrisy that hasn't gone unnoticed by social media users and political commentators alike.

"There’s no hypocrisy in history like MAGA hypocrisy," notes psychologist and author Dennis Lustig, pointing out the egregious double standards of Trump supporters. This sentiment is echoed across the digital landscape, with examples of MAGA-aligned figures engaging in precisely the type of behavior they now decry.

Democratic strategist Matt McDermott highlights this hypocrisy by contrasting the outrage over Comey's post with the silence—or even endorsement—of similar sentiments directed at Biden. The selective outrage is stark, revealing a political strategy that relies on manufactured indignation.

Even more damning is the revelation that "8646," an anti-Biden slogan, has been circulating for some time without eliciting the same level of concern from Trump's camp. This inconsistency lays bare the opportunistic nature of the outrage over Comey's post. It's not about the numbers or the implied message; it's about who's making the implication.

The MAGA response to Comey's "8647" post is a litmus test for gullibility, as suggested by the Capitol Hunters account. If the MAGA crowd were genuinely concerned about violent rhetoric, they would condemn it universally. Instead, their selective outrage serves as a tactic—a way to galvanize their base and demonize opponents.

The hypocrisy doesn't stop at the level of political strategists or social media influencers; it extends to the broader MAGA movement. Conservative columnist Byron York's begrudging acknowledgment of the "86 47" slogan’s existence, followed by an attempt to keep the controversy alive, underscores the movement's desperation to find—or fabricate—offenses where none exist.

In Conclusion:

The controversy over James Comey's "8647" post is a microcosm of the broader political landscape in the United States. It reveals a world where outrage is selectively applied, facts are malleable, and hypocrisy is the currency of the realm. The MAGA movement's response to the post is not rooted in genuine concern for political violence but in a cynical attempt to weaponize perceived slights for political gain.

As observers and participants in this political environment, our challenge is to see through the facade of manufactured outrage. We must recognize these tactics for what they are: desperate attempts to distract and divide. The real scandal here is not a photo on a beach; it's the willingness of a political movement to embrace hypocrisy as a strategy. In the end, the numbers "8647" may indeed signify a need to "throw out" something—but perhaps not in the way Trump's allies fear.
