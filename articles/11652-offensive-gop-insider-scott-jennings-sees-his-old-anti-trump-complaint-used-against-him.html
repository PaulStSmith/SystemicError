'Offensive': GOP insider Scott Jennings sees his old anti-Trump complaint used against him

By Paulo Santos

CNN screenshot

GOP strategist Scott Jennings erupted on Wednesday night after a panelist on CNN noted something unsavory about the president's latest move.

On Tuesday, President Donald Trump deployed more than 1,000 federal troops to Washington, D.C., to address local crime. He described the move as "Liberation Day" and said the military officers were going to "take our capital back." That is despite federal data showing that crime in the city is at a 30-year low.

During CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip," Democrat strategist Julie Roginsky recalled another time when there was a lot of crime in DC. She also noted that Trump chose not to deploy federal troops back then.

"I guess I'm old enough, Scott, to remember when you were appalled, as the rest of us were, on January 6 when he wouldn't..." Roginsky said before Jennings cut her off.

"So, because January 6 happened, we shouldn't enforce the law today?" Jennings shot back. "You want murders today?"

Roginsky, Jennings, panelist Jillian Michaels, and host Abby Phillip then began shouting over one another.

"It's a silly argument!" Jennings shouted.

"Just let her finish, Scott," Phillip said. "We haven't even heard the argument yet."

"She keeps taking pop shots at me, and I'm not going to allow it!" Jennings said. "It's a stupid argument, and I'm not going to allow it."

After the argument subsided, Roginsky said Trump could have deployed the National Guard on January 6, but chose not to.

"We all know that he's doing this because it's a power grab," Roginsky said. "He could have done it when this district was actually in danger on January 6, but he didn't. And I think that is what is so offensive."

"He talks about backing the blue or backing law enforcement, but he doesn't give a d----," Roginsky added.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of Politics: A Critical Look at Trump's "Liberation Day" and the GOP's Defensive Stance

In a recent eruption of political theater on CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip," GOP strategist Scott Jennings showcased the kind of diversionary tactics that have become all too common in our current political discourse. Amidst discussions of President Donald Trump's controversial decision to deploy over 1,000 federal troops to Washington, D.C., under the guise of "Liberation Day," the debate quickly devolved into a cacophony of shouting, accusations, and the all-too-familiar refusal to engage with inconvenient truths.

The Context: Trump's "Liberation Day"

President Trump's announcement of deploying federal troops to tackle crime in D.C.—despite federal data indicating a 30-year low in crime rates—reeks of political maneuvering rather than genuine concern for public safety. Describing the move as an effort to "take our capital back" is a dramatic flourish that seems to serve more as a dog whistle to his base than a coherent policy decision. This action, starkly contrasted with his inaction during the actual crisis of January 6, when a violent mob stormed the Capitol, highlights a disturbing pattern of selective law enforcement based on political convenience rather than the rule of law.

The Clash: A Microcosm of a Larger Problem

The heated exchange between Democrat strategist Julie Roginsky and Scott Jennings is emblematic of the broader polarization and inability to engage in substantive debate plaguing our political landscape. Roginsky's attempt to point out the inconsistency in Trump's stance on law enforcement was immediately met with defensiveness and interruptions from Jennings, who resorted to calling the argument "silly" and "stupid" without addressing the underlying issue.

Jennings's rhetorical question, "So, because January 6 happened, we shouldn't enforce the law today?" is a glaring attempt to deflect from the real criticism: that Trump's current actions are not about law enforcement but rather a "power grab," as Roginsky aptly puts it. The refusal to even let Roginsky finish her point underscores a disturbing trend in political discourse where loudness trumps logic, and scoring points trumps seeking truth.

The Underlying Issue: Selective Enforcement and Political Power Plays

What is truly offensive, as Roginsky points out, is not just the hypocrisy of Trump's actions but the blatant disregard for the principles of law enforcement and public safety he claims to champion. The decision to deploy federal troops in D.C. under current circumstances, while failing to do so when the Capitol was under assault, speaks volumes about the motivations driving such actions. It's not about "backing the blue" or ensuring public safety; it's about wielding power and stoking the fires of political division.

In Conclusion: The Need for Genuine Dialogue

The exchange on CNN, while perhaps entertaining to some, is a disheartening reflection of the state of political debate in America. When strategists and politicians prioritize winning arguments over addressing real issues, the public discourse is impoverished, and the potential for meaningful progress is stifled.

As the dust settles on this latest episode of political theater, it's clear that what we need is not more troops on the streets or strategists shouting down their opponents on national television. What we need is a return to principled, fact-based dialogue where the complexities of issues like law enforcement, public safety, and the role of federal intervention in local affairs can be discussed openly and honestly.

Until then, we remain trapped in a cycle of systemic error, where the truth is drowned out by the noise of the spectacle, and the real losers are the American people, who deserve far better from their leaders and media.
