Trump denies report that golfing buddy is carving up Venezuela for himself in frantic post

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 13, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of the Denial: Trump, Sargeant, and the Venezuelan Oil Saga

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump denounced one of his purported golfing buddies who’s being credited with steering U.S. efforts to revive Venezuela’s oil industry.

The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday night that energy magnate Harry Sargeant III, who the paper described as a 68-year-old former Top Gun pilot and sometimes Trump golfing partner, was perhaps the only American businessman with ties to the U.S. president and recently deposed Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro.

“Sargeant is now in position to be the latest of the president’s allies to reap a windfall based on his second-term policies and actions,” the Journal reported.

“Trump is pressing U.S. firms to move in fast as his aides work to loosen the sanctions restrictions that were imposed on Venezuela during his first administration,” the report added. “Many U.S. companies are nonetheless proceeding cautiously, wary of investing huge sums until the country’s politics are stabilized and a legal framework is in place for foreign companies ... Sargeant, though, isn’t waiting. He met in person last week in Caracas with Maduro’s longtime deputy and economic manager Delcy Rodriguez, to discuss plans to get his businesses up and running.”

The 79-year-old Trump denied the Journal’s claims about Sargeant’s efforts in Venezuela since U.S. forces removed Maduro and handed him over to U.S. law enforcement to stand trial on narcoterrorism and firearms charges.

“Relations between Venezuela and the United States have been, to put it mildly, extraordinary!” Trump posted Thursday morning on Truth Social. “We are dealing very well with President Delcy Rodriguez, and her Representatives. Oil is starting to flow, and large amounts of money, unseen for many years, will soon be greatly helping the people of Venezuela. Marco Rubio, and all of our Representatives, are doing a fantastic job, but we speak only for ourselves, and don’t want there to be any confusion or misrepresentation.”

“There is a story about a man named Harry Sargeant III in The Wall Street Journal,” the president added. “He has no authority, in any way, shape, or form, to act on behalf of the United States of America, nor does anyone else that is not approved by the State Department. Without this approval, no one is authorized to represent our Country. Thank you for your attention to this matter! PRESIDENT DONALD J. TRUMP.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Denial: Trump, Sargeant, and the Venezuelan Oil Saga

In a story that reads like a geopolitical thriller, President Donald Trump’s ties to Harry Sargeant III, an energy tycoon and alleged golfing buddy, have thrust themselves into the limelight, courtesy of a revealing Wall Street Journal article. This piece, seething with implications, suggests that Sargeant is positioning himself to exploit Venezuelan oil amidst the chaotic backdrop of U.S. foreign policy. Trump’s response? A swift, emphatic denial. But as we slice through the rhetoric, the situation begs for a more piercing analysis.

The Power Players: Trump and Sargeant

At the heart of this narrative are two figures: Donald Trump, the ever-controversial U.S. president, and Harry Sargeant III, a character whose resume reads like an action hero’s—former Top Gun pilot turned energy magnate. The Wall Street Journal paints Sargeant as a man on a mission, leveraging his ties to Trump and the newly deposed Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro. The insinuation? That Sargeant stands to gain immensely from Trump’s second-term policies, particularly the loosening of sanctions on Venezuela. Yet, Trump’s vehement denial on Truth Social throws a wrench into this neatly woven narrative.

The Venezuelan Conundrum

Venezuela’s oil, a resource cursed by its abundance and the geopolitical chess game it incites, sits at the center of this quagmire. The Journal’s report highlights a key tension: while U.S. firms are hesitant to dive into Venezuelan waters without political stability and a legal framework for foreign investments, Sargeant is depicted as boldly meeting with Maduro’s deputy, Delcy Rodriguez, ready to ignite his ventures. Trump’s administration, meanwhile, is portrayed as facilitating this environment, eager to see U.S. energy firms replace the vacuum left by sanctions—a maneuver Trump seems to champion as a boon for the Venezuelan people and a testament to his administration’s prowess.

Trump’s Denial: A Closer Look

Trump’s denial on Truth Social is a masterpiece of deflection. By praising the current state of U.S.-Venezuela relations and denying Sargeant’s authority to act on behalf of the U.S., Trump attempts to sever the ties that the Wall Street Journal’s report binds him with. Yet, this denial raises more questions than it answers. If Sargeant has “no authority,” as Trump insists, what explains the energy magnate’s audacious moves in Venezuela?

Moreover, Trump’s statement paradoxically acknowledges and dismisses the issue. He notes, “There is a story about a man named Harry Sargeant III,” a line that subtly undercuts the gravity of the accusations by relegating them to mere ‘story’ status, while simultaneously confirming the narrative’s reach and significance. The denial is classic Trump: dismissive, yet indirectly affirming the narrative’s impact.

The Unseen Hand: U.S. Foreign Policy in Latin America

This saga underscores a larger pattern of U.S. involvement in Latin American politics and economics—a history marred by opportunism and exploitation. Trump’s dismissal of Sargeant’s role, whether factual or not, doesn’t detract from the broader trend: the U.S.’s eagerness to capitalize on Latin America’s political instability for economic gain. Whether through direct intervention or the facilitation of private interests like Sargeant’s, the end game appears consistent—exploitation masked as liberation.

In conclusion, while Trump may denounce the Wall Street Journal’s claims as misrepresentations, the narrative that unfolds paints a damning picture of U.S. foreign policy and the shadowy dance between personal gain and political power. Trump’s denial, far from clarifying the situation, only deepens the intrigue and skepticism surrounding the U.S.’s intentions in Venezuela. As always, the truth lies buried under layers of political maneuvering, awaiting excavation by those willing to dig deeper.
