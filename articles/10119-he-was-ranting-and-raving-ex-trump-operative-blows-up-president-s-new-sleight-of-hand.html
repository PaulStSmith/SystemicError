Soviet-born Lev Parnas, who worked closely with Rudy Giuliani during Trump’s first administration, was purportedly sent to Ukraine to help Trump and Giuliani make contacts there for the purposes of digging up negative info on Hunter Biden. Parnas was later convicted of campaign finance violations, and today reports on Trump from outside the administration.

Parnas, who has said he was a “trusted operative” of Trump during the president’s first term, wrote a new piece in which he purports to expose lies surrounding Ukraine and Russia. In an article called “Trump’s UN ‘Pivot’ on Ukraine Is a Lie — and His NATO Answer Proves It,“ Parnas argued, “The media sees a 180, but when pressed on defending NATO, Trump said only two words: ‘It depends.’ Empty rhetoric for Ukraine, real danger for the world.”

He added, “Folks — I want to start bluntly: don’t be fooled by headlines. The media today is spinning Donald Trump’s appearance at the United Nations as some kind of dramatic turnaround — a ‘Trump 180’ they’re calling it. Suddenly he’s talking about Ukraine taking back their land. Suddenly he’s saying Ukrainian fighters are strong, maybe even capable of winning. Suddenly he’s ‘not happy’ with Vladimir Putin.”

That narrative, according to Parnas, doesn’t line up with reality.

“That’s the story they want you to believe. But as I’ve told you over and over again: watch the actions, not the words,” he wrote. “At the UN podium, Trump did what he always does — he lashed out at the institution itself. He railed against globalism. He mocked the very leaders sitting before him. He fed his base the red meat of nationalism, of borders, of isolation. It was performance, not policy.”

Parnas went on to argue that Trump’s words to Ukraine were just a “sleight of hand.”

“And tucked inside the theatrics was a bone tossed to Ukraine. He called them fighters. He said maybe, just maybe, they could win. And the press — desperate for a narrative shift — latched onto it like it was salvation. But let’s pause. Because this isn’t salvation. It’s sleight of hand,” the analyst wrote. “What happened after the speech tells you everything. Trump told NATO allies they should shoot down Russian planes or drones violating their airspace. A strong line, right? But when asked if America would stand shoulder-to-shoulder with NATO, his answer wasn’t the obvious ‘Of course.’ It was two words: ‘It depends.’”

For Parnas, “it’s all theatrics.” Trump’s true focus is likely elsewhere, according to the former insider.

“And my sources are telling me that while Trump was berating the U.N. and putting on his theatrics about Russia and the Ukraine war, behind the scenes he was ranting and raving — pissed off that Jimmy Kimmel is back on the air and didn’t get fired,” he wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s UN Performance: A Masterclass in Misdirection

In the intricate dance of politics, where every gesture and word is dissected for deeper meaning, former President Donald Trump’s recent appearance at the United Nations was nothing short of a theatrical masterpiece. However, not in the way one might hope for from a leader of the free world. The revelations from Soviet-born Lev Parnas, a self-proclaimed “trusted operative” during Trump’s first term, peel back the layers of this performance to reveal a disconcerting reality.

The Great Distraction

At first glance, Trump’s UN speech seemed to mark a significant pivot. The narrative constructed by the media painted a picture of a changed man — one who suddenly supports Ukraine’s fight against Russian aggression. But as Parnas eloquently points out, “watch the actions, not the words.” Trump’s performance was rife with contradictions and empty promises, a sleight of hand designed to distract from his lack of concrete policy or support.

Empty Rhetoric Versus Real Danger

Trump’s casual dismissal of NATO’s importance with a flippant “It depends” when asked about standing with the alliance is particularly alarming. This statement alone should serve as a glaring red flag regarding Trump’s stance on global security. It’s a stark reminder that behind the bombast and bravado lies a dangerous unwillingness to commit to the collective defense pacts that have kept relative peace since World War II.

Theatrics Over Substance

Parnas reveals an even more troubling aspect of Trump’s focus during what should have been crucial moments for diplomacy and leadership. Instead of concentrating on the global stage’s pressing issues, Trump was reportedly more concerned with late-night TV host Jimmy Kimmel’s career. This pettiness not only underscores Trump’s misplaced priorities but also highlights a disturbing reality where personal vendettas take precedence over national or global well-being.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

Parnas’s insights into Trump’s UN appearance and the subsequent actions (or lack thereof) serve as a crucial reminder of the former president’s approach to governance. It’s a world where theatrics overshadow genuine policy-making, where words are deployed to distract rather than to commit, and where personal grievances can derail attention from global crises.

As the political landscape continues to evolve, it’s imperative that we remain vigilant, scrutinizing the actions behind the words, and recognizing the sleight of hand for what it is. The risks of being drawn into the spectacle, without acknowledging the underlying reality, are too high — for both national and global security. Let us not be fooled by the headlines or the performances. The real narrative lies in the actions taken, or not taken, and the priorities they reveal.

In a world craving leadership and decisive action, the revelation that theatrics can so easily replace substance is not only disappointing but deeply concerning.
