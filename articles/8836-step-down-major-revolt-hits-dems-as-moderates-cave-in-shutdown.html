'Step down!' Major revolt hits Dems as moderates cave in shutdown

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) is facing fresh demands that he quit as the Democrats’ Senate leader after reports reveal that eight moderate Senate Democrats plan to cooperate with Republicans and vote to end the government shutdown — without securing the party’s key demand on health care subsidies.

“Chuck Schumer is unable to lead the Democratic Party,” wrote Nina Turner, former Ohio senator and co-chair of Sen. Bernie Sanders’ (I-VT) 2020 presidential campaign, in a social media post on X Sunday night. “Step down from leadership Schumer.”

Now in its 41st day, the ongoing government shutdown began on Oct. 1 after Democrats refused to support any spending bill that didn’t include an extension of Obamacare subsidies, which are set to expire at the end of this year. If allowed to expire, an estimated 4 million Americans would lose health insurance, and premiums for millions of Obamacare enrollees would more than double.

While Democrats have held firm on their demand since the shutdown began, a pool of eight moderate Senate Democrats have agreed to vote on a spending bill to re-open the government, and without an agreement on an extension of the Obamacare subsidies, instead securing a promise from Republicans that the chamber would vote on extending the subsidies in December.

“Has there ever been a man who’s failed to meet the moment more than Chuck Schumer?” wrote Aaron Regunberg, a former Rhode Island lawmaker and attorney, in a social media post on X. “Relentlessly, incessantly, unstoppably wrong at every turn, Schumer is going to go down in history as the worst Dem legislative leader in modern times, and it’s not even particularly close.”

Even Democratic members of Congress began lashing out at Schumer, including Rep. Ro Khanna, who called for the 74-year-old Democratic leader to be “replaced.”

“Senator Schumer is no longer effective and should be replaced,” Khanna wrote in a social media post on X. “If you can’t lead the fight to stop healthcare premiums from skyrocketing for Americans, what will you fight for?”

As of early Monday morning, the Senate has yet to vote on the spending bill that would re-open the government, though late Sunday the chamber did vote60-40to advance consideration of the bill. Members of the House have been told they should expect the spending bill to pass and to vote on it themselves sometime “this week,” The New York Timesreported.

Our Analysis:

The Schumer Conundrum: Leadership in Crisis

In the latest chapter of what could easily be described as the Democratic Party’s existential crisis, Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) finds himself at the eye of a storm that threatens not just his position but the very principles the party purports to stand for. Reports have surfaced, igniting a firestorm of criticism and calls for resignation from within his own ranks, painting a picture of a leader who, in the eyes of some, has failed to shepherd his party through one of its most pivotal battles: the fight to ensure healthcare subsidies under Obamacare are extended.

The Crux of the Matter

The ongoing government shutdown, now dragging into its 41st day, started over Democrats’ refusal to budge on a spending bill that didn’t include provisions for these crucial health care subsidies. An estimated 4 million Americans stand on the precipice, facing the loss of health insurance, with millions more bracing for a potential doubling of their premiums. Yet, in what can only be described as a stunning revelation, eight moderate Senate Democrats have decided to break ranks, opting to vote on a bill to reopen the government sans the demanded extension of Obamacare subsidies.

A Leadership on Trial

Nina Turner’s scathing demand for Schumer’s resignation underscores a broader sentiment of disillusionment. Aaron Regunberg’s characterization of Schumer as “relentlessly, incessantly, unstoppably wrong” isn’t just a personal attack; it’s a damning indictment of Schumer’s ability to navigate the complex and treacherous waters of political leadership in times of crisis. Rep. Ro Khanna’s call for Schumer to be replaced echoes this sentiment, framing Schumer’s perceived failure as not just a momentary lapse but a fundamental inability to champion the causes most critical to Americans.

The Promise: A Band-Aid on a Bullet Wound?

In a move that could be seen as a desperate bid to salvage some semblance of victory, the moderate Democrats’ agreement to vote on the bill, contingent on a Republican promise to consider the subsidy extension in December, feels less like a strategic compromise and more like a capitulation. History, particularly recent history, is littered with examples of such promises falling through, leaving one to wonder if this decision is a gamble with incredibly high stakes.

Analysis: The Schumer Paradox

Chuck Schumer’s leadership, or the perceived lack thereof, presents a paradox. On one hand, leadership involves making tough decisions, often under less-than-ideal circumstances. On the other, it requires an unwavering commitment to the core values and promises a party stands for. The criticism Schumer faces is not just about a failure to secure a win in a political standoff; it’s about what many see as a failure to fight tooth and nail for a principle that affects millions of American lives.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The Democratic Party, with Schumer at the helm, stands at a crossroads. The decisions made today, and the leadership (or lack thereof) that guides those decisions, will not only determine the immediate future of millions of Americans but will also shape the party’s identity and values moving forward. The question isn’t just whether Schumer should step down, but whether the Democratic Party can afford to continue on its current trajectory without a significant reevaluation of its leadership and direction. The Schumer saga is a symptom of a larger disease plaguing the party: a disconnect between its leadership’s actions and the urgent needs of its base. As the party grapples with these internal conflicts, one thing is clear: the stakes couldn’t be higher, and the time for decisive action is now.
