'Get used to it!' More Trump chaos as feds swarm traffic stop in DC over minor violation

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20254 min read

Law enforcement officers patrol on Kennedy Street NW, after U.S. President Donald Trump’s announcement to deploy the National Guard and federalize the Metropolitan Police Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 12, 2025. REUTERS/Jessica Koscielniak

President Donald Trump’s federalization of Washington, D.C., this month led to more chaos on Wednesday as federal agents swarmed a car over a routine insurance violation, according to a CNN reporter.

On Monday, Trump cited a public safety crisis involving violent crime and homelessness in the nation's capital as justification for taking over its law enforcement operations. He ordered hundreds of National Guard members to D.C. to boost existing law enforcement and patrol key areas, and placed the Metropolitan Police Department under direct federal control.

The move has been widely criticized by experts as a soft-launch of martial law, and led to fears it could create chaos in the city, with large law enforcement responses to routine infractions.

CNN reporter Gabe Cohen shared one such instance on Wednesday afternoon.

Anchor Boris Sanchez told Cohen that the president demanded that Congress act, with National Guard troops patrolling D.C. streets. That led to scenes of military Humvees parked right outside the Washington Monument.

"And, Gabe, the president also just raised questions about the crime data that's being gathered here in D.C. He, without presenting firm evidence, called it bogus," said Sanchez.

"Yeah, that's right. Frankly, the president made a lot of news during that event," said Cohen, noting that the president's wild crime claims fly in the face of actual data.

"Data from the local police department shows that over the past couple of years, violent crime has really been declining at a pretty rapid pace — even after an alarming and significant spike back in 2023," he said. "But the president today saying that that data is not to be trusted — essentially, that the books have been cooked."

Elsewhere in the interview, Cohen shared an anecdote about Trump's federalization.

"Today, I will note, we have been seeing this U.S. Park Police helicopter circling the National Mall for hours, and we just watched a traffic stop a few minutes ago right behind us here. Park Police, along with five vehicles from Homeland Security, pulled somebody over. The driver said it was an insurance issue. I spoke with her—they released her and just towed the car away," he said.

"But it shows the presence of federal law enforcement, and folks in D.C. better get used to it, because, as you said, the president is talking about extending this potentially through executive order—though it’s possible he will also turn to Congress for approval."

Our Analysis:

The Overreach: Trump's Federalization of D.C.

In an unprecedented move that reeks of authoritarianism, President Donald Trump has taken a sledgehammer to the autonomy of Washington, D.C., under the guise of addressing a so-called public safety crisis. The resulting chaos, including an overblown law enforcement response to a mere insurance violation, underscores the recklessness of this decision. Let's dissect the disaster piece by piece.

Martial Law by Any Other Name

Trump's decision to flood D.C. with National Guard troops and place the Metropolitan Police Department under direct federal control is a glaring power grab that has been rightly criticized as a "soft-launch of martial law." The use of military Humvees patrolling the streets outside iconic symbols of American freedom, such as the Washington Monument, is not only a stark visual of this overreach but a chilling reminder of how quickly democratic norms can be eroded in the hands of a leader willing to exploit fear for control.

A Dubious Justification

The rationale behind this draconian measure? A supposed spike in violent crime and homelessness that Trump claims is tearing at the fabric of the nation's capital. Yet, this narrative falls apart under the slightest scrutiny. CNN reporter Gabe Cohen points out that, contrary to the president's alarmist rhetoric, local police data indicates a significant decline in violent crime over the past couple of years, even after an initial spike in 2023. Trump's dismissal of this data as "bogus" without presenting any evidence is a classic move from the authoritarian playbook: create a problem that doesn't exist to justify overreaching solutions.

The Reality on the Ground

The real-life implications of Trump's federalization are already manifesting in disturbing ways. Cohen's anecdote about a traffic stop turned federal operation over a simple insurance issue is a glaring example of how everyday life in D.C. is being upended. The overwhelming response — a U.S. Park Police helicopter circling for hours and a driver pulled over by an intimidating convoy of Homeland Security vehicles — is disproportionate and indicative of a police state, rather than a free society.

The Bigger Picture

What's perhaps most alarming is the hint that this federal takeover could extend indefinitely, with Trump considering an executive order to cement his control. This move represents a dangerous escalation in the president's willingness to use federal power to impose order, sidelining local authorities and, by extension, the will of the people these local bodies represent.

In sum, Trump's federalization of D.C. is not only a solution in search of a problem but a deeply troubling sign of authoritarian tendencies. It's a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance in the face of power grabs disguised as public safety measures. The citizens of D.C., and indeed all Americans, must not become accustomed to the presence of federal law enforcement on the streets of a city that is supposed to epitomize freedom and democracy.
