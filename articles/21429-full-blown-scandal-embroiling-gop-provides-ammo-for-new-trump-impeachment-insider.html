Trump Keeps the Lever, Not the Office

The office is a decoy

The source story is about Todd Blanche staying on as acting attorney general, but the real subject is presidential control. Donald Trump is not preserving a legal office; he is preserving a line of command. An acting administrator is useful precisely because the arrangement stays pliable, unfinalized, and open to pressure. That is not an administrative preference. It is a power move.

The deal is the point

According to the column, Trump would rather leave the attorney general seat in acting limbo than put in writing that he will give up the “slush fund” and IRS immunity deal. That is the center of the scandal. The issue is not whether the Senate Judiciary Committee gets a polite negotiation. The issue is whether the president can use the machinery of justice and tax enforcement as bargaining chips while refusing any written constraint on his own advantage.

Taxpayer money for political loyalty

The source says Tomasky sees “$1.8 billion in taxpayer dollars” being sent to convicted January 6 insurrectionists. That is not a clerical dispute. It is state-backed indulgence for political violence. If the money was “received in the corrupt manner” described in the piece, then the corruption is not an accident around the edges of the system. It is the system being bent toward the people who attacked it.

The misdirection is procedural

The soft frame here is that this is some Senate negotiation gone sour, or some awkward staffing question around an “acting” official. That framing is too small for what is being described. The actual decision-maker is Trump. The actual institutional power sits with the presidency. The actual consequence is that executive authority is being used to preserve immunity, protect allies, and avoid written accountability. Calling that a staffing issue is the kind of language that launders intent.

Impeachment is not overreaction

Tomasky is right to treat this as impeachment material if Democrats retake the House. The complaint is not that Trump is rude, erratic, or norm-breaking in some abstract way. It is that he is using public power to shield his own arrangements and reward people tied to an anti-democratic attack. If there are “hundreds” of possible charges, that does not weaken the case. It shows the scale of the damage.

The pattern is executive impunity

This is the larger political pattern: the presidency treated as private property, the Justice Department treated as a tool, taxpayer money treated as a loyalty subsidy, and institutional checks treated as obstacles to be negotiated away. The story is not about confusion in Washington. It is about a deliberate system of impunity trying to dress itself up as normal governance.
