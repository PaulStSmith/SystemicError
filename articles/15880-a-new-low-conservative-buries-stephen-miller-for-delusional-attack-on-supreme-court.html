'A new low': Conservative buries Stephen Miller for 'delusional' attack on Supreme Court

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller speaks to reporters at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 14, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Donald Trump policy advisor Stephen Miller was raked over the coals late Thursday for what longtime conservative columnist Ed Whelan called his "brazen" Fox News appearance where he attempted to misrepresent a Supreme Court ruling on the return of deported Maryland dad Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia.

In his column for the National Review, Whelan broke down Miller's rambling attempt to defend the administration's decision to blow off a unanimous ruling from the conservative court for the DOH to "facilitate" the return of Abrego Garcia, with what the conservative critic called "delusional" reasoning.

As Whelan sees it, Miller "stooped to a new low" as he tried to defend the indefensible when it comes to denying someone living in the U.S. due process.

"This isn’t complicated stuff. The Court’s order (not including the separate statement of Justice Sotomayor) is three paragraphs long, and the operative portion (from which I draw all of my quotations) is one paragraph long. The Trump administration lost unanimously," he wrote before adding that the Trump advisor began, "First, we won the Supreme Court case. Clearly. Nine-zero," to which Whelan responded, "Nope, you lost. Unanimously."

After writing that Miller only delivered, "More delusional fantasizing about what the court actually ordered," the conservative pointed out that the Trump administration is headed for a fall by writing: "If the White House is going to refuse to accept and abide by its losses on emergency applications, the Supreme Court might well decide to summarily reject those applications."

Our Analysis:

The Delusional Defense: A Critique of Stephen Miller's Misrepresentation

In a recent spectacle of political theater, Stephen Miller, a policy advisor for Donald Trump, managed to ignite a firestorm of criticism with his latest appearance on Fox News. His performance was not just a display of audacious misinformation; it was a stark illustration of the lengths to which some individuals will go to defend the indefensible. Longtime conservative columnist Ed Whelan did not mince words in his National Review column, branding Miller's attempt to misrepresent a Supreme Court ruling as "brazen" and "delusional".

The Case in Question

At the heart of this controversy lies the Supreme Court's unanimous ruling on the case of Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia, a deported Maryland father. The Court ordered the Department of Homeland Security (DOH) to "facilitate" his return, a directive that should have been straightforward. Yet, Miller, in his infinite wisdom, decided to embark on a rambling defense, claiming a victory where there was none.

The Misrepresentation

Miller's assertion on national television that "First, we won the Supreme Court case. Clearly. Nine-zero," is not just an error; it is a willful distortion of the truth. As Whelan points out, this isn't a matter of interpretation or opinion. The Court's order is clear, concise, and, most importantly, unanimous in its decision against the administration's stance. This blatant disregard for the facts is not only irresponsible; it's alarming.

The Larger Implication

Whelan's critique goes beyond the mere fact-checking of Miller's claims. He warns of the potential consequences of the White House's refusal to accept and abide by the Supreme Court's decisions. If the administration continues on this path, it might find its emergency applications summarily rejected by the Court in the future. This isn't just about a single case or individual; it's about the integrity of the judicial system and the foundational principle of due process.

The Scathing Conclusion

Miller's attempt to defend the indefensible not only stooped to a new low but also highlighted a dangerous trend in political discourse: the erosion of truth. In their quest to safeguard their agenda, certain individuals have shown a willingness to distort, misrepresent, and outright ignore the facts. This isn't just delusional; it's a direct threat to the democratic principles that govern our society.

The backlash from both conservative and liberal commentators alike serves as a reminder that truth and justice still hold value, even in an era of polarized politics. Miller's "brazen" appearance on Fox News may have been intended as a defense, but it ended up being a stark exposé of the lengths some will go to twist reality to fit their narrative.

In the end, Whelan's dismantling of Miller's argument isn't just a critique; it's a call to action. It's a demand for accountability, integrity, and, above all, a commitment to the truth. As we navigate these turbulent political waters, let us not forget the importance of these principles. For without them, we are lost.
