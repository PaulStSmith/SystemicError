ICE chief 'gave up game' on Trump's midterm plan — then 'stammered' to fix mess: analysis

The head of Immigration and Customs Enforcement has undermined Donald Trump’s midterm plan after saying there is no need to deploy agents for the election.

The president’s former advisor, Steve Bannon, had previously suggested ICE agents should be manning polling booth centers. He said last week, “You’re damn right we’re gonna have ICE surround the polls come November.” White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt did not rule out ICE’s presence in the midterms.

She said, “I can’t guarantee that an ICE agent won’t be around a polling location in November … but what I can tell you is I haven’t heard the president discuss any formal plans to put ICE outside of polling locations.”

Speaking with progressive podcaster Brian Tyler Cohen, Democratic Party elections attorney Marc Elias analyzed what the statement from ICE head Todd Lyons would mean for Trump and the upcoming midterm elections.

“Obviously we do civil enforcement and criminal law enforcement,” Lyons told the Senate Homeland Security Committee last week. “There’s no reason for us to deploy to a polling station.”

Elias, who specializes in voting rights, said, “He gave up the game, and then he stammered and stammered and stammered when asked what would happen if Donald Trump called to deploy ICE at the polls.

“He gave up the game in that he said the truth, there is no reason, no legitimate reason, why ICE would be at the polls or surrounding the polls or in proximity of the polls. Kudos to him for saying that, but don’t sleep on the second half.

“There may be no reason, there may be no good reason, there may be no lawful reason, but that doesn’t mean Donald Trump won’t insist to Kristi Noem that it won’t happen anyway.”

Elias had previously threatened to sue the administration should ICE agents appear at polling locations during the midterm voting period. He said, “We are facing an existential threat to democracy right now. Steve Bannon didn’t just say that there would be ICE in cities; what Steve Bannon said is that it would be ICE surrounding the polls.

“The images we saw in Minneapolis where they surrounded cars, where they surrounded peaceful protesters, where they surrounded US citizens, and we know in the tragic instances, what that meant for those individuals.

“Is it legal? No. It would be totally illegal. But honestly, look at the images we have seen ICE do. They’re breaking all kinds of laws; they’re breaking laws every single day.

“What, though, is going to stop this is not some law in a book on a dusty shelf, but it’s going to stop because you and I are gonna call it out, everyone watching this is going to take this seriously.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing ICE and Election Integrity: A Closer Look

In a recent development that could only be described as a chilling echo of authoritarian tactics, Steve Bannon, a former advisor to Donald Trump, floated the idea of having Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents “surround” polling places in the upcoming midterm elections. This suggestion, which blatantly undermines the principles of a free and fair democratic process, was met with a somewhat ambiguous response from the White House, further muddying the waters of election integrity.

The ICE Head’s Stand

The head of ICE, Todd Lyons, in a moment of startling clarity amidst the cacophony of fear-mongering, stated to the Senate Homeland Security Committee, “There’s no reason for us to deploy to a polling station.” This statement not only refutes Bannon’s dangerous proposal but also serves as a rare beacon of reason in an administration often criticized for its hardline immigration stance. Lyons’ affirmation that ICE has no place in the electoral process is a crucial acknowledgment of the agency’s intended purpose, starkly contrasting with the politicized role Bannon envisions.

The Legal and Moral Quagmire

Marc Elias, a Democratic Party elections attorney, rightly points out the egregiousness of Bannon’s suggestion, highlighting the absence of any legitimate reason for ICE to be present at or near polling stations. Bannon’s rhetoric, alarmingly echoed by White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt‘s refusal to rule out the presence of ICE agents at polling locations, represents a grave threat to the sanctity of the electoral process.

Elias underscores a pivotal concern: the legality of such a move. Deploying ICE agents to polling places is not only unnecessary but would also be unequivocally illegal, a stark violation of the principles that underpin democratic participation and the rule of law. The images invoked by Elias, of ICE agents surrounding cars and peaceful protesters, serve as a harrowing reminder of the potential for abuse under the guise of law enforcement.

The Existential Threat to Democracy

This situation transcends mere policy disagreements or electoral strategy; it strikes at the very heart of democratic governance. The suggestion that ICE could be used to intimidate voters or influence the electoral outcome is not just a hypothetical scenario—it’s a direct assault on the foundational principles of democracy.

The response by the ICE head, while reassuring to some extent, cannot fully assuage the fears stoked by Bannon’s comments and the ambiguous stance of the White House. It is imperative that this issue is not merely dismissed as political posturing. The potential for abuse and the erosion of democratic norms demand vigilant oversight and a resolute stand by all stakeholders in the democratic process.

Conclusion

In the face of such challenges, the responsibility falls not only on legal experts or election officials but on every citizen to safeguard the integrity of the electoral process. The specter of ICE agents at polling stations, as suggested by Bannon, represents a clear and present danger to the principles of democracy. It is a reminder that the fight for a free and fair electoral process is ongoing, and vigilance is the price of liberty.

In a democracy, the rule of law and the sanctity of the electoral process are sacrosanct. Any attempt to undermine these principles under the guise of security or electoral integrity must be met with unwavering resistance. The acknowledgment by Todd Lyons that ICE has no place in this process is a step in the right direction, but the battle for the soul of American democracy is far from over.
