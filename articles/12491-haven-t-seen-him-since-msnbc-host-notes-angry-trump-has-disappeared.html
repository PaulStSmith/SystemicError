'Haven't seen him since': MSNBC host notes 'angry' Trump has disappeared

The president sued Rupert Murdoch and two Wall Street Journal reporters for $10 billion after the newspaper published a report on a birthday letter he'd written to the disgraced financier in 2003, but MSNBC's Jonathan Lemire said Trump has kept a low profile since the story was reported.

"It's been a rare stretch here," the "Morning Joe" co-host said. "He has not taken a question from the media in a number of days, and, look, for all of the criticisms one can level at President Trump, he's extremely accessible to the press. He is. But he has not taken a single question from the press since the Wall Street Journal broke the story about the birthday letter he allegedly sent Jeffrey Epstein that came out Thursday night."

"We haven't seen him since, but we've heard from him quite a bit on on Truth Social, and it does sound, I'm told from aides, that he has been angry about some of the coverage," Lemire added. "He is trying to change the subject. We do expect that he'll have some public events today, he'll probably take some questions. We'll see where the story goes from here."

Host Joe Scarborough chuckled at Lemire's mention of the president's posting on Truth Social, where he has posted AI-generated videos of Barack Obama getting arrested, demanded pro sports teams to return to derogatory nicknames and lobbed attacks at a wide range of targets.

"The New York Times [is] reporting this morning pretty much what everybody in Washington and New York, anybody that followed politics, have concluded, and that is Donald Trump is throwing everything at the wall," Scarborough said. "Now you can say he's doing it to distract from Epstein, you can say he's doing it to distract from from whatever. But there is no doubt he's he's throwing everything at the wall, from football names to Barack Obama to you name it."

Hunter Biden returned to the news cycle Monday with a profane interview about his father's exit from the 2024 presidential race, and Lemire said that would give Trump another topic to exploit.

"Yeah, it's only a matter of time, probably, before President Trump weighs in on that Hunter Biden interview, because it seems like there's not a topic out of his reach at the moment," Lemire said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tumultuous Tactics: A Masterclass in Distraction

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as classic Trump, the former president has once again managed to dominate headlines, but for reasons that would leave any PR team in a cold sweat. Following a Wall Street Journal exposé revealing a birthday letter Trump sent to Jeffrey Epstein in 2003, Trump has seemingly adopted a strategy of silence and distraction, a move that is both predictable and pitifully transparent.

The Art of Avoidance

Trump's sudden shyness, as highlighted by MSNBC's Jonathan Lemire, is a stark departure from his usual media strategy. Known for his "extreme accessibility" to the press, Trump has not fielded a single question since the story broke. This is not the behavior of a man confident in his innocence or transparency but that of someone who finds the truth inconvenient.

A $10 Billion Tantrum

In a move that reeks of desperation, Trump has filed a lawsuit against Rupert Murdoch and two Wall Street Journal reporters for a staggering $10 billion. This lawsuit appears less about seeking justice and more about attempting to intimidate and silence critical journalism. The irony of suing over a report on a letter that is, by all accounts, a matter of public interest, seems lost on Trump.

Distraction as a Defensive Tactic

True to form, Trump has not taken this lying down—or, more accurately, he has not taken this in silence. Instead, he's taken to Truth Social, his digital megaphone, to churn out a series of distractions. From AI-generated videos to demands for sports teams to revert to derogatory nicknames, Trump's strategy is to create enough noise to drown out the damning revelations of his association with Epstein.

The Scattergun Strategy

Joe Scarborough's observation that Trump is "throwing everything at the wall" to see what sticks is an apt description of a man in panic mode. This approach is not new for Trump; it's his go-to move when cornered. By flooding the media with a barrage of controversies, Trump aims to dilute the impact of any single story, no matter how damaging.

Exploiting Hunter Biden

In a predictable pivot, Trump is expected to capitalize on Hunter Biden's recent controversial interview. This move is classic Trump: find a new target, shift the narrative, and hope the public's attention follows. It's a cynical strategy that underestimates the public's ability to discern genuine scandal from orchestrated distraction.

Conclusion

Trump's reaction to the Wall Street Journal's report is a case study in his playbook: avoid, distract, and attack. By suing for $10 billion, hiding from the press, and creating a cacophony of controversies, Trump is desperately trying to control the narrative. However, the truth has a way of persisting, and no amount of distraction can change the facts. As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: Trump's tactics may be transparent, but they are no less dangerous for it. The real question is, will the public see through the smoke and mirrors, or will Trump's scattergun strategy hit its mark? Only time will tell, but one thing is for certain: the truth deserves its day in court, not buried under a mountain of distractions.
