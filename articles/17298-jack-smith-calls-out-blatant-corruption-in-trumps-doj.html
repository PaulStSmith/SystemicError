The Shield of Policy: How DOJ Traditions Protect the Powerful

The Power to Prosecute, The Choice Not to

When former President Donald Trump secured the presidency again in 2024, the machinery of the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) shifted gears in a manner that is as predictable as it is troubling. Special counsel Jack Smith, tasked with investigating Trump on serious charges including election interference and mishandling classified documents, found his hands ostensibly tied by a DOJ policy that discourages the prosecution of a sitting president. This policy, which Smith adhered to by requesting the dismissal of his cases against Trump, reveals a fundamental flaw in the American justice system: the higher your position, the greater your shield from accountability.

The Discretionary Nature of “Policy”

It’s crucial to scrutinize not just the decision made by Jack Smith but the policy it rested on. DOJ policies are not laws passed by Congress; they are internal guidelines that can be interpreted, bent, or even completely disregarded based on the leadership’s discretion and moral compass. The choice to adhere to such a policy is exactly that—a choice, one made by individuals in power who bear the responsibility for its consequences. This selective invulnerability creates a tiered justice system, contradicting the principle that no one is above the law.

Misdirection and Consequences

The narrative around these decisions often centers on procedural fidelity, as if the hands of justice are tied by unavoidable legal red tape. However, this is a misdirection. The real issue here lies in the selective enforcement and application of these “policies.” This isn’t about legal impossibilities; it’s about power, fear, and favoritism. By dismissing the cases against Trump, the DOJ not only reinforced the notion of unequal treatment under the law but also set a precedent that could deter future prosecutions of high-ranking officials, thereby eroding public trust in federal institutions.

The Larger Pattern of Political Corruption

Jack Smith’s later condemnations of the Trump-era DOJ and his warnings about the threats to the U.S. legal system are illuminating but not surprising. His transition from a silent prosecutor to a vocal critic underscores a larger pattern of political interference and corruption within the DOJ under Trump’s influence. This pattern isn’t just about one man or one administration; it reflects a systemic vulnerability within American democracy where political power can and has been used to warp the legal landscape to its benefit.

Institutional Cowardice and the Cost of Silence

Smith’s scathing critiques post-resignation highlight another disturbing trend: the cost of institutional cowardice. By initially remaining silent, adhering to questionable DOJ policies, and only speaking out after stepping down, Smith himself played into a culture of bureaucratic silence that often prioritizes self-preservation and institutional preservation over justice and accountability. This silence has a price, not just in terms of eroded legal norms but in the very real consequences for those who suffer under unjust administrations without recourse.

Conclusion: A Call for Courageous Reform

The situation encapsulated by the DOJ’s handling of Trump’s cases is a glaring call for systemic reform. Policies that protect the powerful at the expense of justice must be scrutinized and, if necessary, dismantled. It requires courageous leadership willing to challenge entrenched norms and prioritize the long-term health of the republic over short-term political gains. Our democracy depends not just on the existence of laws but on the guarantee that those laws apply equally to all, devoid of deference to power. The time for courageous reform is not just upon us; it is overdue.
