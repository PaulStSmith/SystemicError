'Growing resentments' heating up at FBI after firings of Trump foe

During his year as FBI director, far-right conspiracy theorist Kash Patel has been a major departure from his predecessor, Christopher Wray. Although Wray is a conservative Republican, he got along with former President Joe Biden and ex-U.S. Attorney General Merrick Garland. Patel, in contrast, is an unwavering Trump loyalist who isn’t shy about firing FBI agents he considers hostile to Trump. And according to the New York Times, there is “growing resentment” at the FBI because of it.

In an article published by the Times on February 25, journalists Glenn Thrush, Alan Feuer and Devlin Barrett report that “about ten FBI employees” were fired in late February because of “the investigation into President Trump’s retention of classified documents at Mar-a-Lago, his residence in Florida.”

Thrush, in a February 25 post on X, formerly Twitter, emphasizes, “The firings are likely to stoke growing resentments against Mr. Patel, seen by many agents as a high-flying neophyte willing to sack rank-and-file employees on a whim, without evidence they did anything wrong.”

The Mar-a-Lago/classified documents case was one of two federal cases that former special counsel Jack Smith prosecuted against Trump for the Biden/Garland-era U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ); the other involved election interference. When Trump won the 2024 election, Smith — citing longtime DOJ policy against prosecuting a sitting president — considered both of his cases dead.

According to Thrush, Feuer and Barrett, the FBI firings under Patel are “part of a rolling barrage of retribution aimed at those who worked on the two federal prosecutions of Mr. Trump after his first term in office.” And five Times sources believe that the firings won’t be the last.

In their article, the Times journalists report, “The FBI Agents Association, a professional group representing bureau employees, denounced the dismissals in a statement, describing them as an unlawful termination that ‘violates the due process rights of those who risk their lives to protect our country’…. Mr. Patel said that not only did the FBI under the Biden Administration request phone toll records for himself and Ms. Wiles, which did not include recordings or any information relating to the content of conversations, but officials at the time also sought to conceal they had done so in requesting court approval. Some, possibly all, of those fired were involved in that effort, according to a person with knowledge of the situation who spoke on the condition of anonymity to discuss personnel matters.”

Trump’s decision to nominate Patel as FBI director underscored major divisions within the U.S. Senate. When Patel was confirmed 51-49, in a Senate vote on February 20, 2025, Democrats were united in their opposition. And two GOP senators, Maine’s Susan Collins and Alaska’s Lisa Murkowski, joined them in voting “nay.”

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Shift in the FBI’s Leadership: Analyzing Kash Patel’s Controversial Tenure

The appointment of Kash Patel as the FBI Director has marked a shocking pivot in the bureau’s leadership, diverging sharply from the path treaded by his predecessor, Christopher Wray. Unlike Wray, a conservative Republican who managed a semblance of cooperation with figures like former President Joe Biden and ex-U.S. Attorney General Merrick Garland, Patel has emerged as a staunch Trump loyalist, unapologetically wielding his power to dismiss FBI agents perceived as adversaries to Trump. This action alone paints a grim picture of the current state of affairs under Patel’s leadership, hinting at a deeply politicized environment that could potentially compromise the integrity of the FBI.

The Alarming Trend of Retribution

The article from the New York Times sheds light on the “growing resentment” within the FBI, a direct consequence of Patel’s seemingly impulsive dismissal of around ten FBI employees involved in the investigation of Trump’s handling of classified documents at Mar-a-Lago. This act of retribution is not just a reflection of the personal loyalty to Trump that Patel harbors but also a dangerous precedent that questions the foundational principles of impartiality and due process in the bureau.

Moreover, the involvement of the FBI Agents Association, which vehemently condemned the firings as a violation of due process rights, underscores the severity of Patel’s actions. Such a strong rebuke from a professional group dedicated to representing bureau employees signals a profound discord and a concerning shift away from the rule of law.

The Political Weaponization of the FBI

Kash Patel’s tenure is indicative of a broader, more worrisome trend: the weaponization of the FBI for political vendettas. The reported retribution against those who worked on federal prosecutions of Trump post his first term is a glaring example of how law enforcement agencies can be manipulated to serve the interests of a few, undermining the very essence of justice and accountability. The fact that this retribution is seen as part of a “rolling barrage” suggests a systematic approach to dismantling opposition, a tactic distressingly reminiscent of authoritarian regimes.

The Senate’s Role and Future Implications

The Senate’s narrow confirmation of Patel (51-49) with opposition from both Democrats and notable Republicans like Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski speaks volumes. It reflects the deep divisions within American politics and the Senate’s role in either endorsing or challenging the politicization of critical institutions. While the confirmation itself is a done deal, the long-term implications of such a polarized vote and what it signifies for the FBI’s independence and effectiveness in carrying out its duties remain to be seen.

Conclusion

The transformation under Kash Patel’s leadership is a systemic error, a deviation from the principles of impartiality and justice that should characterize the FBI. This situation is not just an internal issue within the bureau but a national concern that calls into question the integrity of American democracy and its institutions. The political allegiance and retributive actions attributed to Patel’s tenure as FBI director should alarm not only those within the bureau but all citizens who believe in the rule of law and the importance of maintaining a neutral and unbiased federal law enforcement agency. As the situation unfolds, it will be crucial to keep a vigilant eye on further developments and to hold those in power accountable for their actions.
