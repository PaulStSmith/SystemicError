Trump's DOJ just undercut his threats to snub Congress in DC takeover

President Donald Trump's move to federalize law enforcement in Washington, D.C. has a catch: Congress needs to approve it if it lasts longer than 30 days. But Trump has been threatening to invoke national emergency powers to bypass the law and cut Congress out of the loop altogether.

However, in court on Friday afternoon, an attorney with the Department of Justice undercut the idea that was on the table, according to former Department of Defense special counsel and legal analyst Ryan Goodman, who was covering the proceedings on X.

The D.C. attorney general, who filed the suit against the occupation, asked Judge Ana Reyes at the hearing to temporarily block Trump's ability to control D.C. law enforcement through the orders signed by U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi, while the case proceeds through court.

As Goodman noted, at one point in the hearing, "Government attorney acknowledges to Judge Reyes that if Congress does not approve extension beyond 30 days, there's NO AUTHORITY for the president to extend emergency power re Metropolitan Police beyond the 30 days."

According to CNN, Reyes more broadly appeared skeptical of Trump's powers to do everything he is doing in D.C.

Trump has claimed authority to take over the city's police under the Home Rule Act. However, Reyes noted that the law doesn't actually include a provision to let the president assume full command of the city's police; it just allows him to make law enforcement directives to the mayor.

Reyes asked both parties to agree on how the order will be enforced for the time being, saying, “I want to get to a practical solution because time is short and there are people who need to know who they are taking direction from and what they are doing.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Legal Limbo: The Federalization Fiasco

In a bold move that reeks of authoritarian overreach, President Donald Trump's attempt to federalize law enforcement in Washington, D.C. stumbles upon a significant legal roadblock, unveiling a saga that could only befit a leadership style marred by a blatant disregard for democratic norms and procedures. The catch in this high-stakes power play? Congressional approval—a hurdle Trump seems keen to sidestep with threats of invoking national emergency powers, a maneuver as dangerous as it is desperate.

During a court hearing that could only be described as a scene straight out of a legal thriller, an attorney from the Department of Justice delivered a blow to Trump’s grand plans, effectively pulling the rug from under the administration's feet. The revelation, as reported by legal analyst Ryan Goodman, underscores a critical weakness in Trump's strategy: without Congressional nod, Trump's authority to extend his control over the Metropolitan Police fizzles out after 30 days. This acknowledgment not only highlights a glaring oversight in the administration's power grab but also serves as a testament to the checks and balances that remain the bedrock of American democracy.

Judge Ana Reyes emerges as the voice of reason amidst this legal and political cacophony. Her skepticism towards Trump's claimed authority under the Home Rule Act is both warranted and telling. The Act, as Reyes rightly points out, does not grant the president carte blanche over D.C.'s police force but merely allows him to issue directives to the mayor—an interpretation that starkly contrasts with Trump's expansive view of his own powers.

The lawsuit filed by the D.C. attorney general against this occupation of authority, and the subsequent proceedings, underscore a critical juncture in the U.S. where the lines of legal and executive power are being tested. Reyes's call for a "practical solution" is not just a plea for clarity amidst chaos but a beacon of judicial prudence aiming to restore order and accountability.

What stands out in this unfolding drama is not just the audacity of an administration trying to circumvent established legislative procedures but the resilience of the judicial system in holding the line. Trump's attempt to federalize D.C.'s law enforcement under dubious legal grounds is a stark reminder of the lengths to which power can be stretched, twisted, and contorted in an attempt to consolidate control.

This episode is far from a mere legal skirmish; it is a litmus test for the nation's democratic institutions and their ability to withstand attempts at overreach. As the case proceeds through court, one thing is clear: the battle for D.C.'s autonomy is not just about who controls its police force but about safeguarding the principles of democracy against the encroachment of unchecked executive power. In this instance, Trump's playbook not only fails the legal muster but also exposes a dangerous predilection for authoritarianism that should concern every American.
