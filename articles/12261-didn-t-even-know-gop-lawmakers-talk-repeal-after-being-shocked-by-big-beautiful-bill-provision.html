Some Republican lawmakers are starting to regret voting for President Donald Trump's megabill now that they're hearing about some of its provisions for the very first time, NBC News reported.

Sahil Kapur, NBC News senior national political reporter, said one of the problematic provisions is a "tax hike on gamblers" that one professional sports better called "potentially catastrophic for the industry."

The clause "would reduce the tax deduction on wagering losses from 100% to 90% starting in 2026," Kapur said in a report for Politics Now.

"Long story short, gamblers could be stuck with a tax bill even if they have zero net winnings in a year."

Kapur said several lawmakers who voted for the legislation now want to repeal it.

"That includes the House's top tax writer, Congressman Jason Smith (R-MO), who told me this tax hike is a mistake by the Senate and that it needs to be undone," Kapur said.

In addition, Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) called the provision "unfair" and claimed that "most Republicans didn't even know this was in the bill when they voted to pass it," according to the report.

The Senate passed its final version of Trump's 2026 budget legislation at the beginning of July. The vote was 50-50, with Vice President JD Vance breaking the tie.

Republican senators who voted against the bill were Thom Tillis (R-NC), Rand Paul (R-KY), and Susan Collins (R-ME).

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Unseen Pitfalls of Hasty Legislative Approvals

In a recent revelation that might come as a shock to absolutely no one who has been paying attention, some Republican lawmakers are now expressing regret over their votes for President Donald Trump's megabill, a sprawling piece of legislation that, among a plethora of provisions, includes a tax hike on gamblers. This regret is not born out of a sudden epiphany regarding fiscal responsibility or a nuanced understanding of the gambling industry's economics. No, it stems from the age-old political tradition of not reading the bill.

The Devil in the Details

NBC News has highlighted a particular aspect of the bill that seems to have caught lawmakers by surprise: a provision that effectively penalizes gamblers by reducing the tax deduction on wagering losses from 100% to 90% starting in 2026. This change, seemingly minor at first glance, could have significant repercussions for professional gamblers and the industry at large. Sahil Kapur, NBC News senior national political reporter, succinctly puts it: "gamblers could be stuck with a tax bill even if they have zero net winnings in a year."

The Aftermath of Oversight

The reaction from some of the bill's erstwhile supporters is as predictable as it is laughable. Congressman Jason Smith (R-MO), the House's top tax writer, now views the tax hike as a "mistake by the Senate" that needs correction. Meanwhile, Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) decries the provision as "unfair" and claims ignorance on the part of most Republicans regarding the bill's contents. This collective admission of not knowing what they voted for is not just a damning indictment of their diligence but also a frightening glimpse into how legislation is crafted and passed in our times.

The Irony of Unintended Consequences

The irony of this situation cannot be overstated. These are the same lawmakers who often tout the virtues of meticulous scrutiny and fiscal conservatism, yet when it comes to a substantial legislative measure, they seem to have thrown caution to the wind. The claim by Sen. Cruz that "most Republicans didn't even know this was in the bill when they voted to pass it" is a stunning admission of negligence. It reflects a broader problem of legislative rush jobs, where the imperative to "pass something" outweighs the necessity to understand it fully.

The Bigger Picture

This scenario underscores a systemic flaw within the legislative process: the propensity to pass voluminous bills with little to no understanding of their comprehensive impact. It's a cautionary tale about the dangers of legislative haste and the importance of due diligence. The regret expressed by these lawmakers is not just a reflection of a poorly conceived tax policy but a symptom of a larger, more troubling trend of governance by reaction rather than by thoughtful deliberation.

Conclusion

The fallout from the tax hike on gamblers is a microcosm of a larger legislative malaise. It's a reminder that in the rush to legislate, what gets lost are the details—and often, those details matter. Lawmakers would do well to remember that their decisions have real-world implications and that understanding the legislation they pass is not just their responsibility; it's the bare minimum of their job. The lesson here is clear: Read the bill.
