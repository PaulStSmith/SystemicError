'Needed to get him alone': Trump advisers urged tariff pause with 'hovering' Navarro away

FILE PHOTO: Senior trade adviser Peter Navarro speaks next to U.S. President Donald Trump in the Oval Office of the White House, on the day Trump signs executive orders for reciprocal tariffs, in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 13, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

President Donald Trump's major economic cabinet officials, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, were only able to persuade him to temporarily reduce the burden of his "Liberation Day" tariffs because they spoke to him while his most fanatical trade adviser was out of the room, reported the Wall Street Journal on Friday evening.

Peter Navarro allegedly was the individual with the idea in the first place to urge that Trump set tariffs based on the size of the U.S. trade deficit, or balance between imports and exports, with each country — an idea not endorsed by mainstream economists, who don't generally view trade deficits as a universally bad thing.

"Navarro isn’t one to back down during policy debates and had stridently urged Trump to keep tariffs in place, even as corporate chieftains and other advisers urged him to relent. And Navarro had been regularly around the Oval Office since Trump’s 'Liberation Day' event," reported Alexander Saeedy and Josh Dawsey. "So that morning, when Navarro was scheduled to meet with economic adviser Kevin Hassett in a different part of the White House, Bessent and Lutnick made their move, according to multiple people familiar with the intervention. They rushed to the Oval Office to see Trump and propose a pause on some of the tariffs — without Navarro there to argue or push back."

According to the report, Bessent and Lutnick "knew they had a tight window" and didn't even formally schedule the meeting with Trump.

They knew they "needed to get the president alone" — and it worked.

Trump's planned tariffs range from 10 to 49 percent on goods from virtually every country in the world, even Antarctic islands that don't have trade with the U.S. or even people. Under the 90 day "pause," the tariffs still apply but are reduced to the minimum of 10 percent for every country except China, which faces sky-high rates of 145 percent. All told, this is still enough to potentially raise the price of many goods, but briefly calmed markets which had been in freefall.

The two cabinet secretaries dispute this narrative of how the decision happened, with Lutnick, who has reportedly been getting on the president's nerves as of late, telling the Journal in a statement, “The notion that we are playing Gossip Girl games is simply false — we are fully focused on implementing President Trump’s vision.”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Trump's Tariff Tango: An Absurd Dance of Economic Folly

In a recent revelation that reads more like a scene from a poorly scripted political drama than a coherent strategy session at the White House, President Donald Trump's ludicrously named "Liberation Day" tariffs were only momentarily slowed down thanks to a strategic absence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick managed to catch the President's ear (and perhaps a fleeting moment of rational thought) while the architect of this economic chaos, Peter Navarro, was elsewhere engaged. This momentary lapse in the usual pandemonium allowed for a brief respite in what can only be described as a trade war born out of sheer ignorance.

The Navarro Doctrine: A Recipe for Disaster

Peter Navarro's influence over Trump's trade policies has been nothing short of catastrophic. His advocacy for tariffs based on the U.S. trade deficit with each country is a concept so fundamentally flawed that it's rejected by virtually every mainstream economist. Trade deficits are not the boogeymen Navarro and his ilk make them out to be. Yet, his strident voice has been instrumental in pushing the U.S. into economic strategies that seem designed more to provoke than to provide any real benefit.

A Clandestine Coup: Bessent and Lutnick's Brief Victory

The account of Bessent and Lutnick's rush to the Oval Office while Navarro was preoccupied paints a picture of desperation. It's a scene where economic policy is made not through careful deliberation but through opportunistic ambush. The notion that these two had to catch the President "alone" to have a sensible conversation about tariffs is as alarming as it is absurd. Their success, if only temporary, in persuading Trump to ease his tariff tirade reflects a chaotic and fragmented administration where policy is as stable as quicksand.

The Tariffs: A Misguided Missile

Trump's planned tariffs, ranging from 10 to 49 percent on goods from virtually every country, including Antarctic islands (a detail so ludicrous it would be hilarious if it weren't so tragic), highlight a profound misunderstanding of global economics. The temporary pause, reducing tariffs to the minimum of 10 percent (except for China, which faces a staggering 145 percent), did little more than briefly soothe the markets' nerves. It's a band-aid on a self-inflicted wound, a temporary measure that does nothing to address the underlying folly of the tariffs themselves.

Lutnick's Response: A Denial as Hollow as the Policy Itself

Howard Lutnick's dismissal of the reported events as "Gossip Girl games" is a pathetic attempt to downplay the severity of the situation. It's a flippant remark that belies the real damage these policies are inflicting on the global economy. The attempt to wave off criticism and concern with a pop culture reference is emblematic of an administration more concerned with image than impact.

In summary, the entire episode surrounding Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs and the subsequent behind-the-scenes maneuvering to mitigate their damage is a damning indictment of this administration's approach to trade policy. It's a blend of arrogance, ignorance, and incompetence that threatens not just the U.S. economy but global stability. The fact that such significant decisions are influenced by who happens to be in the room at the time speaks volumes about the chaotic and capricious nature of Trump's presidency. It's a systemic error that continues to cost us all dearly.
