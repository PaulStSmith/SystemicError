Border Patrol chief vows retaliation after judge releases wrongly held detainees

U.S. Border Patrol chief Gregory Bovino threatened to retaliate after a federal judge suggested as many as 600 detainees could be released due to violations of a consent decree requiring warrants for arrests.

In an order issued on Wednesday, U.S. District Judge Jeffrey Cummings said that the Department of Justice must release 13 detainees who were caught during the “Operation Midway Blitz” enforcement operation in Chicago. The judge also ordered the review of 600 others.

“Those illegal aliens came from a myriad of situations, whether they were criminals or individuals that were taking jobs from Americans,” Bovino told Fox News on Thursday. “And I’ll tell you what, I’ll tell you what’s going to happen is, we’re going to go even harder on the streets.”

“If he releases those 650, we’re going to apprehend 1,650 on the streets of Chicago,” he insisted.

Bovino also said that reports that the Border Patrol was winding down operations in Chicago were incorrect.

“We’re ratcheting operations up in Chicago,” he remarked. “That’s a very corrupt system in Chicago, whether it’s those elected leaders like Pritzer or those out-of-control judges.”

“Chicago needs some attention,” he added. “We’re not going anywhere out of Chicago. So you’re gonna see some very dynamic operations.”

“But one thing that’s going to happen is Governor Pritzer is going to see a lot more immigration enforcement, Title VIII, by Border Patrol, ICE, and those allied teams, you can guarantee it.”

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Review of the Border Patrol’s Bluster

In what reads like a thinly-disguised threat veiled as law enforcement bravado, U.S. Border Patrol chief Gregory Bovino’s response to a federal judge’s order is as alarming as it is indicative of a deeper systemic malaise. The recent declaration by Bovino in an interview with Fox News serves as a stark reminder of the ongoing battle between the judiciary’s attempts to uphold constitutional rights and the executive branch’s increasingly autocratic tendencies.

The Judicial Order: U.S. District Judge Jeffrey Cummings’ decision to potentially release up to 600 detainees due to violations of a consent decree is a significant moment of judicial intervention. This decree, requiring warrants for arrests, is not a mere bureaucratic hurdle; it is a safeguard of due process—a fundamental pillar of American law. Cummings’ order, starting with the release of 13 detainees from the “Operation Midway Blitz,” underscores a critical check on power. Yet, the response from Border Patrol is not introspection or a pledge to adhere to legal standards, but a promise of escalation.

Bovino’s Threat: “Those illegal aliens came from a myriad of situations, whether they were criminals or individuals that were taking jobs from Americans,” Bovino stated, employing a tactic as old as time: fearmongering. The dichotomy drawn between “criminals” and job “thieves” is a deliberate simplification of a complex issue, serving only to vilify and dehumanize. His vow to “go even harder on the streets” should a federal judge dare exercise judicial oversight is not just troubling; it’s a brazen challenge to the separation of powers that underpins the United States’ constitutional democracy.

The Disturbing Promise: Bovino’s pledge to apprehend 1,650 individuals should the 650 detainees be released is not a statement of law enforcement strategy; it’s a declaration of punitive action against perceived judicial overreach. This is not how a democracy functions. Law enforcement agencies do not—and should not—operate on a quota system, especially not one predicated on the whims of retribution.

Misguided Focus and Misinformation: Bovino’s attack on Chicago’s “very corrupt system” and his singling out of elected leaders and “out-of-control judges” is a diversionary tactic. Blaming systemic issues on individuals, especially without evidence, is a classic strategy to undermine trust in institutions. Moreover, his insistence that Border Patrol operations in Chicago are “ratcheting up” in defiance of judicial scrutiny is a worrying sign of how far some officials are willing to go to resist oversight.

The Real Issue at Hand: What Bovino’s statements and the underlying actions of the Border Patrol reveal is not just a contempt for judicial authority but a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of immigration enforcement. Immigration policy and its enforcement have long been contentious issues, but the solution is not, and should never be, an escalation in hostilities or a disregard for the legal protections afforded to all individuals, regardless of their immigration status.

In conclusion, Bovino’s comments are more than just bluster; they are symptomatic of an agency seemingly at odds with the very foundations of the legal system it purports to protect. The focus should not be on retaliatory measures but on ensuring that enforcement actions are compliant with the law. Anything less is a disservice to the principles upon which the United States was built.
