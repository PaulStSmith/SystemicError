GOP senator goes off at Todd Blanche hearing as scorched-earth tirade demands firing

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

Paper Fixes, Real Power

The Source

At Todd Blanche’s confirmation hearing, Sen. Thom Tillis first blocked then blessed the nomination after receiving two written orders he said satisfied him. The fight centered on a $1.776 billion Justice Department fund critics say would benefit Trump allies, including Jan. 6 rioters, and on Blanche’s legal maneuvering around it. Tillis then used the hearing to recite a familiar dodge: everyone is dirty, both parties have weaponized government, and the current mess is just another chapter in the same story.

Who Actually Holds The Levers

The real power in this story is not the outgoing senator performing outrage on the way out the door. It is Trump, whose written consent is required to void the underlying settlement. It is Blanche, whose order rescinded his own implementation directive without killing the settlement itself. And it is the Republican Senate majority, which can move this nomination before August recess even after weeks of staged resistance.

Tillis and John Cornyn are not resisting power. They are managing it. They are using a brief window of leverage to extract paperwork, then waving the nomination through. That is not accountability. It is procedural theater with a deadline.

The “Fix” Was A Loophole

CBS News reported that Blanche’s order had no legal effect on the underlying deal. That matters. Rescinding a directive that implements a settlement is not the same as killing the settlement. If the core agreement survives, the money survives unless Trump signs it away.

So the clean headline is false comfort. The fund was not decisively buried. It was shrouded in legal language and blessed by senators who can pretend they stopped something while leaving the architecture intact. This is how elite impunity works now: the language changes, the power does not.

Tillis’s Both-Sides Routine

Tillis then did what politicians do when they want to look stern without naming the real culprit: he flattened everything into a generic tale of “weaponization.” Crossfire Hurricane, Lois Lerner, Biden pardons, Comey, all tossed into the same bin so responsibility dissolves into atmosphere.

That is not analysis. It is laundering. It treats deliberate abuse as a bipartisan weather pattern and invites the listener to conclude that since everyone is compromised, no one should be held to account. That is the opposite of democratic scrutiny. It is a shield for the people currently running the machinery.

The Comey Example Is The Tell

Tillis pointed to the Justice Department’s indictment of James Comey over the “86 47” beach photo, then mocked the idea that it was a crime. But that moment exposes the deeper rot: selective prosecution used as political signaling, with Republican politicians acting shocked after spending years normalizing the machinery that enables it.

The point is not that every cited episode is identical. They are not. The point is that Tillis is not trying to defend democratic standards. He is trying to bury the fact that Trump-aligned institutions are being used to reward allies, punish enemies, and dress coercion up as law.

What This Really Shows

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics rarely announce themselves as authoritarian. They arrive through legalistic detours, procedural choke points, and officials who claim to be uneasy while still clearing the path. The spectacle of Tillis scolding Blanche is not resistance. It is a controlled release valve for a party that knows the damage is real but will still vote yes.

This is why the story matters beyond one nomination. When lawmakers reduce organized abuse to “absurdity,” they help normalize it. When they insist everybody is dirty, they make it easier for the dirty side with the bigger apparatus to keep the apparatus.
