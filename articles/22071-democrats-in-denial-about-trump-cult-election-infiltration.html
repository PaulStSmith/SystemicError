Democrats in denial about Trump cult election infiltration

Power Does Not Hide in the Polling Place. It Enters It.

The Real Subject

Marc Elias is warning that the threat to elections is not a few unruly voters or overworked poll workers. It is organized political power: a White House that has already lost court fights over state election meddling, a Justice Department suing 30 states, and a Republican apparatus that has spent years placing election deniers into local positions where they can do real damage. That is the story. Everything else is decoration.

Who Holds the Levers

The people with actual institutional power are not the poll workers Soboroff wants to reassure us about. They are the federal government when it decides to intervene, state governments when they comply or resist, and county-level election officials when they control the machinery of voting on the ground. Elias is pointing at a brutal truth: the weakest actors in the room are being asked to absorb the consequences of decisions made by people far above them.

Misdirection as Strategy

The comforting script says democracy survives because decent local workers will “stop it.” That is not analysis; it is wishful thinking dressed up as civic virtue. Elias cuts through that fantasy by naming the mismatch in force and authority. If the pressure comes from armed federal agents or from locally embedded election loyalists, then the problem is not a lack of good intentions at the precinct. The problem is that power has already been placed in the wrong hands.

Infiltration, Not Confusion

The key detail here is not just that election deniers exist. It is that Republicans have been “putting election deniers in key positions” and, in some places, installing them at the local level where they can shape outcomes without needing to steal the whole system at once. Tina Peters is the example, not the exception. This is not random dysfunction. It is political placement: a long campaign to seed the administrative state with people willing to sabotage it from within.

The States That Matter

Elias names the states where his concern is highest: places where Republican state governments already dominate and where local county offices have also been infiltrated at a MAGA level. That matters because the vulnerability is layered. When state power, county administration, and federal pressure move in the same direction, the election is no longer protected by procedure. It is exposed to capture by design.

The Systemic Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in practice: not one dramatic coup, but a methodical effort to make normal democratic administration unusable. The press often reaches for soothing language about resilience, professionalism, and local guardianship. That framing flatters institutions that are already under siege and excuses the people who engineered the siege. The democratic response cannot be performative confidence in poll workers. It has to be a recognition that the machinery of elections is a site of power struggle, and that power must be taken back from those who are using it to corrode democracy from inside.
