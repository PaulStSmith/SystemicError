A Missouri Republican made an eyebrow-raising acknowledgement to a right-wing podcast that he suspects giants were real.

Rep. Eric Burlison made the comments to BlazeTV host Alex Stein in a clip published July 30. The comments were first flagged by Meidas Touch.

Stein brought Burlison on the show to discuss popular conspiracy theories, including UFOs, interdimensional aliens, and other possible government cover-ups.

"Ok, Rep., this sounds so stupid, you know, but really they say that the military has remote viewing and they can look into other dimensions, I mean, can we — let's not even talk about space travel. Is there interdimensional travel in your opinion? Is that a real thing?"

Burlison pointed to quantum entanglement as evidence that there's "something deeper than the existence that we live on." He conceded that whether other beings exist in a "higher dimension" is another question, however.

"I remain skeptical," he said, clarifying he believes angels exist, and that there could be some overlap between the concept of aliens and what's in the Bible.

"While they call them angels, but at the end of the day they're not from Earth," he said.

Shortly thereafter, Stein asked the congressman his thoughts on another fabled being.

"Hey, Rep. They call me crazy but I think there was giants. I believe in the Nephilim. I believe that was real, Rep. And they're going to clip this and call you crazy. Were giants ever real in your opinion? I hope this doesn't get you canceled."

Burlison replied, "No, I'm going to say I do believe that they were real. I do believe. I do."

Burlison said he went deep down the conspiracy rabbit hole — and hinted at using his oversight power to look for giant bones hidden in the Smithsonian.

"We gotta figure out a strategy behind that," he said.

Our Analysis:

The Giants of Missouri: A Dive into the Depths of Conspiracy

In an era where the line between political discourse and the plot of a science fiction novel becomes alarmingly blurred, Missouri Republican Rep. Eric Burlison's recent foray into conspiracy theory territory is less a cause for surprise and more a damning indictment of the current state of political dialogue. During a conversation with BlazeTV host Alex Stein, a figure not unknown for entertaining the more esoteric corners of political and scientific debate, Burlison made a claim that sounds like it was plucked straight from the pages of an H.P. Lovecraft story: he believes in the existence of giants.

The Quantum Leap from Politics to Pseudoscience

The discussion, initially revolving around popular conspiracy theories such as UFOs and interdimensional aliens, took a bizarre turn when Burlison cited quantum entanglement as proof of "something deeper than the existence that we live on." For those uninitiated in the complexities of quantum mechanics, this leap might seem as vast and unfounded as the belief in giants itself. Quantum entanglement, a real and profoundly misunderstood phenomenon in physics, involves the state of particles being dependent on the state of another, regardless of distance. How this relates to the existence of other dimensions, let alone giants, remains a mystery that Burlison did not elucidate.

Angels, Aliens, and... Giants?

Burlison's skepticism seems to take a back seat when it comes to the existence of angels, which he equates to aliens — a fascinating theological and extraterrestrial blend that would give both theologians and scientists pause. His assertion that "they're not from Earth" might find some common ground in speculative fiction, but its place in serious political discussion is, at best, questionable.

The Smithsonian and the Giant Bones Conspiracy

Perhaps most alarming is Burlison's hint at leveraging his oversight power to investigate the Smithsonian for allegedly hiding giant bones. This isn't just a harmless dive into the "conspiracy rabbit hole"; it's an indication of a willingness to misuse political power for the pursuit of fantasies. The implications of such actions go beyond the absurd and venture into the realm of wasteful misappropriation of resources that could be spent addressing actual, pressing issues facing Missouri and the nation.

The Real Giant: The Problem of Misinformation

Burlison's comments are emblematic of a larger, more insidious problem: the spread of misinformation and the erosion of trust in science and factual evidence. When public officials indulge in and propagate conspiracy theories, it undermines the very foundation of informed democracy. The belief in giants, while harmless in a mythological or religious context, becomes a concerning symptom of the disconnection from reality when it is endorsed by those in positions of power.

In Conclusion

Rep. Eric Burlison's journey down the "conspiracy rabbit hole" might have been intended as a lighthearted exploration of unorthodox ideas. However, it inadvertently shines a light on the dark underbelly of political discourse where facts are optional, and fantasy becomes a tool for political maneuvering. As entertaining as it might be to imagine a world where giants roam and politicians wield the power to uncover ancient secrets, the reality we face requires a far more grounded approach. The giants we need to worry about are not the mythical beings of lore, but the behemoths of misinformation and irrationality that threaten to overshadow reason and evidence in public life.
