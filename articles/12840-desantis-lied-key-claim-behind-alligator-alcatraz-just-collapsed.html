'DeSantis lied’: Key claim behind 'Alligator Alcatraz' just collapsed

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 12, 20254 min read

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis speaking at Lynchburg, Virginia, on April 14, 2023. (Shutterstock.com)

When Florida Republicans unveiled a makeshift detention camp site for migrants in the Everglades known as "Alligator Alcatraz," they characterized it as a facility for hardened criminals who needed to be deported in the interest of public safety.

But a new Washington Post report reveals quite the opposite — that Florida officials are relying in large part on minor traffic stops to find immigrants to throw in the facility.

"We spent 6 hours with Florida Highway Patrol and got an inside look at how they're encountering illegal immigrants at traffic stops and turning them over to Border Patrol and ICE, where they're sent to ‘Alligator Alcatraz,' under DeSantis," wrote Washington Post DHS reporter Anna Giaritelli in a lengthy thread posted to X. What they found, she said, is that "Since March, FL Highway Patrol has turned over 3,300 illegal immigrants found at traffic stops to the feds" — and many were stopped for very low-level reasons.

This is a new practice, as in the past, Florida state troopers couldn't hand over people from traffic stops to federal agents.

Among those who were rounded up in such arrests were "2 illegal immigrants and a 15 y/o who said he had come over the southern border and his family was in Guatemala. Cause of traffic stop was cutting across 3 lanes of traffic, no seat belt," she wrote. Others were stopped for texting and driving, or for not wearing a seat belt, and found to be operating without a license.

And in another case, she wrote, "A large truck delivering plants was pulled over for what the officer suspected was too dark a tint. That showed not to be the case, but while stopped, the 3 men in the backseat disobeyed orders to stay inside so the officer detained them, then asked for ID. That's when their lack of immigration status came out."

"This thread makes clear how much Gov. Desantis lied about about 'Alligator Alcatraz' being for 'the worst of the worst,'" wrote American Immigration Council fellow Aaron Reichlin-Melnick. "Florida Highway Patrol is doing clearly pretextual arrests (window tints, seat belts) and sending every undocumented immigrant they stop to the camp. For example, the claim that Florida Highway Patrol is stopping landscaping trucks for alleged traffic violations is classic Driving While Black/Driving While Brown pretext."

"Alligator Alcatraz" has come under fire for brutal conditions, including inconsistent air conditioning, infestations of "bugs the size of hands," and flooding during storms, even though state officials have insisted the facility is "hurricane-proof."

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of "Alligator Alcatraz"

In what could only be described as a harrowing illustration of political doublespeak, Florida Republicans, under the guise of public safety, have unveiled a detention camp in the Everglades, dubiously dubbed "Alligator Alcatraz." According to them, this facility is a necessary evil, a bastion against "hardened criminals" that threaten the fabric of American society. Yet, a revealing report from the Washington Post shatters this facade, exposing a grim reality far removed from the narrative peddled by these officials.

The Reality Behind Traffic Stops

The report uncovers a disturbing trend: Florida officials, particularly the Florida Highway Patrol, have been engaging in what appears to be a systematic roundup of immigrants for minor traffic violations—an egregious deviation from their initial claim. These are not the so-called "worst of the worst"; these are individuals stopped for reasons as trivial as seatbelt violations, texting while driving, or the purported darkness of a vehicle's tint.

Since March, over 3,300 immigrants have been ensnared in this dragnet, handed over to federal authorities, and ultimately sent to the controversial "Alligator Alcatraz." Among them, a 15-year-old boy and individuals whose only "crime" was a traffic infraction. This practice marks a stark departure from previous policies where state troopers did not have the authority to detain individuals solely to hand them over to federal immigration authorities.

Pretextual Stops: A Tool for Racial Profiling

The report also highlights the use of pretextual stops—a tactic alarmingly reminiscent of racial profiling techniques such as "Driving While Black" or "Driving While Brown." These stops are not about enforcing traffic laws but rather serve as thinly-veiled attempts to identify and detain undocumented immigrants. This strategy not only undermines the trust between law enforcement and the communities they serve but also diverts resources from genuine public safety issues to fuel a politically motivated agenda.

Brutal Conditions at "Alligator Alcatraz"

The conditions within "Alligator Alcatraz" itself are a testament to the facility's true nature. Descriptions of brutal conditions, including erratic air conditioning, infestations of oversized insects, and vulnerability to flooding, starkly contrast with state officials' assurances of a "hurricane-proof" facility. This disparity not only underscores the inhumanity of the detention center but also raises serious questions about the integrity and intentions of those who championed its creation.

Conclusion: A Disturbing Reflection of Political Priorities

The revelations about "Alligator Alcatraz" and the practices leading to the detention of immigrants for minor traffic offenses paint a disturbing picture of Florida's political landscape. It reveals a willingness to sacrifice human dignity and justice on the altar of political expediency. The fact that the state's officials have resorted to such deceitful and inhumane tactics is a stark reminder of the need for vigilance, accountability, and a reevaluation of our approach to immigration and law enforcement.

In essence, "Alligator Alcatraz" is not about safeguarding the public or upholding the rule of law. It is a monument to hypocrisy, a chilling symbol of how far some are willing to go to advance a punitive and exclusionary agenda under the guise of public safety. As this report makes painfully clear, the true "crime" here is not the minor traffic violations of these immigrants but the systematic injustice perpetrated in the name of politics.
