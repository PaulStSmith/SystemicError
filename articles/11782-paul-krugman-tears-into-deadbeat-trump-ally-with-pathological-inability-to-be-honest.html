Paul Krugman tears into 'deadbeat' Trump ally with 'pathological inability' to be honest

New York Times columnist and economist Paul Krugman attacked President Donald Trump after twisting data to make himself look better.

In a Monday column for his Substack, Krugman looked at some of the charts that his ally Stephen Moore brought to the Oval Office to verify some of his claims about the economy.

"What’s wrong with this picture?" he asks. "First, look at the chart. The second line claims that it shows 'medium income; — a term unknown to economics. Clearly, it was supposed to say median income. OK, speling misteaks hapen (sic). But not, usually, in charts prepared for a presentation by the President of the United States."

The numbers presented by Moore are wrong, he said, citing Jared Bernstein, who examined the charges and the data.

The larger problem for Krugman, however, is that Trump was standing there with Moore, "who may be the last person on the planet you’d trust to tell you the economic truth."

It isn't merely that Moore is a far-right ideologue, though he is, said Krugman.

"I don’t even mean that he’s a dishonest hack, although again of course he is. I mean that even among dishonest right-wing hacks, Moore stands out for his pathological inability to get numbers and facts right," the economist blasted.

He promised he wasn't being hyperbolic, citing a Columbia Journalism Review article that reported Moore being blocked from posting further editorials for one newspaper due to incorrect data that didn't pass a fact-check.

"What’s Moore’s problem? I don’t know and I don’t care. The interesting question is why someone so incompetent — apparently he can’t even copy numbers correctly — has consistently failed upward," asked Krugman. "Trump even tried to put him on the Federal Reserve Board in 2019, and might have succeeded if Moore hadn’t also turned out to be a grotesque misogynist and a deadbeat dad who had been held in contempt for failure to pay child support."

Krugman walked through the startling rise of Moore, remarking that even before Trump, conservatives saw "his surreal incompetence not as a liability but as an asset."

The reason Moore was there was to help spin Trump's narrative that everything is fine and the squeeze Americans are facing is imaginary. Meanwhile, Trump is underwater with voters on every issue.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Krugman's Takedown of Trump's Economic Charade

In a recent spectacle of economic misinformation, New York Times columnist Paul Krugman delivers a scathing critique of President Donald Trump and his economic ally, Stephen Moore. Krugman's analysis, rooted in a blend of sarcasm and academic rigor, lays bare the manipulation of economic data to paint a rosier picture of the Trump administration's economic achievements. Let's dissect the layers of this critique and understand the broader implications of such deceptive practices.

The Misrepresentation of Economic Data

Krugman begins by highlighting a glaring error in the charts presented by Stephen Moore in the Oval Office, where "medium income" is cited instead of the correct term, median income. This mistake, while seemingly minor, serves as a prelude to more significant inaccuracies and manipulations. The devil is in the details, or in this case, the lack thereof. Krugman points out that such errors are not merely typographical but indicative of a broader disregard for factual accuracy in the administration's economic presentations.

Stephen Moore: A Far-Right Ideologue or a Dishonest Hack?

Krugman doesn't mince words when describing Stephen Moore. Labeling him as both a far-right ideologue and a dishonest hack, Krugman's critique extends beyond Moore's political leanings to his integrity and competence. Highlighting Moore's "pathological inability to get numbers and facts right," Krugman draws attention to a systemic issue within Trump's circle: the preference for loyalty over accuracy and integrity.

The Unsettling Rise of Incompetence

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Krugman's column is the discussion on Moore's ascent despite his apparent incompetence and unethical behavior. Krugman astutely asks why someone of Moore's caliber continues to fail upwards, gaining more influence despite a track record of inaccuracies and egregious personal conduct. This reflects a disturbing trend within certain political circles where ideological alignment trumps competence and ethical standards.

Spinning Narratives at the Expense of Truth

Krugman posits that Moore's presence and role in the Trump administration are not about providing accurate economic analysis but about spinning a narrative. This narrative, designed to suggest that the economic pressures felt by Americans are "imaginary," is not only misleading but dangerously dismissive of the real challenges faced by the populace.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Integrity

Krugman's critique is not just about the inaccuracies of a single chart or the questionable character of an economic advisor. It's a scathing indictment of a broader political strategy that prioritizes narrative over truth, loyalty over competence. In an era where misinformation can spread rapidly, the need for accountability and integrity in public discourse, especially regarding economic policy, has never been more critical.

In sum, Krugman's column serves as a fierce reminder of the dangers of economic misinformation and the importance of scrutinizing the sources and integrity of the data presented by political leaders. The implications of such deceptive practices extend far beyond partisan politics, affecting the livelihoods and well-being of citizens.
