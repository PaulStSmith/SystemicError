'Something to hide': Ex-DOJ insider says Trump just showed his hand on Epstein

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Epstein Saga Continues: Trump's Veiled Moves and MAGA's Defiance

Shutterstock

Donald Trump is showing his hand on the controversial Jeffrey Epstein files and, at the same time, MAGA is ignoring the president's orders, according to a former prosecutor.

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance, who has 25 years of experience working in that field, wrote a piece called, It's Time to Release the Epstein Files, in which she argued, "People who fight this hard to hide something have something to hide."

After calling out Trump for purported "distractions" from the Epstein story, Vance said, "Trump has been telling supporters to focus on investigating Democrats and to move on from the Epstein issue. They haven’t."

"As recently as today, he claimed calls to release the Epstein files are a 'Democrat hoax that never ends,' something survivors who held a press conference today explicitly took on, insisting that they were real—not a hoax. One survivor went so far as to identify herself as a registered Republican in an attempt to refute the allegation," she wrote. "A release of documents by Congress on Tuesday seemed to be designed to forestall additional controversy on the eve of the survivors’ press conference on Capitol Hill. But it was simply a charade. There was virtually nothing new in the release, and nothing in it advanced public understanding of who, to put it clearly, was committing crimes against girls and women with Jeffrey Epstein. That’s what the public wants to know."

Addressing bipartisan efforts to get the DOJ to release the files, Vance said, "This Justice Department does not function by normal rules, so trying to fall back on regular procedures in this moment is ludicrous."

"Trump promised to release the investigative files repeatedly during the campaign. Unlike grand jury material, which requires a judge’s permission for release, Trump can do this by executive fiat," the legal expert added. "You don’t need to have my 25 years of experience as a prosecutor to understand that people who fight this hard to hide something have something to hide. It’s time to release the files."

The Epstein Saga Continues: Trump's Veiled Moves and MAGA's Defiance

In a world where political spectacle often overshadows the grim realities of power and abuse, the saga surrounding Jeffrey Epstein's dark legacy offers a glaring testament to the intricate dance of visibility and concealment played at the highest levels of power. Donald Trump, a figure who has mastered the art of the spectacle, is at the center of Vance's critique. The former president, according to Vance, has been actively diverting attention from the Epstein files, labeling the calls for their release as a "Democrat hoax." This maneuver is textbook Trump, attempting to muddy the waters of public discourse to the point where truth becomes a matter of allegiance rather than fact. However, Vance points out a fascinating twist in the tale: MAGA supporters, typically in lockstep with Trump's directives, are not heeding his calls to drop the Epstein issue. This divergence is notable, suggesting a fracture in the once-impenetrable facade of MAGA unity.

Vance's analysis underscores a critical point: "People who fight this hard to hide something have something to hide." This statement, simple yet profound, cuts to the heart of the issue. The reluctance to release the Epstein files, despite Trump's previous promises and the lack of legal barriers (as these are not grand jury materials), raises red flags of monumental proportions. It begs the question: What precisely is being concealed?

The release of documents by Congress, dismissed by Vance as a "charade," only adds layers to the intrigue. With virtually nothing new revealed, the public's hunger for truth remains unmet. The essence of the controversy lies not in political squabbles but in the quest for justice for the victims of Epstein's crimes. It's a matter of public interest, transcending partisan lines, as evidenced by the bipartisan efforts to urge the DOJ for transparency.

Vance's assertion that the current DOJ operates outside the realm of "normal rules" speaks volumes about the depth of dysfunction within the justice system's upper echelons. It paints a grim picture of a bureaucratic labyrinth designed not to serve justice but to obfuscate and protect the powerful.

In the end, Joyce Vance's call to release the Epstein files is not just about fulfilling a campaign promise or adhering to legal procedures. As the public, it's our responsibility to demand transparency and accountability, to refuse to let the spectacle distract us from seeking justice. The Epstein saga, with all its twists and turns, is more than a scandal; it's a mirror reflecting the systemic errors of our times. And it's high time we face it head-on.
