'Oops': Expert says FBI's 'irresponsible' move will 'undoubtedly fuel' conspiracy theories

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Politicization of Justice: A Scathing Analysis of the FBI's MisstepsTweets Over TransparencyA Carousel of ErrorsThe Trump FactorA Chilling ProspectIn Summary

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance slammed the FBI Thursday for its ongoing manhunt for Charlie Kirk’s killer, arguing that the entire investigation had become ‘politicized’ under the thumb of the Trump administration.

“We saw tweets instead of press conferences and faulty information presented as fact,” wrote Vance, who previously served as U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of Alabama, on her Substack “Civil Discourse.” “Instead of serving as the credible source for reliable updates that tamps down on conspiracy theories, this irresponsible action will undoubtedly fuel them.”

Kirk was killed Wednesday at Utah Valley University in an incident that drew international media attention and bi-partisan condemnation. His killer, however, remains on the loose two days later, with the FBI’s ongoing investigation already having produced several blunders.

“[Wednesday], the FBI director announced there was a suspect in custody; then, oops, it turned out that it wasn’t the guy, and he had to walk it back,” Vance wrote. “[Thursday], a [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives] agent told a right-wing influencer the shooter’s ammunition was engraved with pro-transgender sentiments, but officials are now saying that ‘could’ be wrong.”

The problem, Vance argued, was that under President Donald Trump, the ongoing investigation had been consumed by the “politics of the moment,” directly contrasting Trump and his allies’ response to Kirk’s killing with past instances of political violence, such as the recent killing of a Minnesota Democratic lawmaker, or the attack on Rep. Nancy Pelosi’s husband, the latter of which “Republicans made jokes” about, including Trump himself.

The “politicizing” of the FBI’s investigation had other fallouts as well, Vance argued, and she pondered whether loyalty to Trump may hamper the investigation further.

“Will investigators be ordered only to follow leads that confirm the president’s narrative about who the shooter is?” she wrote. “Will investigators be afraid to follow the facts, having seen how easily agents who were doing their jobs but ran afoul of Trump can be fired?”

Our Analysis:

The Politicization of Justice: A Scathing Analysis of the FBI's Missteps

In a recent revelation, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance criticized the FBI's handling of the manhunt for Charlie Kirk's killer, illuminating a disconcerting pattern of politicization under the Trump administration. Vance's critique, rooted in her experience and expertise, sheds light on a troubling distortion of justice and integrity within an institution meant to transcend political biases. Her commentary, as dissected below, underscores a series of egregious errors and political puppeteering that have not only marred the investigation but also fed the voracious appetite of conspiracy theorists.

Tweets Over Transparency

In what should have been an operation characterized by meticulousness and discretion, Vance points out how the FBI's approach was anything but. "We saw tweets instead of press conferences and faulty information presented as fact," she writes. This shift from professionalism to what can only be described as a haphazard dissemination of information does not just undermine the credibility of the FBI; it actively erodes public trust.

A Carousel of Errors

Vance brings to light a particularly damning incident where the FBI director prematurely announced a suspect in custody, only to "oops," retract the statement. This blunder was compounded by a claim from a Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives agent, suggesting the shooter's ammunition bore pro-transgender sentiments—a piece of information now tangled in uncertainty. These are not mere slip-ups; they are indicative of a deeper malaise within the investigation, one where facts are casualties of haste or, worse, manipulation.

The Trump Factor

The core of Vance's argument rests on the politicization of the FBI's investigation, directly tied to the influence of President Donald Trump. Vance contrasts the current administration's handling of Kirk's killing with past political violence, highlighting a distressing trend where empathy and justice are contingent on the victim's political alignment. The mention of Republicans, including Trump, making light of the attack on Rep. Nancy Pelosi's husband, is a stark reminder of the moral decay infecting political discourse and, by extension, critical institutions.

A Chilling Prospect

Vance's critical eye then turns to the future, pondering whether investigators will be shackled by the need to toe the president's narrative line. The fear that FBI agents might prioritize loyalty over truth is not just alarming—it's a dire warning. The question of whether facts will be followed or forsaken is a litmus test for the integrity of the FBI under Trump's shadow.

In Summary

Joyce Vance's analysis, while focused on a singular investigation, unveils a systemic issue within the FBI—a deviation from impartiality and accuracy, influenced by the political leanings of the Trump administration. Her insights reveal not just an investigation marred by errors and political interference, but a broader crisis of confidence in an institution that should embody the highest standards of justice.

In essence, Vance's critique is a clarion call for a return to principles that seem to have been forgotten or forsaken in the tumult of political agendas. The FBI's missteps in the manhunt for Charlie Kirk's killer are symptomatic of a larger, more insidious problem: the erosion of public trust in a body that should stand as a bulwark against chaos and conspiracy. This is not just about one investigation; it's about the soul of American justice.
