Tiny Trump just got smaller — and weaker

By Paulo Santos

The Power Play: Trump’s Weakness and Xi’s Strategic Dominance

A Display of Subservience

During a high-stakes summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, former U.S. President Donald Trump exhibited a striking display of subservience. This was not just a meeting between two global leaders; it was a clear demonstration of Trump’s inability to assert American interests on the international stage, particularly concerning key issues like Taiwan, Iran, and artificial intelligence. Trump’s approach was characterized by flattery and deference, starkly contrasted by Xi’s firm stance and unyielding rhetoric.

Misplaced Aggression and Misdirection

Trump’s reaction to his own shortcomings in diplomacy was tellingly redirected towards safer targets. After failing to secure any substantial commitments from Xi, Trump lashed out at New York Times reporter David Sanger, accusing him of treason for his critical reporting. This tactic of attacking the media and domestic political opponents while failing to confront actual geopolitical adversaries highlights a dangerous misdirection. It serves to shift public focus from substantive policy failures to manufactured internal threats.

The Charade of Accomplishment

Despite the lack of concrete achievements from the summit, Trump claimed to have struck “fantastic trade deals” with China, though details remained conspicuously absent. This claim seems to be another layer in the façade of success Trump attempted to construct around his presidency. Meanwhile, Xi maintained a composed and controlled presence, neither reciprocating Trump’s praises nor committing to any significant concessions, thereby positioning China as the more stable and reliable power.

Consequences of Weak Leadership

Trump’s performance at the summit did not just reflect personal failure but underscored a broader issue of American diminishment on the world stage. His inability to effectively advocate for U.S. interests, combined with his penchant for self-aggrandizement, resulted in strengthening China’s hand. Particularly concerning was Trump’s consideration of lifting sanctions on Chinese companies dealing with Iran and his indecision on a significant arms package for Taiwan—moves that would undoubtedly tilt the regional power balance in favor of Beijing.

Systemic Insight: The Cost of Ego in Diplomacy

Trump’s conduct during the China summit serves as a stark example of how personal ego and the quest for adulation can undermine national interest. This is not merely about one leader’s failings but reflects a systemic risk when leaders place personal validation over substantive diplomatic achievements. The pattern of seeking and responding to flattery from authoritarian leaders—while ignoring or undermining democratic norms and alliances—erodes the credibility and stability of American foreign policy.

In summary, the Xi-Trump summit was less about the meeting of two superpowers and more a revealing case of how personal weaknesses and misdirected priorities can lead to diplomatic debacles. Trump’s handling of the summit not only weakened the U.S.’s position but also demonstrated the dangers of leadership that values personal praise over political prudence. This event should prompt a broader reflection on the qualities necessary for effective leadership in the complex landscape of international relations.
