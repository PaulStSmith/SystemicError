“President Trump’s excuses for his party’s losses are getting stranger,” said Sargent, citing Trump’s quote that “I tell Republicans, you want to win elections, you got to talk about these facts,” which includes Trump’s own accomplishments since returning to office.

Trump even claimed “it’s really easy to win elections when you talk about the facts.”

“That strikes me as pretty deep denial,” Sargent told guest Jessica Post, a Democratic strategist. “It seems to me that, in just about every conceivable way, these elections were a repudiation of his record — particularly on the economy.”

Now, desperate and flailing, Sargent and Post said Trump is “openly” responding “to his losses by rigging the 2026 midterms” with “pernicious gerrymandering schemes”

“He actually came out and said it in another quote right after Election Day this week,” said Sargent. “He said this of Democrats: ‘Now, if we do what I’m saying, they’ll most likely never obtain power.’”

“So there you have it — end the filibuster, pass all these voting restrictions, and we will lock in Republican power forever,” said Sargent. “You’re not supposed to admit openly that you want to do these voting restrictions in order to rig the elections in your favor and create one-party rule — you’re supposed to say you want to do it in order to eliminate fraud, which is all b——, but that’s what they say. And at least most Republicans know that they’re supposed to say that. But Trump says straight out, let’s do all this stuff in order to lock in power for ourselves. It’s just amazing.”

“He’s definitely not hiding the ball there,” agreed Post. “Republicans received printed talking points saying, ‘We’re going to make it easier to vote and harder to cheat,’ after they decided they wanted to change all the voting laws leading up to the last 2024 election. And obviously, Trump — if he got that handout — he just decided to ignore it.”

Post said Trump’s gerrymandering scheme could result in “nine to twelve [Republican] House seats — and maybe even more … if Trump’s able to convince more states to act. But she added that people aren’t including Virginia in the calculation, which could add “a number of Dem seats.”

“So there’s really a range of possibilities here, going all the way from Republicans having five extra seats at the end of the day and being able to hold onto the House … or all this backfiring and Republicans actually losing a couple more seats and losing the House as a result.”

But Post added that the positive results for Democrats on Tuesday left her feeling optimistic about the outcome for midterms.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Electoral Strategy: A Masterclass in Transparency or a Descent into Despotism?

In the latest episode of Daily Blast, host Greg Sargent delivers a scathing critique of President Donald Trump’s reaction to the Republican Party’s recent electoral losses, painting a picture of a leader not just in denial, but actively plotting to undermine the democratic process through pernicious gerrymandering schemes. Let’s dissect this bold claim and analyze the implications of Trump’s statements and strategies.

Trump’s Delusion or Denial?

Trump’s assertion that discussing his administration’s “accomplishments” is a surefire electoral strategy reeks of either a profound disconnect from reality or a deliberate attempt to reshape it. Sargent’s analysis suggests Trump is spiraling into a deep denial about the public’s repudiation of his record, especially concerning economic management. This delusion, whether genuine or strategic, underscores a troubling disregard for the electorate’s intelligence and agency.

A Brazen Strategy for Election Rigging?

The most alarming aspect of Sargent’s commentary is the accusation that Trump is openly advocating for election rigging through gerrymandering and voting restrictions. Trump’s quote, as highlighted by Sargent, leaves little to the imagination: “Now, if we do what I’m saying, they’ll most likely never obtain power.” This is not just a dog whistle but a foghorn to his base, signaling an intent to cement Republican power indefinitely through manipulation rather than merit.

The Republican Playbook: Make It Easier to Vote or Harder to Cheat?

The Republican talking point, “We’re going to make it easier to vote and harder to cheat,” as mentioned by Democratic strategist Jessica Post, is a masterclass in political doublespeak. This rhetoric, designed to sound reasonable and security-focused, masks the underlying objective of disenfranchising likely Democratic voters. Trump’s apparent disregard for this subtlety, opting instead for blunt admission of his power-locking goals, is both astonishing and terrifying.

Gerrymandering: A Double-Edged Sword

Post’s analysis of Trump’s gerrymandering ambitions reveals a potential range of outcomes, from a modest Republican gain to a backfire that could see them losing the House. This uncertainty underscores the inherent risks in manipulating electoral boundaries for partisan gain. While Virginia’s inclusion in the calculations hints at possible Democratic advantages, the broader strategy threatens the very foundation of democratic representation.

Optimism in the Face of Adversity

Despite the grim picture painted by Trump’s tactics, Post’s optimism about the Democrats’ midterm prospects offers a glimmer of hope. The recent electoral outcomes suggest that voters are not entirely swayed by Trump’s narrative or Republican manipulation efforts. This resilience of the democratic process, while under threat, indicates a potential for course correction.

Conclusion: A Democracy at a Crossroads

The discussions between Sargent and Post lay bare the stark reality facing American democracy. Trump’s unabashed embrace of gerrymandering and voting restrictions as tools to cement power is a clarion call to all who value democratic principles. The path forward requires vigilance, engagement, and an unwavering commitment to preserving the integrity of the electoral process. The battle for the soul of the nation is far from over, and the outcome of this struggle will define the character of American democracy for generations to come.
