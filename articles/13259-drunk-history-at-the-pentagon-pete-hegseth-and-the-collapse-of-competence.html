Drunk History at the Pentagon: Pete Hegseth and the Collapse of Competence

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 27, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Pete Hegseth’s appointment as Secretary of Defense isn’t just an insult to the military—it’s a flaming middle finger to reason itself.

A man with a public record of misogyny, incompetence, and questionable sobriety was handed the keys to the world’s most powerful arsenal. Why? Because Donald Trump needed a loyalist with a loud mouth and no spine.

And boy, did he deliver.

“President Trump directed the most complex and secretive military operation in history.”
— Pete Hegseth, sober (allegedly)

This wasn’t just a lie. It was a full-on intelligence failure—by the man running our military intelligence. Trump didn’t “direct” a damn thing. The plans to bomb Iran’s nuclear sites predated him by years. Saying otherwise is not only fiction, it’s propaganda.

The real experts—the Pentagon brass, the intelligence community—refused to use the word “obliterated.” Why? Because the operation didn’t obliterate anything. At best, it set Iran back a few months. Senator Chris Murphy confirmed that. But Pete was too busy playing Pentagon karaoke to care.

This Isn’t an Intelligence Briefing. It’s a Loyalty Test.

Pete Hegseth’s entire performance was designed for one purpose: to serve the ego of Donald J. Trump. From ignoring actual damage assessments to steamrolling questions from experienced journalists like Jennifer Griffin, he operated not as a defense secretary but as a regime mouthpiece. And not even a good one.

Let’s be clear—when the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs demurs on bomb damage because the intel isn’t conclusive, and the Secretary of Defense barges in to say “obliterated,” what you’re watching is the military being warped into a PR wing for the MAGA machine.

Misogyny in a Flight Suit

And then there’s his disdain for women in uniform. Hegseth has made it clear: “We need moms, but not in the military.” His refusal to acknowledge the female bomber pilots involved in the Iran strike isn’t just an oversight. It’s ideology. When pressed, he fell back on the worn-out WWII-era phrase “our boys in bombers.”

When cornered, he offered the kind of dismissive, passive-aggressive retort that reeks of a man who still thinks Rosie the Riveter should’ve stayed in the kitchen:

“I'll keep saying things like that whether they're men or women.”

No, Pete. You’ll say what you believe. And what you believe is that women don’t belong in war—unless they’re in your crosshairs.

Don’t Mistake Spectacle for Strategy

From Hiroshima to D-Day to bin Laden’s takedown, actual complex and secretive military operations have defined American history. They required planning, secrecy, and a commander-in-chief capable of listening to experts and knowing when to say “go.”

Trump did none of that. He blabbed on camera about possibly bombing Iran and then rubber-stamped a plan pulled from a dusty Pentagon file. That’s not strategy. That’s playing general on Twitter.

And Pete Hegseth? He tried to package that cowardice as glory.

Final Assessment: Unfit for Command

Pete Hegseth failed his public intelligence assessment—again. He is a case study in what happens when a political movement values loyalty over leadership, volume over vision, and spin over strategy.

America doesn’t need boys in bombers.
It needs adults in charge.
