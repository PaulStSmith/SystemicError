Pope takes swipe at those who attack journalists — as Trump rages about 'fake news'

Pope Leo XIV holds an audience with representatives of the media in Paul VI hall at the Vatican, May 12, 2025. REUTERS/Eloisa Lopez

Pope Leo XIV dropped a political statement in his first conversation with the media on Monday.

Among his comments was praise for, and solidarity with, Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky. The pontiff then took a swipe at the lack of free speech in Russia.

“Let me, therefore, reiterate today the Church’s solidarity with journalists who are imprisoned for seeking and reporting the truth while also asking for their release," the pope said, according to a post on X by Fox News' chief national security correspondent, Jennifer Griffin.

"The Church recognizes in these witnesses – I am thinking of those who report on war even at the cost of their lives – the courage of those who defend dignity, justice and the right of people to be informed, because only informed individuals can make free choices. The suffering of these imprisoned journalists challenges the conscience of nations and the international community, calling on all of us to safeguard the precious gift of free speech and of the press," he said.

“The suffering of these imprisoned journalists challenges the conscience of nations and the international community, calling on all of us to safeguard the precious gift of free speech and of the press," he added. “Thank you, dear friends, for your service to the truth.”

The comments came just before U.S. President Donald Trump attacked an ABC News reporter during a press conference Monday morning.

Rachel Scott pressed Trump on the $400 million “palace in the sky” luxury airplane that Trump plans to accept from Qatar.

Trump called Scott “fake news” and said that she “should be embarrassed” for asking the question.

Our Analysis:

Pope Leo XIV's Political Foray: A Closer Examination

In a recent media interaction that could be easily mistaken for a diplomatic chess move rather than a pastoral outreach, Pope Leo XIV made headlines with a statement that was as politically charged as it was veiled in ecclesiastical concern. His words were not just a mere commentary but a calculated foray into the geopolitical quagmire that is the conflict between Ukraine and Russia, and a not-so-subtle critique of the state of free speech across the globe.

Solidarity with Ukraine and a Jab at Russia

The Pope's praise for Volodymyr Zelensky, the President of Ukraine, coupled with his expression of solidarity, is a significant political statement. It's not every day that the Vatican aligns itself so openly with the political leadership of a country embroiled in a conflict that has global ramifications. The underlying message here is clear: the Vatican does not just observe from the sidelines; it takes a moral stance, aligning itself with what it perceives as the just side in this conflict.

However, it's the Pope's swipe at Russia's lack of free speech that adds an extra layer of complexity to this narrative. By highlighting the plight of imprisoned journalists in Russia, Pope Leo XIV is not merely advocating for freedom of expression; he's implicitly critiquing the Russian administration's broader political and human rights practices. This move, while commendable for its defense of press freedom, risks being perceived as a direct confrontation with Russian authorities.

The Church, Free Speech, and Journalism

The Pope's assertion that the Church stands in solidarity with journalists who risk their lives and freedom to report the truth is a powerful testament to the role of the press in society. His words, "only informed individuals can make free choices," underscore a fundamental principle of democracy. However, the pontiff's call to safeguard the "precious gift of free speech and of the press" transcends mere advocacy. It's a demand for action from nations and the international community, a call that, unfortunately, is likely to be met with silence or token gestures by the very entities that systematically suppress these freedoms.

Trump's Attack on Free Speech

The timing of the Pope's comments is made even more poignant by their juxtaposition with former U.S. President Donald Trump's attack on an ABC News reporter, Rachel Scott. Trump's dismissal of Scott as "fake news" and his admonition that she "should be embarrassed" for questioning him about a controversial luxury airplane deal with Qatar is emblematic of the broader assault on the press witnessed in recent years, particularly in the United States.

Trump's actions, coming hot on the heels of the Pope's defense of journalistic freedom, serve as a stark reminder of the precarious state of press freedom, even in democracies that claim to champion this fundamental right. It's a vivid illustration of the systemic efforts to undermine the press, efforts that are not confined to authoritarian regimes but are increasingly evident in countries with long-standing democratic traditions.

Conclusion

Pope Leo XIV's foray into the political arena is a reminder of the Vatican's potential influence on global affairs. His statements, while grounded in a defense of human rights and dignity, navigate a fine line between moral leadership and political commentary. As the world grapples with the complexities of the Ukraine-Russia conflict, the erosion of free speech, and the relentless assault on the press, the Pope's words are a clarion call for a return to principles of justice, truth, and freedom.

However, the real test lies not in the articulation of these values but in their adoption and implementation by the global community. Without concerted action, the Pope's commendable stance risks being drowned out in a sea of political rhetoric and inaction, a fate that the champions of free speech and democracy can ill afford.
