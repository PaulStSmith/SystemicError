'We may not make it' if we don't stop Trump: Ex-astronauts launch campaign

Based on an article by Roxanne Cooper

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Astronauts Can See the Crisis. Washington Still Pretends It Is a Branding Problem.

More than 100 retired NASA astronauts have launched a “Vote for Freedom” campaign aimed at young voters in swing states, with Charles Bolden warning that Trump is mounting an “assault on democratic norms.” Their plan is simple: hit about 60 college campuses, push turnout, and grade congressional candidates on whether they actually respect the Constitution.

The Power Is Elsewhere

The astronauts are not the center of this story. They are a credibility layer stepping into a vacuum created by people who already hold institutional power and are failing to use it. The actual decision-makers are Trump, the congressional candidates they plan to judge, and the broader political class that keeps treating democratic breakdown like an abstract concern instead of a governing emergency.

That is why this campaign exists at all. Retired astronauts should not have to become civic air-raid sirens. When they do, it usually means the institutions that are supposed to defend the system have become too cautious, too calculating, or too compromised to say the obvious out loud.

The Source Names the Threat, Then Softens It

The reporting is strongest when Bolden directly names Trump and the attack on democratic norms. That is the real frame. The threat is not confusion, drift, or a vague decline in civic habits. It is deliberate political damage carried by a movement that has already normalized contempt for constitutional restraint.

But the piece also reaches for a safer, flatter register: democracy as a universal feeling, voting as a noble ritual, politics as a shared mission seen from 250 miles up. That language is emotionally useful and politically incomplete. It turns a concrete fight against authoritarian politics into a generalized civic uplift campaign. The danger is not lack of wonder. The danger is organized power.

Young Voters Are Not the Problem

The article points its energy at young voters, which makes sense tactically, but the burden is still being pushed downward. The people who have already broken norms, enabled assaults on institutions, and turned constitutional loyalty into a branding exercise are not the ones being asked to do more.

That is the recurring American fraud: whenever the system is damaged by elites, the repair plan gets handed to everyone else. Vote more. Care harder. Show up. The demand is always directed at the least powerful participants, while the most powerful actors keep their leverage, their platforms, and their excuses. Voting matters. But the moral burden should not be dumped on the people least responsible for the wreckage.

The Democratic Job Is Not Optional

If this story points anywhere politically, it points to the necessity of a serious democratic coalition. Not civility theater. Not generic “unity.” Actual institutional resistance to authoritarian politics.

In current U.S. politics, that means the Democratic Party is not a decorative alternative. It is the necessary vehicle for stopping the Trump project and the political enablers around it. The problem is not that the astronauts are partisan; the problem is that too much of the political system still acts as if naming the democratic line of defense is impolite. It is not impolite. It is the job.

If Democrats want to be treated as the institutional backbone against authoritarianism, they have to behave like it: choose conflict, demand accountability, and stop outsourcing urgency to celebrities, veterans, judges, astronauts, or whoever else is available to say what elected officials won’t.

Orbit Does Not Solve Earth

The “one country” view from space is a useful metaphor only if it does not become a lie. From orbit, borders blur. On the ground, power is sorted into offices, committees, campaign cash, candidate cowardice, and coordinated attacks on constitutional limits.

That is the larger pattern here. American democracy keeps depending on moral warnings from people outside the machinery because the machinery itself keeps dragging its feet. The astronauts are not proof that politics is healthy. They are proof that the republic is in such bad shape that even retired spacefarers have to become turnout organizers.
