'Disgusting': Trump DOJ sparks outrage with suggested sentence for killer cop

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Department of Justice is recommending a one-day sentence for the cop who killed Breonna Taylor in 2020, according to reports.

Louisville, Kentucky, police officer Brett Hankison was found guilty of violating Taylor's civil rights last year. Hankinson fired several shots into Taylor's home while she was sleeping in a drug raid gone awry. He faces a maximum penalty of life in prison.

On Wednesday, Harmeet Dhillon, the assistant attorney general for civil rights, told the judge that Hankinson's prosecution was excessive, The New York Times reported. Dillon asked the judge to consider sentencing him to one day in prison, which amounts to time served, and three years of supervised release.

The Times described the request as "a stunning reversal of the unit’s longstanding efforts to address racial disparities in policing."

“The jury’s verdict will almost certainly ensure that defendant Hankison never serves as a law enforcement officer again and will also likely ensure that he never legally possesses a firearm again," Dillon wrote in a court filing.

The request for a light sentence sparked outrage on social media.

"This recommendation is an immense travesty of justice that completely disregards the jury’s verdict," Rep. Jerry Nadler (D-NY) wrote in a post on X. "Breonna Taylor was murdered in her own home by officers of the Louisville Police Department executing a ‘no-knock’ search warrant. Her horrific death underscored the urgent need for police reform in this country."

"This is disgusting. Even in the era of no shame, it’s shameful," former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance wrote on X.

"Brett Hankison blindly fired 10 shots through a covered window into Breonna Taylor’s home," Rep. Morgan McGarvey (D-KY) wrote on X. "The fact that Donald Trump’s DOJ thinks Breonna Taylor’s life is worth just a one-day jail sentence is morally reprehensible and deeply insulting. This is a dark day for our entire city."

"Her life, Black lives, are worth more than a day," former federal prosecutor Maya Wiley wrote on X. "Trump Exec. Order explicitly stated police should be 'unleashed.' Police must be accountable. This isn’t accountability!"

Our Analysis:

A Mockery of Justice: The Insult of a One-Day Sentence for Breonna Taylor's Killer

In a move that can only be described as a gross miscarriage of justice, the Department of Justice's recommendation for a one-day sentence for Brett Hankison, the Louisville, Kentucky police officer who killed Breonna Taylor, is not just a slap in the face to Taylor's memory and her family, but a glaring testament to the systemic failures that plague our justice system. This decision is not just outrageous; it's a stark illustration of the racial disparities and the lack of accountability that continues to corrode trust in law enforcement and the legal system at large.

The Facts That Cannot Be Ignored

Breonna Taylor was asleep in her home when her life was abruptly ended by a barrage of bullets fired blindly by Officer Hankison during a disastrously executed drug raid. The fact that these actions led to a mere recommendation of a one-day sentence, effectively time served, is nothing short of appalling. This recommendation comes despite Hankison facing a maximum penalty of life in prison for violating Taylor's civil rights, a verdict reached due to the egregious nature of his actions.

A "Stunning Reversal"

The New York Times reports this sentencing recommendation as "a stunning reversal" of the Justice Department's supposed commitment to addressing racial disparities in policing. It's a reversal that speaks volumes about the current state of affairs within the Department of Justice, underlining a disheartening departure from the pursuit of justice to what appears to be an appeasement of certain political or societal pressures. The assistant attorney general for civil rights, Harmeet Dhillon's assertion that Hankison's prosecution was "excessive" flies in the face of the facts and is an insult to the very notion of civil rights.

The Outrage Is Justified

The outrage sparked by this recommendation is not only justified but necessary. Figures like Rep. Jerry Nadler, former federal prosecutors Joyce Vance and Maya Wiley, and Rep. Morgan McGarvey have voiced their disgust and disbelief, not just as a matter of legal principle but as a matter of moral outrage. They're not alone. The sentiment on social media and among the public echoes a collective disbelief and anger towards this flagrant disregard for a human life lost at the hands of those sworn to protect.

It's not just about Breonna Taylor. This case, and the DOJ's recommendation, embodies the systemic issues that allow law enforcement officers to escape meaningful accountability for actions that result in the loss of Black lives. The message this sends is clear: the system does not value all lives equally, and it is willing to contort itself to protect those within it, at the expense of justice.

A Call for Real Accountability

This isn't accountability; it's a farce. The reference to Trump's Executive Order to "unleash" police, as mentioned by Maya Wiley, underscores a broader societal issue where the empowerment of police is prioritized over the protection of citizens' rights and lives. True accountability means facing real consequences for actions, especially when those actions result in the loss of life. Anything less is not just a failure of justice; it's an endorsement of the very behaviors that necessitate police reform and a reevaluation of our justice system.

In Conclusion: More Than a Day's Worth

Breonna Taylor's life, and by extension, all Black lives, are worth infinitely more than the one day in jail proposed for her killer. This recommendation is a painful reminder of the long road ahead in the fight for justice and equality. It's a call to action for all those who believe in justice, equity, and the value of every human life to stand up and demand better from our institutions, our justice system, and our society at large.

The Department of Justice's recommendation is not just a failure; it's a systemic error that requires correction. To honor Breonna Taylor's memory, and the countless others who have suffered at the hands of a flawed system, we must not rest until genuine accountability and systemic reforms are realized.
