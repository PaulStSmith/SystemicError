Trump 'runs the risk of humiliating the US' by overselling deal: State Dept veteran

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Victory Before the Paper Exists

The Real Power

The source makes one thing clear: the people with actual leverage are not the ones posing for the cameras. On the Iranian side, the Revolutionary Guard is described as the force “really controlling Iran right now,” with the supreme leader and his circle still the only actors that matter. On the American side, Trump controls the public story, the timetable, and the pressure to declare success before the substance exists.

That asymmetry is the whole problem. One side controls coercive state power. The other controls the narrative machine.

A Deal in Search of Terms

The reporting describes a signed agreement to open the Strait of Hormuz, but also admits the terms have not been publicized and the document is only “a very general document” about a page and a half long. That is not a settlement. That is a placeholder.

Trump’s real move here is not diplomacy. It is preemption. He is trying to convert a vague paper and an ambiguous set of contacts into a finished political victory. That is not negotiating from strength; it is declaring the scoreboard before the game ends.

Who Enabled the Misinformation

The enabling decision is obvious: Trump hyped the agreement as though it were substantive before the substance existed. His vice president’s own caution undercuts the performance. If the administration itself says the text is thin, then the public celebration is not confusion. It is construction.

That matters because overselling a fragile document is not a harmless exaggeration. It sets up a diplomatic failure that can be blamed on everyone except the person who rushed to announce it. The risk is not just embarrassment. It is that the United States becomes the author of its own humiliation while pretending to be the adult in the room.

The Usual Misdirection

This is where the framing starts to do its usual dirty work. The story leans toward the familiar language of uncertainty, as if the problem were a fog of competing interpretations. It is not. The problem is an asymmetry of agency.

Trump is not being misled by chaos. He is generating it by racing ahead of the paper. The Iranian leadership is not a blank space waiting to be clarified; it is a hardened power structure with its own hierarchy, incentives, and constraints. When a U.S. president treats that as a technical misunderstanding, he is not making diplomacy easier. He is disguising the limits of his own control.

Israel Is Not Buying the Performance

The article notes that Israel does not seem supportive and is reacting cautiously. That caution is not a side detail. It is a warning that the region understands what Washington is trying to do here: wrap uncertainty in the language of breakthrough and hope the headline holds longer than the facts do.

Regional actors do not need the administration’s messaging. They need the actual terms. Without them, they are left to absorb the consequences of a U.S. announcement built on political vanity.

The Pattern

This is the modern imperial habit in miniature: claim resolution first, define reality later, and let institutions clean up the wreckage. The public is asked to confuse motion with achievement and announcement with policy. The state is reduced to a stage set for leaderly self-regard.

That is not an accidental byproduct of weak diplomacy. It is the method. Power first manufactures the appearance of control, then forces everyone else to respond to the fiction. The resulting chaos is not a bug in the system. It is how the system protects the people who make the mess.
