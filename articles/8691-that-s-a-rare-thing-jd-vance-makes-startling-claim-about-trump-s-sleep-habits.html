The vice president and president do not travel together for safety and national security reasons, and the 41-year-old Vance told Fox News host Sean Hannity that other administration officials are jealous of his travel arrangements, reported The Daily Beast.

“They are always like, ‘You’re so lucky because if we go on a 20-hour trip somewhere, he does not sleep the entire time,’” Vance said, “and, of course if he’s not sleeping, if he’s working, he expects everybody else to be working, too.”

Hannity recalled a similar experience when he went on a golfing trip with the president.

“Seventeen hours there, 17 hours back, and he’s awake the whole time,” Hannity said, “and he expects you to be awake.”

Katie Miller, the right-wing podcaster and wife of White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, made a similar claim about Trump’s sleeping habits last month to Fox News host Laura Ingraham.

“The president doesn’t sleep, so why does (Stephen) need to?” she said.

Ingraham agreed, saying that she’s heard the same thing about Trump.

“He just stands over him on Air Force One and says, ‘Stephen, are you awake?’” Ingraham said. “‘Why would I be awake, sir? I’m not, it’s the middle of the night.’”

Individuals who worked with Trump on his “Apprentice” reality TV shows have claimed he routinely used stimulants, and a January 2024 Defense Department inspector general report showed the White House Medical Unit during his first term dispensed thousands of doses of the stimulant modafinil, which has long been used by military pilots to stay alert, and a former official told Rolling Stone the White House at that time was “awash in speed.”

However, Vance told Hannity that Trump was “extremely healthy” and insisted his energy is “off the charts.”

“You gotta have that level of energy to do this job successfully,” the vice president said.

“Agree or disagree with President Trump about a given issue, every Democrat and Republican would recognize that this is a guy that has the energy to do the job,” Vance added. “That’s a rare thing, but that’s who you should be electing as a president, somebody who has the energy for it.”

Our Analysis:

The Myth of the Sleepless Leader: Unpacking the Glorification of Trump’s “Energy”

In a recent revelation that seems more akin to a poorly scripted reality TV show than the solemn chambers of the White House, Vice President JD Vance extolled the seemingly superhuman stamina of President Donald Trump. According to Vance, during overseas trips, the President scarcely blinks at the need for sleep, much less succumbs to it. This narrative, peddled with gusto on Fox News, paints Trump as a tireless titan of industry, a man whose energy reserves are limitless, expectations inexorable, and commitment to work, unassailable.

But let’s not be beguiled by this thinly veiled attempt at myth-making. This narrative is not just implausible; it’s dangerous. It glorifies a work ethic that is not only unrealistic but also perilously unhealthy.

Firstly, the suggestion that Trump’s sleepless vigils are a mark of his dedication is deeply flawed. Sleep, as countless studies have shown, is not a luxury; it’s a necessity. The human body is not designed for 24/7 operation without serious health repercussions. The Vice President’s anecdote, therefore, doesn’t highlight an admirable quality; it spotlights a reckless disregard for basic human biology.

Moreover, the claim that Trump expects his staff to mirror his sleepless habits is troubling. It promotes a culture of overwork and burnout, which is antithetical to productivity and well-being. This ‘always-on’ mentality has been debunked time and again for leading to diminished mental health, increased stress levels, and lower overall productivity.

But perhaps the most alarming part of this narrative is the insinuation of stimulant use. The article mentions that during Trump’s first term, the White House Medical Unit dispensed “thousands of doses of the stimulant modafinil”, a drug used to keep military pilots alert. This isn’t just a red flag; it’s a blazing inferno of concern. The casual mention of a workplace “awash in speed” paints a picture of an administration relying on pharmaceutical crutches to maintain an illusion of indefatigability.

Vance’s assertion that Trump is “extremely healthy” and possesses energy “off the charts” flies in the face of these concerns. It’s a narrative that dangerously conflates stimulant-fueled wakefulness with genuine health and vitality. Such a distinction is crucial, yet conspicuously absent in Vance’s glorification of Trump’s energy levels.

The idea that this relentless energy is what qualifies someone for the presidency is not just misguided; it’s fundamentally flawed. Leadership requires wisdom, judgment, empathy, and yes, energy. But it also requires the ability to rest, to reflect, and to approach problems with a clear and rested mind. The glorification of sleeplessness under the guise of dedication is a disservice to the nuanced demands of leadership.

In conclusion, while Vance’s portrayal of Trump as a tireless leader might play well to a certain audience, it’s a dangerous myth that needs debunking. Real strength lies not in eschewing sleep in favor of work, but in balancing the demands of leadership with the needs of the human body. It’s high time we dismantle the toxic glorification of overwork and recognize it for what it is: not a badge of honor, but a serious hazard to our health and well-being.
