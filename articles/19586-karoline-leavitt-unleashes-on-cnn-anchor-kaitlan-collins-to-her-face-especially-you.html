Karoline Leavitt unleashes on CNN anchor Kaitlan Collins to her face: 'Especially you!'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by the White House Press Secretary

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt holds a press briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C. on March 4, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt directly attacked CNN’s Kaitlan Collins on Wednesday during the first press briefing since the United States and its ally Israel launched military strikes against Iran.

Leavitt had made several comments criticizing media coverage of the Trump administration and went after reporters at the White House, specifically Collins and CNN.

The Trump administration has presented several different objectives since launching its military operation five days ago — with Cabinet members and even President Donald Trump giving conflicting information over what prompted the attacks and led to the regional conflict that has now left six American troops dead.

“Is it the position of this administration that the press should not prominently cover the deaths of U.S. service members?” Collins asked Leavitt.

“No, it’s the position of this administration that the press in this room and the press across this country should report on the success of Operation Epic Fury and the damage it is doing to the rogue Iranian regime that has threatened the lives of every single American in this room,” Leavitt said. “If the Iranian regime had their choice, they would kill every single person in this room, and so we can all be very grateful that we have an administration, that we have men and women in our armed forces who are willing to sacrifice their own lives for the rest of us in this room and for every American across the country, and for every troop that is based in the Middle East.”

Collins pushed back on what Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had said earlier Wednesday. When Leavitt started to clash with her, things became personal.

“But Secretary Hegseth was complaining that it was front-page news about these six service members who were killed,” Collins said.

“That’s not what the secretary said, and that’s not what he meant, and you know it!” Leavitt said, appearing visibly upset by Collins’ statement. “You are being disingenuous. There is not — we’ve never had a secretary of defense who cares more...”

Collins then interjected and read the statement directly from Hegseth, who had claimed that the press had purposefully tried to speak badly about Trump.

“The press only wants to make the president look bad,” Leavitt said. As you know, the press, the deaths of U.S. service members under every president. The press does only want to make the president look bad. That’s a fact. Especially, you know, listen to me, especially you, and especially CNN, and the secretary of defense cares deeply about our warfighters and our men and women in uniform. He travels all across this country to meet with them, to connect with them. And your network has hardly ever probably reported on that.”

Collins responded again to Leavitt’s attacks — pointing out that covering the slain military members was not an attempt to attack Trump.

“That’s not making the president look bad, that’s showcasing that,” Collins said.

“And I just told you that the president of the United States will be attending their dignified transfer. So please. So, please,” Leavitt said. “We expect you to cover that as you should, Kaitlan. But you and your network know that you take every single thing this administration says and tries to use it to make the president look bad. That is an objective fact.”

Collins pushed back again.

“I don’t think covering troop deaths is trying to make the president look bad,” Collins said.

“If you’re trying to argue right now that CNN’s overwhelming coverage is not negative of President Donald Trump, I think the American people would tend to agree, and your ratings would tend to disagree with that as well,” Leavitt said.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by the White House Press Secretary

In a recent spectacle at the White House, Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt transformed what could have been a straightforward press briefing into a theatrical performance of evasion and attack, particularly targeting CNN’s Kaitlan Collins. The event underscored the Trump administration’s continued efforts to manipulate narratives, especially surrounding the contentious military strikes against Iran, dubbed “Operation Epic Fury.”

The Facts: A Muddled Narrative

From the get-go, the Trump administration has been anything but clear about the objectives and motivations behind the recent military operations against Iran. The administration’s narrative has been a confusing mix of statements, with President Trump and various Cabinet members providing conflicting information. This lack of clarity is not just a minor oversight; it’s a significant issue when American lives are at stake, with six service members already reported dead because of the conflict.

A Direct Attack on Press Integrity

Leavitt’s confrontation with Collins wasn’t just a disagreement; it was a blatant attempt to discredit the press. By suggesting that the media, specifically CNN, is more interested in tarnishing the president’s image than reporting facts, Leavitt not only undermines the essential role of journalism in democracy but also deflects from the administration’s responsibility to provide clear, consistent information to the public.

The Irony of Caring

The press secretary’s claim that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth “cares deeply” about American service members, juxtaposed with the accusation that the press reporting on their deaths is an attack on Trump, is drenched in irony. Acknowledging the sacrifice of these individuals should never be construed as political ammunition. Yet, Leavitt’s insinuation that such coverage is inherently negative towards the president reveals a disturbing conflation of national tragedy with political image.

The Real Disinformation

Leavitt’s assertion that “the press does only want to make the president look bad” is a dangerous oversimplification. This blanket statement not only discredits the essential watchdog role of the media but also ignores the complexity of reporting in politically charged environments. The idea that coverage of troop deaths—an inherently non-partisan issue—is used to undermine the president is not just disingenuous; it’s a harmful narrative that prioritizes political image over human lives.

CNN and the Ratings Red Herring

Bringing CNN’s ratings into the argument, as Leavitt did, is a classic red herring. The suggestion that negative coverage of Trump correlates with public opinion or the network’s credibility is a misleading diversion. It’s an attempt to discredit the messenger when the message is unflattering, rather than addressing the substance of the coverage.

Conclusion: The Duty of the Press and the Administration

The clash between Leavitt and Collins isn’t just a personal vendetta; it’s emblematic of the Trump administration’s broader strategy to control the narrative at all costs—even at the expense of transparency and accountability. The role of the press is to ask hard questions, seek truth, and report facts, especially in matters of life and death. Conversely, the administration’s duty is to provide clear, honest information to the American public. When officials attack the press for fulfilling their role, it’s not just an affront to journalism—it’s a threat to the democratic principles that govern our nation.
