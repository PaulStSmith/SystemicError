Desperate Trump gaslights voters after self-inflicted mess: Nobel economist

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Trump’s “Golden Age” Is a Cover Story

The Real Story

Paul Krugman’s target is not the economy itself so much as the lie built around it: Trump’s people are trying to sell “golden age” optimism while blaming Joe Biden for the damage Trump’s own return to office has worsened. The article’s real subject is not inflation trivia. It is the machinery of blame-shifting.

Power, Not Confusion

Actual institutional power sits with Trump and the officials carrying his message, including the White House itself, which is advertising a “Welcome to the golden age!” banner while the numbers move the other way. That is not a misunderstanding. It is an attempt to overwrite responsibility after making a mess.

When leaders control the presidency, the messaging apparatus, and the policy levers, they do not get to pose as spectators. If prices rise, rates jump, growth slows, and inequality widens on their watch, they own the outcome. The source material is clear that Trump and his allies are trying to make Biden absorb consequences for conditions that have deteriorated since Trump re-assumed office.

The Biden Detour

The Biden reference is doing the work that weak arguments always do: it diverts attention from who governs now. Krugman’s account notes that inflation surged in 2021-2022, then fell significantly without a recession, and that by the end of Biden’s term the U.S. economy was outperforming other advanced countries. That matters because it destroys the lazy claim that Trump inherited a ruin.

Yet Trump’s circle reaches for Biden first because they need a scapegoat large enough to cover their own failures. This is not an economic explanation. It is a reflex of political self-protection.

The Damage Under Trump

The article says the Trump economy has already deteriorated on several fronts: slower growth despite the AI boom, higher inflation, surging interest rates, and widening inequality in a K-shaped economy. Wages have not kept up with inflation, while corporate profits have soared.

That distribution is the point. The pain is not shared evenly, and the gains are not either. Trump’s promises about cutting energy prices in half and lowering grocery prices on day one were never serious policy commitments. They were a sales pitch. Now the sales pitch has met the household reality that more people say they are worse off than a year ago than better off, by almost two to one.

Propaganda With a Ledger

The White House “fact sheet,” the blanket “golden age” language, the obsession with Biden, the bond-market theater, and the bizarre imported-beef promise all belong to the same category: propaganda strained over material failure. The source captures a government trying to talk down the bond markets while talking up the public, as if repetition can substitute for competence.

That is why the framing in Trump-world is so weak. It does not explain conditions. It obscures agency. It treats consequences as if they arrived from nowhere, then tries to pin them on a predecessor who is no longer in office.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian-style politics works in practice: make bad decisions, flood the zone with slogans, and force the public to argue over a false origin story. The aim is not to persuade honestly. It is to exhaust accountability.

The economic story here is not that voters are confused. It is that Trump’s circle is desperate because the data are becoming harder to launder. Once the economy stops cooperating with the myth, the myth has to do more work. That is always the sign of a government that knows it is failing and is reaching for language to delay judgment.
