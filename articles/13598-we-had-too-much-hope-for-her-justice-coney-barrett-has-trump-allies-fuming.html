'We had too much hope for her': Justice Coney Barrett has Trump allies fuming

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 16, 20254 min read

Justice Amy Coney Barrett (Photo: Official Supreme Court photo)

Supreme Court Justice Amy Coney Barrett's growing alliance with the liberal wing of the court has members of Donald Trump's inner circle kicking themselves for handing her a lifetime appointment.

According to a report from the New York Times' Jodi Kantor, there was some resistance to her nomination to fill the seat previously held by Justice Ruth Bader Ginsburg, but the president overruled them believing she would help him the evangelical vote.

It turns out both camps were right, and now there is dismay in the White House about her "leftward tilt."

Case in point, from the Times: "When Justices Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan agreed on nonunanimous decisions this term, Justice Barrett joined them 82 percent of the time — up from 39 percent of the time in her first term."

The report notes, "Some of Mr. Trump’s allies have turned on her, accusing the justice of being a turncoat and calling her — a mother of seven, with two Black children adopted from Haiti — a 'D.E.I. hire,'" Kantor wrote before adding, "She has become the Republican-appointed justice most likely to be in the majority in decisions that reach a liberal outcome, according to a new analysis of her record prepared for The New York Times. Her influence — measured by how often she is on the winning side — is rising. Along with the chief justice, a frequent voting partner, Justice Barrett could be one of the few people in the country to check the actions of the president."

Far-right legal activist Mike Davis complained, "We had too much hope for her. She doesn’t have enough courage.”

The report goes on to note, "Mr. Trump has privately complained about her too, according to two people familiar with his thinking."

According to Noah Feldman, a Harvard law professor who has been friends with her since they served as clerks together, he is not surprised at the direction she has been taking.

"It’s a mistake by ignorant conservatives and wishful liberals to believe she’s moderating,” he explained. "She’s exactly the person I met 25 years ago: principled, absolutely conservative, not interested in shifting.”

Our Analysis:

The Unpredictable Swing of Justice Amy Coney Barrett

The recent revelation that Supreme Court Justice Amy Coney Barrett appears to be aligning more frequently with the court's liberal wing has sent shockwaves through the conservative establishment, which once championed her appointment. This development, as reported by the New York Times' Jodi Kantor, showcases a fascinating turn of events that not only highlights the unpredictability of judicial tenures but also the inherent folly in the political calculus that often surrounds Supreme Court nominations.

The Conservative Miscalculation

At the heart of this miscalculation was former President Donald Trump and his inner circle, who, in their quest to solidify evangelical support, overlooked the complexities of judicial independence and integrity. The article outlines how Trump overruled resistance within his camp to nominate Barrett, believing she would be a stalwart conservative voice on the bench, particularly on issues important to his evangelical base. Yet, the reality, as it often is with Supreme Court Justices, proved to be far less predictable.

The Leftward Tilt?

The statistics cited from Kantor's report are telling: "When Justices Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan agreed on nonunanimous decisions this term, Justice Barrett joined them 82 percent of the time — up from 39 percent of the time in her first term." This notable shift towards alignment with the court's liberal justices has understandably caused disarray among those who championed her nomination, leading to unfounded and reprehensible accusations of her being a "D.E.I. hire" due to her family composition.

The Misguided Outrage

The outrage from Trump and his allies, including far-right legal activist Mike Davis, who lamented Barrett's supposed lack of courage, is as misguided as it is predictable. It reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of Supreme Court justices, who are not, and should never be, beholden to the political whims of those who appoint them. Their duty is to the Constitution and the law, not to the president or a particular political ideology.

The Reality of Judicial Independence

Noah Feldman, a Harvard law professor and longtime acquaintance of Barrett, offers perhaps the most insightful perspective on her evolving judicial record. Feldman argues that Barrett remains a principled conservative, not swayed by political pressures or expectations. This assertion should serve as a reminder of the inherent independence of the judiciary, a cornerstone of the American legal system that seems to have been forgotten or ignored by those who now criticize Barrett's rulings.

Conclusion: The Folly of Political Expectations

The unfolding narrative of Justice Amy Coney Barrett's tenure on the Supreme Court serves as a poignant reminder of the perils of politicizing judicial appointments. It underscores the fact that justices, once appointed, are under no obligation to toe the line of the presidents who appoint them or the political ideologies of those presidents. They are, instead, guided by their interpretation of the law and the Constitution. This reality, while frustrating to some, is a testament to the strength and independence of the U.S. judicial system.

The backlash against Barrett's perceived "leftward tilt" reveals more about the expectations and motivations of her critics than it does about her judicial philosophy or the merits of her decisions. It's a cautionary tale of what happens when the appointment of Supreme Court justices is viewed through the narrow lens of political gain rather than the broader perspective of legal acumen and integrity.
