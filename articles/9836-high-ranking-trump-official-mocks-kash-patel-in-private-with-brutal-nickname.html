High-ranking Trump official mocks Kash Patel in private with brutal nickname

By Paulo Santos

Kash Patel, U.S. President Donald Trump’s nominee to be director of the FBI, looks on as he testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 30, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Anthony Salisbury, the second in command at the Department of Homeland Security, issued FBI Director Kash Patel a brutal nickname in messages to high-ranking Trump administration officials, according to a series of leaked text messages obtained by the Minnesota Star Tribune and repotted on Friday.

A source provided the outlet with a series of images of Salisbury’s alleged text messages that he wrote while attending a private event in Minnesota, which the White House said was his uncle’s funeral. The messages, the source said, were written “in clear view of others,” and among them included remarks about Patel that suggested Salisbury wasn’t fond of the FBI’s leader.

The message included some praise for Patel over his willingness to fire FBI agents photographed kneeling in solidarity with the 2020 George Floyd protests. The praise was short-lived, however, and was quickly followed by a brutal insult.

“This is how Kash survives,” Salisbury wrote, according to the Minnesota Star Tribune. “He will do this stuff for the man but day to day giant douche canoe.”

Patel has already faced scrutiny for making good, in part, on his pledge to purge President Donald Trump’s political enemies from the agency, which has seen dozens of prosecutors and agency veterans ousted since Trump took office in January. He’s also faced scrutiny from the MAGA base, largely over his handling of the investigation into Jeffrey Epstein, the convicted sex offender whose alleged co-conspirators remain unnamed and uncharged.

Patel also faced blowback over his botched handling of the investigation into the killing of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, an investigation so compromised – including byPatel’s own choice of words, potentially – that the White House and the Justice Department had “no confidence” left in his leadership, according to an anonymous source who spoke withFox News.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Leaked Texts and Political Turbulence within the Department of Homeland Security

In a recent revelation by the Minnesota Star Tribune, a series of leaked text messages from Anthony Salisbury, the Deputy Chief at the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), have exposed a rift within the ranks of high-ranking Trump administration officials. These messages, sent during what was ostensibly a personal event in Minnesota, pull back the curtain on the internal dynamics and discord that seem to plague this administration.

The Unflattering Nickname

Salisbury’s decision to label FBI Director Kash Patel as a “giant douche canoe” is not only a brutal personal attack but also indicative of the deep-seated animosity and division within the Trump administration. This derogatory nickname, while puerile, underscores a more significant concern: the apparent lack of cohesion and respect among top officials tasked with safeguarding the nation’s security. It’s both alarming and telling when officials resort to such pejoratives to describe their colleagues.

A Conditional Praise

The leaked texts reveal a moment of grudging respect from Salisbury towards Patel, specifically regarding Patel’s actions against FBI agents who expressed solidarity with the 2020 George Floyd protests. Salisbury’s fleeting commendation, however, quickly devolves into insult, highlighting a disturbing trend within the administration: the prioritization of loyalty to Trump over professional integrity and ethical conduct. Salisbury’s approval of Patel’s willingness to fire agents for expressing solidarity with a civil rights movement is emblematic of a broader, systemic disdain for dissenting voices within the ranks of government agencies under Trump’s presidency.

Patel Under Fire

Kash Patel’s tenure as FBI Director has been marred by controversy and criticism, much of it stemming from his own actions and the political motivations behind them. His endeavor to purge the agency of Trump’s perceived political enemies—a move that has led to the ousting of numerous prosecutors and agency veterans—speaks volumes about the politicization of what should be an impartial federal agency. Furthermore, Patel’s handling of high-profile cases, including the Jeffrey Epstein investigation and the botched probe into the killing of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, has not only damaged the credibility of the FBI but also eroded public trust in the institution.

A Deeper Issue at Hand

What these leaked messages and the ensuing fallout reveal is a deeper, more systemic issue within not just the Department of Homeland Security, but the Trump administration at large. The blatant politicization of federal agencies, the internal discord among top officials, and the disregard for ethical standards and professional integrity are symptomatic of a larger malaise. It’s a malaise that threatens not only the operational effectiveness of these agencies but also the very principles upon which they were founded.

In this glaring light of discord and dysfunction, one must question the long-term ramifications of such systemic issues on national security and public trust. The leaked messages from Salisbury, while providing a momentary glimpse into the petty squabbles among high-ranking officials, underscore a much more significant and concerning trend of politicization and division. This is not merely about personal animosity or political maneuvering; it’s about the erosion of institutional integrity and the potential consequences for American democracy.
