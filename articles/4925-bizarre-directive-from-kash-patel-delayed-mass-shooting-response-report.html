Bizarre directive from Kash Patel delayed mass shooting response: report

The FBI was delayed in responding to the Brown University mass shooting last December in large part due to a confusing order from FBI Director Kash Patel, a whistleblower and three inside sources recently told Congress, MS NOW reported on Tuesday.

The shooting, which left two students dead and nine others wounded, sparked a chaotic manhunt as the shooter managed to evade authorities for days until he was reportedly found dead from a self-inflicted gunshot wound.

According to the report, Patel was in Florida at the time of the shooting, using one of the FBI’s two available jets. In an order that left FBI officials “confused,” Patel had also ordered the FBI’s second jet to be held “for another team that would not normally respond to the scene,” MS NOW reported.

The whistleblower’s account of events was shared with Sen. Richard Durbin (D-IL), the top Democrat on the Senate Judiciary Committee, who demanded answers in a letter sent to the Justice Department and the Government Accountability Office on Tuesday.

“The Director’s misplaced priorities and poor management of the FBI’s resources – including its aircraft – also harmed the FBI’s ability to respond to the shooting at Brown University on December 13, 2025,” Durbin wrote in the letter, obtained by MS NOW.

“Since his confirmation as Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation, Kash Patel has seemingly engaged in what amounts to irresponsible joyriding on DOJ and FBI-operated aircraft at the expense of the American taxpayer and to the detriment of ongoing Bureau operations.”

Patel has faced scrutiny in the past over his use of FBI resources, including theallegationthat he had used an FBI jet to travel to Nashville, Tennessee, to watch his then-27-year-old girlfriend, a country music singer, perform on stage.

Our Analysis:

The FBI’s Mismanagement: A Deeper Look into the Brown University Tragedy

In an alarming revelation that underscores the dangerous confluence of misplaced priorities and poor management within the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), recent reports have brought to light a distressing series of events surrounding the mass shooting at Brown University last December. The incident, which tragically claimed two students’ lives and left nine others wounded, has now been further marred by the actions—or, more accurately, the inactions—of FBI Director Kash Patel.

A Confounding Decision at a Critical Moment

At the heart of this debacle is Director Patel’s bewildering decision to utilize the FBI’s limited resources for purposes that, to put it mildly, seem grossly misaligned with the agency’s mission. According to credible sources, including a whistleblower’s testimony to Congress, Patel’s orders in the wake of the shooting were nothing short of baffling. With one FBI jet under his personal use in Florida, Patel inexplicably commanded that the agency’s only other available jet be reserved for a team “that would not normally respond to the scene.”

This directive not only left FBI officials in a state of confusion but also critically hampered the Bureau’s ability to swiftly respond to the Brown University shooting. The shooter’s subsequent days-long evasion of the authorities only adds a more tragic layer to this already disastrous scenario.

A Pattern of Recklessness

This incident is not an isolated lapse in judgment on Patel’s part. His tenure as FBI Director seems to be marked by a penchant for what can only be described as irresponsible joyriding at the taxpayers’ expense. Senator Richard Durbin’s (D-IL) letter demanding answers from the Justice Department and the Government Accountability Office paints a picture of a leader whose priorities lie far from the responsibilities of his office. Durbin’s reference to Patel’s alleged use of an FBI jet to attend a country music concert in Nashville serves as a glaring example of this misuse of federal resources.

The Price of Mismanagement

The Brown University shooting and the subsequent failure in the FBI’s response highlight a systemic issue that goes beyond simple misallocation of resources. At its core, this is a matter of leadership and accountability—or, more accurately, the lack thereof. The consequences of such leadership are not merely abstract or administrative; they have real, tangible impacts on public safety and the effective administration of justice.

As we reflect on this situation, several questions arise. How can an agency tasked with some of the nation’s most critical law enforcement responsibilities be led by someone evidently so detached from the gravity of their role? What measures need to be taken to ensure that the FBI’s resources are managed in a manner that prioritizes the safety and security of the American public?

The answers to these questions are crucial, not just for the sake of accountability but to prevent such egregious missteps in the future. It is imperative that the FBI, a cornerstone of American law enforcement, be guided by leadership that understands and respects the weight of its mission. Anything less is a disservice to the nation and the individuals who look to this institution for protection and justice.
