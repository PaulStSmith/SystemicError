'Plunged into rage': Trump biographer says president blames allies for 'haunting' problem

President Donald Trump privately "rages" at UK leaders like Prime Minister Kier Starmer for creating a "haunting" problem for him, according to one of the president's biographers.

Michael Wolff, who has written four books about Trump, discussed the president's trip to the UK on a new episode of "Inside Trump's Head," a podcast he appears on with The Daily Beast's Chief Content Officer, Joanna Coles. Wolff mentioned that the country's leadership appeared to have created a significant problem for Trump by dismissing an ambassador named Peter Mandelson, who had ties to disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein, ahead of Trump's visit.

Wolff said Mandelson's firing "plunged Trump into something of a rage."

"Trump went around saying to aides, 'Why couldn't they wait to do this until after the trip?'" Wolff said. "'This is just going to remind people of Epstein.'"

Wolff said Trump was particularly eager for the UK visit for a couple of reasons. First, it would give the president some time away from domestic issues he's facing, like the Epstein files, questions about the state of the US economy, and the assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk.

Trump also seems to have a "postcard fantasy" about the royal family, Wolff said, and sometimes envisions himself being part of the family.

Our Analysis:

The Royal Rage: Trump's UK Tantrum

In a revelation that reads more like a script from a political drama than the operations of the highest office, President Donald Trump's private temper tantrums have once again surfaced into public discourse. This time, his angst is directed towards the UK, specifically Prime Minister Kier Starmer, over a move perceived to dredge up ghosts from Trump's past. Michael Wolff, a name synonymous with Trump tell-alls, spills the beans on the latest episode of "Inside Trump's Head," painting a portrait of a president who's not just battling the demons of his connections but also, apparently, pining for a crown.

The Mandelson Mishap

At the heart of Trump's latest outburst is the dismissal of Peter Mandelson, an ambassador with connections to the infamous Jeffrey Epstein. This action, according to Wolff, sent Trump into a spiral of fury. "Why couldn't they wait to do this until after the trip?" Trump reportedly lamented, concerned more about the timing of the dismissal than the ethical implications of Mandelson's association with Epstein. This reaction is not just a glaring example of Trump's often criticized moral compass; it's a showcase of his perpetual state of self-interest, where the optics of association weigh heavier than the gravity of the association itself.

A Postcard Fantasy

Wolff's insights into Trump's "postcard fantasy" about the royal family add another layer of absurdity to the saga. The notion that a sitting U.S. president fantasizes about being part of a monarchy is not just bizarre; it's a troubling insight into Trump's understanding and respect for the democratic principles he's sworn to uphold. This yearning for royal association speaks volumes about Trump's values, aspirations, and, perhaps, his misunderstanding of his own role within the democratic framework of the United States.

Escaping Domestic Turmoil

Trump's eagerness for the UK visit, as described by Wolff, wasn't just about fostering international relations or celebrating shared values. It was, in part, an escape route from the mounting pressures at home, including the "Epstein files," the shaky state of the U.S. economy, and the assassination of Charlie Kirk. This penchant for seeking refuge in the ceremonial and the superficial, rather than facing and addressing the pressing issues at home, is a recurring theme in Trump's presidency, revealing a leader more concerned with image than substance.

The Bigger Picture

Trump's private "rages" and his longing for royal validation are not just trivial anecdotes; they're symptomatic of a deeper malaise. They reflect a leader embroiled in personal vendettas and fantasies, often at the expense of governance and ethical leadership. The dismissal of Mandelson, while significant, is merely a catalyst, unearthing the perennial issues of Trump's tenure: a presidency marred by controversies, ethical dilemmas, and an unsettling disconnect from the realities of leadership.

As these revelations continue to surface, one can't help but ponder the implications of a presidency so caught up in personal grievances and fantasies. Trump's "rage" at the UK leadership and his postcard fantasies of royalty aren't just footnotes in his presidency; they are glaring indictments of his priorities and his capacity to lead. In the grand tapestry of Trump's tenure, these episodes are yet another thread of concern, weaving a narrative of a presidency far removed from the ideals of democracy and ethical governance.
