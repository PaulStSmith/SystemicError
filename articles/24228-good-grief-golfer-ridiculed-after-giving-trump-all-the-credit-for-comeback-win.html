'Good grief': Golfer ridiculed after giving Trump 'all the credit' for comeback win

Based on an article by Robert Davis

Paulo SantosSeptember 29, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedCredit without evidencePower courts applauseOutrage as camouflageThe loyalty economyThe systemic error

What happened

RawStory reports that U.S. Presidents Cup captain Brandt Snedeker credited President Donald Trump after the American team erased a three-point final-round deficit and won the tournament on September 27, 2026. Trump attended the final round, but the report identifies no action by him that materially produced the team’s eight singles victories.

Snedeker said Trump had predicted he would bring the players energy and that he had promised Trump “all the credit” if the United States won. RawStory then highlighted three hostile social-media reactions, including criticism from an ESPN writer and an inflammatory accusation from a Canadian radio personality.

Credit without evidence

The athletes produced the comeback. Snedeker chose to transfer rhetorical ownership of their performance to the president.

Nothing in the reporting establishes that Trump devised strategy, coached players or changed the competition’s outcome. The only mechanism offered is “energy,” an elastic explanation that converts attendance into achievement. Snedeker’s remark may have been flattery, humor or sincere admiration; RawStory does not establish which. It does establish that he publicly made Trump the symbolic author of work performed by others.

Power courts applause

Trump already held the only governmental power in this story. Snedeker held authority over the team and control over his own post-match message. The golfers held agency on the course. Online critics controlled none of those institutions.

That distinction matters because the episode was not primarily about powerless posters being mean to a golfer. It was about a prominent sports official using a victory ceremony to elevate a sitting president. Political power did not need to issue an order. A prestigious figure volunteered the tribute.

Outrage as camouflage

RawStory framed the incident around Snedeker being “brutally ridiculed,” then supplied a handful of posts as evidence. That construction makes the backlash the event and the presidential glorification merely its trigger.

The framing is weak. One critic’s inflammatory attack deserved careful attribution, not deployment as outrage bait. More importantly, collecting insults avoids the substantive question: why was a team’s historic comeback repackaged as personal credit for a politician whose reported contribution was showing up?

The loyalty economy

Public flattery is politically useful even when it carries no formal policy consequence. It teaches audiences to associate collective accomplishment with the leader and to treat proximity to power as causation.

Snedeker’s statement fits that pattern precisely: many people performed; one powerful man received the credit. The absurdity is not that Trump accepted praise in the account—RawStory does not report his response—but that an intermediary offered him ownership without evidence that he earned it.

The systemic error

This is how leader-centered politics spreads beyond government. Institutions with their own achievements, labor and expertise begin narrating success through the presence of the president. Voluntary deference does work that coercion never had to do.

RawStory noticed the spectacle but buried its political meaning beneath a compilation of jeers. The sharper story is not that critics groaned. It is that power attracted unearned credit, a captain supplied it, and the press treated the resulting social-media scuffle as more significant than the ritual itself.
