Trump rebate plan slammed: ‘Like hiring someone to rob me, then giving a little back'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the members of the media before boarding Marine One to depart for Scotland, on the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 25, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump says he is considering issuing rebate checks to consumers from the tariffs the federal government has collected. Rebates are a partial refund for the overpayment of taxes or if there is a surplus.

Trump made his remarks on Friday before heading to Scotland for a five-day trip to visit his golf courses. Both Trump and his administration have insisted that consumers do not pay the tariffs, but importers and retailers often end up passing most or all of the additional costs onto the end purchaser in the form of higher prices. Peter Navarro, Senior Counselor to the President for Trade and Manufacturing, has gone as far as to claim that tariffs are tax cuts.

“We have so much money coming in,” from the collection of tariffs, Trump told reporters on Friday, “we’re thinking about a little rebate, but the big thing we want to do is pay down debt, but we’re thinking about a rebate.”

The President went on to describe the possible rebates as being “for people of a certain income level,” which he did not define.

Responding to Trump’s remarks, U.S. Senator Josh Hawley (R-MO) quickly announced he will propose legislation to give “every working person” in America a rebate.

“Rebates would return to consumers some of the higher prices they’ve paid as a result of those tariffs — but could also raise the specter of inflation, similar to previous rounds of government stimulus,” Axios warned.

“While customs duties are giving a boost to US revenues and Trump regularly casts tariffs as being paid by foreign trading partners,” Bloomberg added, “data show those increases are being shouldered by American businesses and consumers.”

NBC News senior national politics reporter Jonathan Allen mocked Senator Hawley’s proposal:

“The suggested policy is to raise prices by taxing imports and then subsidize taxpayers with a rebate — like hiring someone to rob me and then donating a little of the $ back to me. Could just skip tariffs, but this way Big Brother gets a cut and pols claim hero status.”

Congressional reporter Jamie Dupree added: “The current estimate of extra tax revenue from tariffs will still leave the deficits under Trump at over $1.5 trillion per year, in other words, not close to balance.”

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Rebate Charade: A Closer Look

In a recent announcement that seems more like a scene from a political satire than reality, President Donald Trump declared his administration is contemplating issuing rebate checks to consumers from the tariffs the federal government has amassed. This suggestion, made just before Trump jetted off to Scotland for a leisurely five-day visit to his golf courses, is as confounding as it is concerning. Let's dissect the layers of absurdity and contradiction inherent in this proposal.

Tariffs: A Tax on Americans, Not Foreigners

First and foremost, the administration's persistent claim that consumers do not bear the brunt of tariffs is a glaring distortion of economic reality. Despite the fervent assertions from Trump and Peter Navarro, Senior Counselor to the President for Trade and Manufacturing, who astonishingly labels tariffs as "tax cuts," the truth is starkly different. Importers and retailers, faced with increased costs due to tariffs, invariably pass these expenses onto consumers in the form of higher prices. The notion that these tariffs are somehow a financial windfall that can be generously redistributed to the American people is not just misleading—it's economically illiterate.

The Rebate Ruse

Trump's vague proposition of issuing rebates "for people of a certain income level" is as nebulous as it is patronizing. The lack of detail on who qualifies and how much they would receive makes it difficult to assess the impact of such a policy, but one thing is clear: it's a superficial attempt to placate the very consumers who have been penalized by his tariff policies in the first place. U.S. Senator Josh Hawley's (R-MO) swift jump on the rebate bandwagon, promising legislation to give "every working person" in America a rebate, only adds another layer of political theater to an already absurd proposition.

The Economic Fallacies

The argument that tariffs are a boon to U.S. revenues, as Trump suggests, conveniently ignores the broader economic consequences. As Axios and Bloomberg have highlighted, the reality is that American businesses and consumers are shouldering the cost of these tariffs. The idea of using the proceeds from these tariffs to issue rebates is akin to robbing Peter to pay Paul, with the added insult of inflationary pressures and the specter of increased national debt.

NBC News senior national politics reporter Jonathan Allen hits the nail on the head with his critique, likening the policy to being robbed, only to receive a fraction of the stolen money back as a charitable donation. It's a cycle of economic folly: impose tariffs that act as a tax on American consumers, then propose giving back a portion of that money as if it's a benevolent gesture, all while claiming victory over foreign adversaries.

The Deficit Dilemma

To add insult to injury, the notion that this tariff-induced windfall could be used to "pay down debt" is laughably disingenuous, given the deficits under Trump's administration. Congressional reporter Jamie Dupree's observation that the current estimate of extra tax revenue from tariffs doesn't even come close to balancing the over $1.5 trillion per year deficits under Trump's watch is a sobering reminder of the fiscal irresponsibility at play.

Conclusion

The tariff rebate proposal is a masterclass in political obfuscation and economic misdirection. It attempts to mask the detrimental impacts of tariffs on American consumers with the veneer of government generosity. In reality, it's a futile effort to spin a policy that has imposed a hidden tax on Americans into a populist rebate program. The American public deserves policies grounded in economic reality, not political sleight of hand that further complicates our financial wellbeing.
