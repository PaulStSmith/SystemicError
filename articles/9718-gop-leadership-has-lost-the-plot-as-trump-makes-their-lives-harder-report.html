GOP leadership has 'lost the plot' as Trump makes their lives harder: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 9, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Speaker of the House Mike Johnson (R-LA) speaks during a press conference, with Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) behind him, on the first day of a partial government shutdown, at the U.S. Capitol, in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 1, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump’s continuous off-the-cuff remarks about the budget showdown, which has led to the government coming to a standstill, are creating headaches for the GOP leadership as they try to turn voters against the Democrats.

According to report from Politico’s Dasha Burns and Jordain Carney, Republicans are not facing the reality that they have “lost the plot,” because the president keeps giving wildly divergent answers about negotiations, thereby plunging GOP messaging into disarray.

Case in point, as House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) made themselves available to the press where they blamed Democrats for the impasse, claiming they refuse to negotiate, while Trump was telling reporters he was open to sitting down with them.

After hearing from the leadership, Trump was hustled out to walk back his comments on Truth Social where he wrote “I am happy to work with the Democrats on their Failed Healthcare Policies, or anything else, but first they must allow our Government to re-open.”

According to Politico, Trump’s refusal to stay on message is creating “tension” in the Republican Party.

“Tensions surfaced again Tuesday after a White House budget office memo raised questions about a federal law guaranteeing back pay for furloughed federal workers — one that Johnson and Thune both voted for in 2019,” the report continued before adding, “These episodes are among many where the White House and Hill Republicans have been crosswise on strategy and seemingly not communicating in advance about their key moves.”

Politico is reporting that the confusion on messaging has handed Democrats a gift while leaving the ever-available Johnson and Thune “flat-footed” when confronted with the presidents remarks.

“In contrast to the GOP divisions, Democrats have been largely successful so far in their effort to focus attention on health care: — in particular, on Affordable Care Act insurance subsidies that expire at the end of the year. They are pushing Republicans to engage now while Johnson and Thune insist the problem can be dealt with later, after the government reopens,” the report notes before adding that Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA) needled her GOP counterparts with, “I think they are absolutely struggling to figure out how they are going to get out of this.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Messaging Mayhem: Trump’s Budget Blunder

In the latest episode of what can only be described as political theater at its most chaotic, Donald Trump’s penchant for off-the-cuff remarks has thrown the Republican Party’s attempts to navigate the budget showdown into utter disarray. The situation, as detailed in a report by Politico’s Dasha Burns and Jordain Carney, paints a picture of a party leadership at odds with itself, struggling to present a united front against the Democrats. This isn’t just a minor hiccup in GOP messaging strategy; it’s a full-blown crisis.

Trump’s Contradictory Comments: A Recipe for Confusion

At the heart of this debacle are Trump’s wildly divergent answers about budget negotiations. While GOP leaders like House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) and Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) were busy pointing fingers at Democrats for the impasse, Trump was off telling reporters he’s open to negotiation. This glaring inconsistency isn’t just embarrassing; it’s strategically disastrous. It’s as if Trump is playing a different game entirely, leaving his team scrambling to catch up.

Following these remarks, Trump was, predictably, shunted out to issue a clarification on Truth Social. His message, stating a willingness to work with Democrats but insisting on reopening the government first, reeks of too little, too late. The damage, as they say, was already done.

The Tension Within: A Party at War with Itself

The Politico report doesn’t mince words about the “tension” brewing within the Republican Party, a tension exacerbated by Trump’s refusal to stick to a coherent message. This internal strife isn’t just a matter of hurt feelings or bruised egos; it has tangible consequences. For instance, a memo from the White House budget office questioning a federal law guaranteeing back pay for furloughed workers—a law both Johnson and Thune voted for—highlights the lack of coordination and communication that plagues the GOP.

These aren’t isolated incidents. They’re symptomatic of a deeper problem where the White House and Hill Republicans are not only “crosswise on strategy” but also seemingly operating in entirely different realities.

A Gift to the Democrats—and a Missed Opportunity for the GOP

In this chaotic landscape, the Democrats have found an unexpected gift. While the GOP flounders, trying to regain its footing, Democrats have successfully zeroed in on health care, particularly the expiring Affordable Care Act insurance subsidies. Their focused messaging stands in stark contrast to the GOP’s scattered approach, putting Republicans like Johnson and Thune in an increasingly untenable position.

Senator Patty Murray’s (D-WA) barbed comment about the GOP’s struggle to navigate out of this mess is more than just a clever jab; it’s a pointed observation about the current state of Republican leadership. The GOP’s failure to present a unified, coherent strategy not only undermines their position but also squanders a critical opportunity to engage on key issues like health care.

Conclusion: A Party in Peril

What we’re witnessing is not just a failure of messaging but a fundamental breakdown in leadership and strategy within the Republican Party. Trump’s erratic commentary and the GOP’s inability to either rein him in or adapt to his unpredictability have left the party flat-footed and fragmented. In the face of such disarray, one must wonder: can the GOP find its way back to strategic coherence, or is this the new normal for a party in peril?

As the budget showdown continues, the consequences of this chaos will extend far beyond the halls of Congress, affecting real people and real policies. The GOP’s current predicament should serve as a wake-up call: it’s time to get their house in order, or risk losing not just the budget battle, but the confidence of the American people.
