'Increasingly vulnerable to threats': Trump slammed for skipping critical intel briefings

Since his inauguration in January, President Donald Trump has attended just 12 briefings from intelligence officials of the President’s Daily Brief, Politico reported Friday.

During his first term, Trump attended these briefings with intel officials twice a week. The daily brief presidents receive give an overview from the intelligence community’s summary of the key national security challenges.

The limited number of briefings this time has reportedly raised concerns among many individuals within and surrounding the intelligence community.

A former CIA analyst, who was not named, told Politico: "The point of having an $80 billion intelligence service is to inform the president to avert a strategic surprise."

But even when the president is absent, these crucial meetings are attended by his senior national security aides. Cabinet officials are also briefed by intelligence officials, the report notes.

Top administration officials insisted to Politico that Trump is able to receive the information he needs and remains in contact with his intelligence chiefs.

“The president is constantly apprised of classified briefings and is regularly in touch with his national security team,” Davis Ingle, a White House spokesperson, told Politico. But he didn't say why the president has received just 12 briefings.

“‘It’s sadly clear that President Trump doesn’t value the expertise of and dangerous work performed by our intelligence professionals each and every day, and unfortunately, it leaves the American people increasingly vulnerable to threats we ought to see coming,’” Sen. Mark Warner (D-Va.), told Politico.

Trump has been leading efforts to facilitate peace in Gaza and Ukraine, as well as to rekindle nuclear negotiations with Iran — all while facing rising threats from competitors like Russia and China.

"Each president is different in the manner and pace at which they receive their briefings, and Trump is not entirely out of step with some of his predecessors," the report said.

"But with Trump, there is added concern as he is known not to read the accompanying briefing document, referred to as “the book,” that is put together by intelligence analysts in a highly labor-intensive process. This document is delivered in hard copy or on a tablet device to the president and his key advisers five days a week."

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into Trump's Intelligence Briefing Dilemma

In a recent Politico report, it's unveiled that President Donald Trump has attended a mere 12 briefings from intelligence officials of the President’s Daily Brief since his inauguration in January. This revelation is not just a statistic; it's a glaring indictment of Trump's disengagement with the critical intelligence infrastructure designed to safeguard national security.

The Briefings: A Critical Nexus

The President's Daily Brief serves as a concise yet comprehensive overview of the most pressing national security challenges. It's the distillation of the $80 billion intelligence apparatus the United States leans on to avert strategic surprises. The fact that during Trump's first term, these briefings were a twice-weekly affair, and now they've dwindled to a sporadic engagement, raises not just eyebrows but serious alarms.

The Echoes of Disdain

Trump's scant attendance to these briefings isn't just a personal failing; it's a systemic risk. A former CIA analyst lamented the pointlessness of an elaborate intelligence service if its insights fail to reach or influence the president's decisions. This absence becomes doubly concerning given Trump's portfolio of engaging in peace efforts in volatile regions like Gaza and Ukraine and navigating the treacherous waters of nuclear negotiations with Iran.

One can't help but question how these efforts are informed (or not) by the rich insights that the President's Daily Brief is designed to provide. Trump's administration, through White House spokesperson Davis Ingle, assures us that the president remains "constantly apprised of classified briefings" and in regular contact with his national security team. Yet, this assurance does little to quell concerns when the briefing attendance record suggests a cavalier indifference to the structured intelligence channels.

A Dangerous Precedent

Sen. Mark Warner’s critique hits the nail on the head, highlighting a critical vulnerability. Trump's apparent disregard for the "expertise of and dangerous work performed by our intelligence professionals" doesn't just undermine these individuals and their efforts; it directly jeopardizes American security.

Moreover, Trump's notorious disinclination to read the accompanying briefing documents, referred to as “the book,” exacerbates this issue. These documents are not casual reading; they are the product of intense labor by intelligence analysts, offering nuanced insights into global dynamics. Ignoring these documents is tantamount to flying blind in a storm of global uncertainties.

Historical Context and the Road Ahead

While the report tries to offer a semblance of balance by noting that "each president is different" in their engagement with briefings, Trump’s approach is not merely a matter of personal preference. It's a matter of national security. His known aversion to reading detailed reports and reliance on a reduced number of briefings form a dangerous cocktail of ignorance and power.

In conclusion, Trump's lackluster engagement with the intelligence briefings is not just a personal failing; it's a systemic flaw that exposes the United States to unnecessary risks. As geopolitical tensions simmer and domestic challenges abound, the need for a president deeply engaged with the intelligence community has never been more critical. The report from Politico is not just an exposé; it's a clarion call for a change in how the highest office engages with the most critical information it's privileged to.
