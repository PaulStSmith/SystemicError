'They're going to kill a lot of people': Expert delivers dark warning over Trump notice

A legal expert delivered a dark warning Thursday about the Trump administration‘s confidential notice to Congress about “unlawful combatants,” warning, “they’re going to kill a lot of people” — and smear anyone seeking proof.

Aaron Reichlin-Melnick posted on X in response to The New York Times report indicating that President Donald Trump is apparently declaring that the U.S. is at war with drug cartels.

“They’re going to kill a lot of people and claim that everyone of them was a drug trafficker, and call you a terrorist-lover if you ask for any evidence,” Reichlin-Melnick wrote.

The notice was obtained by The Times and sent to multiple congressional committees and includes more details about the three strikes the U.S. military made on boats in the Caribbean Sea that left 17 people aboard dead and how these acts “should be seen as lawful rather than murder.”

“Mr. Trump’s move to formally deem his campaign against drug cartels as an active armed conflict means he is cementing his claim to extraordinary wartime powers, legal specialists said,” according to The Times.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Dangerous Gambit: A Closer Look at the “Unlawful Combatants” Notice

In a move that reeks of desperation and a cavalier disregard for human life, the Trump administration has once again blurred the lines between lawful warfare and outright murder. A recent report from The New York Times has shed light on a confidential notice to Congress concerning “unlawful combatants,” a term as nebulous as it is ominous. Legal expert Aaron Reichlin-Melnick’s response to this revelation is chilling but unsurprising: “They’re going to kill a lot of people” — and then proceed to smear anyone daring enough to question the legitimacy of their actions.

The War on Drug Cartels or a War on Due Process?

At the heart of this controversy is President Donald Trump’s apparent declaration of war against drug cartels, effectively labeling anyone caught in the crossfire as a drug trafficker and, by extension, a legitimate target. This is not merely an aggressive policy stance; it’s a dangerous game of smoke and mirrors that threatens to obliterate the very foundations of justice and accountability.

Reichlin-Melnick’s stark warning on X (formerly Twitter) encapsulates the grim reality of this situation: “They’re going to kill a lot of people and claim that everyone of them was a drug trafficker, and call you a terrorist-lover if you ask for any evidence.” This isn’t just about combating drug trafficking; it’s about wielding unchecked power under the guise of national security.

“Lawful Rather Than Murder” — The Disturbing Justification

The confidential notice, as obtained by The New York Times, details three strikes by the U.S. military on boats in the Caribbean Sea, resulting in 17 deaths. The administration’s audacity to claim these acts “should be seen as lawful rather than murder” is not only a gross misinterpretation of international law but also a blatant attempt to sanitize state-sanctioned killing.

The justification for these killings hinges on the formal declaration of an “active armed conflict” against drug cartels. This maneuver is nothing short of a legal sleight of hand, granting the Trump administration “extraordinary wartime powers” that flout the basic principles of human rights and due process.

A Dark Path Ahead

What we’re witnessing is not a measured response to the threats posed by drug trafficking; it’s a draconian policy that endangers countless lives under the pretense of combating drug cartels. By indiscriminately labeling victims as “unlawful combatants,” the administration seeks to shield itself from scrutiny and criticism, effectively silencing dissent by labeling skeptics as sympathizers of terrorism.

This dangerous precedent not only undermines the United States’ moral standing but also erodes the legal frameworks that govern armed conflict and the use of military force. If left unchecked, this policy will not only lead to the loss of innocent lives but also diminish the country’s credibility on the global stage.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

In light of these revelations, it is imperative that Congress, the media, and the public demand transparency and accountability from the Trump administration. This is not just about the legal implications of declaring war on drug cartels; it’s about safeguarding the principles of justice and human rights that are currently under threat.

The administration’s attempt to cloak its actions in the mantle of wartime authority must be scrutinized and challenged at every turn. The price of silence and inaction is too high, and the cost — measured in human lives — is one we cannot afford to pay. It’s time to peel back the layers of this policy, expose its inherent flaws, and ensure that the United States adheres to its legal and moral obligations on the world stage.
