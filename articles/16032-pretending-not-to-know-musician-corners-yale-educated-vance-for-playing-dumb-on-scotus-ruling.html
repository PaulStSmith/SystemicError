'Pretending not to know': Musician corners 'Yale-educated' Vance for 'playing dumb' on SCOTUS ruling

Vice President JD Vance found himself in yet another argument on the social platform X, this time with renowned musician Mikel Jollett, over the Supreme Court ruling that ordered the administration to bring back a Maryland man who was wrongfully deported to a prison in El Salvador.

"The entire American media and left wing industrial complex has decided the most important issue today is that the Trump admin deported an MS-13 gang member (and illegal alien)," Vance wrote on X on Tuesday, sharing a video from the Oval Office that showed the Trump team aggressively responding to CNN reporter Kaitlan Collins' question about the deported man, Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia.

In response to the vice president's post, Jollett wrote, "No, the most important issue is that the Supreme Court ruled by a 9-0 decision that you must return him."

"Trump is not a dictator or a king. He's a president and a president must follow the Supreme Court's ruling. If he does not, he must be removed from power. It's that simple," Jollett added.

Replying to Jollett, Vance said, "Are you proposing that we invade El Salvador to retrieve a gang member with no legal right to be in our country? Where in the Supreme Court’s decision does it require us to do that?"

Jollett pressed the vice president further, saying, "The ruling clearly states that you are required to take steps to facilitate his return and update the court on your progress. You have plenty of tools at your disposal to do this without invading El Salvador."

"You, of course, KNOW THIS because you are a Yale-educated lawyer. You're pretending NOT TO KNOW IT and playing dumb publicly. Our entire system rests on checks and balances and you just got checked by the Supreme Court in a 9-0 ruling," he added.

"Are you saying Trump is too weak to simply ask Bukele to return him?" the musician asked.

Some political commentators on X agreed with Jollett's view that the vice president is simply pretending not to know what the Court ruling says.

"Intentionally obtuse. JD Vance thinks you’re too stupid to understand the facts. - he’s not a gang member - they’ve been ordered to facilitate his return - Bukele has already returned a dozen migrants - all they have to do is ask for him to be released and put him on a plane," wrote Democratic strategist Sawyer Hackett.

Sparky's Analysis:

Analyzing the Clash on X: Vance vs. Jollett

In the latest installment of political theater on the social platform X, Vice President JD Vance finds himself embroiled in a heated exchange with musician Mikel Jollett. The bone of contention? A Supreme Court ruling mandating the U.S. administration to facilitate the return of Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia, a Maryland man wrongfully deported to a Salvadoran prison, a scenario Vance frames as the unwarranted repatriation of an MS-13 gang member and illegal alien.

Vance's Position: A Masterclass in Deflection and Distortion

Vance's narrative on X is a vivid illustration of political obfuscation. By spotlighting the alleged gang affiliation and illegal status of Abrego Garcia, Vance strategically diverts attention from the core issue: a unanimous Supreme Court decision underlining a fundamental principle of justice and the rule of law. His rhetoric is a deliberate attempt to muddy the waters, framing the debate as a question of national security and immigration policy rather than a straightforward legal mandate.

Jollett's Counter: Cutting Through the Noise

Mikel Jollett, in stark contrast, zeroes in on the essence of the matter. He rightly emphasizes that the crux of the issue is not the character of Abrego Garcia but the unequivocal directive of the Supreme Court. Jollett's responses are a beacon of clarity, highlighting the administration's obligation to comply with the Court's ruling, regardless of the individual's alleged affiliations or legal status.

The Faux Controversy of "Invasion"

Vance's suggestion that compliance with the Supreme Court's decision might necessitate an invasion of El Salvador is a red herring of the highest order. This assertion is not just ludicrous; it's a transparent attempt to dramatize and politicize what is essentially a procedural matter. Jollett's rebuttal underscores the reality that the administration has a variety of diplomatic and legal tools at its disposal to ensure Abrego Garcia's return—none of which involve military intervention.

The Role of Education and Responsibility

Jollett's pointed reminder of Vance's legal education and understanding serves as a damning indictment of the vice president's feigned ignorance. This exchange lays bare a troubling tendency among certain political figures to play dumb on issues where their knowledge and expertise should, instead, guide informed and rational discourse. The implication is clear: Vance's stance is not borne of misunderstanding but of a calculated decision to mislead and manipulate public opinion.

Conclusion: The Essence of Governance and Accountability

This episode is emblematic of a larger malaise within certain segments of the political establishment: the prioritization of partisan point-scoring over adherence to constitutional principles and judicial mandates. Vance's stance, as critiqued by Jollett and observed by political commentators like Sawyer Hackett, represents a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism, where executive power is wielded without regard to legal constraints or ethical considerations.

The unanimous Supreme Court ruling is not merely a directive to the Trump administration; it is a testament to the foundational checks and balances that underpin the American political system. The cavalier attitude towards this ruling, as demonstrated by Vance and by extension the administration he represents, poses a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy when those in power choose to ignore the rule of law.

In the final analysis, the Vance-Jollett exchange on X is more than a fleeting social media squabble. It is a microcosm of the broader battle for the soul of American governance, where the principles of justice, accountability, and the supremacy of law must prevail over political expediency and partisan manipulation.
