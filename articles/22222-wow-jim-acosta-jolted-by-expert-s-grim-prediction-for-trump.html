'Wow': Jim Acosta jolted by expert's grim prediction for Trump

The Real Story Is Not Trump’s Ranking. It’s the Machinery That Kept Him There.

A Punditry Ritual

Larry Sabato says historians will place Donald Trump dead last among American presidents. Jim Acosta nods along, the approval rating is cited at 33 percent, and the segment settles into a familiar media ritual: measure the wreckage, pronounce judgment, move on. That is not analysis. It is after-the-fact moral bookkeeping.

Power Is Not Abstract

The source gives the central fact away. Trump is not some accidental symbolic figure drifting through history. He holds the presidency, and the article notes Sabato’s view that he is dragging his party toward a rough midterm. That means actual institutional power still runs through Trump and the Republican apparatus that keeps accommodating him. The damage is not coming from confusion. It is being enabled.

The Frame Misses the Point

Ranking presidents is an elite parlor game unless it is tied to concrete power, decisions, and consequences. The problem here is not that Trump might “rank” below Buchanan or Nixon in some future survey. The problem is that a president with open contempt for democratic norms still commands the machinery of government while pundits debate where to file him in the imperial scrapbook.

Acosta’s reaction matters because it shows how easily the press converts political degradation into a personality contest. Trump becomes an object of historical disgust instead of a present-tense exercise in power. That is a convenient way to talk around responsibility.

No Symmetry, No Excuses

The source is blunt in one useful way: Sabato does not pretend Trump is just another flawed executive. He says Nixon was “almost, not completely, a choir boy” by comparison. That is the right instinct, though still too gentle for the scale of the problem. Trump is not merely controversial. He is a deliberate force for institutional corrosion, and the people enabling him are making a choice, not a mistake.

What the piece leaves unexamined is the elite habit of treating democratic breakdown as spectacle. The same media class that can clearly see the collapse in historical terms often loses nerve when asked to name the living actors responsible for sustaining it.

The Pattern

This story is not really about presidential rankings. It is about how American institutions absorb authoritarian behavior by turning it into commentary, then into content, then into another segment. Trump’s low approval, Sabato’s verdict, Acosta’s embrace of it all of this may be true, but none of it substitutes for confronting the governing coalition, the party discipline, and the media incentives that keep letting democratic decay masquerade as political theater.

The system does not fail only when villains act. It fails when major institutions learn to narrate the damage more fluently than they resist it.
