'Clearly triggered': Writer flags 'fuming' MAGA's 'strange defense' against Gavin Newsom

Sophia Tesfaye wrote over the weekend a piece called, "Fox News sees the power of Gavin Newsom’s trolling," in which the writer discussed how the right-wing network's stars are "fuming."

"Gavin Newsom is winning. By any measure — the attention economy, donations or algorithmic reach — the Democratic California governor’s response to President Donald Trump’s Texas power grab is accomplishing its mission. Trump and his sycophants at Fox News are clearly triggered by the Democrat’s incessant trolling," the report states. "Last week, the governor’s social media team launched a snippy troll campaign against the White House and its supporters in MAGA media, including Fox News’ Dana Perino and Tomi Lahren, and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, mimicking Trump’s style of capital letters and nicknames. White House communications director Steven Cheung cried that Newsom is a 'coward and Beta Cuck.' Fox News stars clutched their pearls over Newsom’s ribbing."

The writer highlighted how Perino responded on Wednesday: “If you’re doing this and it’s not authentic and you’re trying to do somebody else who you say is Hitler and you think that we don’t get the joke, oh no, we get the joke, it’s just not funny.”

According to Tesfaye, "It’s the same strange defense deployed by Vice President J.D. Vance during his Wednesday interview with Fox News" when the vice president said, “I think [Newsom’s strategy] the fundamental genius of President Trump’s political success, which is that he’s authentic."

Tesfaye continues:

"It’s fascinating to see them all pretend not to get the joke so they don’t anger Trump. The president’s defenders have to frame Newsom’s mimicry as though he means to emulate Trump rather than mock him. But their over-the-top whining belies their true understanding. Newsom is throwing red meat to the resistance while baiting the right. He’s learned from Trump, as well as his years engaging with Fox News, that being an internet troll can consolidate media attention without falling into the 'fact-check' trap that has beguiled Democrats for the entirety of the Trump era."

"And enraged Fox News hosts like Sean Hannity, who acknowledged that 'maybe it wins you points with the looney radical base in your party,' and Greg Gutfeld, who spent the week trolling that Newsom’s antics are somehow doomed to fail, understand that’s why the trolling is funny," the writer added. "Newsom is like the court jester pointing out how stupidly the king is acting. The inability of Trumpists to see it makes it even funnier."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Trolling: A Masterclass by Gavin Newsom

In an era where the line between political discourse and outright entertainment continues to blur, California Governor Gavin Newsom's recent foray into the realm of trolling serves as a fascinating case study. According to Sophia Tesfaye's piece in Salon, titled "Fox News sees the power of Gavin Newsom’s trolling," we're witnessing not just a skirmish in the ongoing culture wars, but a strategic maneuver that's leaving MAGA and its media arm, Fox News, seething and, paradoxically, amplifying the very messages they wish to bury.

The Strategy of Mimicry:

Newsom, in what can only be described as a brazen act of political jujitsu, has adopted President Trump's own rhetorical style—complete with capital letters and nicknames—to launch a campaign against the current administration and its media cheerleaders. This isn't just about poking the bear; it's a calculated effort to dominate the "attention economy," drive donations, and extend algorithmic reach. The reaction from the right has been predictably apoplectic, with accusations of cowardice and pearl-clutching indignation serving as the backdrop to a deeper narrative.

The Irony of Authenticity:

What Tesfaye's analysis brings to the forefront is the MAGA camp's obsession with authenticity, a quality they've long ascribed to Trump's success. Yet, in their rush to defend the president from Newsom's barbs, they fall into a trap of their own making. By insisting that Newsom's mimicry is an attempt to be Trump rather than to mock him, they inadvertently acknowledge the effectiveness of the governor's tactics. It's a delicious irony that the same crowd that prides itself on getting the joke seems to be missing the point entirely.

The Resistance and the Right:

Newsom's trolling serves a dual purpose. It galvanizes the resistance by offering a form of cathartic release, a way to mock the absurdity without getting mired in the fact-checking quagmire that often stifles Democratic rebuttals. At the same time, it baits the right into reactionary responses that only serve to amplify Newsom's profile and the very criticisms he levels against them. Fox News hosts, who are no strangers to the art of provocation, find themselves on the back foot, alternately dismissing Newsom's efforts as doomed to fail or inadvertently praising the strategy's cunning.

The Court Jester and the King:

Perhaps the most poignant observation Tesfaye makes is the comparison of Newsom to a court jester, highlighting the absurdities of the king's reign. The power of the jester lies in his ability to speak truth to power under the guise of humor. The fact that Trump's acolytes fail to see the jest for what it is only underscores the effectiveness of Newsom's approach. In their indignation, they reveal their own vulnerabilities and, unwittingly, validate the very critiques being levied against them.

Conclusion:

In the grand theater of political discourse, Gavin Newsom has proven himself not just a skilled actor but a masterful director, orchestrating a campaign that highlights the absurdities of the current political landscape while avoiding the pitfalls that often ensnare his contemporaries. The MAGA camp's response, ranging from bewildered outrage to grudging admiration, is a testament to the power of well-executed trolling. And as for Fox News, their fuming serves as the perfect accompaniment to Newsom's performance—a chorus of the outraged that only adds to the spectacle.
