'Thought it was a joke': Hegseth belittled as columnist names his worst idea yet

Based on an article by Daniel Hampton

Paulo SantosAugust 18, 20263 min read

The Pentagon’s Testosterone Theater Is a Power Move

Not Readiness

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has announced yearly testosterone screening for troops age 30 and older, with younger service members able to opt in, and he is selling it as a way to keep the force on the “leading edge of lethality.” That is the public packaging. The political meaning is uglier: this is not a serious defense doctrine so much as a masculinity pageant dressed up as policy.

The source gives the game away. Hegseth is not responding to a readiness crisis. He is imposing a symbolic standard, then laundering it through the language of health, fitness, and warfighting.

Who Actually Decides

The power in this story sits with Hegseth, the Pentagon’s top civilian under the Trump administration. Not the commentators mocking the idea. Not the doctors warning about bad screening. Not the lawmakers objecting after the fact.

That matters because the issue is not whether the plan is laughable. It is that a cabinet-level official can convert a personal ideology into institutional practice. Once that happens, satire becomes a consolation prize. The decision has already been made.

Medicine as Misdirection

The article reports the obvious medical objections: testosterone levels vary across the day, a single test can mislead, and testosterone drugs carry risks including high blood pressure and blood clots. It also notes that diagnosable deficiency affects only a small share of men.

So what problem is this supposed to solve? The answer is that solving a real medical problem is not the point. The point is to turn the armed forces into an instrument for enforcing a gender fantasy. Hegseth’s “male standard” language is not a neutral fitness policy. It is a political signal to people who want the military to be a stage for grievance, hierarchy, and performative toughness.

War as Branding

The most revealing line in the source is Hegseth’s claim that the “individual warfighter” is the decisive advantage. That sounds martial, but it is a crude simplification of modern warfare. The source rightly notes that drone operators, AI programmers, and intelligence coordination matter more than brute force in many contexts.

That mismatch is the point. Hegseth is trying to narrate war as a contest of masculine bodies because that story flatters his politics. It is easier to sell testosterone mythology than to explain logistics, intelligence, software, and systems. It is easier to moralize about virility than to govern competence.

The Cost of Punchline Politics

CNN mockery and columnists calling the plan dangerous are understandable reactions, but they can also flatten the stakes. Ridicule alone makes this look like another absurdity from a clownish administration. It is more than that.

When a defense secretary uses the military to advance a culture-war script, the institution absorbs the damage. Service members become subjects of ideological theater. Medical screening becomes a loyalty-signaling device. The chain of command becomes a delivery system for civilian fantasy.

That is not harmless incompetence. It is governance by degradation.

The Larger Pattern

This story fits a broader authoritarian style: take a serious institution, strip it of expertise, and repurpose it for identity politics. Replace evidence with symbolism. Replace actual readiness with rituals of dominance. Then dare critics to argue with the aesthetic of strength.

Democratic politics should not accept this as a quirky Pentagon sideshow. It is a demonstration of how power works when the state is used to project a cult of masculinity instead of competence. The real scandal is not that Hegseth sounds ridiculous. It is that he can make the military serve a political performance in the first place.
