Trump appears to fumble spelling in new attack on his own 'worst' appointee

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 21, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures at the end of an event during a visit to Coosa Steel Corporation, in Rome, Georgia, U.S., February 19, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

In an irate post on social media, President Donald Trump launched into a familiar attack Friday against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, but in doing so, appeared to have fumbled the spelling of the derogatory nickname he’s labeled the monetary policy chief.

Trump has been at war with Powell since last year, and has frequently raged at his refusal to lower interest rates to his liking, often dubbing him as “too late Powell.” Trump again lobbed an attack on Powell with the familiar nickname, only this time with an apparent misspelling, writing “two” instead of “too.”

“LOWER INTEREST RATES,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social. “‘Two Late’ Powell is the WORST!!!”

Trump has labeled the Fed chair as “too late Powell” since last summer, with his administration launching a fraud investigation into the Fed chair over expensive renovations to the central bank headquarters in Washington, D.C. Since then, Trump has threatened to replace Powell, who last month revealed in a defiant statement that Trump’s Justice Department was threatening him with criminal charges.

Trump has frequently published statements with misspellings and typos, including the misspelling of his own name, the misspelling of other figures’ names, and the misspelling of “United States.” Trump’s misspelled social media posts were observed to have increased in frequency as early as 2019, according to an analysis by Columbia University professor John McWhorter.

As for Powell, he’s remained defiant amid Trump’s relentless attacks.

“The threat of criminal charges is a consequence of the Federal Reserve setting interest rates based on our best assessment of what will serve the public, rather than following the preferences of the president,” Powell said in his statement last month.

“This is about whether the Fed will be able to continue to set interest rates based on evidence and economic conditions—or whether instead monetary policy will be directed by political pressure or intimidation.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Powell Saga: A Dance of Defiance and Derision

In an episode ripped straight from the annals of political theater, President Donald Trump’s recent social media tirade against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell not only perpetuates the ongoing feud between the executive branch and the Federal Reserve but also highlights Trump’s penchant for misspellings, a seemingly trivial but telling characteristic of his communication style. Trump’s typo—”two” instead of “too” in his derogatory nickname for Powell—serves as a minor yet illustrative example of the broader chaos and disregard for detail that often characterize his administration’s approach to governance.

Trump’s War on Powell: A Misguided Crusade?

Trump’s relentless attacks on Powell center around a critical economic policy tool: interest rates. Trump’s demand for lower rates is nothing new; it’s a tune he’s been singing since last year, aiming to stimulate economic growth—or perhaps more cynically, to inflate his economic legacy. However, his singular focus on this objective blatantly ignores the Federal Reserve’s mandate to balance its dual objectives of maximizing employment and stabilizing prices, not to mention its foundational need for independence from political pressures.

Powell’s defiance, as reported, is not just a refusal to bend to Trump’s will but a principled stand for the independence of the Federal Reserve. His statement underscores a critical juncture in American economic policy: Will evidence and economic conditions continue to guide monetary policy, or will political intimidation and pressure dictate the course? Powell’s rhetoric suggests a clear line in the sand, defending the integrity and autonomy of the institution he leads.

The Typo as a Metaphor

While Trump’s typo might be dismissed as a minor error, it is emblematic of a larger pattern of carelessness and lack of professionalism. When the leader of the free world consistently makes public errors—from spelling mistakes to factual inaccuracies—it not only undermines his credibility but also reflects poorly on the office he holds. Columbia University professor John McWhorter’s observation of Trump’s increasing frequency of misspellings since early 2019 is more than an amusing tidbit; it’s indicative of a broader disregard for accuracy and detail that is concerning in any leadership context, let alone the presidency.

The Bigger Picture

This ongoing saga between Trump and Powell is more than a personal vendetta or a series of public spats. It’s a reflection of a deeper struggle over the direction of American economic policy, the independence of its institutions, and the role of evidence-based decision-making in governance. Powell’s resistance to Trump’s attacks—rooted in a commitment to the Federal Reserve’s mission and the broader public interest—stands in stark contrast to Trump’s apparent desire to bend national economic policies to his personal preferences and political objectives.

In the final analysis, this episode is a microcosm of the challenges facing American democracy today: the tension between personal power and institutional integrity, between evidence-based policy and political expediency, and ultimately, between the truth and those who would seek to bend it to their will. As this saga unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the importance of maintaining the independence of the Federal Reserve—a cornerstone of American economic stability and prosperity.
