'It's not true': Jake Tapper lectures combative GOP senator on Epstein 'sweetheart deal'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Misdirection: Senator Mullin's CNN Debacle

Jake Tapper, Markwayne Mullin (CNN screenshot)

The Republican senator who admitted that he killed a Senate effort to release the Jeffrey Epstein files in full because he wants to give Donald Trump "cover," was repeatedly corrected on the details about the convicted pedophile's case on CNN on Sunday morning.

Appearing with "State of the Union" host Jake Tapper, a combative Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) insisted over and over again that nothing from the Epstein grand jury presentation can be released by Attorney General Pam Bondi.

That led host Tapper to continually explain that a judge is only blocking the DOJ from releasing testimony and that the Trump-appointed Bondi is free to release federal evidence, which led the GOP lawmaker from Oklahoma to argue with him repeatedly.

In one exchange, Tapper explained, "I understand you're talking about a resolution you offered calling for the grand jury to release transcripts from the grand jury investigation into Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell, and a judge said no. But there's troves of information that the administration could release tomorrow if they wanted to and they've been promising to and they haven't. "

"Well, Jake, that's not that's not true, you know," Mullin shot back. "You know better than that, too, because you know, every piece of file that they have, every video, every every document, every flight log has all been heard by a grand jury. They've all been seen by a grand jury and we want the judges to release it. Pam Bondi has called on the judge to release it. Trump has called on the on them to release it and and the Congress has called on them to release it. But we can't because there is a true co-equal branches of government. So we can't force a judicial branch to do anything."

"Just because something has been shown before a grand jury, that doesn't mean it is only going to be –– it can only be released to the public by a judge. If the FBI has it, they can release it," Tapper explained.

Mullin later stated, "Remember, there was a plea deal that was struck in 2009, way before I was in office, way before Trump was even considering to be in office, way before Pam Bondi was in his office, way before Kash Patel was director. 2009, there was a sweetheart plea deal that was made underneath the Obama administration with Epstein and that sweetheart has not been exposed.”

Tapper countered, “It was 2008. The U.S. attorney at the time was a guy named Alex Acosta. He was a Bush appointee. He went on to become President Trump’s secretary of labor. It all took place in 2008.”

“Who was in office at the time?” Mullin asked to which the CNN host answered, "George W. Bush."

"No, 2009 is when the case came out. And Obama was in office at the time," the GOP lawmaker argued.

“It’s not true,” Tapper stated before later adding, "The point is that the sweetheart deal, which was completed in 2008, was under the Bush administration — US Attorney Alex Acosta."

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misdirection: Senator Mullin's CNN Debacle

In what can only be described as a circus of inaccuracies, Senator Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) took to CNN's "State of the Union" with a performance that was both bewildering and deeply concerning. The Republican senator, infamous for his admission of thwarting the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files to provide "cover" for Donald Trump, showcased a stunning display of factual inaccuracy and combative ignorance.

The Misinformation Parade

Mullin's insistence that Attorney General Pam Bondi is powerless to release anything from the Epstein investigation due to grand jury constraints was immediately dismantled by host Jake Tapper. Tapper's clarification that a judge's blockade only pertains to testimony—not the troves of federal evidence at the DOJ's disposal—seemed to fly right over Mullin's head. His stubborn refusal to acknowledge this distinction raises questions about whether he's genuinely misinformed or strategically obfuscating facts.

Historical Amnesia and the Sweetheart Deal

The senator's attempt to shift blame onto the Obama administration for the "sweetheart plea deal" awarded to Epstein in 2009 is a masterstroke of misdirection—except for the inconvenient truth that the deal was struck in 2008, under the Bush administration, and by Alex Acosta, a Bush appointee who would later serve in the Trump administration. This glaring historical error (or deliberate lie?) was corrected by Tapper, but not without Mullin doubling down on the misinformation, highlighting either a profound ignorance or a disconcerting willingness to distort facts for political cover.

The Core of the Issue

At the heart of Mullin's CNN debacle is a deeper, more systemic problem within political discourse: the blatant disregard for truth. Mullin's performance was not just a failure of fact-checking; it was a deliberate attempt to muddy the waters around one of the most notorious criminal cases in recent history. His motivations—whether to protect Trump, distract from the GOP's connections to Epstein, or simply to mislead the public—are secondary to the chilling effect his misinformation has on public trust and accountability.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

Senator Mullin's interview should not be dismissed as a mere gaffe or an isolated incident of misinformation. It's a symptom of a larger disease plaguing our political system: a willful disregard for the truth in pursuit of political objectives. As viewers, voters, and critics, we must demand better. We must hold our elected officials to a higher standard of honesty and integrity, for without it, our system of governance is fundamentally flawed.

In the end, Mullin's attempt to rewrite history and deflect blame only serves to highlight the urgent need for transparency and accountability in the Epstein case—and in our political discourse at large. The public deserves the full story, unobstructed by political maneuvering and misinformation. Anything less is a disservice to justice and to the victims of Epstein's crimes.
