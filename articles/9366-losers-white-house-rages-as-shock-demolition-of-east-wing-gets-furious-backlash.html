'Losers': White House rages as shock demolition of East Wing gets furious backlash

Paulo SantosOctober 22, 20255 min read

Ongoing construction on the East Wing of the White House, where U.S. President Donald Trump’s proposed ballroom is being built, in Washington, D.C., October 20, 2025. REUTERS/Jessica Koscielniak

The White House reacted late Monday to deafening criticism of its shock move to pull down part of the White House’s historic East Wing to make way for a massive ballroom.

President Trump defended the massive project, declaring, “They’ve wanted a ballroom for 150 years. And I’m giving that honor to this wonderful place. You’re gonna see a ballroom the likes of which... I don’t think it’ll be topped.”

Demolition crews moved in on Monday, ripping off the face of the White House wing to begin building the 90,000-square-foot ballroom, designed to accommodate 999 people and be covered in bulletproof glass. California Governor Gavin Newsom dramatically stating Trump is “Ripping apart the White House just like he’s ripping apart the Constitution.”

But White House officials aggressively defended the renovation. Communications Director Steven Cheung argued, “Construction has always been a part of the evolution of the White House. Losers who are quick to criticize need to stop their pearl clutching and understand the building needs to be modernized.”

He also hit out at a Democratic critic, calling him “moronic” and a “buffoon.”

Trump emphasized that the project is being privately funded by rich donors — including himself. He recently held an exclusive dinner to thank donors who contributed millions. “A couple of you sitting here [were] saying, ‘Uh, sir, would $25 million be appropriate?’,” Trump noted. “I said, ‘I’ll take it.’”

The White House’s official response repeatedly stressed that taxpayers would not bear the cost, with its official X account claiming the ballroom “will be cherished for generations.”

Critics, including Senators Elizabeth Warren and Tina Smith, lambasted the project. CNN commentator Karen Finney called the timing “fairly grotesque,” especially during a government shutdown and high inflation.

In contrast, CNN’s conservative commentator Scott Jennings praised the renovation, saying, “Donald Trump is all about hospitality... This is going to give us a place in the White House to do the biggest and best hospitality we’ve ever done.”

Our Analysis:

The Ballroom Blunder: Trump’s Latest Folly

In a move that has jarred the senses of historians, political pundits, and the public alike, the Trump administration has embarked on a project that seems more at home in the annals of a monarch’s whims than in the sober reality of American governance. The demolition of part of the White House’s historic East Wing to make room for what can only be described as a monument to excess—a massive ballroom—has tongues wagging and heads shaking in disbelief.

Trump’s Defense: A Misplaced Sense of Honor

President Trump’s defense of this grandiose project is steeped in the kind of grandiosity only he can muster. His claim that “They’ve wanted a ballroom for 150 years” is a puzzling one. Who are “they”? Ghosts of administrations past, whispering the need for a dance floor in his ear? His assertion that he is bestowing “honor” upon the White House by adding a ballroom “the likes of which... I don’t think it’ll be topped” is, frankly, ludicrous. It’s not honor that’s being added to the White House; it’s hubris.

The Reaction: From Criticism to Outrage

California Governor Gavin Newsom’s comment that Trump is “Ripping apart the White House just like he’s ripping apart the Constitution” might seem hyperbolic to some. However, it captures the essence of the outrage many feel. This isn’t just a renovation; it’s a radical alteration of a historic symbol of the American presidency.

White House Communications Director Steven Cheung’s retort to critics is equally troubling. Branding those who question the wisdom of this project as “losers” who are “quick to criticize” and accusing them of “pearl clutching” is not just unprofessional; it’s indicative of an administration that views dissent as something to be mocked rather than considered. Cheung’s attack on a Democratic critic as “moronic” and a “buffoon” is beneath the dignity of his office.

The Funding: Rich Donors and the Question of Influence

Trump’s boast that the project is being funded by rich donors, including himself, and his anecdote about a donor’s $25 million contribution does little to assuage concerns. While stressing that taxpayers will not bear the cost might seem like a point in their favor, it raises significant questions about the influence these wealthy individuals wield in the Trump White House. The very idea that access to the President can seemingly be bought with large donations to his pet projects is deeply unsettling.

The Timing: “Fairly Grotesque”

CNN commentator Karen Finney hit the nail on the head, calling the timing of this project “fairly grotesque.” In the midst of a government shutdown and a period of high inflation, the optics of tearing down part of the White House for a lavish ballroom are appalling. It speaks to a level of disconnect and insensitivity to the struggles of ordinary Americans that is difficult to comprehend.

Conclusion: A Folly Wrapped in Hubris

The Trump administration’s decision to proceed with this ballroom project is a folly of the highest order. It smacks of an administration more concerned with legacy-building in the most superficial sense—through bricks and mortar rather than policies and principles. The White House is more than just the President’s residence; it’s a living museum, a symbol of American democracy. This latest move by Trump is less about honoring that legacy and more about defacing it. In his quest to leave his mark on the White House, Trump seems to have forgotten that it’s not his house to deface; it belongs to the American people, and they are not amused.
