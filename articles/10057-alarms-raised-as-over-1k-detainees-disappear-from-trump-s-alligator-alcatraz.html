Alarms raised as over 1K detainees 'disappear' from Trump's 'Alligator Alcatraz'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 27, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

FILE PHOTO: Florida Governor Ron DeSantis visits a temporary migrant detention center informally known as “Alligator Alcatraz” in Ochopee, Florida, U.S., July 1, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

Immigrant rights activists in Florida are expressing alarm as they have found themselves “unable to locate” more than 1,000 detainees who have been “administratively disappeared” from the state’s immigrant internment camp known as ”Alligator Alcatraz.”

Last week, the Miami Herald reported that “the whereabouts of two-thirds of more than 1,800 men detained at Alligator Alcatraz during the month of July could not be determined” after the paper “obtained the names from two detainee rosters.”

The reporters found that around 800 of the people on the rosters do not appear on Immigration and Customs Enforcement’s (ICE) Online Detainee Locator System, which provides publicly available information about the court status and locations of people who have been jailed by immigration enforcement. Another 450 had no location listed and instead merely instructed users to “Call ICE for details.”

The Herald also found that the vast majority of the detainees in the system did not have final orders of removal issued against them by immigration judges, which would be required for their deportation. Nevertheless, the detainees’ families and attorneys have been left unable to find them.

Detainees and other witnesses, including several members of Congress who visited in July, have described the conditions inside Alligator Alcatraz as horrific. The ramshackle tent camp was set up in a matter of days this summer in the Everglades to warehouse thousands of people detained by ICE, often without criminal charges or warrants, and with restricted access to attorneys.

While people in federal immigration facilities are typically able to be tracked through the system, the state-run Alligator Alcatraz works differently.

Shirsho Dasgupta, one of the reporters who broke the story for the Herald, told Democracy Now! on Thursday that attorneys he’s spoken to often “don’t know who to call” to get in contact with their clients.

Operations at Alligator Alcatraz were briefly halted in August when a federal district judge ruled against the facility on environmental grounds. But that ruling was stayed by a federal appeals court just two weeks later, allowing operations to resume.

While the state of Florida runs the facility, it has requested and was promised reimbursement from the Federal Emergency Management Agency’s (FEMA) Shelter and Services Program, which was initially created to provide housing and other services to individuals released from ICE custody who were awaiting immigration court proceedings.

In a statement on Friday, the Florida Immigrant Coalition (FLIC), which has also attempted to track the detainees, said that the Herald’s report shows what they “have been warning about for months,” that “those detained in this detention camp have effectively been administratively disappeared.”

FLIC said that the state of Florida has refused to confirm how many detainees are currently in Alligator Alcatraz and that, in addition to those not listed on the ICE locator tool, they have also seen people deported before scheduled bond hearings. The group also said it had “confirmed data showing Florida is lying when claiming those detained at the Everglades camp had final orders of removal.”

“Since this depraved torture camp funded with state FEMA funds reopened,” said Tessa Petit, FLIC’s executive director, “we have been unable to locate the fathers, brothers, friends, and sons that are caged there without due process in the ICE locator. Hospitalizations for severe medical incidents, which include cardiac incidents and surgeries, go unreported.”

Thomas Kennedy, a policy analyst at FLIC, said: “What we’re seeing at Alligator Alcatraz is basically a new model of immigration detention, where a state-run facility is operating as an extrajudicial black site, completely outside of the previous models of immigration detention in this country. It’s making what was already a terrible system somehow even worse.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Darkness of Alligator Alcatraz

In a revelation that sounds like it’s ripped straight from the script of a dystopian thriller, over 1,000 detainees have seemingly vanished into the murky depths of Florida’s Alligator Alcatraz, an immigrant internment camp that has quickly become synonymous with the term “administrative disappearance.” The Miami Herald’s investigative efforts have peeled back the layers of this horrific reality, yet the true scale of the tragedy remains shrouded in shadows and silence.

The Invisibility Cloak of ICE’s Online Detainee Locator System

A staggering revelation from the Miami Herald uncovered that two-thirds of the more than 1,800 men detained at this facility during July have been effectively erased from public records. Approximately 800 individuals are missing from ICE’s Online Detainee Locator System, and another 450 are listed without any location, their fates reduced to a cold directive: “Call ICE for details.” This chilling directive serves as a stark reminder of the Kafkaesque bureaucracy that ensnares those caught in the immigration enforcement machinery.

The Legal Limbo of Detainees

The majority of these detainees, the investigation found, have not been issued final orders of removal by immigration judges—a prerequisite for deportation. This crucial detail underscores a harrowing reality: many of those ensnared within the confines of Alligator Alcatraz are trapped in a legal purgatory, with their futures hanging in the balance and their whereabouts unknown to their loved ones and legal representatives.

Alligator Alcatraz: A Symptom of a Diseased System

The conditions within Alligator Alcatraz, as described by detainees and visiting members of Congress, paint a vivid picture of despair. Established with haste in the Everglades, this tent camp has become a warehouse for human lives, many detained without criminal charges or warrants and with severely restricted access to legal counsel. This setup starkly deviates from federal immigration facilities, operating in a grey zone that flouts the established norms of detainee treatment and tracking.

The brief halt of operations at Alligator Alcatraz due to environmental concerns did little to stem the tide of human rights abuses, with a federal appeals court swiftly resuming its operations. The facility’s reliance on FEMA’s Shelter and Services Program funds, initially intended to aid individuals awaiting immigration proceedings, adds a layer of irony to the tragedy, as it repurposes humanitarian aid to fuel an operation marked by opacity and despair.

The Florida Immigrant Coalition’s Crusade for Transparency

The Florida Immigrant Coalition’s struggle to shine a light on the operations and occupancy of Alligator Alcatraz underscores the state’s obfuscation and denial. Their efforts reveal a facility that operates as an extrajudicial black site, marking a chilling evolution in immigration detention practices. This “new model” of detention, as FLIC policy analyst Thomas Kennedy describes, represents a significant departure from the already flawed system, spiraling into a realm where due process is discarded, and human lives are rendered invisible.

Conclusion: A Call to Action

The situation at Alligator Alcatraz is not merely an immigration issue; it is a profound human rights crisis unfolding on American soil. The administrative disappearance of over a thousand detainees into this extrajudicial black site underscores a systemic malaise that demands immediate intervention. This facility, operating in the shadows of legal accountability, embodies the worst impulses of an immigration enforcement system gone awry. It is a stark reminder of the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and a reimagined approach to immigration that prioritizes human dignity over draconian enforcement.
