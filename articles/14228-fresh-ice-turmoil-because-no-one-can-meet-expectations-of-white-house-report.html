Fresh ICE turmoil because 'no one can meet expectations' of White House: report

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem watches during a vehicle scan during a tour of the San Ysidro Port of Entry, in San Diego, U.S., March 16, 2025. Alex Brandon/Pool via REUTERS

The leadership at U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) is undergoing yet another change because the number of forced deportations of immigrants are not meeting the expectations of Donald Trump's administration.

While Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and acting ICE director Tom Homan have avoided the chopping block, the Washington Post is reporting that Robert Hammer, the acting head ofHomeland Security Investigations, has been removed from his job. In addition, Kenneth Genalo, who has been serving as acting chief of enforcement and removal operations, is also out via retirement.

With pressure coming from top Donald Trump advisor Stephen Miller going on Fox News to demand "a minimum of 3,000 arrests for ICE every day,” the heat is on with one former DHS official admitting the White House will never be happy.

According to the Post's Marianne LeVine and Maria Sacchetti, the changes were made immediately after Miller's appearance and that, "Border czar Tom Homan said Thursday that he had 'not been satisfied with the numbers,' but that Trump officials had 'increased the teams a lot' and 'we expect a fast increase in the number of arrests.'"

This is not the first time there has been shuffling within the agency due to poor results. In February veteran ICE official Caleb Vitello was booted from his job "following complaints from White House officials over lagging immigrant arrest numbers."

Also in February, DHS officials Russell Hott and Peter Berg were "reassigned" for similar reasons, with DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin explaining, "We have a President, DHS Secretary, and American people who rightfully demand results, and our ICE leadership will ensure the agency delivers.”

In light of the new leadership change, a former DHS official predicted it won't be the last, telling the Post, "... there’s no one that can meet the expectations that are being put on them by the administration.”

Our Analysis:

ICE Leadership Shuffle: A Desperate Grasp for Higher Deportation Numbers

In the latest episode of the Trump administration's ongoing drama with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), the agency is witnessing yet another leadership overhaul. This move isn't stemming from a quest for efficiency or humane immigration policies, but rather, a grotesque scramble to meet the deportation quotas fetishized by former President Donald Trump and his advisors. The core of this issue is not about enhancing national security or upholding laws; it's about satiating a political hunger for higher numbers of forced deportations.

Trump's Deportation Crusade

Reports from the Washington Post reveal that Robert Hammer and Kenneth Genalo, notable figures within ICE, are the latest casualties in this relentless pursuit. Hammer, the acting head of Homeland Security Investigations, was removed from his job, and Genalo, the acting chief of enforcement and removal operations, exited via retirement. These changes occurred in the immediate aftermath of Stephen Miller, a top Trump advisor, appearing on Fox News with demands that would make any authoritarian blush: "a minimum of 3,000 arrests for ICE every day.” This is not a policy based on rational assessments of public safety needs but a clear-cut case of policy making driven by xenophobia and political posturing.

Unrealistic Expectations and Organizational Turmoil

The Trump administration's fixation on deportation numbers has plunged ICE into a cycle of perpetual leadership rearrangements. In February, we saw the removal of Caleb Vitello and the reassignment of Russell Hott and Peter Berg, all due to dissatisfaction with "lagging immigrant arrest numbers." DHS spokesperson Tricia McLaughlin's statement that "We have a President, DHS Secretary, and American people who rightfully demand results," conveniently ignores the fact that the American people are far from monolithic in their views on immigration policy. This relentless churn at the top echelons of ICE is a stark illustration of an administration more concerned with optics than operational integrity or the well-being of communities.

The Impossible Quota

The demand for a daily minimum of 3,000 arrests sets a dangerous precedent. It's a policy motivated by the desire to instill fear in immigrant communities rather than address any real or imagined issues related to immigration. Tom Homan's admission that the administration had "increased the teams a lot" in pursuit of these increased arrests is telling. It speaks to a willingness to divert significant resources and personnel to meet arbitrary and capricious goals, rather than basing such decisions on careful analysis or the genuine needs of the country.

A System Set Up to Fail

A former DHS official's prediction that the leadership changes won't be the last is perhaps the most poignant statement in this saga. It underscores a critical flaw in the administration's approach: an insatiable appetite for higher deportation numbers that no one can realistically satisfy. This is not a strategy designed to enhance the effectiveness of immigration enforcement or to ensure the safety and prosperity of the United States. It's a politically motivated, morally bankrupt campaign that undermines the very principles the country purports to stand for.

In the end, this latest shuffle within ICE's leadership ranks reveals much about the Trump administration's priorities. It's clear that the goal is not to craft sensible, humane immigration policies but to pursue a punitive, hardline stance that serves political interests at the expense of real human lives and the nation's core values. The true systemic error here is not in the failure to meet deportation quotas, but in the relentless pursuit of those quotas to begin with.
