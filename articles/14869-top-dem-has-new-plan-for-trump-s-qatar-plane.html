Top Dem has new plan for Trump's Qatar plane

President Donald Trump walks with Qatar’s Emir Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani at Hamad International Airport, in Doha, Qatar, on Wednesday. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Donald Trump should accept a $400 million plane from Qatar but then sell it and use the money to train air traffic controllers at home, a senior congressional Democrat said.

“I think it's outlandish, it's against the Constitution, the emoluments clause,” Marcy Kaptur (D-OH) told Raw Story at the Capitol on Wednesday, referring to criticism of Trump's stated intent to accept the Qatari gift, from sources including the president's own supporters.

“But my feeling is, if it's worth $400 million or more, he ought to pawn it, get the money and use it to train controllers for this country, because look what's happening at Newark airport.”

The New Jersey hub has been bedeviled by delays and safety concerns, caused by a shortage of properly trained air traffic controllers. The gathering problem adds to concern over the state of U.S. air traffic control which has mounted since January, when 67 people were killed at Reagan airport in Washington D.C., in a collision between a military helicopter and a passenger jet.

Kaptur said she herself was on a delayed flight on Tuesday, “up in the air for a long time.”

“We don't have enough trained controllers for this country. So … I think the president ought not to be thinking about himself, but thinking about the dangers to travel, air travel in this country.”

Trump was in Qatar on an official visit on Wednesday. He has repeatedly said he wants to accept the Qatari plane, which is so luxurious it has been described as a “flying palace.”

The deal for the plane would reportedly see Trump use it as Air Force One while in office, then retain use through his presidential library after leaving the White House.

"Only a FOOL would not accept this gift on behalf of our Country,” Trump wrote on social media on Tuesday.

Ethics experts and politicians of both parties have decried the plan as corrupt

Jamie Raskin (D-MD), the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee, told MSNBC: We’ve got to use this as the teaching seminar for all of America about how utterly unconstitutional this arrangement is.”

Raskin also pointed to why Democrats have so eagerly seized on the plane offer and Trump's eagerness to accept it.

“For some reason now, this flying grift or Con Air Force One, as some people are calling it, has caught the public eye and the public imagination. And it’s really broken through.”

The plane plan is also widely seen as a security risk.

Kaptur told Raw Story: “From a security standpoint, that plane doesn't have what is needed for the President of the United States anyway, but it just shows the excess that attends to certain people in the world. And I don't think our country should be hoodwinked by any multi-trillionaires, right?”

Our Analysis:

The Embodiment of Systemic Corruption: Trump's Qatari Plane Saga

In an audacious display of ethical disregard, former President Donald Trump has made headlines with his intent to accept a $400 million "flying palace" from Qatar, a move that screams of constitutional violations and raises serious questions about the integrity of American politics. Marcy Kaptur (D-OH), a senior congressional Democrat, has proposed a novel yet equally controversial solution: sell the plane and use the proceeds to train air traffic controllers. While her suggestion stems from a pragmatic concern for the U.S. air traffic control's dire state, it inadvertently highlights the absurdity of the situation.

The Emoluments Clause: An Afterthought?

The Constitution's emoluments clause was designed to prevent precisely this sort of brazen graft, yet Trump's cavalier attitude toward ethical norms continues to astonish. By accepting such a lavish gift, Trump not only flouts the Constitution but also openly invites corruption into the heart of American democracy. Kaptur's critique, while aimed at this grotesque proposal, inadvertently underscores a deeper systemic failure: the normalization of corruption at the highest levels of government.

A Farcical Solution to a Real Problem

Kaptur's proposition to pawn the plane for air traffic controller training, though well-intentioned, reads like satire. It's a testament to the bizarre state of current American politics that such a suggestion can even be considered. The real issue at hand—the alarming shortage of air traffic controllers, exemplified by the tragic incident at Reagan airport and the chronic delays at Newark—deserves serious attention and action, not to be a footnote in the saga of Trump's latest excess.

Security: The Least of Our Concerns?

Kaptur also raises a valid point on security, noting the Qatari plane's inadequacy for presidential use. However, this concern barely scratches the surface of the potential risks involved. The very notion of a foreign government gifting a "flying palace" to a sitting president reeks of undue influence and poses a colossal security risk, far beyond the technical specifications of the aircraft.

Con Air Force One: A National Embarrassment

The spectacle has captured the public's imagination for all the wrong reasons. Dubbed "Con Air Force One" by critics, the plane has become a symbol of Trump's presidency—a blend of opulence, corruption, and disregard for democratic norms. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) rightly sees this debacle as a "teaching seminar" on the Constitution's erosion under Trump's leadership, yet one wonders if the lesson will take hold.

The Bottom Line

What this episode lays bare is the systemic erosion of ethical standards in American politics. Trump's eagerness to accept the Qatari plane, far from being an isolated incident, is a symptom of a broader disease—a systemic error, if you will—that plagues our political system. Kaptur's offbeat solution, while highlighting the urgent need for more air traffic controllers, inadvertently draws attention away from the core issue: the blatant disregard for constitutional and ethical norms by those in power.

In the end, the Qatari plane saga is not just about a luxury aircraft; it's a glaring example of how far we've strayed from the principles of transparency, integrity, and accountability. The fact that we are even discussing the possibility of a president accepting such a gift is a damning indictment of the current state of American politics.
