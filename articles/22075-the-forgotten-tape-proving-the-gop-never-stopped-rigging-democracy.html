The forgotten tape proving the GOP never stopped rigging democracy

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Election Sabotage Is the Republican Operating System

The source story describes Wayne Allen Root floating a scheme for Trump to declare a national security emergency and impose election restrictions without Congress, then situates that proposal inside a longer record of Republican election interference and current signs of federal sabotage planning. That is the context. The political meaning is sharper: this is not a hypothetical abuse. It is a public rehearsal for executive lawlessness, surrounded by institutions that either cooperate, stall, or look away.

Power Sits At The Top

The story is not about diffuse civic anxiety. It is about where actual institutional power lives: the White House, federal agencies, and election officials willing to obey. Root is not the point. Trump is. The Post Office is. ICE is. The court system is. The people floating schemes are not fringe comedians improvising in a vacuum; they are testing the limits of executive power in public.

That matters because the threat is not merely rhetorical. If a president can issue directives, pressure agencies, and rely on compliant appointees, then the battleground is the machinery of state, not some abstract contest of ideas. The article is right to treat this as an election continuity problem. The deeper truth is that the continuity being threatened is democratic governance itself.

The Misdirection Is The Point

A weaker frame would pretend this is all confusion, overheated talk, or a fringe guy with a microphone. That would be nonsense. The source lays out concrete behavior: an executive order aimed at ballot delivery, ICE refusing to turn over plans, White House discussions of an emergency declaration and a voting task force, and known election deniers occupying election-running positions.

That is not drift. That is preparation.

The propaganda layer matters because it gives cover to the power grab. “Fraud” talk does not just follow the fact pattern; it is the excuse factory. Republicans have spent years training their voters to hear sabotage as patriotism. By the time the machinery moves, the lie has already done its work.

ICE Should Be Read As A Threat, Not A Bureaucracy

The source notes that ICE is stonewalling a federal judge’s order for internal documents about election plans. That should not be treated as an administrative hiccup. ICE is a coercive arm of the state, and when it is involved in election planning, the problem is structural. It is not a matter of better training or a few rogue personnel.

It is a matter of abolishing ICE.

Any institution that can be imagined as a domestic election instrument, while refusing basic transparency to the courts, has crossed from enforcement into political intimidation. The lesson is not “oversight.” The lesson is that a deportation agency is a bad fit for a democracy already under attack from within.

Democrats Need More Than Alarm

The source is strongest when it insists on preparation: litigation drafted in advance, state authority clarified, election officials backed up, early voting promoted, voter rolls checked, and propaganda anticipated before November. That is the correct instinct. But Democrats cannot just become the party of procedural anxiety.

They need to act like the people defending a real system from people trying to break it.

That means no ritualized faith in institutional restraint that has already failed repeatedly. It means no dependence on last-minute legal heroics as the main defense. It means building a durable political fight around power, not just around norms. If Republicans are willing to weaponize state capacity, Democrats need to meet that with actual state capacity, not press-release moralism.

The Pattern Is Permanent

The article’s historical sweep is doing real work. Nixon, Reagan, Florida in 2000, Trump in 2020 and now again. The pattern is not occasional scandal. It is Republican dependence on manipulation when electoral competition is not going their way. The details change. The tactic does not.

That is why the most dangerous part of the story is not the emergency fantasy itself. It is the normalization of emergency politics as a Republican fallback. When a movement treats election theft as a standing option, every future race becomes a stress test for democracy, and every institution becomes a possible accomplice.

The larger lesson is blunt: the fight is not against a single man or a single stunt. It is against a party and a state apparatus that have learned to treat democratic rules as optional when power is at stake.
