‘Vindictive’ Comey prosecution ‘could get interesting fast’ as new threat emerges: expert

When the State Uses the Courtroom as a Weapon

A Paper-Thin Trigger

James Comey was indicted in April over an Instagram post of seashells arranged to read “86 47.” Trump allies read it as a threat; Comey’s lawyers are now pushing to dismiss the case as selective and vindictive prosecution, and have asked for discovery if the judge does not throw it out immediately. That is the procedural surface. The political substance is simpler: the sitting president’s administration brought felony charges against a former FBI director over a message that can only be made criminal by aggressive interpretation.

Who Holds the Levers

Actual power sits with the Trump administration, not with Comey, not with the commentary class, and not with the judge trying to sort through a politically charged record. This is not a neutral system stumbling into a hard call. It is an administration using prosecutorial authority against a specific enemy. The key fact is not the Instagram post. The key fact is who can indict, who can press the case, and who benefits from turning law enforcement into revenge.

Selective Prosecution Is the Point

Comey’s lawyers are not just making a technical argument. They are naming the structure plainly: selective prosecution because Trump sees Comey as a political enemy, and vindictive prosecution because Trump’s personal animus is the engine. That matters because it strips away the usual fog machine. If a prosecution is driven by personal retaliation, then the state is not enforcing law in any serious sense. It is laundering grievance through legal process.

The Framing Is Too Small

Coverage that treats this as an odd legal twist misses the larger pattern. The article’s own focus on whether the case “could get interesting fast” reduces a abuse-of-power story to courtroom suspense. That is too small. The relevant question is not whether discovery turns up a smoking gun. The relevant question is why the Justice Department is being used in a way that invites the accusation before the merits are even reached. When power behaves like this, the procedure is already part of the punishment.

What This Reveals

The deeper pattern is institutional cowardice in the service of personal rule. Prosecutorial power is supposed to be bounded by evidence, consistency, and restraint. Here it is being tested against loyalty and enmity instead. That does not just imperil Comey. It teaches the system that political vendetta can be dressed up as lawful process if the right people control the machinery.

The Real Lesson

This story is not about one social media post and it is not about one former official’s legal strategy. It is about what happens when executive power treats legal institutions as instruments of retaliation. That is the operating logic: identify an enemy, force the state to move, and let the formality of procedure conceal the political purpose. Once that pattern takes hold, the courtroom stops being a check on power and becomes one more place power goes to punish its critics.
