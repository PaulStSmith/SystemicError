'Donald Trump has lost control' as Epstein scandal engulfs MAGA base: conservative

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 15, 20255 min read

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Conservative journalist David Drucker conceded on Friday morning that Donald Trump is finding out the hard way that he is unable to clamp down on the resurgent Jeffrey Epstein scandal as Republican lawmakers turn their backs on him.

Appearing on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” Drucker, who writes for conservative outlet The Dispatch, somberly explained that since the emails of the notorious pedophile were made public, the president has tried his best to make it go away but has come up empty-handed after he has pressured MAGA allies in Congress to lend him a helping hand.

With MSNBC host Mika Brzezinki reporting that White House officials are admitting the president is “fighting a losing battle,” and co-host Joe Scarborough pointing out that Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) is, so far, balking as the White House pressures her, Drucker offered, “It’s fascinating, Joe, because on the one hand, it’s so rare that we see House Republicans, and really House Republicans break with the president in such an overt way, especially with primary season right around the corner.”

“But I think there are a couple of things going on here,” he proposed, “One, Donald Trump has lost control of the Epstein conspiracy, right? These are flames that he has fanned. And now this conflagration has gotten out of control. And for the first time in his second presidency, we see him as the victim of events rather than in control of events. You know, you may like nothing that he did during the first six months of his presidency, but he was in control of events day to day, both in the executive branch and on Capitol Hill and that has evaporated.”

“And so I think, one, we have Republicans looking over their shoulder after last week’s election and with voters so unhappy about the economy,” he added. “But I think this is just a case of a particular issue being important in its own right to the president’s base and to grassroots Republicans who came to believe earnestly. It turns out that there is some deep-seated conspiracy behind these Epstein files, and they’re not letting it go.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Epstein Quagmire: A Tangled Web of Denial and Desperation

In an era where political scandals often seem to evaporate into the ether as quickly as they arrive, the persistence of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal in clinging to Donald Trump’s legacy is a testament to its deeply disturbing nature and the tangible evidence it brings to the surface. Conservative journalist David Drucker’s recent appearance on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” peeled back another layer of this sordid saga, revealing a stark reality: Trump is floundering in his attempts to suppress the fallout.

The Uncontrollable Firestorm

Drucker’s observations highlight a crucial turning point in Trump’s post-presidential life. The former president, once adept at steering narratives and bending the Republican Party to his will, finds himself impotent against the resurgence of the Epstein scandal. The release of emails tied to the notorious pedophile has ignited a firestorm that even Trump’s MAGA acolytes in Congress can’t extinguish. This isn’t just about scandal management; it’s about the erosion of Trump’s influence over a party he once dominated with an iron grip.

The notion that Trump could be “the victim of events rather than in control of events” is both striking and telling. It dismantles the facade of invincibility that Trump has meticulously crafted over the years. Here, we see a man who fanned the flames of conspiracy for his own benefit, only to find himself ensnared in a narrative he can no longer manipulate. This shift from puppeteer to puppet is a dramatic fall from grace and power, underscoring a vulnerability that Trump has rarely shown.

The Republican Rift

The article also sheds light on the fissures within the Republican Party. The refusal of figures like Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) to align with Trump’s attempts to quell the scandal is not just a minor rebellion; it is a significant crack in the facade of party unity. With primary season looming, the conventional wisdom would suggest rallying around the party’s most dominant figure to ensure electoral success. Yet, here we witness a deviation from the script, driven by a scandal so repugnant that even the most loyal supporters are beginning to distance themselves.

Drucker’s insight into Republicans “looking over their shoulder after last week’s election” speaks volumes about the current state of political calculus within the party. The economic dissatisfaction among voters is a cloud hanging over the GOP, suggesting that allegiance to Trump, especially in light of the Epstein scandal, might not be the electoral panacea it once was. The calculus has shifted; moral and ethical considerations are beginning to weigh as heavily as political pragmatism.

The Deep-Seated Conspiracy and Its Hold

Perhaps the most intriguing aspect of Drucker’s analysis is the acknowledgment of a “deep-seated conspiracy” behind the Epstein files that has captivated the president’s base and grassroots Republicans. This isn’t merely about political loyalty; it’s about a shared narrative that has taken root in the psyche of a significant segment of the American electorate. The unwillingness to let go of this narrative, despite its potential damage to the party and its leaders, is a reflection of how conspiracy theories have morphed from fringe beliefs to central tenets of political identity for some.

In conclusion, Drucker’s commentary on MSNBC offers a window into the tumultuous dynamics at play within the Republican Party and Trump’s dwindling sphere of influence. The Epstein scandal is not just a thorn in Trump’s side; it’s a mirror reflecting the fractures within the party, the shifting allegiances, and the battle between ideology and pragmatism. As the saga unfolds, it will be fascinating to see how these tensions resolve, if at all, and what this means for the future of Trump’s political aspirations and the Republican Party’s identity.
