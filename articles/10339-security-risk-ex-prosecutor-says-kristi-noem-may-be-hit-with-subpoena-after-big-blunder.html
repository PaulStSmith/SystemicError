Analysts have long suggested Noem was a camera hog interested in promoting herself more than doing the job. Now, she has taken that too far, according to ex-federal prosecutor Joyce Vance.

Vance highlighted a recent ICE operation, which Noem personally attended, apparently against protocol. Vance quoted Newsweek as saying that Noem “joined federal immigration agents during an early morning operation in Elgin, Illinois, on Tuesday that resulted in multiple people being led away in handcuffs, and two U.S. citizens being briefly detained.”

"It was another poorly executed raid where people’s rights, in this case, American citizens, were violated," Vance said of the operation. Vance then noted that Noem may have made things difficult for herself by being at the botched op.

"By the way, the Secretary’s presence might seem like a small thing here, but it’s not. It’s not amusing. It’s not cosplay. It’s not cute. It’s not shake your head and then look away. It’s dangerous. And it was done, apparently, for a photo op," Vance wrote.

She added:

"Noem, too, should be concerned about the security risk her presence creates. Furthermore, if Noem accompanied agents to the scene, as the reporting indicates, she made herself a witness. If I’m a criminal defense lawyer for one of the men or a plaintiff’s lawyer in a civil suit, I’m cutting the subpoena for her testimony pronto. This is why smart prosecutors know better than to go along when a search warrant is executed, let alone an attorney general or a cabinet secretary. But Noem likes her photo ops."

Vance further said, "It’s just another sign of the less-than-professional way Trump’s appointees are running government, following Pam Bondi’s comments about prosecuting people for First Amendment-protected speech earlier this week."

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Governance: Kristi Noem's Misguided Photo Op

In what can only be described as a blatant disregard for protocol and a dangerous obsession with self-promotion, U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem has found herself in hot water. Her participation in a recent ICE operation for what appears to be nothing more than a photo opportunity is not just a rookie error; it's a glaring testament to the performative nature of her approach to governance.

Joyce Vance, an ex-federal prosecutor, has shed light on an operation that not only resulted in a poorly executed raid but also in the wrongful detention of U.S. citizens. The presence of Noem at the scene is not just troubling; it's irresponsible. It transforms a serious law enforcement operation into what Vance poignantly describes as neither "amusing" nor "cosplay" but rather a dangerous stunt.

A Dangerous Precedent

The implications of Noem's actions go beyond mere optics. By inserting herself into the operation, Noem has unwittingly made herself a potential witness, opening the door for subpoenas that could further complicate the legal aftermath of this botched operation. This isn't just about poor judgment; it's about a fundamental misunderstanding of the responsibilities and limitations of her role.

The Cult of Personality in Politics

Noem's actions are symptomatic of a larger, more disturbing trend within the Trump administration: the prioritization of self-promotion over duty. As Vance points out, this incident is merely a piece in the larger puzzle of a government run by individuals more interested in their personal brand than in the solemn duties of their office. The reference to Pam Bondi's controversial comments about prosecuting individuals for First Amendment-protected speech earlier in the week only serves to underline this alarming pattern.

The Bottom Line

What we have here is not just a failure to adhere to protocol or an isolated error in judgment. It's a blatant example of the erosion of professionalism in government, where the lines between duty and personal aggrandizement have been dangerously blurred. Noem's participation in the ICE operation is a stark reminder of the need for a return to a government that prioritizes the welfare and rights of its citizens over the fleeting allure of a photo op.

In a world where image increasingly trumps substance, Noem's actions serve as a cautionary tale. The dangerous precedent set by such behavior not only undermines the integrity of the offices these individuals hold but also poses a real threat to the very fabric of democratic governance. It's high time we demand more from our leaders than mere spectacle.
