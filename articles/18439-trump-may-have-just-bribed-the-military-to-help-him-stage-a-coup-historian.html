Trump may have just bribed the military to help him stage a coup: historian

The Dangerous Path of Presidential Power Plays: Trump and the Echoes of Authoritarian Tactics

Snyder’s Claims: A Brief Summary

Renowned historian Timothy Snyder has presented a stark analysis suggesting President Donald Trump may be maneuvering to consolidate power through increased military spending and staged terror attacks, with the aim of undermining the 2026 midterms. Snyder, a Yale historian known for his expertise on authoritarian regimes, draws parallels between Trump’s actions and historical scenarios that have facilitated the rise of dictators.

A Calculated Bribe for Military Fidelity?

Trump’s proposal to hike the defense budget by 50% reeks not of strategic foresight but of political desperation. Snyder interprets this as an attempt to buy the loyalty of the military—ironic given Trump’s history of disrespect towards military personnel and their sacrifices. This move lays bare the dynamics of power at play: Trump, lacking genuine respect or support from military commanders due to his own actions, now seeks to secure their allegiance with money. This is less about national security and more about securing a personal power base, a strategy fraught with peril for the democratic processes of the United States.

Staging Chaos: The False Flag Threat

The second accusation—that Trump might stage or exploit a terrorist attack to cling to power—aligns disturbingly with tactics used by authoritarian leaders like Vladimir Putin. Snyder’s comparison to Putin’s 1999 apartment bombings is particularly chilling, suggesting a willingness on Trump’s part to sacrifice public safety for political gain. This scenario underscores the potential depths of manipulation and deceit Trump could employ, turning fabricated threats into tools to dismantle democratic norms under the guise of national security.

The Role of Public Compliance and Resistance

Central to Snyder’s analysis is the idea that Trump’s potential path to authoritarian control hinges on public acquiescence. If the public preemptively resigns itself to the notion of wartime necessity or fails to critically assess the legitimacy of Trump’s actions, they inadvertently bolster his position. Snyder urges a vigilant, questioning stance as a bulwark against manipulated political crises that could be used to justify extraordinary measures.

Misdirection and the Abuse of Power

Trump’s tactics, as outlined by Snyder, involve a significant amount of misdirection—using external threats and military might to obscure and justify internal power grabs. This strategy is rooted in a broader political pattern where leaders exploit or even create crises to consolidate their control, often at the expense of democratic principles and institutions.

Conclusion: A Systemic Warning

The insights provided by Snyder are not just a commentary on one president’s potential machinations but are indicative of systemic vulnerabilities within American political structures. When a leader with authoritarian tendencies can contemplate such strategies openly, it is a clear signal that the systems intended to prevent such abuses of power are not as robust as they need to be. The real defense against such tactics lies not just in institutional safeguards, but in the vigilance and active resistance of the populace. Recognizing and resisting the misdirection and manipulation of public perception is crucial in safeguarding democratic integrity against those who would subvert it for personal power.
