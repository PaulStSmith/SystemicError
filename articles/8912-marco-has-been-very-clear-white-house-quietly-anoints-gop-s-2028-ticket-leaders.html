'Marco has been very clear': White House quietly anoints GOP's 2028 ticket leaders

A number of White House insiders say the Republican Party already has strong expectations as to who will lead the party in 2028, revealing for the first time the GOP’s ideal presidential ticket to Politico on Friday.

“[Secretary of State] Marco [Rubio] has been very clear that [Vice President] JD [Vance] is going to be the Republican nominee if he wants to be,” said a person close to Rubio, speaking with Politico on the condition of anonymity. “He will do anything he can just to support the vice president in that effort.”

Another person that Politico described as a person “close to the White House” told the outlet that the “expectation is JD as [the presidential nominee] and Rubio as VP.”

A new Politico poll released on Friday shows that among Republican voters, 2% said they viewed Vance as the current leader of the party, and Rubio, 0.1%. But, when asked who they’d prefer to lead the party’s ticket in 2028, 35% said Vance, and 2% said Rubio; 28% said President Donald Trump should mount a third run for office, which is prohibited by the Constitution.

Trump has publicly commented at the prospect of a Vance-Rubio presidential ticket, saying last week that the hypothetical campaign would be “unstoppable.” Another insider told Politico that the two maintain a friendly relationship, suggesting that the pairing was well within the realm of possibility.

“No one expects Marco to resign from the Cabinet and start taking potshots at the sitting vice president,” said another insider, speaking with Politico on the condition of anonymity. “Beyond that, they’re friends.”

Rubio himself has said that Vance would make a “great” GOP presidential nominee, and a former campaign staffer of his told Politico that they “wouldn’t be surprised” if he ended up accepting Vance’s vice presidential spot on the ticket.

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s 2028 Vision: A Critique of Politico’s Latest Expose

In a recent Politico article, the curtains were pulled back to reveal the Republican Party’s supposed dream team for the 2028 presidential election. According to unnamed White House insiders, the GOP harbors grand aspirations for a ticket headlined by Vice President JD Vance, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio as his running mate. This revelation, while intriguing, raises more questions than it answers, not least because of the current standing of these two figures within the party itself.

The Polling Predicament

Let’s dissect the numbers that form the backbone of this political prophecy. A Politico poll indicates a staggering disparity between the current perception of Vance and Rubio as party leaders (2% and 0.1%, respectively) and their supposed 2028 ticket favorability (35% for Vance as the lead and 2% for Rubio). This discrepancy is not just puzzling; it’s a glaring red flag. It suggests either a dramatic undercurrent of support not reflected in their present leadership roles or, more cynically, a narrative being pushed without substantial grassroots backing.

The Trump Factor

Adding to the intrigue is the mention of President Donald Trump, who, despite constitutional barriers against a third term, still captures the imagination of 28% of Republican voters for a 2028 run. Trump’s endorsement of a Vance-Rubio ticket as “unstoppable” is both a tantalizing endorsement and a potential albatross. Trump’s influence within the GOP is undeniable, but his blessing can be a double-edged sword, attracting fervent support and deep-seated resistance in equal measure.

The Reality of Political Ambitions

The notion that Rubio would contentedly shift from a high-profile role as Secretary of State to play second fiddle as Vice President under Vance is, at best, optimistic. Political careers are rarely built on demotions. The leak suggesting Rubio’s eagerness to support Vance “in that effort” and their purported friendship paints a picture of political harmony that anyone with a cursory understanding of political ambition might find naïve.

The Anonymous Source Conundrum

The reliance on anonymous insiders is a necessary evil in political journalism, but it also opens the door to speculation and manipulation. These sources, “close to the White House” and “close to Rubio,” provide the scaffolding for this narrative without accountability. Without named sources, the article leans heavily into the realm of political gossip rather than substantiated reporting.

Conclusion: A Premature Coronation

This Politico piece, while certainly a fascinating glimpse into potential future GOP strategies, feels more like a premature coronation than a robust prediction. The vast difference between current leadership perceptions and future aspirations for Vance and Rubio underscores a disconnect that cannot be ignored. As political observers, we must question the origin of these narratives and whose interests they serve. The GOP’s 2028 dream ticket, as it stands, is a house of cards built on shaky polling data and anonymous optimism, begging for a gust of political reality to test its foundation.
