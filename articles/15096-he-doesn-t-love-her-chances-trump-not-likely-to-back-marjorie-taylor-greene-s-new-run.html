'He doesn't love her chances': Trump not likely to back Marjorie Taylor Greene's new run

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump is concerned one of his most loyal foot soldiers would lose a statewide race in Georgia and is strategizing for which Republican he would back in next year's U.S. Senate primary.

The state's governor Brian Kemp will visit the White House to talk with the president about who would be the best GOP candidate to back for a chance to challenge Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-GA), but sources told Axios that won't likely be MAGA firebrand Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA).

"The president loves MTG," said a Trump adviser. "He doesn't love her chances in a general."

Recent polling shows Greene could easily win a GOP primary but lose to Ossoff, with one Republican group showing that she'd get "smoked" in the general election, according to a source who had reviewed those numbers.

"Greene has not ruled out a race, but those familiar with her thinking say she's aware of the perception that she could not win a general election," Axios reported. "They also note she's a more savvy operator than publicly perceived and knows how to read a poll."

Kemp and the president had a falling out after the 2020 election cycle, in which Trump and both GOP Senate candidates lost, but made peace last year, and both badly wants to win back those seats starting with Ossoff's.

"The president, like the governor, wants someone who can win," said a White House adviser.

MAGA Republican Rep. Buddy Carter (R-GA) has already announced he's running, but insiders say he's not a preferred candidate, and the president and Kemp will strongly consider longtime Trump adviser Rep. Brian Jack (R-GA), who has indicated he'd rather stay in the House but would run if Trump demands it.

They will also consider small business administrator Kelly Loeffler, who lost her Senate seat in a January 2021 special election to Sen. Raphael Warnock (D-GA), but an adviser said she'd prefer to run to replace Kemp as governor, and Rep. Mike Collins (R-GA), who sponsored the Laken Riley Act, could be considered the early favorite.

"He lines up on the Venn diagram," said a top Georgia Republican strategist. "He's at every [Trump] rally. He's a trucker, so he has a blue-collar business background and would be the firebrand, workhorse candidate."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Georgia Dilemma: Optics Over Loyalty

In the swirling vortex of GOP politics, where loyalty often trumps (pun intended) reason, the latest machinations surrounding Georgia's U.S. Senate primary are a stark reminder of the Republican Party's ongoing identity crisis. President Donald Trump, ever the kingmaker in Republican circles, finds himself at a crossroads, torn between backing a loyalist with questionable electability and opting for a candidate with broader appeal. The central figure in this political drama? None other than MAGA firebrand Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), whose potential candidacy has sparked a flurry of strategizing and hand-wringing.

Trump’s Calculated Distance from Greene

"The president loves MTG," a Trump adviser was quoted saying, a sentiment that reveals the personal affinity Trump holds for the controversial congresswoman. However, love, as they say, is blind, but politics is not. The same adviser's acknowledgment that Trump "doesn't love her chances in a general" is a clear-eyed admission of Greene's polarizing nature and the electoral liability she represents in a statewide race. Recent polling, as highlighted in the article, underscores this dilemma, suggesting Greene could dominate a GOP primary only to be "smoked" in the general election against Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-GA).

The Kemp-Trump Reconciliation: A Marriage of Convenience

The political reconciliation between Trump and Georgia Governor Brian Kemp is nothing short of a pragmatic alliance formed out of mutual necessity. After a public fallout post-2020 election, their renewed partnership underscores a shared goal: reclaiming lost Senate seats. Their upcoming meeting at the White House, aimed at discussing potential GOP candidates, is a testament to their uneasy détente. However, one can't help but question the sincerity and durability of this alliance, given its convenience-driven nature.

The Search for a Viable Candidate

The article mentions several names in the mix for Trump and Kemp's endorsement, including MAGA Republican Rep. Buddy Carter (R-GA), Trump adviser Rep. Brian Jack (R-GA), former small business administrator Kelly Loeffler, and Rep. Mike Collins (R-GA). Each potential candidate brings their own set of advantages and drawbacks to the table. Yet, the emphasis on finding someone who "can win" speaks volumes about the GOP's priority: electability over ideological purity, at least in this instance.

The Mike Collins Proposal: A Compromise Candidate?

Rep. Mike Collins is painted as a potentially ideal candidate, embodying the MAGA spirit without the excess baggage associated with figures like Greene. His blue-collar background and consistent presence at Trump rallies position him as a palatable option for both the Trump base and potentially wider electorates. However, one must ponder whether Collins' alignment with Trump is enough to sway moderate Republicans and independents, or if his candidacy would simply be a less controversial continuation of the status quo.

Conclusion: The GOP's Balancing Act

As the Republican Party grapples with its direction ahead of the next election cycle, Georgia emerges as a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the GOP. The party's decision-making in this Senate primary will be a litmus test for how it intends to navigate the delicate balance between appeasing its base and appealing to a broader electorate. In this high-stakes game of political chess, Trump and Kemp's moves are being watched closely, not just by their supporters but by anyone keen on understanding the evolving dynamics of American politics. One thing is clear: In the quest for political victory, loyalty is negotiable, but electability is non-negotiable.
