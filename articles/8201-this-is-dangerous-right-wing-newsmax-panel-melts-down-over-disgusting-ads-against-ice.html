'This is dangerous!' Right-wing Newsmax panel melts down over 'disgusting' ads against ICE

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 1, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Dissection of the Newsmax Panel’s Outrage

A Newsmax panel reacts to a new anti-ICE ad campaign, Nov. 30, 2025. (Screengrab / Newsmax)

A Newsmax panel erupted Sunday in response to a series of advertisements running this weekend encouraging Immigration Customs and Enforcement officers to quit their jobs, with panel participants calling the ads everything from “disgusting” to “classic communism.”

The video advertisement in question was launched by the Women’s March WIN political action committee, and depicts an ICE agent returning home from work to their young daughter, who asks them “how was your day?”

In the ad, a narrator can be heard speaking directly to ICE agents, telling them that “a mask can’t hide you from your neighbors, your children and God,” and that they “can walk away before the shame follows you home.”

For Deneen Borelli, conservative commentator, Newsmax contributor and author, the ad went far beyond what she considered acceptable, and she even argued that it would encourage violence against ICE officers nationwide.

“I think it’s outrageous that the Democrats put out that video, and this is beyond propaganda, this is dangerous!” Borelli said.

“You’re sending a message to the loons out there that you can do whatever you want and put targets on the backs of our law enforcement personnel. And shame on these networks for airing these commercials!”

Borelli’s husband, Tom Borelli, was also a guest on the show, and he launched into a tirade over how the ad somehow was similar to actions by the Soviet Union before its dissolution in 1991.

“What this is is classic communism; turning families against each other,” Mr. Borelli said. “This is part of the agenda, it happened all the time in the Soviet Union, this is what they do! They’re doing it on many issues: transchil -- transgender children as well, turn the families, put the state in charge, not the family, not the parents.”

Launched this week, the ad campaign first aired in Palm Beach, Florida, a strategist target given that President Donald Trump – the architect behind the ongoing mass deportations – is reported to be spending his Thanksgiving weekend at his Mar-a-Lago resort in Palm Beach. The ads are set to also run in Chicago, Illinois, and Charlotte, North Carolina, two cities that have seen significant immigration crackdowns and raids.

One of the Newsmax hosts went on to label the ad as “shocking” and “disgusting,” and went as far as to blame the deadly shooting last week of two National Guard members in Washington, D.C. on “this thinking” displayed in the ad

Our Analysis:

Dissection of the Newsmax Panel’s Outrage

The Newsmax panel’s explosive reaction to the Women’s March WIN political action committee’s advertisement campaign implores a deep dive into the layers of hyperbole and misrepresentation that seem to be their stock in trade. The advertisement, designed to provoke thought and possibly action among Immigration Customs and Enforcement (ICE) officers, has been met with a barrage of criticism that ranges from the exaggerated to the downright absurd.

The Advertisement Itself: At its core, the advertisement in question seems to aim at humanizing ICE agents, suggesting that the moral and ethical implications of their job duties extend beyond the workplace and into their personal lives. The narrative construct of an ICE agent facing a simple, yet profound question from their young daughter, “how was your day?” serves as a potent reminder of the human element often lost in the political discourse around immigration enforcement.

“Disgusting” and “Classic Communism”: Deneen Borelli’s immediate leap to categorize the advertisement as “dangerous” and an incitement of violence against law enforcement personnel is a gross misinterpretation. It’s a classic example of the alarmist rhetoric that seeks to shut down discourse by painting any criticism of law enforcement practices as an attack on the officers themselves rather than the policies they are instructed to follow. Furthermore, Tom Borelli’s comparison of the ad to “classic communism” and an attempt to turn families against each other is a bewildering conflation of issues. The ad’s call for introspection and moral reflection within one’s profession is twisted into a nefarious plot of familial discord and state overreach, evoking Soviet-era paranoia with little to no basis in reality.

Blaming the Advertisement for Unrelated Violence: The claim that the advertisement is somehow responsible for the tragic shooting of two National Guard members in Washington, D.C., is not only unfounded but dangerously speculative. It’s a leap of logic that serves to vilify the creators of the ad and distract from the content and message of the campaign itself. This kind of argumentation is indicative of a broader trend within certain political discussions, where events are manipulated to fit a narrative rather than examined for their actual causative factors.

Strategic Targeting of the Ad Campaign: The choice of Palm Beach, Florida, Chicago, Illinois, and Charlotte, North Carolina, as the initial airing locations for the ad campaign is a calculated move. Each of these areas has its unique context in relation to immigration enforcement and political significance, particularly Palm Beach’s connection to President Donald Trump. This strategic targeting is not uncommon in political campaigning, aiming to maximize impact and relevance to the audience.

In conclusion, the Newsmax panel’s response to the advertisement campaign is emblematic of a broader unwillingness to engage with the ethical considerations of immigration enforcement practices. Instead of addressing the content and intent of the ad, the panelists chose to demonize it through a lens of hyperbole and misinformation. This approach does little to further constructive dialogue and serves only to entrench divisions. The ad, whether one agrees with its premise or not, opens the door for a necessary conversation about the human impact of immigration policy—a conversation that, judging by the response it elicited, is long overdue.
