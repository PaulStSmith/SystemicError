Trump makes midnight announcement of 'stunningly pathological' $15B lawsuit

Just before midnight on Monday, Donald Trump jumped onto his Truth Social account and announced a $15 billion defamation lawsuit against the New York Times, a handful of reporters and book publisher Penguin Random House that both stunned and amused legal experts.

Trump led off with “Today, I have the Great Honor of bringing a $15 Billion Dollar Defamation and Libel Lawsuit against The New York Times, one of the worst and most degenerate newspapers in the History of our Country, becoming a virtual ‘mouthpiece’ for the Radical Left Democrat Party. “

“I view it as the single largest illegal Campaign contribution, EVER. Their Endorsement of Kamala Harris was actually put dead center on the front page of The New York Times, something heretofore UNHEARD OF! The ‘Times’ has engaged in a decades long method of lying about your Favorite President (ME!), my family, business, the America First Movement, MAGA, and our Nation as a whole,” he added.

“I am PROUD to hold this once respected “rag” responsible, as we are doing with the Fake News Networks such as our successful litigation against George Slopadopoulos/ABC/Disney, and 60 Minutes/CBS/Paramount, who knew that they were falsely “smearing” me through a highly sophisticated system of document and visual alteration, which was, in effect, a malicious form of defamation, and thus, settled for record amounts. They practiced this longterm INTENT and pattern of abuse, which is both unacceptable and illegal,” he continued before adding, “The New York Times has been allowed to freely lie, smear, and defame me for far too long, and that stops, NOW! The suit is being brought in the Great State of Florida. Thank you for your attention to this matter. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!”

Politico's Kyle Cheney pointed, "JUST IN: Trump launches a $15 billion lawsuit against the New York Times — naming reporters Peter Baker, Michael Schmidt, Susanne Craig and Russ Buettner — because he doesn't like what they have written/reported about him."

As for reactions, on Bluesky, attorney Ken White was taken aback by the over-the-top assertions by Trump’s three lawyers who made the filing in Florida, and wrote, “This is a stunningly pathological lawsuit even by Trumpian standards. It reads as if he was holding a gun to the head of the child of the lawyer drafting it.”

Conservative attorney George Conway joked, "Is it possible for a legal pleading to be psychotic? I think we have an answer."

NPR reporter Bobby Alllyn reported that the Times had preemptively responded, writing, “NYT attorney David McCraw told Trump lawyers before they sued that their legal claims amounted to ‘a litany of personal complaints’ about the NYT premised on ‘the deeply troubling notion that anyone who dares to report unfavorable facts about a presidential candidate is engaged in ‘sabotage’.”

California State Senator Scott Weiner (D) chipped in, “He’s not even pretending to have an actual defamation claim. He’s just mad they didn’t endorse him, to the tune of $15 billion.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's $15 Billion Defamation Lawsuit: A Masterclass in Absurdity

In what can only be described as a breathtaking spectacle of litigious hubris, Donald Trump has catapulted himself back into the legal spotlight with a defamation lawsuit against the New York Times, select reporters, and Penguin Random House that is as staggering in its monetary claim as it is in its detachment from legal reality. The suit, which Trump announced with characteristic pomp on Truth Social, demands $15 billion for alleged defamation, a figure that seems plucked from the ether of Trump's own valuation of his tarnished reputation.

Trump's announcement was laden with the hyperbolic language and grandiose self-reference that have become his trademarks. He described the lawsuit as having the "Great Honor" of being the largest against what he calls one of the "worst and most degenerate newspapers" in U.S. history. Trump's vilification of the NYT as a "mouthpiece for the Radical Left Democrat Party" and his claims of their "decades-long method of lying" about him and his movements teeter on the brink of self-parody. This lawsuit is less a legal battle and more an extension of Trump's perpetual campaign against any and all critical media scrutiny.

The Alleged Defamation

Central to Trump's grievance is the portrayal of the New York Times' coverage as a covert "campaign contribution" and an endorsement of Kamala Harris, which he claims was given undue prominence. This assertion stretches the concept of defamation beyond recognition, transforming it into a catch-all for any coverage that dares not to flatter. Trump’s statement that the NYT has been "allowed to freely lie, smear, and defame" him without consequence ignores the robust protections afforded to the press under the First Amendment, protections designed precisely to shield journalists from retaliatory suits by powerful figures.

Legal and Public Reaction

The reactions from legal experts and commentators have ranged from amused disbelief to concern over the implications of such a lawsuit. Politico's Kyle Cheney succinctly summarized the suit as Trump's retaliation against unfavorable reporting. Meanwhile, attorney Ken White's characterization of the lawsuit as "stunningly pathological" and George Conway's jest about the "psychotic" nature of the legal pleading underscore the broader consensus that this lawsuit is far removed from any reasonable legal strategy.

The preemptive response from NYT attorney David McCraw, highlighting the lawsuit's basis in "a litany of personal complaints" rather than substantive legal grievances, points to the heart of the matter. This lawsuit is not about rectifying wrongs but about punishing dissent and unfavorable coverage. California State Senator Scott Weiner's observation that Trump isn't even pretending to have a legitimate defamation claim but is instead expressing outrage over not being endorsed captures the essence of this legal maneuver: it is a performative act of grievance rather than a legitimate pursuit of justice.

Conclusion: The Real Sabotage

Trump's $15 billion lawsuit against the New York Times is less a legal challenge and more a theatrical display of grievance politics, aimed more at rallying his base and perpetuating his narrative of victimhood than at addressing any genuine harm. In his quest to cast himself as the perennial victim of a biased media, Trump inadvertently reveals the true sabotage at play: an attempt to undermine the very foundations of free speech and press freedom. This lawsuit, with its baseless claims and astronomical figures, is not just an attack on the New York Times but an assault on the principles that underpin democratic discourse. It stands as a stark reminder of the ongoing battle for truth in an era where fact and fiction are increasingly blurred by those in power.
