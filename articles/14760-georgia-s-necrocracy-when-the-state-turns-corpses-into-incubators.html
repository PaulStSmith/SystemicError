Georgia's Necrocracy: When the State Turns Corpses Into Incubators

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 17, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The state of Georgia has crossed a final, grotesque threshold of dystopia—one that no civilized society should ever contemplate, much less codify. Adriana Smith, a 30-year-old nurse, mother, and daughter, was declared brain-dead in February 2025 after suffering a series of catastrophic brain clots. She was nine weeks pregnant at the time.

Under any remotely humane set of circumstances, her death should have marked the end of a tragedy, ushering in a period of grief, remembrance, and eventual closure for her devastated family. But Georgia is no longer operating under the rules of empathy, reason, or respect for bodily autonomy. Instead, it is being governed by a chilling, fanatical obsession with forced birth—an ideology so rigid and cruel that even the dead are not exempt from its grip.

Rather than honoring Adriana’s life, wishes, or dignity, the state chose to commandeer her body, overriding the protests of her grieving family. They placed her corpse on artificial life support, not out of compassion, but out of dogma. Not to save her—because she was gone—but to exploit her remains as a gestational incubator for a nine-week-old fetus that had no reasonable expectation of survival. Medical experts made it clear: at nine weeks, the fetus was nowhere near viability, and the chances of bringing it to term were vanishingly slim.

Still, the state persisted. For months. Keeping machines running. Pumping fluids into a body that no longer housed a person. All in service of an ideological fantasy that treats embryos as citizens and women—alive or dead—as state-owned property.

Let’s not mince words: Adriana Smith is dead. There is no consciousness. No brain activity. No hope. What the state of Georgia is doing is not medicine. It is not healthcare. It is not ethical. It is ideological necromancy—a perverse ritual in which the state reanimates the dead to perform labor for a fetus, no matter the cost, no matter the human toll.

This is what happens when laws are written not by scientists or ethicists, but by zealots who believe embryos are sacred and women are expendable. Georgia's so-called "heartbeat law"—a pseudoscientific fabrication referring to electrical impulses that mimic cardiac activity around six weeks—elevates the legal status of fetal tissue above that of a woman, reducing her body to a tool, an object, a receptacle.

Adriana isn’t being remembered. She’s being violated. Her dignity shredded. Her humanity erased.

And the fetus? Even if it miraculously survives this macabre experiment, what kind of life awaits it? Born under the most traumatic, unnatural conditions imaginable—one burdened from birth with the weight of a state-sanctioned horror story. Doctors have already raised red flags about developmental complications. But Georgia’s lawmakers do not care. Because this is not about life. This is not about health. This is about control.

Control of bodies. Control of narratives. Control of women. Even in death.

This is the legacy of Dobbs—the Supreme Court decision that gutted Roe and opened the gates for state-level reproductive tyranny. Georgia merely took the invitation and ran with it, sprinting past the boundaries of decency into the realm of the grotesque. And other states are lining up behind them, ready to follow suit.

Let’s not pretend this is an isolated case. It’s a blueprint.

Imagine the chilling precedent: any woman of childbearing age, upon becoming brain-dead, may now be legally reanimated and conscripted into labor. Not because she chose it. Not because her family agreed. But because a legislature, infected with religious extremism and patriarchal fantasies, decided that an embryo matters more than a woman’s life, death, and legacy.

To force a family to stand helpless as their loved one is transformed into an unwilling surrogate is not justice. It is not moral clarity. It is anti-human cruelty, draped in the language of righteousness.

Adriana Smith deserved peace. Her family deserved mourning, not litigation. They should have been allowed to say goodbye with dignity—not watch the state puppet their daughter’s body in the name of an embryo.

This is not civilization. This is necrocracy—rule by the dead, for the unborn, at the expense of the living. And it must be called out for what it is: barbarism with a heartbeat bill stapled to its chest.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.

Written and published by Paulo Santos for Systemic Error.
