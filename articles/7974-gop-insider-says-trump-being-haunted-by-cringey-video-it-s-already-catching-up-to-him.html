GOP insider says Trump being haunted by 'cringey' video: 'It's already catching up to him'

Donald Trump has a “tone deaf” video circulating on right-wing networks, and it’s “already catching up to him,” according to a GOP insider on Saturday.

Brendan Buck, a former key adviser to ex-House Speaker Paul Ryan (R-WI), appeared on MS NOW over the weekend to talk about a video of Trump promoting his line of watches. In late November, Buck flagged a key detail about a MAGA lawmaker’s recent resignation that he says should “give a lot of Republicans pause.”

On Saturday, the strategist was asked by the host if the “cringey” video was “tone deaf.”

Things like this, Buck said, are “all good and fine when things are doing well.”

Trump did similar advertisements during the election, and it “seemed unbelievable but he still got elected,” according to Buck, who stressed that things are different now.

“But when things are not going well, and you’re doing stuff like this, and you’re building the ballroom and you’re traveling around to, you know, you’re spending time at soccer announcements, like, is this person locked in on the things that I care about? And I think it will actually I think it already is catching up to him. I’ll say that. I think that’s part of why his poll numbers are tanking, because he doesn’t seem focused.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tone-Deafness: A Symptom of a Larger Malaise

In what can only be described as a spectacular misfire, Donald Trump has managed once again to alienate not just the broader electorate, but seemingly his own party insiders as well. The latest saga involves a video circulating on right-wing networks that has been lambasted as “tone deaf,” a critique that is as unsurprising as it is telling. Brendan Buck, a former adviser to Paul Ryan, has voiced concerns that should not be taken lightly, pointing towards a deeper disconnect within the GOP and its erstwhile figurehead.

The Video: A Mirror to Trump’s Disconnect

Trump’s video, promoting his line of watches, serves as a microcosm of his broader political and personal ethos. It’s not the act of selling watches that irks; it’s the blatant disregard for timing and tact. As Buck rightly points out, such ventures “are all good and fine when things are doing well.” However, in the current climate, where the GOP is grappling with internal strife and a disillusioned base, Trump’s antics seem not just out of touch but dangerously negligent.

The Bigger Picture: A Question of Focus

Buck’s critique cuts to the heart of the matter: Is Trump focused on what matters to the American people? The answer, if Trump’s recent activities are anything to go by, is a resounding no. Building ballrooms and attending soccer announcements might cater to Trump’s ego, but they do little to reassure a public that is increasingly wary of his leadership. This is not just a question of optics; it’s a matter of priorities. At a time when the GOP needs all hands on deck to navigate through tumultuous waters, Trump seems to be steering his own ship in the opposite direction.

The Fallout: Poll Numbers and Public Perception

Buck mentions Trump’s tanking poll numbers as a direct consequence of his actions, and it’s hard to argue otherwise. The public’s perception of a leader is intrinsically linked to their actions and priorities. Trump’s focus on seemingly trivial matters in the face of pressing national and party concerns is not just politically tone-deaf; it’s a strategic blunder of epic proportions.

The Takeaway: A Party at Crossroads

The GOP is at a crossroads, facing existential questions about its future and identity. Trump, whether he acknowledges it or not, is at the center of this crisis. His actions, as highlighted by the video debacle, are symptomatic of a larger issue: a disconnect with the current political reality and the needs of the American populace.

Buck’s insights are a clarion call for introspection within the GOP. It’s time for the party to reckon with its allegiance to Trump and consider the costs of such loyalty. As the 2024 elections loom, the GOP must decide whether it will continue to be led by the whims of a figure who seems increasingly detached from its core values and, more importantly, from the American people.

In conclusion, Trump’s “tone deaf” video is not just a faux pas; it’s a glaring red flag. It’s a reminder of the need for leadership that is attuned to the times and responsive to the electorate’s needs. For a party in search of its soul, the path forward must be rooted in reality, not in the detached delusions of a bygone era.
