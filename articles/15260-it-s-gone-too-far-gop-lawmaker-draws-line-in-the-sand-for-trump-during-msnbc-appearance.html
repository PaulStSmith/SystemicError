'It's gone too far': GOP lawmaker draws line in the sand for Trump during MSNBC appearance

During an appearance on MSNBC on Saturday morning Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE) served notice to Donald Trump that he is joining with some of his Republican colleagues in the Senate to reclaim the right of Congress to set and approve U.S. trade policy by pointing out that the president has overstepped his his authority.

Speaking with the hosts of the newly revamped "The Weekend," Bacon -- an occasional foe of the president –– urged Trump to back off.

"Article 1, Section 8 [of the Constitution] gives Congress the authority over tariffs and taxes," he began. "It's pretty clear in the Constitution."

Our Analysis:

A Bold Reclamation or Political Theater? Analyzing Rep. Don Bacon's Stand on Trade Authority

In a display of political bravado that masquerades as a principled stand, Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE) took to MSNBC to theatrically distance himself from President Trump's controversial trade policies. Bacon's performance, while compelling, raises more questions than it answers about the true commitment of Congressional Republicans to reclaim their constitutional responsibilities.

The Constitution and Congressional Abdication

Bacon astutely points to Article 1, Section 8 of the U.S. Constitution, which indeed vests the power to "lay and collect Taxes, Duties, Imposts and Excises" and to "regulate Commerce with foreign Nations" squarely in the hands of Congress. This is a fact as indisputable as it is routinely ignored. Bacon's sudden invocation of the Constitution, while accurate, smacks of convenience rather than conviction. Where has this reverence for the Constitution been as Congress, for decades, has steadily ceded its authority, particularly in matters of trade and war, to the executive branch?

Emergency Powers: The Slippery Slope of Convenience

Bacon's critique of the misuse of emergency authorities by the president to impose tariffs on a broad swath of countries is spot-on. However, it's akin to lamenting the rain while holding an umbrella only for oneself. The delegation of such powers was less about foresight into specific emergencies and more about abdicating responsibility and avoiding difficult political decisions. The misuse Bacon decries is not an aberration but a predictable outcome of Congress's own making.

A Coalition of Convenience?

The mention of Senators Chuck Grassley and Rand Paul, alongside other unnamed Republicans, as part of this crusade to reclaim power, is interesting but not particularly reassuring. The inconsistency of Congressional Republicans in challenging overreaches of executive power, especially under a Republican president, has been a hallmark of recent years. This sudden alliance, while potentially powerful, begs the question: Is this a genuine effort to restore constitutional balance, or merely a politically expedient move to distance themselves from unpopular trade policies?

The Bottom Line

Rep. Don Bacon's call to action is a necessary conversation starter but let's not mistake it for a heroic stand. It's a long overdue acknowledgment of a problem Congress helped create. This isn't a moment of revelation but a calculated move in a larger political game. If Bacon and his colleagues are serious, they need to demonstrate a consistent commitment to reasserting Congressional authority across the board, not just where it conveniently aligns with their interests or provides a platform for opposition.

As viewers and constituents, we should welcome the discourse on constitutional responsibilities but remain skeptical of motivations until proven otherwise. The real test will be in the follow-through, not the sound bites.
