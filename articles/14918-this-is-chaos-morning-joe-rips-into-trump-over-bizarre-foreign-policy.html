'This is chaos': Morning Joe rips into Trump over bizarre foreign policy

President Donald Trump has tried to remake the U.S. economy in his own image — and MSNBC's Joe Scarborough said that was causing "chaos" across global financial markets.

The president arrived Tuesday in Saudi Arabia for the start of three days of summits in the Middle East that will blend business and diplomacy, and he sparked backlash from some of his leading MAGA allies by accepting a $400 million Boeing 747 from Qatar's royal family as the next presidential aircraft.

"For Republicans and I think also people in the MAGA crowd, what makes them nervous is, of course, Qatar has been a funder of Hamas, they've been a funder of Hezbollah, they've been a funder of terrorist states for a very long time," said the "Morning Joe" host. "So obviously they're expressing concerns, Republicans are expressing concerns."

Trump has inserted himself personally into foreign business dealings in a way that his predecessors have not, Scarborough said.

"We're talking about the economics of all of this, and, you know, I have been saying from the beginning," he said, "when people are talking about the stock market going down to 20,000 and, and, and the U.S. economy melting down, that, first of all, just with during the Biden administration and before that, the Trump and the Obama administration, people, people want to invest in the United States of America, and they're going to look for excuses to invest in the United States of America.

"But I've also been saying that when Donald Trump makes these deals, the stock market's going to go back up and we're up to 40,000, you know, got down to 36,000. It's up to 42,000, and he has countries that are desperate to make deals with him right now, and we've seen this in foreign policy as well."

"The Iranians never wanted to do deals – he killed [Qasem] Soleimani," Scarborough added, referencing a senior military officer who was assassinated.

"I know a lot of us a lot of us were talking about how dangerous this was and how much it upped the ante, and, of course, we found out that that actually put the Iranians back on their heels, and now they're willing to deal with him."

Trump's insistence on extracting concessions in every deal hurt America's position, Scarborough said.

"I'm saying this is chaos," Scarborough said. "It is hard for foreign leaders to follow, it's hard for people in Wall Street to follow. You just wonder if this isn't just, again, his approach to economics and his approach to foreign policy, because he believes he can intimidate people into making deals with him."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Chaos: Trump's Economic Gambit

In an era where the line between politics and spectacle increasingly blurs, President Donald Trump's economic maneuvers seem more akin to a high-wire act than a carefully charted course for the U.S. economy. According to MSNBC's Joe Scarborough, Trump's attempt to remake the U.S. economy in his own image is not just ambitious—it's precipitating chaos across global financial markets.

The $400 Million Question

At the heart of the latest controversy is Trump's acceptance of a $400 million Boeing 747 from Qatar's royal family, purportedly the next presidential aircraft. This move alone has sparked a significant backlash, not least from his MAGA allies. Why? Because Qatar's financial history is, to put it mildly, contentious. Funding groups like Hamas and Hezbollah, Qatar is no stranger to the global terrorism financing watchlist. Scarborough rightly points out the incongruity here: a president who has championed America-first policies now seemingly entangled with a nation accused of supporting terrorism. The optics are, to say the least, problematic.

Trump's Unconventional Approach

Trump's direct involvement in foreign business dealings marks a stark departure from his predecessors. This is not diplomacy as usual. It's Trump's world, where economic decisions are personal, and foreign policy is seemingly conducted at the whim of deal-making prowess—or lack thereof.

Scarborough's analysis suggests a mixture of awe and apprehension at Trump's ability to influence global markets and foreign policy through sheer force of will (or personality). The stock market's fluctuations are presented as evidence of Trump's Midas touch, with investments flowing into the U.S. as a testament to its unwavering appeal—or perhaps, as a reflection of Trump's erratic but somehow effective negotiation style.

The Iranian Gambit

The assassination of Qasem Soleimani, a high-ranking Iranian military officer, serves as a prime example of Trump's high-stakes foreign policy play. While many feared the move would escalate tensions to the point of no return, Scarborough suggests it actually forced Iran into a more conciliatory posture. It's a bold claim, suggesting that what appears to be chaos might actually be a calculated strategy to intimidate and coerce.

Chaos or Strategy?

Scarborough's use of the word "chaos" is particularly apt. For foreign leaders and Wall Street alike, Trump's approach is unpredictable and difficult to navigate. This unpredictability may be intentional, a way to keep opponents off balance. However, the question remains: is this chaos a byproduct of a strategic mastermind, or is it simply chaos born from a lack of coherent strategy?

Trump's economic and foreign policy, as described by Scarborough, is a high-stakes game of chicken, with global stability hanging in the balance. Whether his approach is the result of strategic calculation or impulsive decision-making, the implications are profound. The U.S. and the world are navigating uncharted waters, with Trump at the helm, steering the ship with the same bravado he applies to business deals.

In sum, Scarborough's analysis paints a picture of a presidency unlike any other, where the rules of engagement have been rewritten. Whether this will lead to sustained economic growth and improved global relations remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: the world is watching, often in disbelief, as Trump reshapes the U.S. economy and its place on the world stage in real-time. The only certainty in this era of uncertainty is that the ripple effects of Trump's presidency will be felt for years to come.
