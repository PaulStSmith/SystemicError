The Battle for 2028: America After Trump Redux

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Democrats: A Fractured Bench with One Clear ContenderThe Republicans: Trumpism Without TrumpWhat This Election Will Really Be AboutFinal Oracle Prediction

When Donald Trump won re-election in 2024, it wasn’t just a political defeat for Democrats—it was a shattering of their strategic illusions. The belief that demographics were destiny, that rational discourse would win over fearmongering, or that Trumpism would burn itself out all went up in smoke as Trump stormed back into the Oval Office.

Now, with Trump constitutionally barred from a third term (unless he pulls a full-blown Banana Republic move and tries to ignore it), the 2028 election looms as the most consequential in modern American history. The stakes aren’t just about reversing Trump’s policies—they’re about whether American democracy can claw its way back from the abyss or slide into permanent authoritarian decay.

This is the post-Trump-redux battlefield. And here's how it looks.

The Democrats: A Fractured Bench with One Clear Contender

With Joe Biden out of the picture, and Kamala Harris carrying all the political weight of a helium balloon in a hurricane, the Democratic Party enters 2028 with a single obvious frontrunner: Gavin Newsom.

The California governor has already all but declared his intentions. He’s debated Ron DeSantis on Fox News, launched public feuds with red-state governors, and turned California into a progressive showcase with national implications. He’s polished, media-savvy, and can raise money faster than the GOP can pass voter suppression laws.

He’s also got baggage: the French Laundry debacle, the homelessness crisis in California, and an image that screams “coastal elite” in a country still nursing resentments from the 2008 foreclosure crisis. But in a field devoid of better options, he’s the last man standing.

Who else is there?

  • Kamala Harris: Tolerated in 2020, ignored in 2024, and unelectable in 2028. Her poll numbers are radioactive, her communication style is robotic, and her 2020 campaign died before Iowa. If she runs, she splits the base and hands the primary to Newsom.

  • Pete Buttigieg: Smart, composed, and utterly forgettable. His mayoral past isn’t enough, and his time as Secretary of Transportation hasn’t lit any fires. A solid VP or cabinet choice, but not a top-tier contender.

  • Gretchen Whitmer: Competent, popular in Michigan, and battle-tested. She could be a surprise, but she hasn’t built the national profile yet. However, she’s an ideal VP pick.

  • Raphael Warnock: Charismatic, Southern, and a compelling speaker. But 2028 may be too soon, and the Senate may be his priority.

  • AOC: Not constitutionally eligible. She turns 35 days before inauguration in 2029. Even if eligible, she’s far too divisive for a general election.

Bottom line? Newsom/Whitmer is the most viable, balanced, and strategically sound ticket the Democrats can assemble. California flash meets Midwest grit. Coastal dollars meet Rust Belt voters. And reproductive rights, climate policy, and anti-authoritarian messaging are front and center.

The Republicans: Trumpism Without Trump

With Trump barred (presumably) from running again, 2028 is the year the GOP finally tests what they’ve become: not a conservative party, but an authoritarian grievance movement wrapped in a flag and carrying a Bible.

And the heir apparent isn’t Ron DeSantis. His 2024 humiliation was too public, his charisma too synthetic. The Republican base wants blood, not PowerPoint slides.

The likeliest ticket? J.D. Vance / Elise Stefanik.

  • Vance: Hillbilly Elegy turned Hillbilly Autocrat. Smart, tech-savvy, articulate, and fully committed to the idea that democracy is optional if it gets in the way of white Christian nationalism. He gives the base what it wants: cruelty with a Harvard pedigree.

  • Stefanik: Once a moderate, now a loyal Trumpist. She balances the ticket with gender optics and appeals to suburban conservatives who miss the days of Paul Ryan but have since sold their souls.

Other GOP contenders?

  • Josh Hawley: Too smug, too reviled in the Senate, and the “insurrection fist pump” is both his badge and his anchor.

  • Kristi Noem: Pretty on camera, a favorite of Fox News, but scandals and dog-shooting stories have tarnished her appeal.

  • Vivek Ramaswamy: A clown with tech money. Entertaining, dangerous, but unlikely to top a ticket. VP fodder at best.

  • Tucker Carlson: Could run. Would break the internet. But governing would bore him to death.

  • Donald Trump Jr.: Even MAGA fans know he’s the Party City version of his father.

The Republican platform in 2028 will be an escalation of 2024: anti-woke hysteria, book bans, attacks on LGBTQ+ rights, forced birth policies, and theocratic nationalism. It won’t pretend to be a small-government party anymore. It will be open about its authoritarian goals.

And the Vance/Stefanik ticket would be the polished face of that darkness.

What This Election Will Really Be About

2028 won’t be about policies. It won’t be about tax rates or health plans. It will be about democracy vs. dictatorship. Period.

The Republican nominee will run on vengeance. On finishing what Trump started. On purging the federal government, prosecuting political enemies, and reengineering America as a white Christian ethnostate in all but name.

The Democratic nominee must run on survival. Not moderation. Not triangulation. Survival.

This is not a time for tepid centrism. If the Democrats run a boring campaign full of platitudes and bipartisanship sermons, they will lose. Again. And this time, there may not be a free election in 2032 to fix it.

Final Oracle Prediction

  • Democratic Ticket: Gavin Newsom / Gretchen Whitmer

  • Republican Ticket: J.D. Vance / Elise Stefanik

This is not an inspiring choice. It is not a Hollywood drama. It’s a grim collision between a slick progressive technocrat and a rising post-Trump fascist class.

But it’s the battle we’re getting. And we’d better stop daydreaming about impossible Obama/Harris fanfic and get to work.

Because if we lose in 2028, it’s not just politics as usual. It’s lights out for the republic.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
