MS NOW astounded as Trump suffers ‘lowest rating ever’ in new poll

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Trump’s Economy, Trump’s Responsibility

The Poll Is Just the Receipt

A new NPR/PBS News/Marist poll found Trump at 33% approval on the economy and 60% disapproval, his lowest rating on that question in the poll’s history. The survey was conducted June 8 to 11, before the administration’s tentative Iran deal, and even that timing did not save the White House from the political damage already baked into the numbers.

Power Sits at the Top

The people actually holding institutional power are not grocery shoppers, renters, or families cutting summer plans short. It is Trump and the administration around him. When the economy is this central to public judgment, blame does not scatter downward into the population. It points upward to the office that sets the agenda, claims credit for outcomes, and owns the consequences.

That is the basic political fact the coverage should never bury: if the president wants the authority of command, he also inherits the liability.

Denial Is the Strategy

Meghan Hays’s line about Trump’s “don’t-believe-your-lying-eyes strategy” is more than a jab. It describes a familiar governing move: deny what people can see, then try to substitute spectacle for experience. The source notes gas prices up over 80 cents, grocery prices up, and people unable to afford health care. Those are not messaging problems. They are material conditions.

The administration’s answer, as described here, is not repair but diversion: talk about ballrooms, reflecting pools, and a fake peace arrangement with Iran. That is not governance. It is image management for a political class hoping aesthetics can outvote reality.

Misdirection as Politics

The weak framing in stories like this is the habit of treating public anger as if it floated in the air. It does not. Someone decided the priorities. Someone enabled the posture. Someone benefits when the public is encouraged to focus on theater while costs climb and confidence collapses.

This is where blame often gets softened. The economy is described as if it were weather. It is not. It is the result of policy choices, institutional power, and a White House that wants applause for symbolism while ordinary people absorb the bill.

The Pattern Beneath It

This is the standard authoritarian-adjacent trick of modern politics: take credit for control, deny responsibility for harm, and flood the zone with pageantry when the numbers turn against you. The poll is not merely bad for Trump. It is evidence that brute confidence and fake certainty eventually run into households that cannot pay their bills.

The larger lesson is simple. When leaders govern by spectacle and insist on denying obvious damage, the economy becomes not a measure of competence but a test of how long propaganda can stand in for results.
