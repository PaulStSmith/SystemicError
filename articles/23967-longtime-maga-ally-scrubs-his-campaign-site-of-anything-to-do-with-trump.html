Longtime MAGA ally scrubs his campaign site of anything to do with Trump

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Vanishing EndorsementDonalds Made Trump His CredentialRebranding Without ReckoningThe Affordability ContradictionMAGA as a Removable LabelThe Politics of Strategic Amnesia

Byron Donalds Built His Campaign on Trump. Now Trump Is Becoming Fine Print.

The Vanishing Endorsement

With 42 days left before Florida’s 2026 general election, Republican gubernatorial candidate Byron Donalds has stripped at least eight prominent references to Donald Trump from his campaign website, according to the Miami Herald. The removed material included a “Trump Endorsed” donation appeal, multiple endorsement banners, and Trump’s 2025 “RUN, BYRON, RUN!” message. Archived versions of the site documented the earlier presentation.

Donalds Made Trump His Credential

This was not a casual alliance imposed on Donalds by reporters. He chose to make Trump’s approval a central political asset.

Donalds described himself during his 2020 congressional campaign as a “Trump supporting” conservative. After Trump endorsed his gubernatorial campaign, Donalds said he was “incredibly honored” and promised not to let the president down. As recently as August 2026, he said Trump had performed “incredibly well” on economic issues.

The institutional power here belongs to Trump, whose endorsement helped define standing within the Republican coalition, and to Donalds, who voluntarily used that endorsement to build his candidacy. Voters did not create this dependency. Donalds did.

Rebranding Without Reckoning

The website overhaul does not establish why Donalds ordered or approved the changes. But its political function is plain: a campaign once marketed through Trump’s name is now foregrounding Florida’s affordability crisis while making its chief national ally less visible.

That is repositioning, not accountability. Donalds has not, in the reporting provided, repudiated Trump’s economic record or explained why the endorsement that once dominated his pitch now warrants removal. Trump remains an ally and endorser. The branding changed; the underlying alliance apparently did not.

This distinction matters because campaigns routinely treat presentation as a substitute for explanation. Donalds wants the advantages of Trump’s endorsement without forcing persuadable voters to stare at it.

The Affordability Contradiction

Donalds is now presenting himself primarily as a candidate concerned with affordability. Yet Trump, whose economic record Donalds praised in August, dismissed economic struggles as a Democratic “hoax” and a “con job.”

That contradiction belongs to Donalds. He cannot credibly pose as a passive casualty of Trump’s rhetoric after repeatedly validating Trump’s leadership and using his endorsement as campaign currency. If Donalds now believes Florida’s affordability crisis requires a different account, the burden is on him to identify what changed—his assessment of the economy, his assessment of Trump, or merely his assessment of what voters want to see.

The article’s speculation about whether Donalds is “confident” he can win is weaker than the observable fact. Whatever his private confidence, his campaign decided Trump had become less useful on the public-facing website.

MAGA as a Removable Label

This is the larger Republican pattern exposed by the episode: loyalty is advertised as conviction when it mobilizes the base, then quietly demoted when it threatens broader electoral appeal.

That is not ideological independence. It is political risk management. Donalds helped make Trump’s endorsement a test of legitimacy, benefited from the association, and is now reducing its visibility amid reported declines in Trump’s approval and defections from his 2024 coalition.

The misdirection would be to portray this primarily as Trump losing an ally. The reporting says Trump remains Donalds’s ally and endorser. What he is losing is placement.

The Politics of Strategic Amnesia

Authoritarian political movements depend not only on loud loyalists but also on officials who treat loyalty as an adjustable campaign setting. The leader is indispensable when his name confers power and suddenly incidental when that name carries a cost.

Donalds’s website edits do not prove a break with Trump. They reveal something more useful: a candidate attempting to separate the benefits of an alliance from responsibility for it. The systemic failure is not confusion. It is a political culture in which officials expect voters to accept a redesigned homepage as evidence that the choices behind it no longer count.
