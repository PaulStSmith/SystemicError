'Overplayed his hand': Experts flag big consequences of Trump's latest 'retreat'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Donald Trump's latest international negotiations aren't going as planned, according to experts.

Trump's recent negotiations in Switzerland resulted in what some saw as a win for the president, because it stopped some of the bleeding in the trade war against China.

But other countries saw that and are taking advantage of what they perceive to be Trump's weakness, according to Bloomberg's reporting.

"China’s defiant stance in negotiating a tariff truce with the US has convinced some countries they need to take a tougher position in their own trade talks with the Trump administration," the report states, adding that, "Trump’s willingness to retreat so much from the earlier 145% duty on China surprised governments from Seoul to Brussels that have so far stuck with the US’s request to negotiate rather than retaliate against its tariffs."

The article quotes Stephen Olson, "a former US trade negotiator who’s now a visiting senior fellow with ISEAS — Yusof Ishak Institute in Singapore," as saying, "This shifts the negotiating dynamic."

"Many countries will look at the outcome of the Geneva negotiations and conclude that Trump has begun to realize that he has overplayed his hand," the expert then added.

According to Bloomberg, world leaders are still staying silent on their approach.

The report adds, "While officials are loathe to signal publicly any hardening of their approach, there are signs particularly from larger nations that they’re realizing they hold more cards than previously thought and can afford to slow the pace of negotiations."

Marko Papic, chief strategist of GeoMacro at BCA Research, had a similar take on what other countries are seeing from Trump.

"There are many countries that may learn from China that the correct way to negotiate with President Trump is to stand firm, remain calm and force him to capitulate," Papic said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Negotiation Missteps: A Closer Look

Donald Trump's latest foray into international negotiations, specifically in Switzerland, has been met with a mixed bag of interpretations, if we're to believe the recent Bloomberg report. At face value, some might argue Trump scored a minor victory by easing the tensions in the ongoing trade war with China. However, a deeper dive into the nuances of international diplomacy and trade negotiations paints a far less flattering picture of Trump's achievements—or lack thereof.

The Illusion of Success

The narrative that Trump's Switzerland negotiations represented a win because they "stopped some of the bleeding" in the trade war with China is a masterclass in setting the bar so low, it's practically underground. To frame this outcome as a victory is akin to celebrating after successfully using a bucket to bail out a single cup of water from a sinking ship. It's an exercise in futility that ignores the larger problem: the ship is still sinking.

Perceived Weakness: An Open Invitation for Exploitation

What's more damning in the Bloomberg report is the perception of Trump's weakness by other countries. This isn't just a minor hiccup in Trump's international negotiation strategy; it's a flashing neon sign to other nations that the U.S. is ripe for the taking. The report's indication that "Trump’s willingness to retreat so much from the earlier 145% duty on China surprised governments" is a diplomatic way of saying Trump folded like a cheap suit.

This perceived capitulation has essentially sent a signal to countries from Seoul to Brussels: The U.S. can be pushed around. This is not just a blow to Trump's reputation as a negotiator but a strategic misstep that could have far-reaching consequences for U.S. trade policy and international standing.

The Expert Take: A Shift in Dynamics

Stephen Olson, a seasoned trade negotiator, sums it up succinctly: "This shifts the negotiating dynamic." This isn't a shift towards a more favorable position for the U.S.; it's a shift towards uncertainty and a weaker bargaining position. When other countries conclude that Trump "has begun to realize that he has overplayed his hand," it's not just an observation; it's an indictment of Trump's negotiation tactics.

A Silent but Strategic Response from World Leaders

The Bloomberg report suggests that while world leaders are keeping mum about their strategies, there's a growing realization that they hold more cards than previously thought. This isn't just a minor recalibration of trade negotiation strategies; it's a fundamental reevaluation of the global trade landscape, with the U.S. increasingly seen as an unreliable and weakened player.

The Bottom Line

Marko Papic's comments perhaps best encapsulate the situation: "There are many countries that may learn from China that the correct way to negotiate with President Trump is to stand firm, remain calm and force him to capitulate." In essence, Trump's approach to international negotiations has inadvertently provided a blueprint for how to outmaneuver the U.S. on the global stage.

In conclusion, Trump's recent negotiations in Switzerland, far from showcasing his prowess in deal-making, have instead highlighted a series of strategic missteps that threaten to undermine the U.S.'s standing and strength in international trade negotiations. The real question we should be asking isn't whether Trump won or lost in Switzerland, but how much his negotiation tactics will cost the U.S. in the long run.
