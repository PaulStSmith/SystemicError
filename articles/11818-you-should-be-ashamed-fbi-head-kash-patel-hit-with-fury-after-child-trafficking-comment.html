'You should be ashamed!' FBI head Kash Patel hit with fury after child trafficking comment

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FBI Director Kash Patel attends a House Intelligence Committee hearing about worldwide threats, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 26, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

FBI Director Kash Patel was hit with furious pushback on Saturday after he made a comment related to child trafficking.

Patel has been under fire since his announcement that the death of disgraced financier Jeffrey Epstein was a suicide, after previously promoting conspiracy theories related to Epstein actually being killed in prison to protect other powerful child molesters.

Over the weekend, Patel took to X to celebrate what he considers to be the Trump administration's accomplishments related to child trafficking.

"200 Days of Trump Admin, From Jan 20 to Present: FBI has arrested over 1,600 people for violent crimes against children, to include 270 arrests for human trafficking," Patel wrote. In a separate post, the head of the FBI added that the bureau "has identified and found 4,000 child victims."

The pushback came almost instantly.

Barry Rubin, Senior Video Director at Republican Accountability Project and The Bulwark, said, "Release the files, bro."

Republicans against Trump asked, "When are you releasing the Epstein files?"

Journalist Jim Stewartson replied with, "You just fired the FBI agent who has made the most arrests of pedophiles in American history. You are hilariously covering up for pedophile Donald Trump. You are legally attacking a blogger for telling the truth about you. You are a Russian joke, and a f------ traitor.

A political strategist who goes by Sunday School Alum said, "But not Trump or any of his friends. You should be ashamed!"

GOP pollster Sarah Longwell also asked, "Are any of them from the Epstein files you promised to release?"

Our Analysis:

Analysis of FBI Director Kash Patel's Comments on Child Trafficking and the Ensuing Backlash

In a recent display of what can only be described as brazen audacity, FBI Director Kash Patel found himself at the center of a storm following his comments on child trafficking. Patel, whose tenure has been marred by controversy, particularly concerning his stance on the death of Jeffrey Epstein, decided to take to X, formerly known as Twitter, to boast about the Trump administration's supposed "accomplishments" in combating child trafficking. His post, which cited the arrest of over 1,600 individuals for violent crimes against children, including 270 arrests for human trafficking, was met with immediate and furious backlash.

The Context: A History of Controversy

Let's not gloss over the fact that Patel's credibility has been under scrutiny long before this incident. His assertion that Epstein's death was a suicide, directly contradicting his previous insinuations of foul play to protect powerful individuals, casts a long shadow of doubt over his statements. This flip-flopping on critical issues not only undermines his authority but also raises questions about the integrity of the investigations under his watch.

Immediate Backlash: A Public Cry for Transparency

The response to Patel's self-congratulatory post was swift and scathing. Barry Rubin's demand to "Release the files, bro," encapsulates the public's growing impatience for transparency and accountability. The call for the release of the Epstein files was echoed by many, pointing to a glaring omission in Patel's narrative: the lack of accountability for high-profile individuals potentially implicated in Epstein's web of crimes.

Jim Stewartson's accusation of Patel's hypocrisy couldn't be more damning. Labeling Patel a "Russian joke" and a "traitor" for allegedly covering up for Donald Trump and attacking whistleblowers, Stewartson's comment reflects a broader distrust in Patel’s leadership and his motivations. The dismissal of the FBI agent with a notable record of arresting pedophiles adds fuel to this fire, suggesting a prioritization of political protection over justice.

Selective Enforcement: A Pattern of Protecting the Powerful?

The critique from Sunday School Alum and GOP pollster Sarah Longwell touches on a critical issue: selective enforcement. The insinuation that high-profile individuals connected to Trump or implicated in the Epstein scandal are being shielded raises serious concerns about the integrity of the FBI's efforts against child trafficking. This selective enforcement, if true, not only undermines the fight against such heinous crimes but also erodes public trust in federal law enforcement agencies.

Conclusion: A Crisis of Credibility

Kash Patel's attempt to highlight the Trump administration's successes in combating child trafficking has backfired spectacularly, unveiling a deeper crisis of credibility and transparency within his leadership at the FBI. The public's demand for the release of the Epstein files is not just a call for justice for the victims but a test of the FBI's commitment to truth and accountability.

In an era where trust in public institutions is perilously low, Patel's actions—or lack thereof—serve as a stark reminder of the systemic errors plaguing our law enforcement agencies. As the head of the FBI, Patel has a responsibility to rise above political partisanship and prioritize the pursuit of justice, especially for the most vulnerable in society. Anything less is not just a failure of leadership; it's a systemic betrayal of public trust.
