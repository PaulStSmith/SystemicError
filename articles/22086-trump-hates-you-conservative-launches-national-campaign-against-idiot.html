'Trump hates you': Conservative launches national campaign against 'idiot'

Trump’s Hatred Is Not the Story. His Power Is.

A Campaign Against the Symptom

Steve Schmidt’s new anti-Trump drive is built around a blunt slogan: “Donald Trump Hates You.” The source material spends most of its time on Trump’s contempt, lies, racism, misogyny, and anti-constitutional language. That is not the hard part to establish. The hard part is naming the machinery that keeps making his damage possible.

The Real Seat of Power

Trump is the central figure, but he does not govern alone. The article itself says House Republicans are rubber-stamping his agenda and Senate Republican leaders are approving his most controversial appointments. That is institutional power in plain sight: elected Republicans are not victims of Trump’s mood. They are his enablers, translating personal authoritarianism into state action.

The Misdirection Problem

A slogan about hatred can be useful, but it can also flatten the problem into personality. Trump’s contempt is real. So is the political architecture around him. If the story stops at “he hates you,” it risks turning structural abuse into a psychological profile. That is a neat diversion for people who would rather condemn Trump’s character than confront the Republican apparatus that keeps rewarding it.

Cowardice with a Formal Dress Code

The source describes a Senate that is “slightly more cautious” while still unwilling to assert congressional authority over the White House. That is the polite version of abdication. Republican legislators are not confused about what Trump is doing. They are choosing submission because it preserves their power, even when it hollows out the institution they were elected to defend.

The Oppositional Theater

Schmidt is a former Republican strategist, and the Lincoln Project’s anti-Trump posture has always been part intervention, part branding exercise. This new campaign may be useful as messaging, but messaging is not power. A plane banner over the Florida panhandle and posters in Washington can spotlight the problem. They do not replace the missing force of a governing coalition willing to break the Republican lock on institutional compliance.

The Larger Pattern

This story is really about how authoritarian politics survives in democracies that still look normal on paper. The leader matters, but the bigger scandal is the chorus of officials, party leaders, and institutional adults who keep laundering the damage into procedure. Trump’s contempt is not merely personal. It becomes consequential because Republican power treats it as acceptable business.
