Nearly 100 billionaires and their spouses have donated to reelect Susan Collins

Based on an article by Josh Keefe, The Maine Monitor

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

Susan Collins, Billionaire Patronage, and the Fiction of a People’s Senate Race

The Setup

Susan Collins is running for reelection with a carefully staged image of retail normalcy, but the money behind her campaign is the opposite of ordinary. A Maine Monitor analysis found that nearly 100 billionaires and spouses have given $9.8 million to her network since early 2025, with most of it flowing through a super PAC that is already blanketing Maine with attack ads against Democrat Graham Platner. That is the real context the shoe-box video tried to hide.

Where the Power Sits

Actual power in this race does not sit with Collins’ campaign messaging or Platner’s populist pitch. It sits with the donors who can move millions through legal machinery, the Senate leadership groups that can amplify it, and the institutional structure that lets a sitting senator chair the appropriations committee while courting the people richest enough to shape the field around her.

This is not incidental support. It is patronage aimed at preserving access, influence, and a Senate seat that could affect control of the chamber. Jim Davis, Ken Griffin, Stephen Schwarzman, Miriam Adelson, Elon Musk, and others are not background figures. They are the funding architecture.

Who Enabled the Outcome

The immediate decision that mattered was Collins’ decision to lean into a campaign structure that now openly links her operation to a super PAC through joint fundraising agreements. The article says this setup became possible after a 2024 FEC advisory opinion, and Collins is the first time in her career to benefit from it. That is not a clerical detail. It is the point.

The larger enabling force is institutional cowardice dressed up as regulation. The FEC’s advisory opinion widened the hole between candidates and supposedly independent super PACs, while the agency itself has been effectively sidelined since April 2025 after losing a quorum. The guardrails did not merely fail. They were sawed down and left unattended.

The Blame Game

The framing in a story like this often drifts toward campaign theater, as if the real contest were Collins versus Platner, candidate versus candidate. That is a useful distraction for the people writing six-figure checks.

Platner’s campaign has drawn small-dollar donations and explicitly attacked super PACs. Collins’ side has the billionaire pipeline, the joint committees, the outside money, and the ad war. Treating this as a simple clash of personalities or campaign styles erases the asymmetry that defines the race.

Even the language of “outside spending” softens what is happening. These are not random political bystanders. They are aligned funders intervening in a Senate race because the seat matters to their class interest.

The Pattern

The story reveals a familiar American arrangement: one side sells itself as local, while being financed by national wealth; the other side runs on mass donations, then gets warned that big money may soon come calling anyway. That is not democracy in a healthy state. It is donor gravity.

The numbers make the pattern plain. Billionaires gave nearly as much to Collins’ side as small donors gave to Platner. Collins’ network raised about $30 million. Platner’s campaign raised far less, but with a donor base that looks like an actual constituency rather than a wealth registry. The article also notes that the billionaire donors backing Collins are worth an estimated $888 billion, none are Maine residents, and just 3 percent of her network’s itemized money came from Maine.

What This Really Shows

This race is not mainly about a senator, a challenger, or a clever campaign video. It is about how modern U.S. politics launders concentrated wealth through legal committees, advisory opinions, and weak oversight, then asks the public to mistake that process for competition.

The result is a system where billionaires can bankroll a Senate incumbent, super PACs can function as shadow campaign arms, regulators can be neutralized, and the public is left to absorb the spectacle as if it were just another election. The corruption is not hidden. It is organized, documented, and normalized.
