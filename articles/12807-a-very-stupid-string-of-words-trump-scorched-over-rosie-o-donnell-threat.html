'A very stupid string of words': Trump scorched over Rosie O'Donnell threat

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 13, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Threat Against Rosie O'Donnell: Distraction or Despotism?

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump's decision to go on Truth Social and threaten the citizenship of native New Yorker Rosie O'Donnell became an object of fascination on Saturday morning with some observers wondering what set him off this time.

Others suggested it was an attempt to distract observers from the Jeffrey Epstein civil war taking place within his administration.

Early Saturday, the president of the free world declared the popular comedian a "Threat to Humanity" on his social media platform.

He wrote, "Because of the fact that Rosie O’Donnell is not in the best interests of our Great Country, I am giving serious consideration to taking away her Citizenship. She is a Threat to Humanity, and should remain in the wonderful Country of Ireland, if they want her. GOD BLESS AMERICA!"

That led Esquire editor Dave Holmes to bluntly suggest, "Everything else aside, 'Rosie O’Donnell is not in the best interests of our Country' is just a very stupid string of words."

Attorney Adam Steinbaugh wrote, "I think you should be able to say things the president doesn’t like and still be a citizen. Maybe we should write that down somewhere," which led Rolling Stone journalist Thor Benson to comment, "Trump is such an alpha that, as president of the United States, he still writes angry posts about Rosie O'Donnell"

"hate to be the 'it’s a distraction' guy but the sole reason Trump’s talking about Rosie O’Donnell is to take the heat off his admin being responsible for loss of American lives in Texas. Everybody is taking the bait. Or they’ll say dumb shit like “we can do two things.” No. Keep your eye on the ball." was posted on the Bluesky account for the band known as the Mountain Goats.

Former MSNBC personality Keith Olbermann went on an extended tirade, writing, "There are absolutely no circumstances under which Trump can 'take away' the citizenship of anyone native-born His mere threat to do so immediately disqualifies him from remaining president To the rest of those in office: Someone else needs to be running this country, today."

He later added, "We have been building to this point since '15. We are here: Trump believes he is above all laws and the constitution. He is a clear and present danger to the future of this country and every one of us in it O'Donnell is right: he shares responsibility for the Texas deaths," before posting, "To MAGA: if YOU don't stop him now, he will eventually threaten to take away your citizenship."

Democratic strategist Adam Parkhomenko contributed, "Rosie O’Donnell must know who’s in the Epstein files if Trump is trying to make her go away."

"Rosie O’Donnell is one of the most caring & generous people I’ve ever known. Her, ‘For All Kids Foundation’ has awarded over $22 million in Early Childhood Care and Education grants to 1,400 child-related organizations And thats a fraction of her work for others. Trump has given nothing to others," wrote actress Mia Farrow.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Threat Against Rosie O'Donnell: Distraction or Despotism?

In a move that seems ripped straight from the playbook of petty tyrants rather than a leader of the free world, Donald Trump's latest tirade on Truth Social against comedian Rosie O'Donnell is not just bizarre; it's a glaring exhibition of authoritarian impulses dressed up in social media bravado. Trump's threat to revoke O'Donnell's citizenship is as absurd as it is alarming, painting a surreal picture of American politics where personal vendettas can translate into threats against individual freedoms.

"A Threat to Humanity," Trump declares, targeting O'Donnell in a manner that would be laughable if it weren't so deeply concerning. This isn't just another episode of celebrity feuding; it's a former president leveraging his platform to intimidate and silence a critic. The notion of revoking citizenship based on disagreement is not only unconstitutional but a frightening dive into authoritarian rhetoric.

Esquire editor Dave Holmes and attorney Adam Steinbaugh provide necessary sanity, pointing out the ludicrousness and danger of Trump's words. Holmes calls the attack a "very stupid string of words," highlighting the absurdity, while Steinbaugh reminds us of a foundational principle of democracy: the freedom to dissent without fear of state retribution.

Rolling Stone journalist Thor Benson and the band the Mountain Goats suggest that Trump's outburst might be a distraction from more serious issues plaguing his administration, notably the "Jeffrey Epstein civil war" and the "loss of American lives in Texas." This tactic—diverting attention from scandal or failure by creating a spectacle—is textbook Trump, but that doesn't make it any less effective or destructive.

Keith Olbermann's response is particularly poignant, emphasizing the constitutional impossibility of Trump's threat and declaring him a "clear and present danger to the future of this country." Olbermann's call to action, urging both MAGA supporters and the wider political community to recognize and resist Trump's authoritarian tendencies, is a sobering reminder of what's at stake.

Democratic strategist Adam Parkhomenko's comment hints at a darker, more conspiratorial motive behind Trump's attack, suggesting O'Donnell might possess damaging information about him. Meanwhile, Mia Farrow's defense of O'Donnell underscores the pettiness of Trump's assault, contrasting O'Donnell's charitable work with Trump's self-serving actions.

In sum, Trump's threat against Rosie O'Donnell is more than a personal vendetta; it's a symptom of a deeper malaise in American politics where authoritarian whims are aired publicly, with little regard for the principles of democracy or decency. The reaction from various observers not only reflects the absurdity of the situation but also the serious implications for freedom of speech and the rule of law. In this ongoing saga of Trump versus decency, one thing remains clear: the battle for America's soul is far from over.
