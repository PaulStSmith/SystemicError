Donald Trump has put more pressure on voters ahead of the midterms, with a former United States Attorney highlighting a new FBI role.

The president is seemingly nervous ahead of the November elections and is doing all he can to retain the GOP’s share of votes. Writing in her Substack, Joyce Vance suggested a new role of “FBI Election Executive,” held by Kellie M. Hardiman, could be a sign that Trump’s team will try to control as much as they can of the election.

The former US Attorney highlighted a recent email from Hardiman as a cause for concern ahead of the midterms.

The email reads, “To prepare for the 2026 US midterm elections, your election partners at the FBI, DOJ, DHS, USPIS, and the EAC would like to invite you to a call where we can discuss our preparations for the cycle, as well as updates and resources we can provide to you and your staff.”

Vance wrote, “With Trump, his complaints about others are always projection: He wants to make sure he can steal the midterm elections if his party loses, and no better way to do that than to get election administration out of the hands of pesky officials who insist on doing a fair count.

“The call is being organized for February 25. No one seems to know precisely what it’s about. But Trump’s claim that majority Black/Democratic counties, like Fulton County, Georgia, aren’t fit to run elections, and they should be taken over by Republican interests, is a pretty good bet.

“The email invite is signed off on by Kellie M. Hardiman, who identifies her role as ‘FBI Election Executive,’ a position I have not heard of previously. As a career federal prosecutor and a U.S. Attorney for eight years during the Obama administration, and as someone whose responsibilities included election protection, I’m fairly familiar with DOJ’s internal architecture for this work.

“NBC reported that one state election official said that, ‘No one has heard of this person — and we’re all wondering what an ‘FBI Election Executive’ is.’”

An FBI spokesperson, in a statement given to NBC, said, “The Election Executive is not a new role. There have been designated executives in previous election cycles to take point on coordinating election-related matters and speaking on behalf of the FBI.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Shadows: Trump’s Midterm Maneuvers and the Mysterious FBI Election Executive

Donald Trump’s political maneuvers have always had the subtlety of a sledgehammer, but his latest play ahead of the midterms is both alarmingly innovative and disturbingly familiar. As reported, the introduction of Kellie M. Hardiman as the “FBI Election Executive” has raised more than a few eyebrows, and for good reason. Let’s dissect this development with the surgical precision it deserves.

The Smokescreen of Election Integrity

The creation—or perhaps revelation—of the “FBI Election Executive” role, as highlighted by Joyce Vance, a former United States Attorney, reeks of desperation and manipulation. The email from Hardiman, which ostensibly aims to prepare for the 2026 US midterm elections, might seem innocuous at first glance. However, as Vance astutely points out, with Trump, complaints about others are a mirror reflecting his own tactics. The notion that Trump is positioning himself to “steal the midterm elections if his party loses” is not far-fetched; it’s a scenario grounded in his historical playbook of projecting his own intentions onto others.

The Role of Kellie M. Hardiman: A Wolf in Sheep’s Clothing?

Kellie M. Hardiman’s sudden emergence as the FBI’s Election Executive has understandably caused a stir. Given the lack of familiarity with this role among seasoned election officials and prosecutors alike, one must question the timing and the motives behind this appointment. The FBI’s statement that this is not a new role but rather a continuation of a practice of designating executives for election-related matters does little to assuage concerns. Instead, it begs the question: Why now? Why has this role suddenly become public-facing under the Trump administration’s influence?

The Projection Game: Trump’s Accusations as a Diversion

Trump’s accusations that majority Black/Democratic counties are incapable of running elections without interference are a classic example of his projection tactics. By claiming these areas are unfit, he creates a pretext for Republican interests to intervene, ostensibly to “save” the integrity of the election. This strategy is not just about winning; it’s about sowing doubt, delegitimizing democratic processes, and, if all else fails, setting the stage for a contested outcome.

The Veil of Cooperation: An Invitation to Collude?

The email invite from Hardiman, which calls for a discussion on preparations and updates for the election cycle, could be seen as a benign effort at collaboration. However, in the context of Trump’s past actions and the opaque nature of this new FBI role, it’s hard not to view it as a veiled invitation to collude. The call for election preparation becomes a Trojan horse, potentially ushering in undue influence and control over the electoral process.

Conclusion: Democracy in the Crosshairs

As we peel back the layers of this development, the picture that emerges is one of calculated subversion. The introduction of an FBI Election Executive at this juncture, especially one shrouded in mystery and linked to Trump’s broader aims, is an ominous sign. It’s a stark reminder that the battle for the soul of American democracy is ongoing, and vigilance is our only defense against those who seek to undermine it from within.

In essence, the real systemic error here is not in the mechanics of our elections but in the manipulation of those systems for personal and political gain. Trump’s latest gambit, with Hardiman as a potentially unwilling or unwitting pawn, is a clarion call to all who care about the integrity of our democracy to stand guard against the usurpation of power through the backdoor of bureaucratic maneuvering. The stakes could not be higher, and the fight for transparency and accountability has never been more urgent.
