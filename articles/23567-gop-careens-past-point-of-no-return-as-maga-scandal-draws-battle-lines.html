GOP careens past point of no return as MAGA scandal draws 'battle lines'

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosSeptember 19, 20263 min read

The Republican Party Is Not “Spinning Out of Control.” It Is Choosing a Direction.

The Scandal Is the Nomination

Bo French’s racist posts are not cryptic, isolated, or newly discovered. The Republican nominee for Texas railroad commissioner has called for Muslims and Chinese people to be rounded up, praised the racial order represented by Rhodesia, and treated the presence of nonwhite University of Texas fans as evidence of a “problem.” The relevant political fact is not merely that French said these things. It is that Republican voters made him their nominee for public office despite a long public record of saying them.

Power Sits With the People Who Enabled Him

French holds power because a major party placed him on the ballot. Donald Trump then used his influence as president and party leader to attack Karl Rove for supporting Democrat Jon Rosenthal, branding dissent from the nominee as disloyalty.

That intervention clarifies the hierarchy. Rove and Senator John Cornyn may condemn French, but Trump is enforcing the rule that partisan obedience outranks any stated objection to racism. French supplies the provocation; the party apparatus supplies legitimacy; Trump punishes defection. None of this is an accidental loss of control.

Racism Has Become a Loyalty Test

French’s defenders did not dispute the substance of his politics. They widened it. Turning Point USA spokesman Andrew Kolvet invoked the declining white share of the population as “demographic replacement.” Jack Posobiec urged the right to defend French “like the Alamo.” Steve Deace treated Indian immigration as suspect because India is not sufficiently Christian.

These are variations on the same proposition: America properly belongs to a preferred racial and religious population, while everyone else enters on sufferance. The message is not hidden because concealment would defeat its current purpose. Open bigotry now demonstrates membership in a faction that defines cruelty as courage and restraint as surrender.

“Out of Control” Hides the Decision

David French describes a Republican Party spinning out of control. That framing is too charitable. A machine is out of control when nobody is steering it. Here, identifiable people are rewarding the behavior, supplying ideological justifications, and threatening Republicans who refuse to comply.

The “New Right” language quoted from Claremont Institute fellow Jeremy Carl makes the intention plain. Condemnation of French is cast as the posture of “beautiful losers,” while racial exclusion is recoded as strength on “immigration and national identity.” This is not confusion about standards. It is a campaign to replace them.

The Respectable Right Built a Weak Firewall

Rove and Cornyn deserve no special credit for recognizing language that is impossible to sanitize. Their opposition matters electorally, but it also exposes how little authority the party’s institutional veterans retain when MAGA figures can define basic repudiation of racism as betrayal.

The supposed post-Trump contest between “normalcy” and the populist right is therefore badly mismatched. One camp issues condemnations. The other commands a mass constituency, influential media figures, ideological institutions, and the president’s loyalty enforcement. Treating those forces as equivalent factions obscures where the power actually lies.

The System Rewards the Escalation

The larger pattern is not that extremists occasionally infiltrate respectable politics. It is that a political system can convert public degradation into factional capital. The target population is marked as alien; the candidate proves his aggression; allied influencers normalize the premise; party leadership disciplines anyone who objects.

Bo French is replaceable. The structure elevating him is not. When racism becomes evidence of authenticity and repudiating it becomes evidence of weakness, scandal no longer functions as a constraint. It becomes an audition.
