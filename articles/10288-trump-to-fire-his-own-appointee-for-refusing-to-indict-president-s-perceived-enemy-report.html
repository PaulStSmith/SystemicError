Trump to fire his own appointee for refusing to indict president's perceived enemy: report

Donald Trump (Reuters)

A U.S. attorney who was appointed to his position by Donald Trump in May will be out of a job by Friday because he refused to file an indictment against New York Attorney General Letitia James for mortgage fraud, according to a new report.

According to a late Thursday report from ABC News, the U.S. attorney, Erik Siebert, could find no evidence against James and has refused, despite pressure from the White House, to bring charges, which will now cost him his job.

The New York AG has reportedly been targeted for successfully prosecuting Trump for criminal fraud.

James, along with Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, both of them Black women, have been accused of making deceptive statements when applying for mortgages, some say as a way to force them from their jobs, and like Cook there is reportedly no evidence of criminality.

That hasn’t stopped the Trump White House from keeping on, ABC News reports.

“Trump administration officials have argued that James committed mortgage fraud because one of the documents related to her 2023 home purchase, they say, falsely indicated the property would be her primary residence. The investigation began after Bill Pulte, the director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency, sent the DOJ a criminal referral about James in April,” the report notes before adding, “Federal prosecutors in Virginia had uncovered no clear evidence to prove that James had knowingly committed mortgage fraud.”

The firing of Siebert is creating alarm because the Eastern District of Virginia is considered one of the most important offices in the country “which handles a bulk of the country's espionage and terrorism cases.”

The report adds, “Sources familiar with the matter said that the administration now plans to install a U.S. attorney who would more aggressively investigate James.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Forced Departure of a U.S. Attorney Over a Refusal to Indict Letitia James

In a move that epitomizes the dangerous politicization of the United States' justice system, a U.S. attorney appointed by Donald Trump, Erik Siebert, is reportedly being ousted for his refusal to fabricate charges against New York Attorney General Letitia James. This incident, as reported by ABC News, sheds light on a deeply troubling effort to weaponize legal mechanisms against political adversaries, an act that not only undermines the integrity of the judicial system but also imperils democracy itself.

The Alleged Crime and the Lack of Evidence

The core of this controversy revolves around accusations of mortgage fraud against Letitia James, a charge that Siebert, despite pressure from the White House, found utterly baseless. The Trump administration's claim hinges on a document from James's 2023 home purchase, which they allege falsely indicated the property as her primary residence. However, federal prosecutors in Virginia, where such matters of alleged fraud would be scrutinized, "uncovered no clear evidence" to support the claim of knowing mortgage fraud by James.

It's essential to underscore the gravity of using such flimsy allegations to attempt to dismantle the career of a prominent legal figure. Letitia James, noted for her role in prosecuting Trump for criminal fraud, appears to be targeted not for any genuine wrongdoing but as retaliation for her legal successes against the former president.

The Disturbing Implications of Siebert's Firing

Erik Siebert's impending termination is not merely a career setback for one individual; it's a red flag signaling the erosion of judicial independence. The Eastern District of Virginia, known for handling significant national security cases, is losing a U.S. attorney because he stood on the side of evidence and integrity. The administration's plan to replace Siebert with someone "who would more aggressively investigate James" is a clear indication of a malicious intent to persecute, rather than a legitimate pursuit of justice.

The Larger Context: Politicization and Persecution

This incident is a stark manifestation of a broader strategy employed by the Trump administration to leverage governmental power against perceived enemies. The targeting of Letitia James—and by extension, Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, both of whom are Black women—raises serious concerns about the intersection of racial and political animus driving these actions.

Moreover, the willingness to dismiss a U.S. attorney for adhering to the principle of evidence-based prosecution lays bare an alarming readiness to sacrifice the pillars of the legal system for political vendettas. This not only threatens the careers of individuals like James and Siebert but also jeopardizes the foundational principles of fairness and justice that should guide the nation.

Conclusion

The circumstances surrounding Erik Siebert's ousting for his principled stand against baseless indictments should alarm every American. This is not simply a matter of internal politics or legal disagreements; it's a symptom of a democracy in distress, where the mechanisms of justice are hijacked for political retribution. As observers and participants in this democracy, it is our responsibility to remain vigilant and vocal against such abuses of power, lest we find the very fabric of our society irreparably torn.
