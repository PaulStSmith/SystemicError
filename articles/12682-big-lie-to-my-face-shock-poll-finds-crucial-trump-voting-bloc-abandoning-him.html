'Big lie to my face': Shock poll finds crucial Trump voting bloc abandoning him

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Regret: Young Men and the Trump Phenomenon

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the press as he arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 13, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

President Donald Trump won re-election with a big boost from younger men — but many of them are apparently already regretting their choice last November.

A new CNN poll found 40 percent of men younger than 35 approve of Trump's job performance and 60 percent disapprove, down from 44 percent approval and 54 percent disapproval in February. And 65 percent of men under 35 are dissatisfied with the government's handling of the Jeffery Epstein case, while only 10 percent are satisfied with the information that's been shared.

“You said you were going to show us (the Epstein files), now you’re just being the very thing you said you were going to end,” said Ben, a chemical worker from Battle Creek, Michigan, who asked CNN not to use his last name. “Not as transparent as what I thought.”

Trump issued a lengthy Truth Social post over the weekend calling on his followers to drop their outrage over the Epstein matter, which left many supporters feeling insulted. Ben told CNN that he now regretted his vote.

If given a chance to go back to November, Ben said, "Maybe I just wouldn’t vote,” he said.

Ben attended a standup comedy performance by Theo Von, who helped cement support from younger men with a viral podcast interview of Trump last August. The comedian has since become a vocal critic of the president who invited him to the VIP section at his inauguration.

“You just really start to feel very disillusioned pretty quickly,” Von said in a recent podcast.

Another fan, 21-year-old autoworker Justin Centers, expressed dissatisfaction with his first-ever presidential vote.

“Completely being honest, I’m a little iffy now,” said Centers, who lives in the Detroit suburbs. “One of the things I primarily voted for was ‘No new wars,’ and unfortunately, that has been a big lie to my face. So, it’s extremely disappointing to see that.”

Other Trump-backing Von fans voiced skepticism for Trump's $5 trillion tax-and-spending package, but not all of them were jumping off board just yet.

“When you go into your first year, you’re not going to do what everybody wanted you to at the beginning, because there are going to be things that you have to retract on, that you have to take care of,” said Tyler Goldsmith, a 32-year-old lawn-care business owner from Constantine, Michigan.

The softening support for the president among younger men should theoretically provide an opening for Democrats to scoop them up in next year's midterm elections, but pollsters are skeptical.

“When a voter has just gone a different direction, they’re the ones that can go back the other way,” said David Winston, a veteran Republican pollster. "They just made a decision and now they’re going to see if that worked or it didn’t work. The idea that they could bounce back to Democrats is certainly very possible. Having said that, they left for a reason.”

Centers, the autoworker from suburban Detroit, told CNN he's concerned that Trump's position on electric vehicles will hurt the value of his recently purchased Tesla, and the expectant father said he's worried that the economy isn't improving – but he said he'd unlikely to vote Democratic.

“A lot of stuff I identify with falls along the conservative agenda,” he said.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Regret: Young Men and the Trump Phenomenon

In the glaring light of political reality, a fascinating narrative unfolds—a tale of disillusionment and regret among young male voters who, enticed by promises and personas, cast their ballots for President Donald Trump, only to find themselves questioning the wisdom of their choice. A recent CNN poll sheds light on this shift, revealing a significant dip in Trump's approval ratings among men younger than 35. This group's dissatisfaction, particularly with the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein case, serves as a poignant reflection of broader concerns over transparency and accountability.

Promises vs. Reality: The Epstein Conundrum

The government's handling of the Epstein case emerges as a critical point of contention, with 65 percent of men under 35 expressing dissatisfaction. This issue symbolizes a larger betrayal; voters like Ben from Michigan express a sense of being duped by Trump's promise of transparency. Trump's call for his followers to abandon their outrage over Epstein, coupled with a dismissive Truth Social post, only adds insult to injury, highlighting a stark departure from the promised ethos of accountability.

The Theo Von Effect: From Support to Skepticism

The influence of cultural figures like comedian Theo Von, once a Trump supporter who played a role in rallying young men, underscores the impact of media and celebrity endorsements. However, Von's transition from supporter to critic reflects a broader disillusionment that is not isolated to political insiders. It is a vivid illustration of how quickly the tides of public opinion can turn when expectations are not met.

The Mirage of Change: No New Wars and Economic Promises

Justin Centers, a young voter, voices a common sentiment of betrayal over broken promises—specifically, the assurance of "No new wars." This sentiment, coupled with concerns over economic policies and their impact on personal investments, such as the value of electric vehicles, underscores a critical misalignment between Trump's campaign assurances and his administration's actions.

A Lingering Loyalty Amid Discontent

Despite the evident dissatisfaction, not all is lost for Trump among these young men. Figures like Tyler Goldsmith represent a segment of the population willing to give Trump the benefit of the doubt, attributing unfulfilled promises to the inevitable compromises of governance. This perspective highlights a complex, albeit waning, loyalty that exists within parts of Trump's base.

The Democratic Dilemma: An Opportunity with Obstacles

The erosion of support among young men presents a theoretical opportunity for Democrats, yet the path to capturing this disenchanted demographic is fraught with challenges. Pollsters like David Winston caution against overoptimism, pointing out that these voters' shift away from Trump does not automatically translate into support for Democrats. The reasons that drove them to Trump in the first place remain significant barriers to a straightforward political realignment.

Conclusion: A Crossroads for Young Male Voters

As the political landscape continues to evolve, the story of young men's flirtation with Trumpism serves as a cautionary tale about the perils of charismatic leadership devoid of substance. The coming elections will test whether these voters' regret translates into a realignment of their political loyalties or if their search for representation and accountability in American politics continues. For Democrats, the challenge will be to address the underlying issues that led these voters to Trump, rather than merely capitalizing on their disillusionment. The task ahead is not just to win votes but to restore faith in the democratic process and the promise of genuine leadership.
