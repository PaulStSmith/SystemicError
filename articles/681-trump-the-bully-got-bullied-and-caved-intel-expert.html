Trump the bully got bullied — and caved: intel expert

Based on an article by John Stoehr

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20263 min read

A Secret Deal for a Manufactured Disaster

What the reporting says

The source material describes a U.S.-Iran “peace deal” whose full terms will not be released until after signature, while Iran has already published its own 14-point terms. It says the White House has not put forward a counterproposal, that the agreement would lift sanctions, withdraw U.S. forces, unfreeze assets, and potentially pay Iran $300 billion, while Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz under arrangements that look a lot like toll collection.

Power sat with the man who started the war

The central fact is not the diplomacy. It is the leverage. Donald Trump launched the war, then tried to end it on terms he would not disclose before the signature line. That is not negotiation from strength. It is a president trying to bury the cost of his own decision before anyone can inspect it.

The source is right to treat secrecy as the story. When a White House withholds its own terms while the other side advertises its demands, the issue is no longer peace. It is concealment. The one actor with actual institutional power here is the U.S. president, and he is using that power to manage optics, not accountability.

This is not a deal, it is capitulation with branding

The terms described in the source read like a one-sided settlement after defeat: end the war, lift the blockade, pull back forces, drop sanctions, unfreeze cash, and hand over reconstruction money. In exchange, Iran gets to “reopen” a strategic waterway under its own arrangements. That is not a triumphant bargain. It is a toll booth dressed up as statesmanship.

The article’s framing is blunt, but the political logic is even uglier than the language suggests. Trump did not force Iran into submission. He created conditions where Iran could hold a chokepoint, extract concessions, and emerge more powerful than before. If the deal restores anything, it restores the old fact that Iran can use the strait as leverage. That is not peace secured. That is coercion normalized.

The real damage is institutional cowardice

The source tries to locate blame in Trump’s arrogance and stupidity, and that is not wrong. But the deeper failure is institutional. A president can drag the country into war, then rush a secret exit, and the governing class treats this as a communications problem. Republicans are already floating JD Vance as a fall guy. Marco Rubio and Vance reportedly kept their distance. That is not accountability. That is preemptive insulation.

The article also notes the obvious escape hatch: Congress is not going to act, and war-crime scrutiny is politically radioactive across administrations. That matters. The system does not merely tolerate this kind of damage; it trains itself to absorb it, rename it, and move on. The result is not just one president lying. It is an entire governing class learning to live with the lie.

Misdirection as a political technique

The source is clear that Trump wants 60 days of spin, delay, and headline management. That is the actual strategy here: keep the public from dwelling on the terms long enough for exhaustion to replace scrutiny. If the deal later fails, the hope is that blame can be shuffled onto aides, enemies, or the next crisis.

That is the signature move of weak power. Strong leaders do not need to hide the terms of their own victory. Weak ones do, especially after they have converted military force into a bad settlement. The attempt to blame underlings, obscure the money, and postpone the political reckoning is not a side story. It is the mechanism by which failures like this survive.

The larger pattern

This is what modern executive power looks like when it is separated from consequence: start the war, withhold the terms, declare success, and let everyone else pay the price in higher costs, strategic instability, and a more emboldened adversary. The source correctly notes that the globe may be living with the economic fallout for years.

That is the system in miniature: force without competence, secrecy without legitimacy, and blame shifted downward once the damage is done. Trump is the immediate author of the mess. The broader political order is what makes it sustainable.
