Meet the rich conservatives bankrolling a major fight against Trump

On Wednesday, November 5 — the day after the United States’ 2025 off-year elections — the U.S. Supreme Court will be hearing oral arguments in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, a case involving President Donald Trump’s ability to unilaterally impose tariffs via executive order under the Emergency Powers Act of 1977.

Many Democrats are hoping that the High Court will rule against Trump in the case, but opposition is coming from the right as well — including a group called the Liberty Justice Institute.

According to Washington Post reporter Kat Zakrzewski, the Institute is generously funding the anti-tariffs argument from the right.

“President Donald Trump is used to battles at the Supreme Court against liberal advocacy groups,” Zakrzewski explains in an article published on November 4. “But Wednesday’s high-stakes argument over his tariff policy features a very different foe — a legal center funded without public disclosure by some of the country’s wealthiest conservatives. The cases on which the Supreme Court is scheduled to hear oral arguments were brought by small businesses, which argue that Trump’s tariffs have harmed them by raising their costs.”

Zakrzewski adds, “Standing behind them, however — and paying for some of the high-priced legal talent — is the Liberty Justice Center, a nonprofit group with a libertarian-leaning agenda that has previously challenged public-sector unions and sued to prevent the ban on TikTok from taking effect.”

According to Zakrzewski, prominent conservatives are funding the Liberty Justice Center.

“Liberty Justice Center does not disclose the names of its donors,” the reporter notes, “but a Washington Post analysis of tax filings found that since 2020, it has received money from Donors Trust, the Walton Family Foundation and the Bradley Foundation, all of which have been prominent conservative donors. Donors Trust is a fund that receives money from wealthy donors whose identities are not disclosed and steers it toward conservative causes.”

Zakrzewski continues, “The group has frequently backed organizations associated with Federalist Society co-Chairman Leonard Leo, who counseled Trump on judicial picks during his first presidential term, but whom Trump denounced in May, in part because of the tariff case. Liberty Justice Center is also listed as a national partner of the State Policy Network, a network of conservative nonprofit organizations with links to Charles and David Koch that also receives funding from Donors Trust.”

Our Analysis:

The Odd Bedfellows of American Politics: Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump

In a twist that sounds like the plot of a political thriller rather than the sober reality of American judiciary proceedings, the Supreme Court is set to hear arguments in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, a case that pits President Donald Trump against not just his usual adversaries — the Democrats — but also an array of figures from his own ideological backyard. The case, centered around Trump’s unilateral imposition of tariffs via executive order, underscores a fascinating clash within conservative ranks, revealing the complex, often contradictory underbelly of American political alliances.

Trump’s Tariff Tantrum: A Quick Recap

At the heart of this legal brawl is Trump’s use of the Emergency Powers Act of 1977 to slap tariffs willy-nilly, a move that has, predictably, ruffled more than a few feathers. Small businesses, perennially celebrated in conservative rhetoric as the backbone of the American economy, have found themselves gasping under the weight of these tariffs. The argument is straightforward: Trump’s tariffs are a financial chokehold on these businesses, inflating costs and squeezing margins to breaking points.

Enter the Liberty Justice Institute

The Washington Post’s Kat Zakrzewski brings to light an intriguing player in this drama — the Liberty Justice Center. This legal center, with its libertarian leanings, has thrown its considerable weight behind the anti-tariffs argument. What’s particularly juicy here is the source of the center’s funding. The Liberty Justice Center operates with the financial backing of some of the wealthiest conservatives in the nation, yet cloaks its benefactors in secrecy. This arrangement raises eyebrows, not least because it includes money from Donors Trust, the Walton Family Foundation, and the Bradley Foundation — all heavy hitters in the conservative funding world.

The Irony of Conservative Infighting

This scenario is rife with irony. The Donald, once the darling of the far-right and the champion of “America First” economics, finds himself at odds with a legal center bankrolled by his erstwhile allies. The Liberty Justice Center, connected to the Federalist Society co-Chairman Leonard Leo (a key figure in Trump’s judicial nominations during his presidency), and a partner of the State Policy Network (linked to the Koch brothers), represents a faction of conservatism that seems to have lost patience with Trump’s brand of economic nationalism.

The Shadows of Conservative Financing

Zakrzewski’s piece sheds light on a broader, more unsettling trend in American politics: the opaque financing of policy advocacy. The Liberty Justice Center’s undisclosed donors underscore a troubling aspect of political influence — the power of unseen money. It’s a reminder that behind every legal challenge, there may lie a web of financial interests, with the potential to shape policy far from the public eye.

Conclusion: The Tariffs, the Court, and the Conservatism Conundrum

As the Supreme Court prepares to hear Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump, the case emerges as more than a legal dispute over tariffs. It’s a window into the fissures within American conservatism, a battle over the direction of economic policy, and a showcase of the murky waters of political financing. Regardless of the outcome, one thing is clear: the fight over Trump’s tariffs is a microcosm of the larger ideological and financial battles roiling the right. And as these battles spill into the public arena, they expose the often shadowy dynamics that drive American politics.
