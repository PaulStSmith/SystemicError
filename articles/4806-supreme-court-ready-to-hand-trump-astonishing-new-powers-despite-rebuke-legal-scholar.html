Supreme Court ready to hand Trump 'astonishing' new powers despite rebuke: legal scholar

Kim Wehle, a former Justice Department attorney and legal scholar, warned Friday that while the Supreme Court may have delivered a stunning rebuke to President Donald Trump last week by ruling against his tariffs, the justices have shown no sign that they intend to curb his ongoing and, in her view, “astonishing” efforts to expand presidential power.

Last Friday, the Supreme Court delivered a fatal blow to many of Trump’s tariffs after deciding in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump that the president had acted outside his authority in imposing them. Wehle, however, warned of getting “complacent,” citing a mountain of evidence to suggest that the court had no intention of stopping Trump’s efforts to expand his executive authority.

“Let’s not lose our heads. Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump is a check – but not a constitutional reset,” Wehle wrote in an analysis published Friday in Zeteo. “Nothing in the ruling suggests that the Trump-aligned majority has had a sudden conversion to muscular judicial oversight.”

To illustrate her argument, Wehle cited three key points: that the Supreme Court’s decision against Trump’s tariffs was not unanimous, that the decision also happened to be beneficial to “the very rich,” and perhaps most importantly, that the court has yet to meaningfully check Trump’s power.

“The Supreme Court may have stalled Trump’s price increases, but we cannot afford complacency. Nor should we fall for the comforting fiction that this marks a change of heart,” Wehle wrote.

“The rule of law is not secured by a single opinion; it is secured by sustained fidelity to principle. Those who joined this majority will have many more chances to prove whether they are guided by the Constitution over politics. Until that record is clear, they must be held to account – praised when they defend the law, and called out, plainly and persistently, when they betray it.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Supreme Court’s Decision Against Trump’s Tariffs: A Closer Look

In a recent dissection of the Supreme Court’s ruling against President Donald Trump’s tariffs, Kim Wehle, a former Justice Department attorney and legal scholar, presents an argument that is as pertinent as it is provocative. Her analysis, published in Zeteo, raises a clarion call against complacency in interpreting this legal development as a significant limitation on presidential power. Let’s dive into her arguments with the same sharp scrutiny she advocates.

The Illusion of a Check on Presidential Power

First and foremost, Wehle’s caution against viewing the Supreme Court’s decision in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump as a complete rebuke of Trump’s authority is well-founded. The ruling, while a blow to Trump’s tariff policies, is hardly the constitutional reset some might wish it to be. Wehle astutely notes that the decision was not unanimous, hinting at the underlying fractures within the Court that could influence future rulings. This is a crucial reminder that the fight against unchecked executive power is far from over.

A Victory for the Very Rich?

Wehle’s second point touches on a bitter truth often ignored in legal analyses: the beneficiaries of court decisions. She suggests that the decision against Trump’s tariffs was also beneficial to “the very rich,” a demographic that hardly needs additional advantages. This observation raises uncomfortable questions about whose interests are truly being served in high-stakes legal battles. It’s a poignant critique of a justice system that, too often, seems to tilt in favor of the wealthy and powerful.

The Supreme Court’s Silence on Presidential Power

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Wehle’s analysis is the assertion that the Supreme Court has yet to meaningfully check Trump’s efforts to expand his executive authority. This is not merely an oversight; it is a glaring omission in the Court’s responsibility to uphold constitutional principles over political motivations. Wehle’s call for sustained fidelity to principle over politics is both urgent and necessary. The Supreme Court’s role is to serve as a bulwark against the overreach of any branch of government, including the executive. When it fails to fulfill this role, the very fabric of democracy is at risk.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

In conclusion, Kim Wehle’s analysis is a sharp reminder that the battle for constitutional governance and the limitation of presidential power is ongoing. The Supreme Court’s decision against Trump’s tariffs is a step in the right direction, but it is only one step. Wehle’s argument serves as a call to action: We must remain vigilant, critically assessing the Court’s decisions, and holding it accountable for its role in shaping the balance of power in the United States.

As Wehle poignantly puts it, “The rule of law is not secured by a single opinion; it is secured by sustained fidelity to principle.” In this era of political tumult, her words are a beacon for all who believe in the principles of justice and democracy. It is up to us, and our institutions, to ensure those principles are not just upheld, but championed.
