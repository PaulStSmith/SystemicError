With Texas Republicans facing the prospect of a bruising battle between Sen. John Cornyn and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton for the nomination for the seat Cornyn now holds, longtime political advisor Mark McKinnon said the GOP in Texas may have a bigger problem.

Appearing on MS NOW’s “The Weekend,” McKinnon noted that he has been historically skeptical about enthusiasm for rising political stars, but Democratic nominee James Talarico seems to have tapped into something and voter turnout this past week should make the GOP leadership sit up and notice.

McKinnon, who has worked for Republicans including ex-President George W. Bush and the late Senator John McCain (R-AZ) told host Jonathan Capehart, “They’re really freaked out about James Talarico. This guy is a once-in-a-generation talent. He’s got a message and a style that really appeals to not only Democrats, but independents and soft Republicans, and obviously, particularly if the runoff, if the Republicans nominate Ken Paxton, who’s very unpopular, has tons of ethics problem, there’s a real shot here.“

“I’m the guy that usually taps the brakes every few years when people come to me and say, ‘Oh, Texas turning, turning blue, turning blue And I’m saying, ‘Now wait a minute, wait a minute. You know, just because there’s more Hispanics doesn’t mean they’re voting Democratic,” he conceded.

He then pointed out, “Democrats saw, by the way, the highest early vote,” adding, “This is the first time the Democrats have exceeded Republican early vote in 20 years — so there’s something going on.”

Our Analysis:

Texas GOP’s Internal Struggle: A Prelude to Democratic Surge?

In an era where political landscapes are as stable as quicksand, the unfolding drama within the Texas Republican Party, as narrated by Mark McKinnon on MS NOW’s “The Weekend,” serves as a testament to the shifting sands beneath the GOP’s feet. McKinnon, with his extensive background in political advising, casts a long shadow over the Texas GOP’s internal discord, highlighting a potential seismic shift in Texan politics that could see the emergence of Democrat James Talarico as a formidable challenger.

The GOP’s Bruising Battle: Cornyn vs. Paxton

The looming specter of a messy nomination fight between Senator John Cornyn and Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton is painted as nothing short of a Shakespearean tragedy for the Texas Republicans. The internal strife is not just a matter of choosing the party’s standard-bearer; it’s an existential crisis. Paxton, with his “tons of ethics problem”, as McKinnon delicately puts it, could inadvertently hand over the keys to the kingdom if he becomes the nominee. The choice between Cornyn and Paxton isn’t just about policy; it’s about survival.

James Talarico: A Democratic Beacon?

Enter James Talarico, the Democratic nominee who, according to McKinnon, could very well be the GOP’s worst nightmare. Described as a “once-in-a-generation talent”, Talarico appears to transcend traditional political boundaries, appealing to not only Democrats but also independents and “soft Republicans”. This isn’t just another Democrat trying to make inroads in a staunchly Republican state; this is a candidate who seems to resonate on a different frequency altogether.

McKinnon’s acknowledgment of Talarico’s appeal, coupled with the historic Democratic turnout in early voting, signals a change that could upend the conventional wisdom about Texas politics. For the first time in two decades, Democrats have outpaced Republicans in early voting. This isn’t just a blip on the radar; it’s a clarion call.

The Myth of the Monolithic Hispanic Vote

McKinnon’s candid admission about the evolving political leanings of Hispanic voters in Texas is particularly revealing. The old calculus that more Hispanic voters automatically equals more Democratic votes is being challenged. The demographic shift isn’t just numerical; it’s ideological. As Texas becomes more diverse, the political affiliations of its residents are becoming more nuanced, complicating the GOP’s strategy in the state.

Conclusion: A Storm on the Horizon

What we’re witnessing, through McKinnon’s insights, is not just a potential upset in a single state. It’s a microcosm of the larger, more profound changes sweeping across the American political landscape. The Texas GOP’s internal battle and the rise of James Talarico point to a broader trend of political realignment and the increasing unpredictability of voter behavior.

The GOP, traditionally seen as an indomitable force in Texas, now finds itself at a crossroads. The choice between Cornyn and Paxton is more than just a nomination battle; it’s a referendum on the party’s future in the state. Meanwhile, Talarico’s ascendancy represents not just a challenge to the GOP’s dominance but a possible harbinger of a new political era in Texas.

In the end, the Texas GOP’s biggest problem isn’t James Talarico; it’s the party’s inability to reckon with the changing dynamics of its electorate. As McKinnon astutely points out, there’s something going on in Texas. Whether the GOP is ready to face that reality remains to be seen.
