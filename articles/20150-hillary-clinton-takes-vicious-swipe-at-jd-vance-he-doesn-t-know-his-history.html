Hillary Clinton takes vicious swipe at JD Vance: 'He doesn't know his history'

Based on an article by David McAfee

Power Wants Its Own Crimes Rewritten

The Story Beneath the Snark

The immediate story is simple: Hillary Clinton mocked Vice President JD Vance after he reportedly downplayed Watergate, compared himself to Nixon, and blamed the “deep state” for Nixon’s downfall. She also used the moment to hit Republicans over book bans and their submission to Donald Trump.

That is the surface. The political substance is uglier: a sitting vice president is helping rehabilitate presidential lawbreaking by recasting accountability as sabotage.

Who Actually Held Power

The article’s own facts make the power structure plain. Nixon was not some helpless victim of faceless bureaucrats. He was the president. Vance is not an outsider being crushed by the state. He is the vice president of the United States, speaking from inside the machinery of power.

That matters because grievance politics depends on erasing hierarchy. It invites the public to imagine that the powerful are the ones under siege, while the institutions they control are somehow neutralized by shadowy forces. That inversion is not confusion. It is political cover.

Watergate Was Not a Mystery

Clinton’s sharpest point is also the simplest: Republicans in the Watergate era ultimately forced Nixon to face reality. The party did not pretend the break-in was a misunderstanding or a witch hunt. Its leaders pushed a law-breaking president toward resignation.

That comparison is devastating because it exposes the modern Republican collapse. Today’s party does not police misconduct. It launders it. It does not act as a brake on executive abuse. It supplies the political insulation that makes abuse sustainable.

The “Deep State” Is the Shield

Vance’s reported claim that Watergate was really the work of the “deep state” is not an innocent historical take. It is a familiar authoritarian move: strip responsibility from the person who made the decision, then assign blame to unelected enemies who cannot vote, answer back, or be held accountable.

This is how political misconduct gets transformed into martyrdom. The act itself disappears. The problem becomes the exposure of the act. In that frame, corruption is not the injury. Accountability is.

That is the misdirection at the center of the story. The blame is shifted downward and outward, away from the officials with power and onto institutions meant to constrain them.

Book Bans and Weaponized Ignorance

Clinton’s line about banned books is more than a cheap jab. It points to a deeper tactic: control the narrative by controlling what people are allowed to know. If the state is already filtering history through bans, then the claim that history can be casually rewritten becomes more than a rhetorical flourish. It becomes an institutional habit.

Ignorance is not always accidental. Sometimes it is produced on purpose, then used as evidence that power should be trusted more, not less. The same political coalition that polices books also wants to redefine scandal as persecution. That is not a contradiction. It is a system.

The Pattern: Loyalty Over Law

The larger pattern here is the merger of executive impunity with partisan obedience. A law-breaking president no longer needs a party willing to confront him. He needs a party willing to lie for him, or at minimum remain silent while he and his allies rewrite the record.

That is why Clinton’s contrast lands. Watergate showed a political class that, however imperfectly, still recognized that presidential power had limits. The current Republican posture is the opposite: protect the leader, punish the record, and call the consequences a conspiracy.

The Real Lesson

The story is not about a stray historical mistake by JD Vance. It is about the ongoing effort to turn accountability into victimhood and lawbreaking into legend. When a ruling coalition starts treating its own crimes as evidence of persecution, the problem is no longer bad memory. It is a governing style built on denial, hierarchy, and obedience.
