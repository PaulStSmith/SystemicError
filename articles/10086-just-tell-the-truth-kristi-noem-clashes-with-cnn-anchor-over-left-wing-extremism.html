'Just tell the truth!': Kristi Noem clashes with CNN anchor over left-wing extremism

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem attends a “Five Eyes” security alliance summit for talks on people smuggling and child sexual abuse, in London, Britain on Sept. 8, 2025. REUTERS/Jack Taylor

Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem clashed with CNN anchor Kaitlan Collins on Wednesday night over the outlet’s coverage of left-wing extremism.

Noem joined CNN’s Kaitlan Collins on “The Source” to discuss the latest updates in the investigation of a shooting at the Immigration and Customs Enforcement field office in Dallas, Texas, on Wednesday that left two detainees dead and one other injured. President Donald Trump and administration officials like FBI Director Kash Patel have said the shooter was a left-wing extremist.

“Is that information the president received from the Department of Homeland Security?” Collins asked.

Noem dodged the question by arguing that Democrats have adopted extreme positions on issues like crime. She added that she would love to see Democrats like California Gov. Gavin Newsom and Rep. Jasmine Crockett (D-TX) “denounce” criminals rather than standing with them.

“But, as the Secretary of Homeland Security, would you label the Democrats a domestic extremist organization? Yes or no.” Collins replied.

“Their views are extreme and they don’t align with the American people at all,” Noem said.

“If you guys would just tell the truth about what these criminals are and what they’ve done in their history, that’d be fantastic,” she continued. “We send out about five or six different press releases every day, correcting stories, and I’ll send you the copies of the ones that you’ve gotten wrong that we’ve been able to correct.”

“But but these law enforcement officers are being demonized, and they should be supported because they have families, too, and they live in these communities,” she added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Analyzing Noem’s Clash with Collins

In a recent exchange that epitomizes the continuing saga of political obfuscation, Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem found herself at odds with CNN anchor Kaitlan Collins. The confrontation, ostensibly about the tragic shooting at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) field office in Dallas, Texas, quickly devolved into a broader debate on left-wing extremism and the alleged complicity of the Democratic Party.

Dodging the Heart of the Matter

When pressed by Collins on whether the information regarding the shooter’s political affiliations came from the Department of Homeland Security, Noem’s response was a masterclass in deflection. Rather than addressing the question directly, Noem launched into a critique of Democratic positions on crime, implying a broad-brush association of the party with extremism. This tactic of shifting the conversation away from the specifics of the incident to a general critique of political opponents is not new, but Noem’s execution of it was notably blunt.

The Question of Extremism

Collins’ follow-up question was both simple and direct: “As the Secretary of Homeland Security, would you label the Democrats a domestic extremist organization?” Noem’s response, that Democratic views are “extreme” and out of alignment with the American populace, skirts dangerously close to conflating political opposition with extremism. This leap from criticizing policies to hinting at an entire political party being extremist is not only a gross oversimplification but a dangerous rhetoric that further polarizes an already divided nation.

The Blame Game

Noem’s insistence that the media—and by extension, Democrats—refuse to acknowledge the criminal history of certain individuals shifts the focus from the systemic issues at play. Her claim of sending out corrections to media stories suggests an administration more focused on controlling the narrative than addressing the root causes of violence and extremism. The mention of law enforcement officers being “demonized” attempts to redirect the conversation yet again, framing the administration as the defenders of family values and community stability.

Conclusion: The Erosion of Discourse

This exchange highlights a concerning trend in political dialogue: the escalation of accusations and deflection over substantive discussion. Noem’s responses to Collins’ questions represent not just a refusal to engage with the specifics of the incident in question, but a broader strategy of demonizing the opposition and muddying the waters of public discourse. In a time when clarity and transparency are more crucial than ever, such tactics only serve to deepen divisions and obscure the truth.

The clash between Noem and Collins is emblematic of a systemic error in political communication: the prioritization of narrative over fact, of deflection over dialogue. As observers and participants in the democratic process, it is incumbent upon us to demand better, to sift through the obfuscation, and to seek the truth with unwavering dedication.
