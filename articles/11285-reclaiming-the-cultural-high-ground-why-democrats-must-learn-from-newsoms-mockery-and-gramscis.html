Reclaiming the Cultural High Ground: Why Democrats Must Learn from Newsom’s Mockery and Gramsci’s Wisdom

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 24, 20257 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

For more than half a century, the Republican Party has waged political war not primarily through policy, but through culture. Theirs has been a strategy of images, symbols, and slogans—each carefully honed to bypass the rational mind and burrow into the cultural bloodstream. The result? A political dominance wildly disproportionate to the actual popularity of their cruel, billionaire-serving policies. Democrats, meanwhile, have too often chosen the role of the hapless technocrat, meeting gut punches with white papers. The AlterNet piece about Gavin Newsom finally meeting Trump on his own turf is more than a curiosity—it’s a flashing neon sign pointing Democrats toward a long-forgotten truth: in politics, culture is king.

The Long Shadow of Gramsci

Antonio Gramsci, the Italian Marxist thinker imprisoned by Mussolini, understood a truth that continues to haunt modern politics. In his Prison Notebooks, he argued that raw state power is never enough to secure long-term dominance. To truly govern, one must shape the culture. This means controlling not just parliaments and courts, but also schools, churches, newspapers, and the stories a people tell themselves about who they are. Gramsci called it “cultural hegemony.”

The right learned from him, even as they slandered “cultural Marxism.” Breitbart made it a bumper sticker (“Politics is downstream from culture”). Steve Bannon made it a White House playbook. Frank Luntz reduced it to linguistic gut-punches. And the GOP has spent decades weaponizing culture with ruthless efficiency, while Democrats—either out of cowardice, incompetence, or arrogance—ceded the battlefield.

From Nixon to Trump: The GOP’s Cultural Offensive

Richard Nixon’s “law and order” was never really about crime. It was coded language, a racist cudgel meant to crush the civil rights movement and intimidate Black communities. His “war on drugs,” as his aide John Ehrlichman later admitted, was explicitly designed to criminalize both Black people and anti-war activists. By linking dissent and Blackness with criminality, Nixon shifted the cultural frame: opposing him became synonymous with chaos.

Ronald Reagan perfected this culture game. His invention of the “welfare queen”—a racially coded lie about poor Black women gaming the system—turned public assistance into a symbol of corruption and laziness. With a single story, Reagan delegitimized the social safety net and made cruelty seem like common sense.

Donald Trump, coached by Bannon and fueled by Fox News, turbocharged the formula. His rallies are carnivalesque spectacles of grievance, his memes and insults viral cultural weapons. He doesn’t need coherent policy; he needs nicknames, chants, and images that stick in the gut. Politics as authoritarian theater.

Democrats’ Technocratic Death Spiral

Democrats, for decades, have been the party of charts and bullet points. They’ve let Republicans seize words like “freedom,” “life,” and “choice,” while countering with sterile jargon. Consider healthcare. Republicans framed opposition to reform as “choose your doctor.” Democrats answered with talk of “exchanges,” “single payer,” and “public options.” Which phrase do you think resonates in a kitchen-table conversation?

Or take taxation. Republicans talk about “tax relief”—casting taxation as a disease. Democrats talk about “marginal rates” and “progressive brackets.” Who wins the cultural argument? The GOP every time. Yet Democrats once knew better. FDR famously said, “Taxes are what we pay for civilized society.” He understood that framing taxation as the cost of shared civilization was not only true, but emotionally resonant.

On guns, Republicans shout “freedom” and “tyranny.” Democrats drone on about “background checks” and “assault weapons bans.” Both are necessary policy discussions, but neither is a cultural vision. Democrats could seize the freedom frame: freedom from being shot in school, freedom from living in fear at the grocery store, freedom to know your child will come home alive. But they’ve refused to.

On immigration, Republicans thunder about “secure borders” and “sovereignty.” Democrats mutter about “pathways to citizenship.” One side makes it about national survival; the other reduces it to bureaucratic paperwork. Republicans turned America into a fortress in the cultural imagination. Democrats forgot they were the party of the Statue of Liberty.

The list goes on: “small government” versus “effective government.” “Red tape” versus “regulation.” “Life” versus “choice.” Republicans understood the game. Democrats pretended the game didn’t exist.

Enter Gavin Newsom: Mockery as a Political Weapon

Which brings us to Gavin Newsom, and why his trolling of Trump matters. Newsom is doing something Democrats have been too afraid to attempt since the Reagan era: seizing the cultural weaponry Republicans have monopolized. By mocking Trump online, by using memes and parody, Newsom is shifting the battlefield. He’s speaking the language of the internet generation, the same memetic shorthand the right has used to brutal effect.

Fox News is sputtering because ridicule, when done well, is devastating. Trump has survived scandal after scandal, but being laughed at—that cuts deeper than any fact-check. Authoritarians crave fear and demand respect; mockery erodes both. Newsom’s strategy is a form of cultural jujitsu: using the opponent’s strength against him.

This is not about reducing politics to snark. It’s about understanding that cultural framing is not trivial. Memes, symbols, and narratives are not fluff—they are the bloodstream of politics. If Democrats want to win, they must learn to dominate this terrain.

The Lost Democratic Art of Storytelling

It wasn’t always this way. During the New Deal and Great Society eras, Democrats were masters of cultural framing. FDR didn’t sell programs—he sold faith. “Freedom from want.” “Freedom from fear.” He turned dry policy into moral crusades. Lyndon Johnson didn’t present Medicare as an insurance scheme; he sold it as part of a vision for a “Great Society” where dignity was universal.

Those were cultural narratives, not policy briefs. They tied Democratic politics to the deepest aspirations of the American people. But from Carter onward, Democrats fell into the trap of technocracy. Obama briefly rekindled the cultural spark with “Hope and Change,” but the message collapsed when his administration served Wall Street bankers and health insurance companies instead of the working people who carried him to power.

The Stakes: Democracy Itself

This is not an academic debate. The stakes could not be higher. Republicans have poisoned America’s cultural river for decades. Nixon did it with “law and order.” Reagan with his “welfare queen” lies. Trump with his authoritarian spectacle. Each step has chipped away at democracy itself, replacing pluralism with paranoia, replacing solidarity with grievance.

The result? A nation where cruelty is normalized, corruption is disguised as patriotism, and the word “freedom” has been hijacked to mean billionaire impunity and mass gun violence.

If Democrats continue to cede this ground, they will not just lose elections—they will lose the Republic. A democracy without cultural legitimacy cannot endure. It becomes an empty shell, waiting for the next strongman to kick it in.

The Path Forward: Reframing Freedom

The lesson of Gramsci, and of Newsom’s trolling, is that Democrats must reclaim the language of freedom and fairness. They must speak in moral terms, cultural terms, visceral terms.

  • Taxes are not a burden; they are the price of freedom, the way we build a civilization where opportunity is real.

  • Regulations are not “red tape”; they are the rules that keep the game fair, that protect your kids from poisoned air and your wallet from corporate fraud.

  • Healthcare is not about exchanges; it’s about the right to live without fear of bankruptcy or death from treatable illness.

  • Guns are not about background checks; they’re about whether your child makes it home alive.

  • Immigration is not about paperwork; it’s about whether America still stands for the promise inscribed on the Statue of Liberty.

This is the cultural high ground. This is the story of America worth telling: not a fortress for billionaires, but a community where everyone has a fair shot and nobody is left behind. A we society instead of the selfish Ayn Rand fantasy of me.

Conclusion: Fight Like the Culture Depends on It—Because It Does

Republicans have long understood Gramsci’s lesson: control the culture, and politics follows. Democrats ignored it at their peril. Today, with democracy itself on the line, the party cannot afford to stay trapped in the language of technocracy. They must speak to the heart as well as the head. They must reclaim freedom from the clutches of fascism and redefine it as something inclusive, humane, and unmistakably American.

Gavin Newsom’s trolling is not the whole solution. But it is a glimpse of what is possible when Democrats fight on the right battlefield. It is proof that Trump, for all his bluster, can be punctured by ridicule, by narrative, by cultural jujitsu. It is a reminder that culture is not an afterthought—it is the riverbed through which politics flows.

If Democrats want to save democracy, they must stop ceding the culture war and start winning it. They must tell the story of America not as a fortress under siege, but as a community bound by freedom, fairness, and dignity. Because if they don’t, Republicans will keep telling their poisoned story—and the nation may not survive the ending.
