Supreme Court conservatives blocked gay marriage challenge to protect GOP: expert

The U.S. Supreme Court denied a request by anti-LGBTQ county clerk Kim Davis to overturn the landmark decision on same-sex marriage — but a legal expert cautioned the decision was politically calculated to protect the Republican Party.

The justices swatted away the former Kentucky clerk’s long-shot appeal of a jury’s order to her to pay a gay couple $360,000 in damages for violating their civil rights after she refused to grant them a marriage license a decade ago. Slate’s Mark Joseph Stern has a theory why the court denied her request to overturn Obergefell v. Hodges.

“Davis’ chief claim is that she should be able to raise a constitutional defense to the lawsuit against her, claiming a First Amendment right to deny the marriage license based on her own religious liberty,” Stern wrote.

“She lost that argument at every court below, because — and this is pretty obvious — an agent of the state who’s performing a government service has no right to unlawfully discriminate against members of the public. Nor does such an individual have any entitlement to take the law into her own hands, defy a court order, and decline to perform the basic functions of her office.”

“Even hard-right judges who oppose Obergefell had to acknowledge that she had no constitutional leg to stand on,” he added.

Davis’ lawyers at the right-wing Liberty Counsel tacked on a request to overturn the 2015 ruling that recognized same-sex marriage after losing several rounds of litigation, which Stern said was added to her Supreme Court petition almost like an afterthought.

“If Liberty Counsel’s primary goal was to draw attention — and, by extension, fundraising dollars — by taking on marriage equality itself, it worked,” Stern wrote. “Media coverage of this case was wildly disproportionate to its (near-zero) chances of success.”

Justices Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito signaled their willingness to revisit Obergefell in a previous Davis appearance before them, in 2020, but not one justice wrote a word about her latest appeal, and none of them expressed interest in taking up her case.

“What does this tell us? Nothing we didn’t already know,” Stern wrote. “Thomas and Alito may still be gunning for Obergefell. But the other Republican appointees appear uninterested in killing it.”

“If this Supreme Court were considering the issue for the very first time today, it would almost certainly hold that the Constitution does not protect same-sex marriage by a 6–3 vote,” Stern added. “But now that Obergefell is entrenched as precedent, and widely supported by Americans, they’ve shown no appetite for spending down their political capital to issue an unpopular ruling that could only hurt the Republican Party.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Supreme Court’s Decision on Kim Davis: A Calculated Political Maneuver?

In a recent development that has sent ripples through the corridors of power and across the social fabric of the United States, the Supreme Court has once again found itself at the center of a heated debate surrounding LGBTQ rights. This time, the controversy orbits around Kim Davis, the infamous anti-LGBTQ county clerk who blatantly refused to issue a marriage license to a gay couple, citing her religious beliefs as a bulwark against the obligations of her governmental role. The court’s decision to deny Davis’ appeal, which sought to overturn the landmark Obergefell v. Hodges decision that legalized same-sex marriage nationwide, is a beacon of hope for LGBTQ rights. However, a deeper analysis suggests that this decision may be more about political strategy than a genuine commitment to justice or equality.

The Thin Veil of Justice

At first glance, the Supreme Court’s refusal to entertain Davis’ plea appears to be a straightforward victory for civil rights. However, Mark Joseph Stern of Slate proposes a cunning rationale behind the justices’ decision, suggesting it was a politically calculated move designed to protect the Republican Party’s image. According to Stern, the court’s ruling was less about upholding the principles of equality and more about avoiding the political fallout that would inevitably ensue from a decision favoring Davis.

Davis’ argument hinged on the First Amendment, claiming a right to deny a marriage license based on her religious liberty. Yet, as Stern astutely points out, “an agent of the state who’s performing a government service has no right to unlawfully discriminate against members of the public.” This argument seems self-evident, yet the fact that it had to be made at all underscores the absurdity of Davis’ claims.

The Right-Wing’s Tactic: Attention and Fundraising

What is particularly insidious about this case is the strategy employed by Davis’ lawyers at the right-wing Liberty Counsel. By tacking on a request to overturn the 2015 ruling on same-sex marriage, they aimed not at legal victory but at drawing attention — and, consequently, fundraising dollars. This tactic, as Stern highlights, capitalizes on media coverage and the controversy surrounding marriage equality, regardless of the legal merits of their case. It’s a cynical exploitation of the judicial system for political and financial gain, showcasing a blatant disregard for the impact such cases have on the lives of real people.

The Supreme Court’s Political Calculus

The silence from the justices, including Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito who previously signaled their willingness to revisit Obergefell, speaks volumes. It reveals a court that is acutely aware of the political landscape and its influence on judicial rulings. Stern’s assertion that the Supreme Court would likely oppose same-sex marriage if it were a fresh issue today, but refrains due to the precedent and widespread support for Obergefell, paints a picture of a court acting out of political expediency rather than a steadfast commitment to constitutional rights.

Conclusion: A Hollow Victory?

While the denial of Kim Davis’ appeal might seem like a triumph for LGBTQ rights, the underlying motivations suggest a more complex and somewhat disheartening reality. The Supreme Court’s decision, seemingly rooted in political strategy rather than an unwavering dedication to justice, highlights the precarious nature of civil rights in a politically charged judicial system.

In essence, this case is a stark reminder of the ongoing struggle for LGBTQ rights and the importance of vigilance in the face of attempts to undermine hard-won freedoms. The fight for equality, it seems, is far from over, and victories, even those that appear clear-cut, must be scrutinized for their broader implications in the ever-evolving battleground of American politics and justice.
