Former Trump associate Lev Parnas, who says he used to be "deep inside the Trump machine" and now reports from the outside, published an article on Saturday called, "Witkoff Exposed — Trump’s Envoy Revealed as Kremlin Pawn," in which he wrote that the president's "fixer plays theatrics for Putin, trading peace for Russia’s power and money."

Parnas is referring to real estate investor-turned-international negotiator Steve Witkoff, who has recently been referred to as a "rogue actor."

According to Parnas, the media is being played for a fool by the Trump admin.

The ex-insider wrote that, "The media is trying to portray Trump’s 'special envoy' Steve Witkoff as incompetent and botching meetings and confusing Moscow’s lies with reality."

"But you know who the real Steve Witkoff is, because for months I’ve been telling you — and now the world is beginning to see it too. My sources are telling me that after a meeting in New York between Andriy Yermak, Sergiy Kyslytsya, and Trump’s envoy Steve Witkoff, the Ukrainians are now more convinced than ever that Witkoff is nothing more than another useful idiot for the Kremlin," Parnas wrote. "Politico and others report how U.S., Ukrainian, and European officials are all frustrated with him. He refuses to consult experts. He shows up unprepared. He parrots back nonsense after sitting with Putin. One day he believes Russia will give up territory; the next he thinks they agreed to NATO-style security guarantees. It’s chaos — but don’t be fooled into thinking it’s just incompetence."

He continued, saying, "Let me make it very clear, Witkoff isn’t confused. He’s obsessed."

"Like Trump, he came up in the real estate world of the 1990s, making money with the same Russian oligarchs who answer directly to the Kremlin. They were both swimming in Moscow’s dirty money long before Ukraine was ever on the headlines," Parnas added. "That’s why I tell you: the real deals aren’t happening in public. They’re not in the leaks you see. They’re not in Witkoff’s babbling reports. The real manipulations are happening behind closed doors with his handler, Kirill Dmitriev, Putin’s fixer."

Parnas also said, "Don’t listen when the media tells you Witkoff is 'confused.' That’s the cover story. This isn’t confusion. It’s a deliberate stalling tactic. A distraction. A way to buy time while Russia pounds Ukraine with drones and missiles."

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Incompetence: A Strategic Farce

In the convoluted theatre of international politics, where the lines between ally and adversary blur, the recent revelations by Lev Parnas about Steve Witkoff, a figure within the Trump administration, unveil not just a narrative of incompetence, but a calculated orchestration of chaos. Parnas, a self-proclaimed former insider of the "Trump machine," has thrown a wrench into the works with his article, "Witkoff Exposed — Trump’s Envoy Revealed as Kremlin Pawn," suggesting that what the media decries as ineptitude is, in fact, a meticulously planned facade.

The Media's Misdirection

The narrative spun by the media, as Parnas points out, paints Witkoff as a "rogue actor," a bumbling envoy who mixes up Moscow's fabrications with reality. This portrayal, however, is dismissed by Parnas as a gross misinterpretation or, worse, a deliberate obfuscation of the truth. According to Parnas, the media is being "played for a fool" by the Trump administration. The real Witkoff, as he suggests, is far from the incompetent figure the media depicts; he is a calculated player in a much larger game.

Witkoff: A Pawn or a Player?

The crux of Parnas's argument revolves around Witkoff's alleged role as a "useful idiot for the Kremlin," a term dredged from the Cold War era, suggesting someone who, wittingly or unwittingly, serves foreign interests. The meetings between Witkoff and various Ukrainian and Russian officials, as described by Parnas, do not showcase a man out of his depth but rather one who is deeply entrenched in a web of influence and manipulation.

Parnas's assertion that Witkoff is "obsessed" rather than confused is particularly telling. He draws a parallel between Witkoff and Trump, noting their shared background in the real estate sector of the 1990s, a period and field notorious for its murky dealings with Russian oligarchs. This historical context is crucial; it suggests that Witkoff's actions are not those of an outsider fumbling in the dark but of an insider with decades of experience navigating the shadowy corridors of power.

The Real Manipulations

Perhaps the most damning accusation Parnas levels against Witkoff is the allegation of behind-the-scenes manipulations with Kirill Dmitriev, a figure described as Putin's fixer. The implication here is clear: the apparent disarray and incompetence displayed by Witkoff are nothing but a smokescreen, a "deliberate stalling tactic" designed to distract from the true machinations occurring out of sight. This tactic, according to Parnas, serves to buy time while Russia continues its aggressive military actions against Ukraine.

Conclusion: The Facade of Incompetence

Parnas's insights into the workings of the Trump administration and its dealings with Russia present a chilling picture of deliberate deception. The portrayal of Steve Witkoff as an incompetent envoy is debunked as a carefully crafted illusion, behind which lies a network of powerful individuals with deep ties to Russia. The real story, Parnas argues, is not one of confusion but of collusion, not of incompetence but of intricate strategy.

In the end, the call to action is clear: one must look beyond the surface-level narratives peddled by the media to understand the full scope of geopolitical strategies at play. The tale of Witkoff is not just a story of individual failure but a cautionary tale of how public perception can be manipulated to mask the true extent of power and influence wielded by a select few.
