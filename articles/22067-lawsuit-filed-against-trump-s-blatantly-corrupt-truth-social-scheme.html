Lawsuit filed against Trump's 'blatantly corrupt' Truth Social scheme

Based on an article by Brad Reed, Common Dreams

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

The President Put a Paywall on Public Power

A short summary

A lawsuit filed by the Freedom of the Press Foundation and The Intercept says Truth API lets Wall Street firms pay up to $100,000 a month for early access to potentially market-moving posts from President Donald Trump. The plaintiffs argue the setup violates the First and Fifth Amendments and gives insiders a paid advantage over everyone else, including news outlets that cannot and will not subscribe.

Power Is the Point

This is not a story about “access.” It is a story about who gets to monetize the presidency.

The institutional power sits with Trump, his personal company, and the system he controls. He is the one with the megaphone, the account, and the authority to decide when a public announcement becomes a paid product. Everyone else is downstream of that decision. Wall Street gets early notice. The public gets whatever is left after the fee structure has done its work.

The Scheme Is the Message

Truth API is not being framed here as a bribe, but it functions like one: pay the president’s company, get ahead of the crowd. That is the corruption structure in plain view.

The complaint calls the setup “profoundly corrupt,” and that is not rhetorical excess. The alleged problem is not simply that some firms have better information than others. It is that the president stands to gain financially by selling privileged timing on information that comes from his office. That turns public communication into a private revenue stream.

Misdirection for the Useful Idiots

The weaker actors in this story are not the plaintiffs, the reporters, or the public. The weaker actors are everyone being asked to pretend this is just a tech platform, a media product, or an ordinary subscription service.

That framing is political cover. The issue is not whether Truth Social is innovative. The issue is that a president is using a personal company to charge for early access to his own public statements, including posts that can move markets. Calling that “insider information” is not exaggeration; it is the correct category for the damage being invited here.

The Press Is Being Made to Wait

The Intercept’s standing argument matters because it exposes the competitive and democratic injury at the same time. If the president can charge for advance access, then public information becomes a luxury good and journalism becomes a second-class consumer.

That is especially ugly in this case because, as the plaintiffs note, Trump uses the same account to berate journalists, threaten lawsuits, and talk openly about criminal investigations. He attacks the press on the public stage, then makes the press pay to see the stage directions early. That is not accidental inconsistency. It is power using price as discipline.

The Pattern Is Bigger Than One App

This is a familiar authoritarian pattern dressed up in business language: privatize the state, punish dissent, reward insiders, and call it normal commerce.

Rep. Jamie Raskin’s probe rightly treats the scheme as an insider-information operation, because that is what it resembles. But the larger lesson is broader than one product or one account. When a president can turn public announcements into a paid advantage for the wealthy, the office stops functioning as a public trust and starts functioning as a revenue machine for aligned elites.

The democratic answer is not gentle reform theater. It is to name the corruption, strip away the euphemisms, and treat this as what it is: the monetization of public power for private gain.
