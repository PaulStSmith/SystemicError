MAGA media war erupts as far-right outlet deems Fox News ‘anti-Trump’

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosNovember 13, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedLoyalty replaces analysisThe scapegoat comes from the topFox built the trapA market for obedience

What happened

AlterNet reports that Newsmax attacked Fox News on Thursday as “anti-Trump,” arguing that Fox’s declining audience reflects its supposed betrayal of Donald Trump. The cited offense was not an editorial campaign against him, but Fox airing reports about his historically low approval rating and including anti-Trump commentators on panels.

Fox’s election-night audience reportedly fell 40 percent from 7.4 million viewers in 2022 to 4.3 million, while its October viewership declined 27 percent year over year. Yet Newsmax’s own daily audience was reportedly down 25 percent this year, averaging 127,000 viewers. The evidence presented therefore does not establish Newsmax’s claim that insufficient loyalty to Trump caused Fox’s decline.

Loyalty replaces analysis

Newsmax is not offering a serious diagnosis of the conservative media market. It is enforcing a political loyalty test. Under that test, reporting unfavorable polling becomes “Trump bashing,” inviting dissenting commentators becomes ideological contamination, and acknowledging inconvenient facts becomes betrayal.

That standard makes journalism subordinate to one politician’s interests. The institutional power here belongs to Trump and the media executives, hosts and operatives who determine which facts their audiences may hear without ideological packaging. Viewers are being trained to treat adverse information not as information, but as evidence that the messenger has defected.

The scapegoat comes from the top

AlterNet traces the campaign against Fox to Trump, who blamed the network’s coverage as poor polling preceded damaging midterm results for his party. His operatives then amplified the accusation across aligned media.

That sequence matters. The story is not principally about audiences spontaneously rebelling against Fox. It is about a powerful political figure supplying a scapegoat and allied outlets competing to validate it. The accusation protects the leader from accountability by recasting political weakness as media treachery.

Newsmax has an obvious competitive interest in that framing. Calling Fox insufficiently loyal offers it a way to solicit Fox’s audience without confronting the inconvenient fact that its own ultra-MAGA programming has not prevented a comparable ratings decline.

Fox built the trap

Fox is hardly an innocent institution ambushed by extremists. AlterNet notes that the network promoted Trump’s voter-fraud claims after the 2020 election, conduct that culminated in nearly $1 billion in settlements and the departure of prominent talent including Tucker Carlson. Yet even that record does not purchase permanent protection from accusations of disloyalty.

The episode exposes the bargain Fox helped normalize: audience retention through partisan affirmation, even when affirmation collides with reality. Once that becomes the product, ordinary reporting feels like a breach of contract. Fox’s problem is not merely that Newsmax is attacking it. Fox helped create a political audience for whom any unwelcome fact can be denounced as sabotage.

A market for obedience

The ratings numbers undercut the central sales pitch. Fox lost viewers while Newsmax, despite its more rigid MAGA posture, also lost viewers at nearly the same rate. That does not prove why either audience shrank, but it does make Newsmax’s single-cause explanation look like propaganda dressed as market analysis.

The larger pattern is a familiar authoritarian one: failure is never allowed to belong to the leader. It must be assigned to disloyal institutions, internal enemies or insufficiently obedient messengers. Rival media companies then compete not over accuracy, but over who can demonstrate the purest submission.

What appears to be a cable-news feud is therefore a struggle over who gets to police the boundaries of acceptable reality. Fox retains far more reach; Trump retains the political authority to define betrayal; Newsmax seeks advantage by enforcing his definition. The audience decline is the pretext. Control over what counts as permissible truth is the prize.
