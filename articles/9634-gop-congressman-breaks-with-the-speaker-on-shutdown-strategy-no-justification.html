Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-CA) gave an interview with CNN’s Manu Raju, who asked the lawmaker, “What do you say to Speaker Johnson, who says there is no reason for the House to come back into session?”

Kiley said there are “a lot of reasons” for the House to come back into session.

“We were supposed to be in session the last two weeks,” he continued, noting that the House still has everyday business to take care of despite the shutdown.

In reality, he added, the fact that there’s a federal shutdown is “all the more reason” to be there, so that they can resolve it.

Raju promoted the interview on social media, writing, “GOP Rep. Kevin Kiley breaks with the speaker’s strategy of keeping House closed and not negotiating with Ds.”

“And says Grijalva should be sworn in,” Raju added, quoting Kiley as saying, “There’s a duly elected member of Congress from that district, and she should be sworn in. I don’t know why this is an issue.”

Our Analysis:

A Stark Break from Party Lines: Rep. Kevin Kiley’s Bold Stance

In a political climate where party loyalty often trumps common sense, Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-CA) has taken a remarkably bold stance. During a CNN interview with Manu Raju, Kiley called out his own party’s leadership, specifically Speaker Johnson, for their decision to keep the House out of session during a critical time. Let’s dissect the implications of Kiley’s statements and the broader context they illuminate.

The Call for Action

Kiley’s assertion that “there are a lot of reasons for the House to come back into session” is a blatant indictment of the current Republican strategy—or lack thereof. The fact that the House was supposed to be in session for the past two weeks but wasn’t due to a federal shutdown underscores a disturbing abdication of duty. Kiley rightly points out that, if anything, the shutdown is a compelling reason for the House to reconvene, not an excuse to avoid it.

Breaking with the Speaker

It’s not every day that you see a Republican congressman publicly break ranks with the Speaker of the House. This move by Kiley is nothing short of political heresy in today’s hyper-partisan environment. Yet, it’s precisely this kind of courage that is desperately needed if there’s any hope of breaking the deadlock plaguing American politics. By calling for negotiations with Democrats (“not negotiating with Ds”), Kiley is advocating for a return to bipartisanship and practical governance over party dogma.

The Swearing-In Controversy

Kiley’s comments on the issue of Grijalva not being sworn in are particularly noteworthy. It’s a stark reminder of how political gamesmanship can impede the functioning of the government. By stating, “There’s a duly elected member of Congress from that district, and she should be sworn in. I don’t know why this is an issue,” Kiley is highlighting a fundamental principle: the will of the voters. In refusing to swear in a duly elected member, the Republican leadership is not just undermining democratic norms but is actively disenfranchising the constituents of that district.

Conclusion: A Call for Reason in an Unreasonable Time

Rep. Kevin Kiley’s interview is a rare glimmer of reason in an increasingly unreasonable political landscape. His willingness to critique his own party’s strategy—and the Speaker himself—demonstrates a commitment to governance over gamesmanship. While his stance may not be popular within his party, it’s a necessary voice in the conversation about how to move forward amid a federal shutdown and broader legislative paralysis.

In a time when political courage is in short supply, Kiley’s interview should not just be seen as a break from party lines but as a call to action for all lawmakers. The message is clear: it’s time to put the country’s needs above party loyalty and get back to work. This isn’t just about resolving a shutdown; it’s about restoring faith in the democratic process and the institutions that are supposed to serve the public good.
