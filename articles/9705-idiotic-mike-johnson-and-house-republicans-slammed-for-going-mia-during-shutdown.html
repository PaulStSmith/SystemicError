'Idiotic!' Mike Johnson and House Republicans slammed for going MIA during shutdown

U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) speaks during a press conference on the seventh day of a partial shutdown of the federal government at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., U.S. October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Punchbowl News co-founder Jake Sherman was animated, speaking to MSNBC on Thursday about Republican members of the U.S. House being missing in action during the government shutdown.

Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has argued that there’s no reason for the House to be on hand to negotiate on healthcare subsidies because the House already passed its portion of the measure that would continue to fund the government.

Johnson got a call from a Republican mom in Virginia who wagged a verbal finger at the speaker on C-SPAN, saying he has the power to bring back the House and end the shutdown.

“My kids could die!” she told him, explaining that her credit was terrible because of the medical bills she faces.

Johnson rambled about soldiers in his district, promised he cared, and said “Republicans are the ones who are delivering for you.”

But Sherman explained that the issue is that “Republicans do not want to extend these Obamacare premium tax credits at all, period.” So, the lawmakers returning to Washington to negotiate may not be effective because they’re the ones refusing to reinstate the subsidies that weren’t extended in the “Big Beautiful Bill.”

Democrats “have effectively made a demand that most Republicans don’t want.”

MSNBC host Katy Tur mentioned the “optics of keeping the House out.”

“Which is idiotic! It’s absolutely idiotic!” exclaimed Sherman. “Like I cannot — and I’m sorry for interrupting you because Mike Johnson’s mad, but I am mad, too, Katie. I mean, it is — it’s crazy that in the middle of a now nine-day shutdown, the House is out of session. I mean, it’s their job to be here. It’s their job to legislate!”

He believes the shutdown might drag on longer because House members face no pressure since they’re not in Washington.

Sherman speculated about the tipping point for Republicans on the Affordable Care Act subsidies, as many Americans will see premiums increase significantly.

“But the health care issue has been kryptonite for Republicans in the past,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Absurd Theatre of the Government Shutdown: A Critique

In the latest episode of governmental dysfunction, we’re treated to a spectacle that underlines the farcical nature of current American politics. The drama, as detailed by Punchbowl News co-founder Jake Sherman on MSNBC, involves Republican members of the U.S. House and their bewildering response (or lack thereof) to the ongoing government shutdown. Let’s dissect this theater of the absurd, where the stakes are incredibly real for many Americans.

Absence in the Face of Crisis

At the heart of this debacle is the astonishing absence of Republican House members during a critical time. Speaker Mike Johnson’s (R-LA) argument that there’s no need for the House to be present because they’ve “done their part” is a glaring testament to the detachment and abdication of responsibility plaguing our political system. The fact that a concerned mother had to remind Johnson of his power and responsibility on a public platform like C-SPAN, citing the dire personal consequences she faces, underscores the tragic reality behind this political theater.

The Unyielding Stance on Healthcare

Sherman’s insight that Republicans are fundamentally opposed to extending Obamacare premium tax credits reveals the crux of the issue. This isn’t just a disagreement on policy; it’s a wilful disregard for the well-being of millions. The term “Big Beautiful Bill” reeks of political branding over substance, a bill that appears to have forgotten the ‘health’ in ‘healthcare.’

The Optics of Inaction

The critique of keeping the House out of session during such a crisis as “idiotic” might be an understatement. It’s a catastrophic failure of governance. The optics—a term so often used to sanitize political negligence—here are indeed horrendous. But beyond optics, it’s a failure to perform the very essence of their jobs: to legislate, especially in times of crisis. Sherman’s frustration is more than justified; it’s a reflection of the exasperation felt by many Americans watching their leaders choose inaction.

The Kryptonite of Healthcare

Sherman’s speculation about the potential tipping point regarding the Affordable Care Act subsidies is poignant. Healthcare has indeed been a stumbling block for Republicans, not because the issue is inherently partisan, but because effective healthcare policy requires acknowledging and addressing complex human needs—something the current Republican stance seems to balk at.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This situation is more than just a shutdown. It’s a glaring spotlight on the systemic failures of our political system, where partisan politics continually trumps the welfare of the citizenry. The absence of Republican members during this crisis isn’t just a failure to show up; it’s a failure to recognize the gravity of their roles and the impact of their decisions (or indecisions).

The frustration voiced by Sherman isn’t just professional outrage; it’s a mirror to the collective anger and desperation of many Americans. It’s high time our political actors start treating these issues with the seriousness they deserve. The government shutdown, and the reasons behind it, should not be treated as just another political impasse but as a failure to fulfill the most basic duties of governance. The time for accountability is now, lest the theater of the absurd becomes the accepted norm in American politics.
