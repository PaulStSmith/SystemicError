‘Make it make sense!’ GOP strategist shut down after downplaying Trump tariff blowback

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 5, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Screengrab / CNN

GOP strategist Scott Jennings was promptly shut down Saturday during an appearance on CNN after attempting to downplay the blowback from President Donald Trump’s trade policy and its impact on American farmers.

Trump has imposed tariffs on hundreds of nations, perhaps most notably China, which was slapped with a 125% tariff rate in April before being reduced as part of a temporary tariff truce, a truce that is currently ongoing. However, China has responded in kind by boycotting American soybeans, which, given China is the largest importer of the crop, has left American soybean farmers enraged.

“China’s massive population is really not a market that we can have completely turned off, it’s like 25% of the entire market!” said CNN’s Abby Phillip, the host of the network’s “Table for Five” weekly news discussion show.

Trump, after “basically admitting” to a major policy blunder, has floated bailing out farmers with billions of dollars. Responding to Phillip, Jennings pushed back on the severity of the situation by stressing the fact that negotiations were still ongoing.

“So then why are we bailing out farmers to the tune of [$10] billion?” Phillip asked Jennings.

“Because it’s apparent China has played hardball with some sectors of our agriculture economy,” Jennings responded.

“While we work this out, what you hope is that the president and the Chinese government can come together on a trade agreement that encompasses everything… [China] is a market for a lot of things, and in the short run, there’s no doubt some farmers have been hurt.”

Jennings’ admission that some farmers had “been hurt” by Trump’s trade policy is consistent with the admission of other GOP figures, including Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA), who on Friday admitted that Trump’s tariffs had caused great harm on American soybean farmers.

In an apparent effort to downplay the damage caused by Trump’s tariffs, however, Jennings then proceeded to highlight what he argued were the benefits of Trump’s trade policy.

“Now in other trade deals, other farmers are ecstatic, the beef guys are really happy with what he’s doing in Europe,” Jennings said. “But on this China deal, what you hope is, when the final trade deal comes in -- which should be soon -- that you’ll get a deal for our [products].”

Jennings was cut short, however, by Phillip, who argued the United States was in a poor negotiating position, something she laid the blame for directly at the feet of the Trump administration.

“What kind of negotiating position are we in when China knows that they’ve put us up against the wall, that our farmers are begging for a bailout, and then we are alsobailing out Argentina, which is now competing with us!” Phillip said. “We’re bailing out the people competing with our farmers. Make it make sense!”

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Deal? More Like the Art of the Steal

In a recent appearance on CNN, GOP strategist Scott Jennings attempted a high-wire act, balancing between defending President Donald Trump’s trade policies and acknowledging their detrimental impact on American farmers. His performance, however, was less than convincing and served as a stark reminder of the ongoing chaos inflicted by Trump’s trade war, particularly with China.

Trump’s Trade Turmoil

Let’s get the facts straight: Trump has indeed imposed tariffs on “hundreds of nations,” a hyperbolic move that sounds more like a shotgun approach to international trade policy than a surgical strike. The most notable victim? China, which faced a staggering 125% tariff rate in April before a temporary truce was called. This maneuver has triggered a tit-for-tat retaliation, with China boycotting American soybeans. Given that China is the largest importer of this crop, the implications for American soybean farmers have been nothing short of catastrophic.

CNN’s Abby Phillip hits the nail on the head, emphasizing the critical market that China represents. Trump’s admission to a major policy misstep and his subsequent proposal to bail out farmers to the tune of $10 billion reeks of desperation and a tacit acknowledgment of failure.

Jennings’ Juggling Act

Scott Jennings’ attempt to downplay the severe backlash from these policies was as transparent as it was futile. His insistence that negotiations were still ongoing and that some sectors might benefit in the long run does little to mitigate the immediate harm inflicted upon soybean farmers. The acknowledgment that “some farmers have been hurt” is a gross understatement, reflecting a broader trend within the GOP to minimize the fallout from Trump’s erratic trade strategies.

While Jennings points to the beef industry’s happiness with European trade developments as a silver lining, this is a classic deflection. The issue at hand is the China deal, or the lack thereof, which has left farmers in a lurch. His optimism for a beneficial trade deal “soon” comes across as wishful thinking, lacking substance and ignoring the current reality of those affected.

Negotiating From a Position of Weakness

Abby Phillip’s counter to Jennings is a knockout punch, spotlighting the absurdity of the United States’ negotiating position. The image of American farmers “begging for a bailout” while simultaneously bailing out competitors like Argentina is ludicrous. It’s a scenario that underscores the Trump administration’s haphazard approach to trade policy, one that not only undermines the US’s position on the global stage but actively harms its citizens.

Conclusion: A Policy in Shambles

The attempt by Jennings and others to put a positive spin on Trump’s trade policy debacle is a testament to the intellectual dishonesty permeating the current political discourse. The reality is stark: Trump’s tariffs have done considerable damage to American agriculture, a fact that no amount of GOP spin can obscure.

The notion that this trade war, marked by retaliatory tariffs and economic uncertainty, could somehow end in a net positive for all involved is fanciful at best. As the situation stands, American farmers are paying a steep price for Trump’s so-called “art of the deal.” It appears, however, that the only artistry on display is the art of self-sabotage, leaving the American people to pick up the tab.
