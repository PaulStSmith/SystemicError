'He said the quiet part out loud': Dem lawmaker says Trump is now attacking his own voters

President Donald Trump just “said the quiet part out loud” when it comes to attacks on his own supporters, a Democratic lawmaker said over the weekend.

US House Budget Committee Ranking Member Brendan Boyle (D-Pa.) appeared on MSNBC on Saturday, where he was asked about Trump’s comments claiming that SNAP benefits expiring will mostly affect Democrats.

“They’re hurting their own people,” Trump claimed without citing evidence.

“Well, you know, Donald Trump revealed in that answer, he tends to say the quiet parts out loud, by the way. And this was another instance of that,” Boyle said. “Donald Trump and other Republicans in congress genuinely believe that most poor people in this country live in urban areas, aren’t white and vote democratic, when in reality two thirds of those states that are most dependent on SNAP are red states.”

He added, “There are states that happen to have large rural areas, rural counties, by the way, that tended to vote by 2 to 1 margin for Donald Trump.”

“So, you know, their malice toward poor people in this country is awful, but it’s also ill informed because they’re hurting a lot of poor folks who are both Democrats, Republicans as well as independents and nonvoters,” Boyle said. “We have to stop looking at them based on partisanship and recognize these are our fellow Americans who are hurting, and they are being hurt by Donald Trump and the Republican majority.”

Our Analysis:

Unveiling the Irony: Trump’s SNAP Comments

In a recent display of political theater that could only be described as astonishingly candid, former President Donald Trump managed to articulate a sentiment that many have speculated but few have heard so bluntly from his lips. During a conversation that peeled back the layers on his views towards the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) benefits, Trump insinuated that the expiration of these benefits would primarily harm Democrats—a statement as revealing as it is divisive.

The Unvarnished Truth?

US House Budget Committee Ranking Member Brendan Boyle (D-Pa.) seized on Trump’s comments during an appearance on MSNBC, suggesting that Trump, in his trademark fashion, “said the quiet part out loud.” Trump’s assertion—that the cessation of SNAP benefits is a strategic blow to Democrats—unwittingly unmasks a profound misunderstanding of the demographic reality of SNAP beneficiaries.

Let’s be clear: the assumption that SNAP benefits predominantly serve urban, non-white, Democratic voters is not just misguided; it’s factually incorrect. Boyle points out that two-thirds of the states most dependent on SNAP are, in fact, red states. This revelation disrupts the narrative peddled by Trump and his allies, laying bare the stark irony of their position.

Rural America: The Silent Casualty

The demographic reality is that many SNAP beneficiaries reside in rural counties that overwhelmingly supported Trump. These areas, often characterized by limited economic opportunities and higher rates of poverty, are precisely where SNAP benefits play a crucial role in mitigating food insecurity. By undermining SNAP, Trump and like-minded Republicans inadvertently inflict harm on the very constituency they claim to represent.

The rhetoric vs. reality gap here is not just wide; it’s a chasm. Boyle’s critique underscores a critical point: the attack on SNAP is not just an expression of malice toward the poor but also a display of ignorance regarding who the poor actually are in America. It’s a blunder of epic proportions, showcasing a blend of cynicism and misapprehension that should concern us all.

Beyond Partisanship: A Call for Empathy

In his comments, Boyle urges a departure from partisan lenses, advocating for a recognition of SNAP beneficiaries as fellow Americans in need. This plea for empathy and understanding is more than a moral argument; it’s a pragmatic one. In a time of increasing polarization, the well-being of millions of Americans should transcend political affiliations.

Donald Trump and the Republican majority’s stance on SNAP—as articulated through Trump’s own words—serves as a stark reminder of the profound disconnect between political rhetoric and the lived realities of Americans. It’s a reminder that, in the realm of politics, the most vulnerable often bear the brunt of ideological battles.

In sum, Trump’s comments and the ensuing critique from Boyle offer a window into the complexities and contradictions of American political discourse. As we navigate these turbulent waters, let’s not lose sight of the real stakes: the health, well-being, and dignity of countless Americans who rely on programs like SNAP to survive. Ignorance, especially when wielded by those in power, is not just dangerous—it’s destructive.
