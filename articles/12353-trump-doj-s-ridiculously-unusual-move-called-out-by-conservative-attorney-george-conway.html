Trump DOJ's 'ridiculously unusual' move called out by conservative attorney George Conway

Conservative attorney George Conway joined CNN on Friday afternoon to express astonishment at the latest "lie" from President Donald Trump.

Conway, a frequent critic of Trump, joined "The Lead" with CNN anchor Jake Tapper to discuss Trump's press conference in which the president insisted the Justice Department never briefed him that his name was included multiple times in its sealed Jeffrey Epstein files.

To state the obvious, the Justice Department, the Trump administration, could release thousands of Epstein files right now if they wanted to," noted Tapper. "They don’t have to go get a judge’s permission for grand jury testimony, which probably wouldn’t have anything in it anyway."

Tapper noted there could be hearsay and outright false information in the thousands of pages.

"And they don’t [release the files," replied Conway. "And it’s kind of odd. And it’s also very odd that they’re going to — that they’re lying about whether or not they told Donald Trump about it. Or that he’s lying about that. I mean, can you imagine being the attorney general or the deputy Attorney general and not telling Trump he was in those files?"

"Of course they told him!" shouted Tapper. "It's their job!"

Conway echoed Tapper as he threw up his hands. "Of course they told him!"

Conway noted it's only the DOJ's "job" because "these are his personal criminal defense lawyers: Emil Bove and Todd Blanche."

"He put them in there precisely to protect himself. And, you know, as you pointed out, it’s ridiculously unusual for the deputy attorney general to go out and interview a witness like this."

Tapper noted that typically, a line prosecutor or someone who knows the case would be sent to interview Maxwell.

"Right. Like the woman they fired," Conway retorted, referring to Maurene Comey, a top Epstein prosecutor in the Manhattan district attorney's office.

"Oh, right," replied Tapper.

"Forgot about that!" Conway quipped.

The lawyer later warned the Trump administration that cutting a deal with Maxwell would "stink to high heaven."

"She is a sexual predator! She was found guilty of doing these things! She was found guilty of, you know, she would take the passports away from these girls who they dragged to Epstein Island. She did all of this stuff. She’s neck-deep. Way in. And so the notion that they would give her clemency is just insane."

He added: "What could she possibly say that would be believable? And, you know, the Justice Department trashed her credibility back when they prosecuted her. They said that she couldn’t be trusted under oath."

"It’s crazy," said Tapper.

Conway and Tapper later tore into MAGA attempts to move on from the Epstein debacle, with MAGA commentators like Dinesh D’Souza and Charlie Kirk dumping the scandal, and it barely getting mentioned on Fox News and other outlets.

"It really just goes to the complete lack of shame about people who want to defend Donald Trump no matter what," said Conway. "And this is something where, I mean, the fire is coming — the call is coming from inside the house. This man, Donald Trump, has revealed himself not to want these files to see the light of day. Now, he’s not a generous person. He’s not a person who’s empathetic and wants to help other people for the sake of helping other people. The only reason why he wants to do this is to help himself. He knows what’s in those files, and he obviously has some concern about it.

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Obfuscation: Trump, Epstein, and the DOJ's Dance

In a recent appearance on CNN, conservative attorney George Conway and anchor Jake Tapper dissected yet another episode in the ongoing saga of President Donald Trump's entanglement with the Justice Department (DOJ) over the sealed Jeffrey Epstein files. The discussion illuminated not just another "lie" from Trump, but a deeper, systemic problem within his administration's handling of sensitive information.

The Alleged Lie

Trump's insistence during a press conference that the DOJ never briefed him on his name appearing in the Epstein files smacks of a desperate attempt to distance himself from the late financier's notorious circle. This claim, as pointed out by Tapper, is easily debunkable. The DOJ, under Trump's administration, could release the Epstein files sans any need for judicial permission for grand jury testimony, which, as Tapper notes, "probably wouldn’t have anything in it anyway."

A Pattern of Deception

It's peculiar, if not outright suspicious, how the administration chooses to keep these files under wraps. Conway's incredulity at Trump's statement is warranted. It's almost farcical to suggest that high-ranking DOJ officials, transformed into Trump's "personal criminal defense lawyers," would neglect to inform him of his inclusion in documents deeply tied to a convicted sex offender. This scenario begs the question: what is the true extent of Trump's involvement or knowledge regarding Epstein's criminal activities?

The DOJ's Compromised Integrity

The discussion also sheds light on the compromised integrity of the DOJ under Trump's presidency. The firing of Maurene Comey, a top Epstein prosecutor, and the unusual step of the deputy attorney general interviewing a witness directly, are not just irregularities but glaring red flags. These actions suggest an institution bending over backward to protect Trump, undermining its own credibility and independence in the process.

Maxwell's Deal: A Stench in the Air

Conway's comments on the potential deal with Ghislaine Maxwell, Epstein's associate and a convicted sexual predator, are particularly damning. The notion of offering clemency to someone so deeply implicated in heinous crimes for potential information speaks volumes about the administration's desperation to control the narrative. This move would not only be morally reprehensible but would also further erode public trust in the judicial system.

MAGA's Selective Amnesia

The latter part of the discussion focuses on the MAGA movement's efforts to brush the Epstein debacle under the rug. Figures like Dinesh D’Souza and Charlie Kirk, and outlets such as Fox News, demonstrate a troubling lack of integrity in their reportage. It's a clear indication of a broader issue within Trump's base—a willingness to overlook moral bankruptcy and criminal entanglement in favor of political loyalty.

Conclusion: The Uncomfortable Truth

What emerges from Conway and Tapper's conversation is a picture of a presidency embroiled in deception, where the lines between personal interest and public duty are not just blurred but obliterated. Trump's reluctance to release the Epstein files is not an act of legal caution but a self-serving maneuver to obscure the truth. The real casualty here is not just the truth but the very pillars of justice and accountability that underpin American democracy. In their complicit silence or active obstruction, Trump and his enablers have not just failed the American people but have also insulted the very idea of justice.
