The View’s Whoopi Goldberg sparked outrage on Newsmax Sunday after accusing President Donald Trump of launching war against Iran partly to distract from his administration’s botched handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

“The liberal media doing what they do best: spin the narrative!” noted one Newsmax host.

Goldberg’s comments were made last week on ABC’s The View, where she wondered out loud why discussions around both Epstein and the disappearance of Nancy Guthrie, the 84-year-old mother of “Today” co-host Savannah Guthrie, had chilled since the Trump administration launched its attack on Iran.

“Why haven’t we been talking about Savannah Guthrie and what’s going on there, why have we not been talking about the Epstein files?” Goldberg said, her question sparking applause. “This is meant to get us so worked up that we are unable to see anything else.”

Goldberg’s suggestion – that Trump launched strikes on Iran to distract Americans from the Epstein scandal plaguing his administration – is supported by a majority of Americans, who in a recent poll said by a margin of 52-40 that they believe Trump was “at least partly motivated to take military action against Iran in order to distract from the Jeffrey Epstein scandal,” Drop Site News reported.

Nevertheless, another Newsmax anchor characterized the suggestion as a conspiracy theory before asking Curtis Houck, conservative news analyst and guest on the network, for their take on Goldberg’s comments.

“There’s no new information about Nancy Guthrie, unfortunately, so that doesn’t make any sense,” Houck said. “Of course, Epstein is the other example that’s going on out there.”

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind Goldberg’s Accusations

In a recent episode that has sparked considerable controversy, Whoopi Goldberg of ABC’s The View put forth a theory that President Donald Trump initiated military actions against Iran as a diversion from the scandal surrounding Jeffrey Epstein’s files. This theory, as reported by Newsmax, has ignited a firestorm of reactions, ranging from outright dismissal to agreement, encapsulated in a poll indicating a majority of Americans share Goldberg’s suspicion. However, to dissect the veracity and implications of this accusation, one must meticulously analyze the core components of power, decision-making, and narrative framing surrounding these events.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

At the heart of Goldberg’s theory is the assertion that President Trump, wielding the institutional power of the presidency, opted for military action against Iran. The president indeed holds the executive power to direct military operations, making him the primary actor capable of initiating such actions. This places Trump at the center of the decision-making process, with the capability to influence public attention through significant geopolitical moves.

The Framing of the Narrative

The framing of Goldberg’s assertion by Newsmax and its commentators reveals a stark division in interpretation. On one side, the suggestion is dismissed as a “conspiracy theory,” while on the other, it’s supported by a significant portion of the public, as indicated by the Drop Site News poll. The critical question is not just about the plausibility of Goldberg’s claim but also about whether the narrative misdirects responsibility toward actors with limited control over the Epstein case or Iran’s geopolitical situation.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

Goldberg’s focus on Trump’s potential motivation to distract from the Epstein scandal suggests a direct line of responsibility from Trump’s administration to the handling of Epstein’s files. However, the complexity of the Epstein case, involving numerous legal and institutional actors beyond the executive branch, dilutes the president’s direct control over the narrative surrounding Epstein. Moreover, geopolitical decisions like military strikes are influenced by a myriad of factors beyond domestic scandals, including national security concerns and foreign policy objectives.

Conclusion: A Closer Look at the Criticism

While it is within the realm of possibility that political leaders may use significant events to shift public focus, the assertion that the strike on Iran was primarily motivated by a desire to distract from domestic scandals oversimplifies the intricate web of decision-making in both foreign policy and legal matters. This critique does not entirely absolve the administration from criticism regarding its handling of the Epstein files or its approach to Iran, but it does call for a more nuanced understanding of the motives and mechanisms of power.

The criticism of Trump in this context might be better directed at the overall transparency and accountability of the administration, rather than positing a direct causality between the Iran strikes and the Epstein scandal without substantial evidence. In the pursuit of holding power to account, it is crucial to distinguish between speculative narratives and the substantiated allocation of responsibility. Misdirecting criticism not only undermines legitimate critiques but also obscures the multifaceted nature of governance and geopolitical strategies.
