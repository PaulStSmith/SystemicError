'Big Kahuna': CNN’s Brianna Keilar schools Republican who claims inflation isn't rising

In a poll released by CNN in late April, 56 percent of respondents isn't doing enough to check President Donald Trump's power.

CNN host Brianna Keilar discussed the poll with Rep. Dan Meuser (R-Pennsylvania) during a Tuesday afternoon, April 29 broadcast. And it didn't take long for them to get into a debate over the results of Trump's tariffs.

Meuser told Keilar, "Well, I'm in Congress, and we have a separation of powers. And we're all equal portions of our government. And we are assuring that the president's actions are within the Constitution. The Supreme Court does the same. We are looking to codify many of the executive orders that have been done, and there's been many, as you brought out, since January 20…. Sometimes, being a disrupter causes some short-term fallbacks."

READ MORE: America is first in the global race to the bottom — thanks to Trump

When Keilar brought the possibility of Trump's tariffs causing higher prices on Amazon, Meuser responded, "Free trade, for the last 35 years, has really meant unfair trade…. We needed this correction. We needed it desperately."

But when Meuser argued that the U.S. isn't seeing any real inflation yet, Keilar noted that some restaurants are already "putting inflation charges on their bills."

Keilar also pointed out that Trump grew defensive over Amazon's Jeff Bezos addressing price increases, asking Meuser "what's the matter with" showing "transparency in pricing."

Meuser told Keilar, "Look, China's not our enemy, as I put it. They are an adversary. They are a competitor. But we need a reckoning with them. They've taken great advantage of us. We are their number one customer, and by far. And they need to appreciate that their economy is very dependent upon having a good relationship with us. And President Trump is aware of that…. Brianna, we have not experienced any inflation as of yet."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Rhetoric: Trump, Tariffs, and Tenuous Arguments

In a world where political discourse often teeters on the brink of absurdity, the conversation between CNN host Brianna Keilar and Rep. Dan Meuser (R-Pennsylvania) gleefully leaps into the abyss. At the heart of their exchange is a CNN poll revealing that a significant 56 percent of respondents believe Congress isn't doing enough to check President Donald Trump's power. This statistic alone should raise eyebrows, yet it's the discussion on Trump's tariffs that truly showcases the intricate dance of political evasion and distortion.

The Art of Deflection

Meuser's immediate retreat to the safe haven of "separation of powers" and the assurance that President Trump's actions are constitutionally checked is a classic misdirection. It's akin to answering a question about climate change with a discourse on the beauty of seasons—technically relevant but fundamentally missing the point. The assertion that Congress and the Supreme Court are effectively overseeing Trump's executive orders sidesteps the public's concern highlighted in the poll.

Tariffs and the Phantom of Free Trade

When confronted with the potential adverse effects of Trump's tariffs, such as higher prices on Amazon, Meuser's response is a masterclass in revisionist history. Claiming that "Free trade, for the last 35 years, has really meant unfair trade," he posits that Trump's tariffs are not a symptom of protectionism but a cure for decades of economic injustice. This narrative conveniently overlooks the complexities of global trade dynamics and the nuanced impacts of tariffs on both consumers and industries.

The Inflation Illusion

Keilar's mention of restaurants adding "inflation charges" to bills is met with a dismissal so cavalier it borders on comedy. Meuser's assertion that "We have not experienced any inflation as of yet" flies in the face of direct evidence to the contrary. This denial of tangible economic indicators reflects a broader trend of political figures negating reality to fit a preconceived narrative. It's a dangerous game of economic gaslighting where the costs are all too real for the average consumer.

The China Conundrum

Meuser's characterization of China as "not our enemy" but an "adversary" and "competitor" deserving of a "reckoning" encapsulates the Trump administration's schizophrenic approach to foreign policy. The acknowledgment of China's economic interdependence with the U.S. is a rare moment of clarity in an otherwise muddled policy stance. However, the idea that Trump's aggressive trade policies are a strategic maneuver to foster a "good relationship" is as believable as a wolf inviting a lamb to a peace dinner.

Conclusion: The Spin Cycle Continues

As the conversation between Keilar and Meuser winds down, the viewer is left with a sense of having witnessed a political spin cycle on overdrive. The discussion is less a debate on the merits of policy and more an exercise in rhetorical gymnastics. The CNN poll serves as a stark reminder that a significant portion of the American public is not buying what politicians like Meuser are selling. Yet, in the face of clear discontent and economic warning signs, the spin goes on. It's a testament to the resilience of political narratives in the face of inconvenient truths and a sobering reflection on the state of American political discourse.
