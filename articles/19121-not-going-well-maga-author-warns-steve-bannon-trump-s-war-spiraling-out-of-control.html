'Not going well': MAGA author warns Steve Bannon Trump's war spiraling out of control

By Paulo Santos

Right-wing author Brandon Weichert warned MAGA influencer Steve Bannon that President Donald Trump’s war in Iran was “not going well.”

During a Thursday interview on the War Room podcast, Bannon insisted that the U.S. had to “win” the war, even though the goals were unclear.

“Regardless of how we got in here, and we can debate that, and I’m sure that even in the next couple days are coming up, we are where we are, we have to win, right?” Bannon told Weichert. “The President of the United States gave a very clear order out to the public, to the country, to everybody, that Iranian infrastructure, oil infrastructure was not to be hit. These gas fields were hit deliberately.”

“Now we’re in the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea, all of it. And it’s rattling, whether you like it or not, whether you support President Trump or not,” he continued. “It’s rattling the world’s economy and President Trump’s economic plan.”

“Clearly, there is a severe breakdown in communication not only between the U.S. and its purported allies in the region, but also between — within the administration itself,” Weichert agreed. “And I would encourage everybody in the administration, get on the same page now. Because this is not — this is not going well. The American people do not understand what’s going on.”

“Furthermore, whatever the Pentagon and Centcom and the intel community is saying publicly, behind the scenes, the Iranians are still popping these missiles off,” he added. “And you asked about Israel. And I would just say that I don’t think we have any idea who is running Israel right now. I have seen no confirmation as to whether or not Netanyahu is still alive or in command.”

“I have seen no confirmation that anyone has succeeded him. I’ve been hearing rumors from people.”

“Hang on!” Bannon interrupted, annoyed with rumors of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s demise. “That can’t be the internet slop that says he’s an AI figure or anything like that. It’s got to be more definitive.”

“It’s a mess in Israel right now,” Weichert said.

“Besides internet slop, why do you say that?” Bannon demanded.

“Well, because the entire Israeli system seems to be in confusion,” the author insisted. “There is clearly a problem right now. And then also you have these intelligence failures going on in Lebanon with Mossad, where they’re blowing up targets that are clearly not military.”

“I think they’re operating off of bad intelligence because Mossad’s capabilities have been degraded.”

Before signing off, Weichert made it clear that he stood with Joe Kent, a former Trump intelligence official who resigned over the war in Iran.

“This show and me personally have been the biggest backer of Joe Kent,” Bannon remarked.

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Blame Game in the Iran Conflict

In the swirling vortex of commentary and analysis surrounding the U.S. involvement in Iran, a recent interview on Steve Bannon’s War Room podcast with right-wing author Brandon Weichert highlighted several critical issues. Yet, the conversation, while engaging, skews the narrative in ways that misdirect responsibility and obfuscate the true centers of power and decision-making in this complex situation.

Identifying the True Holders of Power

First and foremost, the institutional power in the context of U.S. military actions lies squarely with the executive branch, specifically with the President of the United States, Donald Trump. As Commander-in-Chief, Trump has the ultimate say in military engagements and strategies. Hence, any analysis must start with the acknowledgment that the buck stops with him when it comes to decisions made about the war in Iran.

Decision Makers and Their Impact

The article mentions Trump’s clear order regarding Iranian oil infrastructure, indicating his direct involvement in the strategic decisions of the conflict. It also alludes to a broader breakdown in communication within the U.S. administration and between the U.S. and its allies. This breakdown suggests a failure at multiple levels of decision-making, from the Pentagon and Central Command (Centcom) to the intelligence community. These entities, while influential, ultimately operate under the directives of the President. Therefore, while Bannon and Weichert’s discussion points to internal discord, the critical decision-making power and responsibility rest with Trump and his appointed administrators.

Misdirections and Scapegoats

The conversation between Bannon and Weichert, while touching upon valid points of concern, such as the impact on the world economy and the confusion within the Israeli government, veers into territory that risks misdirecting responsibility. By focusing on the lack of public understanding and alleged intelligence failures without directly addressing the central role of the President’s decisions, the dialogue inadvertently shifts the blame towards actors with limited control over the overall situation.

Additionally, their discussion about the situation in Israel and potential intelligence failures in Lebanon introduces a layer of speculation that, while intriguing, serves more to distract than clarify. The mention of Netanyahu’s status and Mossad’s capabilities, without clear linkage to the decisions leading to the conflict in Iran, risks further muddying the waters of accountability.

Conclusion: Cutting Through the Narrative Fog

In the end, the conversation on Bannon’s podcast, though reflective of genuine concerns, falls short of addressing the core issue at hand: the institutional power and decision-making process that led to the current situation in Iran. While it’s crucial to debate the efficacy and consequences of these decisions, any analysis must be grounded in a clear understanding of where the responsibility truly lies.

The narrative presented by Bannon and Weichert, though compelling, sidesteps the critical analysis required to untangle the complex web of accountability. It’s essential to look beyond the surface-level chaos and pinpoint the decisions and policies that have driven the U.S. into its current predicament. Only then can we begin to understand the real story behind the conflict and hold the appropriate parties accountable for their actions.
