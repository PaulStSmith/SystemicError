'Trump said no': Ocasio-Cortez flags source of Marjorie Taylor Greene's GOP 'revenge tour'

The Georgia Republican has spoken out against GOP leadership – and House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), in particular – and continued to press for the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files, which have plagued President Donald Trump for months, and Ocasio-Cortez told her Instagram followers why Greene is speaking out, according to Fox News.

“Here’s some tea for you,” Ocasio-Cortez said Monday in a livestream video. “MTG, people are like, ‘Oh my God, she’s saying all these things, like, what’s gotten into her lately?’ ‘Oh, like, she’s bucking against Trump, she’s bucking against the administration.”

“Marjorie Taylor Greene wanted to run for Senate in Georgia,” the New York Democrat continued. “She wanted to run for Senate earlier this year in the state of Georgia, she wanted to be the Republican nominee for Senate, so she was gearing up for that statewide race, and Trump told her no.”

“Trump said no, and the White House and Trumpland shut down Marjorie Taylor Greene’s personal ambitions to run for Senate — and she has been on a revenge tour ever since,” Ocasio-Cortez added.

Greene announced in May that she would not challenge Sen. Jon Ossoff (D-GA) in her state’s Senate race, which Republicans see as one of the best pickup opportunities in next year’s midterms.

“Even with a few good Republicans in the Senate, nothing changes,” Greene wrote on X at the time. “So no, Jon Ossoff isn’t the real problem. He’s just a vote. A pawn. No different than the Uniparty Republicans who skip key votes to attend fundraisers and let our agenda fail.”

In addition to her criticism of Johnson, especially over his stance on Affordable Care Act subsidies and the government shutdown, Greene has broken with Republicans on Israel’s war in Gaza, which she has called a “genocide” and “humanitarian crisis,” but she told ABC’s “The View” that she still supported the president.

“I do love him,” she said. “When I ran for Congress in 2020, I ran criticizing Republicans and Democrats equally, because I come from a working-class family.”

Our Analysis:

The Political Telenovela: Greene vs. GOP

In the latest episode of the never-ending political telenovela that is American politics, we find ourselves witnessing a peculiar feud that seems to encapsulate the current state of the Republican Party. Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), a figure who has never shied away from controversy, has now turned her sights against her own party, critiquing GOP leadership and particularly House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA). The twist? According to Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY), this rebellion is not rooted in ideological differences or policy disputes but in personal vendettas and dashed Senate dreams.

The Plot Thickens: Personal Ambitions vs. Party Loyalty

Ocasio-Cortez, in a statement that reads more like a gossip column reveal, suggests that Greene’s sudden bout of insubordination stems from a thwarted attempt to secure a Senate seat. Allegedly, Greene had her eyes set on the Senate, only to be rebuffed by none other than former President Donald Trump himself. Trump’s refusal, as per Ocasio-Cortez, sent Greene on a “revenge tour” against her own party.

This narrative does more than just provide fodder for political spectators; it underscores a fundamental flaw within the GOP. The party, already struggling with internal divisions and an identity crisis post-Trump presidency, now faces the additional challenge of managing personal ambitions that threaten to override party unity and policy objectives.

Greene’s Grievances: More Than Just Sour Grapes

Greene’s grievances, however, extend beyond personal ambitions. Her criticism of GOP leadership for its stance on Affordable Care Act subsidies and the government shutdown, as well as her break with Republican consensus on Israel’s actions in Gaza, paint a picture of a lawmaker who is, perhaps, genuinely disillusioned with her party’s direction. Or, at the very least, someone who recognizes the political capital to be gained from positioning herself as an outsider within her own party.

Yet, Greene’s assertion that she still supports Trump, despite her criticisms, reveals the complex relationship many Republicans have with the former president. It’s a dance of loyalty and rebellion, where personal vendettas often dictate the steps.

The Uniparty Problem: A Pawn in Their Game

Greene’s statement that “Jon Ossoff isn’t the real problem. He’s just a vote. A pawn. No different than the Uniparty Republicans who skip key votes to attend fundraisers and let our agenda fail,” touches on a sentiment that resonates with many Americans: the feeling of disillusionment with a political system that seems more interested in power plays and personal ambitions than in addressing the needs of its constituents.

This “Uniparty” critique is a scathing indictment of the current state of American politics, where bipartisan bickering often leads to stagnation and inaction, and where politicians, whether Democrat or Republican, are seen as interchangeable pieces in a game that serves interests other than those of the American people.

Conclusion: The Saga Continues

As this saga unfolds, it becomes increasingly clear that the issues plaguing the GOP are symptomatic of broader systemic problems within American politics. Greene’s feud with her party, driven by personal ambitions and ideological disagreements, is just one storyline in a complex narrative of political dysfunction.

In this telenovela, there are no heroes or villains, just characters driven by a mix of principle, ambition, and spite. And as the plot thickens, the American people are left wondering whether their interests will ever take center stage in a political drama that seems to prioritize everything but.
