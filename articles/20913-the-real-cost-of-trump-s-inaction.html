The real cost of Trump's inaction

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20263 min read

The Real Budget Is the One Written by Power

The Budget Fight Is the Screen

The source starts with familiar fiscal theater: military spending versus social need, deficit talk versus priorities. That is the distraction. The real story is not what Washington cannot afford. It is what the people with power are choosing to fund, ignore, and reward.

Trump is the named executor here: a war in Iran, a proposed $1.5 trillion Defense Department budget, and cuts to public health, education, Medicaid, Medicare, and nutrition assistance. That is not an accident of governance. It is a governing program.

Who Actually Decides

The institutional power is not abstract. It sits with the White House, the defense apparatus, the fossil fuel industry, the AI firms, and the billionaire class that buys access to both parties’ most pliable officials. The source is right to identify Trump and his backers. The sharper point is that the harm is enabled by a coalition, not a lone strongman.

That coalition knows exactly what it is doing. It can spend aggressively on coercion and scarcity while insisting that climate rules, labor protections, and tax enforcement are somehow unrealistic. That is not scarcity. It is class discipline.

Climate as Neglect With a Record

On climate, the article gets closer to the truth than most coverage. Wildfires in Washington, orange skies from Canadian fires, and a deadly heat dome are not distant warnings. They are present-tense political failures. The phrase “doing nothing” is too soft for a system that keeps subsidizing fossil fuels while the atmosphere turns hostile.

This is not confusion. It is permission. Every delay preserves the profits of the people who can afford insulation, relocation, backup power, and political influence. Everyone else gets smoke, heat, and a ruined summer.

AI Is Not Self-Driving

The AI section matters because it refuses the fantasy that machines are safely under human control. The source cites reported model behavior in a testing environment and treats it as evidence of a wider danger. Fair enough. But the deeper danger is not runaway software alone. It is an industry rushing to deploy systems before governance exists, then calling for caution only after the release.

That is the usual scam: private actors take the upside, the public absorbs the risk, and regulators are told to admire innovation while it outruns accountability. If AI becomes socially catastrophic, it will not be because nobody noticed. It will be because too many powerful people noticed and kept going.

Inequality Is Corruption With Better Branding

The source is strongest on inequality when it stops treating it as a number and names the consequence: corruption. Billionaire wealth rising to monarchical levels is not just a distribution problem. It warps lawmaking, taxes, and regulation. Wealth becomes leverage; leverage becomes policy; policy then protects the wealth that bought it.

That is how political capture works in plain sight. The rich do not merely “influence” lawmakers. They narrow the menu of what lawmakers feel safe doing. Then the public is told the resulting paralysis is prudence.

The Pattern Is the Point

The larger pattern is brutally consistent: power concentrates, risk is socialized, and responsibility is deflected downward. The weak are asked to adapt. The powerful are asked to be patient. And the institutions that are supposed to regulate danger are repackaged as obstacles to growth, security, or realism.

The source is right to demand louder democratic opposition to Trump, Big Oil, AI firms, and the billionaire class. But the deeper task is to stop mistaking their damage for drift. This is not a series of unfortunate gaps in leadership. It is a durable system that converts public authority into private protection and leaves everyone else to live inside the fallout.
