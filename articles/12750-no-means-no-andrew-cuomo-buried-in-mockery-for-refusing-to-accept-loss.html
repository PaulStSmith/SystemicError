'No means no!' Andrew Cuomo buried in mockery for refusing to accept loss

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 15, 20255 min read

Mayoral candidate Andrew Cuomo sits on stage after protestors interrupted his speech during a Democratic mayoral forum at Medgar Evers College in New York City, U.S., April 23, 2025. REUTERS/David ‘Dee’ Delgado

Former Gov. Andrew Cuomo (D-NY) was routed in a shock upset last month when Democrats rejected him in the primary for mayor of New York City, in favor of the young democratic socialist candidate Zohran Mamdani, who is running on a platform of freezing rent, expanding free bus service, and creating public grocery stores in food deserts.

After weeks of relative silence from Cuomo, however, he announced Monday that he is remaining in the race and will run on a separate party line in the general election, alongside scandal-plagued incumbent mayor Eric Adams and the pro-Trump Republican candidate Curtis Sliwa.

"Unless you've been living under a rock, you probably knew that the Democratic primary did not go the way I had hoped," said Cuomo in his announcement video posted to X. "To the 440,000 New Yorkers who voted for me, a sincere thank you. Thank you for believing in me, in my agenda, and in my experience. And I am truly sorry that I let you down. But as my grandfather used to say, when you get knocked down, learn the lesson and pick yourself back up and get in that game, and that is what I'm going to do."

Even before Cuomo's video dropped, reports that he would stay in the race had already drawn criticism.

"Andrew Cuomo an un-evolved desperate man searching for meaning in all the wrong places," wrote former New York state Sen. Alessandra Biaggi.

"True to form, Andrew Cuomo once again refuses to accept that no means no," wrote state assemblymember Phara Souffrant Forrest, referencing allegations of sexual misconduct against the former governor.

As soon as the video dropped, it was overshadowed in likes and shares by Mamdani, who replied to the post with a link to his campaign donation page.

"NO MEANS NO. Go away, you gargoyle faced bully," wrote state Sen. Gustavo Rivera.

"Translation: Cuomo is so desperate to prevent a rent freeze that he actually set foot on the streets of New York. Get this jabroni out of New York politics for good!" wrote the account for the activist network Adbusters.

"Took three weeks to edit out passersby shouting 'Go f--- yourself'" wrote satirical political cartoonist Eli Valley.

"Honestly, what do Democrats say about the purpose and utility of the primary process now?" wrote Bernie Sanders' political adviser and More Perfect Union founder Faiz Shakir. "In New York, we're yielding the entire 'process' over to billionaires' whims. Fight Oligarchy."

Our Analysis:

Cuomo's Unyielding Ambition: A Dissection

In a political landscape teeming with surprises, Former Gov. Andrew Cuomo's (D-NY) decision to remain in the New York City mayoral race post-primary defeat is less of a shock and more a testament to his tenacity, or as some critics might argue, his sheer audacity. Following his loss to the young democratic socialist Zohran Mamdani, Cuomo's choice to run on a separate party line illuminates the deep fractures within the Democratic Party and the broader political discourse in New York City.

The Cuomo Comeback: A Strategy or a Misstep?

Cuomo's announcement, delivered via a video on X, was imbued with a tone of resilience, thanking the 440,000 New Yorkers who supported him and echoing his grandfather's advice to "get back in the game." However, this sportsmanlike perseverance fails to resonate with many, especially when considering the backdrop of allegations of sexual misconduct that have marred his political legacy. Cuomo's insistence on staying in the race, despite a clear rejection in the primary, screams of desperation rather than determination.

The Reaction: From Criticism to Ridicule

The response to Cuomo's unconventional move was swift and brutal. Former New York state Sen. Alessandra Biaggi and state assemblymember Phara Souffrant Forrest underscored Cuomo's inability to accept defeat, with Forrest pointedly referencing his troubling history with consent. Meanwhile, state Sen. Gustavo Rivera and Adbusters didn't mince words, casting Cuomo as a desperate figure unwilling to let go of power. Even satirical political cartoonist Eli Valley took a jab at the former governor's announcement video, suggesting it was edited to remove public disdain.

The Larger Implications: A Reflection on Democratic Values

Cuomo's determination to run in the general election, alongside incumbent mayor Eric Adams and pro-Trump Republican candidate Curtis Sliwa, raises pertinent questions about the purpose and utility of the primary process. Bernie Sanders' political adviser, Faiz Shakir, critiqued the situation as indicative of a political system overly influenced by billionaires, suggesting that Cuomo's actions reflect a broader issue of oligarchic control over democratic processes.

The Conclusion: A Battle for the Soul of New York

Cuomo's refusal to bow out gracefully is emblematic of a larger struggle within the Democratic Party and New York City's political arena. His decision to run on a separate party line not only threatens to split the Democratic vote but also underscores the challenges facing the party in defining its values and priorities. While Cuomo appeals to his base and touts his experience, his actions may ultimately serve as a foil to Mamdani's progressive platform, highlighting the stark contrasts between the candidates and the visions they represent for the future of New York City.

In sum, Cuomo's persistence in the mayoral race, despite significant opposition and criticism, is a bold gamble that risks further alienating him from the Democratic establishment and the voters of New York City. Whether this move will reinvigorate his political career or cement his status as a pariah remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: the road to City Hall just got a lot more contentious.
