Ominous warning issued as Trump's 'rage-filled downward spiral' taints holiday

President Donald Trump’s run-up to the Thanksgiving holiday was marked by a series of inflammatory and derogatory comments targeting journalists and politicians — particularly women.

And his uncontrolled lashing out is demonstrating an alarming “rage-filled downward spiral,” a Guardian columnist wrote.

On Thanksgiving, Trump launched into a social media tirade, making an ableist slur against Minnesota Governor Tim Walz and an Islamophobic comment about Representative Ilhan Omar, describing her as “always wrapped in her swaddling hijab.” When confronted by a female journalist about a national guard attack, Trump aggressively demanded, “Are you stupid? Are you a stupid person?”

His attacks on female journalists were particularly vicious, wrote Arwa Mahdawi.

About New York Times reporter Katie Rogers, he wrote: “The writer of the story, who is assigned to write only bad things about me, is a third rate reporter who is ugly, both inside and out.”

To Catherine Lucey of Bloomberg, he jabbed his finger and said “Quiet, piggy” when she asked about the Epstein files.
White House Communications Director Steven Cheung followed Trump’s lead, telling New Yorker writer Jane Meyer to “respectfully, shut the f--k up for trying to politicize this tragedy” after she commented on the fatal national guard shooting last week.

Mary Trump, the president’s niece, suggested these attacks indicate that her uncle is “increasingly comfortable lodging such attacks” and might be feeling “a little rattled.” Cheung’s response was to call her a “stone-cold loser.”

“You don’t have to be a particularly perceptive person to know that Trump loathes most journalists – particularly women,” wrote Mahdawi. “The misogynist-in-chief has a well-documented history of lashing out at women who dare challenge him.

“Still, even by his own low standards, the past few weeks have been remarkable. The president seems to be on a rage-filled downward spiral, and is taking much of his aggression out on female members of the media.”

The pattern of attacks suggests a deliberate strategy of intimidation, with Mary Trump noting that these “misogynistic attacks against reporters are increasing.”

Trump seems unafraid of consequences, with polls showing he maintained significant support among women voters despite his inflammatory language.

But Mahdawi warned the president’s attacks — and America’s apparent contentment to look the other way — are following an ominous path.

“I don’t know if ‘rattled’ is quite the word for what is happening in Trump’s brain,” she wrote. “Rather, it would seem that Trump, who has frequently referred to himself as a ‘king,’ would like to turn himself into another Mohammed bin Salman and the US into another Saudi Arabia. In the prince’s Saudi Arabia, journalists live under heavy surveillance, media outlets stick to the government narrative and a handful of critical tweets can get you locked up.

“The US might not be quite at that point yet, but it’s on the way.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tirade: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In the run-up to Thanksgiving, President Donald Trump’s tirade against journalists and politicians, particularly women, has not just been a series of isolated incidents of inflammatory rhetoric. It represents a disturbing pattern of behavior that reflects an alarming “rage-filled downward spiral,” as highlighted by a Guardian columnist. Trump’s relentless onslaught, marked by ableist slurs against Minnesota Governor Tim Walz and Islamophobic comments about Representative Ilhan Omar, underscores a troubling descent into openly bigoted and misogynistic attacks.

The Assault on Female Journalists

Trump’s vitriolic attacks on female journalists are particularly egregious. His comments range from calling New York Times reporter Katie Rogers a “third rate reporter who is ugly, both inside and out,” to demeaningly telling Catherine Lucey of Bloomberg “Quiet, piggy.” These are not just offhand remarks but a calculated strategy to intimidate and belittle. The White House Communications Director Steven Cheung’s directive to New Yorker writer Jane Meyer to “respectfully, shut the f--k up” further mirrors this administration’s disdain for free press and critical inquiry. It’s a chilling reminder of the misogyny that pervades the highest echelons of power.

A Deliberate Strategy of Intimidation

The pattern of these attacks cannot be dismissed as mere coincidence or Trump’s personal idiosyncrasies. As Mary Trump, the president’s niece, suggests, these misogynistic attacks against reporters are increasing, pointing to a deliberate strategy of intimidation. This is not just about Trump’s personal vendettas but a broader assault on the principles of free speech and accountability.

Despite the outrage these comments should spark, Trump seems unafraid of consequences, buoyed perhaps by polls showing he maintained significant support among women voters despite his inflammatory language. This lack of accountability is not just a failure of the political system but a reflection of societal apathy towards the erosion of democratic norms and values.

An Ominous Path Toward Authoritarianism

The comparison drawn by the Guardian columnist between Trump’s aspirations and Mohammed bin Salman’s Saudi Arabia is not as far-fetched as it might seem. The desire to suppress dissent, control media narratives, and punish those who dare to criticize are hallmarks of authoritarian regimes. While the United States may not yet be at the point where critical tweets can get you locked up, the trajectory towards such a reality is becoming increasingly clear.

Trump’s attacks on the press and his overt misogyny are not just about him but about what he represents: a growing tolerance for authoritarian tendencies and a disdain for the principles of democracy and equality. The United States might not be quite at the point of mirroring Saudi Arabia’s oppressive regime, but with each unchecked tirade, it inches closer.

Conclusion

As the nation watches Trump’s “rage-filled downward spiral,” it’s essential to recognize that these are not merely the ravings of an individual but symptomatic of a deeper malaise. A malaise characterized by a growing disregard for democratic norms, a disdain for women, and an acceptance of authoritarian impulses. The real danger lies not just in Trump’s words but in the collective shrug of indifference with which they are often met. If left unchecked, this path leads not just to the erosion of democratic freedoms but to the very foundations upon which they stand.
