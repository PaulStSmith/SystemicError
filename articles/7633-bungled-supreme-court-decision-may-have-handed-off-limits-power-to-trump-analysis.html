‘Bungled’ Supreme Court decision may have handed 'off limits' power to Trump: analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 16, 20256 min read

The Supreme Court’s decision to back President Donald Trump’s efforts to ban the Chinese social media app TikTok may have inadvertently handed the Trump administration “power that the Constitution puts off limits,” two columnists argued Monday.

“We could have avoided this ending if the court had not bungled the beginning,” wrote columnists Evelyn Douek and Jameel Jaffer in an op-ed published in the Guardian Monday.

“If the court had carefully scrutinized the government’s national security arguments, it would have seen that the TikTok ban, for all of its novelty, is actually just a familiar example of the government exploiting the rhetoric of national security to intimidate courts into giving it power that the constitution puts off limits.”

Trump and members of Congress pushed during his first term to ban the platform under the guise of national security, arguing that the Chinese government was actively collecting data on Americans using the app. However, many lawmakers, political figures and tech industry leaders have also cited their disdain for TikTok exposing young Americans to videos of Israel’s military bombardment of Gaza, warning that it was “undercutting support for Israel among young Americans.”

Since 2024, Trump has reversed his position and has worked to prevent a ban.

The Supreme Court ultimately accepted the argument that TikTok posed a national security threat, a decision that came as a disappointment to critics who say the ruling was a blatant violation of the First Amendment. Douek and Jaffer consider themselves among that group.

“The court would have understood that the ban itself created a serious national security risk – the kind of risk the modern First Amendment was intended to prevent – by giving the government far-reaching influence over public discourse online,” they wrote.

“Over the coming months, the Supreme Court will confront other cases in which the government says that national security requires the suppression of speech. The court’s analysis of those cases should be haunted by the TikTok case and its embarrassing, scandalous coda.”

Now, the Trump administration appears to be brokering a deal for Oracle Corporation – founded by Trump-ally, tech billionaire and staunch Israel supporter Larry Ellison – to take a majority stake in TikTok U.S. Furthermore, censorship of content related to Israel has already seen an uptick on the platform in recent weeks.

Trump first floated banning TikTok near the end of his first term in 2020, but was ultimately blocked by the courts from doing so. Former President Joe Biden signed a bill into law in 2024 that required TikTok’s parent company ByteDance to divest from the platform or face a ban, though Trump, after retaking the White House, delayed its enforcement indefinitely.

Since first extending the date by which ByteDance is required to divest from TikTok – and place the app into the hands of an American-based company – Trump has gone on to extend it four more times, granting Trump the power to shut the app down at a moment’s notice “with a stroke of the president’s pen.”

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court’s Dubious Ruling: A Cloak of National Security?

In a recent opinion piece from the Guardian, columnists Evelyn Douek and Jameel Jaffer dissected the Supreme Court’s controversial backing of President Donald Trump’s ambition to ban TikTok, a move that has sparked widespread criticism and concern. Their argument hinges on the assertion that the Supreme Court, by failing to critically examine the government’s national security claims, has inadvertently bestowed upon the Trump administration a level of power that starkly contradicts constitutional limits. This analysis aims to unpack the complexities and implications of this situation, shedding light on the dangerous precedent it may set for the future of digital freedoms and governmental authority.

National Security or Political Theater?

The Trump administration’s crusade against TikTok was initially framed as a measure of national security, ostensibly to protect Americans from the prying eyes of the Chinese government. However, the narrative was muddled by concurrent concerns over TikTok’s role in disseminating footage of Israel’s military actions in Gaza, a point that has contributed to a broader debate about the platform’s influence on young Americans’ perceptions of international politics. This duality of reasoning exposes a fundamental issue: the manipulation of national security rhetoric to further political objectives. When the Supreme Court accepted the national security argument without rigorous scrutiny, it not only endorsed a questionable premise but also overlooked the broader First Amendment implications of such a decision.

A Dangerous Precedent for Free Speech

Douek and Jaffer argue that the TikTok ban, far from being a straightforward national security measure, represents a significant threat to the principles of free expression. By granting the government sweeping control over online discourse, the court’s decision undermines a core function of the modern First Amendment: to safeguard against undue governmental influence over public communication. The implications extend beyond TikTok; this case sets a concerning precedent for future instances where the government might invoke national security to justify censorship or control over digital platforms.

The Oracle Deal: A Questionable Solution?

Further complicating matters is the Trump administration’s negotiation with Oracle Corporation for a majority stake in TikTok’s U.S. operations. This move, particularly given Oracle founder Larry Ellison’s political affiliations and support for Israel, raises questions about the motivations behind the ban and the subsequent deal. It suggests a scenario where national security concerns may be secondary to political and personal interests, casting a shadow over the purported impartiality of the administration’s actions.

The First Amendment in the Balance

As the Supreme Court prepares to address other cases where speech suppression is justified on national security grounds, the TikTok debacle looms large. The court’s failure to critically evaluate the government’s position in this instance should serve as a cautionary tale. At stake is not just the future of a social media platform but the very essence of free speech protections in the digital age.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

The Supreme Court’s decision to support the TikTok ban, as critiqued by Douek and Jaffer, is not merely a legal misstep; it is a stark reminder of the fragility of our digital freedoms in the face of governmental overreach. This case exemplifies how easily the rhetoric of national security can be exploited to erode constitutional safeguards, emphasizing the need for constant vigilance and rigorous scrutiny of government actions purportedly taken in the name of public safety. As we move forward, the lessons from this episode must not be forgotten, lest we find ourselves in a world where the government, under the guise of protecting us, dictates the boundaries of our digital discourse.
