Judge humiliates RFK Jr. over filing loaded with AI-generated studies that don't exist

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

AI Lies, Abstinence Policy, and the Rebranding of Negligence

Power Is the Point

Robert F. Kennedy Jr. did not stumble into this ruling as a passive bystander. His department tried to justify sweeping changes to teen pregnancy prevention programs after cutting tens of millions in grants. The Trump administration controls the machinery here: the funding, the solicitations, the policy rewrite, and the legal defense. That is actual institutional power, and it was used to push a moral agenda through federal programming.

The Court Caught the Fabrication

Judge Christopher Cooper’s ruling matters because it exposes the HHS filing for what it was: not a serious evidentiary record, but a stack of claims built on nonexistent or misused citations. According to the reporting, some cited studies did not exist, and others were attributed to journals where they were never published. That is not a technical glitch. It is the state presenting invented authority as if it were fact.

Misdirection as Method

The administration framed the policy as if the real issue were protecting teens from “encouraging” sexual activity, then recast prevention as abstinence and “body literacy” programming. That language tries to smuggle ideology in as neutral public health. The harm is already baked in: grants were cut, programs were narrowed, and the burden was shifted onto young people while the government pretended it was simply being prudent. The court saw through the performance and called the changes “likely arbitrary and capricious” because the evidence did not survive contact with reality.

This Is Not an Accident

The important detail is not just that the citations were wrong. It is that this administration keeps reaching for AI-generated sludge to manufacture legitimacy after the fact. Kennedy was already embarrassed last year when the White House’s “MAHA Report” was found to contain garbled references and invented studies. That pattern tells you everything: the goal is not rigorous governance, but the production of something that looks like rigor long enough to survive public scrutiny, litigation, or both.

What the Story Reveals

This is a familiar authoritarian move in a softer suit. Starve a program, recast the ideological motive as concern, then dress the result in fake expertise. The victims are not the politicians caught lying. They are the people who lose services while the government spends its credibility on fabricated citations. When power is willing to invent evidence to justify cuts, the scandal is not that the lie was sloppy. The scandal is that deception has become a working part of administration.
