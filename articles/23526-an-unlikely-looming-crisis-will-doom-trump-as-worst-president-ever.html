An unlikely 'looming crisis' will doom Trump as 'worst president ever'

The AI Boom Has a President—and No Brakes

Power Is the Guardrail

Donald Trump’s AI policy is not confusion. It is a decision to let an industry with enormous financial and political leverage police itself while the presidency supplies promotion, protection, and ridicule for its critics.

The immediate context is simple: public opposition to data centers is growing, technology companies are debating whether development should slow, and AI investment carries consequences for employment, energy use, and financial stability. Trump’s response has been to reject restraints and declare that a “STRONG AND SMART” president is the only safeguard required.

That is not governance. It is personal rule offered as regulatory policy.

The Beneficiaries Are Not Mysterious

The administration holds the relevant institutional power. It can shape federal regulation, energy policy, permitting priorities, procurement, competition enforcement, and the political conditions under which AI companies operate. Trump is choosing acceleration.

The beneficiaries are an industry that provided him substantial financial and political support, according to the reporting, while his family also holds financial interests connected to the sector. Those facts do not, by themselves, prove that every policy choice is corrupt. They do establish an obvious conflict that serious coverage should place near the center: the president is weakening scrutiny of a booming industry entangled with his political fortunes and family finances.

Trump also treats the stock market as a measure of presidential success. With market performance increasingly dependent on AI enthusiasm, regulation threatens more than corporate valuations. It threatens the economic story he tells about himself. The incentive is therefore not merely ideological. His legacy, his preferred scoreboard, and the interests of powerful allies point in the same direction.

Opposition Becomes a “Hoax”

Trump’s familiar maneuver is to convert material disputes into partisan fraud. Communities objecting to data centers are not confronting questions about electricity, water, land, or public subsidy; they are supposedly participating in a “hoax” engineered by “Radical Left Dumocrats.”

That framing launders corporate expansion through culture war. A bipartisan backlash becomes a left-wing plot. Concrete local costs disappear behind an invented partisan enemy. People with relatively little control over national energy and technology policy are blamed for obstructing progress, while the corporations seeking infrastructure, capital, and political protection are cast as victims.

This is propaganda in its most practical form: not an elaborate false history, but a crude story that redirects scrutiny away from power.

Vanity Is Not Oversight

The reported account of Trump’s conversion to AI boosterism is damning because of its triviality. He was allegedly persuaded after seeing ChatGPT improve his social-media posts. A technology capable of reorganizing labor markets and concentrating economic power reached the president through personal flattery and convenience.

The problem is not that Trump enjoys polished copy. It is that he publicly substitutes his own judgment for enforceable safeguards. His claim that presidential intelligence is sufficient protection reduces complex questions of safety, employment, resource consumption, and financial exposure to faith in one man.

That is authoritarian logic applied to technical policy: institutions are unnecessary because the leader is exceptional. Any failure can then be denied, blamed on opponents, or declared unforeseeable—despite the warnings arriving in advance.

Risk Is Being Socialized

The article’s weakest framing is its emphasis on whether AI will make Trump the “worst president ever.” Presidential rankings are a distraction. The consequential question is who captures the gains and who absorbs the losses.

Technology firms and investors receive the upside from rapid deployment. Workers face displacement. Communities bear the physical costs of data-center construction. The public inherits environmental damage and, if speculative investment collapses, the economic fallout. Trump’s government is not standing apart from this allocation of risk. It is helping impose it.

Even the most dramatic predictions about super-intelligent AI are unnecessary to establish the political failure. Mass unemployment need not be guaranteed, an AI crash need not be imminent, and extinction scenarios need not be accepted as fact. The existing distribution of authority is enough: private firms are racing to build systems with broad social consequences, while the president dismisses restraint because restraint conflicts with his alliances, finances, and self-image.

The System Rewards the Refusal to Govern

This episode reveals a larger pattern than one president’s technological credulity. American policy repeatedly treats concentrated private investment as an irresistible force and democratic oversight as sabotage. When political leaders are personally rewarded by the boom, negligence becomes an economic program.

Trump gives that arrangement its bluntest expression. Industry support becomes access. Market inflation becomes proof of competence. Public resistance becomes conspiracy. The president’s confidence becomes the nation’s safety plan.

The looming crisis is not simply artificial intelligence. It is a political system in which those empowered to impose limits profit—politically, financially, or both—from refusing to impose them.
