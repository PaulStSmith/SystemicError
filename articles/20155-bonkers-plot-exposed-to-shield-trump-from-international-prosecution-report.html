Bonkers plot exposed to shield Trump from international prosecution: report

Gaza’s Reconstruction, With Immunity for the Powerful

Power First, Accountability Last

The reported draft resolution around Trump’s Board of Peace is not a bureaucratic footnote. It is a power move: an international body, chaired for life by the president who announced it, is alleged to be exploring legal immunity for itself and its personnel in connection with Palestinian deaths, property loss, and other harms in Gaza. The Board denies the Guardian’s characterization. But the structure described in the draft is the story.

Who Decides, Who Escapes

Actual authority sits with the Board and, under the draft as reported, with Trump personally. That matters. When a body created to oversee reconstruction also seeks broad immunity from the consequences of its own work, it is not acting like a neutral administrator. It is building a fortress around decision-makers before the first liability question is even asked.

The key fact is not confusion. It is design. The draft reportedly gives Trump exclusive power to waive someone’s legal immunity. That is not accountability. That is discretionary pardon authority inside the same political machine that set the rules.

Reconstructing Gaza, Excluding Responsibility

The source also points to section 7 of the draft, where claims for “property loss or damage” and “personal injury, illness or death” would be handled by the Board itself. That is a closed loop: the body accused of causing harm would also help decide what counts as harm, and who gets remedy. Noura Erakat’s warning that this amounts to “no external oversight” is the right frame. A reconstruction authority that insulates itself from outside law is not merely rebuilding; it is governing through exemption.

The Story the Framing Tries to Hide

The White House pushed questions to the Board, and the Board rejected the Guardian’s description as “wrong, misleading.” That denial is predictably convenient. It tries to shift attention from the reported draft’s architecture to the optics of the reporting. But the issue is not whether the legal wording is finalized. It is that the reported intent is to preempt accountability before any damage is assessed.

That is a familiar tactic: call immunity “order,” call seizure “mission support,” call legal insulation “process.” The draft’s language about providing “free of charge” public premises and facilities also reads, in context, like a mechanism for compelled access dressed up as administrative necessity. If that interpretation holds, the board is not just reconstructing Gaza. It is creating a legal corridor for taking what it needs without meaningful recourse.

The Larger Pattern

This is what elite impunity looks like when it is translated into institutional form: power announces a humanitarian purpose, then writes itself out of the consequences. The language is always procedural. The effect is always one-way. Palestinians absorb the losses; officials and their agents get shields.

The broader pattern is not incompetence. It is governance by exception, where the actors with the most leverage also claim the least accountability. In that model, law is not a constraint on power. It is a tool power uses to certify itself in advance.
