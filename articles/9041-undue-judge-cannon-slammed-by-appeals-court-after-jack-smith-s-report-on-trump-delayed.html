'Undue': Judge Cannon slammed by appeals court after Jack Smith's report on Trump delayed

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Judiciary’s Tug-of-War: Unveiling Trump’s Classified Documents Saga

(Photo: Creative commons and FBI exhibit)

The 11th Circuit Court of Appeals has ordered U.S. District Court Judge Aileen Cannon to stop delaying the release of the remaining parts of special counsel Jack Smith’s report on President Donald Trump’s alleged mishandling of classified documents.

In September, the Knight First Amendment Institute at Columbia University and American Oversight asked the appeals court to force Cannon to release the report. Cannon had blocked former President Joe Biden’s Justice Department from releasing the report in January.

“By keeping this order in place, Judge Cannon is undermining both accountability and the rule of law,” American Oversight Director Chioma Chukwu noted. Trump appointed Cannon during his first term.

“There is no legitimate reason for the report’s continued suppression, and it should be posted on the court’s public docket without further delay,” Knight Institute Director Jameel Jaffer insisted..

On Monday, the appeals court ordered Cannon to stop delaying the report’s release.

“Petitioners filed their motions on February 14 and February 24, 2025, and filed notification on July 7 and July 10, 2025, informing the district court that their motions had been fully briefed for more than 90 days. To date, the district court has not ruled or conducted any other further proceedings on the pending motions,” the court noted.

“Accordingly, Petitioners have established undue delay in resolution of their motions to intervene and the petitions for writs of mandamus are HELD IN ABEYANCE for a period of 60 days to allow the district court to fully resolve the motions.”

Our Analysis:

The Judiciary’s Tug-of-War: Unveiling Trump’s Classified Documents Saga

In a decisive move, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals has cut through the judicial red tape, marking a significant moment in the ongoing saga surrounding former President Donald Trump’s alleged mishandling of classified documents. The court’s order, aimed directly at U.S. District Court Judge Aileen Cannon, is a blistering indictment of the delays plaguing the release of special counsel Jack Smith’s report. This is not just a matter of procedural foot-dragging; it’s a stark illustration of how political allegiances and the mechanisms of accountability are at odds in the hallowed halls of justice.

Judge Aileen Cannon’s Controversial Stance

At the heart of this debacle is Judge Aileen Cannon, a Trump appointee who has seemingly used her judicial powers to shield the former president from scrutiny. By blocking the release of Smith’s report, Cannon has not only sparked outrage but has also raised serious questions about impartiality and the influence of political appointments on the judiciary. It’s a classic case of the watchdog being muzzled, as efforts to ensure transparency and accountability are stonewalled by those who should champion these principles the most.

The Knight First Amendment Institute and American Oversight’s Crusade

The push to unveil the contents of Smith’s report has been spearheaded by the Knight First Amendment Institute at Columbia University and American Oversight, who have tirelessly fought against the opacity surrounding this high-profile case. Their legal battle represents a broader struggle for transparency in a system that too often cloaks itself in secrecy. The appeals court’s acknowledgment of their efforts underlines the importance of external pressure in breaking down barriers to information crucial for public discourse.

The Appeals Court’s Intervention: A Beacon of Accountability?

The 11th Circuit Court of Appeals’ order is a rare beacon of accountability, slicing through the inertia that has characterized this case. By setting a 60-day deadline for Judge Cannon to act, the court has effectively put a timer on justice, signaling that the era of indefinite delays is unacceptable. Yet, one can’t help but wonder: why did it take so long? The timelines outlined in the appeals court’s note – with motions filed as early as February 2025 and notifications of briefing completion in July 2025 – paint a damning picture of judicial dawdling.

Political Appointments and the Shadow over Justice

This episode raises uncomfortable questions about the role of political appointments in the judiciary. Judge Cannon’s actions, viewed through a cynical lens, could be interpreted as loyalty to the appointer rather than to the law. This perception undermines the judiciary’s supposed impartiality and erodes public trust in its verdicts. The fact that this case involves a former president accused of mishandling classified documents only adds fuel to the fire, suggesting that power and connections might shield one from accountability.

The Path Forward

As we await the eventual release of Jack Smith’s report, this entire episode serves as a critical reminder of the challenges facing our justice system. The appeals court’s intervention may have set the wheels of justice in motion once again, but the underlying issues of transparency, accountability, and political influence remain deeply entrenched.

In a democracy, the law must apply equally to all, regardless of status or political affiliation. The saga of Trump’s classified documents is more than just a legal battle; it’s a litmus test for the integrity of our judicial system. As the 60-day deadline approaches, all eyes will be on Judge Cannon, whose next move could either restore faith in the judiciary or deepen the scars of skepticism.
