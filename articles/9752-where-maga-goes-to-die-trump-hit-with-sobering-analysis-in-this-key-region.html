'Where MAGA goes to die': Trump hit with sobering analysis in this key region

New England has always been a Democratic stronghold, writes The Boston Globe, and when it comes to the Republican party, “New England is where MAGA goes to die.”

“Trump’s ability to take over state Republican parties, recruit candidates, help them win primaries, and build a lasting legacy has been limited,” the Globe says.

New England is reliably Democratic — all U.S. House members from the six-state region are Democrats, eleven of the 12 U.S. senators are Democrats, and five of the six governors endorsed Kamala Harris for president last year.

New Hampshire, the only state in the region with a Republican governor (Kelly Ayotte), is facing pressure from the White House to redistrict, the Globe says.

“They want Ayotte to agree to redistrict the state’s two US House seats — currently both held by Democrats — just as they’ve asked other Republican governors to do. In theory, the state legislature could take up the matter during special sessions this fall. But Ayotte has flatly refused,” says the Globe.

And while the White House tried to threaten Ayotte with a primary challenger, no one volunteered until senior adviser in the Department of Homeland Security Corey Lewandowski threw his name into the ring via text to a reporter.

“But in reality, Lewandowski was making a bigger admission: once again, he and the broader MAGA movement haven’t convinced anyone else to put their name forward,” the Globe says.

While Ayotte did not endorse Trump in the 2024 election, her predecessor, Chris Sununu (R-NH), endorsed Nikki Haley. Other prominent New Hampshire politicians did not endorse Trump either.

“Similarly, Trump has left little lasting imprint in Maine,” the Globe says. “Every MAGA-backed candidate in that district has lost, despite Trump’s success there. Maine Republicans today are more organized around Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME), who never voted for Trump in the general elections of 2016, 2020, or 2024.”

In Vermont, the Globe notes, “Governor Phil Scott, a Republican who has also never voted for Trump, remains the model.”

Massachusetts “briefly fell under MAGA’s sway,” the Globe says, as “one of the three Republicans running for governor next year hail from the MAGA wing.”

In Rhode Island, where they “tested MAGA loyalties twice,” MAGA also failed twice, the Globe says. “Republicans in the Ocean State struggle to recruit candidates at all, a problem many blame on Trump.”

New England, in which “a national political realignment” has made it “steadily more Democratic,” has clearly and successfully resisted MAGA, the Globe says.

However, it’s not just Democrats there rejecting MAGA.

“But there’s also been a realignment inside the GOP, away from Reagan conservatism and toward Trump’s MAGA style. New England Republicans, however, continue to resist it,” the Globe reports.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Mirage in New England: A Closer Look

The Boston Globe recently painted a vivid portrait of the MAGA movement’s struggle to gain a foothold in New England, a region historically aligned with Democratic values. This analysis, however, goes beyond mere party lines, revealing a deeper, systemic struggle within the Republican Party and the broader American political landscape. Let’s dissect the key elements of this narrative and what it signifies for the MAGA movement and New England’s political identity.

The Democratic Stronghold

It’s no secret that New England has been a bastion for Democratic politics. The Globe’s highlight of the region’s political composition—all U.S. House members and nearly all U.S. senators are Democrats—underscores a significant challenge for any Republican influence, let alone the MAGA faction. The endorsement of Kamala Harris by five of the six governors further cements this stronghold. However, what’s more telling is the nuanced resistance within the Republican ranks against the MAGA influence, a trend that seems to diverge from other parts of the United States.

The MAGA Resistance

The Globe’s analysis points to a stark reality: MAGA’s influence is waning in New England, not just among Democrats but within the Republican Party itself. Kelly Ayotte’s refusal to redistrict New Hampshire, despite pressure from the White House, is a bold stance against a tactic often employed to gain partisan advantage. Moreover, the inability to field a primary challenger against Ayotte, until Corey Lewandowski’s last-minute text, is emblematic of a broader disinterest or disillusionment with the MAGA agenda.

The Endorsement Game

The lack of Trump endorsements among New Hampshire’s prominent politicians, including Ayotte’s and Chris Sununu’s decisions to back other candidates, signifies a clear departure from Trump’s grip on the party. This is not mere political strategy; it represents a fundamental disagreement with the MAGA approach and a yearning for a different Republican identity.

The MAGA Failures

The Globe’s mention of MAGA’s failures in Maine and Rhode Island, along with the minimal influence in Massachusetts and Vermont, is not just a series of electoral defeats. It’s a repudiation of the MAGA ideology in a region that values different political norms. Susan Collins’s and Phil Scott’s distance from Trump, both in terms of policy and electoral support, showcases a Republican ethos that diverges significantly from the MAGA blueprint.

The Bigger Picture

What the Globe’s analysis brings to light is not just a regional anomaly but a microcosm of a national struggle within the Republican Party. The realignment away from Reagan conservatism towards a MAGA style has found resistance in New England, suggesting a broader discontent or, at least, a search for an alternative Republican identity that resonates more closely with traditional conservative values and regional sensibilities.

Conclusion

The Boston Globe’s narrative on the MAGA movement’s challenges in New England is more than a political analysis; it’s a reflection of a broader existential crisis within the Republican Party. New England’s resistance to MAGA is a beacon for those within the GOP seeking a different path, one that perhaps aligns more closely with the party’s historical roots and values. As the 2024 election looms, the struggle for the soul of the Republican Party will undoubtedly intensify, with New England serving as a critical battleground in this ideological war. The MAGA movement’s failure to penetrate New England is not just a loss but a loud, echoing call for introspection and possibly, reinvention.
