Neurologist says Trump’s conduct may be more than cognitive decline

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosOctober 6, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happenedSpeculation is not a diagnosisPower does not require impairmentThe missing guardrailsThe danger of amateur neurologyAccountability without an MRI

What happened

AlterNet reports that geriatric neurologist Dr. Germaine Odenheimer said Donald Trump’s conduct may reflect cognitive changes, medication effects, the removal of institutional restraints, or some combination of those factors. She did not diagnose dementia and stressed that Trump’s medications, laboratory results, and cognitive-test results are unknown.

Speaking Monday on Dr. Steven Hassan’s podcast, Odenheimer described Trump’s recent GOP speech in Dallas as long and rambling but said she detected no specific language deficit. Hassan argued that Trump’s behavior appears less restrained because healthier advisers and other “guardrails” no longer constrain him. Both raised concern that Trump might interfere if Democrats won control of Congress, but the article provides no evidence of a particular plan.

Speculation is not a diagnosis

The strongest medical conclusion in the story is that no reliable conclusion can be drawn. Odenheimer identifies several possibilities—neurological change, medication, another medical condition, or simply fewer constraints—but lacks the clinical information required to distinguish among them.

That makes the headline’s medical tease more confident than the reporting underneath it. An expert listing hypotheses is not evidence that any one hypothesis is true. Trump’s repeated falsehoods, rambling remarks, and attacks on fellow Republicans are observable conduct. Their medical cause remains unestablished.

Power does not require impairment

The fixation on cognition risks converting political agency into a diagnostic mystery. A president does not need dementia, medication side effects, or kidney failure to abuse power, spread false claims, or punish rivals. Those actions can also be choices.

Trump holds executive power. The advisers who indulge him, party officials who provide a platform, and institutions that decline to impose consequences exercise agency too. Medical speculation can obscure that structure by relocating responsibility from decisions and enablers to an unknowable condition inside one man’s brain.

The missing guardrails

Hassan says restraints around Trump have been systematically dismantled, while Odenheimer attributes part of the change to the Supreme Court. But AlterNet does not identify the rulings, officials, or mechanisms behind that claim. “The guardrails are gone” describes an outcome without adequately naming who removed them.

Guardrails are not atmospheric conditions. They are laws enforced or ignored, oversight exercised or abandoned, appointments confirmed, rulings issued, and party leaders deciding what conduct they will tolerate. Without that institutional accounting, a structural failure gets reduced to Trump becoming more “unhinged.”

The danger of amateur neurology

The article’s weakest move is treating public behavior as an invitation to reverse-engineer private medical records. Odenheimer appropriately acknowledges the limits: Trump has not been examined by her, essential information is unavailable, and even an unsteady gait can have multiple explanations.

That uncertainty should narrow the story, not furnish it with more conjecture. Suggesting medication as “most likely” without knowing what medication Trump takes gives political commentary the costume of clinical precision. Readers do not need a speculative diagnosis to evaluate documented statements and official acts.

Accountability without an MRI

The larger pattern is a media habit of medicalizing authoritarian-style political conduct when institutional analysis would be more useful. A diagnosis can make deliberate behavior sound involuntary, while talk of vanished guardrails can make institutional surrender sound natural and ownerless.

The relevant questions are concrete: what power is being exercised, who enables it, what restraints remain, and who refuses to use them? Trump’s health may be unknown. The distribution of political responsibility is not.
