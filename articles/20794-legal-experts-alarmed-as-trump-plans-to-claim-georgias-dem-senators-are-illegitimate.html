Legal experts alarmed as Trump plans to claim Georgia’s Dem senators are illegitimate

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Reported MoveWho Holds PowerThe Lie Is the ToolThe Real Target Is the ElectorateGeorgia Is the Test CaseThe Pattern

Trump Is Not Warning About Fraud. He Is Manufacturing Conditions for It.

The Reported Move

The reporting says Trump is preparing another speech to amplify the same exhausted lie: that the 2020 election was stolen. It also says he may declare Georgia’s two Democratic senators, Jon Ossoff and Raphael Warnock, illegitimate while he keeps pushing legal and procedural tactics meant to make the next election harder to vote in and easier to contest.

That is the context. The meaning is more important: this is not confusion, and it is not an argument about the past. It is a power operation aimed at the future.

Who Holds Power

Trump is not a commenter on the system. He is the system’s most dangerous officeholder when it comes to election legitimacy, because he is using the prestige of the presidency and the reach of his movement to poison the basic mechanics of democratic transfer.

The people targeted in the reporting are weaker actors: voters, Democratic senators, election administrators, and postal systems. That imbalance matters. When a president attacks the legitimacy of elections, he is not “raising concerns.” He is weaponizing state authority and public attention against the next count.

The Lie Is the Tool

The 2020 fraud claim has already been litigated, audited, and rejected. George F. Will’s reminder in the source is blunt: Trump’s side lost the vast majority of the 64 court challenges, and one of the most scrutinized states, Arizona, ended with a private firm selected by Trump’s own allies confirming his loss.

That is why the lie persists. The point is not evidence. The point is to keep the lie politically useful. It gives Trump a pretext for purges, intimidation, procedural sabotage, and loyalty tests dressed up as election reform.

The Real Target Is the Electorate

The source notes efforts to force proof-of-citizenship rules, manipulate who can send ballots by mail, purge voters, and threaten ICE at polling places. That is not a scattered wishlist. It is a coordinated effort to narrow the electorate by fear, friction, and selective enforcement.

And yes: if ICE is being floated as a tool to intimidate voters, then the conclusion is not “better training.” It is abolition. ICE is already built for coercion, racialized suspicion, and state violence. It should not exist as a political weapon hovering over polling places or neighborhoods.

Georgia Is the Test Case

Georgia keeps showing up because it is where Trump’s power play collided with concrete legal consequences. The source reminds us that he was indicted for pressuring Brad Raffensperger to “find 11,780 votes.” That was not a rhetorical flourish. It was an attempt to convert presidential pressure into altered election results.

Declaring Ossoff and Warnock illegitimate fits the same pattern. If a democratic verdict produces the wrong outcome, Trump treats the elected officials as fake rather than his own claims as fraudulent. That is classic authoritarian inversion: the vote is valid only when it serves the ruler.

The Pattern

The deeper story is not that Trump keeps lying. It is that the Republican ecosystem around him keeps translating loss into grievance and grievance into machinery. The lie about 2020 becomes a rationale for disenfranchisement in 2026. The imagined crime becomes the excuse for real coercion. The failed coup becomes policy.

That is the political structure on display here: a movement that cannot reliably win on broad consent, so it works to make consent less necessary. The danger is not that Trump sounds unhinged. The danger is that his lies are being used to reorganize power around the assumption that elections are only legitimate when the right side wins them.
