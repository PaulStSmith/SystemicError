A statute of limitations issue could undercut the pressure Fulton County has been put under by Donald Trump’s administration, an ex-prosecutor said.

Joyce Vance, a former United States Attorney for the Northern District of Alabama, highlighted the glaring issue which can be found in court filings made against the Georgia county. Writing in her Substack, Vance observed a problem the Trump admin had seemingly not taken into account.

She wrote, “At the outset, there appears to be a statute of limitations issue. The government has five years from the time they were committed to prosecute these crimes. The election was in 2020. It’s now 2026.

“Math was never my strong suit, but I don’t see how that can work. There is no explanation of this apparent deficiency in the affidavit. With a marker down for that legal issue, we move on to probable cause.”

The odds are stacked against the admin, which must now prove its claims of election fraud in 2020 hold water. Vance explained, “The government has to demonstrate a reasonable basis to believe that a crime was committed and that evidence of the crime will be found at the location to be searched.

“And importantly here, the government can’t get to probable cause by ignoring evidence that cuts the other way.

“The Eleventh Circuit has held that a ‘warrant affidavit violates the Fourth Amendment when it contains omissions made intentionally or with a reckless disregard for the accuracy of the affidavit... if inclusion of the omitted facts would have prevented a finding of probable cause,’ and that ‘Whether the omitted statement was material is determined by examining the affidavit as if the omitted information had been included and inquiring if the affidavit would still have given rise to probable cause for the warrant.’”

Vance does not believe the case will go much further than it has, adding, “On top of everything that was left out of the affidavit, the whole debunked big lie issue, it’s a mighty slender thread to base probable cause on.

“Given the careful review that’s customary for search warrant affidavits, it’s surprising that this made it out of both the FBI office and the US Attorney’s office, and onto a federal judge’s desk.”

Our Analysis:

A Statutory Misstep in Trump’s Legal Crusade Against Fulton County

In a recent revelation that could only be described as a spectacular oversight, Joyce Vance, a former U.S. Attorney for the Northern District of Alabama, has shed light on what appears to be a fundamental legal blunder by the Trump administration in its pursuit against Fulton County. As outlined in her Substack, Vance points out a glaring statute of limitations issue that could very well undermine the entire case before it even gains traction.

The Statute of Limitations Conundrum

The core of the problem lies in the simple arithmetic of legal timelines. The crimes related to the 2020 election that the Trump administration aims to prosecute are now beyond the five-year statute of limitations threshold as we step into 2026. This isn’t rocket science; it’s basic legal framework 101. The fact that this oversight wasn’t flagged in the affidavit speaks volumes about the level of diligence—or lack thereof—that went into this legal endeavor.

Probable Cause: A Hurdle Too High?

Beyond the statute of limitations, Vance outlines another significant hurdle: probable cause. The administration must prove a reasonable basis for believing that a crime was committed and that evidence can be found at a specific location. Yet, as Vance articulates, this cannot be achieved by turning a blind eye to evidence that contradicts their narrative.

The Eleventh Circuit’s stance on warrant affidavits adds another layer of complexity. Any omissions made intentionally or with reckless disregard for accuracy, especially those that could sway the finding of probable cause, are a violation of the Fourth Amendment. Vance’s critique here is biting; the administration’s case is based on what she refers to as a “mighty slender thread” of probable cause, weakened further by its reliance on the thoroughly debunked narrative of election fraud.

A Surprising Oversight

Perhaps the most striking aspect of this saga is how such a flawed affidavit was not only drafted but also managed to pass through the hands of the FBI, the US Attorney’s office, and onto a federal judge’s desk. This chain of events suggests a breakdown not just in legal strategy but in the basic checks and balances one would expect in such a high-stakes legal process.

Conclusion: A Legal Folly?

In essence, the Trump administration’s legal offensive against Fulton County is marred by a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard for basic legal principles. The statute of limitations issue alone could render the entire effort futile, a fact that undermines the seriousness and credibility of the pursuit. Coupled with the challenge of establishing probable cause amidst a narrative of election fraud that has been debunked time and again, one must wonder if this was a legal strategy or a political gambit gone awry.

As the dust settles, this episode will likely be remembered as a cautionary tale of what happens when legal pursuits are guided more by political vendettas than by sound legal reasoning and facts. In their rush to challenge the outcome of the 2020 election, the Trump administration’s legal team seems to have overlooked the foundational aspects of the law, resulting in a case that, as Vance predicts, may not go much further.
