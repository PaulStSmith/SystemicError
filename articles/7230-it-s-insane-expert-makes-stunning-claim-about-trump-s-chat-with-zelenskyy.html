'It's insane': Expert makes stunning claim about Trump's chat with Zelenskyy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 29, 20253 min read

A military expert revealed on Sunday that President Donald Trump is taking an “insane” approach to ending the war in Ukraine.

Trump held another round of peace talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy at Mar-a-Lago on Sunday, where the two leaders discussed the current 20-point peace plan that is on the table. Ahead of Trump’s chat with Zelenskyy, the president said he had a phone conversation with Russian President Vladimir Putin following a night of heavy bombing by Russian forces against Ukrainian civilians.

Tom Nichols, a staff writer for The Atlantic, said in an interview on MS NOW’s “Alex Witt Reports” that Trump appears to be trying to play the intermediary between Putin and Zelenskyy, a strategy that he said shows either that Trump doesn’t understand the conflict or that he is lying about his efforts to end it.

“From the point of diplomatic practice, it’s insane,” Nicols said. You don’t check in with the aggressor when you’re talking to a putative friend and ally, and you don’t report in afterwards. You talk with your friends, with your allies, with Ukraine, and then you tell the Russians, ‘Here’s the offer. Here’s what you need to do.’”

“And instead, it’s almost like Trump is acting like an intermediary, trying to express Putin’s wishes to Zelenskyy and to the world, which is what he did after the Alaska summit,” Nichols continued. “It’s what he does every time he gets off the phone with Putin. The fact is, Vladimir Putin has this hold over Donald Trump that makes Trump and the American government into basically a de facto ally of Russia.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s “Insane” Diplomacy

In a recent development that can only be described as bewildering, President Donald Trump is reported to have taken what has been characterized as an “insane” approach to brokering peace in the Ukraine conflict. The heart of the matter seems to lie in Trump’s peculiar method of engaging with both Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Russian President Vladimir Putin, a strategy that has drawn sharp criticism for its apparent naivety or, worse, its duplicitous nature.

The Alleged Peace Talks

Trump’s attempt at playing peacemaker involved a series of discussions, notably a round of peace talks with Zelenskyy at Mar-a-Lago. These talks, reportedly centered around a 20-point peace plan, were preceded by a phone conversation with Putin—immediately following a night of aggressive Russian bombardment against Ukrainian civilians. This sequence of actions raises eyebrows, to say the least.

Tom Nichols’ Critique

Tom Nichols, a staff writer for The Atlantic, did not mince words when he lambasted Trump’s diplomatic maneuvers as either a display of profound misunderstanding of the conflict or a deliberate falsification of his peacemaking efforts. Nichols’ criticism hinges on the fundamental principles of diplomatic engagement, which Trump seems to have turned on their head.

According to Nichols, the conventional wisdom—indeed, the sane approach—would have Trump rally with Ukraine, presenting a united front against Russian aggression, before laying down the law with Putin. Instead, Trump’s actions suggest he’s more interested in being Putin’s messenger boy, relaying the Russian leader’s desires to Zelenskyy and, by extension, to the world.

The Putin-Trump Dynamic

Nichols goes further, implying that Putin has a certain inexplicable sway over Trump. This influence, real or imagined, casts the American government under Trump’s leadership as a de facto ally of Russia, a stance that is as dangerous as it is disgraceful.

Conclusion

The implications of Trump’s approach, as outlined in the article, are far-reaching and deeply concerning. Not only does it betray a fundamental misunderstanding (or willful ignorance) of international diplomacy, but it also positions the United States uncomfortably close to acting as an enabler of Russian aggression.

In the grand chessboard of geopolitics, Trump’s strategy resembles not so much a cunning gambit but a blunder of the highest order. It’s a maneuver that not only compromises the principles of international solidarity and justice but also places the very concept of American leadership on the world stage under a cloud of doubt and derision.

In essence, if the article’s portrayal is accurate, Trump’s diplomatic approach to the Ukraine conflict is not just misguided; it’s a systemic error of monumental proportions.
