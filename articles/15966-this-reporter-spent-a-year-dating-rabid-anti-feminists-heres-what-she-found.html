The term "Manosphere" is used to describe a variety of anti-feminist movements and ideologies, which range from PUAs (pickup artists) to MGTOWs (Men Going Their Own Way) to incels (involuntary celibates).

There is plenty of infighting within the Manosphere; PUAs (many of whom consider the controversial Andrew Tate their role model) and MGTOWs have considerable animosity for one another. And incels are so dark that they are controversial even within the Manosphere. But one thing many PUAs, MGTOWs and incels have in common is an intense devotion to President Donald Trump.

Researching an article for Cosmopolitan, New York City-based journalist Vera Papisova spent a year dating men who are active in the Manosphere. And she discussed her findings in an interview with CNN.

The 34-year-old Papisova told CNN's Donie O'Sullivan, "They are the most insecure men I have ever sat down with. It was really difficult to have some of these dates because they were so insecure, because they don't really know who they are — and they don't know how to figure that out."

According to Papisova, the men she dated lived in a Manosphere bubble.

Papisova told O'Sullivan, "They did not listen to anything else…. They're only listening to independent media, and they're only listening to men talking to men."

O'Sullivan didn't get into the ideological differences within the Manosphere but noted the presence of "macho podcasters and influencers." And the Tate-obsessed men Papisova described appeared to be more of a PUA mindset.

While MGTOWs and incels avoid women, PUAs advocate dominating women.

Papisova recalled, "One of these guys brought me to a political meeting…. It was a bunch of men complaining about girlfriends and wives, regurgitating Andrew Tate type of advice about how to train your girlfriend—how to train your wife to be submissive."

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Depths of the Manosphere: A Critique

The article in question dives into the murky waters of the Manosphere, a collective term that encapsulates a range of anti-feminist movements and ideologies, from PUAs (Pickup Artists) to MGTOWs (Men Going Their Own Way) to incels (Involuntary Celibates). At first glance, it might appear as a mere exploration of fringe groups, but the underlying currents reveal a disturbing reflection of wider societal issues.

The Ideological Echo Chamber

One of the most glaring revelations from the article is the description of the Manosphere as an ideological echo chamber. Vera Papisova, through her year-long immersion into dating men active within these movements, found that these individuals are not just living in a bubble but are actively reinforcing it by exclusively consuming "independent media" that echoes their beliefs. This isn't just a quirky trait; it's a dangerous trend. When individuals only "listen to men talking to men," they're not just excluding women's voices; they're actively rejecting any external reality that challenges their worldview.

Boldly put, this isn't just insecurity—it's willful ignorance.

Insecurity Masquerading as Ideology

Papisova's findings that these men are "the most insecure men I have ever sat down with" should not be brushed off as mere anecdotal evidence. It speaks volumes about the psychological underpinnings of the Manosphere. This insecurity isn't just a personal failing; it's the fuel for the fire that these movements stoke. When individuals are unsure of their identity and place in the world, ideologies that offer simple, albeit toxic, solutions become incredibly appealing.

Insecurity, thus, is not just an individual issue but a collective crisis being weaponized.

The Disturbing Role of Political Figures

The article briefly touches on a significant yet underexplored aspect: the intense devotion to President Donald Trump within these circles. This isn't just a political preference; it's a symbiotic relationship. Political figures who embody the machismo and dominance preached by the Manosphere become idols not because of their policies, but because they validate the toxic masculinity these groups espouse.

This isn't politics; it's identity worship.

The Misogyny Under the Microscope

Perhaps the most chilling part of the article is the revelation of the PUA mindset and its manifestations, as shown through Papisova's experiences. The concept of "training" women to be submissive isn't just an outdated idea—it's a dangerous practice that reduces women to objects meant to be controlled. This isn't just about individual relationships; it's a systemic issue that perpetuates violence against women.

Calling it anything less than misogyny would be a disservice.

Conclusion

While the article offers a glimpse into the Manosphere, it barely scratches the surface of the implications of such ideologies on society. The infighting and nuances within these movements are not just petty squabbles; they're indicative of a larger problem of toxic masculinity and systemic misogyny. The devotion to figures like Trump isn't a political phenomenon; it's a reflection of a society that still elevates and rewards toxic masculine ideals.

The Manosphere is not just a fringe group; it's a symptom of a systemic disease.

And as we dissect this phenomenon, we must not lose sight of the real-world implications of such ideologies. It's not enough to critique; we must actively work towards dismantling the systems that give rise to such toxic beliefs. Only then can we hope to address the insecurities and societal failures that fuel the Manosphere.

In the end, the battle against the Manosphere is not just ideological; it's fundamentally a battle for the soul of our society.
