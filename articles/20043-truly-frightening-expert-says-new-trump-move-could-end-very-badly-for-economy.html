'Truly frightening': Expert says new Trump move 'could end very badly' for economy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 28, 20254 min read

As President Donald Trump continues to stand behind his decision to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, one former leader at the agency warns that the consequences of the firing, should it ultimately go through, could be catastrophic.

Trump announced on Monday that Cook would be fired “effective immediately,” alleging the Biden-appointee of mortgage fraud, claims that have yet to be litigated in court. Cook immediately rebuked Trump in declaring her intention to continue to serve out the remainder of her 14-year term.

Bill Dudley, a former president at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, however, warned that should Cook ultimately be fired, the new makeup of the agency’s board could set off a series of “standoffs, showdowns, chaos and uncertainty” that he said “would be truly frightening,” in an op-ed published in Bloomberg Wednesday.

“The attack on Cook represents a major escalation that could end very badly,” Dudley wrote. “Never before has a president tried to fire a Fed governor, and there’s much more at stake than one person’s job.”

Dudley went on to note that, should Cook be removed from her position, Trump would then have appointed four of the central bank’s seven governors, granting him a powerful majority that would grant the president far more leverage at the Fed.

“The Board of Governors could, for example, refuse to reappoint some or all of the 12 regional Federal Reserve Bank presidents, whose five-year terms come up for renewal in February 2026 – and five of whom vote on the FOMC on a rotating basis. In theory, this could be a way to populate the (Federal Open Market Committee) with members that would do Trump’s bidding, empowering the president to get the big rate cuts he seeks.”

The announced firing of Cook comes amid Trump’s ongoing feud with the Federal Reserve over its refusal to do his bidding and slash interest rates. Trump has gone as far as to tease firing the agency’s chair, Jerome Powell, but has, at least for now, backed off that idea.

“Even if Trump stands only a small chance of taking control of the Fed, the effort itself is disruptive and the consequences of success would be dire,” Dudley wrote. “The threat to the Fed’s independence – along with the risk of uncontained inflation, much higher long-term borrowing costs and a significantly weaker dollar – isn’t going away.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Cook Confrontation: A Dangerous Power Play at the Federal Reserve

In what can only be described as a bold power move with potentially catastrophic consequences, President Donald Trump's decision to fire Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook has sparked a firestorm of controversy and alarm. Let's dive into the critical aspects of this situation, which could have far-reaching implications for the U.S. economy and the independence of one of its most vital institutions.

Trump's Unprecedented Action

Trump's move to fire Cook, a Biden appointee, on allegations of mortgage fraud — claims that have not been litigated in court — is a stunning deviation from normative governance practices. The immediate pushback from Cook, asserting her intention to serve the remainder of her 14-year term, sets the stage for a potentially ugly legal and political battle. This is not just about one individual's job; it's a frontal assault on the Federal Reserve's autonomy and integrity.

The Dire Warnings of Bill Dudley

Bill Dudley, a former heavyweight at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, has sounded the alarm. Dudley's prediction of "standoffs, showdowns, chaos and uncertainty" should Cook be dismissed, is not hyperbole. It's a grounded warning from someone intimately familiar with the delicate balance of power within the Federal Reserve System. Dudley’s op-ed paints a truly dystopian picture of what could unfold — a scenario where political interference morphs the Federal Reserve into an instrument of presidential will.

The Stakes Could Not Be Higher

Dudley highlights a particularly harrowing prospect: Trump appointing four of the seven Federal Reserve governors. This would not only shift the balance of power but could fundamentally alter the Federal Reserve's decision-making processes. The potential manipulation of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) to achieve specific economic outcomes desired by Trump — notably, significant interest rate cuts — could undermine the Fed's mission to address inflation and employment without political interference.

A Feud with Far-Reaching Implications

Trump's ongoing feud with the Federal Reserve, particularly his desire for lower interest rates and his flirtation with firing Jerome Powell, the agency’s chair, underscores a dangerous penchant for undermining institutional independence for perceived personal or political gains. The implications of such actions extend far beyond mere policy disagreements; they threaten to destabilize the U.S. economy by injecting uncertainty into financial markets, risking uncontrolled inflation, skyrocketing long-term borrowing costs, and a weakened dollar.

Conclusion: A Threat to the Fed’s Independence

Dudley's closing thoughts on the risks to the Fed's independence and the potential economic fallout should be a wake-up call. The independence of the Federal Reserve is a cornerstone of the American economic system, safeguarding it from political whims and ensuring policy decisions are made based on economic data and analysis rather than political expediency.

Trump’s attempt to remove Cook, regardless of the outcome, is already disruptive. If successful, it could set a dangerous precedent that endangers not just the Federal Reserve's independence, but the economic stability of the United States itself. The situation demands close scrutiny and robust opposition from all who value the principles of economic stability and institutional integrity.
