‘He is not in charge’: Trump mocked for asking what’s in his executive orders

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 25, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Executive Order Spectacle: Leadership or Performance Art?

Maria Korsnick, President and CEO, Nuclear Energy Institute (NEI), speaks in the Oval Office as he attends the signing of executive orders by U.S. President Donald Trump, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump has signed more than 150 executive orders, often with cameras rolling and staff looking on. The ritual rarely varies: seated at the desk in the Oval Office, the President listens as someone—typically the White House Staff Secretary—reads a brief summary of the order. On occasion, Trump interjects with a question, prompting speculation that he may not be fully familiar with the contents. He is seldom seen fully reading the orders themselves, which can span anywhere from a few pages to nearly 70.

On Friday, President Trump signed several executive orders, but according to The Daily Beast, one particularly revealing moment suggested he may not have known what he was signing—describing it as “a telling moment” that implied the president hadn’t read the order.

EXCLUSIVE: Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

“Are we doing something about the regulatory in here?” Trump asked a business person attending the event.

“Several business leaders standing around him were quick to chime in that his order did address the regulations while Interior Secretary Doug Burgum also responded, ‘You are, sir,'” The Daily Beast also reported.

At the end, Trump asked, “Is that it?” and one of the attendees replied, “That’s all we have for you now, sir.”

Then, rather than asking if there were any questions for him about the executive orders, Trump asked if anyone had any questions for the guests in the room, whom he called “brilliant.”

Critics blasted the President.

Fred Wellman is a graduate of West Point and the Harvard Kennedy School, an Army veteran of 22 years who served four combat tours, and a political consultant.

“He is not in charge,” Wellman alleged.

MSNBC columnist Michael A. Cohen snarked, “It’s almost as if Trump has cognitive deficiencies, which from what I hear on CNN is a major scandal.”

“’Is that it?’ while signing orders he doesn’t understand, parroting talking points he didn’t write, and pretending it’s leadership,” wrote investment banker Evaristus Odinikaeze. “Peak performative confusion.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Executive Order Spectacle: Leadership or Performance Art?

The latest escapades from the Oval Office have once again tossed President Donald Trump into the limelight, not for his policy acumen, but for what appears to be a cavalier disinterest in the details of governance. Trump, having signed more than 150 executive orders with the pomp and circumstance befitting a reality TV show, has turned what should be a considered and meticulous process into what can only be described as a spectacle of ignorance.

The Daily Beast has thrown a glaring spotlight on a moment that should give every American pause. During a recent executive order signing, Trump's inquiry, “Are we doing something about the regulatory in here?” was not just a question; it was a revelation. This moment, captured for posterity, suggests a president who signs documents of national importance with less familiarity than one might give a takeout menu.

Critics are quick to point out the farce that this administration has become. Fred Wellman, an Army veteran and political consultant, cuts to the heart of the matter stating, “He is not in charge.” It's a sentiment echoed across the spectrum, highlighting a frightening reality where the person at the helm of the most powerful country in the world may not fully understand or even care about the contents of the documents his hand waves over.

The critics aren’t just whispering in the corridors of power; they’re shouting from the rooftops. Michael A. Cohen, an MSNBC columnist, brings to light concerns over Trump's cognitive abilities, or lack thereof, suggesting an incompetence that veers into the realm of scandal. Investment banker Evaristus Odinikaeze’s remark on Trump’s “Peak performative confusion” is not just a witty jab; it’s a scathing indictment of a leader more interested in the performance of power than its responsibilities.

The evidence, as laid out in the article, paints a picture of a presidency that is anything but normal. The fact that Trump needs his executive orders explained to him in real-time, on camera, is not just embarrassing; it's downright dangerous. This is not leadership; it’s a pantomime of governance where the script is as unknown to the lead actor as it is to the audience.

What's Next?

As observers and critics alike watch these events unfold, the question becomes, what does this mean for the future of American democracy? When the president treats executive orders with the same level of engagement as a photo op, the implications are far-reaching. It's not just a question of ineffective governance; it's a matter of national security, global standing, and the very fabric of the democratic process.

In a world where leadership demands not just decisiveness but an informed and thoughtful approach, the spectacle of ignorance on display is more than just a political gaffe; it's a systemic failure. The responsibility of leadership is understanding the weight of the decisions one makes. If the president is indeed signing documents of monumental importance with little to no understanding of their content, then the system has not just erred; it has fundamentally failed.

The farce playing out in the Oval Office, with its ritualistic signing and performative questions, is a symptom of a larger problem. It's a stark reminder of the importance of not just electing leaders but holding them to account. As the curtain falls on each of these performances, the audience is left wondering: Is this the pinnacle of American leadership, or is it a warning sign of its decline? The answer, while still unfolding, is a narrative that demands not just our attention but our action.
