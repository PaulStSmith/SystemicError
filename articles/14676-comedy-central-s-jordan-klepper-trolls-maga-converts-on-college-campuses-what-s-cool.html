Comedy Central's Jordan Klepper trolls MAGA converts on college campuses: 'What's cool?'

The comedian has been confronting older Trump fans outside MAGA rallies for the better part of a decade, but his new special, "Jordan Klepper Fingers the Pulse — MAGA: The Next Generation," focuses on younger MAGA supporters and the influences that have drawn them rightward, reported The Daily Beast.

“I think there’s a ‘cool’ vacuum that confident, thirsty, influencer types jump into with bravado and promise answers to a generation desperately in search of guidance,” Klepper told the outlet. “This space is political, yes, but it’s mostly cultural. Young men want community, answers, and insight like everyone else, and the left shouldn’t be afraid to enter into those spaces.”

In a promotional clip, the 46-year-old Klepper admits to being out of touch with the young men he interviews, who think being “woke” is “annoying” and the “Gulf of America” is “cool.”

“Clearly I need to better understand these kids,” he says in the clip. “So, much like when I was in college, can somebody please tell me what’s cool?”

“For the most part,” he said, the young MAGA men he spoke with were “open and curious, looking to find themselves and their tribe,” adding, “there’s no shortage of groups and influencers trying to pounce on that vulnerability.”

Klepper did find common ground in Martin Scorsese's "Wolf of Wall Street" with one man wearing a MAGA hat, but he realized they might have liked the film for different reasons.

“Is it because we understand it’s a satire or we just like the financial crisis of it all?” Klepper asked, but the MAGA fan said he liked how much the main character was “able to get away with.”

“Oh, so you see it as a hero’s journey,” Klepper said. “Good to know.”

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Quest for Cool: Klepper's Exploration of Young MAGA Minds

Jordan Klepper's latest comedic venture, "Jordan Klepper Fingers the Pulse — MAGA: The Next Generation," seeks to shed light on a puzzling phenomenon: the magnetic pull of Donald Trump's brand of politics on young men. The Daily Beast's coverage of this special offers a glimpse into Klepper's quest, which, while draped in humor, inadvertently exposes the dire straits of American political and cultural discourse.

The Cool Factor: A Misnomer in Politics

Klepper's assertion that there is a "‘cool’ vacuum" being exploited by "confident, thirsty, influencer types" is an astute observation of the current political landscape. However, the characterization of this trend as something new or unique to the right wing is where Klepper, and indeed The Daily Beast's report, misses the mark. The allure of perceived coolness has long been a tool wielded by political movements across the spectrum. The true issue at hand is not the existence of this vacuum but the content that fills it. The fact that young men find the "Gulf of America" to be "cool" and are annoyed by being "woke" speaks volumes about the failures of our educational and societal structures to instill a deeper understanding of complex issues.

The Search for Community and Answers

Klepper's acknowledgment that young men are "open and curious, looking to find themselves and their tribe" is perhaps the most crucial insight of his exploration. This quest for belonging is a universal one, yet the paths to fulfillment are fraught with manipulation by those with their own agendas. The irony of finding common ground in a movie like The Wolf of Wall Street — a satire that critiques greed and excess, which some may mistakenly admire for its portrayal of financial success at any cost — is a perfect microcosm of the broader misunderstanding fueling the MAGA movement's appeal among the youth.

The Satirical Misstep

While Klepper's venture is commendable for its attempt to bridge understanding, it ultimately falls into the trap of many satirical endeavors: it scratches the surface without offering solutions. To label the engagement with young MAGA supporters as purely a quest to find what's "cool" is to trivialize the deeper issues at play. These include systemic failures in education, the impact of social media on impressionable minds, and the profound sense of disenfranchisement felt by many young people today.

Conclusion: Beyond the Laughter

In MAGA: The Next Generation, Klepper shines a light on the symptoms of a much larger disease. While his approach brings laughter and fleeting moments of insight, it fails to address the root causes of the phenomenon he seeks to understand. The left's inability to penetrate these spaces, as Klepper notes, is not a matter of fear but perhaps a failure to communicate effectively with those who feel left behind by rapid social and economic changes.

The true takeaway from Klepper's special should not be a smug sense of superiority or a dismissal of young MAGA supporters as merely chasing what's "cool." Instead, it should serve as a wake-up call to all sides of the political spectrum: the battle for the hearts and minds of the next generation will not be won through mockery or disdain but through genuine engagement, empathy, and the offering of viable alternatives to the divisive rhetoric that currently dominates our discourse.
