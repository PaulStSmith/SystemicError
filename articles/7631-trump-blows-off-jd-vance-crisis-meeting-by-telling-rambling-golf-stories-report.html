Trump blows off JD Vance crisis meeting by telling rambling golf stories: report

President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance speak after announcing the 2026 FIFA World Cup draw will be held at the Kennedy Center in December 2025, in the Oval Office of the White House in Washington, August 22, 2025. (Via Annabelle Gordon on Shutterstock)

Vice President JD Vance arranged a White House luncheon with conservative pollster Mark Mitchell in hopes of convincing Donald Trump that the Republican Party faces significant losses in the 2026 midterm elections. Instead, the president proved largely disengaged, preferring to discuss his golf game rather than address mounting political concerns.

According to the Washington Post, Vance, whose political fortunes are directly tied to Trump’s second-term success, invited Mitchell to present data on declining voter support. Mitchell discovered Trump was unresponsive to substantive discussion about the economy and the deteriorating relationship with his MAGA base.

Mitchell told Trump, “You said, ‘Fight, fight, fight.’ But nobody ever clarified what that means. And right now, you’re fight-fight-fighting Marjorie Taylor Greene, and not actually fight-fight-fighting for Americans.“

The Post reported that while Trump initially listened and asked questions, he eventually steered the conversation toward golf, one of his preferred topics. “Trump gushed about two of his golf partners, Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) and Fox News host Bret Baier, both of whom are the subject of MAGA-faction ire. Trump also bragged about how much money he had raised during a golf fundraiser for Graham the weekend before,” according to the report.

Mitchell, who follows Vance on X and has discussed polling with the vice president in recent months, expressed frustration about the president’s priorities in an interview with the Post. He argued that Trump’s focus on wealth accumulation undermines his political standing.

“Building billionaire-funded ballrooms and jet-setting around the world and trillion-dollar investment deals looks a lot like oligarchy stuff,” Mitchell explained.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Disengagement: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In an era where political engagement is more crucial than ever, the recent report by the Washington Post unveils a disheartening scenario: Vice President JD Vance’s fruitless attempt to draw President Donald Trump’s attention to the looming disaster for the Republican Party in the 2026 midterm elections. Despite Vance’s strategic move to involve conservative pollster Mark Mitchell, Trump’s disinterest paints a grim picture of a leader more captivated by the leisure of golf than the pressing needs of his party and, by extension, the nation.

The Irony of “Fight, Fight, Fight”

Mitchell’s plea to the president is emblematic of the broader disillusionment within the MAGA base. “You said, ‘Fight, fight, fight.’ But nobody ever clarified what that means,” Mitchell lamented, hitting the nail on the head. This statement encapsulates the vacuous nature of Trump’s rallying cries, which are increasingly seen as devoid of substance. The irony is as palpable as it is tragic: Trump’s belligerence is misdirected towards internal squabbles with figures like Marjorie Taylor Greene rather than addressing the substantive issues facing Americans.

Golf Over Governance

The anecdote of Trump’s diversion to discussing golf during a critical meeting is not just a minor faux pas; it’s symptomatic of a leader who prioritizes personal indulgence over political responsibility. The fact that Trump “gushed” over golf partners like Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) and Fox News host Bret Baier—both controversial figures within the MAGA faction—reveals a disturbing disconnect from the political realities and challenges that his party faces.

Trump’s Oligarchic Tendencies

Mitchell’s criticism of Trump’s focus on “building billionaire-funded ballrooms and jet-setting around the world” is a damning indictment of the president’s oligarchic tendencies. The description of Trump’s lifestyle and priorities as “oligarchy stuff” is a stark reminder of the widening chasm between his proclaimed populist image and his actual engagements and interests. This disparity not only undermines Trump’s political standing but also raises questions about the authenticity and integrity of his leadership.

Conclusion: A Call for Reevaluation

The revelations from the Washington Post’s report are a wake-up call for the Republican Party and its base. The disengagement and misplaced priorities of its leader demand a critical reevaluation of the direction in which the party is headed. If the GOP hopes to avert the significant losses predicted for the 2026 midterm elections, it must confront the reality of Trump’s leadership—or lack thereof.

As the political landscape continues to evolve, the Republican Party finds itself at a crossroads. Will it cling to the coattails of a leader who seems increasingly detached from the realities of governance and the needs of the American people? Or will it seek a new path that aligns more closely with the principles and priorities it claims to champion? Only time will tell, but the choices made today will undoubtedly shape the political terrain for years to come.
