Vice President JD Vance’s ongoing vacation in England took an “embarrassing turn on the golf course” on Friday after he was captured on camera struggling to sink his shot several times in a row on the putting green.

“Having travelled to Scotland to play Trump Turnberry – his boss’s course – Mr. Vance might well have wished he was sealed off from the rest of the planet as a painful four-putt was caught on camera,” reads a story published Friday in the British newspaper The Telegraph.

Vance is currently enjoying a 10-day stay at an 18th-century luxury manor house in Dean, a small community in Southern England, which he rents for nearly $11,000 a week. He’s described the trip as both a vacation and a diplomatic visit, having already met with David Lammy, UK Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs, to fish and discuss the war in Ukraine.

The taxpayer-funded trip has already been fraught with controversy as Vance’s security detail has reportedly harassed locals and disrupted daily life for longtime residents. And now, Vance appears to have drawn further scrutiny – at least from golf enthusiasts – for his performance on the green.

“JD Vance’s UK holiday has been nothing if not eventful, but it took an embarrassing turn on the golf course with an excruciating four-putt,” The Telegraph wrote.

Vance was playing at Trump Turnberry, a golf resort in Scotland owned by the Trump Organization since 2014, and just one of 17 golf resorts and clubs owned by the organization worldwide. His trip comes just weeks after President Donald Trump played on the same course during his visit to Scotland, a trip estimated to cost taxpayers $10 million and described by critics as a marketing campaign.

Our Analysis:

Vance's Vacation Ventures: A Taxpayer-Funded Fiasco?

In what can only be described as a spectacle of privilege, Vice President JD Vance's so-called "diplomatic visit" to England, which doubles as a luxury vacation, has spiraled into a series of embarrassing and controversial moments. The latest? His painful performance on the golf course, which, in the grand scheme of things, is perhaps the least of the issues but emblematic of the broader farce.

The Context: Vance's trip, costing nearly $11,000 a week for his stay at an 18th-century luxury manor, is described as both a vacation and a diplomatic visit. The Vice President has been busy, not with matters of state, but struggling on the putting green at Trump Turnberry, a luxury golf resort owned by none other than the Trump Organization. This event is not just a blip of personal embarrassment but highlights a disturbing trend of using taxpayer dollars for thinly veiled personal indulgences.

The Irony: Vance's golf woes, captured for public amusement, occurred on a course owned by his boss, former President Donald Trump, a man no stranger to controversy himself, especially when it comes to blurring the lines between state matters and personal business. Trump's own visit to Scotland, labeled as a marketing campaign by critics, reportedly drained $10 million from taxpayer coffers.

The Real Issue: Beyond the schadenfreude of Vance's golfing gaffes lies a more sinister narrative. The trip, funded by the taxpayer, has been mired in controversy, from allegations of Vance's security detail harassing locals to the disruption of daily life in a small community. Furthermore, the justification of the trip as a diplomatic venture seems flimsy at best, given the scant outcomes beyond a fishing trip with the UK Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs, David Lammy, to discuss the war in Ukraine.

The Bottom Line: JD Vance's UK holiday is a microcosm of a larger, systemic issue where public officials appear to leverage their positions for personal gain, under the guise of state business. The real embarrassment isn't Vance's struggle on the golf course; it's the blatant misuse of taxpayer money and the lack of accountability in public office. It's high time we demand more transparency and integrity from our leaders, ensuring that their travels and endeavors are genuinely in the public interest, not just an excuse for a lavish getaway on the public's dime.
