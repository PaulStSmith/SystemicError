Families are dumping MAGA relatives —and they're not coming back

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosJuly 10, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Who Chose ThisCruelty Is the PolicyICE Is the InstrumentThe MisdirectionWhat the Silence RevealsThe Pattern

The Rift Is Not Family Drama. It’s Consent for State Violence.

At the level of the source, the story is simple: holiday gatherings are breaking under the weight of Trump-era politics, and people who backed this administration are shocked that their relatives no longer want to play nice. But the actual subject is not awkward barbecues. It is the moral and institutional break created when voters handed power to people who promised cruelty and then delivered it.

Who Chose This

The people with power here are not the relatives being cut off. They are Trump, Stephen Miller, the Heritage ecosystem, and the voters who knew exactly what they were endorsing in 2024. This was not a misunderstanding about egg prices or a vague protest vote against “the system.” The source names the record plainly: racist campaign language, explicit deportation planning, and a White House that is now carrying out the project at scale.

That matters because responsibility sits where the authority sits. Once someone votes for a candidate after hearing the threats, the evasions stop being plausible. The later claim of surprise is not innocence. It is retrospective cover.

Cruelty Is the Policy

The article’s strongest point is that the administration’s cruelty is not accidental fallout. It is the design. More than 675,000 deportations, a swollen ICE detention population, and deaths in custody are not the symptoms of a system under strain. They are the predictable output of a machine built to punish and disappear people.

The administration’s own defense is obscene in its precision: reduce human suffering to a percentage and call that exoneration. That is not governance. It is administrative numbness. It treats death in detention as an acceptable rounding error, which is exactly how authoritarian systems normalize themselves.

ICE Is the Instrument

The story keeps returning to ICE because ICE is the instrument. Not a broken agency in need of better training. Not a few bad actors. A detention regime. A deportation regime. A domestic coercion apparatus that expands when the state decides certain people can be treated as disposable.

That is why “reform” is the wrong frame. Oversight does not fix a machine that exists to cage, separate, and remove. If the political order has reached the point where detention deaths are accelerating and mass removal is the governing logic, then the only honest democratic position is abolition. Anything less accepts the premise that the state gets to maintain a captive population and merely make the cages less visible.

The Misdirection

The right wants this to look like a cultural tantrum, as if queer children, mixed-status families, and immigrant communities are being unreasonable for refusing intimacy with people who voted for their disappearance. That framing is propaganda. It shifts attention from the people ordering the harm to the people naming it.

The source also exposes a second lie: the economic one. The administration says immigrants are “draining” the country, but the facts cited here point the other way. Undocumented immigrants pay billions in taxes. Deportation does not restore civic health; it strips revenue from the very communities that will be forced to live with the consequences. The cruelty is not an accident of bad arithmetic. The arithmetic is the excuse.

What the Silence Reveals

The deeper pattern is not just fascistic policy. It is social cowardice meeting state power. When relatives say, “It’s just politics,” they are asking others to absorb the cost of their vote so they can preserve a fantasy of personal decency. They want the benefits of belonging without the burden of accountability.

That is the real rupture. People are not “being petty” when they withdraw from those who enabled this. They are recognizing that the relationship changed the moment power was used against them and their neighbors. The people mourning family estrangement should be honest about causation: the break did not begin at Thanksgiving. It began at the ballot box, and it hardened every time they defended what they had chosen.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics works in the United States: first, normalize the threat; then, monetize the fear; then, build the detention capacity; then, tell the victims they are overreacting. The final move is always the same. The powerful demand emotional reconciliation after they have already institutionalized harm.

The democratic response cannot be sentimental. It has to be organized, specific, and willing to name the machinery for what it is. The immediate task is to stop the people running it. The larger task is to make sure no future administration can so easily turn immigration enforcement into a white supremacist project and call it public order.
