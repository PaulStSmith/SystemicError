President Donald Trump wants to keep former special counsel Jack Smith’s final report classified, according to a new court filing Tuesday.

In the filing from his personal lawyer, the president told U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon — a judge he appointed and his first direct request of her — to continue an extension on an 11-month order to block the Justice Department from sharing the report, Politico reported.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Legal Maneuvers: A Masterclass in Obfuscation

In an unsurprising twist that would make even the most seasoned of legal acrobats dizzy, President Donald Trump has once again made a bold play in the high-stakes game of keeping his skeletons firmly locked in the closet. This time, his target is the final report by former special counsel Jack Smith, a document Trump is desperate to keep out of the public eye, as revealed in a recent court filing.

The Art of Secrecy

Trump’s request, channeled through his personal legal team, was directed at U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon—a notable choice given she is a Trump appointee. This is not just any request; it’s his first direct plea to her, aiming to extend an 11-month-long gag order that prevents the Justice Department from releasing Smith’s report. This report, submitted right before Trump’s second inauguration, is rumored to detail the criminal case against him for hoarding classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago residence, with locations as unassuming as a bathroom serving as makeshift archives.

Breaking Tradition

What stands out here is not just the audacity of attempting to keep critical information from public scrutiny, but the stark deviation from standard practice. Historically, special counsel reports—especially those detailing findings against the incumbent administration—are shared with Congress and released to the public. This transparency, albeit sometimes grudging, has been a cornerstone of the Justice Department’s protocol, even when findings have been less than flattering for the administration in question. Yet, Trump’s latest maneuver seeks to shatter this tradition, favoring opacity over openness.

Disdain and Dismissal

Trump’s filing is reportedly laced with the same disdain he has habitually shown towards those who dare to investigate him. Labeling Jack Smith a “so-called special counsel” and accusing the case of being “marred by numerous deficiencies and repeated abuses of office,” Trump’s narrative is clear: discredit the investigator, delegitimize the investigation. This tactic is as transparent as it is tired, reflecting a consistent strategy to undermine the legal and procedural norms that govern the country’s justice system.

The Implications

Trump’s relentless quest to keep Smith’s report under wraps is more than a personal vendetta against a prosecutor or a desperate attempt to protect his image. It’s a litmus test for the judiciary’s independence and the public’s right to know. By appealing to a judge he appointed, Trump is not just seeking legal relief; he’s leveraging his presidential appointments for personal gain, blurring the lines between the executive and the judiciary.

Furthermore, the case underscores a worrying trend of eroding transparency in the administration of justice. If successful, Trump’s efforts could set a dangerous precedent, where accountability is optional, and transparency is available only at the discretion of those in power.

Conclusion

In his quest to keep the Jack Smith report classified, Donald Trump is not just fighting to control the narrative but to redefine the boundaries of presidential power and accountability. This move, emblematic of his tenure, challenges not only the norms of transparency and openness but the very principles of justice and democracy.

As this saga unfolds, one must question: what is in Jack Smith’s report that is so damaging, so incriminating, that it warrants such desperate measures to keep it hidden? The answer, it seems, is a truth that Trump believes the public cannot handle—or, more accurately, a truth he cannot afford to let out.
