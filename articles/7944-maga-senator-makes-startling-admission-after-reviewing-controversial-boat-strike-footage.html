MAGA senator makes startling admission after reviewing controversial boat strike footage

Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR) made a startling admission Sunday on NBC News that at no time during his briefings on the potentially “illegal” strike last month on a suspected drug-carrying sea vessel was he provided proof that the boat was actually headed toward the United States.

According to a whistleblower, the Trump administration approved a follow-up strike on a boat last month in an effort to kill its survivors, who were observed clinging to the wreckage, which, if accurate, would likely constitute a war crime.

Cotton has received a private briefing on the strike, which included video of the incident. On Sunday, however, he may have potentially undercut one of the Trump administration’s potential justifications for the strike, that the boat was deemed an imminent threat.

“Senator, is there any hard evidence that shows that this particular boat was headed to the United States?” asked NBC News’ Kristen Welker.

“That didn’t come up in my briefing,” Cotton admitted. “But again, there’s very reliable multiple sources of intelligence that tells us that this boat had drugs on it, that everyone on that boat is associated with these designated terrorist organizations that are trying to kill American children.”

President Donald Trump signed an executive order in February to designate drug cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations, a designation that gives the administration far more leeway in targeting suspected drug traffickers with lethal force. Even so, targeting enemy combatants who pose no imminent threat and are surrendering would still constitute a war crime in most cases, regardless of the combatants designation.

Given as much, Welker pressed Cotton on the matter, asking if he was “comfortable” with the Trump administration targeting a boat that he hadn’t seen evidence proving it was “actually heading to the United States.”

“Any boat loaded with drugs that is crewed by associates and members of Foreign Terrorist Organizations that are trying to kill American kids, I think is a valid target,” Cotton said. “I’m not just comfortable with it, I want to continue it.

Our Analysis:

The Thin Line Between Defense and War Crimes: A Dissection of Senator Cotton’s Statements

In a recent appearance on NBC News, Senator Tom Cotton (R-AR) made statements that not only raise eyebrows but also alarm bells regarding the ethical and legal standards of military engagement under the Trump administration. Let’s dissect the layers of this troubling narrative with the precision of a scalpel, revealing the septic wounds of justification beneath.

The Murky Waters of Justification

Senator Cotton’s admission that he received no proof of the targeted boat’s destination during briefings is a glaring red flag. The boat, allegedly carrying drugs and associated with terrorist organizations, was struck in what has been described as a potentially “illegal” action last month. This strike, and the subsequent approval of a follow-up strike to kill survivors clinging to wreckage, dances dangerously close to, if not directly over, the line of what constitutes a war crime.

The Designation Dilemma

The Trump administration’s decision to designate drug cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations (FTOs) serves as a double-edged sword. On one hand, it grants the administration “far more leeway in targeting suspected drug traffickers with lethal force.” However, wielding this sword without the utmost care and precision sets a dangerous precedent. The notion that any vessel “loaded with drugs” and crewed by individuals linked to FTOs is a “valid target,” as Cotton puts it, without concrete evidence of an imminent threat, especially when it involves targeting individuals in a state of surrender, is not only morally reprehensible but likely violates international law.

A Dangerous Precedent

Cotton’s comfort with and endorsement of these actions under the guise of protecting American children from drugs and terrorism is a simplistic and dangerous justification for potentially unlawful military engagement. The leap from protecting citizens to authorizing strikes without concrete evidence, and on individuals in distress, represents a troubling erosion of ethical and legal standards.

The use of lethal force in international relations must be guided by clear evidence, strict adherence to legal standards, and an unwavering moral compass. When actions are taken based on vague intelligence or broad designations, without solid evidence of an imminent threat, they undermine international law and moral standards.

Senator Cotton’s statements, and the actions of the Trump administration he defends, should not be viewed in isolation but as symptoms of a larger, systemic issue. This approach to foreign policy and military engagement, marked by a cavalier attitude towards the use of force and a troubling interpretation of legal and ethical boundaries, demands rigorous scrutiny and immediate reassessment.

In summary, the narrative presented by Senator Cotton not only underscores a potentially alarming disregard for international law but also highlights a fundamental flaw in the administration’s approach to national security and foreign engagement. The road to safety and security is not paved with indiscriminate strikes and loose justifications but with principled actions grounded in legality and morality. Without this foundation, we are not safeguarding our nation but endangering it further, blurring the lines between defense and war crimes.
