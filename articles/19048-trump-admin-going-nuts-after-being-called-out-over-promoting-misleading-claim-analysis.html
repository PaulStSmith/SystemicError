The White House lashed out Friday after being called out over its misleading suggestion that the Iran war enjoyed majority support among Americans, and to such an extent that one target of the White House’s fury hit back with a particularly scathing rebuttal.

On Thursday, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt claimed that “Americans agree that Operation Epic Fury is an overwhelming success,” sharing a White House press release that included several polls showing the war’s favorability. As countless critics noted, however, the White House’s release only focused on “MAGA Republicans,” who make up less than 20% of Americans.

Among those critics was former MSNBC host Mehdi Hasan, who called Leavitt a “proud and open liar” for her misleading claim.

In response, “Rapid Response 47,” a political communications account affiliated with President Donald Trump’s political team, lashed out at Mehdi on Friday, calling him a “moron” who was “trembling at the idea that Americans support killing terrorists.”

And on Sunday, Mehdi hit back at Trump’s political team and the White House for “going nuts” over critics “telling the truth about their dumb war” in a scathing rebuttal published in Zeteo.

“The Iran war – started by Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu – is now entering its fourth week. It has been an unmitigated disaster from the beginning, and it’s strangling the global economy,” Hasan wrote.

“The president appears to be preparing to deploy US ground troops to Iran, for a mission that looks like sheer suicide. As we’ve reported at Zeteo, the war is starting to tear apart the MAGA elite, and is already a political catastrophe at home for Trump and the Republican Party – and they know it. And America’s allies have decided not to join us on this so-called ‘excursion.’”

Our Analysis:

The Misleading War of Words: Unpacking the Iran War Support Claims

In the recent uproar surrounding the White House’s claims about American support for the Iran war, a critical examination reveals not only the nuances of political messaging but also the deeper issues of responsibility and accountability in governance and media portrayal.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

The primary actors in this scenario are the White House, under President Donald Trump’s administration, and its affiliated political communications team, “Rapid Response 47.” It’s crucial to note that these entities possess significant institutional power, dictating national security policies and controlling the narrative around such decisions. Additionally, the involvement of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu points to an international coalition at the helm of initiating the Iran war, thereby sharing this institutional power.

Decision Makers and Outcomes

The decision to start the Iran war, as reported, was made jointly by President Trump and Prime Minister Netanyahu. This critical choice has led to what has been described as “an unmitigated disaster,” affecting not only the involved nations but also the global economy and political landscape. The White House’s attempt to paint the operation as a success through selective polling data spotlights a deliberate decision to mislead the public about the war’s actual reception and impact.

Misdirection of Responsibility

The article highlights a stark misdirection of responsibility by focusing on the White House’s misleading claim of widespread support for the war among Americans. By selectively citing favorability from a minority (MAGA Republicans), the administration attempts to construct a narrative of majority approval, blatantly ignoring broader public opinion that opposes the continuation of military strikes. This tactic not only misleads the public but also unfairly targets critics like Mehdi Hasan, who challenge the administration’s portrayal with factual counterarguments. The personal attacks against Hasan by “Rapid Response 47” further exemplify an attempt to deflect criticism and silence dissenting voices, rather than addressing the substance of the critique.

Analysis

In this context, it’s evident that the primary responsibility for both the decision to wage war and the subsequent portrayal of its support lies with President Trump, his administration, and allied political figures. The framing of the article, while highlighting these misleading tactics, rightly places the onus on the administration’s deliberate choice to misrepresent public sentiment.

However, it’s also essential to acknowledge the role of media and critics in dissecting and exposing these misrepresentations. Hasan’s rebuttal, as mentioned in the article, serves as a crucial counter-narrative that challenges the administration’s claims, providing a more accurate reflection of public opinion and the war’s ramifications.

Conclusion

The controversy surrounding the White House’s claims about the Iran war support underscores the importance of scrutinizing the narratives presented by those in power. It’s a stark reminder that in the realm of political communication, the truth can often become a casualty of war. As such, it is the responsibility of both the media and the public to critically evaluate these narratives, ensuring that accountability and transparency prevail over misinformation and propaganda.

In this case, while the White House and its affiliates attempt to tout the success of a highly contentious and potentially catastrophic military venture, the reality—backed by broader public opinion and the tangible outcomes of the war—tells a markedly different story. Thus, the critique of the administration’s misleading claims is not only justified but necessary in holding those with institutional power to account.
