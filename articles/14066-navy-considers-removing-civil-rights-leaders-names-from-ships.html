Navy considers removing civil rights leaders' names from ships

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Scathing Review of the Trump Administration's Plan to Rename Navy ShipsErasure Under the Guise of "Warrior Culture"Reactions and RamificationsA Call to Action

In its continued attempt to erase diversity, equity, and inclusion from U.S. history, the Trump administration is planning to rename several navy ships currently titled for civil rights leaders like Harvey Milk, Thurgood Marshall and Harriet Tubman, among others, CBS News reported.

The first ship to be renamed, according to documents reviewed by CBS, is the USNS Harvey Milk, which honors the navy veteran and the first openly gay politician in California's history. Milk was assassinated while serving on San Francisco's board of supervisors in 1978 and became an LGBTQ icon.

CBS News noted that the proposed timeline for renaming the ship comes during Pride Month, "the monthlong observance of the LGBTQ+ community that also coincides with the anniversary of the Stonewall uprising of 1969."

Also on the Navy's renaming "recommended list" are the USNS Thurgood Marshall, USNS Ruth Bader Ginsburg, USNS Harriet Tubman, USNS Dolores Huerta, USNS Lucy Stone, USNS Cesar Chavez and USNS Medgar Evers.

CBS News also found "that a December 2024 web article from Naval Sea Systems Command about the laying of the keel for the future USNS Thurgood Marshall has been deleted."

Speaker Emerita Nancy Pelosi told CBS News in statement, "The reported decision by the Trump Administration to change the names of the USNS Harvey Milk and other ships in the John Lewis-class is a shameful, vindictive erasure of those who fought to break down barriers for all to chase the American Dream."

She added, "Our military is the most powerful in the world – but this spiteful move does not strengthen our national security or the "warrior" ethos. Instead, it is a surrender of a fundamental American value: to honor the legacy of those who worked to build a better country."

CBS cited the navy memo that said "the renaming of naval ships was to realign the U.S. military with Trump administration priorities of 'reestablishing the warrior culture.'"

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Review of the Trump Administration's Plan to Rename Navy Ships

In an audacious display of historical revisionism, the Trump administration has set its sights on erasing symbols of diversity, equity, and inclusion from the annals of U.S. history. According to a report by CBS News, the administration plans to rename several Navy ships that currently honor civil rights leaders and trailblazers, including Harvey Milk, Thurgood Marshall, and Harriet Tubman, among others. This move is not just a slap in the face to the legacy of these individuals but a blatant attempt to whitewash the diverse and complex fabric of American history.

Erasure Under the Guise of "Warrior Culture"

The first ship on the chopping block, the USNS Harvey Milk, pays homage to a Navy veteran and the first openly gay politician in California's history. Milk's assassination in 1978 made him a martyr for the LGBTQ+ community, symbolizing the fight for equality and acceptance. The Trump administration's decision to rename this ship during Pride Month, of all times, is a calculated insult to the LGBTQ+ community and a gross neglect of Milk's contributions and sacrifices.

The list of ships slated for renaming reads like a who's who of American civil rights and social justice champions: Thurgood Marshall, Ruth Bader Ginsburg, Harriet Tubman, Dolores Huerta, Lucy Stone, Cesar Chavez, and Medgar Evers. Each of these figures played a pivotal role in challenging and dismantling systemic barriers in pursuit of a more equitable society. Their recognition on Navy ships serves as a powerful reminder of our nation's ongoing struggle towards inclusivity and justice.

CBS News' discovery of the deletion of a December 2024 web article from Naval Sea Systems Command about the laying of the keel for the future USNS Thurgood Marshall adds a layer of secrecy and subterfuge to the whole affair. It suggests an attempt to quietly rewrite history without public scrutiny or opposition.

Reactions and Ramifications

Speaker Emerita Nancy Pelosi's condemnation of this decision as a "shameful, vindictive erasure" hits the nail on the head. This move by the Trump administration does nothing to enhance our national security or bolster the morale of our military personnel. Instead, it represents a capitulation to the narrowest and most regressive elements of American society. It is a rejection of the fundamental American value of honoring those who have strived to make this country a better, more inclusive place.

The justification for these renamings, to "realign the U.S. military with Trump administration priorities of 'reestablishing the warrior culture,'" is both laughable and disturbing. It implies that acknowledging and celebrating diversity somehow weakens our military's warrior ethos, a notion that is as baseless as it is bigoted. The strength of our military—and our nation—lies in its diversity and its ability to honor and learn from the experiences of all its members.

A Call to Action

This decision must not go unchallenged. It is a deliberate attempt to erase the very progress and pluralism that define the best of America. We must resist this and any attempt to rewrite our history to suit the agendas of those who seek to divide us. Our diversity is our strength, and the legacy of those who fought for justice and equality must be preserved and celebrated, not discarded to satisfy the whims of an administration hell-bent on promoting a monolithic and exclusionary vision of America.

In the end, this move by the Trump administration is not just about renaming ships. It's about who we are as a nation and what we stand for. Let's stand on the right side of history and fight for an America that honors all its heroes, not just those who fit a particular narrative of heroism.
