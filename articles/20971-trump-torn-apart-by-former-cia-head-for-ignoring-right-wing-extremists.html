Trump torn apart by former CIA head for ignoring right-wing extremists

The State Is Being Retasked for Revenge

Who Holds the Levers

The source is not really about one former CIA director warning about a bad mood in Washington. It is about an administration with actual institutional power redirecting intelligence and counterterrorism capacity toward political aims. Brennan’s complaint is blunt: the government is pulling professionals away from genuine threats and toward undocumented workers and invented left-wing specters.

That matters because the people making these choices are not marginal. Trump and his appointees control the machinery that decides what the intelligence state looks at, what it ignores, and who gets labeled a threat.

The Target Is Not Safety

The administration’s reported logic is political, not protective. Brennan says counterterrorism resources are being shifted away from the groups that actually worry national security professionals and toward “undocumented workers” and the vague construction of “Antifa.” That is not a security strategy. It is a domestic enemy search.

The point is not that the state has discovered new danger. The point is that it has decided which populations are convenient to police and which threats are inconvenient to confront.

Manufactured Panic, Deliberate Blindness

The framing in the article muddies this on purpose by treating the issue as a broad debate over “terrorism” designations. But the substance is uglier. Antifa is not a structured organization, yet the administration is reportedly treating it like one. Meanwhile, Brennan says neo-Nazi groups are still real and still active, but Trump refuses to confront them.

That is the pattern: inflate the threat from the left, blur the line between ideology and criminal organization, and use that fiction to justify surveillance and repression. At the same time, minimize or ignore right-wing violence because it is politically useful to do so.

The Intelligence Community Is Being Broken on Purpose

Julia Curlee’s account sharpens the picture. She describes senior officers fired publicly, clearances revoked for dissent, and “selected reporting” declassified to support claims the intelligence community’s own finished analysis would not support. That is not normal oversight. That is discipline by humiliation and selective disclosure.

Once intelligence becomes a loyalty machine, its job changes. It stops informing power and starts serving it. That is how you get an agency that is quieter about actual extremist networks and louder about the administration’s preferred enemies.

The Deeper Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics does in practice: it repurposes state capacity for internal combat. The problem is not confusion or excess zeal. The problem is direction. The machinery of national security is being bent toward prosecuting opponents, intimidating dissent, and manufacturing threat narratives that flatter the regime.

The danger here is not just that intelligence is politicized. It is that politicization becomes the point. When the state trains itself to look everywhere except at the violence that fits the ruling clique’s politics, it is not failing at security. It is choosing a different mission.
