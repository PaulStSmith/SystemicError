After 'circling the drain' Trump’s DJT stock finally plops in

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosJune 25, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

DJT Is Not a Stock Story. It Is a Power Story.

The Asset, Not the Business

One paragraph of context: Trump’s Truth Social vehicle, trading under DJT, has collapsed to a record low of $7.53 per share after being hyped near $94.20, briefly relaunching around $60 in early 2024, and then losing nearly 90 percent of its value. The company has burned through cash, posted a first-quarter net loss above $400 million, and kept lurching between abandoned plans and new gimmicks, including a merger pitch tied to a fusion power company.

Who Actually Holds Power

The real power in this story does not sit with the retail investors left holding the bag. It sits with Trump and the corporate structure built around his political brand. DJT exists as a financial wrapper for influence: a publicly traded vessel for a personal megaphone, not a serious operating business. The stock is treated as an instrument of loyalty, speculation, and access.

That is the point. The platform’s value is not being measured by user demand or durable revenue. It is being propped up, attacked, repackaged, and relabeled as political theater. Trump does not need a healthy company if the company can still function as a hype machine.

Misdirection as Business Model

The article’s most revealing detail is the company’s habit of improvising its own purpose. Prediction markets, cryptocurrency, a fusion merger with TAE Technologies, then abandonment of a spinoff plan with Texas Ventures Acquisition III, all in the name of “future” growth. That is not strategy. It is narrative laundering.

This is how accountability gets blurred. Failure is recast as confusion. Speculation is presented as innovation. A collapsing media venture is dressed up in the costume of frontier technology. The goal is not coherence; it is to keep the price, the story, and the attention alive long enough to extract value.

The Grift Is the Signal

Futurism’s reporting points to a company burning cash, cycling executives, and tying itself to speculative crypto assets while its fundamentals deteriorate. That is the economic side of the operation. The political side is worse: the broader accusations described in the piece include insider trading claims and allegations that Trump personally bought stock and then praised companies publicly to inflate their prices.

Those allegations matter because they show the governing logic. When the same figure can operate as politician, promoter, and market mover, the line between public power and private enrichment stops existing in practice. The system does not merely permit conflict; it monetizes it.

Regulation Arrives Late, If At All

The SEC complaints mentioned in the article underscore the institutional weakness here. Complaints about options manipulation are not proof, but they are evidence that market participants saw enough suspicious behavior to escalate. That is the larger pattern: watchdogs show up after the damage is done, and even then only in fragments.

Meanwhile, the company keeps operating inside the same permissive ecosystem that lets political branding double as financial extraction. The visible collapse of DJT does not mean the scheme failed. It means the structure was always designed to transfer risk downward while keeping control at the top.

The Pattern

DJT is what happens when political power, media vanity, and speculative finance are fused into one machine. The product is not a stable company. The product is leverage: over followers, over markets, over institutions too timid or too captured to call the operation what it is.

The stock’s collapse is not just a market event. It is a record of what happens when public authority is turned into private promotion and then shielded by the usual fog of “uncertainty.” The fog is the mechanism. The damage is the outcome.
