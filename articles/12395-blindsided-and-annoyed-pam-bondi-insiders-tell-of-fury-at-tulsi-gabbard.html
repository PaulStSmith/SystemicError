'Blindsided and annoyed': Pam Bondi insiders tell of fury at Tulsi Gabbard

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 26, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Attorney General Pam Bondi found herself scrambling to contain the political fallout after Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard hijacked her handling of the Jeffrey Epstein crisis and launched an uncoordinated attack on Barack Obama, according to several sources close to the AG.

Gabbard, reportedly desperate to repair her standing with Trump after being "excoriated" and excluded from recent meetings, suddenly demanded Bondi investigate what she called a "treasonous conspiracy" by Obama officials regarding the 2016 Russia investigation.

The move caught Bondi completely off-guard, the sources told The New York Times. Fresh off a nasty fight with top FBI officials over the mess regarding her announcement that an Epstein client list didn’t exist, the attorney general was given "little warning" that Gabbard was about to dump the Obama investigation in her lap, sources said.

Sources inside her camp told the Times she "felt blindsided and annoyed."

Gabbard made the announcement earlier this week, then went into detail during a surprise appearance at a White House press conference on Wednesday.

"She's, like, hotter than everybody. She's the hottest one in the room right now," Trump declared at a White House event Tuesday, signaling Gabbard was back in his good graces after her diversionary attack relieved pressure from the "never-ending Epstein file crisis."

But the stunt put Bondi in an "nearly untenable position." Her staff scrambled for a solution that would satisfy Trump without committing to a politically explosive Obama investigation with "unpredictable legal and political consequences."

Hours after Gabbard's provocative White House briefing, Bondi's deputies posted an ambiguous statement announcing a "strike force" to examine the accusations—though details about the group's operations and timeline remained absent.

A spokesman for Obama dismissed the attacks as "ridiculous and a weak attempt at distraction."

Current and former officials warned that building a coherent conspiracy case against Obama-era intelligence officials would be "challenging," while prosecuting Obama himself would be "practically impossible" given Supreme Court immunity protections.

Gabbard stepped far outside traditional intelligence boundaries by directly accusing Obama of criminal wrongdoing, the Times reported.

"The evidence that we have found and that we have released directly point to President Obama leading the manufacturing of this intelligence assessment," she claimed, though she "produced no evidence of wrongdoing."

Republican senators offered Bondi an escape route by suggesting a special counsel—forcing her into a "tactical U-turn" since she's opposed such appointments in political cases.

Our Analysis:

The Gabbard Gambit: A Desperate Dive into Diversion

In a political landscape that often feels more akin to a sensationalized reality TV show than the sober realm of governance, the recent escapades of Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard and Attorney General Pam Bondi have managed to raise the bar for eyebrow-raising antics. The episode in question—a brazen, baseless attack on Barack Obama—is not merely a new chapter in political absurdity but a textbook example of distraction politics at its most transparent.

The Fallout of Uncoordinated Ambition

At the heart of this debacle is Tulsi Gabbard, whose actions bespeak a desperation to claw back into Donald Trump's good graces. Her method? Launching an unprovoked and unfounded investigation into what she terms a "treasonous conspiracy" by Obama officials concerning the 2016 Russia investigation. This move, as reported, caught AG Pam Bondi "completely off-guard," showcasing not only a lack of coordination within the administration but a reckless disregard for the consequences of such allegations.

The Misdirection Play

Trump's reaction to Gabbard's maneuver—a comment on her being "hotter than everybody" in the room—underscores the spectacle over substance nature of this entire episode. It's a diversion, a sideshow to distract from the "never-ending Epstein file crisis" that has plagued his administration. This isn't governance; it's drama.

The Bondi Bind

Pam Bondi, for her part, finds herself in what can only be described as a political quagmire. The task of satisfying Trump's appetite for vengeance against his political adversaries while navigating the legal and political minefield such an investigation entails is, as the article notes, "nearly untenable." The announcement of a "strike force" to examine Gabbard's accusations is a nebulous solution at best, an attempt to appease without committing.

The Obama Obfuscation

What stands out most egregiously in this tale is Gabbard's direct accusation against Obama, a move that doesn't just step outside the bounds of traditional intelligence operations—it leaps. Claiming to have evidence of Obama's involvement in manufacturing intelligence assessments without producing said evidence is not just irresponsible; it's perilously close to the kind of demagoguery one expects in authoritarian regimes, not democratic republics.

The Reality of the Situation

Current and former officials have rightly pointed out the challenges of building a coherent conspiracy case against Obama-era intelligence officials, not to mention the "practically impossible" task of prosecuting Obama himself due to Supreme Court immunity protections. The suggestion from Republican senators of appointing a special counsel, forcing Bondi into a "tactical U-turn," is a clear indication of the untenable position she's been placed in—a position that, it should be noted, serves no one but Trump and his incessant need for political spectacle.

Conclusion: The Spectacle Over Substance

This episode is emblematic of a broader problem in American politics: the elevation of spectacle over substance, of diversion over governance. It's a dangerous game, one that threatens not just the credibility of those who engage in it but the very fabric of democratic governance. As we move forward, one can only hope for a return to a politics rooted in reality, not the reality TV ethos that currently pervades.
