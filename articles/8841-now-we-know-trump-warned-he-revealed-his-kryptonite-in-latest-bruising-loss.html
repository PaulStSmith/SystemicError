'Now we know': Trump warned he revealed his 'kryptonite' in latest bruising loss

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as he speaks with the media in the Oval Office, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 5, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

Elections last week marked a significant shift in American political discourse — with sweeping Democratic Party wins being less a direct reaction to Donald Trump rule and more a result that the opposition has discovered his “kryptonite,” a columnist wrote Monday.

Zohran Mamdani, in particular, was an antidote to Trump’s message of anger and revenge — and his message of empathy and hoped destroyed his competition, wrote E. J. Dionne Jr. for the New York Times.

He articulated a vision of political engagement that went beyond criticism, appealing to those “who see politics today as too cruel for the flame of hope to still burn,” wrote Dionne Jr. In his victory speech, he declared, “New Yorkers have spoken in a clear voice: Hope is alive.”

Abigail Spanberger captured the essence of this new political approach in her race to become the Democratic governor of Virginia, observing that “points of pride for people matter” when discussing work and community. She emphasized the importance of listening, stating, “If we’re going into communities and listening to what matters to people, there’s just a value in grounding our discussions in people’s sense of place and who they are.”

Democrat Mikie Sherrill championed workers’ rights in her New Jersey governor race, asserting, “When you lift workers up, all workers get lifted up.” Her campaign approach defied traditional political divisions, winning 50 percent support among voters without college degrees.

The candidates recognized the fundamental emotional need that Trump had previously exploited, Dionne Jr. wrote. As Spanberger noted, “The first thing that people are looking for, I think, in political engagement, is some level of respect.”

Mamdani’s campaign strategy was particularly innovative. Instead of condemning Trump voters, he simply asked them why they felt disconnected from traditional politics. This approach of genuine curiosity replaced the rhetoric of division, the columnist wrote.

The election results revealed the limitations of Trump’s approach. His focus on billionaires and pursuit of a lavish White House ballroom symbolized a disconnect from everyday Americans’ experiences.

The victories of Spanberger, Sherrill, and Mamdani demonstrated a powerful political alternative: an approach based on empathy, respect, and genuine engagement with voters’ lived experiences, Dionne Jr. wrote. The piece was titled, “Now We Know Trump’s Kryptonite.”

As Spanberger reflected on a local hospital’s reopening, she captured the underlying message of her successful campaign, Dionne Jr wrote: “Kind of back to the notion of hope, right?”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at “Trump’s Kryptonite”

The recent article by E. J. Dionne Jr. in the New York Times offers a fascinating narrative on the shifting tides of American political discourse, attributing sweeping Democratic victories to an evolved strategy that seems to have decoded the enigma of countering Trump’s America. Dionne Jr. posits that these wins signify less of a backlash against Trump’s tenure and more of a discovery of his political “kryptonite.” Yet, while the optimism and insights of Dionne Jr. are commendable, they warrant a deeper, more critical analysis.

The Myth of a Single Antidote

To assert that figures like Zohran Mamdani are Trump’s outright “kryptonite” oversimplifies the multifaceted landscape of American politics. Dionne Jr. celebrates Mamdani’s message of empathy and hope, contrasting it sharply with Trump’s rhetoric of anger and revenge. While it’s refreshing to see candidates like Mamdani succeed, it’s naive to believe that empathy alone is the silver bullet that can consistently dismantle the Trumpian appeal. Politics, especially in a nation as divided as the United States, is more complex than a battle between hope and anger.

The Power of Listening and Respect

The article shines in its spotlight on candidates like Abigail Spanberger and Mikie Sherrill, who, through their emphasis on listening, respect, and workers’ rights, have managed to resonate with voters beyond traditional party lines. Dionne Jr. highlights an essential truth: people crave respect and genuine political engagement. This observation is critical, yet it’s not a newfound strategy. The real story here is not that Democrats have discovered some novel “kryptonite” but that they are finally applying basic principles of effective communication and respect, principles that should have been the bedrock of political engagement all along.

Innovative Campaign Strategies or a Return to Basics?

Mamdani’s approach of engaging Trump voters with genuine curiosity rather than condemnation is lauded as innovative. However, one could argue this isn’t innovation as much as a return to the basic tenets of political canvassing and engagement. The “rhetoric of division” has been a tool employed by many political figures, not just Trump. What Dionne Jr. describes as a groundbreaking strategy seems more like a much-needed correction to the course, a pivot back to politics that prioritize understanding and addressing the concerns of the electorate over sensationalism and division.

The Limitations of Trump’s Approach

The article adeptly points out the limitations of Trump’s political strategy, emphasizing his disconnect from the everyday experiences of Americans. Dionne Jr. draws a stark contrast between Trump’s focus on billionaires and lavishness and the Democratic candidates’ focus on empathy and lived experiences. While this observation is valid, it’s essential to remember that Trump’s approach did resonate with a significant segment of the American populace. The limitations of his strategy, as highlighted by the recent elections, do not necessarily signal its demise but rather indicate the potential for a more empathetic and respectful approach to gain ground.

Conclusion: A Step Forward, but Not a Cure-All

Dionne Jr.’s article, “Now We Know Trump’s Kryptonite,” offers an optimistic view of a potential shift in American political discourse. However, it falls short by portraying empathy, respect, and genuine engagement as novel strategies rather than fundamental expectations of political leadership. While the Democratic victories discussed offer hope, it’s crucial to approach this analysis with a critical eye. Empathy as Trump’s “kryptonite” is an appealing narrative, but it’s far from a complete solution to the deep-seated divisions and challenges facing American politics. The real kryptonite, if it exists, is in sustained, genuine engagement with the electorate, grounded in respect and a willingness to address the systemic issues that fuel division and disenchantment.
