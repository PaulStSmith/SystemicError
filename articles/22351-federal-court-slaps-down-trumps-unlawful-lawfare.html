Federal court slaps down Trump’s 'unlawful' lawfare

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20262 min read

Trump’s DOJ as a Revenge Machine

The Real Story

A federal court just said the quiet part out loud: the Trump-aligned U.S. attorney who went after New York Attorney General Letitia James was not lawfully in office when he issued the subpoenas. That is not a procedural footnote. It is the state catching a political operation using Justice Department machinery it had no right to wield.

Power Was Never the Mystery

The institutional power sat with Trump’s DOJ, which had the authority to investigate, subpoena, and indict. The decision-maker was Trump’s political operation inside the department, which chose to turn federal law enforcement toward a personal enemy. The target was James, the attorney general who beat Trump in civil court and got him hit with a massive fraud penalty before that financial judgment was later thrown out on appeal.

This is what revenge looks like when it puts on a suit and calls itself governance.

Lawfare With a Badge

Sarcone’s subpoenas were not some neutral search for facts that happened to land on James. According to the source, they were part of Trump’s broader campaign to retaliate against her after she brought civil cases against him, his family business, and the NRA. Trump had already said out loud that James should be “arrested and punished accordingly.” Once back in office, his DOJ indicted her on mortgage fraud allegations.

The pattern is unmistakable. Trump does not merely attack opponents rhetorically. He tries to convert personal grievance into institutional force.

The Framing Is Too Small

If this story is told as an appointment dispute, it shrinks the crime scene. The illegal appointment matters because it exposes the mechanism, but the underlying abuse is the deployment of federal prosecutorial power for political payback. The court quashed the subpoenas and upheld Sarcone’s disqualification because he lacked lawful authority. Fine. But the larger scandal is that this machinery was activated in the first place.

That is not confusion. It is deliberate escalation dressed up as procedure.

What The Courts Caught

The irony is brutal: the same kind of unlawful appointment that doomed the case against James also helped expose the weakness of the revenge campaign against her. The DOJ appealed, lost 2-1, and the court noted that the government did not even bother to argue against Sarcone’s future disqualification. That is not the behavior of a disciplined law enforcement apparatus. It is the behavior of a political operation improvising around its own defects.

The judiciary is doing damage control after the fact because the executive branch is treating legality as optional when the target is politically convenient.

The Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics does when it has access to state power: it personalizes prosecution, corrupts institutional roles, and calls retaliation law enforcement. Democrats are not facing a symmetric opponent here. They are facing a governing faction that wants the coercive tools of the state while discarding the constraints that make those tools legitimate.

The lesson is larger than Letitia James. Once the Justice Department becomes a vehicle for a leader’s vendettas, the issue is no longer one rogue lawyer or one bad subpoena. The issue is a regime that confuses power with entitlement and treats public office as a private weapon.
