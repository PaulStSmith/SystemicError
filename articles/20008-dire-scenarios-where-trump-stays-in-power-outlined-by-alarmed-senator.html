Dire scenarios where Trump stays in power outlined by alarmed senator

By Paulo Santos

One of Capitol Hill’s most prominent — and most vocal — Democrats is warning about what he says are the ways President Donald Trump could try to remain in power beyond his current term.

Trump has long hinted that he is interested in a third term, and even has had red “Trump 2028” caps as part of his merchandise offering. And while he recently appeared to put to rest questions about a third term — prohibited under a plain reading of the U.S. Constitution — by saying he has been told he cannot run, doubts among some still linger.

U.S. Senator Chris Murphy (D-CT) on Friday shared what he suggested were possible ways Trump could try to stay in power past 2028 — and warned he thinks it’s possible that he will.

“I think he is right now trying to scheme a way to be able to stay,” Murphy told The Bulwark’s Sam Stein at the 2025 Texas Tribune Festival.

“I think you have a potential, two potential Supreme Court vacancies coming up,” Murphy noted, “and it may be very important for him to install folks on the Supreme Court who may be willing to entertain radical ideas about the restrictions on the Constitution, about a third term.”

Murphy continued with an alternative theory, suggesting that Trump “may just be interested in installing Donald Trump Jr. or another family member in the White House.”

But then the Connecticut Democrat served up a warning.

“Whatever he’s planning on doing, he can’t get away with it unless he destroys the ability of the people to speak their mind in elections because he and his party are going to lose in 2026 and 2028 unless he’s successful in rigging the election,” Murphy declared.

He vowed, “We’re going to do everything in our power, and we need to order all of our advocacy in the United States Senate and the House to stop him from doing it.”

Murphy continued with his warnings.

“I don’t think anybody with ambition right now should be planning on running for president in 2028 because we may not have a free and fair election in 2028,” Murphy declared. “We all have to be in the business of saving our democracy right now.”

“I do think we have to, all of us,” he added, “be traveling the entire country, whether it be an early primary state or not, to build this political resistance movement.”

Our Analysis:

The Perpetual Power Play: Unpacking the Trump Dynasty’s Ambitions

In a political climate that often feels more like a high-stakes drama than the sober proceedings of governance, the latest comments from Senator Chris Murphy (D-CT) offer a chilling glimpse into a potential future where the lines between democracy and dynasty blur alarmingly. At the heart of Murphy’s warnings is a scenario that reads like a political thriller: former President Donald Trump, not content with a mere two terms, allegedly entertains notions of extending his stay in the White House, directly flouting the U.S. Constitution’s clear limits on presidential tenure.

Trump’s Third-Term Temptations

The specter of a “Trump 2028” campaign, complete with its own merchandise, might seem like a clever marketing gimmick at first glance. However, it belies a deeper, more disconcerting ambition. Trump’s flirtation with a third term, despite a recent concession that constitutional constraints make this impossible, has done little to quell the anxieties of those who take the rule of law seriously. The very notion that a former president would even jest about circumventing the Constitution is not just a matter of poor taste; it’s a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic norms.

Supreme Court Scheming

Murphy’s assertion that Trump could seek to exploit upcoming Supreme Court vacancies is particularly alarming. The idea that the highest court in the land could be manipulated to “entertain radical ideas” about presidential term limits is not merely speculative fear-mongering; it’s a testament to the lengths to which Trump and his enablers might go to cling to power. This strategy, while far-fetched to some, underscores the critical importance of the Supreme Court’s composition and its role as a guardian of constitutional principles.

Dynastic Designs

The alternative theory posited by Murphy, that Trump might seek to install a family member in the presidency, is no less concerning. Such a move would not only mock the notion of a merit-based ascension to the nation’s highest office but also cement the Trump family’s hold on American political life, edging the country towards oligarchy.

The Assault on Electoral Integrity

At the core of Murphy’s warnings is a dire prediction: that the integrity of future elections is at risk. The suggestion that Trump could only maintain his grip on power by “rigging the election” is a grave indictment of the state of American democracy. Murphy’s call to action, emphasizing the need for concerted efforts to safeguard electoral integrity, underscores the urgency of the moment.

A Call to Arms

Murphy’s bleak outlook for the 2028 election is a clarion call for vigilance. His urging for a nationwide political resistance movement is not just about opposition for opposition’s sake; it’s a recognition of the existential threat to democratic governance posed by the prospect of a Trumpian return to power, whether directly or through a surrogate.

Conclusion

The dialogue surrounding Trump’s future political ambitions, as highlighted by Senator Murphy, is more than speculative political banter. It’s a warning sign of potential constitutional crises and a test of the resilience of America’s democratic institutions. As we navigate these troubled waters, the importance of remaining informed, engaged, and ready to defend the principles of democracy has never been more critical. In facing these unprecedented challenges, complacency is not an option. The stakes, quite simply, are too high.
