Former Trump associate Lev Parnas, who says he used to be "deep inside the Trump machine" and now reports from the outside, earlier flagged an example of Donald Trump "saying the quiet part out loud."

After that, Parnas filed a report ahead of the weekend called, "Trump’s Team Scrambles to muzzle Dan Bongino." In the essay, he argues, "It wasn’t just about Epstein. It was about stopping Bongino from going rogue."

"As I sit here watching the media circle the story about the dinner that was supposed to happen—every network now catching up to the leak we dropped days ago—I can’t help but notice what they’re all missing," Parnas wrote on Friday. "They talk about the dinner. They talk about the people who were supposed to be there. They talk about what was likely discussed. But not a single outlet, not one reporter, is talking about the name that wasn’t there. The name that matters most. Dan Bongino."

According to Parnas, "Because what I’m telling you now, is that this dinner—this meeting Todd Blanche was supposed to lead at J.D. Vance’s residence—was never just about Epstein. It was about Dan. About bringing him to heel. About getting him in line. And when that dinner got exposed and canceled, the conversation didn’t go away. It just moved behind closed doors."

He then continued:

"According to my sources, that conversation happened yesterday at the White House. Kash Patel sat down with Pam Bondi in private. The topic? Bongino. The rift. The refusal. The threat. Because since July 6th—when Bondi’s now-infamous memo claimed there was no Epstein 'client list,' and that no further prosecutions would take place—Bongino hasn’t been on board. He’s stayed quiet. No statements. No tweets. No Fox hits. Just silence."

Parnas went on to criticize the handling of the purported cover-up.

"What they’ve done is try to paper over the cracks. Just like Trump always does. He put a band-aid on it. He called Bongino. We don’t know if he begged him, threatened him, promised him something—but whatever he said, Bongino stayed. Physically. On paper. But not in spirit. Not on the train," he added. "And this is what I warned about. Weeks ago I told you this was another volcano waiting to erupt. This was the next weak link in the chain they’re using to bind the Epstein narrative. And they knew it. That’s why the meeting was so important. That’s why it was supposed to include everyone but him. Because Dan Bongino is a problem they can’t control."

Parnas further notes, "[H]e’s dangerous to them now."

Our Analysis:

The Alleged Conspiracy to Silence: Unraveling the Bongino Affair

In a recent revelation that reads like a script from a political thriller, former Trump associate Lev Parnas has blown the whistle on what appears to be a convoluted plot to silence FBI official Dan Bongino. The narrative, situated at the nexus of power, silence, and manipulation, revolves around a reportedly clandestine meeting at JD Vance's residence. Originally thought to concern the infamous Epstein files, Parnas asserts the real agenda was far more insidious: to muzzle Dan Bongino, a figure apparently gone rogue.

The Quiet Part Loud and Clear

Parnas, positioning himself as a former insider now turned external critic of the "Trump machine," has laid bare an alarming scenario. His accusations pivot on a dinner at Vance's residence, orchestrated under the guise of discussing the Epstein scandal but allegedly intended to rein in Bongino. The intrigue deepens with Parnas’ critique of the media's failure to spotlight the absence of Bongino's name from the discussion—a glaring omission, in his view, that underscores the media’s lackluster investigative spirit.

Behind Closed Doors

According to Parnas, the cancellation of the dinner did not deter the alleged conspirators; it merely shifted the venue to the White House, where Kash Patel and Pam Bondi purportedly convened to deliberate on the "Bongino problem." The essence of the issue seems to stem from Bongino's silence following a memo by Bondi, which controversially claimed the absence of an Epstein client list and negated the need for further prosecutions. This silence, a stark departure from Bongino's typically vocal stance, signals a rift that Parnas suggests the administration is desperate to mend.

A Band-Aid on a Bullet Wound

Parnas criticizes the administration's approach to managing the fallout as emblematic of Trump's penchant for superficial fixes. Allegations of Trump reaching out to Bongino hint at a frantic attempt to secure his allegiance—or at the very least, his silence. Yet, Parnas insinuates that such efforts are futile; Bongino, physically present but spiritually absent, remains a thorn in their side, a "volcano waiting to erupt."

The Danger of Dissent

The narrative culminates in the portrayal of Bongino as a looming threat to the establishment, a "problem they can’t control." Parnas' portrayal of Bongino as "dangerous" to their agenda underscores a broader theme of power dynamics and the lengths to which some will go to maintain control.

Conclusion: The Specter of Silence

What stands out in this convoluted tale is not just the alleged plot to silence a dissenting voice but the profound implications it holds for transparency, accountability, and the integrity of political discourse. If Parnas' allegations hold water, they expose a disturbing strategy of suppression and manipulation, revealing a systemic error in the mechanics of power that demands rigorous scrutiny and, ultimately, correction.

In light of these assertions, one is compelled to question: How many more silenced voices linger in the shadows of political machinations? The Bongino affair, as outlined by Parnas, may just be the tip of an iceberg, a stark reminder of the lengths to which the pursuit of power will go to preserve itself—silence, after all, is a weapon of the powerful.
