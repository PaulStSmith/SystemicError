Scaramucci lands a sneaky punch on Scott Jennings

The Press Secretary Search Is a Distraction From the Power That Matters

A Vacancy Without Authority

The reported contest over who might become White House press secretary is presented as a personnel drama: CNN commentator Scott Jennings wants the job, White House officials are reportedly unimpressed, and President Donald Trump remains undecided. But the decisive fact is simpler. Trump controls the appointment, the access granted to the press, and the administration’s communications strategy. Everyone else is auditioning for delegated power.

Jennings Is Campaigning for Submission

According to the reporting, Jennings promoted himself by sending video clips to White House staff while Trump solicited opinions about him from allies. That is not evidence of corruption or ideological conversion. It does, however, reveal the degrading incentive structure surrounding the position: public performance becomes a private application, and proximity to presidential favor becomes the qualification that matters.

The tension is especially conspicuous because Jennings reportedly urged Republicans three years earlier to abandon Trump. The article offers no evidence explaining that reversal. It is therefore impossible to establish whether Jennings changed his convictions, made a strategic accommodation, or simply wants the job. What is established is that his candidacy now depends on the approval of the politician he once opposed.

The Gossip Buries the Governing Decision

The weakest part of the framing is its fixation on whether Jennings appears “job hungry.” Ambition is not the consequential issue. The consequential issue, raised only indirectly through Anthony Scaramucci’s comments, is presidential control over press access.

Scaramucci referred to the removal of credentials belonging to CNN, Politico, and MS NOW and linked the episode to a judge’s decision that week. The supplied report does not explain the ruling, identify the judge, or establish the legal circumstances, so broader conclusions about that dispute would outrun the evidence. Still, treating the matter as background color in a story about Jennings’s self-promotion reverses the proper hierarchy. A commentator’s lobbying is gossip. A White House deciding which news organizations retain access is an exercise of institutional power.

A “Pushback” Theory With No Safeguard

Scaramucci argued that former press secretary Karoline Leavitt might have challenged Trump and spared him embarrassment. That is Scaramucci’s assessment, not a demonstrated account of what Leavitt would have done. More importantly, it exposes the fragility of the supposed restraint: the system depends on whether one presidential employee is willing and able to persuade the president privately.

A press secretary has no independent mandate to protect press access. If Trump prefers rotating surrogates or acting as his own spokesperson, as Scaramucci speculated, he can reduce even that informal resistance. The vacancy matters less than the concentration of authority behind it.

Access Journalism Meets Patronage Politics

CNN’s participation sharpens the conflict. One of its commentators is reportedly pursuing a White House communications post while its hosts discuss that pursuit on air with a former Trump official. The report does not establish improper coordination by CNN, but the overlap illustrates how easily political commentary, career advancement, and court politics collapse into one another.

In that environment, media figures do not merely analyze power. They can become applicants to it. Coverage then gravitates toward who is up, who is disliked, and who sent too many clips—precisely the details that make presidential patronage look like entertainment instead of governance.

The Courtier Is Not the System

Jennings may get the job or may never have been a serious contender; the supplied reporting establishes no outcome. Either way, concentrating on his appetite for the title misidentifies the center of gravity.

The larger pattern is a presidency in which communications authority flows personally from Trump, access can become part of the administration’s power struggle with news organizations, and internal resistance depends on advisers whose authority exists only at his pleasure. The courtier search is colorful. The system it serves is the story.
