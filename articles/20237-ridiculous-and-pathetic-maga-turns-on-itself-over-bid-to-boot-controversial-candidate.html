'Ridiculous and pathetic': MAGA turns on itself over bid to boot controversial candidate

Florida Republicans Are Trying to Contain Their Own Fire

The Setup

Florida Lt. Gov. Jay Collins has sued Republican candidate James Fishback to knock him off the governor’s ballot, arguing that Fishback did not meet the state’s seven-year residency requirement because of his D.C. voting, property records, mortgage documents, and homestead deduction. Fishback, who is trailing Byron Donalds in most polls, has already built a campaign around racial agitation, including telling a Black commentator he “should be lynched,” while his running mate has been exposed for racist online posts.

Power Is Not the Same as Noise

The people with actual institutional power here are not the online provocateurs. It is Collins, the governor’s office, the courts, and the party machinery deciding which candidates are acceptable liabilities. Fishback can rage, bait, and posture, but ballot access is enforced by institutions, not by his own self-promotion. That is the real center of gravity in the story.

This Is Not About Principle

Collins frames the lawsuit as constitutional housekeeping, but his own language gives the game away: the real fear is not dishonesty, it is losing the governor’s mansion to Democrat David Jolly. That is not civic virtue. That is party management under stress. If Republicans believed Fishback was merely unserious, they had months to treat him as such. They did not. They waited until he became a problem they could no longer contain.

The Misdirection

The easier scandal is the one the article risks softening: a Republican candidate who traffics in white supremacist theatrics is being made into a legal case about residency. That does not erase the racism; it launders it into process. Meanwhile, some MAGA commentators are furious not because Fishback is decent, but because they understand the optics: if the system moves against one of their own, he can be cast as a martyr to “the establishment.” The grievance machine is always ready to turn accountability into persecution theater.

The Party Built This

Fishback’s behavior did not emerge from nowhere. A campaign that tolerates lynching rhetoric, racial slurs, and dehumanizing language does not have a discipline problem. It has a recruitment problem. The party keeps elevating figures who understand that outrage is a credential, then acts shocked when the most reckless candidate starts behaving like a reckless candidate. The lawsuit is not the beginning of the rot; it is one more attempt to mop up after it.

Systemic Error

This is what modern political breakdown looks like: extremists are tolerated until they become electorally inconvenient, then institutions are asked to pretend the issue was paperwork all along. The operative logic is not truth, but containment; not accountability, but brand protection. When a party normalizes racial extremism for long enough, the resulting crisis will always be sold as a technical dispute.
