America's billionaires plan for a world without us in it

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

(Reuters)

Billionaire Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the drop in the stock market is nothing to be concerned about because Americans aren’t looking at the “day-to-day fluctuations” in their retirement savings.

Billionaire Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick says we shouldn’t be concerned that mass layoffs in the Social Security Administration have caused delays because his mother-in-law wouldn’t be worried if she didn’t get her monthly Social Security check.

The richest person in the world, Elon Musk, whose minions are busily slashing the Social Security Administration, calls Social Security a “Ponzi scheme.”

Billionaire Trump says he “couldn’t care less” if automakers raise their prices because of his tariffs.

On what planet do these people live? Surely not this one.

To be fabulously wealthy today means not having to come across anyone who isn’t. It therefore means having not a clue about how average working people live or what they worry about it.

The billionaire class doesn’t care if producers raise their prices, because prices mean almost nothing to them. They aren’t concerned about retirement savings, because they don’t have to prepare for retirement.

If they’re preparing for anything, it’s the “event,” as they call it — the thing that will cause them to secede even further from the rest of the world into isolated, sanitized survival chambers. The “event” could be massive social unrest, an unstoppable virus, a malicious computer hack that takes everything down, or environmental collapse.

Taking their cue from Musk, they’re considering ever more elaborate escape routes — colonies on Mars, solar-powered SeaPods, anti-aging health-span extenders, and uploading their minds into supercomputers.

For America’s billionaires, the future of technology has less to do with making the world a better place than escaping from the rest of us.

As Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor put it, the billionaire bros are basing their secessionist plans on:

“the principle that those with means have the right to walk away from the obligations of citizenship, especially taxes and burdensome regulation. Retooling and rebranding the old ambitions and privileges of empires, they dream of splintering governments and carving up the world into hyper-capitalist, democracy-free havens under the sole control of the supremely wealthy, protected by private mercenaries, serviced by AI robots and financed by cryptocurrencies.”

A bevy of billionaires in Washington is now attacking Social Security and Medicaid, obliterating health and safety regulations, destroying much of public education, and deciding who in America should be abducted and sent to a brutal prison in another country.

Even before Trump, America’s extreme wealth and privilege combined with the most unequal distribution of income and wealth in more than a century had rigged the economy for the benefit of those at the top.

Their tax rates had plummeted. Their profits had exploded. Their fortunes had soared. When they took excessive risks with the economy and faced ruin, they were bailed out while banks foreclosed on millions of homeowners.

The reaction among America’s working middle class has been fury. Trump exploited that fury by telling them he was on their side.

But he’s not. He never was. He and his billionaire appointees feel no connection to the rest of America. They want only to dismantle government and insulate themselves from the ensuing chaos.

As Klein and Taylor say, the most powerful people in the world are preparing for the end of the world, an end they themselves are frenetically accelerating.

Our Analysis:

The Billionaire Bubble: A Scathing Indictment

In a world increasingly polarized by wealth, the recent comments from the upper echelons of the United States' financial and political hierarchy serve as a stark reminder of the widening chasm between the billionaire class and the average American. The article in question paints a vivid picture of a reality so far removed from the everyday struggles of the populace that it borders on dystopian.

The Tone-Deafness of the Billionaire Class

The lack of empathy and understanding from figures like Billionaire Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, Elon Musk, and Donald Trump isn't just alarming—it's a clarion call to the systemic issues festering at the heart of American society. Bessent's dismissal of stock market fluctuations ignores the fact that for many Americans, these "day-to-day" shifts can mean the difference between a secure retirement and financial ruin. Lutnick's flippant remarks about Social Security delays showcase a grotesque detachment from the reality of those who rely on these checks for their very survival.

Elon Musk and the Social Security "Ponzi Scheme"

Elon Musk's characterization of Social Security as a "Ponzi scheme" not only demonstrates a profound misunderstanding of its function and importance but also highlights the dangerous rhetoric being used to undermine it. Social Security serves as a lifeline for millions of elderly and disabled Americans, a fact Musk and his ilk conveniently ignore as they plot their escapes to Mars or the depths of the ocean.

The "Event" and Billionaire Escapism

The concept of the "event" as described in the article is perhaps the most chilling revelation of all. It speaks to a mindset so consumed by self-preservation and so disillusioned with the concept of community and shared responsibility that the only logical conclusion is to flee the planet or reality itself. This escapist fantasy, fueled by unparalleled wealth, only further distances the billionaire class from the plights of the common person.

Hyper-Capitalist Havens: A New Feudalism

The article's mention of Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor's analysis is particularly poignant, drawing parallels between the billionaire's plans for secession and a new form of feudalism, marked by hyper-capitalist havens free from the nuisances of taxes, regulations, and democracy. This new world order, serviced by AI and financed by cryptocurrencies, would see the ultra-wealthy ruling over their own insular kingdoms, protected from the chaos of the outside world—a world they themselves have played a significant role in destabilizing.

The Realization of a Rigged System

The article concludes with a somber reflection on the state of the American dream. The realization that the system has been rigged in favor of the wealthy at the expense of the working middle class is not new, but the extent to which this rigging has occurred—and the blatant disregard for its consequences displayed by those in power—is particularly egregious. The fury of the American working class, exploited by figures like Trump, underscores the deep sense of betrayal felt by many.

In Conclusion: A System in Desperate Need of Correction

The narrative spun by the article is not just a critique of the billionaire class; it's an indictment of a system that allows such disparities to exist and flourish. The disconnect between the ultra-wealthy and the average American has never been more apparent, or more dangerous. As the billionaires prepare for their escape, one must wonder what will become of those left behind in a world increasingly unfit for anyone but the richest among us.

The question now is not whether the system is broken—it clearly is—but whether there is the will and the way to fix it. The future of American democracy, and indeed the global community, may very well depend on the answer.
