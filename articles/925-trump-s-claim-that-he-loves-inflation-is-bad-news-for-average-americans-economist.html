Trump's claim that he 'loves' inflation is bad news for average Americans: economist

Based on an article by Robert Davis

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

Trump Owns the Inflation, Everyone Else Pays

The Decision Is the Story

The source is straightforward on the facts: Trump says he “loves” the inflation tied to his war in Iran, while an economist on CNN says the latest inflation reading is 4.2% and that regular Americans are absorbing the cost. The war has lasted 104 days, cost about $100 billion, and pushed energy prices up through the Strait of Hormuz, with gasoline, groceries, shipping, and mail all getting more expensive.

That is the story. Not uncertainty. Not drift. A president made a war choice, the war distorted markets, and households got billed.

Power Sits at the Top

Actual institutional power sits with the president. Trump is not commenting from the sidelines; he is defending the outcome of a war he owns. The article makes that plain, even if its framing tries to smooth the edges by treating the inflation as an economic side effect rather than a direct political decision with distributional consequences.

This is what executive power looks like when it stops pretending to be neutral: it creates costs, then speaks as if those costs are weather.

The Bill Gets Pushed Downward

The real mechanism here is not complicated. Strategic disruption in a global chokepoint drives up energy prices. Higher energy prices spread into gasoline, groceries, shipping, and mail. The source says the average household is paying about $460 more per year for gas alone, and that does not include the rest of the price increases.

The burden lands where it always lands in these setups: on households with no control over the decision and no way to pass the cost along. The war is a policy choice. Inflation is the invoice.

Trump’s Favorite Kind of Accounting

Trump’s response is the usual political fraud in plain sight. He says he loves the numbers, promises inflation will “drop like a rock” when the war ends, and treats a 4.2% reading as if the country should applaud because it is not worse. That is not reassurance. It is damage control disguised as optimism.

Henrietta Treyz does the useful thing here by refusing the fake comfort. Her point is not that inflation is exploding uncontrollably. It is that the president is celebrating a result that is already eating into wages. That is a much sharper critique than conventional coverage usually allows.

Blame Needs a Better Target

The article correctly identifies the economic harm, but the wider political issue is the normalization of deliberate harm as if it were a regrettable accident. When a president wages war, drives up energy costs, and then talks about the numbers as if they are abstract data, the problem is not confusion. It is governance by externalized suffering.

The weaker actors in the story are the ones paying the bill: households, workers, consumers, and shippers. The stronger actor is the one with the power to start the war, claim credit for the outcome, and pretend the pain is just a statistic.

The Pattern

This is the standard operating procedure of modern power: concentrate decision-making at the top, disperse the costs downward, and then frame the damage as an economic condition rather than a political act. War becomes inflation. Inflation becomes inconvenience. Inconvenience becomes something the public is supposed to absorb.

That is not mismanagement. It is a governing style built on coercion, misdirection, and the expectation that ordinary people will pay for decisions they did not make.
