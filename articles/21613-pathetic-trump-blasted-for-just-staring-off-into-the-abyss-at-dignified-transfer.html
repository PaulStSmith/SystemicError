'Pathetic' Trump blasted for just 'staring off into the abyss' at dignified transfer

A War Without Strategy Is Still a Choice

The Men With the Power

The reporting is simple enough: Rep. Pat Ryan, a Democratic veteran, says Trump’s Iran war is being run by “an absolute clown car,” and the House has now voted to pass a war powers resolution rebuking it. That is the context. The point is not the insult. The point is that Trump and his administration hold the institutional power to keep escalating, while Congress is left trying to claw back authority after lives have already been put in the line of fire.

“No Strategy” Is Not an Excuse

Ryan says the administration does not have a strategy and that the conflict is costing more lives. That framing matters because it gets one thing half right and one thing wrong. The absence of a coherent plan is real, but it is not the core scandal. The core scandal is that Trump is willing to use military force without demonstrating any durable public purpose beyond display, domination, and political spectacle.

Calling this confusion softens the responsibility. If a president keeps sending people into danger while unable or unwilling to explain the objective, that is not drift. That is governance by impulse, with bodies attached.

Republicans Are Not Bystanders

Ryan is right to put Republican lawmakers on the hook. If they refuse to vote for the war powers resolution, they are not merely “complicit” in some abstract sense. They are helping preserve the machinery that lets Trump continue the war while pretending the legislature is merely watching from the sidelines.

This is how institutional cowardice works: the people with power create distance from the consequences, then ask for credit when they finally object after the damage is visible. A vote against restraint is a vote to keep the pipeline open.

Dover Was the Point

Ryan’s anger at Trump’s behavior during the dignified transfer at Dover Air Force Base cuts to the heart of the matter. The image is not just offensive; it is revealing. A president who can stand beside the coffins of dead service members and project indifference is not failing at empathy. He is performing the basic contempt that underwrites militarized politics: sacrifice from others, insulation for himself.

Ryan also pointed to Trump as a draft dodger willing to send other people’s children to fight while his sons profit from defense industry stocks. That accusation is politically potent because it connects war to class privilege. The people issuing the orders do not absorb the costs. They monetize them.

The Real Story Is Accountability Delayed

This article is less about one remark from one Democrat than about a recurring American pattern: executive aggression, congressional delay, and public grief staged after the fact. Trump and his team made the decision to continue the war. Republicans who refuse to stop it are helping him own that decision. And the language of “no strategy” serves as a familiar fog machine, turning deliberate escalation into managerial incompetence.

The democratic coalition should be saying this plainly: wars launched and sustained without clear strategy are not accidents. They are expressions of power. When leaders treat dead service members as a backdrop and lawmakers treat war powers as optional, the system is not malfunctioning. It is revealing who gets to spend other people’s lives.
