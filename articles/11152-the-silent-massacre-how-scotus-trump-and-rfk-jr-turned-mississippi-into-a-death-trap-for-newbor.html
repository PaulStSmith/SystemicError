The Silent Massacre: How SCOTUS, Trump, and RFK Jr. Turned Mississippi Into a Death Trap for Newborns

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The headlines are grim, but the reality is worse: Mississippi has formally declared a public health emergency after recording 9.7 infant deaths per 1,000 live births in 2024—a rate that dwarfs the national average of 5.6. More than 3,500 babies have died before their first birthday since 2014. For a modern, wealthy nation, this should be unthinkable. Instead, it is becoming the new normal, deliberately engineered by political negligence and judicial cruelty.

At the heart of this catastrophe lies the Supreme Court’s Dobbs decision, which originated in Mississippi itself. By overturning Roe v. Wade, SCOTUS handed states carte blanche to force women to carry pregnancies to term—regardless of medical risk, personal circumstances, or the capacity of local health systems to provide care. Mississippi, already a maternity care desert with half its counties lacking OB-GYN services, was the perfect test case. The Court knew exactly what it was doing: empowering a state already notorious for neglecting maternal and infant health.

The result? More pregnancies carried under duress, more preterm births, more low-birthweight infants, and more coffins.

A Manufactured Crisis

Let’s be clear: this isn’t an act of God. It’s an act of policy.

  • Hospital closures: Rural hospitals across Mississippi have shuttered labor and delivery wards, leaving pregnant women to drive hours for basic care. Time lost in transit translates directly into higher rates of preterm birth and infant mortality.

  • Medicaid failure: Mississippi has refused full Medicaid expansion, keeping thousands of low-income women uninsured between pregnancies. Postpartum coverage was grudgingly extended to 12 months in 2023, but without expansion, the systemic gap remains.

  • Racial disparity: Black infants in Mississippi die at a rate of 15.2 per 1,000, nearly triple the white infant mortality rate of 5.8. The crisis is not only medical but racial, reflecting entrenched inequities that SCOTUS and the state legislature have chosen to deepen.

The emergency declaration in 2024 is less a response than a confession: the state is drowning in deaths it could have prevented.

Enter Trump and Kennedy: Malpractice as Policy

If Mississippi’s crisis was predictable after Dobbs, the federal response under Trump and RFK Jr. has been worse than silence—it has been sabotage.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr., installed at Health and Human Services, has treated public health as his personal crusade against science. His tenure has been marked by:

  • Vaccine sabotage: Kennedy dismantled advisory panels, froze grants, and fueled vaccine skepticism during the worst measles outbreak in 33 years. Six major medical organizations sued his department, accusing him of placing children at “grave and immediate risk.”

  • CDC turmoil: He ousted Director Susan Monarez weeks into her tenure after she resisted political meddling. At least three senior CDC officials resigned, citing erosion of scientific integrity. Over 750 health employees signed an open letter condemning Kennedy’s hostility.

  • Lawless data sharing: HHS under Kennedy unlawfully handed Medicaid enrollee data to DHS, turning health systems into tools of immigration enforcement.

  • Fraudulent science: His “Make America Healthy Again” Commission report cited studies that didn’t exist, with AI-generated phantom references. The White House waved this off as “formatting issues.”

Combine Kennedy’s malpractice with Trump’s weaponization of government, and you have a regime not just indifferent to health outcomes but openly hostile to them.

Prognosis: A Darker Tomorrow for Mississippi’s Infants

If current trends hold, Mississippi’s infant mortality rate is poised to climb beyond 10 deaths per 1,000 live births by 2025, solidifying its position as the deadliest state in America to be born. The drivers are obvious:

  1. Forced births from abortion bans will continue to swell the number of high-risk pregnancies.

  2. Eroding healthcare access as hospitals close and OB-GYNs flee the state.

  3. Federal sabotage of vaccination, maternal health programs, and scientific integrity under Kennedy’s HHS.

  4. Economic strangulation as Trump’s administration starves safety-net programs in favor of tax cuts and culture-war theatrics.

What we are witnessing is not just statistical drift but systemic collapse. Infant mortality is the canary in the coal mine of public health. When babies cannot survive their first year, the society around them is terminally ill.

SCOTUS’s Bloody Handprints

Let us not forget: this began at the feet of the Supreme Court. By overturning Roe, the Court forced women into pregnancies the system cannot support. Mississippi was the spearhead, and now its babies are the casualties. The conservative justices—Roberts, Alito, Thomas, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett—own these coffins. They knew Mississippi’s healthcare record. They knew the racial disparities. They knew the consequences. And they chose ideology over life.

Conclusion: The Politics of Neglect

Mississippi’s emergency is not a local story. It is a national parable of what happens when ideology trumps science, when cruelty replaces policy, and when courts and cabinets alike abandon their duty to protect life. SCOTUS laid the groundwork, Trump provided the permission structure, and RFK Jr. is administering the poison.

The prognosis is clear: without radical change, Mississippi will not only remain the deadliest state for infants—it will drag the nation further into a health abyss. The so-called pro-life movement has birthed a system where babies die faster, mothers suffer more, and science is strangled at the altar of power.

This is not a failure of foresight. It is a deliberate choice. And every tiny coffin lowered into Mississippi soil is a testament to that choice.
