Trump doesn't understand 21st-century warfare — and it cost America dearly

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 29, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Misguided Militarism of Trump’s “Warrior Ethos”

The Misguided Militarism of Trump’s “Warrior Ethos”

Misleading Revitalization Claims

President Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth are touting a return to traditional military values by rejecting what they label as “woke” policies and promoting a “warrior ethos.” This rhetoric serves a dual purpose: it distracts from their administrative failures and appeals to a base enamored with an antiquated notion of military might. However, their claims of revitalizing the military ignore the realities of modern warfare and technological evolution, as highlighted by Jonathan V. Last’s critique in The Bulwark.

Misunderstanding Modern Warfare

Last’s analysis exposes a critical flaw in Trump’s military strategy: a failure to adapt to the changing landscape of war. The focus on traditional combat readiness, including the bizarre procurement of cavalry horses as Last metaphorically suggests, starkly contrasts with the advancements our adversaries have made, particularly in drone technology. Trump’s initiation of a war against Iran and the subsequent strategic missteps underscore a dangerous ignorance about the capabilities and economic efficiencies provided by drones, which have reshaped military engagements worldwide.

Economic Realities of War

The economic aspect of warfare, which Trump reportedly misconstrues, is where the U.S. could face significant disadvantages. Last points out the cost-effectiveness of drones in modern conflict, using the example of Ukraine’s strategy against Russian drones. The U.S. reliance on expensive, large-scale military hardware does not just drain resources but is also increasingly ineffective against cheaper, scalable technologies like drones that can be deployed en masse by adversaries.

Strategic Blunders and Global Influence

Trump’s strategic blunders have broader implications beyond just military inefficacy. Last argues that these missteps have contributed to a decrease in American influence globally. By failing to prepare the military for modern threats and clinging to outdated concepts of power, Trump has not only jeopardized U.S. national security but has also diminished its standing on the world stage, potentially heralding the “end of the American-led global order.”

The Systemic Danger of Misplaced Priorities

Trump and Hegseth’s focus on a so-called “warrior ethos” and their rejection of modern military strategies represent a broader systemic issue in American politics: the glorification of past military prowess at the expense of necessary adaptation and innovation. This misdirection serves not only to placate a certain voter base but also dangerously misaligns national defense priorities. It is a vivid example of how leadership, entrenched in nostalgic nationalism, can lead a country astray.

Conclusion: The Need for Realistic and Forward-Thinking Defense Strategies

The narrative pushed by Trump and Hegseth is more than just political posturing; it is a serious misstep in policy that could have dire consequences for U.S. national security. The focus should not be on reviving an outdated “warrior ethos” but on preparing for the realities of contemporary and future conflicts. Leadership requires not only strength but also wisdom and foresight—qualities sorely missing from the current administration’s approach to military strategy.
