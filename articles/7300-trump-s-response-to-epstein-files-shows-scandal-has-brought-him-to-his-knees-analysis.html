Ipaper columnist Simon Marks argues every “strategic effort” by President Donald Trump’s administration to protect him from the most damaging Epstein files “is not working.”

“The U.S. president is now twisting in the wind as reporters wait for each batch of files placed into the public domain. On Monday, the release of an additional 30,000 pages of documents, emails and images raised fresh questions for Trump, who makes numerous appearances throughout the material.”

Meanwhile, lawmakers are accusing the government of deliberately slowing the release of all files pertaining to convicted sex-trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, including one-time ally Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.), who shared a screenshot from the files and with the claim that “only evil people would hide this and protect those who participated.” Marks points out that other MAGA leaders, like Steve Bannon and MAGA influencer Laura Loomer, have also criticized the administration for its handling of the matter.

“The Epstein files have torn through Trump’s base, with typically supportive Fox News hosts warning in the summer that Trump risked losing support over the matter,” Marks said.

And Trump’s faithful administrators appear powerless to do anything but accidentally incriminate him further. Trump’s highly politicized Department of Justice failed to deliver a predictable reaction to one letter’s explosive contents alleging Epstein telling fellow child predator Larry Nassar that “When a young beauty walked by [Trump] loved to ‘grab snatch’, whereas we ended up snatching grub in the mess halls of the [prison] system … Life is unfair,” Epstein allegedly wrote.

Seemingly caught flat-footed by the attention the letter drew, the DOJ issued a statement on X claiming officials were “currently looking into the validity of this alleged letter … and we will follow up as soon as possible.” Two hours later, the department was calling the letter “FAKE” and claiming the handwriting didn’t match that of Epstein, but without including its forensic analysis. This, said Marks, “raised serious questions about the process behind the scenes.”

“Trump’s second term has not even seen its first full year, yet the Epstein scandal is bringing him to his knees. Congressman Thomas Massie, a dissident MAGA Republican from Kentucky, accused Attorney General Pam Bondi of ‘working fervently to redact, omit and delete Epstein files she is legally required to release.’” Marks said. “Together with Democrat Ro Khanna of California, he is drafting articles of impeachment to lay against Bondi, accusing her of a ‘bogus Epstein release … protecting the rich, powerful and politically connected.’”

Trump ran for office promising to release all the files, exploiting right-wing conspiracy theorists claiming of a secret sex-trafficking ring involving powerful Democrats, but Marks said “now, hard questions are mounting for Trump, and his steady political demise based on these revelations will only be hastened by the hapless behavior of his Department of Justice.”

“His conduct could be innocent, but many of his supporters want to know why his own government is managing to make him look guilty,” Marks said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tangled Web: The Epstein Files Debacle

In a recent column for Ipaper, Simon Marks delivers a scathing analysis of President Donald Trump’s handling of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. Marks argues that the Trump administration’s attempts to shield the president from the fallout of the Epstein files are failing spectacularly, exposing a chaotic and possibly culpable stance towards the allegations linked to Trump. Let’s dissect this mess with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism it duly warrants.

First, the sheer volume of documents—30,000 pages of emails, images, and more, implicating Trump in the sordid Epstein saga—is staggering. Trump’s frequent appearances in the material are more than just embarrassing footnotes; they are blaring sirens calling for a thorough investigation. The administration’s strategy, or lack thereof, seems to be crumbling under the weight of its own contradictions.

Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle are raising their voices against the administration’s apparent foot-dragging. When figures like Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene, a staunch Trump ally, accuse the government of protecting the guilty, you know the ship isn’t just taking on water; it’s on fire. The bipartisan outcry, including efforts from MAGA dissident Congressman Thomas Massie and Democrat Ro Khanna to draft articles of impeachment against Attorney General Pam Bondi, underscores the gravity of the situation. Their allegations of Bondi’s “bogus Epstein release” efforts highlight a desperate attempt to shield the rich and powerful at the expense of transparency and justice.

The Department of Justice’s (DOJ) bungled response to the alleged Epstein letter mentioning Trump is a masterclass in ineptitude. By first promising to investigate the letter’s validity and then quickly labeling it “FAKE” without providing any forensic analysis to support the claim, the DOJ did nothing but fan the flames of suspicion. One cannot help but marvel at the department’s ability to simultaneously appear inept and complicit.

Marks astutely points out the irony of Trump’s predicament. The president, who capitalized on right-wing conspiracy theories about a secret sex-trafficking ring involving powerful Democrats, now finds himself ensnared in a similar narrative. The difference here is the mounting evidence and the administration’s own goal of making Trump look guilty through its mishandling of the situation.

The Impact on Trump’s Base and Political Future is perhaps the most fascinating aspect of this debacle. The Epstein scandal has cut through Trump’s base, with even Fox News hosts—longtime defenders of the president—warning of waning support. The fractured MAGA movement, with influential figures like Steve Bannon and Laura Loomer criticizing the administration’s handling of the files, signals a significant shift. Trump’s second term is barely underway, yet this scandal threatens to undermine his presidency and erode his support base further.

In conclusion, Simon Marks’ column is not just a critique but a stark warning. It reveals the deep fissures within the Trump administration and the MAGA movement, exacerbated by the Epstein scandal. The administration’s attempts to protect Trump have backfired, leaving him “twisting in the wind.” This debacle is not merely about the mishandling of documents; it’s a glaring testament to the administration’s moral and ethical bankruptcy. The question now is not if this will affect Trump’s legacy, but how devastating the impact will be. The answer, it seems, is unfolding in real time, with each new batch of documents released to the public.
