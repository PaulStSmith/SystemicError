'Take a little rest': Trump makes remarkable 180 after blasting longtime ally as 'traitor'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unraveling of MAGA Loyalties: Trump vs. Greene

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as he walks to board Marine One to depart for Joint Base Andrews, from the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 22, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

President Donald Trump has openly gloated about Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s (R-GA) resignation announcement — going so far as to describe the longtime MAGA ally a “traitor” — but told NBC News he’d be happy to see her career resume, after a little break.

Trump reamed into Greene on his Truth Social platform, writing to followers, “Marjorie ‘Traitor’ Brown, because of PLUMMETING Poll Numbers, and not wanting to face a Primary Challenger with a strong Trump Endorsement (where she would have no chance of winning!), has decided to call it ‘quits.’ Her relationship with the WORST Republican Congressman in decades, Tom Massie of Kentucky, also known as Rand Paul Jr. because he votes against the Republican Party (and really good legislation!), did not help her,” he wrote.

His post came after the firebrand Republican lawmaker announced her surprise intention to resign in January, writing, “I’m going back to the people that I love to live my life to the fullest as I always have and I look forward to a new path ahead. I’ll be resigning from office with my last day being January 5, 2026, and I look forward to seeing many of you again sometime in the future.”

But the president eased his tone a bit to NBC News, later saying in a short phone call, “it’s not going to be easy for her,” to resurrect her career, but that he’d “love to see that.”

The president said she needs to “take a little rest” first, but that he can “patch up differences with anyone.”

Greene has publicly broken with Trump on major issues, including criticizing him over his reluctance to release the Jeffrey Epstein files. Trump hit back this month with his announcement that he was no longer supporting her re-election campaign. A person close to Greene told NBC News she felt confident she could still win her district even without the president’s endorsement. Instead, Greene’s decision was made due to threats and scorn she and her family have received, including harassment at restaurants and airports.

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of MAGA Loyalties: Trump vs. Greene

In a theatrical display of political loyalty and betrayal that could rival any Shakespearean drama, President Donald Trump has once again captured the spotlight by openly gloating over Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s (R-GA) resignation announcement. Labeling the one-time MAGA stalwart a “traitor”, Trump’s response is a vivid illustration of the volatile nature of political alliances within the Republican Party, especially those orbiting around the former president himself.

Trump’s Reaction: A Study in Political Opportunism

Trump’s scathing critique on his Truth Social platform, branding Greene as Marjorie ‘Traitor’ Brown and attributing her resignation to “PLUMMETING Poll Numbers,” is a classic Trump maneuver. It showcases his penchant for public shaming and the abandonment of allies when they are perceived as weak or disloyal. His assertion that Greene feared facing a primary challenger “with a strong Trump Endorsement” lays bare the power dynamics and the cult of personality that Trump continues to wield over the GOP.

Moreover, Trump’s denouncement of Greene’s association with Congressman Tom Massie, whom he disparagingly refers to as Rand Paul Jr., further underscores the former president’s intolerance for dissent within his ranks. Trump’s definition of disloyalty appears to include not only personal betrayals but also deviations from his political line, as seen in his criticism of Massie’s voting record.

Greene’s Resignation: A Forced Retreat?

Greene’s resignation announcement, characterized by a desire to return to a semblance of normalcy away from the political arena, hints at the intense pressure and scrutiny faced by those in Trump’s orbit. Her statement speaks volumes about the personal toll that political life can exact, especially for controversial figures under constant public and media scrutiny. The mention of looking forward to a “new path” and “resigning from office with my last day being January 5, 2026,” suggests a strategic retreat rather than a complete withdrawal from the political scene.

Trump’s Olive Branch: A Conditional Reconciliation

Trump’s subsequent softening of tone in a conversation with NBC News, stating he would “love to see” Greene resume her career after a “little rest,” is emblematic of his transactional approach to relationships. His claim that he can “patch up differences with anyone” is a testament to his belief in his personal charm and political acumen. However, this olive branch comes with implicit conditions: loyalty and alignment with Trump’s political agenda.

The Fallout: Beyond Trump and Greene

This saga is not merely about the fallout between two high-profile political figures. It’s a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the Republican Party, grappling with internal divisions and the overarching shadow of Trump’s influence. Greene’s public break with Trump over issues like the Jeffrey Epstein files and her subsequent estrangement underscores the precarious position of those who dare to deviate from the party line.

Conclusion: The MAGA Movement’s Identity Crisis

As the MAGA movement continues to navigate its post-Trump identity, the rift between Trump and Greene serves as a cautionary tale about the perils of political loyalty in an era defined by polarizing figures and divisive politics. The real question remains: Can the Republican Party reconcile its internal divisions, or will it continue to be torn apart by loyalty tests and the whims of its most charismatic, yet polarizing, figures? The drama between Trump and Greene is just another chapter in this unfolding saga, highlighting the systemic errors within American political partisanship.
