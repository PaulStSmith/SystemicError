The first lady publicized the $MELANIA coin in a Truth Social post on Jan. 19, hours before her husband was inaugurated for a second term as president, and two dozen digital wallets bought $2.6 million of the tokens in the two and a half minutes before her post went live, reported the Financial Times.

Prices for the tokens took off after Melania Trump announced the cryptocurrency, and the wallets in which they'd been deposited quickly sold off most of their coins, with 81 percent of those transactions taking place within 12 hours.

One wallet spent $681,000 on the memecoin in a single transaction 64 seconds before the public announcement, and that account made $39 million from selling most of its holdings within 24 hours. That wallet made another $4.4 million from selling the rest in the next three days.

Two dozen accounts bought up to 16.7 million of all the 200 million $MELANIA tokens available during the launch period, and another 22 accounts purchased about $900,000 worth of tokens in the 42 seconds after launch.

The $MELANIA memecoin has no function beyond being a vehicle for speculation, with early buyers having an opportunity to make enormous profits if interest surges. It was launched about two days after the president-to-be launched his own $TRUMP token.

The Financial Times analyzed purchases of the $MELANIA tokens and found the entities which launched the coins, which are separate from the wallets that trade in it before the first lady's announcement, have raked in $64.7 million in profits from fees and primary sales.

Texas-based crypto entrepreneur Hayden Davis, who was also involved in the launch of the $LIBRA memecoin briefly endorsed by Argentinian president Javier Milei, has said he was involved in the launch of $MELANIA, and FT confirmed the first wallet to buy the tokens ahead of the launch was funded by an account that was previously used in ventures involving the 28-year-old investor – who denied speculating in the coins.

“There was no money made from the Melania team," Davis told the Coffeezilla website. "We didn’t take any liquidity out. Zero.”

The first account, which FT potentially linked to Davis, bought $40,000 of $MELANIA 141 seconds prior to launch and went on to sell 86 percent of its holdings within two hours, which came to profits of $2.5 million.

Analysis of the Melania Trump Cryptocurrency Token Fiasco

The unveiling of the $MELANIA cryptocurrency token has exposed a glaring exhibition of speculative frenzy, insider trading, and the glaring loopholes in the regulation of digital assets. Let's dissect the facts laid bare by the Financial Times and scrutinize the underbelly of this digital gold rush.

A Preface to Profiteering

Before the public was even aware of the existence of the $MELANIA cryptocurrency, a select group of investors had already positioned themselves to rake in vast fortunes. The article reports that two dozen digital wallets engaged in a buying spree of $2.6 million worth of tokens in the mere two and a half minutes prior to Melania Trump's announcement on Truth Social. This preemptive strike on the market reeks of insider knowledge and manipulation, a stark illustration of how the cryptocurrency market remains the Wild West of the financial world.

The Windfall

The aftermath of the announcement saw the price of $MELANIA tokens skyrocket, with the early birds swiftly unloading their holdings at steep profits. One particular wallet, which invested $681,000 mere seconds before the public unveiling, managed to transform that into $39 million within a day. The scale of this profit is not just staggering; it's a slap in the face of every notion of fair and equitable market practices.

A Token Without Purpose

The $MELANIA memecoin, as pointed out, serves no functional purpose beyond being a speculative vehicle. This revelation is hardly shocking yet underscores the speculative mania that has often gripped the crypto markets. It's a potent reminder of the tulip mania of the 17th century, albeit in a digital avatar. The primary lure for early buyers was the anticipation of a surge in interest post-launch—a bet that seemingly paid off handsomely for those in the know.

Profit from Thin Air

The entities behind the launch of the $MELANIA tokens, as per the Financial Times' investigation, have pocketed $64.7 million from fees and primary sales. This figure, while astronomical, also highlights the opaque nature of cryptocurrency launches and the urgent need for regulatory frameworks that can mitigate these brazen profit-making schemes at the expense of the uninformed majority.

Hayden Davis: A Curious Involvement

Hayden Davis, a Texas-based crypto entrepreneur with a history of involvement in speculative tokens, emerges as a focal point in this saga. Despite Davis' denial of profiteering from the $MELANIA launch, the Financial Times draws a connection to an early purchasing wallet that reaped millions in profits. The denials, in light of the evidence presented, appear hollow and raise serious questions about the ethical compass of those orbiting the cryptocurrency launch space.

Conclusion: A Mirror to the Crypto Wild West

The $MELANIA token launch saga is a textbook case of speculative excess, insider trading, and the exploitation of unregulated spaces for massive profit. It lays bare the urgent need for comprehensive regulation in the cryptocurrency market to protect investors and maintain market integrity. As long as entities can generate millions from speculative instruments devoid of intrinsic value, the crypto market will remain a treacherous terrain for the average investor.

In a world where transparency, fairness, and accountability are already in short supply, the cryptocurrency market, as illustrated by the $MELANIA token debacle, continues to operate in a shadow realm, far removed from these principles. It's high time for a regulatory reckoning.
