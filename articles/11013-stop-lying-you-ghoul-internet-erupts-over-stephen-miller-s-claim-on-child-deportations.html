'Stop lying, you ghoul': Internet erupts over Stephen Miller's claim on child deportations

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Distortion: Stephen Miller's Misleading CrusadeConclusion: The Real Villains

White House deputy chief of staff for policy Stephen Miller attends the annual White House Easter Egg Roll with his family, including his wife, Katie Miller, left, on the South Lawn of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 21, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

The internet erupted on Sunday after President Donald Trump's immigration advisor attempted to smear a judge for refusing to deport hundreds of children in the middle of the night.

Stephen Miller, the White House deputy chief of staff, wrote on X that U.S. District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan of the District of Columbia was "refusing" to allow hundreds of Guatemalan children to be reunited with their parents. His comments came after Judge Sooknanan issued an order prohibiting the Trump administration from deporting the children.

"The minors have all self-reported that their parents are back home in Guatemala," Miller wrote in a post on X. "But a Democrat judge is refusing to let them reunify with their parents."

Lawyers and legal analysts shredded Miller's claims on social media.

"Stop lying, you ghoul," Timothy Bellman, a Democrat in West Virginia, posted on X.

"Stephen Miller's rant is pure garbage, total fake news from Trump's anti-immigrant goon!" activist Mike Young posted on X. "Judge Sooknanan blocks hasty deports of 700 unaccompanied kids for just two weeks to confirm safe homes, upholding laws you ignore! Your boss separated thousands in 2018, crying wolf now? We defend children from bullies like you, not exploit them."

"Stephen Miller is a liar," immigration lawyer Aaron Reichlin-Melnick posted on X. "Here is a sworn declaration from one of the children saying that their mother is dead and that they left because they suffered abuse and neglect at the hands of their family, including their surviving father. The child wants to stay and seek protection."

"This is devoid of factual or legal relevance," Kyle Cheney, Politico's senior legal affairs reporter, posted on X. "Lawyers for the children say some were fleeing abusive situations, and say the deportation effort was undertaken abruptly overnight and without legally required notice or protections to ensure safety. Whether their parents are in Guatemala + whether admin complied with law are distinct questions for court to resolve."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Distortion: Stephen Miller's Misleading Crusade

In what can only be described as a masterclass in misinformation, Stephen Miller, President Donald Trump's immigration advisor and White House deputy chief of staff, launched an offensive against U.S. District Judge Sparkle Sooknanan. Miller's weapon of choice? A blatantly misleading narrative on the social media platform X, formerly known as Twitter, aimed at discrediting the judge's lawful decision to protect hundreds of Guatemalan children from hasty and potentially unsafe deportations.

Miller's Misrepresentation: Miller accused Judge Sooknanan of obstructing the reunification of these children with their parents in Guatemala. This claim not only distorts the situation but also conveniently omits crucial context. The judge's order was a protective measure, ensuring that before any deportations took place, there would be a confirmation that the children would be returning to safe environments. Miller's framing of this precaution as a refusal to reunify families is not just misleading; it's manipulative.

Social Media Strikes Back: The reaction on social media was swift and scathing. Figures from various quarters — including Democrats, activists, and legal analysts — condemned Miller's portrayal of events. Their responses highlighted a glaring omission in Miller's narrative: the administration's own controversial immigration policies, which have previously resulted in the separation of thousands of children from their parents.

The Facts Speak Louder: Beyond the political grandstanding, the real issue at hand is the well-being of the children. Legal representatives and journalists pointed out that some of these children were fleeing abusive situations, contradicting Miller's insinuation that all were eager to return to their families in Guatemala. The case of a child expressing a desire to stay in the U.S. due to abuse and neglect at home starkly undermines Miller's simplistic depiction of the situation.

Legal and Moral Obligations: Kyle Cheney, Politico's senior legal affairs reporter, emphasized the legal and moral responsibilities ignored by Miller's narrative. The deportation effort, reportedly undertaken abruptly and without the legally required notices or protections, raises serious concerns about the administration's compliance with the law and its disregard for the children's safety.

Conclusion: The Real Villains

Stephen Miller's attempt to cast Judge Sooknanan as the villain in this saga is a transparent effort to divert attention from the administration's own failings. The real story here is not about a judge "refusing" to allow reunification but about a system that too often prioritizes political expediency over human rights and legal safeguards.

Miller's narrative is not just devoid of factual or legal relevance; it's a dangerous distortion of reality. It's a reminder of the lengths to which some will go to defend indefensible policies and to smear those who stand in their way. In this case, however, the facts are too strong, and the moral ground too solid, for such tactics to succeed.

The backlash against Miller's statements is a beacon of hope. It shows that there are still many who are willing to speak out, to correct the record, and to defend the most vulnerable among us. It's a testament to the resilience of truth in the face of deceit and manipulation.
