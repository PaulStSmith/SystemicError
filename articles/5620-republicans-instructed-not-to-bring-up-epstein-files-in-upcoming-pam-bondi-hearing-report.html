Republicans instructed not to bring up Epstein files in upcoming Pam Bondi hearing: report

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a Senate Judiciary Committee oversight hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Attorney General Pam Bondi is scheduled to testify before the House Judiciary Committee on Wednesday, Feb. 11, but according to journalist Aaron Parnas, Republicans on the committee have been instructed not to mention the Justice Department’s botched release of files on Jeffrey Epstein.

“Epstein survivors tell me that they were told in meetings with Republican lawmakers that everyone was supportive of asking Pam Bondi hard questions about the Epstein files,” Parnas wrote in a statement shared on social media Saturday.

“But, afterwards, they were told by a source in the Republican establishment that those questions won’t be asked. Republicans members have been told to avoid any questions about the files and to only focus on the ‘positives’ that Bondi accomplished.”

Bondi is among the most scrutinized Trump administration officials regarding the DOJ’s botched handling of its investigation into Epstein. Last year, Bondi told Fox News that Epstein’s supposed “client list” of powerful figures was “sitting on my desk right now,” only to later sign off on a DOJ memo that stated Epstein never maintained a client list.

Under Bondi’s leadership, the DOJ appeared fully prepared to shut down all further inquiries into Epstein until it was forced last fall to produce files after the passage of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, a bill that passed amid fierce pushback from President Donald Trump, at least initially.

Even with the recent release of around 3.5 million Epstein files, critics say that the DOJ and the Trump administration are still in direct violation of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which required the DOJ to release all of its files on Epstein, and with redactions limited to protecting the identity of minors and victims.

Instead, the DOJ has said it intends on withholding millions of more files. The agency appeared to have also redacted published files beyond what the law permits.

Our Analysis:

The Evasive Tactics of the GOP and DOJ on the Epstein Files: A Closer Look

In an era where the veneer of accountability in politics continues to crack, the recent developments surrounding Attorney General Pam Bondi’s scheduled testimony before the House Judiciary Committee on the botched release of files on Jeffrey Epstein are not just disappointing, but alarmingly indicative of a deeper rot within the political and justice system.

Journalist Aaron Parnas brings to light a troubling account where Republicans on the committee have been explicitly told to dodge any mention of the Justice Department’s mishandling of the Epstein investigation. This directive to focus solely on the so-called ‘positives’ of Bondi’s tenure, as reported by Parnas, is a blatant attempt to sweep under the rug a scandal of monumental proportions. It’s a stark reminder of how political allegiances are often prioritized over justice and transparency.

Pam Bondi’s role in the DOJ’s handling of the Epstein case has been under intense scrutiny, and for good reason. Her claim of having Epstein’s “client list” on her desk, followed by a DOJ memo denying the existence of such a list, reeks of misinformation and evasion. Under her leadership, the DOJ was seemingly prepared to bury any further inquiries into Epstein, only relenting when forced by the passage of the Epstein Files Transparency Act. This act, which faced initial opposition from President Donald Trump, was a rare moment of legislative backbone meant to shed light on Epstein’s nefarious activities and connections.

However, the recent release of approximately 3.5 million Epstein files, while significant, falls dramatically short of the full transparency demanded by the Epstein Files Transparency Act. The DOJ’s intention to withhold millions more files, and its excessive redaction of the ones released, is a clear violation of the law. It’s a slap in the face to the survivors of Epstein’s crimes and to the public, whose right to know is being blatantly disregarded.

The bipartisan effort to hold Bondi in contempt for her defiance of the Epstein Files Transparency Act is a necessary step in holding public officials accountable. Yet, the immediate pushback from the DOJ against these plans is not surprising. It’s emblematic of a department that has, time and again, placed political survival over its duty to the American people.

The avoidance of hard questions about the Epstein investigation by Republican committee members is more than a political maneuver; it’s a moral failure. It underscores a disturbing willingness to ignore the heinous crimes associated with Epstein in favor of political expediency. The silence and obfuscation surrounding this case only serve to protect the powerful at the expense of the vulnerable.

This situation is a litmus test for the integrity of our political and justice systems. The evasion, the misinformation, and the outright defiance of the law by figures like Bondi and the institutions they represent, cannot be the final word on the Epstein saga. The public deserves answers, survivors deserve justice, and the law demands compliance. Anything short of complete transparency and accountability is a systemic failure that we must not accept.
