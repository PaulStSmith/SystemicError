Trump vs. Newsom: The Accidental Launch of the 2028 Democratic Nominee

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

It’s a bitter irony of American politics that the louder a demagogue howls, the more power he accidentally bestows upon his opponents. In his feverish quest to punish California and flex his imagined kingly authority, Donald J. Trump may have done what Gavin Newsom’s own advisors could only dream of: launched his 2028 presidential campaign with all the force of a prime-time propaganda blitz.

The backdrop? Los Angeles in chaos. Protesters flooding the streets in the wake of another round of federal aggression. And in the middle of it all, Governor Gavin Newsom—standing defiant, measured, and visible. Very, very visible.

Trump, in typical fashion, escalated the crisis beyond reason. Against the explicit objections of Newsom, he deployed the National Guard, and in a move as reckless as it was unconstitutional, threatened to send in the U.S. Marines. Marines. Into a major American city. Over protests. That alone would be historic—if it weren’t such a grotesque echo of authoritarianism. But what Trump fails to realize is that in doing so, he has created the perfect foil to his madness: Gavin Newsom.

While the former president plays dress-up dictator from his bunker-palace in Mar-a-Lago, Newsom is out in public, giving press conferences, reassuring Californians, and articulating a vision diametrically opposed to martial law and paranoia. And in the process, he’s becoming more than just a regional figure. He’s becoming the guy who stood up to Trump when it mattered.

Let’s be honest—Democrats are starving for a post-Biden figure with spine, presence, and a camera-ready profile. Kamala Harris’s approval ratings have stagnated. Pete Buttigieg, while competent, lacks mass appeal. Gretchen Whitmer and Wes Moore have potential, but they’re still orbiting the national consciousness. Meanwhile, Newsom is being rocketed to center stage courtesy of Trump’s tantrum machine.

He already has the bona fides: a governor of the largest state economy in the country, a long record on progressive policy, and a polished media presence honed by years in the political trenches. Add to that the simple narrative punch of standing firm against federal overreach, and you have the makings of a Democratic standard-bearer.

And let’s not pretend this is new for Newsom. His clashes with Trump go back to the pandemic era—recall when California’s aggressive lockdowns and mandates made it a punching bag for the right-wing media echo chamber. Newsom didn’t just survive the scrutiny; he thrived in it. He beat back a recall effort with ease. And now, as Trump once again tries to run roughshod over state sovereignty, Newsom is turning crisis into capital.

Even the optics are in his favor. Newsom is everything Trump is not: poised, articulate, and unfailingly professional. He speaks in complete sentences. He shows up. He doesn’t rant on Truth Social at 3am about light bulbs and windmills. For Americans exhausted by chaos, this isn’t just refreshing—it’s oxygen.

The strategy unfolding before our eyes isn’t even Newsom’s doing. That’s the beauty of it. Trump is handing him a spotlight and Newsom, wisely, is stepping into it with dignity and purpose. He’s not grandstanding; he’s governing. And in 21st-century America, that’s more revolutionary than it should be.

Meanwhile, the GOP base continues to cheer for military boots on civilian streets. And in doing so, they’re driving independents—and even disillusioned conservatives—into the arms of someone who seems to actually care about the Constitution.

The more Trump threatens, the stronger Newsom looks. The more chaos Trump sows, the more presidential Newsom appears. It’s a political judo move: letting your opponent’s force do all the work. And right now, Trump is throwing the full weight of his grotesque personality into a campaign that isn’t even his own.

So let’s call it what it is. The 2028 Democratic primary may still be years away, but the narrative has begun. It won’t be about whose resume is thicker, or whose stump speech is cleverer. It’ll be about who had the guts to stand up when America was teetering on the edge.

And unless something changes drastically, that man may very well be Gavin Newsom.

Congratulations, Mr. Trump. You may have just picked your successor.
