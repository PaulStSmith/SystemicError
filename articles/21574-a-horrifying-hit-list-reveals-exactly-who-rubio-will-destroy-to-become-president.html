A horrifying hit list reveals exactly who Rubio will destroy to become president

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

The Border Was the Pretext. The State Is the Product.

The Target Was Never Stable

The source story is about a familiar political trick: build a coercive machine in the name of a broadly popular grievance, then widen the target until the machine starts eating dissent itself. The first excuse was border security. The next was “the worst of the worst.” Now the same apparatus is being turned on legal residents, protesters, journalists, and progressives.

That is not drift. It is the logic of the program.

Power Built the Machine First

The people with actual power here are not the ones being dragged into vans. It is Trump, Vance, Miller, Rubio, ICE, and the institutions shielding them. The source makes the central point plain: the wall, the detention centers, the arrest infrastructure, and the databases all came before the open political repression. The administration did not stumble into this system. It constructed it.

The story also names the enabling class: Republicans who sold the crackdown as public safety, and a political culture that let mass detention become normal so long as the victims were first framed as outsiders.

The Lie Moved the Goalposts

The article’s strongest claim is also its most important. Once the state stopped limiting itself to people accused of crimes and started targeting gardeners, maids, and other ordinary workers, the original justification collapsed. That is the tell. When a policy begins by promising to isolate dangerous people and ends by rounding up brown and Black laborers, the stated rationale was never the real one.

Democrats only began to retreat when the racial and authoritarian nature of the project became too obvious to hide. That matters. It shows how long the democratic coalition tolerates abuse when the first victims are politically easy to discard.

Masks, Guns, and Impunity

The March 2025 shift described in the source is the move from mass detention to masked force. ICE agents began concealing their identities and using deadly violence against immigrants and protesters. The article says at least 30 people have been shot, at least eight killed, and at least 50 died in custody in the last 16 months, with no meaningful investigations and no prosecutions.

That is not a “rough patch” in enforcement. That is impunity becoming routine. A police force that can hide, shoot, and kill without consequence is not failing the rule of law. It is operating above it.

Speech Is the New Crime

The arrest of Salah Sarsour makes the next step impossible to miss. A legal permanent resident, a mosque president, a father and grandfather, was held for 80 days and, according to the judge, targeted for his activism on Palestinian rights. He was not accused of a crime. He was punished for a viewpoint.

That is the bridge from immigration enforcement to political policing. NSPM-7 then widens the aperture further by naming broad ideological categories as domestic-terror threats, and the Rubio document follows by listing activists, elected officials, and organizations as if political dissent were an intelligence file. The point is not precision. The point is to build a permission structure for repression.

ICE Is Not Broken

This story should not be read as a call for better training, smarter oversight, or a few more internal audits. ICE is doing what a coercive deportation agency does when insulated from democratic restraint: expanding, masking up, networking with surveillance systems, and testing how far it can go before anyone stops it. The article’s own facts point in one direction only.

ICE should be abolished. A state that uses immigration enforcement as a pilot program for political policing does not deserve a cleaner version of itself.

The Pattern Is the Point

The larger political pattern here is not confusion; it is conversion. A public safety panic is converted into detention capacity. Detention capacity is converted into racial sorting. Racial sorting is converted into censorship, intimidation, and political targeting. Once that sequence is in motion, the enemy only needs to be renamed.

That is how authoritarian power advances in a democracy that keeps mistaking its own institutions for guarantees. The institutions do not fail all at once. They are repurposed, one “reasonable” expansion at a time, until the machinery of enforcement is ready for dissidents.
