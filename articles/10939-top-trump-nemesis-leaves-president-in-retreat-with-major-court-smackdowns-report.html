Top Trump nemesis leaves president in retreat with major court smackdowns: report

Behind the scenes, one of President Donald Trump’s top nemeses is scoring major victories and leaving the president on the defensive. The Atlantic reported Monday the details of a highly organized, remarkably effective “legal resistance” to Trump’s ongoing efforts in his second term.

Former ethics czar, impeachment prosecutor, and frequent thorn in Trump’s side, Norm Eisen, “has pursued more than 100 legal matters” against Trump, according to the report.

With strong leadership counterbalancing a divided Democratic Party, Eisen has mobilized a coalition of lawyers, advocacy groups, unions, and state attorneys general to mount fast and aggressive legal challenges that have knocked down many Trump initiatives.

Eisen’s campaign operates on multiple fronts, coordinating legal strategy calls with a diverse group—including former Obama administration attorneys, Democratic communications strategists, and neoconservative critics. Their shared goal: to stop Trump’s worst actions and keep the public informed about ongoing litigation.

While Eisen’s group has taken on over 100 lawsuits, the broader resistance is making lawsuits great again, with an initiative bringing 384 complaints. So far, 130 of those suits have successfully blocked Trump's efforts, with dozens more still pending.

Eisen believes the legal battles are just beginning.

“All of our lawsuits tell stories,” said Randi Weingarten, the head of the American Federation of Teachers. “But you can’t ask the judge to do all the work. If you want to prevail, you have to put real stories and real harm in front of the judge.”

The most recent Trump victory was about firing federal officials at the Department of Education, whom Eisen said, Trump has no power to fire because it's a congressionally crafted program. Lower courts won each case, but the Supreme Court decided in Trump's favor.

“He did not win that round. We did not win either,” Eisen told The Atlantic, “but we held our own, and that in itself is a victory.”

Meanwhile, Trump continues to generate more lawsuits.

"Aggressive tactics by ICE have spawned new cases, as has the deployment of federal officers and National Guardsmen to crack down on crime in D.C," the report said.

Eisen is also preparing a lawsuit against Trump to challenge the financial benefits he and his family have received since taking office. The report concludes, “The battle, in short, is just beginning.”

“The demand for lawyers willing to defend people from the government is exponentially greater today than it was on day one,” Skye Perryman, Democracy Forward’s president, told The Atlantic. “We believe the next 200 days are going to be even more significant than the first 200 days.”

Our Analysis:

Norm Eisen: The Legal Nemesis in Trump's Side

The Atlantic's recent dive into Norm Eisen's concerted efforts against President Donald Trump's administration unveils a narrative that's as enthralling as it is critical. Eisen, a former ethics czar and impeachment prosecutor, has positioned himself as a formidable opponent to Trump’s second term, orchestrating a "legal resistance" that has not only put the president on the defensive but has also scored significant victories.

The Coalition of Resistance

Eisen’s strategy is both broad and deep, leveraging a coalition that spans lawyers, advocacy groups, unions, and state attorneys general. What's remarkable here is the diversity of this coalition, which includes former Obama administration attorneys, Democratic communications strategists, and even neoconservative critics. This is not just a battle; it's a full-scale war waged on multiple fronts, aiming to curb what they see as Trump's most egregious actions.

The article mentions over 100 legal matters pursued and a staggering 384 complaints initiated by this broader resistance, with 130 of those suits successfully blocking Trump initiatives. These numbers aren't just impressive; they're a testament to the efficacy and relentless nature of Eisen's campaign.

The Strategy: Tell the Story

Randi Weingarten's quote, “All of our lawsuits tell stories,” highlights a crucial aspect of this legal resistance. It's not just about filing lawsuits; it's about weaving narratives that showcase real harm and injustice, making it imperative for the judiciary to act. This approach underscores the power of storytelling in legal battles, aiming to put a human face on abstract legal challenges.

A Victory in Holding Ground

Eisen's pragmatic admission regarding a Supreme Court decision in Trump's favor reflects a nuanced understanding of legal warfare. Here, the victory is in the resilience, in “holding our own,” which speaks volumes about the long-haul perspective this resistance embodies. It's a strategic acknowledgment that not all battles will be won but standing firm is a victory in itself.

The Lawsuit Factory: Trump's Administration

One cannot ignore the sheer volume of lawsuits generated by Trump's actions, from "aggressive tactics by ICE" to the deployment of federal officers in D.C. Each lawsuit tells a story of opposition to policies perceived as overreaches or outright abuses of power, emphasizing the reactive nature of this legal resistance.

The Never-Ending Battle

The narrative concludes on a note that's both ominous and hopeful. Skye Perryman's remarks about the exponential demand for lawyers willing to defend people from the government, and the anticipation of an even more significant next 200 days, set the stage for a continuous struggle. It's a clear indication that, for Eisen and his coalition, the battle is far from over.

Final Thoughts

What this article elucidates is not just the story of a legal resistance against a presidency but a larger tale of democracy in action. It's about checks and balances, about holding power to account, and about the mobilization of a diverse coalition united against what they perceive as a threat to democratic norms and values.

In essence, Eisen’s campaign against Trump is a stark reminder of the legal system’s role as a battleground for political disputes. It’s a scenario where victories are measured not just by cases won, but by the ability to challenge, to resist, and to ensure that every action is scrutinized under the law. This isn't just legal resistance; it's a formidable assertion of democratic resilience.
