The obvious reason Republicans won't let the Clintons testify in public

Under Republican control, the aims of the House Oversight Committee are to promote partisan narratives rather than to reveal facts and advance public understanding of national issues. Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.), its chairman, has displayed that routinely self-serving approach in the committee’s “investigation” of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal -- and especially in his zeal to subpoena Bill and Hillary Clinton.

Comer was never among the tiny handful of Republicans who demanded that the Trump administration release the government’s files on the deceased sex predator. Instead, the leaden Comer dutifully followed President Donald Trump’s lead in defecting public anger over the case. Focusing on the Clintons, who know little (Bill) or nothing (Hillary) about this matter, is exactly how Trump has handled his own troubling connections with Epstein for the past several years.

With tens of thousands of mentions of Trump in the released Epstein materials, that distraction is more urgent than ever. And the Clintons somehow remain enticing targets for politicians like Comer and even some of the Democrats on his committee.

But after resisting the subpoenas for months -- until it became clear that a vote to hold them in contempt would pass the House -- the Clintons have flipped Comer’s script. Rather than give depositions behind closed doors, as the Republicans evidently prefer, the former president and secretary of state have demanded that the committee question them in a public hearing.

On Feb. 5, Hillary Clinton posted this challenge on X:

“For six months, we engaged Republicans on the Oversight Committee in good faith. We told them what we know, under oath. They ignored all of it. They moved the goalposts and turned accountability into an exercise in distraction.”
In a followup post, she urged Comer to “stop the games.”

“If you want this fight, @RepJamesComer, let’s have it -- in public. You love to talk about transparency. There’s nothing more transparent than a public hearing, cameras on. We will be there.”

Comer is not about to accept that challenge, which he ignored.

First, he knows how that worked out when Hillary Clinton showed up to testify about the Benghazi terror attack for 11 hours, at the behest of his predecessor, former Rep. Trey Gowdy (R-S.C.) -- in short, not well for Gowdy and the Republicans, who made themselves look stupid as Clinton briskly schooled them. It’s not at all clear that Comer, a plodding character often mocked in whispers by his fellow Republicans, would fare better against both Clintons.

Second, Comer is obviously planning to pursue the devious strategy that proved more successful for Gowdy during the Benghazi farce -- to record the depositions and then selectively leak snippets that create a misleading impression of the testimony. That is how Gowdy abused Sidney Blumenthal, the journalist and former Clinton White House aide called to testify privately for nine hours during that inquest in 2015.

I wrote extensively about that clown show -- and the complicity that Gowdy enjoyed from the New York Times’ Washington bureau, which eagerly lapped up the leaks -- in a series of posts. Gowdy and his stooges fabricated a tale about Blumenthal’s supposed “business interests” in Libya and how they had influenced Clinton’s policy. Having invented that diverting story, the Republicans could not afford to let the public see and hear Blumenthal demolishing it.

Despite protests from Democrats, notably the late and highly esteemed Rep. Elijah Cummings (D-Md.), a much sturdier figure than the current ranking Democrat, Blumenthal’s testimony was kept under wraps -- where it remains to this day. Neither Gowdy nor his fellow Republicans wanted the public to see how they had misused their power to spread falsehoods, pursue partisan grudges unrelated to Benghazi, and generally make fools of themselves.

Will House Democrats, the Epstein victims and the media allow Comer to get away with the same game? For all their rhetoric about “transparency,” not to mention similar high-minded blather from the Republicans, why would they permit this scam?

Comer’s scheme to conceal and then distort the Clintons’ testimony is the latest episode in the ongoing Trump coverup. It would be a shame indeed to allow such a deception to proceed.

Our Analysis:

A Display of Partisan Puppetry: The Oversight Committee’s Misdirection

In a recent unfolding that could only be described as a political theatre of the absurd, the House Oversight Committee, under the guidance of Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.), has taken a nosedive into the realm of partisan hackery. This isn’t merely an investigation into the deeply troubling Jeffrey Epstein scandal; it’s an unequivocal masterclass in diversion, targeting Bill and Hillary Clinton with the zeal of a zealot, while conveniently sidestepping a veritable mountain of implicating materials linked to Donald Trump.

The Misguided Crusade Against the Clintons

Comer’s fixation on the Clintons is as baffling as it is misguided. His decision to subpoena the Clintons, especially given their tangential (at best) connection to the Epstein saga, reeks of a desperate attempt to deflect from Trump’s all-too-real connections to Epstein. The Clintons, with Hillary having next to no involvement and Bill’s connections being peripheral, are bizarre choices for the committee’s scrutiny, unless viewed through the lens of partisan obfuscation.

With “tens of thousands of mentions” of Trump in the Epstein files, the urgency to deflect becomes glaringly obvious. Yet, the choice to pursue the Clintons as targets reveals not just a strategic diversion but an embarrassingly transparent one at that.

The Counteroffer: A Public Hearing

In a move that flips the script, the Clintons have called Comer’s bluff, demanding that any questioning be done in a public hearing. This demand for transparency puts Comer in an unenviable position, reminiscent of the Benghazi hearings, where Hillary Clinton’s 11-hour testimony served only to embarrass her Republican interrogators. It’s a clear message: if you want to play the game of accountability, let’s play it in the full view of the American public.

A History of Selective Leaking

Comer’s reluctance to accept the Clinton’s challenge is telling. It speaks to a strategy not of truth-seeking but of narrative crafting through selective leaking. This tactic, previously employed during the Benghazi hearings against Sidney Blumenthal, illustrates a modus operandi focused on distortion rather than discovery. It’s a playbook that relies on the withholding of information, cherry-picking evidence, and the creation of misleading narratives to serve partisan ends.

The Transparency Farce

The outcry for transparency from both sides of the aisle rings hollow when the actions of the Oversight Committee under Comer’s leadership are scrutinized. The demands for public hearings and the full revelation of testimonies are brushed aside in favor of behind-closed-doors depositions and the strategic release of information. This is not the pursuit of truth; it’s the manipulation of perception.

Conclusion: A Diversion Too Far

The ongoing saga with the House Oversight Committee is more than just a political skirmish; it’s a glaring example of the systemic errors plaguing our political landscape. Comer’s tactics are not just an affront to the Clintons but to the very notion of accountability and transparency in governance. They underscore a willingness to sacrifice truth at the altar of political expediency, a tactic that not only disrespects the victims of Epstein but also undermines the integrity of the political process itself.

In a just world, Comer’s scheme would be seen for what it is: a desperate attempt to shield Trump from scrutiny by casting aspersions elsewhere. It’s a game of smoke and mirrors that should not just be called out but vehemently opposed. For the sake of transparency, accountability, and the semblance of decency left in politics, let’s hope this deception does not proceed unchallenged.
