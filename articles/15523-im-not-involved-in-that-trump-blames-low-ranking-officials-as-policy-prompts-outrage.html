'I’m not involved in that': Trump blames low-ranking officials as policy prompts outrage

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 29, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Deflection: Trump's Controversial Policy and the Blame Game

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a meeting with Norway’s Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Stoere (not pictured), at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 24, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump washed his hands of one of his most controversial policies and blamed it on lower-ranking officials.

The president sat down for an extended interview with The Atlantic's Ashley Parker and Michael Scherer, who wrote that he seemed to acknowledge that his executive powers have limits, even as he continually seeks to expand them, and they added that Trump seemed to understand the Constitution would not allow him to disregard a judicial order.

“I think the judge is horrible,” Trump said, referring to U.S. District judge James Boasberg.

That federal judge has tried to stop the deportation of Venezuelan immigrants to an El Salvador prison under the Alien Enemies Act, but Trump cited a controversial Supreme Court granting him immunity from criminal prosecution for anything he does as part of his “official” duties as president.

"I’ve had a lot of horrible judges, and I won on appeal, right?" Trump said. "I got immunity on appeal."

The reporters questioned him on the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, an El Salvadoran national who had been living legally in the U.S. before he was mistakenly deported, but the government's own admission, to the notorious CECOT prison, and Trump denied any responsibility for his case or others like it.

“You know, I’m not involved in that," Trump said. "I have many people, many layers of people that do that. I would say they are all extremely tough, dangerous people. I would say that, and, don’t forget, they came in the country illegally.”

The government has argued that Abrego Garcia was a gang member, which his attorneys and family deny, and he was never granted due process for an opportunity to challenge the allegation, but Trump's advisers argue that his approach is working – even if the public doesn't seem to agree.

“Think about everything that’s happened immediately on immigration,” said Cliff Sims, who has served as an aide in both Trump administration. “Oh, we’re just going to ship gang members to a prison in El Salvador? ‘Sure.’ We’re going to send [border czar] Tom Homan to kick down the door of every criminal illegally in the country? ‘Have at it.’ It is the ultimate example of the ruthless efficiency of Trump 2.0.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Deflection: Trump's Controversial Policy and the Blame Game

In an astonishing display of deflection and denial, President Donald Trump has managed to wash his hands of one of his most controversial policies, laying the blame squarely on the shoulders of those beneath him. This maneuver, detailed in an interview with The Atlantic's Ashley Parker and Michael Scherer, is nothing short of a Houdini-esque escape from accountability. Let's dissect this act of political escapism, where Trump attempts to distance himself from the deportation of Venezuelan immigrants to an El Salvador prison under the Alien Enemies Act.

The Art of the Blame Game

Trump's strategy is as transparent as it is reprehensible. By claiming ignorance and detachment from the actions executed under his administration, Trump not only absolves himself of responsibility but also undermines the very essence of executive accountability. "I'm not involved in that," Trump states, a line that might have been more believable if it weren't for the glaring detail that these policies were enacted under his directive.

The Misuse of Executive Powers

Trump's assertion that a Supreme Court decision grants him immunity from criminal prosecution for actions taken in his "official" duties is a gross misinterpretation and an alarming indication of his view on the limits of presidential power. This stance is not only legally dubious but also morally bankrupt, suggesting that the president sees himself as above the law, a dangerous precedent for the leader of a democratic nation.

A Horrible Judge or a Convenient Scapegoat?

Trump's criticism of U.S. District Judge James Boasberg as "horrible" for attempting to halt the deportation is a classic Trump move—attack the judiciary when it does not align with his views. The judiciary's role is to interpret and uphold the law, often serving as the last line of defense against executive overreach. Trump's disdain for this balance of power reveals a fundamental misunderstanding, or perhaps a wilful ignorance, of the U.S. Constitution.

The Human Cost

At the heart of this debacle is the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, an El Salvadoran national who was mistakenly deported to the notorious CECOT prison. Trump's denial of responsibility for Garcia's fate and others like him illustrates a shocking lack of empathy and a failure to recognize the human cost of his administration's policies. The government's defense, branding Garcia a gang member without due process, is a stark reminder of the dangers of such unchecked executive actions.

Ruthless Efficiency or Moral Bankruptcy?

Cliff Sims, an aide in both Trump administrations, praises the "ruthless efficiency of Trump 2.0" in dealing with immigration. However, one must question whether efficiency is a virtue when it comes at the expense of human rights and due process. Shipping individuals to foreign prisons without a fair trial is not an achievement; it's a violation of the principles upon which the United States was founded.

Conclusion

Donald Trump's attempt to absolve himself of one of his most controversial policies is a masterclass in political deflection. By blaming lower-ranking officials and claiming ignorance, Trump seeks to escape the consequences of his actions. However, the facts remain: under his administration, policies were enacted that led to the suffering of individuals like Kilmar Abrego Garcia. The attempt to rewrite history cannot and should not absolve Trump or his administration of their role in these human rights violations. Accountability must not be another casualty of Trump's presidency.
