Marjorie Taylor Greene suggests ally could be pressuring Trump to block Epstein files

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (right) appears on CNN with Dana Bash, Nov. 16, 2025. (Screengrab/CNN)

A nervous-looking Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) took to CNN Sunday for her first interview since her falling out with President Donald Trump and strongly suggested that the Israeli government may be “pushing to keep” files on Jeffrey Epstein “covered up.”

Greene was asked by CNN’s Dana Bash about a social media post she had shared on Saturday in which she alleged that lobbying from the American Israel Public Affairs Committee, or AIPAC, was the reason Trump had attacked her, and openly questioned “what country is putting so much pressure on him.”

“I think it’s a question that many Americans are asking, especially when we saw information recently come out in these emails that the Oversight Committee – that I serve on – has released,” Greene said.

“We saw Jeffrey Epstein with ties to [former Israeli Prime Minister] Ehud Barak, we saw him making business deals with them, also business deals that involved the Israeli government and seems to have led into their intel agencies. I think the right question to ask is was Jeffrey Epstein working for Israel?”

Bash moved to have Greene go on record with what she was implying.

“I just want to be clear,” Bash said. “Are you saying Israel is pushing the president of the United States to cover up the Epstein files? And what evidence do you have that that is the case?”

A flustered Greene refuted Bash’s characterization of her comments, before ultimately backtracking and suggesting that she was, in fact, implying that the Israeli government was pressuring Trump on keeping files on Epstein “covered up.”

“No, I simply – no, I simply asked – no, I simply just asked out loud is there a foreign government – it could be any foreign government – but is a foreign government pushing to keep this covered up?” Greene said.

“Well, you had AIPAC up there, it’s pretty clear which government you were talking about,” Bash noted.

“I’m questioning that government in particular, and questioning any other foreign government,” Greene clarified.

Recent email leaks have suggested that Epstein did, in fact, do extensive work on behalf of the Israeli government. A recent report from Drop Site News revealed that Epstein had helped broker a defense agreement between the West African nation Côte d’Ivoire and Israel, a revelation uncovered from recently leaked emails of Barak’s.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Greene’s Allegations and the Epstein-Israel Connection

In an interview that could only be described as a masterclass in political deflection and conspiracy mongering, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) brought forth a narrative that reeks of desperation and a profound misunderstanding of geopolitical nuance. Her appearance on CNN, marked by a nervous demeanor that betrayed either a lack of confidence in her own statements or an acute awareness of the controversy they would ignite, was nothing short of a spectacle.

Greene’s Baseless Accusations: The heart of Greene’s argument hinges on an audacious claim: that the Israeli government may be exerting pressure on former President Donald Trump to suppress files related to Jeffrey Epstein. This assertion, rooted more in speculative fiction than in any discernible fact, raises significant questions about Greene’s motivations and the sources of her information.

A Misguided Focus on AIPAC: Greene’s attempt to single out the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) as a scapegoat for Trump’s criticisms of her is a transparent effort to deflect from her own contentious history with the former president. Her claim that AIPAC’s lobbying efforts are somehow linked to a grand conspiracy to suppress information is not only baseless but dangerously veers into the realm of antisemitic tropes about Jewish control and influence.

The Epstein Connection: The article mentions recent email leaks suggesting Epstein’s involvement in facilitating deals that benefitted the Israeli government. While these revelations, if true, merit serious investigation, Greene’s leap to insinuate a vast conspiracy involving Trump and the Israeli government is both irresponsible and unfounded. The work Epstein may have done, as described in reports from Drop Site News, does not inherently point to a cover-up at the behest of Israel, much less one involving the former President of the United States.

A Lack of Evidence: When pressed for evidence to support her claims, Greene’s response was evasive and non-committal. This tactic, familiar to those who traffic in conspiracy theories, allows her to insinuate wrongdoing without providing substantial proof. It’s a strategy designed to stoke suspicion and divert attention from substantive issues, rather than foster a meaningful discussion on Epstein’s relationships and the extent of his influence.

Conclusion: Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene’s comments on CNN represent a troubling descent into conspiracy theory territory, marked by a blatant disregard for evidence and a readiness to leverage serious allegations for apparent political gain. Her focus on the Israeli government, based on the thinnest of connections provided by recent email leaks, serves only to distract from the real and pressing issues surrounding Epstein’s criminal activities and the individuals and institutions that may have enabled him.

In the absence of concrete evidence, Greene’s allegations should be viewed with skepticism. They reflect a broader trend of political figures exploiting unfounded conspiracy theories for their own ends, a practice that undermines public trust and detracts from the pursuit of truth and justice in the Epstein case and beyond.
