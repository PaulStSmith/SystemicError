Trump left 'rattled' by cold response from military leaders: Ex-general

Members of the military attend a meeting convened by U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, at Marine Corps Base Quantico, in Quantico, Virginia, U.S., September 30, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

According to a former general who spoke with former colleagues at the gathering in Quantico, Virginia, where Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth addressed the military leadership of the U.S., the president was knocked off his stride by the cool response he received.

Appearing on MSNBC, retired Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling, now a military analyst for the network, claimed Trump is used to adoring crowds — and the stoic response from the military professionals “rattled” him.

After labeling Trump’s speech as “meandering,“ he told the “Morning Joe” hosts, “He gave so many things to talk about from that speech. But the ‘war from within,’ as you just mentioned, and putting soldiers on American streets to train how to react to different enemies from within were two of the highlights that I wrote down, and it was just a combination of two things that I believe were leading to what we might see next.”

“And the other thing I’d say is, President Trump thrives on applause and cheering and interactions with an audience,” he continued.

“And when he didn’t get that and instead got quiet professionalism from the soldiers, sailors, airmen and Marines in the audience, that likely rattled him from the very beginning.”

“And that silence wasn’t disrespect,” he elaborated. “It was restraint and discipline and adherence to the oath of office.”

“Yesterday was, to me, a collective reminder of our professional culture that we have in the military that transcends politics. It was on full display, and it was interesting to watch from both sides of the audience,” he observed.

Our Analysis:

The Discomfort of Silence: Trump’s Rattled Ego in the Face of Military Professionalism

In an eye-opening account relayed by retired Lt. Gen. Mark Hertling, a recent assembly in Quantico, Virginia, starring none other than former President Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, has provided a stark glimpse into the former president’s psyche. The event, intended as an address to the U.S. military leadership, veered off into what can only be described as an uncomfortable display of Trump’s vulnerability to the atmosphere of his audience. Trump, renowned for his affinity towards raucous rallies and unbridled adulation, found himself bewildered by the muted, professional response of the military attendees.

Hertling’s analysis, delivered with the precision of a seasoned military analyst on MSNBC, cuts through the facade often maintained by political figures, revealing a Trump who is not just surprised but “rattled” by the lack of visible support or enthusiasm. This isn’t merely about a preference for applause; it’s a telling insight into how Trump’s ego potentially influences his leadership and decision-making processes. His speech, described as “meandering,” touched on dangerously divisive topics, including the notion of deploying soldiers on American streets to counter “enemies from within.” Such rhetoric not only exacerbates internal divisions but also dangerously blurs the lines between military roles and civilian law enforcement.

What stands out in Hertling’s critique is the delineation of professional military conduct from the political theatrics Trump appears to thrive on. The military’s reaction, or lack thereof, wasn’t a show of disrespect but a testament to their “restraint and discipline and adherence to the oath of office.” This stoicism, a fundamental aspect of military professionalism, starkly contrasts with the often chaotic and impulsive nature of Trump’s leadership style.

The event in Quantico serves as a microcosm of the larger challenges facing American democracy. Trump’s discomfort in the face of silence underscores a broader issue: a leader’s dependency on constant validation can lead to a governance style that prioritizes personal ego over the nation’s well-being. The military’s non-reaction highlights a commitment to professionalism and duty above political partisanship, a reminder of the stability and integrity required in turbulent times.

Hertling’s observations offer a “collective reminder of our professional culture in the military that transcends politics”. This incident sheds light on the fundamental differences in values between military discipline and the brand of political leadership Trump embodies. It’s a stark reminder of the importance of stoicism, professionalism, and a commitment to duty—not just in the military, but across all branches of governance.

This episode at Quantico is not just an awkward moment for Trump; it’s a mirror reflecting the dissonance between the ethos of military service and the tumultuous nature of political populism. As we navigate these troubled waters, let’s not forget the value of silence, the strength of discipline, and the virtue of professionalism. In the end, these are the qualities that will steer the ship of state through the storm, not the applause of the moment.
