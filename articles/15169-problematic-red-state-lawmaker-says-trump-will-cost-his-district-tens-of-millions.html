'Problematic': Red state lawmaker says Trump will cost his district 'tens of millions'

Thanks to President Donald Trump's trade war, Kentucky's alcohol exports to Canada have plummeted, endangering the economy of one of the reddest states in the country. Now, the lone Democrat in Kentucky's Congressional delegation is sounding the alarm.

In a recent interview with ABC News, Rep. Morgan McGarvey (D-KY), who represents the commonwealth's largest city of Louisville, said that Canadian liquor stores have effectively stopped buying bourbon from the Bluegrass State as a result of the Trump administration's trade policies. And after Trump told Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney during Tuesday's Oval Office meeting that his approach to tariffs hasn't changed, McGarvey said many of his constituents are paying a heavy price for Trump's decisions.

"With Kentucky, Canada is our largest trading partner," McGarvey said. "We're going to lose tens of millions of dollars in bourbon sales in Ontario province alone, not to mention the whole country, because of what Trump's policies are doing."

McGarvey further observed that while the Trump administration has said that the tariffs aren't going to change, it has also suggested that the president's import duties are a negotiating tactic toward obtaining future trade deals. The Kentucky Democrat said those two positions were incongruous. And he added that Trump's haphazard implementation of the new trade taxes has proven chaotic for Louisville's distilleries (which include mass-distributed brands like Old Forester, Angel's Envy, Evan Williams and Rabbit Hole, among others).

"If you're using tariffs as a negotiating tactic, but then you say there's nothing you can do to get rid of it, that's going to be problematic," he said. "There was one week I was working with the bourbon companies in my district where, quite literally, on Monday, the tariffs were on. On Tuesday, they were off. On Wednesday, they were on. On Thursday, they were off again."

McGarvey isn't the only Kentucky lawmaker speaking out against Trump's tariffs. Sen. Rand Paul (R-Ky.) has been an outspoken opponent of the new import taxes specifically because of harm to Kentucky's bourbon industry, and has proposed legislation to undo the state of emergency Trump declared to impose tariffs without Congress. Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) has also come out against the tariffs, penning an op-ed in the Louisville Courier-Journal outlining the damage they would do to the commonwealth's distilleries.

"Broad-based tariffs could have long-term consequences right in our backyard," McConnell wrote in February. "Consider our state’s 75,000 family farms that sell their crops around the globe, or the hardworking Kentuckians who craft 95% of the world’s bourbon, or our auto industry that relies on global supply chains to support the livelihoods of thousands of workers in the commonwealth."

Our Analysis:

The High Cost of Trade Wars: A Kentucky Tale

Kentucky's bourbon industry, a cornerstone of the state's economy and a symbol of American craftsmanship, finds itself in the crosshairs of President Donald Trump's trade war, with devastating effects. The irony is palpable: a state that has shown overwhelming support for Trump is now reeling under the weight of his policies. In a recent revelation by Rep. Morgan McGarvey (D-KY), it's clear that the tariff tango is more than just a political skirmish; it's a threat to livelihoods.

Tariffs: A Double-Edged Sword

Trump's unwavering stance on tariffs, despite the evident backlash from Canada—a significant trading partner—highlights a stubborn approach to international trade. The result? Canadian liquor stores are backing away from Kentucky bourbon, a move that McGarvey describes as catastrophic, with losses estimated in the tens of millions in Ontario province alone. This isn't just a number; it's a blow to the economy of one of the reddest states in the nation.

Political Paradox

The dichotomy between Trump's tariffs as a negotiating tactic and the apparent chaotic implementation of these trade taxes is striking. McGarvey's anecdote about the on-again, off-again nature of these tariffs paints a picture of unpredictability and disorder, traits that are anathema to the stability required for businesses to thrive. This isn't strategy; it's recklessness disguised as policy.

Bipartisan Backlash

Interestingly, McGarvey isn't alone in his critique. Sen. Rand Paul (R-Ky.) and Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) have also voiced their opposition, transcending party lines to defend Kentucky's bourbon industry. McConnell's commentary, highlighting the broader impact on the state's agriculture and automotive sectors, underscores the far-reaching consequences of broad-based tariffs. It's a rare moment of unity in a polarized political landscape, united by a common threat.

The Real Cost

At the heart of this issue is the tangible impact on Kentucky's distilleries, family farms, and the auto industry. It's a stark reminder that trade policies are not just fodder for political debate but have real-world implications. The 95% of the world’s bourbon that comes from this state is more than a statistic; it represents the craftsmanship and labor of thousands.

Conclusion: A Call for Reason

Trump's trade war, marked by its haphazard implementation and stubborn rhetoric, is a gamble with high stakes. The potential fallout in Kentucky serves as a microcosm of the broader economic risks posed by such an approach. It's a cautionary tale of how political decisions can have dire consequences, particularly for those who placed their trust in the architects of those policies.

In the end, the plight of Kentucky's bourbon industry is a sobering reminder of the need for measured, thoughtful trade policies. As this saga unfolds, one can only hope for a resolution that veers away from brinkmanship and toward a more stable economic future. For the sake of Kentucky and indeed, the global economy, let's raise a glass to the hope of wisdom prevailing over the allure of political posturing.
