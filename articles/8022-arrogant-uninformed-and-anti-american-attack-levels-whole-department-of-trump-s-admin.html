'Arrogant, uninformed and anti-American': Attack levels whole department of Trump's admin

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 6, 20256 min read

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt enters the room to hold a press briefing at the White House in Washington, U.S., April 29, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

President Donald Trump is opening yet another front in his war on the free press, columnist Brian Karem wrote for Salon on Friday — and it’s getting harder to overlook his true vision for the media.

“In the latest round of professional malfeasance, the president’s sycophants in the communications office have launched a ‘Media Bias Portal’ on the official White House website,” wrote Karem.

“They are encouraging people to give them ‘tips’ when the public believes a member of the press has produced ‘Fake News.’ Naturally, I took the opportunity to fill out the page and offered the White House a tip that its own communication staff is publishing ‘fake news.’ I wonder how long I’ll be waiting for a response.”

All of this comes as Trump’s Department of Defense, which he declared to be the “Department of War” through an executive order, kicks off with a “press corps” of right-wing bloggers because all reputable media outlets, even most right-leaning ones, bailed rather than abide by the agency’s new terms limiting their ability to report.

The New York Times is currently suing over those new restrictions.

“Trump is now in the end stages of his campaign to destroy free speech, independent media and all forms of dissent,” wrote Karem. “He’s like the WWE Undertaker in the White House — which fits the bill nicely since the head of his communications staff, Steven Cheung, formerly worked with the WWE and loves to play the bully with members of a young and inexperienced press corps. Trump’s entire communications staff, come to think of it, is unpredictable, inattentive, arrogant, uninformed and anti-American.”

In Trump’s last administration, the press often pushed back when Trump moved to attack, demean, or strip them of rights, wrote Karem — but this time the protests are a lot weaker, and there’s a reason for that.

“Trump has engineered an inexperienced, sycophantic press corps. It’s handicapped from the moment reporters begin asking him questions, and Trump gets bolder, less professional and increasingly confrontational every time he appears before a pool spray, largely because of his success in undermining the Fourth Estate,” wrote Karem.

And Democratic lawmakers around the country are starting to take notice, he said: Rep. Jake Auchincloss (D-MA) introduced a package of reforms to combat misinformation online while taxing ad revenue from big tech platforms, and multiple states, including New Jersey and Pennsylvania, have either passed or are debating measures to give public funding to local news, putting those reporters on stronger footing.

In the meantime, though, he concluded, the press needs to remember that they don’t work for the president.

“The White House press corps I worked with, after I walked into the briefing for the first time in 1985, rarely failed to call presidents out in public,” he wrote. “That’s part of the job. Of course we must show respect for the office. But we should not have to stand for a president demeaning us, insulting us, refusing to interact with us and failing to recognize why we’re there. It’s not the press who are being insubordinate. He is. We’re not the enemy of the people. He is.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of “Trump’s Latest Assault on the Free Press”

In the latest article penned by Brian Karem for Salon, we witness not just a critique but a clarion call against what is depicted as the ongoing assault on the free press by President Donald Trump. Karem’s piece, laden with sharp accusations and grim portrayals, offers a profound glimpse into the current state of the United States’ media landscape under Trump’s administration. Let’s dissect the key elements of this narrative, peeling back the layers of hyperbole to examine the underlying truths and implications.

The “Media Bias Portal” Farce

At the heart of Karem’s argument is the White House’s “Media Bias Portal” initiative. Described as a tool for the public to report “Fake News,” this move is emblematically Trumpian, blending the lines between public engagement and a veiled attempt at media censorship. Karem’s own submission to the portal, accusing the White House’s communication staff of publishing ‘fake news,’ serves as a poignant, if not slightly humorous, act of defiance. However, beneath this act lies a darker implication; the normalization of a government apparatus designed to undermine and delegitimize the press.

Militarization of the Media Landscape

Equally concerning is the transformation of the Department of Defense into the so-called “Department of War,” alongside the establishment of a “press corps” filled with right-wing bloggers. This move, as Karem notes, marks a significant shift from reputable media engagement to a controlled narrative echo chamber. The refusal of most established media outlets to participate under the new restrictive terms speaks volumes about the erosion of press freedom and integrity within governmental reporting. Furthermore, the New York Times’ lawsuit against these restrictions underscores the legal battle brewing over the right to free and fair press coverage.

The WWE Undertaker Analogy

Karem’s comparison of Trump to the “WWE Undertaker” is both vivid and telling. It encapsulates the performative, confrontational, and often reality-distorting tactics employed by Trump and his communications team. Steven Cheung, highlighted for his WWE background, is emblematic of the administration’s preference for spectacle over substance—a dangerous trend when it comes to transparent and accountable governance. This approach not only misleads the public but dangerously blurs the lines between entertainment and the sober responsibilities of political leadership.

A Weakened Fourth Estate

The deterioration of a robust and confrontational press corps is among the most alarming developments highlighted by Karem. The engineered environment of “inexperienced, sycophantic press corps” under Trump’s tenure represents a strategic gutting of journalistic rigor from the White House. This, combined with Trump’s increasing boldness and confrontational demeanor, signals a deliberate effort to weaken the Fourth Estate’s role as a check on power.

Hope in Legislative Reforms?

Karem points to legislative efforts, like those spearheaded by Rep. Jake Auchincloss and various state initiatives, as beacons of hope in combating misinformation and supporting local news. While these are indeed vital steps towards safeguarding the integrity of the press and democracy, one must ponder if they are enough to counterbalance the systemic onslaught spearheaded by figures like Trump.

Conclusion: The Press’s Indispensable Role

In his closing, Karem reiterates a fundamental truth: the press does not serve at the pleasure of the presidency but rather serves the public interest. The adversarial relationship between the press and the presidency is not just traditional but necessary for the health of American democracy. Trump’s actions, as described by Karem, do not merely challenge this dynamic; they seek to obliterate it.

As we reflect on Karem’s stark depiction of Trump’s war on the free press, it’s crucial to remember the stakes involved. This isn’t just about one administration’s tactics but a broader battle for the soul of public discourse and democratic governance. The resilience of the press, legislative reforms, and public vigilance are all that stand between a fully informed public and the encroaching shadows of authoritarianism.
