Busted: Republican tries to delete gaffe — but not before reporters take screenshots

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 9, 20254 min read

A House Republican in Missouri attacked a group of California voters this month in a post on X for trying to interfere in her state’s politics — but then quietly deleted her post after realizing these voters were not from California at all. Or at least, not the California she was thinking of.

The drama began with a post by the local paper, the California Democrat, which stated, “Concerned citizens gathered at California City Hall Railroad Park on 500 South Oak Street to stop gerrymandering in Missouri,” which detailed people signing a ballot petition to overturn the GOP legislature’s aggressive mid-decade redraw of congressional maps that would delete a Democratic district in Kansas City.

The story rubbed Rep. Ann Wagner (R-MO) the wrong way.

“Missouri’s elections aren’t decided in California,” the suburban St. Louis congresswoman wrote. “The real threat isn’t our map but the Soros-funded network trying to manufacture outrage. Missourians will choose Missouri’s future.”

The problem: this article was not talking about voters in the state of California. It was talking about the town of California, Missouri, a community of just over 4,000 people in Moniteau County, in the center of the state.

Wagner deleted the post later — but not before reporters, including HuffPost’s Jennifer Bendery, managed to take a screenshot of it.

Voting rights groups across the state are gathering signatures for a ballot referendum that would allow Missouri voters to directly decide whether the newly gerrymandered map is adopted, which would also suspend the use of the new map until the vote can be held. Republicans have done everything in their power to try to stop the referendum, from lawsuits to declaring certain signatures invalid, to even accusing a company collecting the signatures of human trafficking.

Our Analysis:

A Blunder of Geographic Proportions: Misplaced Outrage in Missouri Politics

In a world where facts often fall victim to the speed of social media, Rep. Ann Wagner (R-MO) delivered a prime example of the pitfalls awaiting those who leap before they look. In a hasty attempt to chastise supposed out-of-state meddlers, Wagner directed her ire at a group of voters mobilizing against gerrymandering. However, her target, much to her chagrin, was not a cabal of Californian political operatives but rather concerned citizens from California, Missouri. This blunder is not just a geographical misstep but a glaring testament to the reflexive nature of political discourse, where assumptions often eclipse accuracy.

The Real Issue at Hand: Gerrymandering in Missouri

At the heart of this debacle is a contentious effort by Missouri Republicans to redraw congressional maps mid-decade, a move that would conveniently erase a Democratic district in Kansas City. This aggressive maneuvering aims to reshape the political landscape to their advantage, sidelining the democratic principle of fair representation. Wagner’s misplaced attack attempts to divert attention from the crux of the matter, framing the opposition as outsiders rather than Missourians fighting for their electoral integrity.

The Soros Boogeyman: Wagner’s invocation of a “Soros-funded network” is a tired trope, trotted out with the intention of discrediting legitimate civic engagement by painting it as the machination of shadowy, external forces. This knee-jerk blame game is not just intellectually lazy; it’s a deliberate obfuscation of the real and present concerns of Missouri voters over the fairness of their electoral maps.

The Irony of Misplaced Outrage

Wagner’s error is emblematic of a larger, more disconcerting trend in political communication: shoot first, ask questions later. By hastily condemning a local newspaper report as an instance of external interference, Wagner not only exposed her disregard for the details but also her readiness to dismiss the voices of her constituents. The fact that this criticism was aimed at residents of a town within her state adds a layer of irony to the episode, underscoring a disconnect between elected officials and the electorate.

Voter Suppression Tactics: A Disturbing Pattern

The efforts to derail the referendum on the new gerrymandered map reveal a disturbing willingness among Republicans to suppress voter input. Declaring signatures invalid, resorting to litigation, and even hurling unfounded accusations of human trafficking against a signature-collecting company are tactics that betray a fundamental discomfort with democracy. When the will of the people becomes a hurdle to be overcome, rather than the guiding principle of governance, the democratic fabric of society is strained, if not outright torn.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Integrity

Rep. Ann Wagner’s gaffe and the subsequent scramble to erase it from the digital record are symptomatic of a broader malaise: a political environment where deflection and misinformation are deployed to distract from substantive issues. As Missouri faces a critical juncture in its democratic processes, the need for clarity, honesty, and respect for the electorate’s intelligence has never been more acute. The saga of misplaced outrage serves as a cautionary tale, reminding both politicians and the public of the importance of fact-checking, the value of local journalism, and the imperative of engaging with the issues at hand with sincerity and depth. As we navigate these troubled waters, let us demand more from our elected officials than knee-jerk reactions and baseless accusations. Our democracy deserves no less.
