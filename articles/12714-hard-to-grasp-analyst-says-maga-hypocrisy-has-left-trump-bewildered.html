'Hard to grasp': Analyst says MAGA hypocrisy has left Trump 'bewildered'

Donald Trump's panicked Saturday meltdown over the Jeffrey Epstein files revealed a president desperately trying to wave off his MAGA base from a growing clamor that threatens to expose embarrassing or incriminating evidence about his relationship with the dead sex trafficker, Amanda Marcotte wrote for Salon.

But his base's refusal to let it pass has him baffled, she wrote.

"Let's keep it that way, and not waste Time and Energy on Jeffrey Epstein, somebody that nobody cares about," Trump pleaded with supporters, insisting the furor is being driven by "selfish people" trying to hurt him. He claimed claims that there are client files incriminating those associated with disgraced pedophile Epstein — files that his own administration has said exist — must be fabricated "by Obama, Crooked Hillary, Comey, Brennan, and the Losers and Criminals of the Biden Administration."

But MAGA isn't buying it. At the Turning Point USA conference, right-wing pundit Megyn Kelly commanded the crowd to "make some noise if you care about the Jeffrey Epstein scandal," leading to thunderous cheers. Co-host Charlie Kirk crowed that "every hand of 7,000 people" went up when asked if they cared. Attendees accused Trump of "covering for pedophiles."

"MAGA voters have made it abundantly clear that they do not care about sexual abuse," Marcotte wrote. "They didn’t care when a tape of Trump bragging about sexual assault was released in 2016. They didn’t care when he was found liable for sexual assault by a civil jury in 2023. They don’t mind that Trump goes out of his way to associate with men accused of sex crimes, from Epstein to Vince McMahon to Pete Hegseth to Matt Gaetz and numerous others.

"For Trump, this has to be bewildering. His supporters do not treat sexual violence like it’s a bad thing. So why do they care about Jeffrey Epstein’s crimes, or the identities of his alleged co-conspirators?"

The answer lies in MAGA's desperate need for moral absolution, she wrote.

Unlike Trump's adult victims, Epstein abused underage girls. In that narrow space between misogynist violence and pedophilia, MAGA masses have found their salvation.

"Despite their acceptance of misogynist violence, they still get to be the 'good guys' because they draw the line at raping minors<' the columnist wrote.

"By being very loudly against pedophilia, they can recast themselves as the heroes, ignoring all the other villainous behavior, including their love of a man who boasted about how he grabs unsuspecting women by their genitals."

Marcotte went on, "This is, no doubt, hard for Trump to grasp. He has never shown any sign of having a conscience, and he likely can’t imagine how his supporters might be suppressing feelings of guilt for their complicity in backing a sexual predator.

"The Epstein conspiracy theory serves as a coping mechanism for cognitive dissonance. Yes, Trump assaulted E. Jean Carroll—but if Bill Clinton is on a mythical "Epstein client list," Democrats are worse. Yes, Trump's cabinet undermines children's safety—but fantasizing about Bill Gates as a child molester lets them pretend they're protecting kids."

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Conundrum: Trump's Bewilderment Over Epstein Scandal

In a recent article dissected with surgical precision by Amanda Marcotte for Salon, the bewildering spectacle of President Donald Trump wrestling with the backlash from his base over the Jeffrey Epstein scandal is laid bare. This situation is dripping with ironies so rich, they could only emerge from the convoluted realm of current American politics. Let's peel back the layers of this paradox with fierce clarity.

The Irony of MAGA's Selective Outrage

The article highlights a Trump caught off-guard by the ferocity of his MAGA base's reaction to the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. This is the same base that shrugged off Trump's past sex scandals, including his infamous "grab 'em by the pussy" comment and his recent civil jury finding of liability for sexual assault in 2023. The contrast couldn't be starker. When it comes to sexual violence, MAGA's track record of apathy is well-documented, yet the Epstein scandal has somehow ignited their fury. Why?

Marcotte posits an intriguing theory: MAGA's desperate need for moral absolution. Epstein's crimes involved underage girls, a red line that even MAGA seems unwilling to cross. This delineation allows them to don the cloak of heroism in their fight against pedophilia, conveniently ignoring their complicity or indifference towards other forms of sexual violence. It's a narrow ledge of morality for MAGA to stand on, but stand on it they do, with the thunderous applause at the Turning Point USA conference as evidence.

Trump's Desperate Deflections: A Sign of Guilt or Fear?

Trump's reaction to the uproar — blaming it on an elaborate conspiracy fabricated by Obama, Hillary Clinton, and various other boogeymen of the right — is as predictable as it is pathetic. His plea to his base to ignore the Epstein scandal reveals a man scrambling to maintain his grip on a base that's suddenly become unpredictable. But it's his own administration that acknowledged the existence of incriminating client files linked to Epstein, making his denials all the more hollow.

The Role of Cognitive Dissonance

The mental gymnastics MAGA supporters must perform to maintain their support for Trump, while also condemning Epstein's crimes, is a masterclass in cognitive dissonance. Marcotte cleverly identifies the Epstein conspiracy theory as a coping mechanism for this dissonance. By focusing their ire on Democrats allegedly linked to Epstein, MAGA supporters can ignore the uncomfortable truth about their leader's behavior and pretend they're on the right side of history.

Conclusion: The Maze of MAGA Morality

In a twisted way, the Epstein scandal has held up a mirror to MAGA, revealing the warped logic that underpins their selective outrage. Trump's bewilderment is perhaps the only genuine emotion he's displayed throughout this ordeal, as he grapples with the realization that even his Teflon base has its limits. Marcotte's analysis is a damning indictment of the moral gymnastics required to remain a Trump supporter in the face of overwhelming evidence of his unsavory character and behavior.

The MAGA base's reaction to the Epstein scandal is not a sign of a newfound moral compass but rather a desperate attempt to salvage some semblance of righteousness. In their zeal to distance themselves from pedophilia, they inadvertently highlight their tolerance for other forms of abuse and misogyny. It's a selective outrage that speaks volumes about their priorities and the depth of their cognitive dissonance.

As this saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the MAGA movement's moral maze is as convoluted as it is contradictory. And at the center of it all is a bewildered Trump, struggling to navigate a scandal that finally seems to stick.
