'This is what you like!' Fury after Sean Hannity backpedals defense of Jack Smith attack

Based on an article by Robert Davis

Paulo SantosSeptember 30, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

RawStory reports that Fox News host Sean Hannity deleted an X post praising Sen. Eric Schmitt for putting former special counsel Jack Smith “on the spot” during a Tuesday hearing. Schmitt had suggested that Smith attended a professional basketball game in Atlanta and somehow coordinated with former Fulton County District Attorney Fani Willis over her prosecution of Donald Trump.

Later in the hearing, Smith and Sen. Amy Klobuchar said Smith had instead attended a women’s college basketball game in Maryland against the University of Iowa that night. Hannity removed his post after fuller footage circulated, but he did not publicly acknowledge the error in the material RawStory describes. Commentator Brian Beutler preserved a screenshot and criticized Hannity and Schmitt; other journalists likewise mocked the unsupported “gotcha.”

The accusation came from power

Schmitt was not a spectator repeating stray internet gossip. He was a United States senator using a formal hearing to advance an allegation against a former federal prosecutor. That office supplies a microphone, an audience and the appearance of investigative legitimacy.

The exchange described by RawStory offers no evidence connecting Smith to Willis. The reported correction instead directly contradicts the premise that placed him in Atlanta. An insinuation delivered from a Senate dais does not become substantial merely because a senator phrases it as a question.

Hannity amplified, then erased

Hannity exercised a different kind of institutional power: the ability to package a senator’s insinuation for a large partisan audience. His caption supplied the desired interpretation before viewers encountered the underlying facts. Schmitt had supposedly cornered Smith; the clip was presented as the proof.

Deleting the post limited further exposure, but deletion without a visible correction leaves the original propaganda mechanism intact. The audience receives the accusation with fanfare and the collapse, if it notices at all, through critics elsewhere. RawStory’s account does not establish why Hannity declined to explain the deletion. It does establish the asymmetry: promotion was public; accountability was absent.

The correction is not the scandal’s whole shape

The easy version of this story is that Hannity and Schmitt embarrassed themselves. That framing is satisfying and insufficient. It turns an abuse of institutional attention into a failed performance.

The consequential act was not getting a sports venue wrong. It was using that false premise to suggest improper coordination in prosecutions involving Trump. The location claim mattered because it was the bridge to the insinuation. Once that bridge collapsed, so did the reported basis for the attack.

Misdirection as method

The target of scrutiny was made to answer an allegation whose factual premise, according to the later exchange, was false. Meanwhile, the officials and media figures spreading it faced no comparable burden before publication or amplification.

RawStory centers the fury after Hannity’s deletion, but online outrage is secondary. The power lies with the senator who can manufacture suspicion under the authority of a hearing and the broadcaster who can circulate the most useful fragment. Critics can document the maneuver; they cannot give the correction the same reach or institutional weight.

The durable system

This episode reveals a political-media system in which insinuation is cheap, correction is fragmented and reputational penalties are optional. A senator can convert a faulty claim into official spectacle. A television host can turn that spectacle into partisan content. When contrary facts emerge, the post can simply disappear.

That is not public accountability functioning imperfectly. It is an information structure that rewards the accusation at every stage and treats the correction as disposable cleanup. The error fades; the suspicion it was designed to plant remains.
