'Blindsided': South African newspaper accuses Trump of hoodwinking nation's leader

The Sunday Times based in Johannesburg, South Africa, accused President Donald Trump of blindsiding the African nation's leader who arrived in Washington Wednesday for an Oval Office meeting.

"US president Donald Trump blindsided his counterpart President Cyril Ramaphosa during their meeting in the White House's Oval Office by playing a montage of video clips of EFF leader Julius Malema singing about killing white people," the Times published.

The EFF, or Economic Freedom Fighters, are described as a "South African Marxist–Leninist and pan-Africanist political party."

"Just moments after Ramaphosa answered a question on the false white genocide narrative, Trump asked for the lights to be dimmed and the collation of the Malema clips was played," the Times reported.

Malema can be heard singing "Kill the Boer," referring to the Dutch-speaking colonists now known as Afrikaners.

Trump repeated his unsubstantiated claim that "thousands" of white Afrikaners were clamoring to get into the United States as refugees because they were "afraid of being killed" in a "white genocide." Trump called it "the opposite of apartheid" and said the U.S. press would never report on it.

"Ramaphosa had just told Trump that he would allow, in their private meeting, his delegation... to explain to him that there was no white genocide in the country."

That's when the paper said Trump "jumped in and directed that the video clip be played."

Ramaphosa's delegation included his minister of agriculture and Democratic Alliance leader John Steenhuisen, billionaire Johann Rupert and golfer Ernie Els, "all of whom are white," according to the paper.

"The playing of the clip...came after a South African reporter asked Trump what it would take for him to be convinced that there was no white genocide in South Africa," the Times reported.

"Ramaphosa jumped in to respond to the question, saying, 'Well I can answer that for the President,' with Trump agreeing, 'I would rather have him answer,'" the paper reported.

“It will take President Trump listening to the voices of South Africans, some of whom are his good friends like those who are here," Ramaphosa continued. "But when we have talks between us around a quiet table it will take President Trump to listen to them, I'm not going to be repeating what I've been saying, I would say if there was Afrikaner farmer genocide I can bet you these three gentlemen would not be here, including my minister of agriculture, he would not be with me.

“So it would take him, President Trump, listening to their stories, to their perspectives, that is the answer to your question.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Unsettling Diplomacy of Trump with South Africa

The recent meeting between President Donald Trump and South African President Cyril Ramaphosa at the White House has surfaced as a glaring example of the unconventional and, frankly, disturbing approach Trump takes towards international diplomacy. As reported by The Sunday Times of Johannesburg, this encounter took an unexpected turn, displaying a level of insensitivity and provocation that has become a troubling hallmark of Trump's interactions with global leaders.

Trump's Provocative Maneuver

In a move that can only be described as blindsiding, Trump chose to confront President Ramaphosa with a montage of video clips featuring Julius Malema, leader of the Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF), a South African Marxist–Leninist and pan-Africanist political party. These clips showcased Malema singing about killing white people, specifically referring to "Kill the Boer," a term historically linked to Dutch-speaking colonists in South Africa.

This action took place immediately after Ramaphosa addressed a question on the false white genocide narrative in South Africa, a controversial subject that Trump has repeatedly spotlighted with his unsubstantiated claims. Trump's insistence on thousands of white Afrikaners seeking refuge in the United States due to fears of being killed in a so-called "white genocide" — a claim he boldly labels "the opposite of apartheid" — is not only misleading but dangerously amplifies a fringe conspiracy theory.

A Question of Motive

One must scrutinize the motive behind Trump's decision to play such provocative content during a diplomatic meeting. It appears to be a calculated attempt to undermine Ramaphosa's position and to cast doubt on the South African leader's dismissal of the white genocide narrative. By doing so in front of a delegation that included prominent white South Africans, Trump seemed to be pushing a divisive agenda, rather than fostering a constructive dialogue.

The Deafening Silence on Real Issues

The timing and manner of Trump's actions are particularly alarming. They occurred just as Ramaphosa tried to steer the conversation towards a more productive and less confrontational path, offering to have his delegation clarify the situation regarding the alleged white genocide. Trump's choice to literally dim the lights and spotlight Malema's controversial statements served to distract from the real issues at hand, including the complexities of South Africa's socio-political landscape and the strides being made towards racial reconciliation and economic equality.

An International Relations Fiasco

Trump's approach in this meeting is emblematic of his broader disregard for the nuances of international relations. By choosing to provoke rather than listen, to divide rather than unite, Trump not only disrespects his counterparts but also undermines the potential for constructive international dialogue. His actions raise serious questions about the long-term impact of such diplomacy on America's global standing, particularly in relations with African nations.

In conclusion, the incident as reported by The Sunday Times is not just a simple diplomatic misstep; it is a glaring reflection of Trump's contentious and, arguably, detrimental approach to international relations. This event should serve as a wake-up call regarding the importance of empathy, understanding, and respect in diplomacy — qualities that seem to be in short supply in Trump's playbook.
