'Absolute fugazi!' Expert says Trump can't escape blame as Americans 'can't catch a break'

President Donald Trump delivers remarks on the U.S. economy and affordability at the Mount Airy Casino Resort in Mount Pocono, Pennsylvania on Dec. 9, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump can no longer escape blame over the declining economy, a data analyst said Thursday.

CNN’s Harry Enten told anchor Brianna Keilar that criticizing former President Joe Biden for the high cost of living hasn’t worked for Trump and pointed to recent polls that show growing dissatisfaction with the Trump administration.

“Americans at this point just don’t feel like they can catch a break,” Enten said. “Money consistently taken out of their pocket. It feels like the cost of living is going higher. And if those ACA subsidies are not, in fact, extended, that, of course, just adds to the cost burden. And this whole idea that Donald Trump and the Republicans can escape blame when costs climb ever higher is absolute fugazi! It’s fantasy,” Enten said.

“Why do I say that? Because just take a look here. More responsible for the current economy. The last administration, Joe Biden or the current one? Donald Trump, 62% say the buck stops in the White House with the Republican president, Donald Trump,” Enten said.

With the Affordable Care Act subsidies slated to run out and no plan in sight to help people have affordable healthcare, Americans see Republicans in power and aren’t fooled, which could be a boon for Democrats heading into the 2026 midterm elections, he added.

“Just a third say Joe Biden. So look, the ACA subsidies, the ACA as a whole, health care. This right now is an anchor on Republicans. And it’s something I think Democrats will be able to use in 2026, especially as we head towards those midterm elections,” Enten said.

Our Analysis:

The Blame Game: Trump vs. The Economy

In an enlightening segment, CNN’s Harry Enten delivered a blistering analysis that effectively strips away the Teflon coating that President Donald Trump has seemingly worn throughout his tenure. The conversation, sharply focused on the declining economy and the rising cost of living, exposes the flimsy narrative that Trump and his Republican allies have desperately clung to: that somehow, the ghost of former President Joe Biden’s policies is to blame for America’s financial woes. Let’s dissect the points raised and the glaring holes in Trump’s economic defense strategy.

The Economy Under Trump: A House of Cards

Enten’s analysis brings to light a truth that is becoming increasingly difficult for the Trump administration to sidestep: Americans are feeling the financial squeeze, and the blame is landing squarely at the doorstep of the current administration. It’s a significant shift in perception that reveals a growing awareness among the American public. The once-effective tactic of redirecting blame to Biden is losing its potency, with 62% of people recognizing that the responsibility for the current economic climate rests with Trump. This statistic isn’t just a number; it’s a deafening wake-up call to an administration seemingly adrift in its economic policy—or the lack thereof.

The ACA Subsidy Cliff: An Impending Disaster

Enten astutely highlights a ticking time bomb in the form of the expiring Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies. This looming crisis could thrust the cost of healthcare into the stratosphere for countless Americans, exacerbating an already dire economic situation. The administration’s apparent lack of urgency or a coherent plan to address this impending calamity not only underscores a disturbing disregard for the wellbeing of the populace but also serves as ammunition for the Democrats as they gear up for the 2026 midterm elections.

The narrative being peddled by Trump—that his administration is an innocent bystander caught in the crossfire of Biden’s economic mismanagement—is, as Enten perfectly puts it, “absolute fugazi! It’s fantasy.” This denial of responsibility is not just a failure of leadership; it’s a calculated disconnection from the reality faced by millions of Americans. The strategy of obfuscation and blame redirection is a house of cards on the verge of collapse.

A Boon for Democrats?

As the Republican stronghold on economic narratives shows cracks, Democrats are presented with a unique opportunity to capitalize on this vulnerability. The ACA, and healthcare in general, remains a battleground that could decisively tilt the scales in favor of the Democrats, should they navigate the discourse strategically. The 2026 midterms loom on the horizon, and if current trends hold, we could witness a significant political realignment, driven by the electorate’s frustration with an administration that seems more concerned with evading blame than addressing pressing economic challenges.

Conclusion: The Buck Stops Where?

The essence of Harry Enten’s analysis is a stark reminder that political maneuvering and blame-shifting can only get you so far. The American public, battered by an unforgiving economy and the specter of unaffordable healthcare, is casting a critical eye on those in power. The attempt by Trump and his Republican allies to escape accountability for the nation’s economic downturn is not just a failure of policy; it’s a stark illustration of a broader systemic error in governance. As 2026 approaches, the question of responsibility—of where the buck truly stops—will undoubtedly be at the forefront of voters’ minds. And as Enten suggests, the answer to that question might just redefine the political landscape.
