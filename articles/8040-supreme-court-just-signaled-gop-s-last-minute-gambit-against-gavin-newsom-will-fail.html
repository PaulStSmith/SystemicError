Supreme Court just signaled GOP's last-minute gambit against Gavin Newsom will fail

The right-wing justices on the Supreme Court handed Republicans a win on Thursday evening by allowing Texas’ extreme mid-decade gerrymander of its congressional seats to take effect, staying a lower three-judge panel’s finding that it was illegally determined using racial criteria. But buried in a concurrence by the three farthest-right justices was a clear indicator that the GOP’s efforts to stop California’s retaliatory map will also fail.

The line came in a passage written by Justice Samuel Alito, joined by Justices Clarence Thomas and Neil Gorsuch.

“The dissent does not dispute — because it is indisputable — that the impetus for the adoption of the Texas map (like the map subsequently adopted in California) was partisan advantage pure and simple,” said the passage.

What this indicates, some legal experts flagged, is that if a lower court also tries to block Proposition 50 — a voter-approved referendum in California that draws out five Republican congressional seats there in response to Texas — it will also get reversed by the high court.

“NOTABLE: Alito, Thomas and Gorsuch say they view California’s new map as ‘indisputably’ driven by the pursuit of partisan advantage, as opposed to impermissible race-based goals,” wrote Politico’s Kyle Cheney. “Could bode well ... in legal challenges to the CA map.”

Proposition 50 was the brainchild of California Gov. Gavin Newsom, who demanded Democrats must respond to GOP efforts to rig congressional maps by offsetting those gains in blue states.

Republicans have filed multiple lawsuits against the referendum in response, arguing that the approved new map, too, relies illegally on racial criteria. One of these lawsuits came from the Justice Department itself, even as DOJ lawyers at Trump’s direction gave the original legal warnings to Texas Republicans that they must redraw their maps to eliminate minority districts.

Our Analysis:

The Gerrymander Gambit: Supreme Court’s Partisan Play

In a move that reeks of partisan manipulation, the right-wing justices of the Supreme Court have once again demonstrated their willingness to put political interests above fair representation. By allowing Texas’ blatantly gerrymandered congressional seats to proceed, the Court has not only endorsed a map carved out with partisan precision but has also signaled a green light for similar strategies across the nation, namely in California.

Texas Tango: A Dance of Partisan Gerrymandering

The article highlights a critical moment where the Supreme Court, led by its conservative majority, effectively sanctioned Texas’ mid-decade gerrymandering. This decision came despite a lower court’s finding that the map was crafted using racial criteria, a method that should raise legal and ethical red flags. However, it seems these concerns are secondary to the pursuit of partisan advantage.

California’s Countermove: Proposition 50

Enter Proposition 50, California’s response to the GOP’s gerrymandering gymnastics. Crafted by Governor Gavin Newsom, this referendum is a strategic attempt to balance the congressional map by drawing out five Republican seats. It’s a bold move, undoubtedly, but one born out of necessity in a political landscape where the rules seem increasingly malleable, depending on who wields the gavel.

The Alito Indicator: Reading the Judicial Tea Leaves

Justice Samuel Alito, joined by Justices Clarence Thomas and Neil Gorsuch, penned a concurrence that might as well have been a roadmap for how the Court views partisan gerrymandering. By acknowledging the Texas map’s partisan genesis as “indisputable,” they’ve set a precedent that could shield California’s Proposition 50 from legal jeopardy. Politico’s Kyle Cheney points out this development as a potentially positive omen for the legal battles ahead concerning California’s map.

The DOJ’s Dubious Dance

The Justice Department, under Trump’s guidance, once warned Texas Republicans of the need to redraw their maps to avoid racial bias. Yet, here we are, with those same maps receiving the Supreme Court’s blessing. The irony is as thick as it is disheartening, showcasing a glaring inconsistency in the application of justice and the interpretation of the law.

Conclusion: A Partisan Precedent

What we’re witnessing is a troubling trend towards the normalization of partisan gerrymandering, with the Supreme Court’s latest decision serving as a glaring endorsement. The implications are far-reaching, potentially setting the stage for a future where electoral maps are drawn not with the aim of fair representation but with the goal of partisan dominance.

In this high-stakes game of political chess, it’s the American people who are left in checkmate, their voices diluted by maps that prioritize party over principle. As the battle lines are drawn and redrawn, one thing remains clear: the fight for fair representation is far from over, and it’s a fight we cannot afford to lose.
