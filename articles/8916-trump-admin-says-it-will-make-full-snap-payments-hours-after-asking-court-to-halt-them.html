Trump admin says it will make full SNAP payments hours after asking court to halt them

The U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) notified states that it would fulfill obligations to fully fund the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) in November, just hours after the Trump administration appealed a ruling requiring the payments to be made.

In a memo to state agencies on Friday, USDA’s Food and Nutrition Service said it was “working towards implementing November 2025 full benefit issuances in compliance with the November 6, 2025, order from the District Court of Rhode Island.”

“Later today, FNS will complete the processes necessary to make funds available to support your subsequent transmittal of full issuance files to your EBT processor,” the memo stated.

Earlier on Friday, the Trump administration asked a federal appeals court to pause a ruling from U.S. District Judge John McConnell in Rhode Island that said the government must make full SNAP payments for November by Friday at 5 p.m.

“People have gone without for too long,” McConnell said in his Thursday ruling. “Not making payments to them for even another day is simply unacceptable.”

The Trump administration previously claimed that partial SNAP payments would be made in November due to the government shutdown.

President Donald Trump later insisted that the benefits would only resume when “the Radical Left Democrats open up government.”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking USDA’s SNAP Funding Announcement Amidst Political Tug-of-War

The latest maneuver by the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) to fully fund the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) in November, mere hours after the Trump administration’s fervent appeal against a court ruling mandating these payments, unfolds like a political thriller, yet the stakes are disturbingly real for millions. This scenario isn’t just a testament to bureaucratic agility but underscores a tumultuous saga of partisanship affecting the most vulnerable.

The Court’s Mandate vs. The Administration’s Resistance

At the heart of this saga is a District Court of Rhode Island’s order, compelling the USDA to fulfill its obligations towards SNAP beneficiaries. The court’s directive, coming from U.S. District Judge John McConnell, was clear and unequivocal, born out of an urgency that reflects the dire straits of those dependent on SNAP. McConnell’s statement, “People have gone without for too long,” is a poignant reminder of the human suffering behind political gambits.

The Administration’s Gambit

The Trump administration’s decision to appeal Judge McConnell’s ruling is not just a legal maneuver; it’s a stark illustration of the administration’s willingness to leverage vital assistance as a bargaining chip in its standoff with the so-called “Radical Left Democrats.” This approach, where the administration openly admits to withholding SNAP benefits to force a political outcome, reveals a troubling disregard for the welfare of millions who rely on this assistance.

President Trump’s assertion that benefits will resume once Democrats “open up government” is a dangerous simplification of a complex issue. It blatantly ignores the fact that government operations, especially those as critical as SNAP, should transcend partisan squabbles. The framing of this deadlock as a battle against “Radical Left Democrats” further polarizes an already divided political landscape, diverting attention from the critical issue at hand: the survival and well-being of countless Americans.

The Real Victims

Amidst the political back-and-forth, the true casualties of this standoff are the SNAP beneficiaries. These individuals and families, already marginalized and struggling, find themselves pawns in a larger political game. The implications of delayed or partial SNAP payments are severe, translating into missed meals and deeper poverty for many.

Conclusion: A Call for Decency and Urgency

The USDA’s efforts to comply with the court’s order, despite the administration’s appeal, offer a glimmer of hope. However, this episode should not merely end with the disbursement of November’s SNAP benefits. It should prompt a broader introspection about the morality of using essential food assistance as a leverage point in political negotiations.

The necessity for a swift, non-partisan resolution to funding disputes becomes glaringly obvious when the immediate well-being of millions is at stake. Political leaders must prioritize the basic needs of their constituents over strategic victories. In the end, the measure of a government’s efficacy and humanity is not in its ability to outmaneuver opponents but in its commitment to safeguarding the welfare of its most vulnerable citizens.
