'Psychopath': JD Vance hammered after shocking joke about Trump's deadly boat strikes

Paulo SantosSeptember 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Vice President JD Vance speaks at Hatch Stamping in Howell, Michigan, U.S., September 17, 2025. REUTERS/Jessica Koscielniak

Vice President JD Vance cracked jokes about President Donald Trump's lethal attacks on who he purported were drug traffickers hauling drugs into the United States, a video posted by independent journalist Aaron Rupar showed.

Twice, Trump has bombed boats the administration claimed were in international waters, calling them both an "imminent threat," though there are legal questions about whether Trump broke the law.

"I wouldn't go fishing right now in that area of the world," said Vance, speaking to an audience in Michigan.

It earned swift condemnation and anger among those online.

There are already conspiracy theories from some, saying that the boats could very easily have been people fishing and that there is no way to tell if they are carrying drugs on board without seizing the boats. So, the comment about not going fishing prompted several to ask if Vance was saying that because he was somehow aware that they were fishermen.

Aaron Reichlin-Melnick, a senior fellow at the American Immigration Council, posted sarcastically, "Get it? The joke is that we might kill some totally innocent people! Haha that’s funny, right?"

Former Chicago Tribune metro editor Mark Jacobs wrote his own translation, "When JD Vance brags about the U.S. blowing up alleged drug boats and says, "I wouldn't go fishing right now in that area of the world," he's really saying: 'We're so reckless and dishonest that fishermen should worry that the U.S. will murder them and then falsely accuse them of drug trafficking.'"

Legal analyst Ken White used the moment to mock a recent op-ed by New York Times podcaster Ezra Klein. "By making jokes about killing innocent brown people at a political rally, JD Vance is doing politics the right way. —Ezra Klein," he wrote.

A historian noted that the comment came after Vance said people should be fired for saying mean things.

"The ability of Vance to pivot from demanding that people be reported and fired for saying mean things about a murder victim to glibly celebrating extrajudicial assassination is just incredible, if he's not a clinical psychopath, he sure plays one well," said Jonathan Parkes Allen on X.

It was a sentiment echoed by several who remarked that Vance could joke about murder, but no one else enjoys the same freedom.

"In addition to everything else that's reprehensible about this 'joke' about 'accidentally' murdering poor fishermen, don't forget that Trump made this same 'joke' as well, so JD is also debasing himself by mindlessly mimicking Trump's degeneracy," observed New Republic columnist Greg Sargent.

Our Analysis:

The Grim Comedy of JD Vance and the Trump Administration's Maritime Mayhem

In a display of macabre humor that would make even the most cynical of satirists blush, Vice President JD Vance was caught on video, courtesy of independent journalist Aaron Rupar, chuckling over the Trump administration’s lethal antics on the high seas. The subject of Vance's ill-conceived jest? President Donald Trump's decision to bomb boats in international waters, boats the administration labeled as imminent threats due to their alleged role in drug trafficking. The punchline, as Vance sees it, is that now might not be the best time to go fishing in certain parts of the world. Hilarious, if your sense of humor skews towards the dark and deadly.

The Administration’s Dubious Legal Standing

Let's dissect the core issue before delving into Vance's controversial quips. President Trump has twice ordered bombings on vessels in international waters, actions he defends as strikes against drug traffickers. However, these assertions float in murky legal waters themselves. The critical question remains: Did these actions contravene international law? Without clear evidence that these boats were engaged in illegal activities, Trump's aggressive strategy seems not only reckless but potentially unlawful. The administration’s cavalier approach to international law underscores a disturbing readiness to bypass due processes.

Vance’s Jokes: A Disturbing Levity

Vance’s commentary, intended to elicit laughs, instead shines a spotlight on a grotesque indifference to human life. The suggestion that these boats could simply be manned by fishermen casts a shadow of doubt over the legitimacy of the administration's targets. This grim scenario, where innocent lives are snuffed out under the guise of combating drug trafficking, is no laughing matter. Yet, Vance, with a cavalier flippancy, finds humor in the potential for tragic misidentification and the consequent loss of innocent lives.

Reaction and Condemnation

The backlash was swift and fierce. Critics from various quarters lambasted Vance for his insensitivity and callousness. Aaron Reichlin-Melnick of the American Immigration Council and former Chicago Tribune metro editor Mark Jacobs were among those who decried Vance’s remarks, highlighting the perverse glee in potentially killing innocent people. Legal analyst Ken White and historian Jonathan Parkes Allen pointed out the hypocrisy and moral bankruptcy of Vance’s stance, especially given his previous advocacy for punitive actions against those he deems disrespectful or offensive.

A Reflection of Deeper Issues

Vance's jokes are symptomatic of a broader, more disturbing trend within the administration: a disregard for the rule of law, human rights, and the sanctity of life. His readiness to jest about such grave matters betrays a lack of empathy and a troubling propensity for authoritarianism. Moreover, Vance's mimicry of Trump's own dark humor reveals a sycophantic adherence to Trump's most dangerous impulses, further debasing the office he holds.

In Conclusion

JD Vance's comments are not merely inappropriate; they are repugnant. They reflect a broader disdain for international norms and human life pervasive within the Trump administration. This episode should not be dismissed as a simple gaffe or poor taste in humor. Instead, it should serve as a clarion call for accountability and a reevaluation of the individuals entrusted with the highest offices in the land. The cavalier attitude towards extrajudicial killings and the mockery of potential innocent casualties betray a profound systemic error that must be addressed.
