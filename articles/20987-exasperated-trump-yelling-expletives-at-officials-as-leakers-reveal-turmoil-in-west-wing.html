'Exasperated' Trump 'yelling expletives' at officials as leakers reveal turmoil in West Wing

Based on an article by Elizabeth Preza

Paulo SantosAugust 11, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Owns the War He Can’t Explain

Trump is now “exasperated” by the war in Iran, furious about his options, and reportedly lashing out at the people below him. That is the surface drama. The real story is simpler: a president who sold the conflict as a “short-term excursion” now presides over a war in its sixth month, with no clear endpoint, no coherent public strategy, and a bill that has already reached staggering levels.

The Seat of Power

The institutional power sits where it always sits in a presidential war: with the president. Not the press secretary. Not the staffers absorbing the yelling. Not the unnamed officials trying to survive the chaos. Trump is the final decision-maker, and the White House itself leans on that fact when it wants to defend him.

That makes the current confusion his ownership, not his excuse. If the military lacks “clear guidance,” that is not a clerical mishap. It is a command problem. A president who keeps the war machine moving while withholding a strategy is not being overruled by events. He is producing them.

A Strategy Built on Drift

The reporting makes clear that there was “not a real strategy” for how long this would last or what the endpoint should be. That is not a minor gap. It is the central fact. The war is now being run like a slogan that ran out of shelf life.

Trump apparently imagined a quick hit, a neat political product, then reality did what reality always does to fantasies: it imposed duration, resistance, and cost. The result is a conflict that is expanding toward more combat operations while the administration is still improvising the basic answer to a question that should have been settled before the first strike: where is this supposed to go?

The Bill Is the Policy

The numbers in the story matter because they expose the lie of “limited” war. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth puts the cost at $37.5 billion. A separate analysis says the true figure is $151.3 billion. Even the lower number is obscene. The wider point is not accounting trivia. It is that military adventures are always sold as controlled, cheap, and decisive until the invoice arrives.

That invoice is political. It is paid in depleted capacity, in missed priorities, in the normalization of executive recklessness, and in the decision to spend public power on escalation without admitting what escalation actually is. The bill is not a side effect of the policy. It is the policy.

Blame Shift as Cover

The familiar move is already in place: blame the staff, blame the options, blame the friction of execution. The White House says the reports of Trump’s outburst are false and insists he has a team that knows he is the final decision-maker. That defense is supposed to reassure. It does the opposite.

If he is the final decision-maker, then the chaos is his. If the team “trusts” him, then the team is functionally part of the machinery that keeps him insulated from consequences. Either way, the public is being asked to confuse tantrum with leadership and confusion with governance. That is not a mistake. It is a method of laundering responsibility through hierarchy.

What This Pattern Means

This is what authoritarian-adjacent politics looks like in practice: centralize power, personalize every decision, then treat the wreckage as a problem of messaging, morale, or insufficient loyalty. The president gets to claim omnipotence when things look strong and ignorance when they collapse. Everyone beneath him becomes either a shield or a scapegoat.

The deeper pattern is not just that Trump is bad at war. It is that he treats war like a performance of dominance while refusing the discipline that actual command requires. He wants the authority of empire without the burden of planning, the optics of control without the accountability of control. That is how civilian institutions get hollowed out from the top: by a leader who mistakes volatility for strength and leaves everyone else to manage the damage.
