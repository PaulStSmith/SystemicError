'Trump's fixations blinded him': Fox reporter turns on president over 'ticking time bomb'

U.S. President Donald Trump sits in the Oval Office of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 7, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

A conservative who raves about Donald Trump's business acumen is saying the president just made a bad deal.

FOX Biz senior correspondent Charles Gasparino, who reports on Trump often and earned the ire of MAGA when he recently reported that conservatives were wrong to suggest Trump outsmarted the world with tariffs, wrote an article in which he said Trump just set off a "ticking time bomb."

"Most of the time, on key issues like tariffs, he knows when to hold ’em and when to fold ’em. He cut deals on his onerous 'Liberation Day' levies and stocks have soared. And with TikTok, he’s reportedly negotiating a multibillion-dollar fee for the US government to broker the deal," Gasparino wrote before pouring cold water on Trump's plan for TikTok with a reference to the president's own book. "Yet when you parse what else we know about his 'framework' with Xi over the future of the app, it’s hard to find art here."

He continued, saying, "Instead, Trump has allowed his own fixations to blind him to the reality that TikTok isn’t worth saving."

"There are many reasons why TikTok should be shut down in the US, despite its popularity particularly with younger Americans who make up a huge swath of its 170 million users. Oddly, the president used to be pretty good at ­articulating those reasons," Gasparino added. "The old Trump, during his first term, saw it as a conduit for the Chinese Communist Party not only to spy on Americans but also rot their brains."

Our Analysis:

Trump's TikTok Tangle: A Misstep or Misunderstanding?

In an article that could only be described as a wake-up call wrapped in a reality check, FOX Biz senior correspondent Charles Gasparino outlines what he views as a significant blunder by President Donald Trump concerning the popular social media platform, TikTok. Gasparino, who has not shied away from critiquing Trump despite often reporting on his administration's economic policies, breaks ranks with the MAGA faithful to highlight a flawed strategy in dealing with TikTok's future in the United States. Here's a breakdown of his arguments and why this episode is more than just a bad deal—it's a misjudgment of priorities, consequences, and the very essence of leadership.

The Illusion of a Deal

Gasparino applauds Trump's past successes with tariffs and acknowledges his knack for negotiation, citing instances where the president appeared to play his cards right. Yet, when it comes to TikTok, Gasparino suggests Trump might have overplayed his hand. The deal, or rather the "framework" negotiated with Xi Jinping, is critiqued not for its ambition but for its lack of substance. In what Gasparino describes as a departure from Trump's own proclaimed business wisdom, the TikTok saga unfolds more like a cautionary tale than a chapter from "The Art of the Deal."

A Ticking Time Bomb

The phrase "ticking time bomb" is not thrown around lightly by Gasparino. It encapsulates the essence of a haphazard approach to national security, youth culture, and digital sovereignty. The assertion that TikTok "isn’t worth saving" is a bold one, especially considering the app's immense popularity and its entrenchment in the digital lives of millions of Americans. Yet, it's here where Gasparino's critique finds its sharpest edge: Trump's fixation with a deal, any deal, has overshadowed the larger issues at play, including those the president himself once vocally opposed.

The Spy Who Loved Me Not

Perhaps the most damning part of Gasparino's analysis is the reminder of "the old Trump" who viewed TikTok through a lens of skepticism and concern over Chinese espionage and influence. This shift from viewing TikTok as a threat to national security to a bargaining chip in the U.S.-China tech war signifies not just a change of heart but a miscalculation of the app's strategic importance and potential harm. It's a pivot that seems to have forgotten the original reasons behind the apprehension, swapping principle for pragmatism, or worse, populism.

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity for Leadership

In the grand scheme of things, the TikTok debacle as outlined by Gasparino isn't just about a failed deal or a misunderstood app; it's a reflection of a broader issue of leadership and vision. The willingness to compromise on previously stated principles for the sake of appearing to win a negotiation reveals a troubling approach to governance and diplomacy. It's a narrative that Gasparino captures not with glee but with a sense of disappointment—a sentiment that might resonate with those who expected more from a leader who prides himself on his business acumen.

In essence, the article serves as a stark reminder that leadership, especially in the digital age, requires more than just deal-making prowess. It demands an understanding of the broader implications of those deals and the wisdom to know when to walk away. Trump's TikTok misadventure, as Gasparino eloquently argues, might just be a symptom of a larger ailment—a failure to recognize the forest for the trees.
