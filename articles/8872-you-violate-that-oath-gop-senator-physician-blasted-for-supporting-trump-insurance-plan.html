'You violate that oath': GOP senator-physician blasted for supporting Trump insurance plan

Trump over the weekend was met with ridicule after he unleashed a major plan that he said will provide a multibillion dollar payout directly to Americans as an alternative to funding government health care subsidies that have divided lawmakers amid the ongoing government shutdown.

“I am recommending to Senate Republicans that the Hundreds of Billions of Dollars currently being sent to money sucking Insurance Companies in order to save the bad Healthcare provided by ObamaCare, BE SENT DIRECTLY TO THE PEOPLE SO THAT THEY CAN PURCHASE THEIR OWN, MUCH BETTER, HEALTHCARE, and have money left over,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

Cassidy, a medical doctor, shared Trump’s quote on X and added, “I agree with President Trump! We should give the money directly to the patients.”

That led to outrage from observers.

Political scientist Norman Ornstein responded to the comment from Cassidy, saying, “First, do no harm. You violate that oath every single day. You’re opening up the door to sham plans that will not cover anybody, and reinstate the pre-existing conditions scam. You used to be a good and honest senator.”

“What happened?” Ornstein asked.

Union communications official Collin Reischman chimed in, “’We should end subsidies for insurance by taking that exact same amount of money and giving it directly to people to buy insurance’ yes masterfully done sir, why didn’t we think of that.”

Virginia city councilman R. Kirk McPike said, “Replacing insurance with direct cash transfers to people would mean no one but the very rich could ever receive treatment for a chronic condition like heart disease or diabetes or an expensive disease like cancer.”

Columnist Stephanie Grace also weighed in:

“Two things here. 1) Donald Trump knows nothing about the health care industry’s economics. 2) Bill Cassidy does and retweeted this anyway.”

Personal injury attorney James Abrenio also added, “I’m convinced Trump has no clue how health insurance works but just hates that Obama did something could never come close to accomplishing.”

Healthcare expert Heath Veuleman asked the senator, “Will physicians be able to offer direct care benefits unbridled by Stark and Antikickback? Will physicians be able to compete with physician-led enterprises? Any ideas on the physician-owned hospital moratorium? Or is all of this just a ploy to divert more taxpayer and consumer spending to hospitals and health systems? Are we going to restore the physician-patient relationship?”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Senator Cassidy’s Endorsement of Trump’s Health Plan: A Dangerous Gamble or Political Theatre?

In an era where political rhetoric often supersedes rational policy discussion, the recent endorsement by U.S. Senator Bill Cassidy, M.D., (R-LA), of a health care proposal by Donald Trump serves as a prime example of the dangerous intersection between populist messaging and the complexities of health care policy. Trump’s proposal, which aims to redirect billions of dollars from insurance subsidies directly to Americans, has sparked a maelstrom of criticism from various corners, encapsulating the broader debate over health care in America.

Trump’s Proposal: A Simplistic Solution to a Complex Issue

Trump’s plan, as articulated in his post on Truth Social, suggests a drastic overhaul of the current system by eliminating government subsidies to insurance companies under Obamacare, instead funneling that money directly to citizens. The simplicity of Trump’s proposition belies the intricate realities of the health care system, a fact that appears lost on both Trump and Cassidy. This plan, ostensibly aimed at empowering individuals, ignores the fundamental economics of health care, which cannot be distilled into such black-and-white terms.

Cassidy’s Endorsement: A Physician’s Betrayal?

Senator Cassidy, a medical doctor, should ostensibly understand the nuances of health care better than most. His endorsement of Trump’s plan, therefore, is not just perplexing; it’s alarming. Critics, including political scientist Norman Ornstein, have lambasted Cassidy for abandoning his medical oath to “first, do no harm,” suggesting that the senator’s support opens the door to inadequate coverage and the resurgence of pre-existing condition exclusions. Cassidy’s stance raises serious questions about his motivations, given his background and the potential consequences of such a policy shift.

The Critics Speak

The criticism of Trump and Cassidy’s proposal is widespread and scathing, touching on several key points:

  • The Feasibility and Impact on Coverage: Critics argue that direct cash transfers in lieu of subsidies would essentially dismantle the insurance market, making comprehensive coverage unaffordable for all but the wealthiest Americans. This criticism gets to the heart of why health insurance exists: to pool risk and make care affordable.

  • Economic Ignorance: Columnist Stephanie Grace highlights a critical issue: Trump’s lack of understanding of health care economics, a sentiment echoed by personal injury attorney James Abrenio. Cassidy, with his medical background, should theoretically counterbalance Trump’s ignorance but instead has chosen to amplify it.

  • Potential for Abuse: The proposal raises numerous questions about its implementation and the potential for increased health care costs, reduced competition, and the prioritization of profits over patient care. Health care expert Heath Veuleman‘s inquiries underscore the lack of clarity and potential for exploitation inherent in Trump and Cassidy’s plan.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Diversion

Senator Cassidy’s endorsement of Trump’s health care proposal is more than just political allegiance; it’s a dangerous flirtation with policy that could have dire consequences for the American health care system. By prioritizing populist rhetoric over substantive, evidence-based policy, Cassidy risks undermining the very foundations of accessible health care in the U.S., betraying both his medical oath and the trust of his constituents.

In essence, this episode reveals the perilous path of aligning medical policy with political expediency, a journey fraught with risks and devoid of the ethical compass required in health care discourse. As we dissect the rhetoric and examine the potential impacts, one thing becomes abundantly clear: health care, in the hands of politicians more concerned with soundbites than solutions, is in peril.
