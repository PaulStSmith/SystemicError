Trump skewered by analyst over 'single stupidest' statement in US foreign policy history

Bulwark editor Jonathan Last delivered scathing criticism of President Donald Trump on Wednesday after the president dropped his plan to escalate military pressure on Venezuela in a Truth Social post rant.

Last described in his Substack how White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles‘ revealing Vanity Fair interview, paired with Trump’s most recent comments, laid the groundwork for a potential military offensive — and what the motives actually are behind them — “that Trump is seeking regime change in Venezuela.”

Trump wrote this on Truth Social late Tuesday:

“Venezuela is completely surrounded by the largest Armada ever assembled in the History of South America. It will only get bigger, and the shock to them will be like nothing they have ever seen before — Until such time as they return to the United States of America all of the Oil, Land, and other Assets that they previously stole from us.”

Last slammed Trump for the comments.

“This is a suboptimal way to conduct foreign policy. It is erratic, unfocused, vague, and ultimately dangerous—both to America’s short-term and long-term interests,” he wrote. “Pick whatever foreign policy disaster you’d like from American history—Bay of Pigs; Iran-Contra; Mission: Accomplished. The thinking behind Trump’s Venezuela adventure is worse.”

“In fact, I’d go so far as to say that Trump’s ultimatum last night is the single stupidest and most irresponsible presidential statement in the 249-year history of American foreign policy. I defy you to find something—anything—dumber,” Last wrote.

Last argued that Nicolás Maduro is no saint or even “good guy.”

“Maduro is a b-------,” he wrote. “A corrupt, authoritarian gangster who has oppressed the Venezuelan people for years.”

Trump, who has spent the last few months striking alleged drug boats in the area and claimed the move is to combat drug trafficking, has signaled plans to elevate the conflict.

Wiles, in one of the 11 interviews conducted with Vanity Fair journalist Christopher Whipple, described Trump’s goal:

“He wants to keep on blowing boats up until Maduro cries uncle. And people way smarter than me on that say that he will,” she said.

The White House has denied this.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Foreign Policy Fiasco: A Closer Look at the Venezuela Blunder

In the latest chapter of what can only be termed as the theatre of the absurd, former President Donald Trump has once again catapulted himself into the limelight with remarks that not only defy logic but also flirt dangerously with the boundaries of responsible governance. Jonathan Last, editor at Bulwark, has courageously dissected Trump’s recent outburst concerning Venezuela, revealing the layers of folly embedded within.

Trump’s Erratic Foreign Policy: A Dangerous Game

Trump’s declaration on Truth Social about Venezuela being “completely surrounded by the largest Armada ever assembled in the History of South America” is not just an extravagant claim; it’s a manifestation of a deeply troubling approach to foreign policy. Describing this strategy as “erratic, unfocused, vague, and ultimately dangerous,” Last accurately captures the essence of Trump’s governance style — one that prioritizes bravado over strategy, impulse over planning.

The Historical Context of Missteps

Last’s comparison of Trump’s Venezuela strategy to some of the most notorious foreign policy disasters in American history — Bay of Pigs, Iran-Contra, and “Mission: Accomplished” — is not only apt but also necessary. Such comparisons serve as a stark reminder of the consequences of ill-conceived policy decisions. However, Trump’s recent comments, according to Last, sink to new depths, branding them as potentially “the single stupidest and most irresponsible presidential statement in the 249-year history of American foreign policy.” This is no small claim, yet the gravity of Trump’s words lends it credence.

The Misguided Crusade Against Maduro

While Nicolás Maduro’s regime in Venezuela is far from laudable, Trump’s approach, as outlined by his own words and the Vanity Fair interview with Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, seems less about championing democracy and more about a personal vendetta or, at best, a misguided attempt at regime change. The notion that escalating military pressure could lead to a simple resolution, as Wiles suggests Trump believes, is naive. It ignores the complex socio-political landscape of Venezuela and the potential for catastrophic fallout.

The Rhetoric vs. Reality of Drug Trafficking

Trump’s justification of his military posturing as a means to combat drug trafficking is a thinly veiled attempt to legitimize his actions. It’s a narrative that, while compelling on the surface, crumbles under scrutiny. The simplistic equation of blowing up “alleged drug boats” to a strategic anti-drug trafficking campaign is laughable if it weren’t so terrifyingly indicative of Trump’s propensity for oversimplification and grandstanding.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Disregard for Diplomacy

Jonathan Last’s critique of Trump’s latest foreign policy blunder is not just a condemnation of a single incident but a broader indictment of a presidency marked by reckless, impulsive decision-making. The situation with Venezuela is a glaring example of Trump’s dangerous disregard for the nuances of international relations and diplomacy. As we dissect this latest episode, let it serve as a reminder of the importance of thoughtful, informed, and strategic leadership on the global stage — qualities sorely lacking in Trump’s approach.
