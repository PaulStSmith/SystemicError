'Off the charts territory': Strategists say GOP on electoral thin ice over Trump megabill

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 11, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Democrats have renewed hope heading into the 2026 midterm elections that what they see as a major blunder on the part of the GOP could prove costly for Republicans, several Democratic strategists said recently.

President Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act made several changes to Social Security, among them being tax deductions that critics say could accelerate the popular program’s road to insolvency. Will Van Nuys, the deputy executive director for the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, said the issue could prove to be a huge political win for Democrats next year.

“This is the first time since 2006 that I would say Republicans went ahead and grabbed the third rail of politics,” Nuys said, speaking with NOTUS in a report published Wednesday.

“This issue fits right into our broader frame, which is a frame on costs of the economy. And further, when you look at the midterm electorate, they are older voters, they are working voters who are having to help support their elderly parents, or folks who just feel left behind by the economy.”

Republicans have long sought to either gut or privatize Social Security, a New Deal-era program that many conservative lawmakers have sought to trim or outright eradicate since its establishment nearly a century ago. Former President George W. Bush launched a failed push to partially privatize the program, and former House Speaker Paul Ryan repeatedly sought to trim and privatize the program.

And more recently, Rep. Rick Scott (R-FL) unveiled a plan to outright sunset Social Security with his “Rescue America” plan, a proposal so politically toxic that it was immediately seized upon by Democrats for campaign material.

Trump has treaded carefully when discussing Social Security, having repeatedly vowed to not touch the program, and even warning Republicans not to touch the program either. His signature bill, however, will impact the program, and to such an extent that Democrats believe they can carry news of the bill’s impact on Social Security to electoral victory next year.

“This is off the charts territory,” said Dan Adcock, who leads government relations and policy at the National Committee to Preserve Social Security and Medicare, speaking with NOTUS. “We’ve never been in this situation.”

Republicans, however, say they’re not afraid, at least according to Mike Marinella, spokesperson for the National Republican Congressional Committee, who told NOTUS that Democrats’ messaging around Social Security was a “load of bull----.”

“At this point, Democrats roll out a new load of bullshit every week and think they can fool voters with baseless claims and tired fear-mongering,” Marinella said. “It only proves they have no message besides lies, gaslighting, and recycled attacks. No wonder their party’s poll numbers are buried 50 feet below rock bottom.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Social Security Snafu: A Political Goldmine for Democrats?

The Democrats smell blood in the water, and it's tinted with the ink of President Donald Trump's One Big Beautiful Bill Act. In what can only be described as a political faux pas of monumental proportions, the GOP has seemingly decided to play fast and loose with Social Security, a program as American as apple pie and just as sacrosanct to the electorate. According to Democratic strategists, this could be the blunder that tips the scales in the 2026 midterm elections.

A Taxing Decision: The Act's introduction of tax deductions, critics argue, could fast-track Social Security towards insolvency. This is not just a minor policy dispute; it's akin to lighting a fuse on a powder keg in the heart of American politics. Social Security has long been considered the "third rail" — touch it, and your political career is as good as dead. Will Van Nuys of the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee didn't mince words, suggesting that the GOP has not only touched the third rail but has grabbed it with both hands.

Republicans and Social Security: A History of Hostility: The GOP's contentious relationship with Social Security is well-documented. From George W. Bush's privatization attempt to Paul Ryan's budgetary scalpels, the Republican party has danced around the edges of Social Security reform with the finesse of a bull in a china shop. Trump's bill, however, represents a departure from his previous promises to leave the program untouched, a volte-face that Democrats are eager to exploit.

The Electoral Calculus: Democrats believe that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act could be the linchpin of their electoral strategy, particularly given the demographics of the midterm electorate — older, working voters with a vested interest in Social Security. The narrative is ripe for political weaponization: the GOP, in its quest for fiscal conservatism, endangering the financial security of millions of Americans.

Republican Defiance: The response from the GOP, as articulated by Mike Marinella of the National Republican Congressional Committee, is a cocktail of defiance and denial, seasoned with a dash of vitriol. Dismissing the Democratic outcry as "bullshit" and fear-mongering, Marinella's retort encapsulates the Republican party's apparent strategy: deflect, deny, and deride.

However, this bluster may belie a deeper concern within the GOP ranks. Social Security is not just another policy issue; it's a lifeline for millions of Americans and a pillar of the social contract. Messing with it doesn't just threaten fiscal stability; it risks political suicide.

In Conclusion: The GOP's gambit with Social Security could indeed be the albatross around its neck come the 2026 midterms. Democrats, sensing an opportunity, are poised to hammer this point home at every turn. The Republican party, meanwhile, finds itself in a precarious position, defending a policy move that flies in the face of its leader's promises and the interests of a significant portion of its electorate.

The political theater of the 2026 midterms is shaping up to be a clash not just of policies, but of fundamental visions for America's future. And in this battle, the GOP's Social Security strategy may just be the own goal that cedes the field to the Democrats.
