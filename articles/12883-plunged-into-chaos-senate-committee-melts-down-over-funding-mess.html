'Plunged into chaos': Senate committee melts down over funding mess

Paulo SantosJuly 11, 20255 min read

U.S. Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) holds a press conference following the GOP weekly policy lunch on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 9, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

Samantha Handler with Punchbowl News live-tweeted as the Senate Appropriations Committee "plunged into chaos" Thursday over budget markups on the Commerce, Justice, Science bill that includes funding for NASA and the new FBI headquarters.

In an article on the debacle, Handler, John Bresnahan, and Laura Weiss wrote, "The Senate Appropriations Committee often passes each of its 12 annual spending bills on a bipartisan basis. This is what makes the Senate the Senate — and not the House.

"Yet the Senate Appropriations panel has its first FY2026 markup this morning, and senators still don’t have a deal on what they want to spend this year. There’s also no sense that they’ll be able to reach one."

Handler posted on X that Sens. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD), Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) and Jerry Moran (R-KS) were "trying to figure out a way forward," and were "working on a compromise on the FBI issue." All the while, Republicans were changing their votes to no, with Democrats changing their votes to yes.

The result was "chaos," Handler wrote.

According to the article, the two sides "appear to have a tacit agreement on two bills – Agriculture and Legislative Branch – to spend slightly more than FY2025. This puts the Senate at odds with House Republicans and the White House, which are seeking tens of billions of dollars in cuts to non-defense discretionary spending, aka social programs," the report said.

"However, a third bill — Commerce-Justice-Science — has become a partisan battleground over where the FBI will eventually land after leaving the old J. Edgar Hoover Building.

Van Hollen claimed, “This is a bigger issue than just the FBI building," adding, "I do think this goes to the prerogatives of the committee, because it’s the FBI building today. It could be any other project tomorrow.”

Punchbowl News claimed that the back-and-forth bickering over the bill was a "good example" of why there's a very real chance that the federal government could shut down this fall."

Our Analysis:

Senate Appropriations Committee Descends into Pandemonium

In what can only be described as a masterclass in governmental dysfunction, the Senate Appropriations Committee's recent session over budget markups for the Commerce, Justice, Science bill—a bill crucial not just for its implications on NASA but also on the contentious issue of the new FBI headquarters—devolved into utter chaos. This debacle, as chronicled by Samantha Handler of Punchbowl News, serves as a stark reminder of the fragile state of bipartisan cooperation in the Senate, a body once revered for its ability to reach across the aisle.

The Illusion of Bipartisanship

The Senate Appropriations Committee, historically known for its ability to pass spending bills with bipartisan support, has hit a wall. The recent FY2026 markup session illuminated a troubling reality: senators are at a standstill, lacking agreement on spending for the current year and showing no signs of reaching a consensus anytime soon. This deadlock undermines the very essence of what supposedly sets the Senate apart from the more tumultuous House of Representatives. The notion that the Senate is the bastion of bipartisanship is quickly becoming a relic of the past.

A Partisan Battlefield Over the FBI Headquarters

The crux of the matter appears to be the future location of the FBI headquarters, a debate that has spiraled into a partisan quagmire. Senators Chris Van Hollen (D-MD), Lisa Murkowski (R-AK), and Jerry Moran (R-KS) have been reported as attempting to navigate through this impasse, seeking a compromise. Yet, as Republicans and Democrats flip-flopped their votes in a bewildering display of political theater, the committee's proceedings descended into what can only be described as legislative anarchy.

Van Hollen's assertion that the issue transcends the mere relocation of the FBI headquarters and touches upon the broader prerogatives of the committee is particularly telling. It underscores the existential threat these squabbles pose to the integrity and functionality of the Senate Appropriations Committee. Today, it's the FBI headquarters; tomorrow, it could be any project of national significance, held hostage by partisan bickering.

Implications for Federal Funding and Governance

The discord within the Senate Appropriations Committee is symptomatic of a larger, more concerning trend: the erosion of functional governance. With two bills tentatively agreed upon to marginally increase spending from FY2025 levels, the Senate finds itself at loggerheads not only with House Republicans but also with the White House's directive seeking substantial cuts to non-defense discretionary spending, essentially targeting social programs. This schism raises the specter of a federal government shutdown this fall, a scenario that would have dire consequences for millions of Americans relying on government services and programs.

Conclusion: A Precipice of Dysfunction

The chaos that ensued during the Senate Appropriations Committee's markup session is not just a temporary lapse in judgment or a minor hiccup in the legislative process. It is a manifestation of a deeper, more systemic problem plaguing American governance. The inability of the Senate to function as a cohesive, bipartisan entity threatens not only the immediate projects and programs under consideration but also the very foundation of effective governance. If left unaddressed, this disarray signals a troubling trajectory towards legislative paralysis, where the needs of the American people are sidelined in favor of political posturing and brinkmanship.

In the end, the Punchbowl News article does not just recount the events of a single day's chaos; it serves as a clarion call for a return to principled, effective governance. As the Senate Appropriations Committee flounders, the question remains: will our legislators heed this call, or will they continue down the path of systemic dysfunction? Only time will tell, but for the sake of the nation, one can only hope for a resurgence of the collaborative spirit that once defined the "world's greatest deliberative body."
