Trump's 'hysterical rage' mocked as he rants about 'stuff I don't like'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump (Reuters)

In a Monday column, MSNBC producer Steve Benen mocked President Donald Trump for his rage about the way that famous actors, musicians and influencers trash him publicly.

Last week, rock star Bruce Springsteen told a UK audience that his homeland was "currently in the hands of a corrupt, incompetent and treasonous administration."

He doubled down in another show over the weekend, according to reports.

It triggered Trump to announce early Monday that he wanted an investigation into Springsteen, along with other famous supporters of former Vice President Kamala Harris during the 2024 election such as Beyoncé and Oprah Winfrey. Trump wrote without evidence that they were paid to endorse her.

Trump also named Bono in the group. However, the U2 singer never did any endorsement concerts or appearances for Harris. He did dedicate a song to Dr. Jill Biden in March 2024, along with "all the great women in our lives."

After including an excerpt of the Trump rant, Benen remarked, "It’s also probably worth mentioning in passing that Trump’s hysterical online communications don’t do any favors to his 'very stable genius' description of himself."

While it was the big news story that began the week, Benen said that it wasn't worth the energy to "get too worked up about every Trump tantrum, his rage toward celebrities who’ve dared to criticize him, his weird approach to pop culture, or his use of the word 'illegal' as a synonym for 'stuff I don’t like.'"

What Benen takes issue with is that Trump will "use the power of the state to pursue critics in authoritarian-style fashion."

It's all unfolding while Trump has an attorney general, who Benen said "sees herself as an extension of the White House and its political agenda." It raises the possibility of federal investigations actually attempting to go after celebrities Trump doesn't like.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Celebrity Battle: A Disturbing Display of Authoritarian Impulses

In a recent MSNBC column, Steve Benen offers a scathing critique of President Donald Trump's latest public meltdown. Trump's outrage towards celebrities criticizing him—specifically his demands for an investigation into figures like Bruce Springsteen, Beyoncé, and Oprah Winfrey—exposes a disturbing trend in his administration: the use of state power to silence dissent. This move, as ludicrous as it is alarming, showcases Trump’s penchant for authoritarianism, thinly veiled under the guise of combating alleged corruption.

Trump's Unfounded Claims and Celebrity Obsession

Trump's assertion—without evidence—that celebrities were paid to endorse Kamala Harris in the 2024 election is not just laughable; it's pathetically transparent. The inclusion of Bono in his tirade, despite the U2 frontman's lack of political endorsement for Harris, further highlights Trump's reckless disregard for truth in his vendetta against critical voices. This isn't about political strategy or fighting corruption; this is about an ego bruised by the outspoken disdain from the entertainment world.

The "Very Stable Genius" Unravels

Benen aptly points out the irony in Trump’s self-description as a “very stable genius,” a claim that grows more farcical with each unhinged tweet. Trump's online rants are not just a source of national embarrassment; they are a live feed of a presidency spiraling into authoritarian fantasy. His fixation on celebrity critics and his definition of "illegal" as anything he personally dislikes are not just bizarre; they're dangerous precedents.

A Threat to Democracy

The most concerning aspect of this debacle is not Trump's fragile ego, but his willingness to "use the power of the state to pursue critics in authoritarian-style fashion." This isn't some far-fetched dystopian scenario; it's unfolding in real-time under Trump's directive. With an Attorney General who views her role as an extension of the White House's political arm, the threat of federal investigations being weaponized against Trump's detractors is chillingly real.

Benen's Call to Rationality

Benen’s reluctance to expend energy on every Trump tantrum might seem like a call for complacency. Yet, it is anything but. It’s a call to focus on the underlying issue at hand: the erosion of democratic norms and the rise of authoritarian impulses in the highest office of the United States. The spectacle of Trump's rages might be momentarily distracting, but the systemic abuse of power they signify is where our attention should urgently lie.

Conclusion: A Mockery of the Presidency

Donald Trump's latest outburst against celebrities is more than a petty squabble—it's a stark reminder of his authoritarian tendencies. His attempt to mobilize state resources against personal critics not only undermines the very principles of free speech and democracy but also makes a mockery of the presidency. It's imperative that we recognize these actions for what they are: a dangerous conflation of personal vendettas with state power, indicative of a leader more concerned with silencing dissent than governing a nation.
