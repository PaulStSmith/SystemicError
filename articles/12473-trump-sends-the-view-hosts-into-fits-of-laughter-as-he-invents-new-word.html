Trump sends 'The View' hosts into fits of laughter as he invents new word

The View co-hosts Whoopi Goldberg and Alyssa Farah Griffin (Photo: Screen capture/ABC Video)

The co-hosts of "The View" had a good laugh at President Donald Trump on Wednesday after he either slurred his words — or made up a new one altogether.

At issue is Trump's new conspiracy that President Barack Obama invented the evidence that Russia meddled in the 2016 election. It's nearly 10 years later and, Sunny Hostin remarked, Obama still "lives rent free in his head."

"This was treason," Trump said on Tuesday from the Oval Office. "This was every word you could think of. They tried to steal the election. Irrefutable proof that Obama was sedatious, that Obama led — was trying to lead a coup."

The women at the table cracked up laughing while Whoopi Goldberg sat confused.

"Did — what did he say?" she questioned.

"I think he wants to be sedated," the president's former communications director Alyssa Farah Griffin said with a laugh.

"He wants to be sedated?" Goldberg repeated.

"Okay, you know, the thing about him is that he's so jealous of Obama, because Obama is everything that he is not," said Joy Behar. "Trim, smart, handsome."

"Happily married," Hostin jumped in.

"You see, he will never be cool," Hostin later added of Trump.

Griffin pivoted to note that all of the accusations are a distraction from Trump's ongoing Jeffrey Epstein scandal. Republicans are now talking about having Ghislaine Maxwell testify before Congress.

"I would say if Ghislaine Maxwell would testify, I wouldn't believe a word that a convicted sex trafficker said because her only incentive and motivation would be to lighten her own sentence because she's currently doing about — sentenced to 20 years."

She recalled that she was working for Vice President Mike Pence when he went to Russia and told President Vladimir Putin to his face that the U.S. was aware of what he did in the election and he better not do it again.

Our Analysis:

The Circus of Politics: A Case Study

In a recent episode that could only be described as a tragicomic display of political theater, the co-hosts of "The View" found themselves in stitches over former President Donald Trump's latest verbal faux pas—or as some might argue, a creative addition to the English language. The focal point of their amusement? Trump's accusation that former President Barack Obama fabricated the evidence of Russian meddling in the 2016 election, coupled with a peculiar choice of words that left viewers bewildered and entertained in equal measure.

Trump's Lexical Innovation: "Sedatious"?

Trump, in a statement that might leave linguists scratching their heads, accused Obama of being "sedatious" and attempting to lead a coup. This term, which seems to be a blend of "seditious" and perhaps "sedate," does not exist in the English language, but it certainly adds a unique flavor to Trump's already colorful political rhetoric. It's a testament to his unparalleled ability to mangle the English language in ways that keep fact-checkers, comedians, and the general public on their toes.

Why Obama, Again?

The fixation on Obama is a phenomenon that deserves its own analysis. Sunny Hostin's remark that Obama "lives rent-free in his head" hits the nail on the head. Nearly a decade later, Trump's obsession with his predecessor remains as strong as ever, suggesting a blend of envy and a strategic diversion from his own controversies. Obama, with his calm demeanor and scandal-free presidency, stands in stark contrast to Trump's tumultuous tenure, marked by endless controversies and a deeply divided nation.

Distraction Tactics: The Jeffrey Epstein Scandal

Alyssa Farah Griffin's pivot to Trump's ongoing Jeffrey Epstein scandal is particularly noteworthy. It's a classic Trump maneuver: when faced with damaging accusations, deflect and distract. The mention of Ghislaine Maxwell testifying before Congress is a smokescreen, an attempt to shift the narrative away from Trump's own entanglements and to undermine the credibility of potential testimonies against him.

The Irony of Credibility

Griffin's skepticism about Maxwell's potential testimony due to her status as a convicted sex trafficker is laced with irony. The implication is clear: why should we trust the words of a convicted criminal? Yet, this is the same political landscape where individuals with questionable ethics and legal entanglements are often given platforms to spread misinformation and manipulate public opinion. The hypocrisy is palpable.

Final Thoughts

The episode of "The View" serves as a microcosm of the larger absurdity that pervades American politics. It's a realm where language is twisted, past presidents are vilified without basis, and serious legal scandals are brushed off with a laugh. Yet, beneath the laughter lies a troubling reality of distraction, division, and the degradation of public discourse. As observers, we must sift through the noise, question the narratives presented to us, and seek the truth, no matter how uncomfortable it may be.
