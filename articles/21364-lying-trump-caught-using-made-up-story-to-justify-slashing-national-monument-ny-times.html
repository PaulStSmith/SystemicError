'Lying' Trump caught using made-up story to justify slashing national monument: NY Times

Based on an article by Matthew Chapman

Trump Needed a Fiction to Shrink Bears Ears

The Lie Was the Policy

Trump did not discover a hidden access problem at Bears Ears. He invented one, then used the invention to justify stripping more than 1.2 million acres of protection from a monument that already allowed recreation. The government documents make the sequence plain: the false story came first, the rollback followed.

That matters because it shows how executive power now works in practice. The decision was not driven by evidence, but by a preferred outcome in search of a public justification.

The Briefing Already Existed

The public record undercuts the whole performance. Utah officials had already briefed Trump’s team on the monument’s actual recreational access, including trails open to off-road vehicles and a backcountry corridor used by backpackers. Utah officials also allow hunting and fishing with the proper licenses.

So the claim that people were being barred from the land was not a mistake born of distance or ignorance. It was a political pretext. The administration had the facts, and chose the lie anyway.

Interior Chose Collaboration

Kate MacGregor’s “That’s exactly right, sir” was not a stray line. It was institutional obedience in real time. The Interior Department did not serve as a check on Trump’s falsehood. It echoed him.

That is the deeper corruption in stories like this: not just the central figure’s dishonesty, but the bureaucracy’s willingness to launder it into policy. Once subordinates validate the fiction, the lie stops being rhetoric and becomes administrative action.

Tribal Sovereignty Treated as Obstacle

Bears Ears was first designated a monument in 2016, over the objections of local tribal groups when Trump first targeted it, and the Biden administration later restored the full monument. That history exposes the real political conflict: not access versus closure, but preservation and tribal stewardship versus extraction-friendly rollback.

Trump’s assault on Bears Ears was never about hikers being locked out. It was about weakening protections on land that development interests have long wanted open to grazing, drilling, and mining. The phony “access” complaint is a convenient mask for a much older agenda.

The Pattern Is Manufactured Permission

This is the modern authoritarian habit in miniature: announce a grievance, ignore contrary facts, recruit subordinates to affirm it, then convert the lie into state action. The target here was public land. The method is portable.

Bears Ears shows how power abuses public language to clear the way for private extraction and political domination. The lie is not incidental to the policy. It is the policy’s permit.
