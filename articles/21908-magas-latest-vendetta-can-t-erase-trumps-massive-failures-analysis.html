MAGA’s latest vendetta can't erase Trump’s massive failures: analysis

The GOP Needs a Pandemic Ghost to Feed Its Base

A Hearing With a Prewritten Verdict

The source story is simple: a GOP-controlled Senate committee voted to recommend contempt for Anthony Fauci after he invoked the Fifth Amendment, with Rand Paul leading the attack. Fauci left his post four years ago. The country is dealing with costs, jobs, and a damaged political order. Republicans dragged an 85-year-old former health official back into the spotlight anyway.

That is not oversight. It is ritualized revenge dressed up as institutional procedure.

Power, Not Confusion

The people with actual power here are not the clerks, not the public, and not Fauci. The power sits with Senate Republicans who control the committee, the politicians who choose the target, and the media apparatus that turns the spectacle into fuel. They are not trying to solve a policy problem. They are choosing a scapegoat that can be blamed without requiring them to answer for the broader failures of the pandemic years.

The article tries to frame the vendetta as something that “defies logic and reason.” It does not. It is politically coherent. MAGA politics runs on grievance, victimhood, and a permanent enemy. Fauci is useful because he symbolizes expertise, restraint, and public authority. Those are exactly the things the movement wants to discredit.

The Real Purpose of the Performance

Marcotte’s central point is the right one: MAGA longs for 2020 because the pandemic gave it a stage. Not because the virus itself was good for anyone, but because it let the right turn public-health rules into a culture war. Mask mandates became props. Grocery-store tantrums became content. Refusal became identity.

That is the pattern. When a movement cannot defend its governing record, it reaches for outrage theater. It does not matter that the pandemic killed more than a million Americans. It matters that the crisis gave MAGA a way to organize resentment, especially among people primed to believe that basic rules are persecution if they apply to them.

The underpaid clerks in grocery stores were not the oppressors. The evangelical leaders claiming persecution were not victims. The people filming themselves refusing masks were not defending liberty. They were participating in a politics of domination while pretending they were being bullied.

Fauci Is the Target, Not the Story

Fauci is being used as a stand-in for something larger: the idea that institutions can impose obligations on powerful people and ordinary citizens alike. That is what MAGA cannot tolerate. It wants authority when it serves them and rebellion when it does not. It wants the state to punish enemies and disappear when the rules apply to its own voters.

That is why the contempt threat matters less as legal action than as message discipline. The point is to keep the base agitated, keep pandemic resentment alive, and keep attention away from the present. The article notes that most voters are focused on living costs and a tough job market. Republicans are answering that with a hearing about a disease that most people are trying to leave behind.

This is not a mistake. It is misdirection with a committee subpoena attached.

Trump’s Weakness Is the Subtext

The story also exposes something Republicans do not want to admit: Trump is politically weak. The article points to poor approval ratings, a faltering economy, and even the Iran war as part of the backdrop. That is the reason old pandemic grievances are being reheated. When a presidency is failing on current terms, its defenders dig up old enemies and force them back into the frame.

The pattern is familiar. Authoritarian politics cannot live on performance alone forever, so it scavenges for unresolved fear. If it cannot deliver competence, it can still deliver enemies. Fauci becomes the convenient object because attacking him lets Republicans avoid talking about prices, jobs, governance, or the damage their movement has caused in office.

The Pattern Is the Point

What this story reveals is not confusion but method. MAGA does not need a coherent public philosophy to survive. It needs recurring enemies, ceremonial rage, and enough institutional control to convert resentment into official action. A former health official becomes the target because he represents the kind of public authority that fascistic politics must eventually humiliate.

That is the larger lesson. When Republican power is weak in substance, it grows loud in theater. It cannot fix the present, so it resurrects a dead conflict and calls it accountability.
