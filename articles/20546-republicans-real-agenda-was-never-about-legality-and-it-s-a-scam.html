Republicans' real agenda was never about legality —and it's a scam

Based on an article by John Stoehr

Paulo SantosJuly 7, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Birthright Citizenship Survived. The Anti-Immigration State Did Not Blink.

The Court Decision Was Not the Story

The Supreme Court upheld birthright citizenship, and the right wing reacted like a failed coup had been interrupted. That reaction matters more than the ruling’s legal trivia. The point is not that Republicans misunderstood the law. The point is that many of them have moved past pretending to care about law at all.

Republicans Are Not Arguing Policy

The source material is clear: in GOP circles, immigration is increasingly treated as “civilizational suicide,” and the target is not “illegal” conduct but the people themselves. That is the real politics here. Federal law and the Constitution are not guardrails to these reactionaries. They are obstacles. The language of “border security” is camouflage for a racial and cultural purge dressed up as public order.

The Deportation Machine Keeps Moving

While the party hurls propaganda, the state keeps grinding. The source cites 10,000 immigrant arrests in five days, 63,000 people in detention, deaths in custody that the administration no longer plans to report, 6,200 minors detained since the start of last year, and 500 of those minors being literal babies. Those are not administrative mistakes. That is a coercive system doing what it was built to do: trap, disappear, and normalize cruelty.

Democrats Are Still Negotiating With Delusion

The weaker part of the story is not Republican extremism. It is Democratic timidity. The source is right to say the party’s center still treats Republican bad faith as if it were sincere disagreement. That habit is political self-harm. “Pro-immigration, pro-security” is not a strategy if it leaves the boot on immigrant necks while Democrats argue over the upholstery. It concedes the premise that immigrants are a problem to be managed, not people whose rights the state must presume.

The Real Pattern

This is the familiar American pattern of authoritarian politics: define a group as dangerous, turn punishment into procedure, then ask moderates to find common ground with the machinery of exclusion. The Republicans are the active force here, but Democratic inertia helps them by refusing to name the project clearly enough. If the goal is to defend immigration as a democratic good, then the system itself has to be torn out by the roots and rebuilt around human equality, not bureaucratic suspicion. Anything less leaves the same state ready for the next round of persecution, just with cleaner branding.
