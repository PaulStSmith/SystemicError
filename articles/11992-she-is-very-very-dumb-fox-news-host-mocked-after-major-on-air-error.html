'She is very, very dumb': Fox News host mocked after major on-air error

Fox News host Lisa Kennedy Montgomery (also known as "Kennedy") recently committed a major on-air error, and is being roundly mocked for it on social media.

On Monday, Kennedy was criticizing California Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) over his proposal to allow California voters to decide this November whether to get rid of the Golden State's independent redistricting commission. Newsom floated the idea in response to Texas Republicans carrying out a mid-decade redistricting plan aiming to strip Democrats of five seats in the U.S. House of Representatives.

"This is why California is a monoparty state. That's why Democrats have a supermajority and, you know, that's why they have two Democrat senators. That's why they have a Democrat governor," she said. "And it's such a massive majority because of this type of redistricting, which is incredibly insincere."

Many commentators, journalists and other experts took to social media to remind Kennedy that statewide elected officers like U.S. senators and governors are not impacted by redistricting efforts, which are confined to U.S. House districts. Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen wrote: "Can anyone explain to me how California has 2 Dem Senators and a Dem Governor because of gerrymandering?"

"Um... how does gerrymandering impact the election of Senators and the Governor?" St. Mary's College political science professor Todd Eberly tweeted.

"It was generally assumed you were smarter than to think a STATE WIDE VOTE is affected by how congressional districts are shaped or reshaped IN that state," former MSNBC host Keith Olbermann posted to X. "It's not one county, one vote."

"She is very very dumb," quipped Democratic commentator and 2024 Democratic National Convention delegate Kaivan Shroff.

Our Analysis:

A Monumental Misstep: Kennedy's Redistricting Rumble

In what can only be described as a spectacular blunder, Fox News host Lisa Kennedy Montgomery, better known as "Kennedy", has catapulted herself into the limelight for all the wrong reasons. Her recent on-air commentary slamming California Governor Gavin Newsom's redistricting proposal has not only backfired spectacularly but has also exposed a glaring gap in her understanding of the political landscape she critiques. Let's dissect this fiasco with the precision of a surgeon and the ruthlessness of a critic.

The Faux Pas

Kennedy's argument hinges on a fundamentally flawed premise: that California's status as a "monoparty state" with Democratic dominance in the Senate and gubernatorial office is a direct result of insidious redistricting strategies. This assertion would be hilarious if it wasn't so tragically misinformed. The roles of U.S. Senators and Governors are decided through statewide elections, rendering them immune to the effects of redistricting, which concerns itself solely with U.S. House districts.

The Fallout

The backlash was swift and merciless, with commentators from across the political spectrum rushing to highlight Kennedy's error. Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen's incredulity, St. Mary's College political science professor Todd Eberly's bewilderment, and former MSNBC host Keith Olbermann's scathing critique all underscore a collective disbelief at the level of misunderstanding displayed by Kennedy. The cherry on top? Democratic commentator Kaivan Shroff's blunt assessment of Kennedy's intelligence—or the perceived lack thereof.

The Irony

What makes Kennedy's blunder spectacularly ironic is the platform from which she launched her ill-fated tirade. Fox News, a behemoth in the conservative media landscape, prides itself on political commentary. Yet, here we have a prominent figure on the network demonstrating a fundamental misunderstanding of how our electoral system operates. If the goal was to critique Governor Newsom's proposal, the effort was not just a failure; it was an own goal that has served only to undermine Kennedy's credibility and, by extension, that of the network.

The Real Issue at Hand

Governor Newsom's proposal to allow Californian voters to decide on the fate of the state's independent redistricting commission is a significant political maneuver, especially in light of Texas Republicans' controversial mid-decade redistricting plan. This plan, which Kennedy conveniently glossed over in her criticism, represents a genuine concern for the integrity of democratic processes. Instead of a factual and nuanced discussion on these matters, Kennedy's commentary has devolved into a circus of misinformation.

Conclusion: A Teachable Moment

Lisa Kennedy Montgomery's misstep should serve as a cautionary tale for political commentators. In an era where misinformation can spread like wildfire, the responsibility on figures like Kennedy to be well-informed and accurate in their commentary has never been greater. As we navigate these tumultuous political waters, let's hope for a future where discourse is driven by facts, not fallacies. In the meantime, Kennedy's gaffe will stand as a stark reminder of the importance of understanding the basics of the political structures one chooses to critique.
