'Verbal jujitsu:' ABC host ends interview as JD Vance refuses to answer bribery question

U.S. Vice President JD Vance addresses the Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC) in National Harbor, Maryland, U.S., February 20, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

ABC’s George Stephanopoulos, a host and former White House Communications Director, shut down an interview with JD Vance after the vice president refused to answer a question about a reported bribery scheme.

Vance spoke with Stephanopoulos on ABC, and the host asked questions about reports that “Border Czar” Tom Homan had accepted a $50,000 bribe as part of an operation by the FBI years ago.

Instead of answering the direct question on whether Homan had accepted the money, Vance pivoted to talk about the shutdown, on which he blamed Democrats. He called the question a “left-wing rabbit hole.”

“It’s not a weird left-wing rabbit hole. I didn’t insinuate anything,” the host said as he abruptly ended the interview on Sunday. “I asked you whether Tom Homan accepted $50,000, as was heard on an audiotape recorded by the FBI in September 2024. And you did not answer the question. Thank you for your time this morning.”

MSNBC correspondent Ken Dilanian said Vance had “engaged in verbal jujitsu as he avoided answering whether Homan kept $50K in taxpayer money from undercover FBI agents,” and highlighted ABC’s response on social media.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Evasion: JD Vance’s Interview Tango

In a recent display of political evasion so blatant it could be considered an art form, JD Vance, the Vice President, took to ABC for an interview with George Stephanopoulos, only to dance around a critical question like a contestant on Dancing with the Stars. But unlike the TV show, the stakes here involve potential corruption and the integrity of a government official, specifically “Border Czar” Tom Homan.

The Unanswered Question

At the heart of the controversy is a report that Tom Homan accepted a $50,000 bribe as part of an FBI operation years ago. When pressed by Stephanopoulos on whether Homan had indeed accepted the bribe, Vance took a detour into a completely unrelated topic: the government shutdown, for which he conveniently blamed the Democrats. His dismissive reply labeled the question as a “left-wing rabbit hole,” a tactic as transparent as it is tired.

Stephanopoulos Holds His Ground

Stephanopoulos, a seasoned journalist and former White House Communications Director himself, was not about to be sidestepped. His response was both firm and factual: “It’s not a weird left-wing rabbit hole. I didn’t insinuate anything,” he stated, before cutting the interview short in a move as abrupt as it was warranted. “I asked you whether Tom Homan accepted $50,000, as was heard on an audiotape recorded by the FBI in September 2024. And you did not answer the question. Thank you for your time this morning.”

The Verbal Jujitsu of JD Vance

MSNBC correspondent Ken Dilanian aptly characterized Vance’s performance as “verbal jujitsu,” highlighting his skill in avoiding a direct answer. This evasion is particularly concerning given the gravity of the accusation against Homan. The refusal to address the question head-on not only undermines public trust but also signals a disturbing level of comfort with the possibility of corruption within the ranks.

ABC’s Stand and the Public’s Right to Know

ABC’s decision to end the interview and to subsequently highlight the evasion on social media is a commendable stance on transparency and accountability. In an era where political figures often dodge difficult questions with impunity, Stephanopoulos’s refusal to let Vance off the hook serves as a reminder that journalists have a critical role to play in holding power to account.

The Bottom Line

JD Vance’s interview with George Stephanopoulos is a textbook example of political evasion. His refusal to answer a straightforward question about a serious allegation of corruption is not just frustrating—it’s alarming. It raises questions about what he, and perhaps others in the administration, might be trying to hide. The public deserves clear, honest answers, especially when it comes to the integrity of those in high office. This incident should not disappear into the news cycle but rather be a catalyst for deeper investigation and accountability.

In the end, Vance’s attempt to sidestep a critical question only spotlighted his apparent disregard for transparency and accountability. It’s a stark reminder of the importance of persistent, fact-based journalism in the fight against political obfuscation.
