'Ticking time bomb': Pro-Trump investor says trade war could end GOP control of Congress

On Wednesday, Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell warned that President Donald Trump's tariffs could spike inflation and harm economic growth. But one investor who has publicly backed Trump believes there could also be a political cost next year.

In a CNN interview, Canadian investor Kevin O'Leary — also known for his role on the CNBC show "Shark Tank" — opined that most of Trump's tariffs would be quickly reversed after negotiating with trade partners. But he was less optimistic about the prospect of a deal being worked out with China. And he warned that the longer Trump's standoff with China went on, the worse his party would fare in next year's midterm elections.

"Let's be pragmatic. The midterms are less than 18 months away," O'Leary said. Trump can't have this on his back at the midterms. He risks losing a majority. So he's got to resolve this quickly, and that's why they're going at breakneck speed."

O'Leary went on to say that no prior administration has tried to negotiate trade deals with "60-plus countries at once," but believed Trump would still be able to stave off negative consequences from tariffs with Canada and Mexico. He worried that the ongoing standoff between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping would result in lasting economic and political consequences.

"The big deal here is China. That's the behemoth. We're the biggest market. Whey know it. So much of what's made in China goes to the United States. And so that's really the one to focus on," he said. "I think the rest is a sideshow because it's going to get resolved. Probably equivalent tariffs or no tariffs at all. But the China deal, that's a big deal."

"This is just posturing. We've got two behemoth leaders that don't want to look like they're weak," he added. "I get it, but this is a ticking time bomb, and it's gotta get resolved. There's no question about it."

Our Analysis:

Tariffs, Trump, and Ticking Time Bombs: A Critical Analysis

In a recent development that should surprise exactly no one, Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell has sounded the alarm on President Donald Trump's tariffs, suggesting they could spike inflation and harm economic growth. This warning slices through the administration's rhetoric like a hot knife through butter, exposing the potential economic turmoil lying beneath. Yet, in an almost tragicomic twist, Canadian investor and television personality Kevin O'Leary, a public backer of Trump, attempts to swathe this grim prognosis in a layer of misguided optimism during a CNN interview.

O'Leary's Optimism: Misplaced or Misguided?

O'Leary's comments offer a fascinating glimpse into the cognitive dissonance that plagues some of Trump's supporters. He acknowledges the peril inherent in Trump's tariffs, particularly with respect to China, yet clings to the hope that most of Trump's tariffs will be "quickly reversed" after negotiations. This is a bold claim, especially considering the less than optimistic outlook on reaching a deal with China. His assertion that the standoff with China could spell disaster for Trump's party in the upcoming midterm elections is one of the few moments of lucidity in an otherwise foggy analysis.

"Let's be pragmatic." Indeed, let's. The belief that Trump's administration, which has been characterized by its erratic approach to both domestic and international policy, can swiftly and effectively reverse the potential damage of these tariffs is at best, wishful thinking. O'Leary's statement that no prior administration has attempted to negotiate trade deals with "60-plus countries at once" is not a badge of honor; it's a red flag.

The China Conundrum

O'Leary correctly identifies China as the "behemoth" in this scenario, a fact that anyone with even a cursory understanding of global economics could affirm. However, his portrayal of the situation as a mere posturing contest between "two behemoth leaders" trivializes the complexity of international trade negotiations and the very real consequences of these tariffs. To call this situation a "ticking time bomb" is to acknowledge the potential for disaster while seemingly absolving Trump of the responsibility for creating the crisis in the first place.

The analogy of a sideshow used to describe the negotiations with Canada and Mexico further demonstrates a lack of understanding or acknowledgment of the interconnectedness of global economies. Dismissing the potential impact of tariffs with these countries overlooks the nuanced and deeply intertwined nature of North American trade relations.

A Scathing Reality Check

The narrative pushed by O'Leary and by extension, the Trump administration, is dangerously simplistic. It fails to grasp the full breadth of the potential economic fallout from these tariffs. Powell's warning should not be taken lightly, nor should it be overshadowed by the baseless optimism of individuals who refuse to acknowledge the gravity of the situation.

In conclusion, as the midterm elections approach, Trump indeed "can't have this on his back." However, the suggestion that these issues can be quickly or easily resolved is not only misguided but potentially harmful. The real sideshow here is the attempt to downplay the serious economic risks posed by these tariffs. It's a high-stakes game of economic roulette, and unfortunately, it's the average American who may end up paying the price.

The clock is indeed ticking, and it's high time for a dose of reality in this discussion.
