'They've nailed it': Analyst sees 'wild' consequence for Trump as shutdown plan flounders

Donald Trump gestures to reporters from the roof of the White House. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump’s efforts to distract from, or deflect blame on, the federal government shutdown have been an utter failure, Axios’ Sara Fischer told CNN’s Audie Cornish on Monday morning.

The shutdown, which has lasted for weeks with no indications of an end in sight, began over the expiration of Affordable Care Act subsidies for millions of people. Democrats are refusing to provide Republicans with the votes to reopen the government until they negotiate over extending those subsides, and there are also demands for a guarantee that Trump’s budget director cannot unilaterally cancel the money.

Polls have consistently found more voters blaming Trump and Republicans for the current situation.

“What’s wild ... is that he brokered this historic peace deal between Gaza and Israel amid the shutdown,” said Fischer. “And you would have thought that that would be reflected in his approval ratings. What we’re seeing is that the country has basically forgotten about that peace deal and is totally focused on not getting access to their health care benefits, their SNAP and food benefits.”

This means two things, she continued: “A, it shows you where the country’s priorities are in terms of domestic policy over foreign policy, but B, it also shows you that he could do as much as he says he wants to do in terms of brokering peace and Nobel Peace Prizes, and Ukraine and Russia and Israel, Gaza. And it doesn’t matter if people are struggling here at home. That’s all that’s going to matter.”

“And the attempt to put it on Democrats seems to have floundered, right? To call it the ‘Schumer Shutdown’ or that sort of thing,” said Cornish. “The numbers, at least in this early polling, aren’t suggesting that that’s how people receive that information.”

“Democrats have done a terrible job for the most part of messaging throughout the end of the Biden administration and into the 2024 election, but they’ve nailed it in terms of the shutdown messaging around Republicans are taking away your health care,” agreed Fischer. “People generally seem to understand that that’s where the Democrats are trying to hold firm. And I think that’s why you’re seeing that reflected in these approval ratings.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Attempt to Deflect Blame: A Miserable Failure

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of political ineptitude, President Donald Trump’s attempts to shift the blame for the current federal government shutdown have fallen flat on their face. According to Axios’ Sara Fischer in a conversation with CNN’s Audie Cornish, not even Trump’s “historic peace deal” between Gaza and Israel has managed to distract the American public from the shutdown debacle. Let’s dissect this calamity piece by piece.

Domestic Woes Over Shadow Historic Peace

Firstly, the audacity to think that an international peace deal could overshadow the immediate and tangible impact of a federal shutdown on American citizens is a testament to the administration’s disconnect with reality. The shutdown, precipitated by the expiration of Affordable Care Act subsidies, has left millions without essential services. Yet, the Trump administration seemed to operate under the delusion that foreign policy achievements could serve as a smokescreen for domestic policy failures.

Voters Aren’t Distracted

Polls have consistently shown that voters place the blame squarely on Trump and the Republicans. This is a stark reminder that when citizens are denied access to healthcare, food benefits, and other basic services, they are unlikely to be placated by diplomatic victories abroad. Fischer’s observation that the country has “basically forgotten about that peace deal” is a damning indictment of where the administration’s priorities lie—or, more accurately, where they should have been.

The ‘Schumer Shutdown’ Narrative Falls Flat

Trump’s attempt to pin the shutdown on the Democrats, coining it the ‘Schumer Shutdown’, has been an exercise in futility. Early polling data has made it abundantly clear that this narrative isn’t resonating with the public. It’s a classic case of political misdirection failing to find its mark. The people know who controls the White House. They know who’s at the helm, steering the ship directly into the storm.

Democratic Messaging Finds Its Footing

It’s worth noting that despite previous struggles with messaging, Democrats seem to have found a narrative that resonates with the American people regarding the shutdown. They’ve successfully communicated that Republicans are jeopardizing healthcare, clearly a sore spot with the electorate. Fischer’s acknowledgment of this fact is a rare nod to a strategic success in what has otherwise been a sea of missteps for the Democrats leading up to the 2024 election.

Conclusion

The crux of this debacle lies in the fundamental misjudgment of American priorities. The Trump administration’s failure to grasp that domestic issues, particularly those as vital as healthcare and food security, will always trump (pun intended) foreign policy achievements in the eyes of the electorate. This miscalculation has not only exacerbated the suffering caused by the shutdown but has also significantly dented the administration’s credibility and approval ratings.

In a desperate bid to deflect blame, the administration has only managed to further alienate itself from the American people. As the shutdown drags on with “no indications of an end in sight,” one can’t help but wonder how much more damage will be done before the lesson is learned: You cannot distract from systemic failures at home with triumphs abroad.
