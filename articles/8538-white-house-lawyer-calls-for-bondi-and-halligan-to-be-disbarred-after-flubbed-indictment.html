White House lawyer calls for Bondi and Halligan to be 'disbarred' after flubbed indictment

Former Trump White House lawyer Ty Cobb (image via screengrab)

Former White House attorney Ty Cobb berated U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan for neglecting to put indictments against former FBI head James Comey before a full grand jury — one of the most entry-level procedural steps a prosecutor faces.

“You couldn’t find a high school stockboy at Home Depot who could have handled this more ineptly than Lindsey Halligan did,” said Cobb, who worked for President Donald Trump in his first term. “Taking am indictment that the grand jury never saw, having the foreman sign it and then presenting it to a judge? That’s the height of ineptitude and misconduct.”

“There is no indictment if only the foreman and some grand juror who was hanging out in the courtroom saw it — that’s two. A quorum is 12,” Cobb added. “You need a quorum to return an indictment. So, the indictment that was presented and they’ve been proceeding on really is a nullity.”

It doesn’t even count as an indictment, said Cobb, so it doesn’t even have to be dismissed. Coupled with Comey’s argument over misconduct issues, miscarriage of justice issues, the denial of due process issues, and the vindictiveness issues, “you know, this case is dead,” said Cobb.

Cobb added that the Wednesday revelation made a critical argument for Trump’s appointed Attorney General Pam Bondi to lose her law license.

“I do think that both Halligan and Bondi should be disbarred,” said Cobb. “Bondi has twice submitted affirmations to this court about the propriety of Lindsey Halligan’s grand jury presentation. She knew this. There’s no way she could not have known this. And that just means that she lied or that she’s equally incompetent, but more likely that she lied.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Ineptitude in Prosecutorial Procedures: A Case Study

Former White House attorney Ty Cobb’s scathing critique of U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan’s handling of indictments against former FBI head James Comey brings to light a glaring example of procedural ineptitude at a level that should alarm anyone with even a cursory understanding of the legal process. Cobb’s comparison of Halligan’s actions to that of a “high school stockboy at Home Depot” handling the situation “ineptly” isn’t just a harsh critique; it’s an indictment of the competence expected at the highest levels of our legal system.

The Height of Ineptitude and Misconduct

Cobb details a process so fundamentally flawed that it borders on farcical. The idea that an indictment could be maneuvered around the grand jury process, with a presentation to a judge sans the necessary quorum, is not just inept; it’s potentially unlawful. The grand jury system is a cornerstone of the American legal process, designed to prevent arbitrary actions by prosecutors. It’s a check on power that was, according to Cobb’s account, flagrantly bypassed.

The Nullity of a Non-Indictment

The assertion that “there is no indictment” if proper procedure isn’t followed underscores a basic principle of law and order: procedural safeguards exist to ensure fairness and justice. If an indictment is presented without the backing of a quorum, it’s not merely a procedural error; it’s a nullity. This isn’t a trifling matter of paperwork; it’s a fundamental failure that calls into question the legitimacy of the proceedings against Comey.

The Dead Case Walking

Cobb’s declaration that “this case is dead” isn’t just an observation; it’s a prognosis for a prosecution fatally wounded by its own misconduct and ineptitude. When combined with the myriad issues already plaguing the case — from misconduct to denial of due process — the botched indictment is the final nail in the coffin.

The Case for Disbarment

The recommendation that both Halligan and Pam Bondi, Trump’s appointed Attorney General, should be disbarred is a severe but unsurprising conclusion. Cobb’s argument hinges on the inescapable conclusion that Bondi, having affirmed the propriety of Halligan’s actions not once but twice, was either woefully ignorant of basic legal procedures or, more damningly, complicit in their circumvention. The suggestion that Bondi “lied” isn’t made lightly; it’s a serious accusation that speaks to the integrity of the legal profession and the fundamental principles of justice.

Conclusion

The debacle outlined by Ty Cobb isn’t just a story of individual failure; it’s a cautionary tale of what happens when the guardians of the law forsake their duties for political or personal gain. The procedural missteps described are not minor errors but grave misjudgments that threaten the very fabric of legal accountability. This isn’t merely an indictment of Lindsey Halligan or Pam Bondi; it’s a stark warning about the dangers of compromising legal standards, no matter the defendants or the stakes involved.
