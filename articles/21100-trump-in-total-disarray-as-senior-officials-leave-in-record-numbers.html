Trump in total disarray as senior officials leave in 'record numbers'

Trump’s Government Is Eating Its Own

The Numbers Tell On The Boss

In 18 months, 27 Senate-confirmed officials have left Trump’s second administration, a pace far above his first term and far beyond recent presidents. Some quit. Some were fired. Among the departures are Kristi Noem at Homeland Security, Pam Bondi at Justice, Lori Chavez-DeRemer at Labor, plus a string of exits at HHS, Treasury, and the intelligence post once held by Tulsi Gabbard.

Loyalty Was Never A Stable Contract

The point of this administration was supposed to be obedience. Trump filled the place with MAGA loyalists less likely to challenge him, then began discarding them anyway. That is not a staffing problem. It is a governing model that treats every appointee as temporary expendable material, useful only until they become inconvenient, embarrassing, or insufficiently submissive.

The Damage Starts At The Top

Trump is still the central decision-maker here. He installed the people, set the tone, and created the conditions for the churn. Noem’s exit followed scandals and spectacle. Bondi’s fell over the Epstein files and her refusal to do the political work Trump wanted. Chavez-DeRemer resigned amid complaints and allegations of misconduct. In each case, the institution is not drifting on its own; it is being run by a president who rewards performance art, not competence.

Misdirection As Management

The soft framing is to call this “desertion” and leave it at that, as if anonymous officials simply lost faith in the office. That flatters the White House and hides the chain of responsibility. People are leaving because this administration is built on fear, humiliation, and arbitrary punishment. When even loyalists are fired, the message to everyone else is simple: there is no job security, no institutional dignity, and no principle beyond Trump’s mood.

A Hollowed-Out Executive Branch

The deeper pattern is not churn for its own sake. It is the thinning of the federal government into a shell where power concentrates upward and accountability disappears downward. HHS loses confirmed officials under Robert F. Kennedy Jr.; Treasury bleeds departures under Scott Bessent; intelligence gets handed from one placeholder to another. This is what authoritarian administration looks like in practice: not efficient control, but brittle rule, where loyalty is demanded, expertise is distrusted, and the state is kept unstable on purpose.
