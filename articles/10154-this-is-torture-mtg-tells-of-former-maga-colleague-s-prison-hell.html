'This is torture': MTG tells of former MAGA colleague's prison hell

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Distorted Compass of Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Advocacy

Congresswoman Marjorie Taylor Greene attends pro-Trump supporters rally at New York criminal court on April 4, 2023. (Shutterstock.com)

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) is sounding the alarm over what she described as the inhumane conditions her former congressional colleague George Santos is being subjected to while incarcerated, saying they amount to “torture.”

A former Republican House representative, George Santos has been imprisoned at the Federal Correctional Institution, in New Jersey since late July after having been convicted of identity theft and wire fraud charges. He made frantic pleas out of concern for his own safety leading up to his sentence and, according to Greene, has been in solitary confinement for nearly 30 days as of Monday.

“They say [he’s in solitary confinement] ‘for his safety’ due to threats. I’m told he is in his cell 24 hours per day and he is only allowed to get a shower 3 times a week,” Greene wrote Monday evening in a social media post on X.

“He does not get any sunlight. He’s only allowed to buy stamps from the commissary and is drinking water from the sink… This is torture.”

Alongside her comments, Greene also shared a photo of a hand-drawn floor plan of Santos’ cell, both before and after being placed in solitary confinement. The lack of windows and small space Santos is now apparently subjected to, Greene said, has led her to call for a full pardon of her former GOP colleague.

“I have sent a letter asking for his sentence to [be] commuted, 87 months is way too long for the crimes he was sentenced for,” Greene wrote.

“There are criminals as we speak serving in Congress and many other former government officials that are criminals walking free that did far more heinous things than George Santos! George Santos had a better voting record than MOST members of Congress! Including EVERYONE who voted to expel him! Honestly, George should be pardoned!”

Santos was elected to represent part of New York in Congress in 2022, but was expelled by the House following his federal indictment, as well as after an investigation by the House Ethics Committee on his conduct. Santos was also found to have fabricated much of his life story leading up to his election victory, including falsely stating that he was Jewish and that he attended schools that had no record of his enrollment.

Our Analysis:

The Distorted Compass of Marjorie Taylor Greene’s Advocacy

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as a theater of the absurd, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene has taken it upon herself to be the herald of justice for her disgraced former colleague, George Santos. The narrative she weaves is rich with irony and selective amnesia, as she decries the conditions of Santos’ incarceration as “torture.” Let’s dissect this with the precision it deserves, shall we?

Firstly, it’s imperative to note that George Santos is not some political prisoner caught in the crosshairs of an authoritarian regime; he is a convicted criminal who pleaded guilty to identity theft and wire fraud. These are not trifling matters. They are serious crimes that undermine the very fabric of trust and legality upon which our society stands. Yet, Greene’s narrative would have you believe that Santos is a martyr, subjected to undue cruelty.

The conditions described by Greene—solitary confinement for his safety, limited showers, restricted commissary access, and drinking water from a sink—are indeed harsh, but they are not uncommon within the United States’ prison system. It’s peculiar, if not outright hypocritical, that Greene’s outrage is so narrowly focused. Where is this fervor when it comes to the inhumane conditions faced by thousands of other inmates across the country? The selective empathy is staggering.

Greene’s call for a full pardon because Santos had a “better voting record than MOST members of Congress” is an argument so flawed that it borders on satire. Since when does a voting record absolve someone of criminal behavior? This is not how justice works, nor should it ever be. The suggestion undermines the very principle of accountability that is foundational to our legal system and democracy.

Moreover, Greene’s insinuation that there are worse criminals in Congress and former government officials walking free is a dangerous deflection. It’s an attempt to muddy the waters and distract from the issue at hand. Yes, there are likely corrupt individuals in positions of power, but this does not, in any way, serve as a justification for pardoning Santos. The “others are worse” defense is a child’s argument, not befitting a member of Congress.

Lastly, let’s not forget the irony in Greene’s staunch defense of Santos. George Santos was expelled from the House after being found guilty of fabricating much of his life story, including his heritage and educational background. His entire career is built on a foundation of lies and deceit. It’s baffling, then, that Greene would choose this hill to die on, defending a man whose only contribution to politics has been scandal and fraud.

In conclusion, Greene’s outcry is not about justice; it’s about political theater. It’s a distraction, a way to rally her base around the perceived injustices against one of their own, no matter how undeserving he may be of such defense. The real torture here is having to endure the cognitive dissonance required to take Greene’s arguments seriously. The conditions of Santos’ confinement should indeed spark a broader conversation about the state of the American prison system, but let’s not be fooled into thinking that Greene’s motivations are anything but self-serving.
