'Con job!' Trump snaps at reporter over fact check on his big Thanksgiving claim

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump hosts a bilateral lunch with Hungary’s Prime Minister Viktor Orban at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 7, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump lashed out at an NBC reporter after she fact-checked him on the affordability of grocery prices.

During a White House meeting with Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán on Friday, Trump touted Walmart’s Thanksgiving meal promotion, which the company said was cheaper than in 2024. Fact checkers, however, pointed out that the promotional meal contained fewer items, used generic brands, and featured a smaller turkey.

“Mr. President, since you brought up the Walmart Thanksgiving meal, and it is cheaper, but it also contains less,” the reporter noted.

“Well, I haven’t heard that, you’re telling me,” Trump shot back. “Who are you with?”

“I’m with NBC News, sir,” the reporter replied.

“Fake news!” Trump bellowed. “NBC, you’re fake news! NBC’s gone down the tubes along with most of the rest of them.”

“Why do you think there’s such a disconnect between the economy that you’re describing today and the way many Americans say that people in and are concerned about our country right now?” the reporter pressed.

“Other than the shutdown, obviously, which is caused by the Democrats, could be ended by the Democrats in two minutes,” Trump insisted. “They feel much better. We have more jobs.”

“So I just heard this yesterday that Walmart said that the Thanksgiving was 25 more expensive, 25% more expensive under Biden,” he continued. “That’s a big, to me, that’s a big number, because Walmart’s respected. I mean, Walmart is Walmart, and, you know, they’re giving you prices.”

“So that would mean that the whole series of pricing and costs, you know, the groceries and everything else, it was a con job. It was a con job, affordability, they call it, was a con job by the Democrats. The Democrats are good at a few things, cheating in elections and conning people with facts that aren’t true.”

Trump repeated the claim that the Thanksgiving meal cost was down 25%.

“Walmart just announced it!” he exclaimed. “Two days ago, 25% cheaper! This year will be cheaper to have Thanksgiving than it was a year ago under Sleepy Joe Biden!”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misdirection: Trump vs. Reality

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the chaotic theater that is modern American politics, President Donald Trump engaged in a verbal joust with an NBC reporter, showcasing not just his disdain for the press, but also a remarkable detachment from economic realities faced by ordinary Americans. The stage was a White House meeting with Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, but the spotlight swiftly shifted to a contentious exchange over Walmart’s Thanksgiving meal promotion.

The Claim: A Cheaper Thanksgiving, Courtesy of Walmart

Trump lauded Walmart for allegedly making Thanksgiving dinner more affordable than in the previous year, 2024. However, this praise was quickly met with skepticism. Fact-checkers revealed that the promotional meal was, indeed, cheaper, but at the cost of quality and quantity. The items were fewer, generic brands replaced name brands, and the turkey was smaller. When confronted with these facts, Trump’s response was as predictable as it was evasive. “Fake news!” he retorted, brushing off the reporter with a label he’s wielded like a cudgel against any media scrutiny.

The Reality: Skirting Facts with Bluster

Trump’s interaction with the NBC reporter is emblematic of his broader approach to governance and public discourse: when faced with inconvenient truths, deflect, deny, and discredit. His claim that the Democrats are to blame for the economic strain felt by Americans, and his assertion that they could end this strain “in two minutes,” is a masterful display of misdirection. Blaming the opposition for systemic issues while taking credit for any perceived positive is a tactic as old as politics itself, but rarely executed with such brazen disregard for the facts.

The Disconnect: Economic Strain vs. Political Gain

The reporter’s question about the disconnect between Trump’s economic narrative and the reality of American concern was met with more than just deflection; it was answered with an outright falsehood. Trump claimed, without evidence, that Walmart had announced a 25% decrease in the cost of Thanksgiving meals compared to the previous year under President Joe Biden. This statement, designed to paint the Democrats as the architects of economic hardship, was not just misleading; it was an attempt to rewrite reality in real-time.

Conclusion: A Con Job or a Reality Check?

Trump’s closing remarks about the Democrats being “good at a few things, cheating in elections and conning people with facts that aren’t true,” are an ironic self-reflection of his own tactics. The real “con job” here is the attempt to manipulate public perception with unfounded claims, hoping that the repetition of falsehoods will somehow render them true. The manipulation of economic realities to serve a political narrative is not just intellectually dishonest—it’s a disservice to the American people, who deserve far more than smoke and mirrors.

In the end, this episode is a stark reminder of the chasm between political rhetoric and the lived experiences of everyday Americans. As we navigate these turbulent waters, it’s imperative to question, verify, and challenge the narratives presented to us, lest we fall victim to the very “con job” we’re warned about.
