'In too deep: Right-wing media remains loyal to Trump even as economy tanks — here's why

Fox News host Jeanine Pirro in Tampa, Florida in 2022 (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

Far-right Fox News host Jeanine Pirro drew plenty of scathing criticism from liberal and progressive media outlets for a comment she made during a panel discussion on April 3, when she remarked, "I don't really care about my 401k today. We've got to have manufacturing in this country."

Pirro made that remark at a time when the stock market was plummeting because of President Donald Trump's steep new tariffs. And many critics slammed Pirro as painfully tone-deaf, reminding her that for older Americans and retirees who need 401k money to live on, a tanking stocking market is no joke.

But in right-wing media, many pro-Trump pundits had no problem with what she said.

In an article published on April 12, The Guardian's Ed Berger examines right-wing media responses to the volatility the stock market is experiencing because of Trump's economic policies.

"The chaos of last week posed a serious challenge to many aspects of right-wing U.S. media, which often acts as a largely unquestioning cheerleader for Trump and his MAGA movement," Berger explains. "The story was sometimes played down, sometimes cheered but rarely seriously questioned — even amid warnings of price rises, recession and cratering investments, especially precious 401(k) retirement accounts."

Berger notes that some media outlets pride themselves on being to the right of Fox News.

"The most popular conservative news source in the United States is Fox News, which has a much larger audience than CNN and the left-wing MSNBC network," Berger observes. "Its hosts, such as Sean Hannity and Jesse Watters, consistently praise Trump and bolster his inaccurate claims. But Fox News has faced new competition from Newsmax and One American News Network (OANN), networks that positioned themselves as even more reliable Trump supporters…. While Fox Business, a sibling network, had guests who criticized the tariffs, Fox News personalities told viewers nervous about their investments that everything would work out well."

Berger points out that according to Media Matters' Matt Gertz, Fox News hosts are afraid to criticize Trump because they're "in too deep."

Gertz said of Fox News, "They have, for nearly a decade now, sold their audience on the sense that Donald Trump would be a good president. Now, he is single-handedly causing a worldwide market collapse…. (but) they can't abandon him."

Our Analysis:

The Echo Chamber of Unwavering Loyalty: Analyzing Far-Right Media's Response to Economic Turmoil

In the swirling vortex of political and economic discourse, Jeanine Pirro's comments on prioritizing manufacturing over 401k concerns, amidst a plummeting stock market due to Trump's tariffs, serve as a stark emblem of the far-right media's tone-deafness. This scenario, as dissected by Ed Berger in The Guardian, lays bare the unyielding loyalty of right-wing U.S. media to Trump's MAGA movement, even when faced with economic policies that threaten the financial stability of the very audience they purport to serve.

The Cult of Personality vs. Economic Reality

Pirro's remarks, while potentially aiming to underscore the importance of domestic manufacturing, ironically highlighted a dangerous disconnect. For many Americans, especially older citizens and retirees, the health of their 401k is not a trivial concern but a lifeline. Yet, in the echo chambers of right-wing media, this anxiety is dismissed or downplayed.

The Guardian article meticulously peels back the layers of this phenomenon, revealing an ecosystem "largely unquestioning" in its support for Trump, even as his policies send shockwaves through the economy. This unwavering allegiance, even in the face of potential recessions, price rises, and the erosion of retirement savings, is a testament to the power of political loyalty over economic pragmatism.

Beyond Fox News: The Far-Right Media Landscape

While Fox News has long been perceived as the bastion of conservative media, its coverage of Trump's tariff-induced market chaos is part of a larger narrative. Berger's exploration into the responses from networks like Newsmax and One American News Network (OANN), which vie to outdo Fox in their Trump loyalty, underscores a competitive zeal among right-wing outlets to be the premier cheerleaders of the Trump agenda.

This competition for the most fervent Trump support does not only highlight a media landscape skewed towards partisanship but also raises questions about the responsibility of media in reporting economic realities. The fact that some personalities within this sphere are painted as "afraid to criticize Trump" for fear of alienating their base, as noted by Media Matters' Matt Gertz, speaks volumes about the priorities of these outlets.

Conclusion: The Consequences of Unquestioning Support

The Guardian's analysis exposes a critical systemic flaw within the right-wing media ecosystem: an unwavering loyalty to Trump that blinds it to the economic hardships of its audience. This allegiance, prioritizing political ideology over factual economic analysis, not only misinforms the public but potentially exacerbates the very economic issues it chooses to ignore or downplay.

As the far-right media continues to navigate its role as both a news source and a cheerleader for Trump, the broader implications for American economic discourse and policy awareness remain deeply concerning. The juxtaposition of prioritizing abstract ideals over tangible financial health reveals a systemic error in media priorities, one that could have long-lasting repercussions for the very audience it claims to represent.
