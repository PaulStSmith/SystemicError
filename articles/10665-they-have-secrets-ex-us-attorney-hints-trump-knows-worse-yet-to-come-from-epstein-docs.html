'They have secrets': Ex-US attorney hints Trump knows worse yet to come from Epstein docs

Barbara McQuade on MSNBC (Screenshot)

The stunning release of a photograph of convicted pedophile Jeffrey Epstein holding a joke novelty check as payment for a woman at Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago estate may be a precursor to even worse revelations about Trump and his former friend.

Appearing on MSNBC on Tuesday morning, former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade claimed the photo, which has been described as “Epstein and a longtime Mar-a-Lago member joking about selling a 'fully depreciated' woman to Donald Trump for $22,500," is far more alarming than the actual birthday letter Trump allegedly wrote to his pal Epstein, which was also revealed on on Monday after being hinted at weeks ago.

McQuade was asked on “Morning Joe, “Where do you see this going from a legal perspective?”

“I found this item even more troubling than the drawing because of what it suggests,” she began. “Now, of course, it appears that it was created by some member, It may be a completely fabricated joke, but, you know, people make jokes based on some grain of truth because that's what makes it funny.”

“So I think it raises a lot of questions,” she continued. “I also think the drawing suggests, you know, the drawing itself is sort of bawdy, but it suggests a close relationship with Trump — if it's authentic — referring to Jeffrey Epstein as his pal and that they have secrets together. So I think it raises a lot of questions as to where it's going.”

“I am very cognizant of protecting the identities and the privacy of the survivors here, that's very important,” she elaborated. “I'm even cognizant of protecting the identities of subjects of investigation for whom there was insufficient evidence to charge. But one thing President Trump could do immediately is to say, in an effort to clear my name once and for all, I hereby direct the Justice Department to disclose every piece of material, whether it's a document, an image, or a video that includes me. Let's put that out there and let's put this scandal to rest.

"He could do that, but he's not, and why is he not doing it? I think it is for fear that there are things like this, or other things that would cause him embarrassment, if not shame, within his base.”

The Epstein-Trump Nexus: A Deep Dive into a Disturbing Photograph

The recent surfacing of a photograph depicting convicted pedophile Jeffrey Epstein with a joke novelty check at Donald Trump's Mar-a-Lago estate has sent shockwaves through the media and public alike. But let's peel back the layers of sensationalism and examine the core issues here, with the precision of a scalpel cutting through the fog of conjecture.

The Photograph: A Snapshot of Depravity?

At first glance, the photograph in question, described as Epstein and a Mar-a-Lago member joking about selling a "fully depreciated" woman to Donald Trump for $22,500, is abhorrent. It's not just the content of the image that's disturbing—it's the casual dehumanization and sexism on display, served up as humor among the elite. This isn't just a bad joke; it's a window into a deeply troubling mindset that views women as commodities.

However, as former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade keenly points out, the photograph's existence and its content may hint at something far more sinister. It's the implication that such "jokes" are rooted in a grain of truth—the kind of truth that's only funny in the darkest corners of human behavior. And when these jests are connected to names like Epstein and Trump, the implications become even more alarming.

Legal Implications and Trump's Silence

McQuade raises a critical point regarding the legal landscape this photograph and its associated behaviors navigate. The photo, coupled with a previously revealed birthday letter from Trump to Epstein, suggests not just a casual acquaintance but a close relationship. This relationship, fraught with bawdy humor about buying and selling women, points to a level of complicity and familiarity that demands scrutiny.

Yet, what's equally troubling is Donald Trump's response—or rather, his lack of one. McQuade's suggestion that Trump could direct the Justice Department to release any and all documents related to him in an effort to clear his name is met with silence. This silence speaks volumes. Why indeed would he not take such a step if it could exonerate him? Is it the fear of what else might be uncovered? The potential for embarrassment or shame within his base seems to be a stronger deterrent than the desire for transparency.

Beyond the Scandal: The Real Victims

McQuade's concern for protecting their identities and privacy is a sobering reminder of the human cost of such scandals. It's easy to get lost in the political and legal implications, but we must remember that at the heart of this are individuals who have suffered immeasurably.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

As the media continues to unravel the tangled web of associations between Trump, Epstein, and their elite circle, the public must demand accountability. Not just in the form of legal repercussions for those involved in criminal activities, but a societal reckoning with the attitudes and behaviors that allow such "jokes" and actions to exist unchallenged.

The photograph at Mar-a-Lago isn't just a piece of evidence; it's a symbol of the systemic rot within circles of power that view women as less than human. And as this scandal unfolds, one can only hope it leads not just to outrage, but to lasting change.
