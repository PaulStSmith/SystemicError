The High Price of Cruelty: How Cutting Medicaid Raises Your Insurance and Kills Competition

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Imagine this: you have private insurance. You pay your premiums. You do everything "right." So why, every damn year, do your costs keep going up? Premiums rise, deductibles skyrocket, and you still end up arguing with some bored insurance rep about whether your procedure is "cosmetic."

Now imagine the answer isn’t some secret cabal or shadowy conspiracy—it’s something much simpler and far more infuriating: Medicaid cuts.

You might be thinking: Why would I care about Medicaid? That’s for poor people. I have Blue Cross. I have Kaiser. I have options.

Except you don’t. Not really. Because when we slash Medicaid, the ripple effects drown everyone. Here’s how:

The Uninsured Still Show Up

Emergency rooms are legally required to treat everyone, regardless of ability to pay. That means when someone without insurance—because we cut their Medicaid—breaks their arm or has a heart attack, the hospital eats the cost. And that cost is not imaginary.

Estimates put unpaid hospital care at tens of billions per year. So, hospitals pass the cost along. They inflate prices for the insured. Your insurer picks up the tab—and promptly jacks up your premiums to compensate. You pay more. For someone else's care. Because we yanked the safety net.

Preventive Care vs. Emergency Chaos

Medicaid doesn’t just pay for broken legs. It funds preventive care: check-ups, diabetes management, mental health support. Take that away, and what do you think happens? People wait. And wait. Until they end up in the ER with advanced, expensive problems.

Preventable illnesses become acute, costly emergencies. And guess who funds those chaotic, resource-draining visits? The same hospitals trying to stay afloat. And you, through your higher premiums.

And that’s not all. Emergencies don’t just cost more in dollars—they cost more in time. A delayed diagnosis often means a more difficult recovery. More time in the hospital. More time off work. That hurts individuals, yes, but it also hammers businesses. Companies are forced to cover for absent workers, bringing on temps, absorbing delays, or even missing deadlines. Those operational costs don’t disappear either—they’re passed on to consumers. Higher prices at the register, because someone had to wait three weeks to get their infection treated.

Hospital Closures: A Hidden Crisis

Here’s the brutal truth: rural and low-income hospitals depend on Medicaid reimbursements to survive. When those funds dry up, they shutter. It’s not just a "budgetary adjustment" — it’s a death sentence for healthcare access.

Your local ER might close. Your nearest trauma center could now be two hours away. And with fewer hospitals, you lose competition. That gives surviving hospitals license to hike prices even higher. Because where else are you going to go?

The Myth of the "Government Handout"

The conservative narrative loves to paint Medicaid as a freebie for the lazy. But the reality is that Medicaid pays for services. Services you rely on. Services you might need when you fall off a ladder or get hit by a car.

And when those services disappear? Everyone suffers. Medicaid isn’t charity. It’s infrastructure. Strip it away, and the whole damn system buckles.

When Ideology Meets Reality

The GOP, ever eager to score points with the "small government" crowd, has spent the last decade gutting Medicaid in red states. The result? Higher costs, more uninsured, hospital deserts, and worse outcomes for everyone. Including their own voters.

It’s performative cruelty masquerading as fiscal discipline.

The states that expanded Medicaid under the ACA saw fewer hospital closures, better preventive care access, and slower insurance cost growth. But try explaining that to someone who thinks helping poor people is a moral failing.

You’re Already Paying for It

Here’s the punchline: you’re already paying for those ER visits. You just do it through hidden markups, inflated premiums, and longer wait times. It’s the least efficient way to fund healthcare, and the most morally bankrupt.

Would you rather pay taxes to fund Medicaid, ensuring your neighbors don’t flood your ER and spike your premiums? Or would you rather pay $300 more a month because Billy in rural Kansas can’t afford insulin?

Because that’s the choice.

Ignorance Isn’t Free

This is what happens when ideology trumps practicality, when cruelty is baked into policy, and when voters are kept ignorant of how systems interlock.

Cutting Medicaid doesn’t make you freer. It makes you broker, sicker, and more vulnerable. And the worst part? You were told it would save you money.

It doesn’t.

It never did.

Maybe if more people had understood this before the 2024 election, we wouldn’t be watching hospitals die while insurers rake in record profits.

But here we are.

Next time someone tells you Medicaid isn’t your problem, tell them this: You’re already paying. You’re just not getting anything for it.
