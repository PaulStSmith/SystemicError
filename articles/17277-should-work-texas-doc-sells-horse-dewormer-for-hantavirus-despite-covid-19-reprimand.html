'Should work': Texas doc sells horse dewormer for hantavirus despite COVID-19 reprimand

By Paulo Santos

Profiting from Panic: The Unchecked Spread of Medical Misinformation

Selling Snake Oil in the Age of Viral Fears

Dr. Mary Talley Bowden, previously reprimanded by the Texas Medical Board, is back in the spotlight for her latest venture: selling ivermectin as a treatment for hantavirus. Despite having no proven efficacy against the virus, Bowden has publicly declared this antiparasitic medication, typically used as a horse dewormer, as a viable treatment based on its supposed antiviral properties. The audacity of her claim is matched only by the steep price—charging up to $110 for 100 pills, a significant markup from their availability on platforms like Amazon for $10.

The Power to Mislead: Exploiting Institutional Vacuum

What should be emphasized here is not just the action of an individual doctor but the environment that allows such dangerous assertions to flourish. The Texas Medical Board’s response to Bowden’s previous misconduct—merely a public reprimand—highlights a grossly insufficient regulatory response to direct threats to public health. By not taking stronger action, institutional authorities inadvertently empower figures like Bowden to exploit public fear for personal gain.

Misdirection and Profit in Medical Crises

Bowden’s strategy cleverly manipulates current health anxieties by presenting ivermectin as a preemptive solution to an emerging threat. This tactic is not just misleading but dangerous, as it encourages the public to trust and consume unverified information. The assertion that “ivermectin should work” against hantavirus, based on her non-peer-reviewed claims, showcases a blatant disregard for scientific consensus and established medical protocols.

The Larger Pattern: Systematic Failure and Opportunistic Exploitation

This scenario is not isolated. The pattern of individual opportunists taking advantage of systemic failures has become a familiar playbook in times of crisis. The real issue here is the lack of a robust and decisive system to prevent and penalize the spread of medical misinformation. Without stringent checks, the cycle of misinformation will continue, fueled by those looking to profit from the desperation and fear of vulnerable populations.

Conclusion: Need for a Stronger Institutional Response

Dr. Mary Talley Bowden’s case should serve as a wake-up call for regulatory bodies and the medical community. It’s imperative to establish and enforce stricter guidelines and consequences for medical professionals who choose to sideline scientific integrity for personal profit. Moreover, this incident should prompt a broader discussion about how medical misinformation can be combated at the institutional level, ensuring that the health of the public is not compromised by the irresponsible actions of a few.
