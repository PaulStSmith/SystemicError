MAGA world 'appalled' as RFK Jr. scorched at hearing: 'Purge the CDC of medical tyrants!'

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., testifies before a Senate Finance Committee hearing on President Donald Trump’s 2026 health care agenda, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 4, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Supporters of President Donald Trump and those who consider themselves part of the MAGA movement melted down Thursday after watching Health and Human Services Secretary Robert Kennedy Jr. be forced to answer tough questions about his leadership of the department in the few short months he's run it.

Kennedy, who had promised never to deny vaccines to anyone who wants them, removed the HHS recommendations for vaccines, making it difficult for anyone under 65 to access the COVID-19 booster without their doctor's intervention. Republicans hammered him on that fact as well as dramatic cuts from rural health care passed in the "big, beautiful bill," deep cuts to Medicaid, and the firings of the top official at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, and the entire vaccine advisory board.

Among the most surprising to go after Kennedy were Republican lawmakers, two of whom are physicians, who caught Kennedy in some inconsistencies.

It prompted a meltdown of MAGA supporters and lawmakers who support Kennedy's agenda.

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt wrote on X that Kennedy is right on "target" and that's why he "is taking flak." She claimed, "The Trump Administration is addressing root causes of chronic disease, embracing transparency in government, and championing gold-standard science. Only the Democrats could attack that commonsense effort."

As one medical doctor told MSNBC, Kennedy believes that vaccines essentially cause chronic illness.

"He believes that vaccination has simply substituted chronic diseases for infectious diseases. So, therefore, his war on vaccines, he believes, is a war against chronic diseases," said Dr. Paul Offit, director of the Vaccine Education Center at the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia.

Longtime Trump ally Steve Cortes responded to a video of Kennedy attacking the Academy of Pediatrics for taking donations from pharmaceutical companies. The implication is that anyone who takes money from someone is aligned with them.

"The American Academy of Pediatrics is a corrupt menace…and does untold damage to the health of America’s children. MAHA," claimed Cortes.

Kennedy's presidential campaign took donations from anti-vaccine groups and activists. His anti-vaccine advocacy group also paid him a six-figure salary, STAT News reported. As the Daily Beast reported in January, Kennedy made his fortune from it, and he stands to profit from lawsuits against vaccine companies, ABC News said.

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) demanded, "Purge the CDC of the medical tyrants that are responsible for making America sick and Doctor Frankenstein 'science' that has killed people."

JD Vance complained on X, "When I see all these senators trying to lecture and 'gotcha' Bobby Kennedy today all I can think is: You all support off-label, untested, and irreversible hormonal 'therapies' for children, mutilating our kids and enriching big pharma. You're full of s--- and everyone knows it."

Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) chimed in, "I find it appalling, the way RFK Jr has been treated by our Senators. The ivory tower career politicians are out of touch with America and their arrogance is disgusting. Bottom line is that they’re shilling for big pharma and our children are being generationally injured. Some of us are willing to fight for our kids, and I stand with RFK Jr against government mandated experimental COVID vaccines for babies. Good Lord, this shouldn’t even be a fight."

Scandal-plagued former Rep. Madison Cawthorn (R-NC) said, "Lots of Dems saying, 'RFK JR mUsT rEsIgN!!!' (sic) How about no. We voted to Make America Healthy Again!"

During the hearing, Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), a self-certified ophthalmologist, told the press he thinks there's a lot of doubt around vaccines thanks to "the establishment."

"If the establishment keeps telling me things that are incorrect, that's where doubt comes from," he claimed.

Conservative anti-government podcaster Kevin Smith commented, "Bill 'Pfizer pays my bills' Cassidy really showing what he finds important here. Not that as a senator and a doctor we’ve never been more sick… but let’s go back to where his money gets made… COVID."

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Meltdown: A Critical Analysis

In a spectacle that can only be described as theater of the absurd, the recent uproar surrounding Health and Human Services Secretary Robert Kennedy Jr.'s handling of vaccine recommendations and healthcare policies has laid bare the MAGA movement's fraught relationship with science, healthcare, and reality itself. The article in question presents a chaotic tableau of reactions from MAGA supporters and Republican lawmakers, all centered around the controversial figure of Kennedy. Let's dissect the layers of misinformation, irony, and outright hypocrisy that pepper this narrative.

Kennedy's Controversial Moves

First, the critical fact to note is Kennedy's decision to remove HHS recommendations for vaccines for those under 65, effectively making it a Herculean task to access COVID-19 boosters without a doctor's explicit intervention. This move is, at best, a baffling step backward in public health policy, and at worst, a dangerous play into anti-vaccine narratives that have no basis in scientific fact. It's ironic, then, that Kennedy's actions have caused an uproar among MAGA supporters, given their historical skepticism towards vaccine mandates and public health measures.

Republican Infighting: A Symphony of Irony

The article highlights a particularly delicious irony: Republican lawmakers, some of whom are physicians, have caught Kennedy in inconsistencies, leading to a "meltdown" among MAGA supporters. This infighting is a spectacle of contradictions, considering the MAGA movement's previous endorsements of anti-science and anti-vaccine rhetoric. When White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt defends Kennedy's actions as "addressing root causes of chronic disease" and "championing gold-standard science," one can't help but marvel at the audacity of such claims. This is the same administration that has, time and again, flouted scientific consensus for political gain.

The Warped War on Vaccines

Dr. Paul Offit's comments cut to the heart of the matter, exposing Kennedy's fundamentally flawed belief that vaccinations are a cause of chronic illness rather than a safeguard against infectious diseases. This misguided war on vaccines is not only scientifically baseless but dangerously misleading, particularly during an ongoing global pandemic. Steve Cortes' attack on the American Academy of Pediatrics further underscores the MAGA movement's penchant for conspiracy theories over concrete evidence.

Financial Motivations: The Unspoken Truth

Perhaps the most egregious revelation is the financial incentive behind Kennedy's anti-vaccine stance. With reports of his campaign taking donations from anti-vaccine groups and his advocacy group paying him a six-figure salary, one must question the integrity of his positions. The notion that Kennedy stands to profit from lawsuits against vaccine companies adds a layer of self-interest that cannot be ignored.

The Hollow Rhetoric of Health

The commentary from MAGA-aligned figures like Marjorie Taylor Greene, JD Vance, and Clay Higgins reveals a deep-seated hypocrisy. Their rhetoric, filled with terms like "medical tyrants" and "government mandated experimental COVID vaccines," ignores the empirical success of vaccines and the real medical tyranny of preventable diseases. Moreover, their attacks on "big pharma" ring hollow when juxtaposed with their own party's historical financial entanglements with the pharmaceutical industry.

Conclusion: The MAGA Paradox

In summary, the MAGA movement's meltdown over Kennedy's scrutiny exposes a paradox at the heart of their ideology. On one hand, they champion anti-science narratives that undermine public health. On the other, they decry the consequences of policies born from those very narratives when they manifest in reality. It's a self-defeating cycle, emblematic of a movement more concerned with political victories than the well-being of the American people. As this saga unfolds, one thing remains clear: the MAGA movement's flirtation with anti-vaccine rhetoric is not just a systemic error—it's a public health hazard.
