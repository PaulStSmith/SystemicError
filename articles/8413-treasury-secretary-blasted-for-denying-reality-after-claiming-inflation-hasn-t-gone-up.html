Treasury secretary blasted for 'denying reality' after claiming 'inflation hasn't gone up'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 25, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Denial of Inflation RealityThe Bizarre AdviceThe Echoes of Past MistakesConclusion

Image via Screengrab.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Sunday refused to admit inflation has gone up for Americans after NBC “Meet The Press” host Kristen Welker confronted him with the numbers.

“Inflation has gone up,” Welker said Sunday. “It’s at 3 percent now up from 2 percent in April when the tariffs were imposed.”

“No, no no no,” Bessent replied. “So, inflation hasn’t gone up. The one thing we’re not gonna do is do what the Biden administration did and tell the American people they don’t know how they feel. They are traumatized.”

Bessent’s remark sparked outrage from observers who noted President Donald Trump’s administration is doing the same thing it accused its predecessor of doing — telling consumers not to believe their own pocketbooks.

As policy analyst Evaristus Odinikaeze posted on X, “the inflation went from 2 percent to 3 percent, literally and no amount of ‘no, no, no’ changes basic math.”

“Telling Americans inflation hasn’t risen right after tariffs pushed prices higher is the same gaslighting they accused others of,” Odinikaeze continued. “You don’t fight economic anxiety by denying lived reality. You solve it. But instead, Trump’s making it worse and lying about it.”

Bulwark Deputy Digital Director Evan Rosenfeld likewise argued “Trump and Republicans have learned nothing from how badly Joe Biden and the Democrats bungled inflation.”

“Instead they’re repeating some of the same mistakes,” Rosenfeld wrote on X.

Bessent also drew condemnation after offering advice for Americans feeling the pain from Trump’s economic policies.

“You know the best way to bring your inflation rate down? Move from a blue state to a red state. Blue state inflation is half a percent higher,” Bessent told Welker.

“Scott Bessent cannot stop staying really stupid things,” journalist John Harwood said of Bessent’s suggestion.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Denial of Inflation Reality

In a recent exchange on NBC’s “Meet The Press,” Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s outright refusal to acknowledge the rise in inflation rates post-tariff imposition is not just a denial of factual data, but a glaring exhibit of the current administration’s approach to economic policy and public communication. Kristen Welker’s presentation of inflation figures – a rise from 2 percent to 3 percent since April – was met with a barrage of denials from Bessent, a tactic that reeks of the very gaslighting the Trump administration has previously accused its predecessors of engaging in.

“No, no no no,” Bessent replied, as if the mere repetition of a denial could somehow alter the reality of numbers. This isn’t just an evasion; it’s a deliberate attempt to muddy the waters of public discourse about the economy. Evaristus Odinikaeze’s critique on X succinctly captures the absurdity of Bessent’s stance: “the inflation went from 2 percent to 3 percent, literally and no amount of ‘no, no, no’ changes basic math.” This isn’t rocket science; it’s elementary-level arithmetic.

The irony of Bessent’s statement — “The one thing we’re not gonna do is do what the Biden administration did and tell the American people they don’t know how they feel. They are traumatized.” — is thick enough to cut with a knife. By outright denying an objective truth, Bessent and, by extension, the Trump administration, are not just telling Americans how to feel about their economic situation but are negating their lived experiences of financial strain.

The Bizarre Advice

In a stunning display of being out of touch, Bessent’s advice for Americans struggling with inflation — “Move from a blue state to a red state. Blue state inflation is half a percent higher,” — is as impractical as it is insensible. It’s a classic case of offering a band-aid solution to a gunshot wound, showing a fundamental misunderstanding (or perhaps a willful ignorance) of the economic challenges facing everyday Americans. John Harwood’s remark, “Scott Bessent cannot stop saying really stupid things,” might seem harsh at first glance, but it’s a fair assessment of a suggestion so detached from reality.

The Echoes of Past Mistakes

Evan Rosenfeld’s observation on X — “Trump and Republicans have learned nothing from how badly Joe Biden and the Democrats bungled inflation.” — serves as a critical reminder that the cycle of mismanagement and miscommunication around economic issues is not confined to a single administration or party. However, the refusal to learn from past errors and the decision to double down on denial and gaslighting only exacerbates the problem, leaving Americans trapped in a cycle of economic anxiety and political frustration.

Conclusion

The conversation between Bessent and Welker is more than just a contentious TV moment; it’s a microcosm of the larger issues plaguing American political and economic discourse. When faced with inconvenient truths, the current administration’s strategy appears to be denial and diversion, rather than acknowledgment and action. This not only undermines public trust but also hampers any genuine efforts to address the economic challenges facing the nation. In the end, it’s the American people who pay the price for such willful ignorance.
