'His days are numbered': Trump official blasted after 'publicly humiliating the president'

Politico reported Monday that Bill Pulte — who is the director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency and the chairman of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — is behind Trump’s post about 50-year mortgages that led to swift backlash from the mortgage industry. White House officials are reportedly “furious” with Pulte over the stunt, and various MAGA figures like Laura Loomer and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) have publicly come out against the idea.

According to the outlet, Pulte approached Trump at Mar-a-Lago last weekend with a 3x5 piece of posterboard that read “Great American Presidents,” and showed a picture of Franklin Delano Roosevelt with the text “30-year mortgage” on the left, and a picture of Trump with the text “50-year mortgage” on the right. Trump then posted the image to his Truth Social account, which is still live as of this writing.

“Thanks to President Trump, we are indeed working on The 50 year Mortgage - a complete game changer,” Pulte tweeted on Saturday.

Gennadiy Goldberg, who is the head of U.S. rates strategy at TD Securities, criticized 50-year mortgages as “a stopgap bandaid to address affordability,” and said it would also be a disadvantage to homeowners as equity would be built at a far slower rate. Typically, the earliest years of a mortgage consist of paying interest, rather than building equity.

Politico further reported that there was more “fallout” over the 50-year mortgage proposal than essentially any other policy of Trump’s second term. One of the outlet’s confidential sources familiar with the Mar-a-Lago incident told Politico that Pulte may have permanently soured his reputation with Trump due to the response his 50-year mortgage idea got from both the industry and from MAGA.

“The thing that became clear from this latest episode — if it wasn’t already clear — is that Bill Pulte doesn’t know the first f—— thing about how the mortgage markets operate,” the source said. “After publicly humiliating the president with his moronic 50 year mortgage plan it’s safe to assume that his days are numbered.”

Pulte later appeared to backtrack, saying that 50-year mortgages were merely part of “a WIDE arsenal of solutions” Trump was proposing to address the affordability crisis. Other ideas reportedly included portable mortgages (which can switch to a different property mid-term) as well as assumable mortgages (which can be transferred to a new buyer once a property is sold).

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Mortgage Melodrama: A Dissection

In a recent political spectacle that could only emerge from the tumultuous theatre of Trump’s administration, we’ve witnessed a policy proposal that veers off the path of rationality straight into the wilderness of political farce. The protagonist of our story, Bill Pulte — the director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency and chairman of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — has found himself in hot water after pitching the idea of a 50-year mortgage, a concept that has sparked both ire and incredulity across the political and financial landscape.

The Pitch That Backfired

Pulte’s approach to Trump, armed with nothing more than a 3x5 piece of posterboard comparing Trump to Franklin Delano Roosevelt, is emblematic of a broader issue within the administration: a penchant for grandiose gestures over substantive policy understanding. The poster, adorned with the promise of a 50-year mortgage, is less a thoughtful policy proposal and more a crude attempt at legacy-building, positioning Trump as a transformative figure in the American housing market.

MAGA’s Fractured Front

The backlash from within Trump’s own camp is particularly telling. Figures like Laura Loomer and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) have publicly denounced the proposal, illustrating the MAGA movement’s internal divisions and its struggle to coalesce around coherent policy positions. This dissent from the ranks underscores a fundamental disconnect between Trump’s populist rhetoric and the pragmatic considerations of governance.

The Industry’s Ire

The mortgage industry’s swift condemnation of the 50-year mortgage idea as a “stopgap bandaid” to housing affordability challenges speaks volumes. Gennadiy Goldberg’s critique highlights a crucial flaw in Pulte’s proposal: the trade-off between extending mortgage terms and the rate at which homeowners can build equity. This criticism cuts to the heart of the matter, revealing the proposal not as a solution but as a potential exacerbation of the very problem it seeks to address.

The Fallout

Politico’s reporting on the “fallout” from the proposal underscores a fundamental truth about the Trump administration: it often prioritizes spectacle over substance. The unnamed source’s scathing review of Pulte’s understanding of mortgage markets—and the subsequent public humiliation faced by Trump—paints a vivid picture of an administration in disarray, propelled by personalities rather than policies.

A Wider Arsenal or a Wider Problem?

Pulte’s attempt to backtrack, framing the 50-year mortgage as merely one tool among many, does little to salvage his credibility. The mention of portable and assumable mortgages as part of a “WIDE arsenal of solutions” comes off as a hasty addition to a poorly thought-out plan, further muddying the waters of an already convoluted policy space.

In Conclusion

The 50-year mortgage fiasco is not just a story about a policy pitch gone awry; it’s a microcosm of the larger systemic issues plaguing Trump’s presidency. It reflects an administration often led astray by the whims of its leaders and their desire for legacy, at the expense of practical and effective governance. As Pulte’s star seems to set within Trump’s orbit, one can only hope that future policy proposals are approached with a greater degree of thoughtfulness and a deeper understanding of the issues at hand.
