Trump just threatened to start another war

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosOctober 25, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

AlterNet reports that President Donald Trump told a Friday rally in Fort Myers, Florida, that the United States would “take Cuba on our way back from Iran.” He described American ships stopping off Cuba and Secretary of State Marco Rubio “finish[ing] up the thing.” The remark invoked military force but, as reported, was not accompanied by a formal policy announcement or operational plan.

The comments came amid an oil blockade that AlterNet says has caused widespread harm in Cuba and an ongoing U.S.-Iran war associated with higher fuel prices. Trump claimed oil prices were falling substantially; the Wall Street Journal reporting cited by AlterNet said they had instead reached successive five-month highs as markets feared further escalation and attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure.

The power is in Washington

Cuba does not control the blockade, the American fleet or the prospect of military escalation. The Trump administration does. Trump commands the armed forces; Rubio runs the State Department and, according to a longtime acquaintance quoted by the Guardian, has wanted greater authority to pursue Cuba for years.

That distribution of power matters. Trump was not describing a dispute between comparable actors. He was publicly imagining how the world’s most heavily equipped military might force the submission of a neighboring country already under severe economic pressure.

A threat disguised as banter

The rally performance makes coercion sound effortless: ships pass by, one stops offshore, Cuban officials surrender, and Rubio closes the deal. That is not a serious account of warfare. It is domination repackaged as an amusing detour.

The unserious delivery does not erase the institutional authority behind it. But the reporting establishes rhetoric, not an adopted invasion plan. The responsible conclusion is that Trump publicly normalized the prospect of attacking Cuba while leaving the policy status unclear—not that an operation was definitively ordered.

Rubio is an actor, not a prop

Trump cast Rubio as the official who would “finish up” Cuba. The Guardian quotation supplied by AlterNet portrays Cuba as a longstanding Rubio priority and says he finally possesses authority to pursue it. That makes Rubio part of the administration’s power structure, not merely the ethnic symbol Trump’s performance made of him.

Still, the quoted acquaintance’s characterization is evidence of perceived ambition, not proof that Rubio designed a military plan. The established fact is narrower: he holds substantial institutional power inside an administration already imposing material pressure on Cuba, and Trump publicly assigned him a role in the fantasy of capitulation.

War abroad, denial at home

Trump also claimed oil prices were dropping while the cited market reporting showed them rising. The political maneuver is plain even without inferring motive: celebrate military supremacy, promise another easy victory and contradict the economic evidence confronting voters.

The costs disappear from that script. Cuban civilians become leverage. American consumers encounter higher fuel prices. Regional infrastructure faces further attacks. Yet power is presented as spectacle while foreseeable consequences are treated as irrelevant—or simply denied.

The imperial shortcut

The larger pattern is government by coercive fantasy: economic punishment softens a target, military force is advertised as quick and clean, and surrender is assumed before anyone accounts for the human or economic damage.

That framing protects the officials making the decisions. It converts deliberate exercises of state power into casual improvisation and shifts attention from blockade, escalation and price shocks to Trump’s performance style. The systemic error is not merely that a president speaks recklessly. It is that enormous coercive authority can be discussed as a joke while the people exposed to its consequences are written out of the scene.
