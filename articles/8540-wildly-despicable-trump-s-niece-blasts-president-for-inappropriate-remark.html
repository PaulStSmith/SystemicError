'Wildly despicable': Trump's niece blasts president for 'inappropriate' remark

Donald Trump’s niece, Mary Trump, has hit out at the president for a “wildly inappropriate and despicable” remark.

While speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One, the president interrupted a Bloomberg News journalist who was asking a follow-up question on the Jeffrey Epstein files. He told the journalist, “Quiet. Quiet, piggy.” The remark has horrified members of the public, and Mary Trump, the niece of the president, has said these comments are “par for the course”.

In a video posted to X, Mary Trump said, “Donald is a lifelong, hardcore misogynist, so he doesn’t really like it when women ask him difficult questions.” Mary Trump then shared the clip of Trump saying “quiet, piggy” to the Bloomberg News reporter. She added after the clip, “Now obviously that was wildly inappropriate and despicable, but it’s also par for the course.”

“I’d say that the only pig in that scenario is Donald. But actual pigs are intelligent and affectionate creatures, while Donald is just a vile, corrupt criminal who, also, unlike pigs, has absolutely nothing of value to contribute to the world.”

The White House has flatly refused to condemn Trump for the comment made on Air Force One, instead suggesting reporter Catherine Lucey was to blame for the comment. Lucey had asked the president why he was being defensive over the Epstein files question “if there’s nothing incriminating in the files.”

A White House official speaking to The Guardian said, “This reporter behaved in an inappropriate and unprofessional way towards her colleagues on the plane. If you’re going to give it, you have to be able to take it.”

David Urban, a GOP strategist, was asked about a comment Trump made to a reporter on Air Force One on Monday during an interview on CNN’s “Anderson Cooper 360.”

“There’s no excuse for that, right? No excuse for calling anybody piggy,” Urban said on Tuesday. “It’s my understanding that one reporter interrupted another reporter. The president got angry with that. And it was two female reporters. But again, no excuse for calling anybody those names. It’s just it’s kind of silly and distracts from so many things.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump’s Despicable Comment and the White House’s Transparent Defense

In a recent display of the depths to which political discourse in the United States has sunk, Donald Trump has once again showcased his unparalleled ability to demean, degrade, and disrespect with a mere utterance. While aboard Air Force One, Trump interrupted Bloomberg News journalist Catherine Lucey, who was merely performing her duty by asking a follow-up question on the Jeffrey Epstein files, with a remark that can only be described as “wildly inappropriate and despicable.” He told Lucey, “Quiet. Quiet, piggy.” This incident not only horrifies due to its blatant misogyny but also reveals the systemic rot within Trump’s administration that allows, and indeed, defends such behavior.

Mary Trump, the president’s niece and a vocal critic, has succinctly put the incident into context, stating that these comments are “par for the course.” In her sharp condemnation, Mary Trump called out the president for being a “lifelong, hardcore misogynist” who cannot tolerate tough questioning from women. Her assessment is not just an opinion; it’s a well-documented pattern of behavior that Trump has exhibited throughout his presidency and before. Yet, what’s equally troubling is the White House’s response—or rather, the lack thereof—to Trump’s reprehensible comments.

The White House’s refusal to condemn Trump’s words, instead suggesting that Catherine Lucey was to blame for the comment, is a textbook example of victim-blaming. A White House official, in a statement to The Guardian, accused Lucey of behaving “in an inappropriate and unprofessional way towards her colleagues on the plane,” thus suggesting that she somehow deserved the president’s insult. This defense is not only laughable but a dangerous precedent that further entrenches the normalization of misogyny within the highest echelons of power.

David Urban, a GOP strategist, provided a rare moment of sanity in stating on CNN’s “Anderson Cooper 360” that there’s “no excuse for calling anybody piggy,” acknowledging the inappropriateness of Trump’s remarks. However, even this condemnation falls short. Calling the incident “silly and [a distraction] from so many things” underplays the severity of the president’s misconduct and the chilling effect it has on free press and the respectful discourse expected in democratic societies.

The Larger Implication

What this incident and the subsequent reactions highlight is a systemic issue within the current administration: a culture of misogyny and disrespect, coupled with an unwillingness to hold the president accountable for his actions. The defense mounted by the White House and the tepid rebukes from some within the GOP not only enable Trump’s behavior but signal to the public that such conduct is acceptable, or at least, not worthy of outright condemnation.

Mary Trump’s remarks that her uncle is “just a vile, corrupt criminal who, also, unlike pigs, has absolutely nothing of value to contribute to the world” might seem harsh to some. Still, in the context of the ongoing degradation of political and social norms, they serve as a necessary wake-up call. Actual pigs, as she pointed out, are “intelligent and affectionate creatures,” unlike the derogatory caricature her uncle attempted to paint of a professional woman doing her job.

Conclusion

This incident is not an isolated one, nor will it likely be the last of its kind. It’s a symptom of a broader, more troubling trend of misogyny, disrespect, and unaccountability. It’s high time that the public, and more members of Trump’s own party, demand better from their president and hold him accountable for his words and actions. Until then, we’re left to navigate the murky waters of a society where the leader’s word is law, and respect is a currency he’s unwilling to trade in.
