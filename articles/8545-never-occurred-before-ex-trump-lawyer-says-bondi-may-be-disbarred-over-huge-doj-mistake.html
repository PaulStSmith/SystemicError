'Never occurred before': Ex-Trump lawyer says Bondi may be disbarred over huge DOJ mistake

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi speaks during the announcement of a law enforcement action during a press conference at the Department of Justice in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 19, 2025. REUTERS/Tom Brenner

An ex-Trump attorney said that U.S. Attorney Pam Bondi could be disbarred over potential misconduct in the case against James Comey, former FBI director.

The case was against Comey was never shown to or voted on by a full grand jury before it was presented in open court — which could lead to Bondi and Lindsay Halligan, interim U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia — to be disbarred, Ty Cobb, former White House attorney during President Donald Trump’s first administration, told MSNBC anchor Chris Jansing on Wednesday.

Comey’s defense team argued that development should prevent further prosecution in the case, saying “there is no indictment,” and Judge Michael Nachmanoff gave the Department of Justice until 5 p.m. to respond to the revelations.

“Because typically, if an indictment is dismissed, the government has six months to represent, notwithstanding the expiration of the statute of limitations,” Cobb said. “This is an indictment, though, that doesn’t really have to be dismissed. It doesn’t really exist. It was never properly returned. So I think this is I think what we heard today, shocking never, never occurred before in American jurisprudence. I think it was will be dispositive. But on the other hand, there’s so many dispositive issues here, including her illegal appointment.”

Cobb called the move surprising and pointed to Halligan’s “illegal appointment.”

“It’s shocking you couldn’t find a high school stock boy at Home Depot who could have handled this more ineptly than Lindsey Halligan did,” Cobb said. You know, taking an indictment that the grand jury never saw, having the foreman sign it and then presenting it to a judge? That’s the height of ineptitude and misconduct.”

Cobb also noted Bondi had backed up Halligan’s documents in court, which could lead to Bondi herself being disbarred.

Jansing also asked Cobb about the Epstein files and what could happen next as the legislation heads to his desk.

“ Trump has no intention of releasing any documents. He and Bondi will scheme and prevent the release, in my view. I don’t think we’ll see any meaningful or consequential documents come out after this based on Trump’s order to prosecute Democrats, not Republicans.

Bondi could use a legal loophole to try and argue the information is still tied to litigation. Trump, he argued, has no interest in releasing the files and only made his move when Republicans said they would push for the files to be released.

“It’s not sincere or genuine. He could have released the documents himself without the legislation,” Cobb said.

But Trump wants to “be on the winning side,” and that’s why he reversed his stance.

“Contrary to Bondi’s lies this morning, there is no new information. The government has all this information,” Cobb said.

Bondi has been on record saying no more new cases could be brought against anyone now.

“Whatever’s happening now is just a fraud,” he added.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Comey Case Fiasco: A Masterclass in Legal Ineptitude

In a stunning revelation that reads more like a script from a legal drama gone awry than real-life jurisprudence, Ty Cobb, a former attorney for President Donald Trump, has laid bare a series of potential legal malpractices that could lead to the disbarment of U.S. Attorney Pam Bondi and Lindsay Halligan. Their handling of the case against James Comey, the former FBI Director, has been described in terms that are nothing short of a judicial circus, underscoring a disturbing deviation from legal norms and ethics.

The Grand Jury Debacle

At the heart of this debacle is the claim that the case against Comey was presented in open court without a full grand jury’s review or vote—a fundamental procedural step that seems to have been casually sidestepped. This glaring oversight, or rather, gross misconduct, raises serious questions about the competence and integrity of the attorneys involved. As Cobb points out, this is an indictment that “doesn’t really exist.” It’s a phantom of legal process, purportedly conjured into existence without the foundational mechanism of grand jury endorsement. This isn’t just a minor procedural hiccup; it’s a blatant affront to the principles of justice.

The Illegal Appointment

Cobb doesn’t pull punches when he lambasts Halligan’s handling of the indictment as “the height of ineptitude and misconduct.” But perhaps even more damning is his assertion regarding Halligan’s “illegal appointment.” This accusation adds another layer of controversy and potential illegality to the proceedings, suggesting a deep-seated disregard for legal protocols and ethical standards at play. The imagery Cobb employs, comparing the legal mismanagement to a scenario a “high school stock boy at Home Depot” could outperform, is both biting and embarrassingly apt.

Bondi’s Complicity

The potential for Pam Bondi to face disbarment stems not only from her support of Halligan’s dubious actions but also from her own role in backing up questionable documents in court. The intertwining of their actions paints a picture of a legal team marching in lockstep toward professional disgrace, seemingly unconcerned with the ethical boundaries they trample along the way.

The Epstein Files Entanglement

Adding another layer to this sordid tale is the discussion surrounding the Epstein files. Cobb’s insights suggest a deliberate obstructionist tactic by Trump and Bondi, aimed at preventing the release of potentially incriminating documents. Trump’s apparent disinterest in transparency, unless politically expedient, underscores a cynical manipulation of legal processes for personal and political gain. Cobb’s assertion that Trump’s reversal on the document release was not out of any sincere intention but rather a strategic maneuver to “be on the winning side” is a stark reminder of the politicization of justice.

A Fraught Legal Landscape

What emerges from Cobb’s revelations is a portrait of a legal landscape fraught with ethical landmines and procedural irregularities. The actions—or inactions—of Bondi and Halligan as described by Cobb are not merely errors in judgment; they are indicative of a systemic erosion of legal norms and accountability. The potential disbarment of these figures, while severe, is portrayed as a necessary response to safeguard the integrity of the legal profession and the principles of justice it is supposed to uphold.

In the final analysis, this saga is not just about the mishandling of a case against James Comey. It’s a glaring symptom of a deeper malaise affecting certain quarters of the American legal system—a malaise characterized by a cavalier disregard for legal standards and ethical conduct. As the dust settles, one can only hope that this episode serves as a cautionary tale and a catalyst for a rigorous reexamination of legal ethics and accountability mechanisms. However, given the track record, one might be forgiven for tempering their optimism with a healthy dose of skepticism.
