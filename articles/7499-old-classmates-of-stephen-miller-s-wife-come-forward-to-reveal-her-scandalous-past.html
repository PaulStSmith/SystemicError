Old classmates of Stephen Miller's wife come forward to reveal her scandalous past

Katie Miller, the wife of President Donald Trump’s notoriously nativist speechwriter and policy adviser Stephen Miller, has for months been running a podcast widely panned as a dull and uninteresting attempt to whitewash her husband’s image and portray a softer side of Trump administration figures — a role she appears to relish as she plays up her own role as a wife to a powerful and influential figure.

But according to an extensive new profile in Slate, Katie Miller, née Waldman, used to be a much more driven person with her own political ambitions, and was active in student government at the University of Florida — until it was derailed in a peculiar scandal.

“Back then, Florida’s student government had a reputation of operating a little like an autocracy,” wrote Tess Owen. “The governing party — which has changed its name over the years but was known as the Unite Party at the time Miller joined — was ruled by the iron fists of privileged frat boys and sorority girls, who controlled the school’s spending and were tapped for access to the exclusive Florida Blue Key society. After graduation, they were funneled into elected office and law clerkships.” The Unite Party was also notorious for strongarming Black and Hispanic affinity groups, cutting off their funding when they didn’t back their campaigns.

She “quickly established herself as a power player in this world and operated like a ‘henchman’ for the ruling party, said one former student,” according to the report — showing none of the typical genial attitude that members of opposing parties in student government usually gave each other. An opposition party senator, Jordan Ball, said, “Most people in student government would be cordial and nice and would try to work together. I feel like it was Katie’s whole life. She took it all very personally.”

“In 2012, her sophomore year, while serving on the rules and ethics committee of student government, Miller was embroiled in a scandal,” said the report. “The head football coach had endorsed a candidate from the opposition Students Party, and the Independent Florida Alligator, the college paper, ran a front-page story publicizing it. Two people — later revealed to be Miller and former Student Government President Pro Tempore Jason Tiemeier — were spotted dumping 268 copies of the newspaper on the eve of student elections.”

This incident led to a frenzy of finger-pointing and attacks, with numerous editorials demanding Miller be removed from her committee. Ultimately, despite going on offense against the newspaper, the Unite Party didn’t invite her to run for office again — and in her farewell address, she laid on the grievances.

“She was bitter about not being asked to run again and felt betrayed. She delivered a glowering, chaotic speech that was full of tacit threats,” wrote Owen. One opposition senator, Ford Dwyer, said, “The sense that everyone got was that they’d better all be careful or else, because she had information on them, and she better be respected because she had secrets that she might or might not reveal ... I think she felt like a victim.”

Our Analysis:

Katie Miller: From Student Government Strongarm to Podcast Propagandist

Katie Miller, née Waldman, has transitioned from a power player in the University of Florida’s student government to running a podcast in an attempt to rehabilitate the reputations of Trump administration figures, including her husband, Stephen Miller, a man synonymous with some of the administration’s most controversial policies. This journey from college politics to attempting to soften the image of one of the most divisive administrations in recent history is both fascinating and deeply troubling.

The Irony of Autocracy in Student Government

The Slate profile of Katie Miller paints a vivid picture of a student government at the University of Florida that operated more like an autocracy than a democratic body, dominated by the Unite Party. Privileged frat boys and sorority girls controlled the school’s purse strings, dictating who received funding and who didn’t, particularly targeting Black and Hispanic affinity groups unless they fell in line. This is not democracy; it’s political strongarming at its infancy, a precursor to the tactics Miller would later stand behind in the Trump administration.

A Henchman, Not a Herald

Miller’s role as a henchman for the Unite Party, as described by a former student, is particularly disturbing. Politics, even at the collegiate level, should be about advocacy, representation, and compromise, not coercion and threats. The fact that she was involved in a scandal for dumping copies of the Independent Florida Alligator to suppress a story unfavorable to her party is antithetical to the very essence of free speech and press. This action alone should have been a significant red flag about her respect for democratic norms and ethics.

The Art of Playing the Victim

Fast forward to her post-college career, and we find Katie Miller attempting to use her podcast to cast a softer light on figures from an administration known for its hardline stances on immigration, among other issues. The irony here is rich. After actively participating in and benefiting from a system that bullied and silenced opposition, she now seeks to rewrite the narrative, portraying these same figures as misunderstood or unfairly vilified.

Her farewell address, filled with grievances and veiled threats, is particularly telling. It speaks to a victim mentality that is all too common among those who wield their power without regard for the consequences, only to cry foul when the tables are turned. It’s a tactic we’ve seen time and again from officials within the Trump administration: engage in questionable, if not outright unethical or illegal behavior, and then claim to be the victim when called out.

Conclusion

Katie Miller’s journey from a college student embroiled in scandal to the wife of one of Trump’s most notorious advisers, now trying to sanitize the administration’s image, is a stark reminder of the persistent threat to democratic norms and values. It’s not just the high-profile names we need to watch; it’s the Katie Millers of the world, too, those who work behind the scenes, willing to do the dirty work, all while claiming to be the real victims when their tactics are brought to light.

The transparency and accountability we demand from our leaders must also be demanded from their enablers and propagandists. Otherwise, we risk normalizing a political culture where the ends always justify the means, democracy be damned.
