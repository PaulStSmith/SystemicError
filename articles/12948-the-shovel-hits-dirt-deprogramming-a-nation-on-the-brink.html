The Shovel Hits Dirt: Deprogramming a Nation on the Brink

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In 1945, General Dwight D. Eisenhower, Supreme Commander of the Allied Expeditionary Forces, walked through the gates of Ohrdruf, a sub-camp of Buchenwald. The stench of death was thick. Corpses lay in piles. Survivors—skeletal, barely alive—looked on. Eisenhower didn’t just recoil. He didn’t just weep. He acted. He ordered the documentation of everything. “Get it all on record now,” he said, because, even in that moment, he knew: someone, someday, will try to deny this ever happened.

And he was right. Holocaust denial metastasized like a tumor in the decades that followed. “Revisionists” crawled out of the sewers with their cherry-picked statistics, faux-scholarly pamphlets, and bold-faced lies. But Eisenhower had beaten them to the punch. The footage. The testimonies. The blunt, indisputable horror. All preserved.

We had the evidence.

But here we are, seventy-nine years later, and truth is not enough. We’re staring into the abyss again—and this time, there are no camps. Not yet. There are no gas chambers. Not yet. No mass graves. Not yet. Instead, we have something more insidious. More deniable. More democratic in its cruelty.

We have slow-motion atrocity.

Children in cages. Families drowned in floods that could’ve been prevented. Pregnant women denied life-saving medical care by politicians hiding behind theological smokescreens. Police kneeling on necks for nine minutes while onlookers scream. COVID mismanagement killing hundreds of thousands. All of it engineered by policy, reinforced by propaganda, and consumed with a shrug.

The Banality of Death by Spreadsheet

There will be no dramatic liberation of camps this time. No single “aha!” moment where the whole world gasps in unison. The machinery is too dispersed. The cruelty, too normalized. Death by bureaucracy. Injustice by algorithm. Evil in a three-piece suit clicking “approve” on a budget cut that guts FEMA.

We can’t drag the deniers into a camp and make them look at piles of corpses. The new corpses are underwater, or overdosed, or uninsured, or simply disappeared into the statistical haze of “excess deaths.”

And unlike post-war Germany, there will be no forced reckoning. Because the very people who should be reckoning are celebrating instead.

We live in a time when entire media ecosystems are built to undo reality. To unsee. To forget. We call it misinformation, but let’s name it more precisely: weaponized ignorance. And it is winning.

“They’re Not Nazis, They’re Patriots”

Every era has its apologists, but we’re currently drowning in them. People who will bend the English language into knots to avoid saying what’s obvious: that cruelty is the point. That racism is a feature, not a bug. That the gutting of social programs, the demonization of immigrants, the assault on bodily autonomy—these aren’t byproducts of bad governance. They are the ideology.

Trump didn’t invent this. He just lifted the veil. Like a con man selling arsenic in a bottle labeled ‘freedom,’ he gave people permission to be who they always were under the surface. Not all of them. But enough.

And here lies the existential problem: How do you deprogram half a country?

You can’t fact-check fascism. You can’t debunk someone out of a death cult. The idea that reason and evidence will win this fight is a myth—a comforting lie we tell ourselves because the alternative is too frightening: that we’re past persuasion.

There Are No Neutral Bystanders

In 1945, many Germans claimed ignorance. “We didn’t know,” they said. But they did know. They just didn’t care. Or they were afraid. Or they looked away. It’s no different now.

You can’t be neutral on a moving train, and this train is picking up speed. You either resist or you enable. You either organize or you normalize. There is no middle.

We need to stop asking, “How could they believe this nonsense?” and start asking, “What are we doing to stop it?”

It starts with truth. Relentless, unflinching, uncomfortable truth. Not the sanitized version fit for prime time. The real thing. The bones and the blood. The ICE detention centers. The hospitals turning away women with sepsis. The kids drinking lead in Mississippi. The black bodies swinging under red, white, and blue lights.

We don’t wait for the camps. We don’t wait for the bodies to stack up in a way that even the most hardened denier can’t ignore. Because by then, it’s already too late.

A Reckoning Without Liberation

We must create the record now. Not because it will persuade the cult—but because the world needs witnesses. Just as Eisenhower insisted in 1945, we need film. We need testimony. We need to tell it all, even if our voices shake. Because there will come a day, maybe sooner than we think, when someone stands on a stage and says, “None of that happened.”

And when that day comes, we must have more than just outrage. We must have evidence. Receipts. Screenshots. Footage. Names.

History is not written by the victors—it’s written by whoever shouts loudest and leaves a paper trail. Let ours be unignorable.

So What Do We Do?

We fight. Not with fists—though self-defense may become necessary—but with truth, solidarity, and strategy. We organize at every level: school boards, city councils, state legislatures. We protect the most vulnerable. We name the enemy clearly and without euphemism.

No more “culture war.” This is a class war wrapped in white supremacy and religious authoritarianism. No more “political polarization.” This is fascism creeping through the cracks of our institutions. No more “economic anxiety.” This is grievance weaponized by billionaires who laugh all the way to offshore banks.

It’s not about Trump. It never was. It’s about the machine he juiced with steroids—the one that was already humming along, killing people quietly.

The Truth Will Not Save Us, But It Is the First Step

This is our 1945. But there will be no Eisenhower this time. No mass unveiling. No moment when the world collectively gasps. Just us, shouting into the algorithmic void, hoping someone’s still listening.

We must become our own documentarians. Our own archivists. Our own chroniclers of the slow-motion catastrophe.

Because someone, someday, will say this never happened.

And we’ll need to hold up the bones, still slick with dirt, and say: “Yes, it did. We were there. And we fought back.”
