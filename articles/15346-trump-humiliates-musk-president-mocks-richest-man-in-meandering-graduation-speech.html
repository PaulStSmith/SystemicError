'Trump humiliates Musk': President mocks richest man in meandering graduation speech

Paulo SantosMay 3, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump took a shot at his billionaire benefactor Elon Musk during a commencement address at the University of Alabama.

The president spoke Thursday night to graduating students, where he aired grievances about the 2020 election, offered advice – “think of yourself as a winner,” “be an original” and “never, ever give up" – and jabbed "internet people" like Musk and other tech moguls, reported The Daily Beast, which framed it as "Trump humiliates Musk."

“They all hated me in my first term,” the president told students and their families, "and now they’re kissing my a--.”

“It’s true,” he added. “It’s amazing. It’s nicer this way now.”

Musk spent nearly $300 million of his own money on Trump's re-election and has directed sweeping cuts to government agencies and the federal workforce as head of the so-called Department of Government Efficiency, but he is stepping back from his role in the administration and focusing on his floundering Tesla electric vehicle company.

“Change is never easy, and the closer you get to success, the more ferociously those with a vested interest in the past will resist you they want to resist,” Trump said. “You have to break the system a little bit and follow your own instincts. But if your vision is right, nothing will hold you down, nothing. You have to have the right vision.”

Trump told the students they were "the first graduating class of the golden age of America,” but he then launched into a campaign-style rant complaining the U.S. had been "ripped off" during Joe Biden's presidency, performed a grunting impression of a transgender weightlifter and attacked the judicial branch for blocking his mass deportations.

“Judges are interfering, supposedly based on due process,” Trump said. “But how can you give due process to people who came into our country illegally?”

Hundreds of protesters gathered at a park about a mile away at a rally hosted by College Democrats, where one student said she felt "betrayed" by the university's invitation to Trump, but his speech was generally well received in a state he won all three times he's run for president.

“I think that no matter what political party or whatever you believe in, I think that it’s super cool that we get to experience and make history and be a part of this,” said 21-year-old Sophie Best, who is graduating with a communications degree. “I don’t think that we could have had a greater person come to speak.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's University of Alabama Commencement Address: A Cocktail of Ego, Grievances, and Misguided Advice

In an event that could only be described as a spectacle of self-aggrandizement mixed with political vendettas, President Donald Trump delivered a commencement address at the University of Alabama that veered wildly off the rails of traditional advice into a labyrinth of personal grievances and controversial jabs. Notably, he took aim at Elon Musk, a figure who, despite reportedly pouring nearly $300 million into Trump's re-election campaign, found himself on the receiving end of Trump's scorn.

The Billionaire Benefactor in the Crosshairs

Trump's attack on Musk, an individual who has been a colossal figure in both the tech and automotive industries, is particularly jarring given Musk's financial contributions to Trump's political endeavors. Despite Musk's efforts to prop up Trump's administration through substantial monetary support and his role in the so-called Department of Government Efficiency, Trump's remarks suggest a fracturing relationship, highlighting the volatile and often unpredictable nature of Trump's alliances. The irony of Trump biting the hand that feeds him cannot be overstated, revealing a penchant for burning bridges with the same billionaires who once bolstered his political and financial standing.

A Cornucopia of Controversial Statements

The address did not stop at merely alienating billionaire benefactors; it spiraled into a series of contentious statements that ranged from a rehashing of election grievances to divisive comments on transgender individuals and the judicial system. Trump's advice to "think of yourself as a winner" and "never, ever give up" was overshadowed by his insistence on airing personal and political grievances, including a disturbing mimicry of a transgender weightlifter and an attack on the judicial system's protection of due process for immigrants. These remarks, far from being enlightening or inspirational, served only to underscore Trump's continued commitment to divisive rhetoric and an us-versus-them mentality.

The Dissonance Between Reality and Rhetoric

Trump's declaration of the graduating class as "the first graduating class of the golden age of America" contrasts sharply with his portrayal of an America "ripped off" during Joe Biden's presidency. This dissonance between the glorification of his era and the vilification of his successor's underscores a fundamental inconsistency in Trump's narrative. It's a portrayal of America that fluctuates wildly depending on his need to aggrandize his tenure or denigrate his opponents, leaving little room for objective analysis or constructive criticism.

The Reaction: A Microcosm of a Polarized Nation

The reaction to Trump's address—a mix of enthusiastic support and vehement protest—mirrors the broader polarization within the United States. While some, like 21-year-old Sophie Best, viewed Trump's presence as an historic and unmissable event, others felt betrayed by the university's decision to invite such a controversial figure. This dichotomy reflects the larger societal divide, where the value of free speech and the importance of diverse viewpoints are increasingly weighed against the potential harm of giving a platform to divisive or harmful rhetoric.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Judgment

The decision by the University of Alabama to host Trump as a commencement speaker, given his track record and the content of his speech, represents a systemic error in judgment. It prioritizes the spectacle over substance, controversy over constructive dialogue, and does a disservice to graduates looking for genuine guidance and inspiration. In his address, Trump managed to encapsulate the essence of his presidency: a relentless pursuit of personal vindication and division, wrapped in the guise of advice and guidance. As the nation moves forward, one can only hope for a future where commencement addresses will once again inspire rather than inflame, unite rather than divide.
