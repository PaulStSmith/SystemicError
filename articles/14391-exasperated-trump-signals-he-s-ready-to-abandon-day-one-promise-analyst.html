Exasperated Trump signals he's ready to abandon 'day one' promise: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Fading Illusions of Peace: A Critical Examination

U.S. President Donald Trump meets with El Salvador President Nayib Bukele (not pictured) in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 14, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump is signaling he’s close to giving up on broaching a peace deal between Russia and Ukraine — and simply walking away, an analyst wrote Monday.

The warning comes as multiple cracks appear in the relationship that Trump has repeatedly bragged he has with Russian President Vladimir Putin, wrote Matthew Chance, CNN’s Chief Global Affairs Correspondent.

His comments came after Trump wrote over the weekend, “I’ve always had a very good relationship with Vladimir Putin of Russia, but something has happened to him.

But Chance wrote that Putin has remained exactly as he always has been.

What has changed is Trump’s certainty that he can bring peace to the region.

“The real question is whether Trump has changed, or at least if his attitude toward Putin has started to shift amid what looks like an increasingly futile US effort to forge peace in Ukraine, something Trump bragged he could do – let’s not forget – in short order," he wrote, referencing the president's promise that he would end the war on his first day in office.

Chance pointed out it was the sixth time that Trump had publicly shown annoyance with the Russian leader. He called him “absolutely crazy” after renewed attacks on Ukraine in recent days — with the largest ever strikes on Ukraine coming this weekend.

He’s also expressed exasperation with Putin’s refusal to sign ceasefire deals.

“More likely, the latest upsurge in violence in Ukraine may further convince an already frustrated US president that he is simply unable to bring the warring parties together any time soon,” wrote Chance.

“And, amid all his anger and bluster about Putin, Trump may simply choose to walk away.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Fading Illusions of Peace: A Critical Examination

In an eye-opening analysis by Matthew Chance, CNN’s Chief Global Affairs Correspondent, the complexities and apparent disillusionment President Donald Trump faces in his efforts to broker peace between Russia and Ukraine are laid bare. This article not only underscores the deteriorating relationship between Trump and Vladimir Putin—a relationship Trump has often boasted about—but also signals a potential abandonment of peace efforts in the region. Let's dissect the underlying issues and the stark realities presented in this narrative.

Trump's Misplaced Confidence

First and foremost, the article highlights a fundamental misjudgment by President Trump: his overestimation of his ability to influence and negotiate with Putin. Trump's assertion, "I’ve always had a very good relationship with Vladimir Putin of Russia, but something has happened to him," reflects a perplexing mix of naivety and denial. Yet, as Chance aptly points out, it's not Putin who has changed—it's the situation that has evolved, and perhaps, Trump's realization of the limits of his purportedly "very good relationship."

The Illusion of Quick Fixes

Trump's initial promise to end the war in Ukraine "on his first day in office" now stands as a testament to his administration's misunderstanding of international diplomacy and the complexities of regional conflicts. This overpromise and underdelivery scenario is not unfamiliar territory in political realms; however, when it comes to matters of war and peace, such bravado is not only misleading but dangerously simplistic.

The Reality of International Politics

The article sheds light on Trump's repeated frustrations with Putin, especially in the wake of renewed and intensified attacks on Ukraine. Trump's branding of Putin as “absolutely crazy” following these attacks illustrates a sharp deviation from his previous admiration, hinting at a belated awakening to Putin's unwavering stance and objectives in Ukraine.

Walking Away: A Defeatist Solution?

The notion that Trump may "simply choose to walk away" from efforts to secure peace in Ukraine is both a telling and troubling prospect. It suggests a retreat not just from the challenges of diplomacy but from the responsibilities of leadership on the global stage. The implications of such a move—both for the region and for the credibility of the United States as a mediator in international conflicts—are profound and far-reaching.

Conclusion: A Call for Realistic Diplomacy

The unraveling of Trump's ambitions to broker peace between Russia and Ukraine serves as a stark reminder of the importance of realistic diplomacy. It underscores the need for a deep understanding of regional dynamics, the interests of involved parties, and the limitations of personal rapport with authoritarian leaders in resolving conflicts. As the situation continues to develop, one can only hope for a reevaluation of strategies and a renewed commitment to peace through more pragmatic and informed approaches.

In summary, the narrative laid out by Matthew Chance is not just a critique of Trump's faltering efforts in Ukraine; it is a cautionary tale about the perils of overconfidence in diplomacy and the harsh realities of international politics. The lessons here are clear: diplomacy is a nuanced art, and peace is not a product of bravado but of persistent, informed, and realistic engagement.
