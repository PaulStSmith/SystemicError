Katie Miller sought Fauci's guidance during COVID-19 despite new attacks

Based on an article by María Teresita Armstrong-Matta

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Miller Case Shows What This Politics Really Runs On

Power Was Never the Point

Dr. Anthony Fauci’s diaries reportedly show that Katie Miller, now calling for his imprisonment and rejecting his pardon, once relied on him for repeated personal medical support during 2020: pregnancy anxiety, her husband Stephen Miller’s COVID-19 diagnosis, and a newborn health scare. That is the only factual contradiction this story needs. The rest is the familiar machinery of elite bad faith.

The important actors are not obscure. Fauci held institutional authority in public health. Stephen Miller sat close to the center of Trump-world power. Katie Miller moved in that same orbit. This is not a tale of an ordinary citizen discovering public policy. It is a story about a political class that used state power aggressively, then tried to rewrite the human relationships that complicated its own hardline posture.

The Performance of Hostility

Katie Miller’s call for Fauci’s imprisonment is not just personal animus. It is political signaling from someone who understands that cruelty is currency in the Trump ecosystem. The point is not consistency. The point is loyalty to a faction that rewards public punishment, especially when it can be directed at a symbol.

Her later claim that the relationship was purely professional, and that friendliness was a strategic tool, only makes the contempt more obvious. That is not a defense. It is a confession that politeness was instrumental, not sincere. She did not expose Fauci; she exposed how the right’s grievance politics works when it collides with private dependence. Use the expert when you need him. Demonize him when the audience demands it.

The Real Misdirection

The story is tempting as gossip because it gives the illusion of symmetry: the anti-Fauci crusader was once helped by Fauci. But the deeper pattern is not hypocrisy in the abstract. It is the right’s routine conversion of public institutions into targets after extracting whatever value those institutions can still provide.

Fauci’s notes are evidence of a public official doing what public officials often do in a crisis: answering calls, calming fears, giving medical guidance. That does not excuse every aspect of his record. But the article’s real political content is not about Fauci’s tenderness. It is about how people adjacent to power feel entitled to personal care from the very institutions they later denounce as illegitimate.

Editing, Redaction, and Control

The report’s note that the original release contained more references to Katie Miller than a later redacted version matters because it points to a second layer of institutional control: who gets to shape the record after the fact. Redaction is never neutral when it obscures a politically useful embarrassment. It may be administrative. It may be legal. It may be damage control. It is still power deciding what history is allowed to remain visible.

That is the part worth watching. The struggle is not only over what happened, but over which version of what happened survives long enough to be repeated.

What This Story Reveals

This is what authoritarian-adjacent politics looks like in practice: private dependence paired with public vengeance, bureaucratic access paired with anti-bureaucratic propaganda, and strategic friendliness repackaged as moral clarity. The goal is not truth. The goal is narrative dominance.

The Democratic lesson is not to imitate this rot with better manners. It is to name the power structure plainly: people close to Trump used the institutions they attacked, then demanded immunity from the consequences of that hypocrisy. They are not confused. They are managing blame.
