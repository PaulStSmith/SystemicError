Experts sound dementia alarms over Trump’s wobbly walking

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosNovember 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The White House supplied the footageConcern is warranted; certainty is notThe real obligation belongs to the presidency

President Donald Trump’s visibly unsteady gait during a November 18 walk with Philippine President Bongbong Marcos has renewed public questions about his health, but the available reporting does not establish that he has dementia. The footage shows a concerning physical irregularity. It does not identify its cause.

That distinction matters because the headline converts a medical hypothesis into an alarm. Psychologist and cognitive neuroscientist Frank George told David Pakman that Trump’s movements could fit behavioral-variant frontotemporal dementia when considered alongside other behavior George has observed. Yet George also named stroke and vestibular problems as possible explanations for the same S-shaped walking pattern. His analysis is a differential judgment, not a reported diagnosis.

The White House supplied the footage

The video did not emerge from a covert recording or an adversary’s camera. The White House’s official “Rapid Response” account posted Trump walking across the grounds of Malacañang Palace in Manila with Marcos during Trump’s Asia trip. Viewers then focused on Trump veering from a straight path and appearing unstable on the red carpet.

The New Republic cited multiple videos from the Manila appearance and pointed to an October clip in which Trump seemed to swerve while approaching a newly installed White House helipad. George has also highlighted Trump dragging his right leg and tapping his thigh while walking. AlterNet reported that John Paul Garrison has separately suggested Trump displays signs consistent with dementia and a possible stroke.

Those observations justify scrutiny. The president exercises extraordinary authority, and evidence that may bear on his physical or cognitive capacity is not private trivia. The institutional question is whether the White House will provide a credible account of conspicuous symptoms visible in footage distributed by the White House itself.

Concern is warranted; certainty is not

George’s reasoning was more qualified than the headline built around it. He said Trump’s gait should be evaluated as part of an “overall constellation of symptoms.” He argued that the pattern did not show other features he would associate with Parkinson’s disease or tardive dyskinesia, while frontotemporal dementia remained consistent with what he observed.

“Consistent with” is doing essential work. George said he did not eliminate frontotemporal dementia; he did not report that it had been confirmed. His own list of possible causes makes a categorical conclusion impossible from the material described.

AlterNet’s plural “experts” framing also outruns the substance presented. The article explains George’s assessment in detail and notes Garrison’s similar suspicions, but it reports no medical finding establishing dementia, a stroke or any other condition. Trump’s walking may be abnormal without proving the particular disease that attracts the most political attention.

The real obligation belongs to the presidency

Online spectators do not control Trump’s medical disclosures. Trump and the White House do. That is where institutional power sits, and where responsibility for resolving legitimate questions belongs.

Speculation flourishes when visible evidence and official presentation diverge. A communications account meant to project presidential strength instead circulated footage that prompted questions about Trump’s stability. Treating every irregular movement as proof of dementia would be irresponsible. Treating repeated, publicly visible motor difficulties as beneath scrutiny would be institutional cowardice.

The defensible conclusion is narrower than the viral one: Trump was recorded walking unsteadily in Manila, similar movement reportedly appeared in an October video, and two specialists cited by the reporting believe the pattern may indicate serious neurological impairment. The footage raises a question the White House has the power to answer. It does not answer it for them.
