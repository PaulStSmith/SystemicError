'Unbelievable': Pentagon's massive troop withdrawal plan sparks immediate backlash

The Pentagon Is Considering a Gift to Moscow—and Calling It Strategy

The Decision Sits in Washington

The Pentagon is considering withdrawing at least 25,000 American troops—nearly a third of the U.S. force in Europe—along with aircraft, ships, and weapons. That is the relevant fact. Online outrage and diplomatic anxiety are consequences, not causes.

Institutional power rests with President Donald Trump’s administration and the Pentagon officials evaluating the drawdown. NATO allies can warn about Russia, former ambassadors can object, and critics can post accusations. None of them controls the deployment. Washington does.

Russia Gains Without Making the Decision

The proposed withdrawal would reduce American military capacity in Europe while NATO governments are warning of a growing Russian threat. That does not prove Trump “works for Putin,” as former Republican congressman Joe Walsh claimed. The source provides no evidence for that allegation.

It does establish a simpler and more consequential point: Moscow would benefit strategically from a major reduction in U.S. forces and equipment. Motive may remain disputed. Effect does not become mysterious merely because motive is unproven.

The Speculation Is a Distraction

The article devotes substantial attention to social-media theories, including the claim that Trump is retaliating because the European Union and Canada strengthened their relationship. No evidence is offered for that explanation.

This framing weakens the story. It turns an exercise of presidential and military power into a carousel of posts, exclamations, and amateur motive-reading. The central question is not which online critic produced the most dramatic theory. It is why the administration believes removing troops, ships, aircraft, and weapons advances U.S. security under current conditions—and whether it has supplied an answer at all.

A Pattern of Coercive Withdrawal

This is not an isolated proposal. The Pentagon reportedly ordered about 5,000 troops out of Germany earlier in 2026 amid tensions with Berlin. During Trump’s first term, he ordered roughly 12,000 withdrawn from Germany; bipartisan opposition followed, and President Joe Biden later froze the plan.

The repetition matters. Military deployments are being treated not only as security commitments but as leverage over allies. When force posture tracks political resentment toward Berlin or broader conflict with European partners, deterrence becomes subordinate to punishment. That is coercive alliance management: comply with Washington’s preferences or watch Washington weaken the structure built to protect you.

Europe Bears the Risk

Some officials argue that a future drawdown could make sense once European countries have built greater defense capacity. The proposal under consideration reverses that sequence. It would remove a large share of American forces while allies are still expanding their capabilities and while they are warning about Russia.

That transfers risk from the administration making the decision to the countries living beside the threat. If deterrence erodes, European governments and populations absorb the consequences first. The White House gets leverage and ideological satisfaction; allies inherit the security gap.

Strategic Ambiguity as Political Cover

The larger pattern is the use of uncertainty itself as power. Repeatedly floating or ordering withdrawals forces allies to plan around an American commitment that can be reduced during a political dispute. Russia need not control Washington to exploit that instability. It only needs Washington to make NATO less predictable and Europe more vulnerable.

Calling the proposal “thoughts” about withdrawal softens the agency involved. Governments do not accidentally contemplate moving 25,000 troops and major military assets. Officials choose to develop such options, presidents establish the incentives around them, and allies change their behavior in response. The systemic error is not confusion about who might benefit. It is coverage that foregrounds speculative outrage while the people holding actual power convert collective security into a weapon against their own partners.
