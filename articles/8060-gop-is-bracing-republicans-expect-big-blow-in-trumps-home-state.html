‘GOP is bracing’: Republicans expect big blow in Trump’s home state

Republicans have started to sense a potential loss in Miami, where a Democrat and a Republican backed by President Donald Trump will face off next week.

The Dec. 9 runoff between former County Commissioner and Democrat Eileen Higgins against former Miami City Manager and Republican Emilio Gonzalez has Republicans worried, Politico reported Thursday.

“The GOP is bracing for a possible loss in one of the state’s remaining blue areas — but in an office Republicans have held for nearly 30 years,” according to the outlet. “It would come as a blow in a state President Donald Trump calls home, and in a city where he plans to build his future presidential library.”

“It’s a tough district,” Evan Power, chair of the Republican Party of Florida, told Politico.

Power called Miami the “Kamala district.” In the 2024 election, Trump lost the city of Miami to former Vice President Kamala Harris, “even as he won the far more populous surrounding county by 11 points,” according to the outlet.

“My expectation is, it probably doesn’t perform for Republicans, but we have to do what we have to do, fight in every place,” Power said.

The election comes a week after a special election in Tennessee that put pressure on Republicans to secure a House seat in the deep-red state that the president had previously won by 22 points in the 2024 election.

On Tuesday, Republican Matt Van Epps, a Trump-endorsed military veteran, beat Democrat Aftyn Behn in the special election for the Tennessee 7th Congressional District seat. Van Epps won the election by 9 points. Some election experts have said that the result is a sign of the momentum Democrats have as the 2026 midterm elections approach and could be a significant sign of what’s to come.

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Miami Mirage: A Closer Look

In the heart of Miami, a political drama unfolds that could very well be a bellwether for the broader national landscape. The upcoming Dec. 9 runoff between Democrat Eileen Higgins and Republican Emilio Gonzalez, both formidable candidates in their own right, has sent ripples of concern through the Republican ranks. According to Politico, this election has the GOP on edge, fearing the loss of a stronghold they’ve comfortably occupied for nearly three decades. Let’s dissect the dynamics at play and what this means for the grand old party and the political climate at large.

A Republican Bastion on Shaky Ground

The GOP’s worry over Miami isn’t just a case of pre-election jitters. It’s a manifestation of a larger, more systemic issue within the party. The fact that this concern is surfacing in what’s described as one of the state’s “remaining blue areas” speaks volumes. Miami, a city where President Donald Trump plans to erect his presidential library, might just be slipping from the Republican grasp. This isn’t just about a single seat or a solitary city; it’s about the shifting sands of political allegiance and the potential for significant realignment.

The Kamala District Phenomenon

Evan Power’s reference to Miami as the “Kamala district” is particularly telling. It underscores a critical miscalculation in the Republican strategy — assuming that the support for Trump in surrounding areas would automatically translate to urban centers. The 2024 election results, where Trump lost Miami to Kamala Harris despite winning the surrounding county by a notable margin, should have been a wake-up call. Yet, here we are, with the GOP seemingly caught off-guard by the prospect of a Democratic victory in a traditionally Republican stronghold.

The Tennessee Special Election: A Canary in the Coal Mine?

In juxtaposition to the Miami race, the special election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District offers a stark contrast. Republican Matt Van Epps, another Trump-endorsed candidate, secured a victory over Democrat Aftyn Behn. However, the margin of victory — 9 points in a district Trump won by 22 points in the 2024 election — is hardly a cause for celebration. It’s a glaring indication that the Republican grip, even in deep-red territories, is loosening.

Election experts are reading the tea leaves, suggesting that these results signal a momentum shift favoring Democrats as we edge closer to the 2026 midterm elections. If a staunchly red state like Tennessee is showing cracks, what does that foretell for the GOP nationally?

The Bigger Picture

The Miami runoff isn’t just a local skirmish; it’s a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the Republican Party. It’s a stark reminder that demographic shifts, urbanization, and changing political sentiments are reshaping the American electoral landscape. The GOP’s apprehension about Miami reflects a deeper anxiety about its future and its ability to adapt to a rapidly changing America.

Conclusion

As Miami goes to the polls, the outcome of the Higgins-Gonzalez runoff will be more than just a local headline. It will be a litmus test for the GOP’s relevance in urban America and a harbinger of the party’s fortunes in the looming 2026 midterms. The Republican Party’s Miami malaise is a symptom of a party at a crossroads, grappling with the daunting task of redefining its identity and strategy in an America that’s not what it used to be. The question now is, will the GOP heed the warning signs, or will it continue to be caught off-guard by the tides of change?
