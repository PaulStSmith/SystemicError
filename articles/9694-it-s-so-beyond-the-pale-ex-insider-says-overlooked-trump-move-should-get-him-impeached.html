'It's so beyond the pale': Ex-insider says overlooked Trump move should get him impeached

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unbearable Lightness of Political Amnesia

Donald Trump (Shutterstock)

Americans mostly “shrugged” when Donald Trump made a move that should get him impeached, according to a former GOP lawmaker.

Recently, the president called for two of his perceived political enemies from Illinois to be sent to prison. It was covered by the media, but people mostly moved on quickly, according to former Tea Party Republican Joe Walsh.

In an article called You’re Damn Right I’m Shocked, Walsh unleashed a profanity-laced “meltdown” on the subject.

“The President of the United States today publicly said that the Mayor of Chicago and the Governor of Illinois should be behind f------ bars. He called for their imprisonment. Publicly. We should all be shocked by that,” he wrote. “I read his post this morning, and I admit that I wasn’t shocked. My reaction was: That’s a fascist. He’s a fascist. That’s fascism. He’s militarizing our streets. He’s trying to create a police state. And now he’s demanding that his political opponents—state and local officials—are thrown in jail. Fascism.”

Walsh went on to condemn his own lack of surprise at the news.

“But there was no surprise. That’s who and what he is. And then I stopped myself and said, ‘No, dammit! Joe, no.’ Because that’s wrong. It’s so out of line. It’s so beyond the pale. To not be shocked by that is a failure on my part. Shame on me,” he wrote. “Then, you know the drill. If any other president had said that—Barack Obama, George W. Bush, any other president—if they had called publicly for two state and local officials that they didn’t like to be thrown in jail for no reason, it would be the biggest story in the world. If we had a functional Congress, there would be impeachment proceedings. Immediately.”

He continued:

“But because it’s Trump, we just shrug. Because it’s Trump, we say, ‘That’s just Trump.’ Because it’s Trump, we just look away. Because it’s Trump, we ignore it. Because it’s Trump, we are no longer shocked. We are no longer outraged. That’s on us. He wins. When we stop being shocked, when we stop being outraged, he wins. We lose.”

According to Walsh, “He should be tossed out of office because of that. He should be impeached for calling for the Mayor of Chicago and the Governor of Illinois to be jailed.”

Our Analysis:

The Unbearable Lightness of Political Amnesia

In a display of what can only be described as a political circus, former GOP lawmaker Joe Walsh voiced his outrage over President Donald Trump’s call for the imprisonment of his political adversaries, the Mayor of Chicago and the Governor of Illinois. This moment, detailed in an article aptly titled “You’re Damn Right I’m Shocked,” serves as a perfect microcosm of the schizophrenic nature of American political discourse. Walsh’s shock, or rather, his admission of the lack thereof, encapsulates the desensitization to the absurd that has become a hallmark of the Trump era.

Fascism on the Front Lawn

Walsh, in his self-described “profanity-laced meltdown,” does not mince words: “That’s a fascist. He’s a fascist. That’s fascism.” Such statements, while bold, are not without precedent in discussions surrounding Trump’s tenure. The militarization of our streets and the attempts at creating a police state are themes all too familiar in the past four years. Yet, Walsh’s surprise at his own lack of surprise reveals the depth of the normalization of the abnormal under Trump’s administration.

The Failure of Collective Memory

What stands out in Walsh’s tirade is not just his condemnation of Trump’s actions but his critique of the collective American response—or lack thereof. “But because it’s Trump, we just shrug.” This sentence alone should send shivers down the spine of any reader with a vested interest in the health of American democracy. The normalization of calls for the jailing of political opponents, a tactic reminiscent of authoritarian regimes, is not just a failure of Trump’s presidency but a failure of the American people to hold their leaders accountable.

The Specter of Impeachment

Walsh’s conclusion that Trump “should be tossed out of office” and “should be impeached for calling for the Mayor of Chicago and the Governor of Illinois to be jailed” touches on a raw nerve in American politics. The invocation of impeachment, especially in the post-impeachment trial world, is fraught with partisan warfare and the scars of recent political battles. Yet, the ease with which such calls for drastic measures are now made (and subsequently ignored) highlights a deeper systemic issue: the erosion of the mechanisms of accountability.

A Shrug of Despair

The article, through Walsh’s lens, paints a grim picture of the current state of American democracy where the outrageous becomes mundane, and the call for accountability becomes a whisper lost in the wind. The collective shrug at Trump’s actions reveals a desensitization to threats against the fabric of democracy, a dangerous precedent for future administrations.

In essence, the reaction—or lack thereof—to Trump’s call for the imprisonment of his political adversaries is not just a reflection of his presidency but a mirror held up to American society. It asks an uncomfortable question: When did we stop being shocked, and what does it say about us? The answer, much like Walsh’s article, is uncomfortable, complex, and a call to action that goes beyond the confines of partisan politics.
