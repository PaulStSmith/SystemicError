'Humiliated' Trump has discovered 'world leaders are harder to bully': analyst

Donald Trump's wavering on tariffs and admission that he has not been able to bring the war in Ukraine to a halt is a sign he's finding out that world leaders are increasingly not taking him seriously.

According to an analysis by CNN's Stephen Collinson, bullying may work on Republican party lawmakers who fear he'll ruin their careers, but it is quite another thing to make demands of foreign diplomats in countries where Trump is considered either an irritant or a joke.

As Collinson wrote, Trump's inability to get anything done internationally is frustrating him and there is no relief in sight for the putative leader of the free world.

In large part Trump's growing problems stem with him "being ignored and humiliated" by Russian President Vladimir Putin, with the Moscow media piling on and "portraying Trump as the tough talker who always blinks and never imposes consequences."

Trump's wavering on China –– which has given rise to the "Trump Always Chickens Out" (TACO) joke on Wall Street –– is seen as yet another sign that he is a paper tiger, suggested the analyst.

"Trump is busily squandering this legacy and shattering US soft power — ie. the power to persuade — with his belligerence," the CNN analyst wrote before predicting, " The first four months of the Trump presidency, with its tariff threats, warnings of US territorial expansion in Canada and Greenland and evisceration of global humanitarian aid programs show that the rest of the world gets a say in what happens too. So far, leaders in China, Russia, Israel, Europe and Canada appear to have calculated that Trump is not as powerful as he thinks he is, that there’s no price for defying him or that their own internal politics make resistance mandatory."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Faltering International Influence

Donald Trump's oscillation on tariffs and his admission of failure in halting the war in Ukraine reveal a glaring truth: his influence on the global stage is waning. This scenario, as outlined by CNN's Stephen Collinson, underscores a stark reality. Bullying tactics, which may have intimidated Republican party lawmakers into submission, are proving to be futile against foreign diplomats who view Trump with a mixture of disdain and amusement.

The Ineffectiveness of Trump's Bullying

Trump's approach to international relations has been marked by aggression and threats, a strategy that might have yielded results within the domestic arena of partisan politics but falls flat on the global stage. The respect and fear he commands within the Republican party do not translate into the international sphere, where leaders and diplomats have their own constituencies to consider and are not beholden to Trump's whims.

Humiliation by Putin and the TACO Joke

A significant aspect of Trump's dwindling global influence is his relationship with Russian President Vladimir Putin. The Moscow media's portrayal of Trump as a leader who talks tough but ultimately capitulates is both a symptom and a cause of his diminishing stature. The emergence of the "Trump Always Chickens Out" (TACO) joke on Wall Street, in reference to his vacillation on China, encapsulates this sentiment succinctly. It's not just a joke but a symbol of his perceived weakness.

The Squandering of US Soft Power

Collinson's analysis points to a deeper issue: Trump's erosion of the United States' soft power. This term, which refers to the ability to persuade and influence others without coercion, is vital in international relations. Trump's tenure, marked by tariff threats, territorial expansion fantasies, and the gutting of global humanitarian aid, exemplifies a misunderstanding of soft power's importance. The world does not revolve around the whims of any single leader, and Trump's failure to recognize this has led to an international defiance that is both unprecedented and predictable.

The World's Calculated Defiance

Leaders from China, Russia, Israel, Europe, and Canada have seemingly reached a consensus: Trump is not as formidable as he believes. This collective realization has emboldened them to defy the U.S. without fear of retribution, understanding that their own internal political dynamics offer more immediate concerns than Trump's displeasure. This scenario is not just a reflection of Trump's faltering strategy but a testament to the complexity of global politics, where internal considerations often trump (pun intended) external pressures.

Conclusion

The narrative spun by Collinson is not just a critique of Trump's foreign policy but a broader commentary on the limitations of a bullying approach to international relations. The world stage demands nuance, understanding, and respect—qualities that are conspicuously absent from Trump's arsenal. As the United States' influence wanes under his leadership, one wonders if the damage can be undone or if this marks a permanent shift in global dynamics. The lesson here is clear: leadership, especially on the global stage, requires more than bluster—it demands a strategic understanding of the world's complexity, something Trump has repeatedly failed to demonstrate.
