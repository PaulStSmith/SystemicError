'They started yelling': Insider says aides 'had to stop Trump' from leaking secret order

There was a highly charged confrontation at the White House that resulted in Donald Trump overriding Mike Johnson and others on the religious right, and after that the president had to be stopped by aides from leaking the details to the public, according to a new WSJ report.

Josh Dawsey of the Wall Street Journal over the weekend dropped a report on how exactly it came to be that Trump made the decision to reschedule marijuana, an idea that has chilly support among many Republicans.

Posting the story on social media, the reporter wrote, “How a weed CEO ran an aggressive influence campaign to convince Trump to reschedule marijuana, which culminated in a dramatic Oval Office meeting with a Florida sheriff, Dr. Oz and many others — and Mike Johnson on the phone.”

In the story itself, Dawsey quotes people who were in the room, including the Florida Sheriff, Gordon Smith.

“House Speaker Mike Johnson (R., La.) on speakerphone urged the president against the decision and senior aides warned the move could be dangerous to some Americans,” Dawsey reported. “After listening, Trump, a teetotaler who eschews alcohol and drugs, sided with the pro-marijuana camp and delivered the biggest softening of federal cannabis policy since U.S. states began legalizing recreational marijuana in 2012.”

The report continued:

“When Speaker Johnson called in, the president put him on the phone with the sheriff, who tried to persuade Johnson. ‘It’s a gateway drug,’ Johnson argued, according to the sheriff. Smith said Johnson was a ‘nice guy’ and he answered Johnson’s questions. Another person familiar with the meeting said Johnson cited studies and research. Oz argued for rescheduling as Schedule II, Smith and others said.”

Trump ultimately sided against Johnson and numerous Republicans, but was perhaps too excited to get out the announcement.

“The lawyers and his staff, they started yelling, ‘No sir, you can’t yet; there’s a 30-day period, it’s gotta go through this and that,’ ” Smith said, according to the WSJ. “They had to stop him from posting.”

The report notes, “Trump then instructed the sheriff and staffers to go into another room and put together an executive order. Trump wanted to put the ‘real story of why we are doing this in the order,’ Smith said.”

“I was in awe of the whole thing,” Smith added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Influence: Trump’s Marijuana Rescheduling Drama

In a spectacular display of political theater, the White House became the stage for a highly charged confrontation between Donald Trump and figures from the religious right, notably Mike Johnson. This episode, meticulously reported by Josh Dawsey of the Wall Street Journal, peels back the curtain on the chaotic interplay of influence, ambition, and policy-making within the Trump administration. The narrative culminates in Trump’s decision to reschedule marijuana, a move that deviates markedly from the conservative stance typically associated with the Republican Party.

The Players and The Play

At the heart of this drama is a cast of characters as diverse as it is unexpected. A weed CEO, a Florida sheriff, the omnipresent Dr. Oz, and others, including House Speaker Mike Johnson on speakerphone, convened in the Oval Office. The scene described by Dawsey is less a policy discussion and more a battle of wills and ideologies. Mike Johnson, embodying the conservative cautionary stance on marijuana as a “gateway drug,” found himself pitted against a coalition of the unconventional.

It’s telling that Trump, a man who prides himself on his abstention from alcohol and drugs, sided with the pro-marijuana camp. This decision is not just a defection from his party’s more traditional views but also an indicator of the unpredictable nature of Trump’s decision-making process. It’s as if the presence of celebrity (Dr. Oz) and the allure of the novel (a weed CEO’s aggressive influence campaign) swayed the President more than the cautious advice of his party peers and senior aides.

The Fallout

The aftermath of Trump’s decision is as chaotic as the meeting itself. Aides scrambled to prevent the President from prematurely leaking the announcement, highlighting a frequent theme of the Trump administration: the struggle to manage the President’s impulses. Trump’s insistence on drafting an executive order then and there, to capture the “real story of why we are doing this,” is a stark illustration of his desire to control the narrative, often at the expense of procedural prudence.

Analysis

This episode raises critical questions about how decisions are made in the highest office of the United States. The influence of external actors, the role of celebrity, and the impact of personal persuasions over policy analysis are all deeply concerning. It underscores the volatile nature of policy-making under Trump’s administration, where decisions that can affect millions seem to be made on whims, influenced by the last person who has the President’s ear, rather than through careful consideration of research, expert opinion, and potential consequences.

Furthermore, Trump’s attempt to bypass procedural safeguards to rush the announcement is indicative of a broader disregard for institutional norms and processes. Such behavior not only undermines the structured decision-making processes designed to ensure thoughtful policy development but also poses significant risks to the governance of the country.

Conclusion

The rescheduling of marijuana, while a significant policy shift, is almost secondary to the larger narrative this episode reveals about the Trump administration. It serves as a vivid example of how decisions were made in a tumultuous era marked by unpredictability, influence peddling, and a stark departure from traditional policy-making processes. As such, it demands not just our attention but our scrutiny, for the implications of such decision-making processes extend far beyond the tenure of any single administration.
