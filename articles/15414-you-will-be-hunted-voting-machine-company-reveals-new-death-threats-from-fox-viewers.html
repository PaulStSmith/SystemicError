'You will be hunted': Voting machine company reveals new death threats from Fox viewers

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 2, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A leading voting machine manufacturer recently unveiled multiple threats made against its employees as part of its ongoing defamation lawsuit against Fox News.

The Washington Post reported Wednesday on the latest federal court filing by Smartmatic, which is seeking $2.7 billion in damages from the leading conservative cable news network. Smartmatic alleged that the company's false claims about its machines being rigged in favor of former President Joe Biden caused significant harm to the company's finances and reputation, and resulted in the company having to lay off hundreds of employees.

In its most recent filing, Smartmatic alleged that the company made a choice to "lean into election fraud claims they personally believed were baseless" in response to backlash from its audience after it preemptively put Arizona in Biden's win column before any other major networks. That backlash included President Donald Trump instructing his son-in-law, Jared Kushner, to call Newscorp chairman Rupert Murdoch and urge him to withdraw the early projection.

The company further alleged that Fox News' on-air talent made knowingly false claims about its machines, which resulted in a wave of death threats from viewers. One threat read: "The only way to resolve your interference with Western democracy is to shoot dead your company executives." Another viewer warned Smartmatic: "You lot are in for a rude awakening."

"You will be hunted," another threat read.

Smartmatic has been litigating its suit since 2021, and has already won settlements from far-right outlets that defamed the company in its coverage including Newsmax and One America News Network. The company has said it doesn't plan to settle with Fox and is aiming to win a judgment for the full $2.7 billion its seeking.

William & Mary Law School constitutional law professor Timothy Zick told the Post that Smartmatic was in for an uphill battle, as it would need to prove the network acted with "actual malice" and knowingly lied to its audience about the company's machines. Voting machine company Dominion Voting Systems settled a similar lawsuit with Fox News for $787 million last year.

Our Analysis:

The Saga of Smartmatic vs. Fox News: A Tale of Defamation, Damages, and Democracy

In the latest chapter of a legal drama that feels ripped from the pages of a political thriller, Smartmatic, a leading voting machine manufacturer, has thrown down the gauntlet against Fox News. According to a report by The Washington Post, Smartmatic is seeking a staggering $2.7 billion in damages for what it claims are defamatory statements made by the network, which allegedly led to a significant downturn in the company's fortunes, including financial losses and layoffs.

The Allegations: A Web of False Claims

Smartmatic accuses Fox News of deliberately peddling falsehoods about its voting machines being rigged in favor of former President Joe Biden during the 2020 election. This narrative, as per Smartmatic's allegations, wasn’t just a series of unfortunate misstatements but a calculated decision by Fox News to "lean into election fraud claims" despite knowing these claims were baseless. This strategy was purportedly adopted in the wake of a viewer backlash when Fox News declared Biden the winner of Arizona ahead of other networks, a move that even caught the ire of President Trump himself.

The Consequences: Threats and Intimidation

The fallout from Fox News' reporting, as detailed by Smartmatic, wasn't confined to the courtroom or the court of public opinion. It spilled over into the real world in the most alarming way — through death threats against company executives. Quotes from these threats, such as "The only way to resolve your interference with Western democracy is to shoot dead your company executives," highlight the chilling impact of misinformation on the public psyche.

The Legal Battle: An Uphill Fight

Smartmatic's legal challenge against Fox News is no small undertaking. As noted by constitutional law professor Timothy Zick, the company faces an "uphill battle" to prove that Fox News acted with "actual malice" — a high legal standard requiring proof that the network knowingly lied or recklessly disregarded the truth. This case echoes the recent settlement where Dominion Voting Systems won a $787 million lawsuit against Fox News under similar allegations.

The Bigger Picture: Democracy Under Siege

This lawsuit transcends a mere legal battle over defamation and damages; it's emblematic of a larger, more disturbing trend of misinformation and its corrosive effect on democracy. When news outlets, especially those with the reach and influence of Fox News, disseminate false claims, the repercussions are profound. Not only do they undermine public trust in the electoral process, but they also fuel a climate of intimidation and violence against those caught in the crosshairs of conspiracy theories.

In Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

As this legal saga unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the responsibility borne by media outlets to uphold the truth, especially on matters as consequential as the integrity of our elections. The Smartmatic lawsuit is not just about a company seeking redress for damages; it's a litmus test for the accountability of media in an era where misinformation can, and does, endanger lives and the very fabric of democracy.

Whether Smartmatic will succeed in its formidable quest for justice remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: the outcome of this case will have far-reaching implications not just for the parties involved, but for the broader battle against misinformation and the safeguarding of truth in our democratic discourse.
