Firestorm over Republican's social media posts ignites calls for his ouster

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 18, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Screenshot/MS Now

Critics are demanding the censure and even expulsion of a Republican US congressman after his latest bigoted remarks against Muslims.

The firestorm began on Sunday when Rep. Randy Fine (R-Fla.) responded to a sarcastic social media post by Palestinian American activist Nerdeen Kiswani, who jokingly suggested that New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s election meant that “NYC is coming to Islam,” and it was time to consider banning dogs as pets because “like we’ve said all along, they are unclean.”

“If they force us to choose,” Fine wrote, “the choice between dogs and Muslims is not a difficult one.”

Kiswani insisted that her post was not meant to be taken at all seriously, and was rather her attempt to satirize some Americans’ fears about having a Muslim elected to lead the largest US city.

“If Americans cared as much about abused children as they do about posts joking about dogs,” she wrote, “we might actually be facing a national reckoning. Instead watching some of the same politicians who resisted transparency around the Epstein files discover their outrage over this is telling.”

Many Democratic lawmakers were quick to condemn Fine for suggesting that the US expel Muslims.

“We must call this what it is. Disgusting bigotry,” wrote Rep. Ro Khanna (D-Calif.). “Fine must be censured. It’s about morality and decency, not politics.”

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) joined Khanna’s call to censure Fine.

“This is genuinely one of the most disgusting statements I have ever seen issued by an American official,” she wrote. “It should not stop shocking us that the Republican Party openly embraces this. Fine should be censured and stripped of committees. To ignore this is to accept and normalize it.”

Rep. Rob Menendez (D-NJ) argued that Fine’s statement “is what it looks like when Islamophobia and outrage are the only two items on your political agenda,” and demanded that House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) issue a formal condemnation.

Jennifer Jenkins, a Democrat running against Fine in Florida’s 6th Congressional District, said that censuring the Republican lawmaker was not enough.

“Randy Fine has spent years spewing hate: attacking entire faiths, calling for violence, targeting kids and families, and dehumanizing Americans,” she wrote. “He’s only escalated. Congress must act now and expel Randy Fine immediately.”

Fine appears undeterred by the condemnation he’s received, appearing on Newsmax Tuesday morning to push false claims about Democrats wanting to ban pet dogs.

“People should know Democrats like [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez] are saying ‘we are going to get rid of your dogs,’” Fine falsely claimed. “Americans need to keep that in mind when they go and vote in November.”

Our Analysis:

The Incendiary Remarks of Rep. Randy Fine: A Case Study in Political Bigotry

In a recent blaze of controversy, Rep. Randy Fine (R-Fla.) has ignited a firestorm with his unabashedly bigoted remarks against Muslims. This episode is emblematic of a deeper, systemic dysfunction within American politics, where xenophobia and Islamophobia are not only present but, alarmingly, wielded as weapons by elected officials.

The Catalyst of Controversy

The incident in question unfolded when Fine responded to a deliberately satirical social media post by Palestinian American activist Nerdeen Kiswani. Kiswani’s post, a satirical jab at Islamophobia, suggested that with the election of New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani, an era banning dogs—a facetious nod to a misinterpretation of Islamic teachings—might commence. Fine’s reaction, however, stripped of any semblance of understanding or humor, was to starkly frame the issue as a choice “between dogs and Muslims,” implicitly advocating for the expulsion of the latter.

A Misguided Missile in Political Discourse

Fine’s remarks are not just misinformed; they are dangerously inflammatory. To suggest that an entire religious community is less valuable or less welcome than pets is to descend into the lowest echelons of hate speech. It’s a stark reminder of how xenophobia has been normalized and weaponized within certain political circles. Fine’s attempt to double down on his comments by appearing on Newsmax and propagating false claims about Democrats wanting to ban pet dogs only adds fuel to the fire. This maneuver is a textbook example of distraction, a feeble attempt to shift the focus from his unacceptable bigotry to an unfounded and ludicrous political conspiracy.

The Response: A Litmus Test for Political Morality

The response from Democratic lawmakers has been swift and unequivocal. Rep. Ro Khanna (D-Calif.) and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) have called for censure, rightly labeling Fine’s comments as “disgusting bigotry.” This is more than a matter of party politics; it’s about drawing a line in the sand regarding moral and ethical decency in public discourse. Rep. Rob Menendez (D-NJ) has aptly pointed out that Fine’s statement is indicative of a political agenda fueled solely by Islamophobia and outrage, demanding formal condemnation.

Jennifer Jenkins, challenging Fine in Florida’s 6th Congressional District, has called for his expulsion, highlighting a history of hateful rhetoric. This is not a one-off incident but the latest in a series of inflammatory and divisive statements. The fact that Fine remains undeterred, using platforms like Newsmax to perpetuate falsehoods, underscores a disturbing trend of politicians engaging in fear-mongering and division for political gain.

A Reflection on Political Decay

The incident involving Rep. Randy Fine is a microcosm of the systemic rot plaguing American politics. When elected officials feel emboldened to spew hate speech without fear of repercussion, it signals a dangerous shift in societal norms and values. The calls for censure and expulsion are not merely about penalizing Fine for his remarks; they are about reaffirming a collective stance against bigotry and hate.

As this debacle unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the work that remains to be done in combating xenophobia and Islamophobia. The actions taken—or not taken—by political leaders in response to Fine’s comments will be telling of the direction in which American politics is headed. Are we willing to accept and normalize hate, or will we stand against it, demanding accountability and decency from those in power? The choice is clear, but whether it will be made is another matter entirely.
