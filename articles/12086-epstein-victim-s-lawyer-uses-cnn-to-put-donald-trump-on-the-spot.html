Epstein victim's lawyer uses CNN to put Donald Trump on the spot

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 4, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Specter of Pardon: Trump, Maxwell, and the Mockery of Justice

The attorney for Jennifer Araoz, who says Jeffrey Epstein sexually assaulted her at age 15, challenged President Trump on CNN Saturday, inviting him to meet with her “one on one” amid speculation he may pardon Epstein’s associate Ghislaine Maxwell.

“Jennifer would like to invite the president to meet with survivors like her so he can understand the pain they went through and why a pardon should not be granted under any circumstances,” said Eric Lerner, Araoz’s attorney, extending the invitation for the first time to CNN’s Fredricka Whitefield.

Trump has faced increased scrutiny over his Justice Department interviewing Maxwell last week, with speculation growing that the president may be considering pardoning the convicted sex offender, and in exchange for compromising information on Democratic figures like former President Bill Clinton, who, like Trump, has had a well-documented relationship with Epstein.

Epstein died in 2019 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges, and is alleged to have operated a blackmail operation targeting powerful figures.

And on Friday, Trump sparked further outrage when Maxwell was quietly moved from a maximum-security prison in Florida to a minimum security prison camp in Texas, a move that required the Bureau of Prisons to bend the rules to facilitate.

Lerner said that Araoz and other victims of Epstein “can’t understand why a convicted sex trafficker like Maxwell would be transferred to the lowest-level security prison in our federal system,” noting that at Maxwell’s new prison, “prisoners can literally walk off the facility,” as most minimum security prisons have “limited or no perimeter fencing.”

Whitefield asked Lerner how he intends on delivering the invitation to meet with Araoz to Trump, to which Lerner suggested he would get it to the president one way or another.

“We hope to get the message out today, we hope that this will be reported on, and we hope that the president is watching; if it's not reported on, or the president isn't watching today, we're going to keep talking about it,” Lerner said.

“I think they could talk one on one and she could really explain to him, face to face, what she's been through and what the other survivors like her have been through. How, under no circumstances, should a pardon for Ghislaine Maxwell be considered.”

Lerner also laid into Trump’s supposed reason for facilitating a meeting with Maxwell, which was to, as his DOJ Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche put it, “to ask: what do you know?” Instead, Lerner said, the Trump administration had ample tools at its disposal to find additional co-conspirators in Epstein’s crimes.

“The justice system clearly has Epstein's emails: Google has it and the justice system has it,” he said. “Those emails would reveal a lot of what is being looked for. The Justice Department confiscated over 70 computers, hard drives, iPads, cell phones, so release what they have, but getting names from Ghislaine is not worth giving her a pardon or an easier sentence.”

Our Analysis:

The Specter of Pardon: Trump, Maxwell, and the Mockery of Justice

In a move that reeks of the highest echelons of power manipulating justice to its grotesque advantage, the attorney for Jennifer Araoz, a survivor of Jeffrey Epstein’s sexual assault, has thrown down the gauntlet in the face of President Trump. The proposition? A one-on-one meeting with survivors like Araoz to grasp the profound agony inflicted by Epstein and his accomplices, including Ghislaine Maxwell. This challenge comes amidst swirling rumors and outright indignation at the possibility of Trump pardoning Maxwell, Epstein’s associate and a convicted sex offender.

The Insidious Link Between Power and Sexual Exploitation

Epstein’s death in 2019 did little to sever the complex web of influence, blackmail, and sexual exploitation he weaved through the corridors of power. His association with figures like Trump and former President Bill Clinton paints a sordid picture of the elite’s murky entanglements. The recent speculation about Trump considering a pardon for Maxwell, ostensibly in exchange for dirt on political adversaries, is not just appalling; it's a stark reminder of how justice is often a pawn in the game of political chess.

A Travesty of Prison Transfers

Adding fuel to the fire, Maxwell’s transfer from a maximum-security facility to a minimum-security prison camp underlines a justice system bending over backwards for the privileged. At her new abode, where inmates can “literally walk off the facility,” the mockery of accountability couldn’t be more blatant. This maneuver not only undermines the severity of Maxwell’s crimes but also spits in the face of survivors who seek closure and justice.

The Hollow Justification for a Meeting

Trump’s administration, as per DOJ Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, seeks to meet Maxwell to inquire, “what do you know?” Yet, this rationale falls flat when considering the wealth of evidence, including emails and electronic devices, already in the hands of the justice system. The desperate scramble to secure names from Maxwell, thereby dangling a pardon or a lighter sentence as bait, is a misguided and morally bankrupt strategy.

A Call to Action

Eric Lerner, Araoz’s attorney, is not merely extending an invitation to Trump; he’s spotlighting the dire need for the president to confront the human wreckage left by Epstein and Maxwell. This is about more than political maneuvering; it’s about acknowledging the survivors’ trauma and ensuring that a pardon for Maxwell remains unthinkable.

The narrative unfolding is one of power, privilege, and a justice system that appears malleable to the whims of the influential. The potential pardon of Ghislaine Maxwell would not only be a slap in the face to the victims but also a grim testament to the depths to which political and personal interests can corrode the very fabric of justice. As this saga continues to unfold, one can only hope that the voices of survivors like Jennifer Araoz resonate louder than the whispers of political expediency.
