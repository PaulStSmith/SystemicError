A clear example was the Liberal Party's rout in Canadian elections this week, mere months after polling had them all but doomed to lose their majority to the Conservatives.

However, Burnett was quick to note, "It's not just Canada. The other country closest to the U.S. is Mexico. How has Trump impacted Mexico since the election?"

"Yeah, it's not just north of the border, it's south of the border as well," said Enten. "The Mexican president. Look at her net approval rating. Look how much it has risen since the November election here in the States. Hello? Up 28 points since November, Claudia Sheinbaum's approval rating. And I will tell you this. More than that, look at her overall approval rating. It is now north of 80 percent ... well, who was the last president who had an approval rating north of 80 percent in this country? It was George W. Bush after 9/11. She has a massive rally-around-the-flag event and her net approval rating and her overall approval rating, way up since Donald Trump took office."

"That is incredible," said Burnett. "Now let's go with some other allies. There's an election coming up soon in Australia. What do you see there?"

"So if we went south, well, let's go way to the east or way to the west, depending on which way you want to go, right?" said Enten. "And what we see there was the Labor Party there looked like an underdog when Trump took office, right? But then all of a sudden, what happened? You see Trump takes office, and hello, the odds that they win the lower house in that election, look at that, up to 89 percent right now ... they were losers, and Trump has turned them into winners. And why is that? Because the conservative coalition in that country tied themselves very much to sort of the wokism type, wokism is bad. And then the Labor were able to say, you know what, the tariffs are bad. Donald Trump put the tariffs in, you can trust us. And we will make sure that Donald Trump doesn't beat us up."

Our Analysis:

The Global Trump Effect: A Scathing Analysis of Political Dominoes

In an era where political boundaries are as porous as sieves, the reverberations of domestic policies and personalities on the global stage cannot be overstated. This brings us to the curious case of President Donald Trump, whose influence—or rather, the global repulsion towards his administration's antics—has transcended American borders, shaking up political landscapes in allied nations. CNN data analyst Harry Enten's discussion with Erin Burnett throws a glaring spotlight on this phenomenon, but let's peel back the layers with a bit more skepticism and attention to the underlying currents.

The Canadian Conundrum: Liberal Party's Surprising Victory

The Liberal Party's unexpected triumph in the Canadian elections is a stark reminder of how closely foreign electorates are watching the U.S. political circus. Enten suggests Trump's plummeting polls and the international distaste for his brand of politics played a significant role in rallying support for the Liberals, despite pre-election predictions favoring the Conservatives. This isn't just about Canadian domestic policy but a clear rejection of Trumpian politics. It's ironic, isn't it? A leader who prides himself on winning has inadvertently become a catalyst for conservative losses abroad.

South of the Border: Mexico's Approval Rating Surge

Moving southward, Mexico presents an even more dramatic shift. Claudia Sheinbaum's approval rating soaring by 28 points post-Trump's election is a phenomenon Enten likens to a "massive rally-around-the-flag event." Comparing Sheinbaum's popularity spike to George W. Bush's post-9/11 approval ratings is both insightful and alarming. It underscores the profound impact U.S. politics has on its neighbors—not through policy or diplomacy, but through sheer antipathy towards Trump. What does it say about a presidency when its most notable contribution to international relations is boosting the popularity of foreign leaders by virtue of opposition?

The Australian Angle: Labor Party's Fortunes Reversed

The discussion then leaps to Australia, where the Labor Party's fortunes did a complete 180 with Trump in office. Enten's analysis suggests that the conservative coalition's alignment with anti-wokism, echoing Trump's rhetoric, alienated voters. This allowed the Labor Party to position itself as the rational choice, capitalizing on opposition to Trump's tariffs and broader policy approach. The transformation of "losers" into winners with an 89 percent chance to win the lower house starkly illustrates the global backlash against Trumpian politics and its unintended consequences for conservative movements worldwide.

The Bigger Picture: A Global Rejection of Trumpism

What this analysis brings to light is not just the localized political shifts in Canada, Mexico, and Australia, but a broader global repulsion towards Trumpism. The irony is palpable—Trump, who aimed to strengthen conservative movements globally, has instead galvanized opposition, uniting disparate political entities in their rejection of his policies and persona.

The question then arises: Is Trump aware of the international chessboard his actions are playing on? His administration's policies and rhetoric have not just domestic but global implications, reshaping political allegiances and party fortunes across continents.

In Conclusion: Trump's Unintended Political Legacy

As we dissect the ripple effects of Trump's presidency on global politics, it's clear that his impact extends far beyond American borders. While he may revel in the chaos of domestic politics, the international arena tells a story of unity against what many see as a disruptive and divisive force. Trump's legacy, it seems, will be as a global unifier—not of allies under American leadership, but of political factions rallying against his vision of governance.

In a twisted turn of fate, Trump has become the world's most unlikely kingmaker, inadvertently propelling left-leaning parties to victory against his conservative comrades-in-arms. One can't help but ponder: Is this the art of the deal, or the art of defeat?
