Trump admin eyes major new seizure: 'We're gonna take it back'

In a further expansion of its occupation of the District of Columbia, the Trump administration announced it will take control of operations at Washington, D.C.’s historic Union Station, citing crime and homelessness as justification. Crime in the nation’s capital fell by double digits last year, and dropped by double digits again this year.

“We’re gonna take it back, and we’re gonna drive out the homelessness,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy told Fox News on Wednesday. “We’re gonna drive out the crime.”

“And we are going to, I think, bring in more retailers, I think more customers. It’ll be better revenue,” he said.

“And again, this is our capital city. It should be beautiful, and the president wants a beautiful capital. I want a beautiful capital. And so, this plan is gonna make Union Station represent the president’s vision of what America can and should look like.”

READ MORE: Trump Orders Death Penalty for All D.C. Homicides, Defying Long Ban

The Department of Transportation owns Union Station, but will now exercise more control. Secretary Duffy “said it will involve a cooperative agreement with the nonprofit Union Station Redevelopment Corp., which controls the station, and Amtrak, which leases space from the USRC,” The Washington Post reported.

“I think the president has been pretty clear on what he wants for Union Station, for American travel, for travel,” Duffy said at an Amtrak event on Wednesday. “He wants Union Station to be beautiful again. He wants transit to be safe again, and he wants our nation’s capital to be great again.”

Axios added that “Union Station has been a focal point for the Trump administration’s military deployment in D.C. Top members of the Trump administration — including Vice President Vance — bashed the station’s management and cast it as crime-ridden at an Aug. 20 press conference.”

Earlier this month, Fox News reported that “about seven National Guard Humvees and other military vehicles, and around 25 to 30 troops, stood guard outside D.C.’s Union Station.”

The Department of Transportation has also wrested control of the renovations of New York City’s Penn Station. Some news reports earlier this year have suggested that Penn Station’s name might be changed and named for President Donald Trump.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Iron Grip: Trump's Union Station Takeover

In a move that's as audacious as it is alarming, the Trump administration has declared its intention to seize operational control over Washington, D.C.’s Union Station. Citing crime and homelessness as its knights in shining armor, the administration rides into this battle with a banner that reads more like a thinly-veiled excuse for expansion of power rather than a genuine concern for public welfare. Let's dissect this narrative with the scalpel of scrutiny.

The Justification: A Cloak of Concern

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy took to Fox News, proclaiming a crusade against crime and homelessness, pledging to "take it back" and "drive out" the social issues plaguing the capital. This would be a commendable endeavor, if not for the inconvenient truth that crime in D.C. "fell by double digits last year, and dropped by double digits again this year." This glaring contradiction between the administration's justification and reality paints a stark picture: the problem they're so vocally committed to solving is, in fact, already on a significant decline.

The Vision: Beautification Over Substance

Duffy's vision for Union Station, as a beacon of beauty and commerce, mirrors President Trump's grandiose vision of America—a vision deeply intertwined with aesthetics and commercial success. However, the focus on bringing in "more retailers" and "more customers" for "better revenue" skirts around the pressing issues of homelessness and crime, except as nuisances to be expelled. This approach raises a critical question: Whose capital city are we beautifying, and at what cost?

The Method: Centralization of Power

The Department of Transportation's move to exercise more control over Union Station—through a "cooperative agreement" with Union Station Redevelopment Corp. and Amtrak—signals a further centralization of power. Coupled with the administration's military presence in D.C. and the takeover of New York City’s Penn Station renovations, a pattern emerges: a pattern of using federal power to physically and symbolically assert control over key national infrastructures.

The Military Deployment: A Show of Force

The Trump administration's deployment of military assets, including "about seven National Guard Humvees and other military vehicles, and around 25 to 30 troops," outside Union Station is particularly chilling. This show of force in the nation’s capital is a stark reminder of the administration's readiness to use military might to enforce its vision of order, further blurring the lines between governance and militarization.

The Bottom Line: Democracy or Dictatorship?

The Union Station takeover is emblematic of the Trump administration's broader approach to governance: one where power is consolidated, dissent is quashed, and public spaces are militarized. In Duffy's words, the goal is to make Union Station—and by extension, America—"beautiful again." But beauty, as they envision it, seems to come at the expense of democratic principles and the public good.

In a democracy, beauty arises from the freedom and well-being of its citizens, not from the grandiose visions of its leaders. As the Trump administration marches forward with its plans, one must ask: Are we witnessing the beautification of America, or the erection of a gilded facade over a system increasingly resembling autocracy?

In conclusion, while the administration wraps its actions in the guise of public service, the reality is a concerning amalgamation of power grab, aesthetic obsession, and militarization. As citizens, it’s imperative that we question, critique, and hold accountable those in power—lest we find ourselves admiring the beauty of a capital city that no longer embodies the democratic values it's supposed to represent.
