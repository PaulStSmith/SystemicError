Lindsey Graham complains about having to work ‘on a Saturday’ while ‘beautiful outside’

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republican Senator Lindsey Graham had argued his calls to Georgia’s secretary of state were protected under a US Constitution clause shielding members of Congress from questioning about official legislative acts [File: Win McNamee/Pool via Reuters]Published On 1 Sep 2022

Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) opened a speech on the Senate floor Saturday by complaining about having to work on a Saturday, while also endorsing President Donald Trump’s proposal to take the billions of dollars earmarked for Obamacare and send them directly to Americans.

“So here we are on a Saturday… beautiful outside,” Graham mused as he opened his remarks on the Senate floor.

The Senate is working a rare Saturday session in an effort to bring about an end to the ongoing government shutdown, now in its 39th day, the longest shutdown in U.S. history. Graham, however, was apparently not pleased about the rare weekend work, but did voice full support for an unorthodox proposal floated by Trump earlier in the day.

On social media, Trump announced that he would be recommending Congress to sign off on a multibillion dollar payout directly to Americans, and to do so with funds earmarked for Obamacare, also known as the Affordable Care Act (ACA).

The ACA lies at the heart of the dispute that kicked off the government shutdown more than a month ago, with Democratic lawmakers refusing to back any spending bill that didn’t include an extension to ACA subsidies, which are set to expire this year and cause 4 million Americans to lose health insurance, and increase ACA premiums by 114% for millions.

Republicans have refused to support extending the subsidies, and on Saturday, a growing number of GOP lawmakers are now backing Trump’s alternative.

“Mr. President, in case you’re listening out there, I think you’ve come up with a good idea – count me on board,” Graham continued. “To my colleagues on this side, this is a fight worth having; to my Democratic colleagues, let’s open up the government, act like adults, and see if we can get this problem in a better spot.”

Graham is not the only lawmaker to back Trump’s proposal: Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) also voiced support for the idea on social media Saturday, writing that she “totally” agrees with the plan.

“Health savings accounts and across state line purchases are the way to go,” Blackburn wrote in asocial media poston X Saturday.

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Senatorial Discontent and Dubious Proposals

In an episode that could only emerge from the theater of the absurd that is contemporary American politics, Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC) took to the Senate floor, not with a message of urgency concerning the longest government shutdown in U.S. history, but with a complaint about the inconvenience of working on a Saturday. This opening salvo of petulance, “So here we are on a Saturday… beautiful outside,” perfectly encapsulates the disconnect between the political elite and the realities faced by everyday Americans.

The government shutdown, now dragging into its 39th day, has left hundreds of thousands of workers without pay, compromising everything from air travel safety to food inspections. Yet, the gravity of this situation seems lost on Graham, who instead of addressing the dire consequences of the shutdown, chose to lament his lost weekend.

But the true pièce de résistance of Graham’s remarks was his endorsement of President Donald Trump’s proposal to dismantle the Affordable Care Act (ACA), colloquially known as Obamacare, and redirect its funding directly to Americans. This suggestion is not just unorthodox; it’s a brazen display of fiscal irresponsibility and a stark betrayal of the millions of Americans who rely on the ACA for affordable health insurance.

The ACA, despite its flaws, has been a lifeline for millions, reducing the number of uninsured Americans by 20 million since its inception. The proposal to gut its funding amid a government shutdown is not only politically tone-deaf but a cruel gamble with the health and well-being of millions of Americans. The irony of proposing such a measure under the guise of alleviating the fallout from a government shutdown initiated over healthcare funding should not be lost on anyone.

Moreover, Graham’s rallying cry, “Mr. President, in case you’re listening out there, I think you’ve come up with a good idea – count me on board,” is a vivid illustration of the sycophantic tendencies that pervade the current political landscape. It’s a testament to the lengths some lawmakers will go to align themselves with Trump’s policies, no matter how detrimental they may be to the public welfare.

Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) echoes Graham’s sentiments, advocating for health savings accounts and the ability to purchase insurance across state lines as viable alternatives. While these ideas merit discussion, presenting them as panaceas to the complex issues plaguing the U.S. healthcare system is both misleading and overly simplistic.

In conclusion, the spectacle of Sen. Graham bemoaning the necessity of weekend work while championing a proposal that would effectively dismantle a crucial piece of the American healthcare system is indicative of a broader systemic malaise. It’s a stark reminder of the need for a more empathetic, informed, and responsible approach to governance—one that places the needs and well-being of all Americans above political gamesmanship and personal inconvenience.
