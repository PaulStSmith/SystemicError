Ex-Trump official goes nuclear over DOJ's Epstein 'cover-up': 'It's coming from the top'

Ex-Trump administration official Sarah Matthews went hard at her former boss on Tuesday during an appearance on MS NOW, accusing President Donald Trump of being involved in a massive “cover-up” concerning Jeffrey Epstein and his past ties to the disgraced financier.

“This administration loves to claim that they’re the most transparent administration in history, and that’s obviously just a bunch of bologna,” Matthews said. “It’s not true.”

Matthews, who previously served as Trump’s deputy press secretary during his first administration, pointed to new reporting that claimed dozens of pages from the Justice Department’s public online database of files on Epstein appeared to be missing, pages that, based on context, are believed to include serious allegations against Trump, including that he sexually assaulted a child.

“There is no reason as to why you would not publish these 53 pages that NPR discovered were missing in the Epstein files that relate to Trump,” Matthews continued.

“If this was an allegation that he claims has no merit, then why not publish it, why not let the public see it and make that judgment for themselves? Clearly there is a cover-up happening… I think it’s coming from the top, I think Donald Trump is dictating what he wants to happen when it relates to the Epstein files.”

NPR’s reporting was also corroborated by Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), who on Tuesday confirmed that the files in question were indeed missing after visiting the Justice Department’s headquarters in Washington, D.C. to personally view unredacted files.

“There’s a reason why these 53 pages were not released to the public – there is seriously some sort of cover-up happening,” Matthews said. “I know that sounds like I have a tinfoil hat on and that I’m making a jump here, but why would they not release these files? It just doesn’t make any sense to me.”

Our Analysis:

The Alleged Trump-Epstein Cover-Up: Unveiling the Facade

In a recent and blistering critique, ex-Trump administration official Sarah Matthews took to the airwaves on MS NOW, laying bare the ostensible transparency of her former employer, President Donald Trump. Matthews’ assertions revolve around a purported massive “cover-up” involving Trump and his connections with the notorious financier Jeffrey Epstein. This accusation is not just a mere whisper in the political corridors but a loud siren calling attention to the murky depths of Trump’s administration’s dealings.

Transparently Opaque

Matthews, with her insider perspective, dismantles the administration’s self-proclaimed badge of transparency. Describing it as “a bunch of bologna,” her words cut through the administration’s narrative like a knife through butter. It’s a bold statement, especially coming from someone who once served as the mouthpiece of the Trump administration. The claim that the Trump administration is the most transparent in history falls apart under Matthews’ scrutiny, revealing a façade of openness.

The Missing Pages Mystery

Central to Matthews’ argument is the disappearance of dozens of pages from the Justice Department’s public online database concerning Epstein—a man whose name is synonymous with criminal sexual conduct and high-profile connections. These aren’t just any missing pages; they are believed to contain allegations so grave against Trump that they could shake the very foundations of his public persona, including claims of sexually assaulting a child.

Matthews raises a question that echoes in the minds of many: Why conceal these 53 pages? If Trump is as innocent as he proclaims, transparency would ostensibly serve his interests, allowing the public to see the baselessness of the allegations for themselves. Yet, the choice to hide these documents suggests a fear of scrutiny, a hallmark of guilt rather than innocence.

Corroboration and Cover-Up

The seriousness of Matthews’ claims gains further legitimacy with the corroboration from Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), who confirms the absence of these critical files after a personal visit to the Justice Department. This isn’t just speculation; it’s backed by physical evidence and political testimony. Matthews’ allegation of a cover-up, therefore, isn’t the ramblings of a conspiracy theorist but a conclusion drawn from a series of unsettling facts.

“Clearly there is a cover-up happening,” Matthews asserts, pointing the finger directly at Trump. This isn’t just about hiding documents; it’s about manipulating the narrative, controlling what the public gets to know, and safeguarding a legacy built on sand.

Conclusion: The Uncomfortable Truth

What we’re witnessing, according to Matthews, is a concerted effort from the highest echelons of power to bury the truth. The refusal to release these files to the public isn’t just an act of omission; it’s an active suppression of potentially damning evidence against Trump. The implications of these actions are far-reaching, challenging the integrity of the administration and its commitment to transparency and accountability.

Matthews’ explosive revelations demand a thorough investigation. The American public deserves to know the full extent of Trump’s connections with Epstein and the nature of the allegations hidden in those missing pages. Anything less is a disservice to the principles of justice and transparency that the United States purports to uphold. The veil of secrecy must be lifted. Only then can the truth—whatever it may be—come to light.
