GOP told to promise it'll rid nation of Trump at first 'creeping senility' sign

Washington Post columnist Megan McArdle wants assurances that the GOP will do to Trump what it insists Democrats should have done to Biden -- remove him from office when he shows signs of cognitive decline.

In a new opinion piece, McArdle argued that "a good deal of what [Trump] says is nonsense. But it’s mostly the same kind of nonsense he’s been spewing for years. That said, it’s reasonable to worry about Trump’s cognitive capacity."

As Trump ages, the odds of "creeping senility" will increase, McArdle wrote, and it will be up to Republicans to do something about it, not cover it up like the Democrats are accused of doing with Biden.

McArdle referenced Trump's own vow to "'absolutely' step down if his health deteriorated in office," but the country knows by now that the current president abhors even the appearance of weakness. Thus, it will be up to Republican leadership to step in if it becomes necessary.

"So Republicans need to ask whether they’ll do any better than Democrats if the president shows signs of serious impairment," McArdle wrote. "It’s easy to proclaim what other families should do when it’s time to take Dad’s car keys away. But as generation after generation discovers, it’s also easy to put it off when it’s your dad who needs the intervention."

Of course, it will be hard to stand up to a man whose second term has been bent on inspiring fear and seeking retribution, McArdle admitted.

"The people surrounding the president also have a financial stake in his success — cross the boss and touch off an embarrassing intraparty fight, and you can say good-bye to your salary, your future consulting contracts and your speaking gigs," McArdle wrote.

But the good of the country must always take precedence "over partisanship, money and personal loyalties," she emphasized, and offered a challenge to Republican leadership: "If those are your true beliefs, will you pledge now to live up to them?"

Our Analysis:

Analysis of McArdle's Opinion on Trump and GOP's Future Actions

Washington Post columnist Megan McArdle boldly steps into the quagmire of political loyalty and cognitive health in her latest opinion piece, putting the GOP's integrity under the microscope. Her central thesis rests on a provocative challenge: will the GOP have the fortitude to remove Donald Trump from office if he exhibits signs of cognitive decline, as they have so vociferously demanded of the Democrats with Joe Biden? McArdle's commentary navigates through the murky waters of political obligations versus moral imperatives, setting the stage for a critical examination of party loyalties and the broader implications for American governance.

The Hypocrisy of Political Parties: A Tale as Old as Time

McArdle's argument hinges on a painfully obvious yet widely ignored truth: political parties often exhibit a glaring double standard when evaluating the fitness of their leaders versus those of their opponents. She points out the GOP's loud calls for Biden's removal on grounds of cognitive decline, juxtaposing this with Donald Trump's own potential trajectory towards "creeping senility." It's a stark reminder of the selective outrage that characterizes much of our political discourse. McArdle's assertion that "a good deal of what [Trump] says is nonsense" yet is dismissed or even celebrated by his supporters, underscores the partisanship over principle mentality plaguing our political system.

The Unspoken Truth About Aging Leaders

Addressing the elephant in the room, McArdle dares to voice concerns over Trump's age and its inevitable toll on cognitive capacity. The reality of aging leaders, often cloaked in diplomatic silence or outright denial, raises critical questions about the sustainability of leadership in the highest echelons of power. Trump's own promise to step down if his health deteriorated is met with skepticism by McArdle, who rightly points out the chasm between public declarations and private willingness to reveal weakness. This skepticism is not unfounded, given the historical reluctance of leaders to admit vulnerability, painting a grim picture of political valor over personal health.

The Financial Chains Binding Republican Loyalty

One of the most piercing observations McArdle offers is the financial entanglement that dissuades Republican leaders from opposing Trump, even if it were in the nation's best interest. The threat of losing salaries, consulting contracts, and speaking gigs creates a financial hostage situation, where the good of the country is sacrificed at the altar of personal gain. This stark reality lays bare the transactional nature of political loyalty, where money often speaks louder than morality.

A Challenge to Republican Leadership

McArdle concludes her piece with a direct challenge to the GOP: to prioritize the country's welfare over partisanship, personal loyalties, and financial interests. It's a call to action that resonates with the ideal of what governance should be— a service to the people, not a self-serving enterprise. However, the cynic might question the likelihood of such a paradigm shift, especially in a political landscape deeply scarred by division and motivated by self-interest.

In Conclusion

Megan McArdle's opinion piece is not just a critique of the GOP's potential hypocrisy; it's a mirror reflecting the broader issues of aging leadership, the influence of money in politics, and the often hollow nature of political promises. Whether her challenge to the Republican leadership will inspire introspection or merely add to the cacophony of political discourse remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the questions she raises are both urgent and necessary, demanding a reckoning with the realities of power, age, and accountability in American politics.
