'Shadow chair': Trump may resort to secret weapon if Fed defies him

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to reporters aboard Air Force One en route to New Jersey, U.S., June 6, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

If Federal Reserve officials fail to cut interest rates as Donald Trump wants, the president may be ready to deploy a secret weapon — his own "shadow chair" — to help meet his objectives.

It would be an "unusual step," according to CNBC, but a "shadow chair" would be able to monitor current Chair Jerome Powell "until a permanent chief can be installed next year."

CNBC quoted Krishna Guha, head of global policy and central bank strategy, as saying, "There is 'fresh buzz' around the idea that Trump could announce his choice to succeed Powell soon, 'as a shadow Fed chair in the interim,'" until Powell's term ends.

“The idea would be to accelerate the timeframe over which the administration can put its stamp on the Fed and influence rate markets while avoiding the nuclear option of trying to fire Powell,” according to Guha.

The report said the impact of such a move "likely would be minimal," however.

"It takes seven votes on the Federal Open Market Committee to move policy, and it would be hard to find more than one or two right now who would be in favor of the aggressive interest rate cuts Trump is seeking," wrote economic editor Jeff Cox.

Since he took office in January, Trump has been pressuring Powell to lower interest rates to ease inflation.

On Wednesday, new data showed consumer prices rose 0.1% while the annual inflation rate increased to 2.4%, slightly lower than the 2.5% economists were expecting for May, CNN reported.

Trump took a victory lap over the numbers, posting to Truth Social, "CPI JUST OUT. GREAT NUMBERS! FED SHOULD LOWER ONE FULL POINT. WOULD PAY MUCH LESS INTEREST ON DEBT COMING DUE. SO IMPORTANT!!!"

But experts warned that it was still too soon for celebration because "the effects of President Donald Trump’s tariffs have yet to take hold."

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the "Shadow Chair" Gambit: Trump's Federal Reserve Manipulation

In a move that reads like a subplot from a political thriller, President Donald Trump appears poised to sidestep conventional processes and norms governing the Federal Reserve by potentially appointing his own "shadow chair." This maneuver, as reported by CNBC and echoed by Krishna Guha, head of global policy and central bank strategy, is not just an "unusual step"; it's a brazen attempt to undermine the independence of a pivotal institution in the American financial system. Let's dissect this strategy and its implications with the unflinching scrutiny it deserves.

The Mechanics of Manipulation

At the heart of Trump's discontent is Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell's reluctance to slash interest rates according to the president's wishes. Trump's solution? Introduce a "shadow chair"—an ominous term that conjures images of clandestine operations rather than transparent governance. This figure would ostensibly monitor Powell "until a permanent chief can be installed next year," a proposition that reeks of political leverage rather than economic prudence.

Krishna Guha's commentary reveals the underlying strategy: to "accelerate the timeframe over which the administration can put its stamp on the Fed and influence rate markets while avoiding the nuclear option of trying to fire Powell." This approach seeks to sidestep the institutional checks that preserve the Fed's autonomy, a cornerstone of its ability to manage the nation's monetary policy free from political pressure.

The Illusion of Impact

The CNBC report, through economic editor Jeff Cox, anticipates the impact of such a shadow chair to be "minimal." This assessment hinges on the Federal Open Market Committee's (FOMC) voting structure, which requires a majority to enact policy changes. Given the current composition of the FOMC, the likelihood of securing the necessary votes for Trump's "aggressive interest rate cuts" appears slim.

Yet, to dismiss the potential impact of this strategy on the mere basis of voting mechanics is to overlook the broader implications for the Federal Reserve's independence and the dangerous precedent it sets for future administrations to exert undue influence over what should be a neutral body.

A Misguided Victory Lap

In his characteristic bravado, Trump hailed the slight dip in inflation rates as a triumph, using it to bolster his call for rate cuts. His message, delivered with typical enthusiasm on Truth Social, ignores the nuanced realities of economic indicators and prematurely celebrates an unsteady victory. Experts caution that the full effects of Trump's tariffs have yet to manifest, suggesting that the current inflation figures may not be a reliable harbinger of economic stability.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Trump's flirtation with appointing a "shadow chair" to the Federal Reserve is more than an "unusual step"; it's a direct assault on the institution's independence and a flagrant attempt to politicize monetary policy decisions. While the practical effects of such a move may be debated, the symbolic damage is unequivocal. It represents a fundamental misunderstanding of the Federal Reserve's role and undermines the delicate balance between government and economic policymaking.

In a political era increasingly characterized by the erosion of norms and institutions, the introduction of a shadow chair to the Federal Reserve should not be seen as a mere tactical maneuver, but as a warning sign of the lengths to which the administration is willing to go to assert control over independent bodies. The implications for the Federal Reserve and, by extension, the economic well-being of the nation, are profound and deeply concerning.
