Born Into Debt: The High Price of Breathing in America

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 25, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Health Care by Class, Not by NeedThe Myth of “Choice”The Corporate Peasant SystemA Baby's First Breath—Billed MonthlyThe Sick Joke of ReformConclusion: We Deserve Better Than This

In the land of the free, freedom often stops at the hospital doors—especially if you're not carrying the right kind of plastic. In a country that prides itself on being a global leader, the simple act of giving birth has become a luxury commodity—packaged, billed, and sold like a boutique service. And heaven help you if your insurance plan isn’t blessed by the oligarchs of the healthcare cartel.

A short video recently cast a harsh spotlight on this grotesque reality. It begins with a woman being told, calmly and bureaucratically, that her insurance isn’t accepted at the hospital where she’s about to give birth. Her co-worker, in the next room, is covered. The difference? Her coworker has “Health Shield Bronze.” The woman has a “different” plan. When pressed, the clerk admits the truth: “different” means “worse.”

This isn’t fiction. This is America.

Health Care by Class, Not by Need

Welcome to the United States, where your medical treatment isn’t determined by urgency, humanity, or need—but by network coverage and employer perks. Where an HMO versus PPO can mean the difference between a private birthing suite or a janitor’s closet. Where corporate executives get catered care, and everyone else gets an invoice stapled to a shrug.

In this supposed beacon of liberty, the moment your child enters the world, the billing department is already circling. A single room is $2,000 per night. That doesn’t include the delivery doctor, the anesthesiologist, or the “ab fees” (whatever fresh acronym hell that is). By the end, you’re staring down a $20,000 bill—for the privilege of giving birth.

This isn’t healthcare. It’s a shakedown in scrubs.

The Myth of “Choice”

Politicians and health industry ghouls love to toss around the word “choice,” as if navigating healthcare is akin to picking between flavors of soda. But when one plan is accepted and the other gets you bounced to a clinic “that’s been there for years” (read: neglected, underfunded, and probably downwind from a freeway), it’s clear what’s happening.

This isn’t choice. It’s enforced stratification.

The Corporate Peasant System

Let’s not gloss over one of the most telling lines in the video: “Corporate takes care of the managers, and the rest of us get the scraps.” This isn’t metaphor. This is policy. Corporations design benefit tiers like medieval lords handing out rations to serfs. If you’re not in upper management, you're not getting the care—you’re getting the leftovers.

And God forbid you're a contractor, gig worker, or part-time employee. In the eyes of the American healthcare system, you're disposable.

A Baby's First Breath—Billed Monthly

In most developed nations, childbirth is a moment of joy—not a financial catastrophe. In the UK, it’s free. In France, they pay you. In Finland, new parents receive a baby box packed with essentials. In America, you receive an itemized list of everything that went into the birth—including the oxygen.

And if you’re unlucky enough to be Black, Hispanic, poor, or simply uninsured, your chances of complications skyrocket. Because healthcare inequality in this country doesn’t just cost money—it costs lives.

The Sick Joke of Reform

Every election cycle, the healthcare debate is dragged back onto the stage like a corpse with fresh makeup. The left pushes for single-payer. The right clutches pearls about “socialism” and “death panels,” conveniently ignoring the very real death panels we already have: insurance adjusters denying coverage.

And meanwhile, hospitals merge, insurers profit, and patients hemorrhage their savings. Reform? We don’t need reform. We need demolition—followed by a humane, universal system built from the rubble.

Conclusion: We Deserve Better Than This

This video struck a nerve not because it was outrageous, but because it was ordinary. This happens every single day. In a nation with trillion-dollar tech giants and billion-dollar bailouts, we are still sending mothers into debt for the crime of reproduction.

It’s not just inequality. It’s not just injustice.

It’s cruelty by design.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
