Tom Homan wound up in FBI probe into prison contracts-for-cash scheme: report

President Donald Trump’s so-called “border czar,” Tom Homan, was ensnared in a $50,000 bribery sting last year that the incoming administration ultimately ignored, according to a report.

According to MSNBC reporters Carol Leonnig and Ken Dilanian, Homan, whose official title is executive associate director for Enforcement and Removal Operations, was part of an investigation into a private prison contracts scheme involving Homan’s former ICE colleague Julian “Jace” Calderas.

“And in May 2023, he proposed a scheme, according to the FBI, whereby for $1 million, Homan could get them contracts and continued proposing that to the point that the FBI concluded they had to begin investigating Tom Homan for potential bribery,” said Leonnig on MSNBC Monday.

Homan assumed he would become the Secretary of Homeland Security ahead of the election, said NBC News reporter Jacob Soboroff.

He was recorded accepting $50,000 in cash from undercover FBI agents posing as businessmen seeking government contracts. They had promised Homan $1 million. The recorded meeting involved Homan appearing to agree to help the undercover agents secure lucrative immigration-related contracts if Trump won a second term. The cash was delivered in a bag from the restaurant chain Cava.

“At that same meeting, the document shows, Calderas accepted $10,000 from the undercover FBI agents,” the report said. “His alleged proposal to undercover FBI agents, which had not been previously reported, led to a full-blown Justice Department investigation of Homan, who at the time was a private consultant helping clients obtain government contracts.”

It then set off a federal investigation into potential bribery, wire fraud, and conspiracy.

The investigation began under the Joe Biden administration and gained momentum as the election neared. However, after Trump took office in 2025, the Justice Department, led by officials closely aligned with Trump, decided to terminate the probe. They cited insufficient evidence to prove that Homan had committed a crime or provided favors in exchange for the funds, as he was not yet in an official government role at the time of the meeting. The outcome frustrated some law enforcement officials who believed more evidence could have been gathered.

The Trump White House, the FBI, and the DOJ claimed that the sting was politically motivated. White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt went further, calling it outright entrapment.

Leonnig noted that at no point has Homan denied receiving the bribe. The White House, however, has denied that he took the bribe in a press statement.

Dilanian pointed to a clip of Leavitt from the press briefing last week in which she said, “The FBI agents knew very well that Tom Homan would be taking a job in the Trump administration.”

“That’s not true,” Dilanian said. “This happened in September 2024, according to our reporting. So they had no idea who was going to win the election at that time. They were just following the investigation where it took them.”

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of a Political Bribery Scandal: A Closer Look

In an era where political scandals erupt with the frequency and intensity of summer thunderstorms, the case involving Tom Homan, President Donald Trump’s so-called “border czar,” stands out not just for the allegations themselves but for the subsequent handling by the administration. According to a report by MSNBC reporters Carol Leonnig and Ken Dilanian, Homan found himself at the heart of a $50,000 bribery sting—an affair that the current administration seemingly chose to ignore, brushing it under the political rug.

The Allegations: A Classic Case of Quid Pro Quo?

Homan, who held the official title of executive associate director for Enforcement and Removal Operations, was implicated in a scheme that reads like a textbook example of political corruption. The FBI’s findings, as reported, suggest that Homan was willing to sell his influence for a hefty price. In a move that reeks of brazen confidence, he reportedly accepted $50,000 in cash from undercover FBI agents, with the promise of $1 million to secure lucrative immigration-related contracts should Trump secure a second term. The visual of cash being delivered in a bag from a restaurant chain adds a touch of the absurd to the already disconcerting allegations.

The Investigation: A Political Hot Potato

The investigation into Homan—a saga involving accusations of bribery, wire fraud, and conspiracy—began under the Joe Biden administration, only to lose steam once Trump took office in 2025. The decision by Trump-aligned Justice Department officials to terminate the probe, citing “insufficient evidence,” is a move that, at best, raises eyebrows and, at worst, screams of a miscarriage of justice. This decision, particularly frustrating to law enforcement officials, appears to be a clear case of political protectionism. The claim of “insufficient evidence” rings hollow, especially in light of the fact that such investigations are rarely black and white and often require a nuanced, thorough examination of the facts at hand.

The Response: Denials and Deflections

The Trump White House’s response to the allegations, framing the sting as “politically motivated” and tantamount to “entrapment,” is a predictable, albeit disappointing, tactic. It’s a diversion, a way to shift the focus from the substance of the allegations to the motivations behind the investigation. The press secretary’s claim that the FBI agents knew Homan would be taking a job in the Trump administration, despite the timing of events suggesting otherwise, is a bold assertion that seems to lack a factual foundation.

Conclusion: A Systemic Problem

At no point, according to Leonnig, has Homan denied receiving the bribe, a detail that speaks volumes. Yet, the White House has denied the bribe took place, a contradiction that leaves us with more questions than answers. This case is emblematic of a broader, systemic issue within American politics: a troubling tolerance for corruption and a willingness to overlook serious allegations when it’s politically expedient to do so.

The implications of this scandal, and its subsequent handling, extend far beyond the individuals involved. They touch on the integrity of our political system, the standards to which we hold our public officials, and the cynicism with which we view their actions. As observers, analysts, and citizens, it is our duty to demand accountability, transparency, and justice—without these, the very foundation of our democracy is at risk.
