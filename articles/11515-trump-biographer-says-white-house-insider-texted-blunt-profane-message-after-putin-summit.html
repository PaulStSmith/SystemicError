Trump biographer says White House insider texted blunt, profane message after Putin summit

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks with Russian President Vladimir Putin, as they meet to negotiate for an end to the war in Ukraine, at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S., August 15, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump has sought to present his recent summit with Russian President Vladimir Putin as a constructive encounter, but several inside accounts and media analyses suggest otherwise.

Analysts highlighted how Putin managed to shift U.S. policy rhetoric toward a broader “peace agreement," a move that aligned with Kremlin narratives — without offering any tangible concessions, giving him symbolic and diplomatic gains at U.S. expense.

On The Daily Beast Podcast Saturday, Trump biographer Michael Wolff said a close aide texted him simply: “F--- --.”

READ MORE: 'Failure': Former UK PM claims Trump learned a lesson after meeting with Putin

Wolff concluded that Trump emerged from the meeting in Alaska clearly disadvantaged, a sentiment seemingly shared by those inside his circle. “Trump got f--- --,” he said.

“We got nothing out of this," Wolff said. "This is, you know, this is really, really a loss for Trump, and potentially a loss for Ukraine.”

The summit began with direct and symbolic gestures. U.S. airmen rolled out a red carpet and fighter jets and a B‑2 bomber performed a flyover as Putin arrived at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage. Trump and Putin shook hands and exchanged smiles before the U.S. president invited the Russian leader into the presidential limousine, “The Beast,” where they waved to onlookers.

Following the conclusion of their Anchorage summit, both leaders made succinct joint remarks at the base. Trump called the outcome “very productive” but said that key issues remained unresolved and that no formal agreement had been reached.

In later interviews, he shifted focus onto Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European leaders, urging them to broker a peace deal.

When asked what Trump received in return for such optics, Wolff said, “Practically nothing."

He added: “It was a failure.”

The Trump biographer recalled his pre-summit threats. “If we don’t get a deal, I’m going to walk away. I’m going to do tariffs. I’m going to do, you know, all of this...” — none of which were carried out.

Our Analysis:

A Summit of Symbolism Over Substance: The Trump-Putin Encounter

In what could only be described as a diplomatic charade, President Donald Trump's recent summit with Russian President Vladimir Putin has been dissected across various platforms, revealing a stark imbalance in the so-called "constructive encounter." Analysts, insiders, and the sharp tongue of Trump biographer Michael Wolff have painted a picture not of triumph but of capitulation, where the scales of gain tipped heavily in favor of the Kremlin. Let's dive into the layers of this farcical summit with the precision of a scalpel.

Symbolic Gestures Over Substantial Gains

The summit was a showcase of pomp and pageantry, starting with U.S. airmen rolling out a red carpet and military jets performing flyovers as Putin arrived. Such grand gestures, including the handshake and the ride in "The Beast," were symbolic victories for Putin, projecting an image of camaraderie and equal footing on the world stage. Yet, beneath this veneer of diplomacy, the substance was as thin as the air at the summit's Anchorage venue.

The Illusion of Productivity

Trump's declaration of a "very productive" meeting was a masterclass in obfuscation. The reality, as highlighted by insiders and analysts, was a session where the U.S. appeared to bend its policy rhetoric towards a "broader peace agreement" that played right into the Kremlin's narrative. The tangible concessions from Russia? Non-existent. The diplomatic gains? All on Putin's side, leaving the U.S. holding an empty bag of promises.

Trump’s Tactical Fumble

Michael Wolff's commentary on the summit is both scathing and illuminating. Trump, who had previously blustered about walking away without a deal and threatening tariffs, instead "basically embraced Vladimir Putin." It's an embrace that signifies not a strategic partnership but a glaring misstep, trading away leverage for a photo op. Trump's pre-summit threats evaporated faster than his claims of productivity, leaving one to ponder the true cost of this diplomatic endeavor.

A Victory for Putin, A Loss for Trump

The summit's outcome can be succinctly summarized by Wolff's contact: "F--- --.” These words, though crude, encapsulate the sentiment of those who witnessed the U.S. president's performance. Trump walked away disadvantaged, a fact noted not only by critics but by those within his own circle. The message to Ukraine and its allies is particularly troubling, signaling potential vulnerabilities in their support system.

Conclusion: A Summit Shrouded in Failure

As the dust settles on the Anchorage summit, the narrative being woven by Trump and his team stands in stark contrast to the reality dissected by analysts and insiders. It was a meeting where symbolic gestures were mistaken for diplomatic victories, and where Putin managed to extract symbolic and diplomatic gains at the expense of the United States. The only "peace" achieved was the quiet before the storm of realization that this summit may indeed be, as Wolff concludes, a "failure."

In the grand chessboard of international politics, Trump's move was less a checkmate and more a blunder, a misstep that could have lasting implications not just for his administration but for the global balance of power. As we continue to sift through the aftermath, one thing becomes abundantly clear: in the realm of diplomacy, substance should always triumph over symbolism. The Trump-Putin summit, unfortunately, was a stark reminder of what happens when it does not.
