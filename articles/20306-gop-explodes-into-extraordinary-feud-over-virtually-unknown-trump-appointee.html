GOP explodes into 'extraordinary feud' over 'virtually unknown' Trump appointee

The GOP’s Foreign-Policy Civil War Is Really a Fight Over Empire Management

Not a Feud, a Power Struggle

The reporting centers on Elbridge Colby, a “virtually unknown” undersecretary of defense for policy with real Pentagon experience and real leverage. That description matters because this is not a squabble over personality. It is a fight over who gets to steer the machinery of American force: troop deployments, nuclear deterrence, alliance pressure, and the terms of Trump’s “America First” line.

The Source of the Damage

The key decision described here was the removal of an Army brigade that had fortified NATO’s eastern flank after Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Colby says no final order had been issued when he met with Rep. Mike Rogers. That may settle the narrow question of what he knew when. It does not change the larger fact that the administration moved toward a consequential military withdrawal while keeping Congress in the dark or half-informed.

Congress Is Angry Because It Was Managed, Not Consulted

Rogers was “stunned and betrayed,” and Republican lawmakers have since tightened oversight, blocked nominees, and accused Colby of dishonesty. That is not simply institutional indignation. It is what happens when the White House and Pentagon treat Congress as a venue for after-the-fact notification rather than actual governance. The outrage is real, but it arrives late, after the decision architecture has already done its work.

The Real Split Inside the GOP

The Post’s “proxy war” language is the clearest thing in the report. One faction wants a harder line on allies, more demands for foreign defense spending, less American burden-sharing, and more transactional discipline. Another faction still wants the old imperial bargain preserved, especially when it comes to NATO, Taiwan, and Ukraine. Both camps invoke Trump. Neither is trying to shrink American power. They are arguing over how aggressively to use it, and against whom.

Colby as Enforcer

Colby is being cast as the administration’s enforcer of a transactional approach to allies, and that framing is fair based on the record in the article. He has pushed partners to spend more, tried to block a congressional trip to Taiwan, and drawn heat for poor communication with lawmakers. He also shifted from dismissing a war with Iran to supporting Trump’s chaotic conflict with the country. The consistency is not principle. It is submission to the current direction of power.

Systemic Error

The deeper pattern is familiar: the political class stages policy as confusion, then pretends the damage is merely procedural. It is not. A small circle inside the executive branch makes high-stakes choices, Congress complains after the fact, and the press packages the result as a feud among Republicans. That framing flatters everyone involved. It turns deliberate conduct into drama and masks institutional cowardice as ideological debate.
