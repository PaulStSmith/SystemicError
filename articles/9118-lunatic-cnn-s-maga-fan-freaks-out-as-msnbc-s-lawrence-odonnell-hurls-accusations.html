'Lunatic!' CNN's MAGA fan freaks out as MSNBC's Lawrence O’Donnell hurls accusations

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 1, 20254 min read

MSNBC’s Lawrence O’Donnell. (Shutterstock)

CNN resident conservative Scott Jennings went a furious meltdown aimed at MSNBC’s veteran commentator Lawrence O’Donnell after the latter accused him of lying live on air.

The vicious feud erupted as O’Donnell said Jennings, who frequently gets into rows with CNN panel members as he defends President Donald Trump, of being a “paid liar.”

“During the first Trump presidential campaign and presidency, CNN regularly paid Trump supporters to lie about Trump on CNN,” O’Donnell argued on his MSNBC show, The Last Word with Lawrence O’Donnell.

O’Donnell specifically criticized Jennings’ evolution from a former Sen. Mitch McConnell (R-KY) aide who could once “criticize some of the more extreme Trump positions,” to what he called “the JD Vance of CNN”.

Jennings responded forcefully on his radio show, dismissing O’Donnell as “irrelevant” and a “lunatic.”

“There’s this lunatic on MSNBC at 10 o’clock every night named Lawrence O’Donnell,” Jennings said. “I had sort of forgotten that he was a thing, but I guess he still has a show, and he went crazy, crazy on yours truly the other night.”

The CNN commentator defended himself against the accusations of lying, asking rhetorically, “Someone at MSNBC is accusing someone else of lying? From that propaganda outfit over there? They’re accusing me or anyone else of lying? Outrageous.”

Jennings also said of O’Donnell: “He’s irrelevant, nobody likes his show” and asserting that Abby Phillip’s CNN show, which airs at the same time and has Jennings as a frequent guest, was “kicking his rear end by 30 percent on a nightly basis”.

However, viewership data tells a different story. O’Donnell’s show actually brings in twice as many viewers as NewsNight, making it the second most popular show on MSNBC and the 84th overall on television. NewsNight ranks fifth on CNN and 157th overall.

Jennings, an ardent supporter of President Trump who recently published a book about Trump’s second term, concluded his critique with a blunt message to O’Donnell: “You’re a failure.”

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Political Commentary: Jennings vs. O’Donnell

In the latest episode of what can only be described as the circus maximus of cable news, we have Scott Jennings, CNN’s resident conservative, and Lawrence O’Donnell, MSNBC’s seasoned commentator, locked in a vitriolic tussle that underscores the current state of political discourse in America.

Jennings’ meltdown, as it’s been characterized, wasn’t just a display of personal animosity but a vivid illustration of how deeply entrenched the business of political punditry has become in the art of personal destruction. O’Donnell’s accusation of Jennings being a “paid liar” is a serious charge, one that reflects the broader criticism of how networks like CNN have, at times, been accused of platforming individuals more for the sake of controversy than honest debate.

The Evolution of Scott Jennings

O’Donnell’s critique of Jennings’ metamorphosis from a critic of “some of the more extreme Trump positions” to what he derisively calls “the JD Vance of CNN” speaks volumes about the transformation of political loyalty and the exigencies of cable news. This transformation is emblematic of a larger trend where the lines between political advocacy, analysis, and outright partisanship have been blurred, if not altogether obliterated.

Jennings’ response to O’Donnell’s critique is equally telling. Labeling O’Donnell as “irrelevant” and a “lunatic” does nothing to address the substance of the accusation leveled against him. Instead, it serves to redirect the conversation from a potentially substantive critique of his positions to a personal attack on O’Donnell’s character and the legitimacy of his program. This is a classic deflection, one that is all too common in the political media landscape.

The Ratings Game

Jennings’ claim that Abby Phillip’s CNN show is “kicking [O’Donnell’s] rear end by 30 percent on a nightly basis” is not only factually incorrect, as viewership data suggests, but it also highlights another problematic aspect of cable news: the obsession with ratings over substance. The fact that O’Donnell’s show brings in twice as many viewers as NewsNight on CNN contradicts Jennings’ assertion and raises questions about the motivations behind such claims. Is the truth a casualty in the war for ratings?

A Reflection of Our Times

This feud, while perhaps entertaining to some, is a distressing indicator of where political discourse is today. It’s a world where facts are often secondary to personal attacks, where the line between news and entertainment continues to blur, and where the complex realities of political life are reduced to sound bites and sensational squabbles.

Jennings’ parting shot, calling O’Donnell a “failure,” is an unfortunate encapsulation of this trend. It’s not just an attack on a fellow commentator but a symptom of a deeper malaise affecting political commentary today - one where the merit of arguments is too often measured by ratings, retweets, and the capacity to demean.

In the end, this feud isn’t just about Jennings and O’Donnell; it’s a microcosm of a larger, more troubling reality. It’s about how we, as a society, engage with political ideas and the mediums through which those ideas are conveyed. If the discourse continues down this path, we all lose, regardless of which side of the political divide we find ourselves on.
