Rolling Stone over the weekend dropped an in-depth piece on White House deputy chief of staff for policy Stephen Miller. As part of that reporting, the outlet noted that "paranoid" Trump officials were "whispering" nicknames for Miller behind his back.

The report also included an anecdote from someone who says Miller once hit on her. Building on that story, the outlet reported, "Female strangers aren’t the only ones in Washington who think he’s weird."

According to the article, Trump himself has lobbed insults at his "awkward" aide.

"Over the years of their close working relationship, President Trump — ever the gossipy Mean Girl with a nuclear arsenal — has not shied away from insulting Miller behind his back," according to the report. "According to two sources with direct knowledge of the matter, Trump has commented to others in the past about Miller’s intense, awkward, and at times off-putting demeanor."

That being said, according to the report, Trump does find Miller to be valuable.

"But to Trump, Miller is a useful battering ram, the policy answer to his lingering question of 'Where’s my Roy Cohn?'" the report states before quoting the White House.

“Stephen Miller has been one of President Trump’s longest serving and most trusted advisers for nearly a decade, and I can personally attest to the respect the president has for Stephen because I witness it every day,” says White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt. “That’s why Stephen serves as the deputy chief of staff for policy and Homeland Security adviser, because the president has the utmost faith in him and his proven leadership abilities. In addition to being extremely effective at his jobs, Stephen is a loyal colleague and friend. Any suggestion otherwise is false gossip from people who don’t actually know him.”

The report adds:

"In addition to the press secretary’s statement, the Trump White House sent Rolling Stone a lengthy list of testimonials — resembling an extremely MAGA LinkedIn endorsements section — from Republican lawmakers to prove that people like Stephen Miller personally."

Our Analysis:

The Mean Girls of the White House: Trump's Insults and Miller's Awkwardness

In what reads like a script lifted straight from the most dramatic of high school dramas, the latest exposé from Rolling Stone reveals the inner workings and personal dynamics of the Trump White House with a focus on the peculiar relationship between Donald Trump and his "awkward" adviser, Stephen Miller. The article paints a picture of a workplace rife with backbiting and paranoia, a fitting scenario for a reality TV show but a disturbing lens through which to view the highest office in the United States.

Trump's Gossip Mill: Trump, described with a razor-sharp wit as a “gossipy Mean Girl with a nuclear arsenal,” reportedly hasn't held back in sharing his less-than-flattering opinions on Miller. Despite the public facade of unity and strength often showcased by administrations, it's abundantly clear that the personal dynamics within Trump's team were more akin to those found in a snake pit rather than a cohesive unit working towards the betterment of the country. The detail that Trump has commented on Miller’s “intense, awkward, and at times off-putting demeanor” behind his back, as per two sources with direct knowledge, speaks volumes about the toxic work environment and the character of those at its helm.

Miller’s Value: Despite Trump's personal insults and the apparent awareness of Miller's peculiarities, the article notes that Miller is still seen as valuable to Trump. Described as a "useful battering ram," Miller embodies the hardline policy enforcer for Trump's agenda, raising the question: Is effectiveness in advancing a divisive agenda the only measure of an adviser's worth? The comparison to Roy Cohn, infamous for his role as Senator Joseph McCarthy's chief counsel during the anti-Communist Senate hearings in the 1950s, is chilling. It underscores the lengths to which Trump is willing to go in finding loyalists who will push his policies, regardless of their methods or the divisiveness they sow.

White House Denial: The White House's response, through Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt, paints a starkly different picture, emphasizing respect, loyalty, and leadership qualities in Miller that Trump allegedly admires. This statement, coupled with a list of testimonials resembling a "MAGA LinkedIn endorsements section," attempts to brush off the reports as mere gossip. However, the attempt feels more like a desperate PR move rather than a genuine dismissal of the concerns raised by the article.

Conclusion: This latest report from Rolling Stone not only sheds light on the personal dynamics within the Trump administration but also serves as a reminder of the disturbing reality that personal vendettas, gossip, and backstabbing were seemingly just as much a part of the daily operations as policy-making. The implications of such an environment on the governance of the country are profound. The fact that Miller, despite being viewed as "awkward" and "off-putting," remained a trusted adviser due to his utility in pushing a divisive agenda, speaks to the prioritization of loyalty and ideological purity over competence and unity. This article serves as a cautionary tale not just about the individuals it describes but about the dangers of leadership that values loyalty and effectiveness in enforcing a particular agenda above all else.
