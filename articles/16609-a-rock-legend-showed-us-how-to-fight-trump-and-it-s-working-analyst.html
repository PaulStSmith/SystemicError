A rock legend showed us how to fight Trump — and it's working: analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 25, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real Resistance: Power, Performance, and Political Engagement in the Age of Celebrity

The Real Resistance: Power, Performance, and Political Engagement in the Age of Celebrity

Celebrity Power vs. Institutional Authority

Bruce Springsteen’s latest tour has been characterized not just as a series of concerts but as a vehement act of political resistance, as reported by Steven Greenhouse in the Guardian. Springsteen uses his platform to directly confront and criticize Donald Trump and his administration. Unlike many celebrities who risk alienating their audience or succumbing to corporate pressures, Springsteen, with his long-standing credibility and financial independence, faces only the threat of increased personal danger in the form of death threats. This positions him uniquely among entertainers who attempt to leverage their influence against entrenched political figures.

The Mechanism of Springsteen’s Message

Springsteen’s approach to activism is described as engaging rather than alienating; he communicates with his audience as equals, fostering a collective spirit of resistance. This method of interaction is crucial—it’s where Springsteen’s power intersects effectively with political discourse. His concerts are framed as rallying points, not just entertainment, blending classic rock with pointed anti-Trump rhetoric. This strategy not only amplifies his message but also solidifies his role as a “leader of the resistance,” as Greenhouse notes.

The Contrast with Traditional Political Actors

In an era where many feel disillusioned with traditional political figures, Springsteen’s role fills a void. His direct, unfiltered criticism of Trump and his administration, alongside his calls for collective action, resonate in a way that the calculated statements of many elected officials do not. This highlights a significant shift where individuals outside the conventional political sphere reframe the narrative and engage public sentiment more robustly than traditional political mechanisms seem capable of.

The Limitations of Celebrity Activism

While Springsteen’s efforts are commendable and stir significant public sentiment, they also underscore the limitations of relying on celebrity activism to effect political change. Despite his extensive reach and the deep connection with his audience, Springsteen, alone, cannot enact policy or directly alter political structures. His concerts raise awareness and inspire action, but the systemic change needed to address the issues he highlights—such as corruption, racism, and policy brutality—requires sustained, organized political engagement beyond the concert hall.

Expanding the Battlefield

The call by Greenhouse for Springsteen to host free, large-scale concerts is an intriguing one, suggesting a strategy to transform celebrity political engagement from episodic events into broader, more inclusive movements. This could potentially amplify the impact of Springsteen’s resistance, turning it into a more sustained, visible force akin to historical movements for civil rights or anti-war protests.

Conclusion: The Role of Public Figures in Modern Politics

Springsteen’s blend of entertainment and activism offers a blueprint for how celebrities can effectively engage in political discourse without falling into the traps of alienation or insignificance. However, it also serves as a reminder of the need for these efforts to be part of larger, structured movements that leverage institutional power effectively. Celebrity voices like Springsteen can ignite and inspire, but real change is a comprehensive chorus that requires persistent and organized public engagement. The challenge remains for both public figures and the general populace to transform the momentary applause of a concert into the lasting change of a movement.
