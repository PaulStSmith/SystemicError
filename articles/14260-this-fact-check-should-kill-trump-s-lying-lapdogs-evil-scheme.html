One of my purposes in sending you this daily letter is to give you the truth about an important issue that Trump and his lapdogs in Congress are demagoguing — so you can spread the truth.

Right now, the Senate is taking up Trump’s “Big Beautiful budget bill” (really a Big Bad Ugly Bill) that just emerged from the House.

If enacted, it would be the largest redistribution of income in the nation’s history — from the poor and working class to the rich and super-rich.

How? The tax cut mainly benefits the wealthy. A major source of funding is at least $715 billion of cuts in health care spending, mostly from Medicaid.

It also contains a poison pill that would remove the power of federal courts to hold officials in contempt of court — fining or imprisoning them if they fail to follow court orders. As the courts push back against Trump, this is a critical power.

And the bill cuts Medicaid spending by requiring Medicaid recipients to work.

Republicans are spreading lies about this work requirement.

Here are the facts you need to know — and share:

1. 64 percent of adult Medicaid recipients already work.

Many recipients work in jobs that don’t typically offer health insurance and pay little — which makes Medicaid vital. These people aren’t freeloaders mooching off the system, as Republicans claim. They’re barely scraping by.

2. Adults on Medicaid who aren’t working have good reasons not to.

— 12% are primary caregivers.

— 10% have an illness or disability.

— 7 % are attending school.

3. So, 93 percent of all Medicaid recipients either already working or having good reason not to.

The entire work requirement would affect 7 percent at most. In reality, a work requirement would cause many more who are eligible to lose their Medicaid coverage. The current estimate is at least 8.6 million people.

4. The work requirement kicks eligible people Medicaid because of its burdensome and confusing reporting requirements.

It’s not really meant to put people to work. It’s a shady way of kicking people off Medicaid to fund tax cuts mainly for the wealthy.

In Arkansas, which tried a work requirement for Medicaid, more than 18,000 people who were eligible lost coverage mainly because of the paperwork reporting hoops they had to jump through.

5. When Arkansas enacted work requirements, there was no significant change in employment rates.

Because, again, Medicaid recipients already have high rates of employment to begin with.

6. If Republicans really want to put people to work, they’d make it easier to get Medicaid — not harder.

After Ohio expanded Medicaid, enrollees had an easier time finding and holding down a job.

Access to healthcare means people can manage chronic conditions, afford medication, or receive mental health treatment — all of which helps people keep their jobs.

Republicans are spouting lies about a work requirement for Medicaid because they’re really trying to push eligible people off it — to help finance their big tax cut mainly for the rich.

Senate Republicans can afford to lose only three Republican votes. Otherwise, the Big Bad Ugly Bill is dead. Please share these facts.

Our Analysis:

Trump's "Big Beautiful Budget Bill": A Masterclass in Misdirection and Malice

In the latest spectacle of political theatre, we're presented with what's been dubbed Trump’s “Big Beautiful budget bill,” a title so dripping with irony it would be comical if the stakes weren't disastrously high. Robert Reich, a voice of reason amidst the cacophony of political spin, slices through the rhetoric to reveal the bill for what it truly is: a Big Bad Ugly Bill designed to orchestrate the largest redistribution of income in the nation’s history. But who stands to benefit? Spoiler alert: not the poor and working class.

The Rich Get Richer on the Backs of the Vulnerable

At the heart of this legislative monstrosity is a tax cut that predominantly benefits the wealthy, a move as predictable as it is infuriating. Funding this largesse for the rich? A staggering $715 billion in cuts to healthcare spending, primarily from Medicaid. It's a classic case of robbing Peter to pay Paul, except Peter is the working class struggling to make ends meet, and Paul is the super-rich lighting cigars with hundred-dollar bills.

A Poison Pill to Undermine the Judiciary

The bill contains a particularly nefarious provision that would strip federal courts of the power to hold officials in contempt for failing to follow court orders. This isn't just a minor procedural change; it's a direct assault on the judiciary's ability to check the executive branch. In an era where the courts are one of the few institutions pushing back against Trump's overreach, this poison pill could have catastrophic consequences for the rule of law.

The Myth of the Medicaid Moocher

Republicans, in their infinite wisdom, have decided that Medicaid recipients must work to receive their benefits. It's a classic straw man argument, painting recipients as freeloaders living off the government's dime. But Reich cuts through this fiction with cold, hard facts: 64 percent of adult Medicaid recipients are already working, often in jobs that don’t offer health insurance. Another 29 percent are either primary caregivers, ill, disabled, or students. That leaves a measly 7 percent who might be affected by the work requirement, shattering the myth of widespread Medicaid mooching.

Arkansas: A Cautionary Tale

Need evidence of how misguided the work requirement is? Look no further than Arkansas, where such a policy led to over 18,000 eligible people losing their coverage, not because they weren't working, but because they couldn't navigate the bureaucratic labyrinth of paperwork. The kicker? This Kafkaesque policy didn't even achieve its stated goal of boosting employment among Medicaid recipients.

The Bottom Line

What we're witnessing is a brazen attempt to kick people off Medicaid under the guise of promoting employment, all to fund tax cuts for the affluent. The irony is palpable: if Republicans truly wanted to help people work, they'd make it easier to access healthcare, not harder. Healthcare access is a proven factor in helping people find and maintain employment, a fact conveniently ignored by those championing the bill.

This "Big Bad Ugly Bill" is not just a policy proposal; it's a moral failing. It's a testament to a government more interested in lining the pockets of the wealthy than in safeguarding the health and wellbeing of its most vulnerable citizens. Reich's analysis isn't just a call to action; it's a wake-up call to anyone still under the illusion that this administration has the interests of the working class at heart.

Senate Republicans teeter on the brink of passing this legislative abomination. If they succeed, it won't just be a defeat for healthcare; it'll be a resounding victory for inequality and injustice. Share these facts. The truth is our most powerful weapon against the lies and spin trying to disguise this bill for anything other than what it is: a declaration of war on the working class.
