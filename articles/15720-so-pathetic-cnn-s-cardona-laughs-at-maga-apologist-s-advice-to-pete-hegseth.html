'So pathetic': CNN's Cardona laughs at MAGA apologist's advice to Pete Hegseth

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 24, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

CNN

CNN's Maria Cardona laughed out loud at advice offered to embattled Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth by conservative commentator Shermichael Singleton.

The Signalgate scandal flared up again after new revelations about a second group chat involving war plans in a non-secure setting, and Singleton told "CNN News Central" that national security adviser Mike Waltz had already accepted responsibility for adding a journalist to the first group chat, and he urged Hegseth to do the same for inviting his wife, brother and personal attorney to another.

"If the acknowledgment is one step, how are you going to clean this up?" Singleton said. "How are you going to have efficiencies in terms of communications of classified information about the Signal group chat with his wife and his brother?"

Host John Berman asked whether Hegseth has ever acknowledged those group chats had been a mistake, and Singleton dismissed the question as ridiculous.

"Come on, John, I think, you know the answer to that question," Singleton said. "My point is, though, that he should say, 'Hey, look, in the past I made some errors here moving forward, this is how I have communicated about highly classified information with my staff or my advisors,' and I think that would, for the most part, settle this, politically speaking. I think the president is probably reluctant to make a decision at this point because he did spend so much political capital getting Pete Hegseth confirmed. If you recall, we reported on this exclusively, it was not an easy process for the secretary, and so I think if you're Trump, you're looking at this trying to engage – 'Well, if I do have to make a decision, do I want to make it within the first 100 days, or do I want to give the secretary an opportunity to sort of clean this up and see what happens moving forward?'"

"Again, this occurred around the same time as the previous Signal chat," Singleton added. "We'll see if the secretary has improved his communications moving forward. I would hope that he has."

Berman piggybacked off Singleton's remarks to ask Cardona whether she believes Hegseth has improved his communications, and she tilted her head back and laughed.

"Absolutely not, John," Cardona said. "Look, it is so pathetic for Republicans to be coming out and, No. 1, trying to defend him, and I'm glad Shermichael is not doing that, but we all know that no one is going to do what Shermichael says they should – and they should. But here's the issue, how much more possibly criminal, but certainly dangerous, chaotic incompetence is this White House going to expose the country to? I mean, the job that Hegseth has is one of the most important in national security terms, and so he is not only diminishing the U.S. brand, he is making us less safe on the global stage."

"He is completely turning the Defense Department into a chaotic snake pit, and we have heard how everything has been turned upside-down, and that's why everyone is now, you know, key people are resigning and talking to the media about it," Cardona added. "They're not leaking because they're talking to them on the record about how incompetent and how crazy things are at the Defense Department, and so how much longer is this going to take for the president and the administration to understand that this is making us less safe?"

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Signalgate Scandal Through a Prism of Incompetence and Political Calculus

In the latest circus act that has become the norm in Washington, we find ourselves dissecting the Signalgate scandal, a story that unfolds with the drama and intrigue of a poorly written spy novel, yet, unfortunately, is all too real. The scandal, involving Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and a series of jaw-droppingly irresponsible group chats about war plans in non-secure settings, has once again flared up. This time, thanks to new revelations about a second group chat that included Hegseth's wife, brother, and personal attorney. Yes, you read that right. The kind of characters you'd expect in a family group chat discussing holiday plans, not sensitive national security matters.

Maria Cardona's Laughter: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

Maria Cardona's reaction to conservative commentator Shermichael Singleton's advice to Hegseth isn't just a moment of levity; it's a damning indictment of the current state of political discourse and accountability. Singleton's suggestion that Hegseth simply acknowledge his mistake and outline his future communication strategies regarding classified information is met with disbelief and laughter. And rightfully so. The idea that a mere acknowledgment could clean up this mess is as laughable as it is naive.

The Singleton Perspective: Too Little, Too Late

Singleton's advice, while well-intentioned, completely misses the mark. His proposition that an admission of error could politically "settle this" underestimates the gravity of the situation. We're not talking about a minor faux pas; these are potentially criminal acts of negligence that compromise national security. Moreover, his assertion that President Trump's reluctance to act is based on the political capital spent on Hegseth's confirmation is a stark reminder of how political considerations often trump (pun intended) national interest.

The Hegseth Debacle: A Microcosm of a Larger Problem

Hegseth's actions, and the subsequent defense or dismissal by political allies, are emblematic of a broader issue plaguing American politics: the prioritization of loyalty over competence and security. The fact that such sensitive information was discussed in a setting as insecure as a Signal group chat is mind-boggling. Yet, what's even more astounding is the tepid response from those in power. Cardona's comments, scathing as they are, hit the nail on the head. Hegseth's incompetence has not only endangered national security but has also turned the Defense Department into a "chaotic snake pit."

The Real Victims: American Security and Global Standing

As Cardona eloquently points out, the real casualty in this ongoing saga of incompetence and chaos is America's security and its standing on the global stage. Hegseth's mishandling of sensitive information does more than just embarrass the nation; it actively diminishes its brand and makes it less safe. The resignations and the on-the-record talks with the media by key personnel are not just acts of defiance; they're desperate cries for accountability and competence from an administration that seems to have forsaken both.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

In the final analysis, the Signalgate scandal serves as a stark reminder of the importance of competence and accountability in government. The fact that such a scandal can unfold with what appears to be minimal repercussions for those involved is a damning indictment of the current state of affairs. The laughter of Maria Cardona is not just a moment of ridicule but a clarion call for a serious reevaluation of how national security matters are handled. It's high time for those in power to stop treating these issues with the seriousness they deserve, rather than as mere political inconveniences to be laughed off or dismissed.
