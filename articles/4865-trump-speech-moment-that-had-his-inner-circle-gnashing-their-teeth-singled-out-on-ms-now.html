According to Financial Times columnist Ed Luce, there was one moment during Donald Trump’s long and lie-filled State of the Union address that will cause his White House no small measure of problems as the president’s poll numbers are in freefall.

Appearing on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,“ the longtime journalist agreed with co-host Jonathan Lemire that Trump’s speech was “boring” but added that Trump’s comments about affordability was not what his inner circle wanted to hear coming out of his mouth.

“I don’t think, you know, this is effective campaigning, although it is campaigning, was him rolling the word affordability around in his mouth, as if it’s some very strange word that the Democrats have just learned, and that it isn’t really a serious issue at all,” he suggested to Lemire.

“I imagine Susie Wiles and others were gnashing their teeth as they watched that, because this was clearly supposed to be, at least in part, a speech that set up Republicans for the midterms as serious on questions of affordability,” he added. “And he didn’t really do that. He told Americans that their prices are all falling. Don’t believe your own lying eyes, etc. He at one point said that drug prices have dropped 100 percent, which would mean they’re now zero.”

“So you know, I can’t I can’t get excited about this speech. But I’m and I guess the fact that we’re so used to these torrents of lies and the whoppingness of these lies and that it’s actually boring, does say something about us and about this president,” he wryly admitted.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Missteps in the Realm of “Affordability”

In a recent commentary that slices through the facade of political rhetoric with the precision of a scalpel, Ed Luce of Financial Times cast a scathing spotlight on a particularly jarring moment from Donald Trump’s latest State of the Union address. Described as both long and “lie-filled”, Trump’s presentation has evidently done more to undermine his credibility than to bolster it, especially amidst plummeting poll numbers.

The Affordability Gaffe

Luce’s analysis, shared on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” zeroes in on Trump’s bewildering dance around the concept of affordability. Trump’s attempt to trivialize affordability as a newfound term in the Democratic lexicon, rather than a pressing national concern, is not just political malpractice; it’s a glaring misread of the room. As Luce aptly points out, Trump’s inner circle, presumably including figures like Susie Wiles, likely found themselves in a state of exasperation as Trump fumbled what was meant to be a key message in setting up Republicans for the midterms.

The Economic Fantasy Land

Trump’s claims about falling prices and the absurd declaration that drug prices have dropped “100 percent” push the boundaries of believability into the realm of farce. It’s one thing to employ hyperbole; it’s another to suggest a reality so detached from the everyday experiences of American citizens. To assert that drug prices have plummeted to zero is to insult the intelligence of the American public, who, despite what Trump implies, are not suffering en masse from visual hallucinations when they check out at the pharmacy.

The Boredom of Lies

What Luce identifies, and what is perhaps most alarming, is the normalization of deceit. The fact that Trump’s speech, brimming with falsehoods, could be considered “boring” is a testament to the desensitization of the audience to political lies. This desensitization does not speak to a flaw in the public’s character but rather to the relentless onslaught of falsehoods that have come to characterize Trump’s discourse.

Conclusion

The critique here is not just about a misstep on an issue like affordability; it’s about a pervasive pattern of misinformation that seeks to warp reality to fit a preferred narrative. Trump’s State of the Union address, as dissected by Luce, was not just a missed opportunity but a microcosm of a larger problem: a disconnection from the tangible needs and truths of the American populace. As poll numbers suggest, this strategy is not just ethically bankrupt—it’s politically untenable.
