'Resign in disgrace!' Stephen Miller has conniption as he hurls insurrection rage at Dems

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 21, 20255 min read

White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller looks on at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) CEO summit in Gyeongju, South Korea on Oct. 29, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Stephen Miller erupted in fury against six lawmakers who urged U.S. military and intelligence personnel to refuse potentially illegal presidential commands, accusing them of stoking “insurrection” and demanding they “resign in disgrace.”

Speaking on Fox News, the White House chief of staff described the lawmakers’ video as a “general call for rebellion… from the CIA and the armed services.” He declared emphatically, “It is insurrection, plainly, directly, without question.”

The six lawmakers—who include Senators Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) and Mark Kelly (D-AZ) — released a video reminding service members that their allegiance is to the Constitution, not the president. In the video, they repeatedly stressed, “Our laws are clear” and “You can refuse illegal orders.”

Miller didn’t mince words, stating, “There is nothing graver… than encouraging, urging, directing members of the Armed Forces… to defy their president.”

The lawmakers fired back. Slotkin argued that reminding troops of their duty was “the law passed down from our founding fathers,” telling Miller to “buff up” on military code.

Kelly, a retired Navy captain, responded directly: “I got shot at serving our country in combat… I know the difference between defending our Constitution and insurrection.”

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche added fuel to the fire, calling the video “alarming” and a “disgusting and inappropriate display of supposed leadership.” He dramatically claimed, “I felt like I was watching a propaganda video by one of our enemies trying to recruit the military to become spies.”

Miller escalated his rhetoric, claiming Democrats “don’t believe in systems… don’t believe in rules… don’t believe in laws” and had embraced “Third World” tactics.

The confrontation highlights ongoing tensions surrounding potential presidential overreach and military accountability, with both sides claiming to defend constitutional principles.

Our Analysis:

Insurrection or Instruction? The Melodrama of Military Loyalty and Legal Orders

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the theater of American politics, Stephen Miller, the White House chief of staff, has launched a verbal barrage against six lawmakers for their audacity to remind U.S. military and intelligence personnel of a fundamental principle: their allegiance is to the Constitution, not to the president. This incident, as reported, reveals not just a clash of ideologies but a fundamental misunderstanding—or, more cynically, a willful misrepresentation—of loyalty, legality, and leadership.

Miller’s reaction, characterized by fury and accusations of “insurrection,” is a masterclass in hyperbole. Speaking on Fox News, he described the lawmakers’ video as a “general call for rebellion,” a statement so loaded with exaggeration that it would be comical if it weren’t so dangerously misleading. The lawmakers in question, including Senators Elissa Slotkin (D-MI) and Mark Kelly (D-AZ), made a video that did nothing more revolutionary than remind service members of their oath to the Constitution—an oath that includes refusing illegal orders.

The charge of insurrection is, in itself, a gross mischaracterization of what the senators did. They did not call for an overthrow of the government or a refusal to follow lawful commands; they called for adherence to the law. Miller’s rhetoric is not just incorrect; it’s irresponsibly inflammatory.

The Law and Military Orders

The crux of the matter lies in the distinction between lawful and unlawful orders. The U.S. military is built on a hierarchy of command, but it is also built on the principle that unlawful orders—those that violate the Constitution or other laws—should not be followed. This is not a controversial stance; it is a cornerstone of military justice and accountability. The lawmakers’ video, far from inciting insurrection, was a reminder of this principle.

Responses from the Accused

Senators Slotkin and Kelly, both with backgrounds that lend credibility to their understanding of military codes, fired back with dignity and authority. Slotkin reminded Miller to “buff up” on military code, while Kelly, a retired Navy captain and combat veteran, underscored his firsthand experience in defending the Constitution against actual insurrection. Their responses highlight not only the legitimacy of their initial message but also the absurdity of Miller’s accusations.

Further Flames from Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche

Adding to the melodrama, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche described the video as akin to enemy propaganda, a characterization so far removed from reality that it borders on parody. To compare a reminder of legal and constitutional duties to the tactics of America’s adversaries is not just hyperbolic; it’s an insult to the intelligence and integrity of the U.S. military and its personnel.

Miller’s Escalation

Miller didn’t stop at accusations of insurrection; he went on to claim that Democrats “don’t believe in systems… don’t believe in rules… don’t believe in laws,” and accused them of embracing “Third World” tactics. This escalation of rhetoric from the absurd to the offensive demonstrates a troubling trend in political discourse: when faced with a disagreement, escalate rather than engage.

The Real Issue at Hand

This confrontation is not about insurrection. It is about whether the U.S. military serves the Constitution or the president. It is about reminding those who serve that their loyalty to the law supersedes their loyalty to an individual. The real insurrection is not in the actions of these lawmakers but in the attempts to mischaracterize their defense of constitutional principles as a call to rebellion.

In a political landscape increasingly characterized by division and hyperbole, this incident stands out as a particularly egregious example of how far some will go to twist the narrative. The lawmakers’ reminder was not only appropriate but necessary in a time when the lines between lawful authority and personal loyalty are being blurred by those in power. It’s a sad day for American democracy when the reminder of a foundational principle is met with accusations of treason.

In sum, the furor unleashed by Stephen Miller against these lawmakers reveals not a defense of constitutional order but a profound misunderstanding of—or disregard for—the very principles that underpin it. It is a reminder that in the current political climate, the battle is not just over policies, but over the truth itself.
