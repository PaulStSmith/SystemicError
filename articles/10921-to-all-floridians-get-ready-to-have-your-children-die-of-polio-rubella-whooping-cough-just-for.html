SYSTEMIC ERROR / COMMENTARY

To all Floridians: get ready to have your children die of polio, rubella, whooping cough. Just for starters.

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Florida’s surgeon general Joseph Ladapo has once again dragged the state into a grotesque parody of public health. Standing shoulder to shoulder with Gov. Ron DeSantis, he announced the end of vaccine mandates in Florida. And in case the recklessness wasn’t obvious enough, he equated mandates with “slavery.”

That’s not just reckless. That’s lethal.

A calculated assault on public health

Vaccine mandates are not some authoritarian fever dream — they are why children don’t routinely die of smallpox, why polio was eradicated in the United States, why measles was nearly wiped out before anti-vaxxers resurrected it. They are why kids can grow up without iron lungs, blindness, or lifelong complications from diseases once considered inevitable.

Joseph Ladapo knows this. He’s not some small-town crank shouting outside a pharmacy. He has both an MD and PhD from Harvard. But he has chosen to serve as a political prop for Ron DeSantis’s war on science, wrapping deadly policy choices in the hollow flag of “freedom.”

To call vaccine requirements “slavery” is to spit in the face of Black history, trivializing centuries of actual enslavement to score cheap political points. It’s rhetorical gasoline on the fire of America’s worst impulses: grievance politics, conspiracy thinking, and disdain for expertise.

The measles outbreak that exposed the rot

If Ladapo’s words weren’t enough, his record speaks louder. In March 2024, Florida saw a measles outbreak in eastern counties. Measles — one of the most contagious viruses known to humanity, capable of killing one to two children out of every thousand cases. Ladapo’s response? He told parents of unvaccinated kids to “use their best judgment” on whether to send them to school.

That wasn’t guidance. That was dereliction of duty. It was an open invitation for the virus to spread unchecked through classrooms, playgrounds, and homes.

Public health leadership is about protecting the vulnerable, not pandering to the paranoid. Ladapo chose politics over science — and children paid the price.

What’s at stake if mandates are gutted

Florida currently requires seven childhood vaccines for daycare and schools, including protection against polio, chickenpox, hepatitis B, measles, mumps, and rubella. These aren’t arbitrary requirements. They are the minimal firewall standing between children and mass outbreaks.

Without them, here’s what Floridians can expect:

  1. Polio: Once a scourge that left children in wheelchairs or iron lungs, eliminated in the U.S. by 1979. With declining vaccination, we’ve already seen poliovirus detected in New York wastewater as recently as 2022. Florida, with its large unvaccinated population, would be ripe for a comeback.

  2. Measles: Before vaccines, 400–500 Americans died every year from measles, mostly children. The virus is so contagious that one infected child can infect 90% of those unvaccinated around them. Florida already proved its vulnerability in 2024.

  3. Whooping cough (pertussis): Once routine and deadly, it kills infants too young to be fully vaccinated. Mandates keep herd immunity high enough to protect them. Remove that, and newborns become collateral damage.

  4. Rubella (German measles): Mild for most, but catastrophic for pregnant women — causing birth defects, deafness, heart disorders. Without vaccine requirements, congenital rubella syndrome could once again ruin lives.

This is not hypothetical. These are diseases that ravaged children within living memory. Vaccine mandates ended that nightmare. Ending the mandates will bring it back.

The cynical political theater

DeSantis and Ladapo didn’t make this announcement in isolation. They packaged it with a shiny new gimmick: the “Make America Healthy Again Commission,” a grotesque parody of public health policy.

Rather than being led by independent scientists, it will be chaired by Florida’s first lady Casey DeSantis and Lt. Gov. Jay Collins. Ladapo will sit on it too, alongside other political loyalists. The supposed mission? “Parental rights” and “promoting nutrition.” In reality, it’s yet another MAGA-branded platform for gutting real health protections while pretending to empower parents.

It’s the same cynical playbook we’ve seen with book bans, classroom censorship, and attacks on LGBTQ+ youth: weaponize “parental rights” as cover for dismantling public goods. The result is always the same — kids pay the price, while politicians reap the headlines.

Historical amnesia as public policy

The success of vaccines is so complete that people like Ladapo can now pretend the diseases they eradicated don’t matter. It’s the ultimate irony: public health victories are so invisible that opportunists can attack them without the public realizing the abyss we stand over.

Consider smallpox. It killed an estimated 300 million people in the 20th century alone before vaccines eradicated it in 1980. That victory required global coordination, mandates, and mass campaigns. Without them, smallpox would still stalk humanity.

Or polio. Parents once lived in terror of summer outbreaks, watching children paralyzed overnight. The March of Dimes raised money for research because the disease touched nearly every family. The polio vaccine, mandated in schools, ended that terror.

But Ladapo and DeSantis would toss that history aside for short-term political gain. They are betting that Floridians’ memories are too short and that ideology can erase science.

The broader MAGA war on science

This is not just about Florida. It’s part of a larger war on public health waged by the MAGA movement. From Trump’s bleach injections and hydroxychloroquine quackery to Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s anti-vax crusade at the federal level, the GOP has turned contempt for science into a badge of honor.

DeSantis, hungry for national relevance after his failed presidential run, is doubling down. By positioning Florida as the vanguard of “medical freedom,” he cements his MAGA credentials — at the cost of Floridians’ lives.

The human toll

When vaccines disappear, children die. That’s not rhetoric. That’s fact.

Every child who gasps for air with whooping cough, every infant born with congenital rubella syndrome, every teen who loses hearing from mumps — their suffering will be the direct consequence of Ladapo’s policies. Every outbreak will trace back to this moment, this press conference, this obscene declaration that vaccine mandates are “slavery.”

Parents who trusted the state to keep classrooms safe will find themselves facing preventable epidemics. And Florida’s hospitals, already strained, will shoulder the burden of diseases that had been banished for decades.

Conclusion: dangerous, cynical, and unforgivable

What Ladapo and DeSantis are doing is not freedom. It is negligence disguised as liberty. It is political ambition dressed up as parental rights. It is gambling with children’s lives to score points in the MAGA culture war.

The tragedy is that the victims will not be the politicians. It will be children too young to choose, parents misled by propaganda, and communities shattered by outbreaks.

Florida is on the brink of becoming ground zero for the return of preventable disease. That is not hyperbole. It is the logical consequence of stripping away the protections that built modern childhood.

When the first child dies of polio in Florida, when the first measles epidemic tears through unvaccinated classrooms, remember this day. Remember Joseph Ladapo’s words. Remember Ron DeSantis’s smirk. And remember that it didn’t have to be this way.

Because this isn’t freedom. It’s a death sentence.
