Republicans scramble to rescue security agency after gutting it to appease Trump: report

Based on an article by Robert Davis

They Hollowed Out the Fire Alarm, Then Acted Surprised by Smoke

Power Decided This

Republicans on Capitol Hill voted to gut the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency to satisfy Donald Trump, and now some of the same lawmakers are pretending to rediscover the value of basic national defense. The source story treats this as a change of heart. It is better read as what it is: a repair job after deliberate vandalism.

The Real Actor Was Trump

Trump made CISA a political target after Christopher Krebs said the 2020 election was among the most secure in American history. That fact did not matter to him. What mattered was punishment for an agency that did its job and refused to flatter his lie. Once Trump marked CISA as disloyal, Republican lawmakers followed the signal. They did not merely fail to defend the agency. They helped isolate it.

Sabotage, Then Buyer’s Remorse

The names matter. Andy Ogles and Morgan Luttrell voted to defund the agency, with Luttrell even backing a measure to block CISA from paying its director’s salary. Now those same Republicans are talking about rehiring staff and restoring funding. That is not institutional seriousness. That is a party trying to reverse a self-inflicted wound only after the consequences became impossible to ignore.

The Framing Is Too Polite

The article’s setup leans on the recent OpenAI incident and the Trump administration’s election rhetoric, as if external danger suddenly awakened Republican responsibility. That is a misdirection. The danger here is not confusion. It is governance by retaliation, where agencies are punished for telling the truth and then rediscovered only when the damage becomes politically visible.

Cybersecurity Was Never the Point

CISA “fell out of favor” because it collided with Trump’s ego and the Republican need to keep pace with his grievance machine. That is the pattern: dismantle the parts of government that constrain fraud, pressure, and chaos, then invoke security once the collapse threatens everyone, including the people who caused it. The same politicians who starved the agency now want credit for remembering it exists.

The Systemic Lesson

This is what authoritarian politics does in practice. It turns public institutions into loyalty tests, treats expertise as insubordination, and converts prevention into an afterthought. Republicans did not stumble into a cybersecurity problem. They helped create one by subordinating institutional defense to Trump’s personal vendetta. The eventual scramble to restore CISA is not a sign of wisdom. It is evidence that the governing class keeps learning the same lesson the hard way: break the shield first, then pretend to be shocked when the hits start landing.
