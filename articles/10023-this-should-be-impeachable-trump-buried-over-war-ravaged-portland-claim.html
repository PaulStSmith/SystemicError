'This should be impeachable': Trump buried over 'war-ravaged Portland' claim

President Donald Trump faced a wave of scrutiny Saturday after dubbing Portland, Oregon as “war-ravaged,” a characterization he cited to justify ordering a new military deployment to the city.

“Oh, okay, I get it now. YOU took a lot of Tylenol, didn’t you?” wrote X user “Nickie B,” a self-described critic of Trump and the MAGA movement, mocking Trump’s recent announcement linking, with disputed evidence, autism to Tylenol. “Now I get why we should stay away from it. Thanks for your attention to this matter!”

Trump has already deployed the military to patrol the streets of Washington, D.C., and has threatened on numerous occasions to do the same to Chicago, Illinois, and doing so in an ominous manner alongside an artificial intelligence-made image depicting the U.S. military invading the Illinois capital.

Trump’s announcement came in the wake of threats from Portland city officials threatening to evict federal immigration officials from a facility over alleged permit violations, which itself was followed by a sudden, unexplained “influx of federal agents” in the city.

Now that Trump has actually pulled the trigger and announced a military deployment to Portland, while also declaring the city to be “war-ravaged,” critics quickly pounced on the president’s rhetoric.

“Portland saw 17 homicides in the first half of 2025, a decline of 51%,” wrote Jay Bookman, journalist and author, in a post on X to his more than 20,000 followers.

“Yet Trump and the ‘secretary of war’ are sending the U.S. military into the city, authorizing American troops to use ‘Full Force’ against American civilians. What are we doing here, people?”

Some even argued that Trump’s characterization of Portland as “war-ravaged” was an impeachable offense, including Mehdi Hasan, journalist and former MSNBC host.

“If ever there was a time not to normalize Trump’s authoritarian fever dreams, this is it,” Hasan wrote to his more than 1.8 followers on X.

“This should be impeachable. ‘War ravaged’ Portland? He’s insane – and insanely power hungry. The script is set – call an imaginary group a terror group and then send in the troops.”

The city of Portland has violent crime rates higher than the national average, though the figures have declined dramatically in recent years.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s “War-Ravaged” Portland and the Theater of the Absurd

In what can only be described as a grotesque display of political theater, President Donald Trump has once again blustered his way into the headlines, this time declaring Portland, Oregon, a city he describes as “war-ravaged.” This assertion, aside from being blatantly disconnected from reality, serves as a pretext for an unprecedented military deployment within the United States. The absurdity of this scenario is compounded by Trump’s recent bizarre linkage of autism to Tylenol, a statement mocked by critics like Nickie B, who sarcastically thanked Trump for his “attention to this matter.”

The Facts vs. The Fiction

Let’s dissect the fiction: Trump’s portrayal of Portland as “war-ravaged” is not just an exaggeration; it’s a fabrication. The city, as pointed out by journalist Jay Bookman, has seen a significant decline in homicides, with a 51% drop in the first half of 2025. Yet, Trump and his so-called “secretary of war” have deemed it necessary to send U.S. military forces to patrol its streets, with authorization to use “Full Force” against American civilians. This move is not only disproportionate but downright dystopian.

Now, for the facts: Portland’s violent crime rates, though higher than the national average, have been on a steady decline, especially in recent years. Following a pandemic-era spike, homicide rates began to fall in 2023, with further reductions observed in subsequent years. The narrative of a city under siege by violence is not supported by data; it is a concoction brewed for political gain.

The Impeachment Worthy Hyperbole

Mehdi Hasan, a journalist and former MSNBC host, has argued that Trump’s characterization of Portland as “war-ravaged” and his subsequent military posturing should be considered impeachable offenses. This stance underscores the severity of Trump’s actions, which go beyond mere political hyperbole into the realm of authoritarian fever dreams. By inventing crises and responding with disproportionate force, Trump is not only misusing his power but also setting a dangerous precedent.

Trump’s strategy appears to be a sinister script: label an imaginary group as a terror threat, then send in the troops. This tactic is not only deceitful but also dangerously escalates tensions and sews discord within American cities. The deployment of military forces against American civilians based on fabricated pretexts is a stark violation of the principles upon which the United States was founded.

Conclusion

In sum, Trump’s characterization of Portland as “war-ravaged” is not just a gross misrepresentation; it’s a deliberate manipulation of public perception to justify authoritarian measures. The decline in violent crime rates in Portland tells a story starkly different from the narrative spun by the Trump administration. This latest episode is a chilling reminder of the lengths to which Trump will go in his quest for power, even at the expense of truth, justice, and the American way.

The situation in Portland is not a call to arms; it’s a call to sanity. It’s time to reject the theater of the absurd orchestrated by Trump and demand accountability. The stakes are too high, and the cost of inaction is too great.
