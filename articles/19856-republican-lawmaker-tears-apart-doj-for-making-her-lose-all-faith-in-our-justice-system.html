Republican lawmaker tears apart DOJ for making her lose 'all faith in our justice system'

U.S. Rep. Nancy Mace at the 2025 Young Women’s Leadership Summit in Grapevine, Texas on June 14, 2025 (Gage Skidmore/Flickr)

While the Justice Department has focused largely on pursuing charged against President Donald Trump’s perceived enemies, those who participated in horrific crimes are getting a pass, one GOP lawmaker said to CNN.

Speaking to Manu Raju on Wednesday, Rep. Nancy Mace (R-S.C.), a sexual assault victim, said the DOJ has made “no effort, not at all to prosecute predators in the [Jeffrey] Epstein cases.” As a result, she said, “I personally have lost all faith in our justice system.”

She called it absurd that there would be thousands of victims while only one accomplice sits in jail.

“Despite the memo released by DOJ last night, not all the Epstein files have been released. And the excuses provided for not releasing all the files will not hold up in a court of law. This isn’t going away until people go to jail,” Mace wrote on X, Sunday morning.

“You’re either on the side of protecting vulnerable children or you’re on the side of protecting predators who prey on vulnerable children. There is no middle ground. You’re either with the kids or you’re against them. I know where I stand, and I’ll stay standing until every predator is held accountable,” she continued.

The message comes after a Saturday night statement from Mace, who is running for governor in her state, claiming the “Work Product Privilege” wouldn’t save the DOJ from having to release further information in the files. It was reported that there were at least 6 million pages. And less than 4 million has been released thus far.

She noted that the DOJ also claims in its report that “no records were withheld or redacted on the basis of embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity, including to any government official, public figure, or foreign dignitary.”

“Then explain this: an internal FBI email from March 17, 2025, shows agents requesting ‘clear and specific guidance’ on redacting photographs depicting ‘former U.S. Presidents, Secretary of State, and other celebrities’ from the Epstein files. So which is it?” Mace asked.

“You didn’t redact to protect the powerful, or you needed specific guidance on how to do exactly that? Your government is withholding information and files whether it’s SDNY, FBI, CBP, CIA, DOJ or elsewhere. Also — they’re missing names on the list disclosed this evening. Stay tuned,” she closed.

Trump ally, Gen. Mike Flynn, noted that the discussion over the files is “not going away.”

One far-right activist told Mace to name the names on the floor of Congress as others have pledged to do.

Mace later posted, “Epstein will go down as one of the greatest cover-ups in American history.

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Litany of Systemic Failures

In a recent declaration that has sparked both controversy and a dire need for introspection within the American justice system, Rep. Nancy Mace (R-S.C.) has lambasted the Department of Justice (DOJ) for its glaring inaction on the Jeffrey Epstein cases. Her criticisms, voiced in an interview with CNN’s Manu Raju and further echoed across her social media, cast a searing spotlight on the systemic deficiencies plaguing our justice system. This isn’t merely a failure; it’s a catastrophic dereliction of duty.

The DOJ’s Selective Blindness

Rep. Mace’s allegations point to a disturbing trend within the DOJ: a perceived prioritization of political adversaries over the pursuit of justice for victims of heinous crimes. The assertion that the DOJ has made “no effort, not at all to prosecute predators“ in the Epstein saga is not just troubling; it’s a damning indictment of a system that appears broken at its core. The fact that amidst thousands of victims, only one accomplice finds themselves behind bars is not just an anomaly; it’s a systemic failure.

The Unsettling Reality of Inaction

The figures mentioned in the discourse are alarming—6 million pages of evidence, with less than 4 million disclosed. The DOJ’s claims of non-withholding based on “embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity” are directly contradicted by internal communications within the FBI, seeking guidance on redacting photographs of high-profile individuals. This dichotomy between public statements and internal actions raises significant questions about transparency, accountability, and the protection of the powerful at the expense of justice for the vulnerable.

Between Rhetoric and Reality

Rep. Mace’s stance, articulated through a binary of protecting children versus protecting predators, while rhetorically compelling, simplifies a complex issue. The implicit accusation here is not just of inaction but of complicity—a suggestion that the government, through various agencies, is actively choosing to shield the powerful. This is a grave accusation that requires more than just impassioned declarations; it necessitates thorough investigation, transparency, and accountability.

The Political Underpinnings

It’s impossible to ignore the political context within which Rep. Mace’s statements are made. As a GOP lawmaker and a gubernatorial candidate, her words carry weight but also a potential political agenda. The mention of Trump allies and far-right activists in the discourse around the Epstein files injects a partisan element into what is fundamentally a question of justice and moral integrity. This does not invalidate her concerns but necessitates a critical examination of motives, timing, and the broader political implications.

Conclusion: A Clarion Call for Accountability

Rep. Mace’s declaration that the Epstein saga will be remembered as “one of the greatest cover-ups in American history” is a stark reminder of the work that lies ahead. This is not a matter that can be relegated to the annals of conspiracy theories or partisan bickering. It’s a clarion call for a systemic overhaul—a demand for transparency, accountability, and a justice system that prioritizes the welfare of the vulnerable over the protection of the powerful. The DOJ, along with every implicated agency, must take decisive action to restore faith in an institution that appears, to many, to be irrevocably broken. Failure to do so not only undermines the very foundation of justice but signals a troubling acquiescence to the erosion of moral and ethical standards at the highest levels of power.
