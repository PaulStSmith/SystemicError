During an appearance on MS NOW on Friday morning, a former Donald Trump appointee to the State Department expressed alarm at the president’s collapse when it comes to addressing voters’ concerns and suggested age maybe taking it’s toll.

Speaking with “Way Too Early” host Ali Vitali, Matthew Bartlett, now a GOP strategist, called the atmosphere for the GOP dangerous with the midterms right around the corner while suggesting the party should have anticipated the president’s decline.

With host Vitali pointing that it is “Striking to see Trump losing out on an issue that he has had a vice-like grip on,” the very animated Bartlett replied, “Listen, it is absolutely brutal economically to have a family, live , work, eat, breathe.“

“American groceries, healthcare, and again, the same powerful notion in American politics, affordability, that returned Republicans to power are the ones plaguing them now!”

“Again, the president — if we only had this test case scenario of an elderly president running around the country telling Americans that everything is fine — if we knew how that went,” he added.

“So, yeah, I think it is going to continue to be challenging,” he continued. “Affordability is a hard thing to solve. In the first administration, I’ll say this, we deregulated, we cut taxes, and everybody was getting jobs. You got out of jail, we got a job for you. Now what do we have? Tariffs.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of GOP Alarm Bells and the Age-Old Issue of Age

In an almost comically predictable turn of events, a former Donald Trump appointee, Matthew Bartlett, now wearing the hat of a GOP strategist, vocalized his concerns about the party’s direction and, notably, the president’s age, on MS NOW. His remarks, aired during a conversation with host Ali Vitali, serve as a prime example of the GOP’s late-to-the-party realization and their peculiar knack for acknowledging issues only when the water is already boiling over.

The GOP’s Dangerous Atmosphere

Bartlett’s comments underscore a sense of urgency and foreboding within the GOP as the midterms loom large. Describing the atmosphere as dangerous, he suggests that the party should have seen this coming. However, this assertion raises eyebrows — not for its revelation, but for its blatant obviousness. The GOP’s sudden alarm at the president’s decline, presumably both in popularity and capability, reeks of reactive strategy rather than proactive planning. The party, known for its staunch defense of Trump, seems to be caught in a self-made whirlwind of denial and belated acknowledgment.

Trump’s Grip Loosens

The focal point of Bartlett’s alarm is the erosion of Trump’s once vice-like grip on key voter concerns, particularly the economy. This shift is not so much a revelation as it is a testament to the transient nature of political capital. Bartlett’s animated lamentations about the economic hardships of American life under the current GOP landscape serve as a belated echo of what many Americans have voiced for years. The irony of his shock is palpable, considering these issues have long been at the forefront of American discourse, only seemingly invisible to those within the GOP bubble.

Age as a Convenient Scapegoat

Bartlett’s insinuations about the president’s age impacting his performance are a convenient redirection of the issue at hand. While age may indeed be a factor in any individual’s capacity to serve effectively, the broader issue lies in the party’s policies and their impact on the very affordability crisis Bartlett bemoans. The mention of deregulation, tax cuts, and job creation during Trump’s first administration is touted as a panacea that once worked, ignoring the deeper, systemic issues that continue to plague American families.

The Tariff Quandary

Ending on a note of what seems like regret, Bartlett points to tariffs as a significant misstep. This acknowledgment, while valid, is a drop in the ocean of the GOP’s broader economic miscalculations. The reduction of the party’s struggles to a single policy failure is an oversimplification that fails to address the comprehensive policy overhaul needed to genuinely address affordability and quality of life for the average American.

Conclusion

Matthew Bartlett’s comments, while striking, are less a revelation and more a testament to the GOP’s ongoing crisis of identity and direction. The party’s sudden concern with Trump’s age and the economic dissatisfaction of the electorate is indicative of a deeper, systemic failure to anticipate and adapt to the evolving needs of the American public. The GOP’s reliance on age as a scapegoat and tariffs as the crux of their economic woes underscores a refusal to engage with the more profound, structural changes needed to address the issues at hand.

In essence, the GOP’s alarm bells over Trump’s decline, both in terms of age and voter support, ring hollow against the backdrop of the party’s larger existential quandary. As the midterms approach, the GOP finds itself at a crossroads, not just of political strategy, but of ideology and relevance in an America that is increasingly disillusioned with their offering.
