'Something is wrong': Concern mounts over rambling and slurring Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 8, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Spectacle of Speculation: Trump’s Health Under the Microscope

U.S. President Donald Trump uses a gavel after signing the sweeping spending and tax legislation, known as the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act,” at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 4, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

As President Donald Trump slurred his way through a speech about AI, a conservative group urged people to recognize that “something is seriously wrong with him.”

The Lincoln Project shared a clip on X of Trump talking about “the grid.” As he rambles about how “we’re fixing the grid,” the president starts to slur, seeming to say “nobody’s gonna challenge you or challenge me, I think this was my idea, I said, wait a minute,” although it is unclear. From there Trump says “we will need triple the energy” and continues on that tangent before the clip cuts off.

“No one can say this sounds normal at this point,” The Lincoln Project posted on X. “Something is seriously wrong with him.”

Last month, American psychologist, psychiatrist and former Johns Hopkins Medical School assistant professor Dr. John Gartner told iPaper that he believes Trump may have had a stroke or be suffering from the same Alzheimer’s that struck down his father, Fred Trump.

“The main way to diagnose dementia is that we see a deterioration from someone’s own baseline in these four areas: language, memory, behaviour, and psychomotor performance,” Gartner told iPaper. Referring to a video of Trump weaving as he disembarked a plane to a conference in Davos, Switzerland, Gartner said that this “relates to one of the signs of what I think he has: frontotemporal dementia. That walk is called a wide base gait where he swings his right leg in kind of a semicircle and that drives him to the left. That seems to have gotten dramatically worse recently.”

In addition to possibly being Alzheimer’s, Gartner speculated that “it may be related to the stroke I think he’s had on the left side of his body.”

Gartner is not alone in speculating that Trump has had a stroke. In January Dr. Bruce Davidson, a professor at Washington State University’s Elson S. Floyd College of Medicine, said he believes the 79 year-old president experienced a stroke “six months ago or more.”

“I think his stroke was on the left side of the brain, which controls the right side of the body,” Davidson told authors Sidney Blumenthal and Sean Wilentz on their podcast. “There are videos of him shuffling his feet, which is not what we’d seen previously when he was striding on the golf course. We’ve seen him holding his right hand cradled in his left. Earlier in 2025, he was garbling words, which he hadn’t done before and which he’s improved upon more recently.” He also speculated that Trump’s apparent excessive daytime sleepiness could also be linked to a potential stroke.

New York Magazine’s Ben Terris, writing in February, argued that Trump is able to obscure observations about his health because his past behavior has been so erratic.

“Donald Trump is about to be 80 years old,” Terris said. “Just by dint of that fact, it’s a worthwhile story to cover. One reason I think that Trump is able to ‘get away’ with some things that could be signs of aging is that they could also just be signs of Donald Trump being Donald Trump. He has been a chaotic figure for a long time. He’s got this rambling way of talking. He says unhinged, outrageous stuff. He did that 15 years ago. He does that now.”

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Speculation: Trump’s Health Under the Microscope

In a spectacle that’s as much about political theatre as it is about public concern, the health of former President Donald Trump has once again vaulted into the spotlight. This time, the trigger was a speech about AI where Trump, true to form, delivered his remarks with a characteristic slurring of words. The Lincoln Project, never one to miss an opportunity for criticism, swiftly pointed out that “something is seriously wrong with him.” While the intention behind their concern is as clear as their political stance, it does raise legitimate questions about the intersection of age, health, and leadership capability.

The Echo Chamber of Speculation

The article highlights how Trump’s health has become a subject of widespread speculation, with notable figures like Dr. John Gartner and Dr. Bruce Davidson weighing in with their diagnoses from afar. Gartner’s observations, based on Trump’s gait and speech patterns, suggest a possible battle with frontotemporal dementia or the aftermath of a stroke. Davidson, meanwhile, leans towards the stroke theory, citing changes in Trump’s physical demeanor and speech.

This wave of armchair diagnosis, while well-intentioned, veers dangerously close to the edge of ethical practice. Diagnosing a public figure without a formal examination is a slippery slope, one that could potentially muddy the waters of public discourse. While it is crucial to consider the health of those in leadership, especially when it comes to the demanding role of a president, the leap to conclusions without solid evidence only serves to amplify the circus of speculation.

The Age-Old Question of Fitness for Office

At the heart of this discussion is an age-old question (pun intended): At what point does one’s age and health become a disqualifying factor for holding office? Trump, “about to be 80 years old,” as noted by Ben Terris, embodies this debate. His erratic behavior, a hallmark of his public persona for decades, complicates the matter further. Is it age, health, or just Trump being Trump?

Terris makes an astute observation, pointing out that Trump’s longstanding chaotic demeanor could be masking potential signs of aging. This raises an important issue: the challenge of separating the man from the persona and the persona from potential medical concerns. In Trump’s case, his ability to “get away” with behaviors that might be scrutinized in others speaks to a larger issue of how personalities can overshadow objective analysis of a leader’s physical fitness for office.

Conclusion: The Need for Clarity and Compassion

In the swirling vortex of speculation and political point-scoring, what’s often lost is a clear, compassionate approach to discussing the health of public figures. The conversation around Trump’s health, mired in political bias and speculative diagnoses, misses an opportunity to address broader questions about aging, leadership, and the rigorous demands placed on those in the highest echelons of power.

As we navigate these murky waters, it’s crucial to remember the importance of grounding our discussions in facts, empathy, and a sincere concern for the well-being of those who lead. Whether one is a supporter or critic of Trump, the discourse around his health should not descend into a partisan free-for-all but rather serve as a catalyst for a more nuanced conversation about age, capability, and the future of political leadership.

In the end, the spectacle of speculation offers little in the way of constructive dialogue. Instead, it reflects the deeply polarized state of our political discourse, where even a question as personal and complex as one’s health becomes fodder for the relentless mill of partisanship.
