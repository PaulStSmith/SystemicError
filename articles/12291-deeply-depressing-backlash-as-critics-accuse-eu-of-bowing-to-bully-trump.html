'Deeply depressing': Backlash as critics accuse EU of bowing to 'bully' Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 29, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump shakes hands with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, after an announcement of a trade deal between the U.S. and EU, in Turnberry, Scotland, Britain, July 27, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

The leadership of the European Union on Sunday struck a deal with U.S. President Donald Trump that will leave tariffs significantly higher for many of the bloc's exports—including cars, pharmaceuticals, and semiconductors—and at 50% for steel and aluminum.

News of the deal was met with sharp criticism, including from some European officials. François Bayrou, France's prime minister, wrote on social media that "it is a dark day when an alliance of free peoples, gathered to affirm their values and defend their interests, resolves to submission."

Nick Dearden, director of the United Kingdom-based advocacy group Global Justice Now, warned that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen "has just handed Trump the biggest victory he could hope for."

"We will all pay the price because in the process, she has strengthened him and his fascist project. Deeply depressing," Dearden wrote, arguing that the deal "simply empowers the bully" and likely won't last.

In her statement announcing the agreement with Trump, von der Leyen suggested the deal would avert further escalations from the U.S. president and bring "stability" to markets unsettled by his erratic threats.

"Today with this deal, we are creating more predictability for our businesses," she said. "In these turbulent times, this is necessary for our companies to be able to plan and invest."

The sweeping 15% tariff on E.U. products entering the U.S. is half the rate that the president threatened to impose earlier this month, but it is far higher than the estimated 1.5% rate prior to Trump's second White House term. The E.U. is the United States' largest trading partner.

Cailin Birch, global economist at the London-based Economist Intelligence Unit, told CNBC that while the deal represents "a climb down from a much worse place," the 15% tariff "is still a big escalation from where we were pre-Trump 2.0."

Wolfgang Niedermark, a board member of the Federation of German Industries, called the deal "an inadequate compromise" that "will have a huge negative impact on Germany's export-oriented industry."

Trump and his team wasted no time bragging in bombastic terms about the agreement. Trump called it "probably the biggest deal ever reached in any capacity, trade or beyond trade," while the president's deputy chief of staff gushed that it is "impossible to overstate what a staggering achievement President Trump delivered for America today."

"Stephen Miller is boasting about Trump hitting us with a HUGE tax increase," responded economist Dean Baker, alluding to the fact that tariffs are often passed to consumers in the form of higher prices.

As part of the agreement, the E.U. pledged to buy $750 billion worth of U.S. energy over three years—including LNG and oil.

Andreas Sieber, associate director of policy and campaigns at 350.org, said in a statement Monday that "it's deeply shortsighted to see the E.U. strike a so-called 'deal' with the U.S. that locks us into expensive, polluting gas."

"Fossil gas is not only worse for the climate than coal, it comes at a higher cost," said Sieber. "This risks locking Europe into decades of fossil fuel dependence, volatile energy bills, and accelerating the wildfires and flooding already wreaking havoc across the continent. While Trump celebrates this as a win, communities on both sides of the Atlantic are suffering with deadly climate impacts."

Our Analysis:

The EU-US Tariff Deal: A Submission to Autocracy Masked as Strategy

The recent deal struck between the European Union leadership and U.S. President Donald Trump is a stark testament to the EU's capitulation to higher tariffs on a swath of its exports, including critical sectors such as cars, pharmaceuticals, and semiconductors, with a staggering 50% on steel and aluminum. This agreement, far from a diplomatic success, signals a deeply troubling shift in international trade policies, one that prioritizes short-term appeasement of autocratic whims over long-term economic stability and the principles of free trade.

A Victory for Trump, A Loss for the Free World

François Bayrou, France's Prime Minister, aptly noted that it is a "dark day" for the alliance of free peoples, highlighting the submission rather than negotiation. The criticism from Nick Dearden, director of Global Justice Now, cuts to the heart of the matter, framing the deal as not only a victory for Trump but a dangerous empowerment of his "fascist project." The fact that European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen views this agreement as a mechanism to bring "stability" to markets reveals a misguided concession to the erratic threats that have become a hallmark of Trump's foreign policy.

The Deal's Economic Fallout

The imposition of a sweeping 15% tariff on EU products entering the U.S. represents a significant escalation from pre-Trump norms. This is not a step back from the brink but a leap into a chasm of economic uncertainty and strife. The assertion that this deal provides "predictability" and "necessary" stability for businesses is a thinly veiled attempt to put a positive spin on what is, by all accounts, a significant setback for EU-US trade relations.

Global economist Cailin Birch's comments underscore the gravity of the situation, describing the deal as a "climb down from a much worse place," yet still a "big escalation" from the status quo ante. The perspective from Wolfgang Niedermark of the Federation of German Industries further highlights the detrimental impact on Europe's export-oriented industry, particularly for Germany.

The Boastful Triumphalism of Trump's Team

The self-congratulatory tone adopted by Trump and his deputies in the wake of the deal is nothing short of boastful triumphalism. Describing the agreement as "probably the biggest deal ever reached" not only strains credulity but also dismisses the significant economic sacrifices foisted upon European industries and consumers. Economist Dean Baker's critique brings to light the inherent contradiction in celebrating what is effectively a "HUGE tax increase" on consumers on both sides of the Atlantic.

Environmental Repercussions: A Shortsighted Gamble

The agreement's environmental dimensions, specifically the EU's commitment to purchase $750 billion worth of U.S. energy, including LNG and oil, over three years, represents a colossal failure in addressing the urgent climate crisis. Andreas Sieber's comments from 350.org highlight the myopic vision underlying this deal, locking Europe into "decades of fossil fuel dependence" and exacerbating the climate catastrophes that are already causing widespread devastation.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

This deal is not a diplomatic triumph but a dangerous capitulation that sets a troubling precedent for international trade negotiations. It demonstrates a willingness to compromise fundamental values and economic principles in the face of autocratic pressure. Moreover, it exposes the EU's vulnerabilities and internal divisions, offering a playbook for other autocratic leaders on how to exploit these weaknesses for their gain.

In essence, the EU-US tariff deal is a stark reminder of the need for unity and resilience in the face of authoritarian tactics. It is a call to action for the EU to reassess its strategies and principles, ensuring that future negotiations do not sacrifice the long-term well-being of its citizens and the global community for short-term political expediency.
