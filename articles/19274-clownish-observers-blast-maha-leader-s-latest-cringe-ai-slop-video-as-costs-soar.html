Political analysts and observers blasted Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s latest “cringe AI slop” video on Sunday, promoting his Make America Health Again agenda.

Kennedy posted a video of himself wrestling against a Twinkie mascot in a WWE SmackDown-style match. It includes an AI version of a shirtless Kennedy throwing the mascot around the ring with famous WWE announcer Jim Ross narrating the events. It is also set to the metal band Limp Bizkit’s hit song “Rollin’.”

Kennedy shared the video on his professional X account with the caption, “MAHAMania: SnackDown.”

The video was posted at a time when nearly a third of Americans have borrowed money or skipped meals to pay for their health care costs, the New York Times reported.

Political analysts and observers blasted the video on social media.

“Clownish,” journalist John Harwood posted on X.

“The only way these guys can feel capable is [through] fake AI,” former Republican lawmaker Adam Kinzinger posted on X. “Pathetic isn’t a strong enough word.”

“For a moment I thought it was SNL,” cultural studies professor Jennifer Slack posted on Bluesky.

“Americans can’t afford groceries, and because of food safety deregulation, the food we do get is filled with dangerous chemicals,” Melanie D’Arrigo, executive director at the Campaign for New York Health, posted on X. “And what is the Trump administration doing about it? Posting cringe AI slop.”

“Nobody needs to be told Twinkies are bad. Anti-junk food awareness campaigns don’t move population-level diet,” Dr. Jake Scott, an infectious disease expert, posted on X. “Food environment, pricing, school nutrition, labeling, and access do. All being defunded or ignored, but he gets to take his shirt off again.

Our Analysis:

The Misdirected Circus: Unpacking the Critique of Kennedy’s AI Stunt

In the latest spectacle to capture the political and public imagination, Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s AI-generated video—depicting him in a WWE SmackDown-style brawl with a Twinkie mascot—has drawn a firestorm of criticism. The video, intended to promote his “Make America Health Again” agenda, instead became a lightning rod for commentators decrying its timing and perceived insensitivity. However, a closer examination reveals a layered scenario of misplaced blame, misunderstood power dynamics, and the essential question of effective communication in policy promotion.

Identifying Institutional Power

Firstly, it’s paramount to recognize who holds institutional power in this context. As Health Secretary, Kennedy is a central figure in shaping and promoting health policies. His role grants him significant influence over public health initiatives, regulatory measures, and the national discourse on health. Yet, the power to enact broad legislative changes or to allocate funding for health initiatives ultimately lies with Congress and the broader administration.

Decision-Making Dynamics

Kennedy’s decision to use an AI-generated video as a medium for his message is a modern, albeit controversial, communication strategy. It underscores a personal choice in how to engage with the public on health issues. While the content and style of the video can be critiqued, it’s crucial to distinguish the medium from the message and the individual from the institution.

Framing and Responsibility

The framing of the backlash, as presented in the article, skews toward a narrative that places disproportionate blame on Kennedy for broader systemic failures. Critics label the video as “clownish” and “pathetic,” with some extending the critique to implicate the entire Trump administration in neglecting pressing health care issues, such as affordability and food safety.

This framing risks obscuring the multifaceted nature of the health care crisis. It diverts attention from the collective responsibility of government institutions and from the specific actors who have direct control over the policies that affect food safety, health care costs, and access to nutritious food. While Kennedy’s video might be deemed in poor taste, especially given the current health care affordability crisis, it is a simplistic leap to link this communication effort directly to policy outcomes without acknowledging the broader context of decision-making and power within the health sector and the government at large.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating

The critique also veers into false equivalence territory, suggesting that a controversial promotional video equates to substantive policy neglect. This conflation distracts from a constructive discussion about effective health policy and the real challenges facing the health care system. Moreover, it positions Kennedy as a convenient scapegoat for broader systemic issues that are the result of collective action or inaction by multiple stakeholders, including legislative bodies and the wider administration.

Conclusion

In dissecting the uproar over Kennedy’s AI video, it becomes evident that while the method of communication may be questionable, the critique often misdirects responsibility toward an individual for systemic failures. Effective critique must not only target the superficial aspects of political communication but also engage with the underlying power structures and decision-making processes that shape health policy outcomes. To move the needle on health care, the discourse must transcend the spectacle and address the systemic errors and inefficiencies that plague American health care.
