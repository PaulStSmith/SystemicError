'Sheer idiocy': Experts pounce on Stephen Miller's comments about history's 'brown people'

Paulo SantosDecember 29, 20254 min read

White House deputy chief of staff for policy Stephen Miller attends the annual White House Easter Egg Roll with his family, including his wife, Katie Miller, left, on the South Lawn of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 21, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

White House deputy chief of staff for policy Stephen Miller came under fire over the weekend for purported racism hidden in comments he made about inventions in history.

Stephen Miller said, “Someone should write an alternate historical novel where Americans are the first to master the automobile, the first in flight, the first to harness the atom, the first to land on the moon — but just keep going and never open our borders to the entire third world for sixty years.”

Miller added, “For those who don’t know, the US had negative migration for the half century between the first nonstop transatlantic flight and the moon landing.”

That caused an eruption of comments from critics and experts alike.

Geopolitics blogger Anatoly Karlin said, “The first automobile was German, the Manhattan Project was primarily the work of immigrant Budapest Jews (the Martians), and German immigrants of dubious political provenance likewise played an important role in getting the US to the Moon.”

Political scientist and right-wing personality Richard Hanania said, “America kept inventing stuff! It’s the center of global innovation, disproportionately due to immigration!”

“What planet are you living on?” he asked.

Hanania added, “Stephen Miller reveals how stupid nativism is as an ideology. Let him keep posting and showing how intellectually empty it all is.”

Attorney Danny Miller also chimed in on Sunday, “I keep writing different responses to this and I just can’t wrap my head around its sheer idiocy. Is he not aware that we got the atom bomb first because of a bunch of Jewish scientists fleeing Nazi persecution?”

Dem candidate Fred Wellman said, “Lots of people are pointing out this little racist Nosferatu looking troll doesn’t know an overwhelming majority of our achievements came because of immigrants including our nuclear and space programs.”

“He means white immigrants folks. He knows exactly what he’s saying,” he wrote on X. “His people were the ‘good immigrants.’ He’s against brown people.”

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of Ignorance: Stephen Miller’s Historical Revisionism

In a recent foray into what can only be described as a bizarre rewriting of history, White House deputy chief of staff for policy, Stephen Miller, sparked outrage with comments that not only betray a profound ignorance of historical facts but also unmask a thinly-veiled racist undertone. Miller’s fantasy of an America that leads the world in innovation without opening its borders to the “entire third world” for sixty years is not just a gross misrepresentation of history; it’s a clear illustration of the nativism that continues to plague certain corridors of power.

Firstly, let’s dissect the inaccuracies. Miller’s dream of an alternate history where Americans are pioneers in every major technological and scientific achievement conveniently overlooks critical contributions from immigrants. The first automobile? German. The monumental success of the Manhattan Project? Largely attributed to immigrant scientists, often referred to as “the Martians,” who fled Europe’s turmoil. And let’s not forget the significant role German immigrants played in the U.S. space program. This isn’t just a minor oversight; it’s a willful ignorance of the multifaceted tapestry that is American innovation.

Secondly, Miller’s comment about negative migration between the first nonstop transatlantic flight and the moon landing attempts to paint a picture of an America that thrived in isolation. This is a fantasy. The reality is that the U.S. has consistently been a hub of global talent, drawing bright minds from across the world, a fact that has undoubtedly propelled the country to the forefront of technological and scientific advancement.

Critics were quick to eviscerate Miller’s narrative. Geopolitics blogger Anatoly Karlin and right-wing personality Richard Hanania both pointed out the critical flaw in Miller’s argument: America’s position as a global leader in innovation is disproportionately due to immigration. The suggestion that America could maintain, let alone advance, its global standing without the contributions of immigrants is not just foolish; it’s dangerously naive.

Attorney Danny Miller and Dem candidate Fred Wellman highlighted another layer of Miller’s comments - their inherent racism. By romanticizing an America that succeeds without “opening our borders to the entire third world,” Miller isn’t just engaging in historical revisionism; he’s dog-whistling a preference for immigrants of certain racial and ethnic backgrounds over others. Wellman’s characterization of Miller as a “racist Nosferatu looking troll” might be colorful, but it underscores the insidious nature of Miller’s rhetoric.

In conclusion, Stephen Miller’s comments are more than just a failed attempt at alternate history; they’re a stark reminder of the nativist and racist ideologies that still find harbor in the halls of power. The uproar following his remarks is not just about the factual inaccuracies; it’s a rejection of a worldview that seeks to erase the invaluable contributions of immigrants to American society. It’s a reaffirmation that America’s strength lies not in isolation, but in its diversity and openness to the world.

Let Miller keep posting. With every word, he only further exposes the intellectual bankruptcy of nativism as an ideology.
