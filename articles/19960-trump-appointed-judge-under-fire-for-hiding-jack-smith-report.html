Trump-appointed judge under fire for hiding Jack Smith report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 11, 20266 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Judge Aileen Cannon in 2021 (Creative Commons)

Before concluding his work as special counsel for the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ), Jack Smith submitted a two-volume report on his federal prosecutions of Donald Trump — the first volume on his election interference case (which was assigned to Judge Tanya Chutkan), the second volume on his Mar-a-Lago documents case (which was assigned to Judge Aileen Cannon, a Trump appointee).

Volume 1 was released in January 2025, although Chutkan dismissed the election interference case without prejudice at Smith’s request. After Trump defeated Democratic nominee Kamala Harris in the 2024 election, Smith cited DOJ’s policy against prosecuting a sitting president when he made his request to Chutkan.

Volume 2, however, remains unreleased, and two groups — American Oversight and Columbia University’s Knight First Amendment Institute — are urging the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to order Cannon to make it public in redacted form.

Law & Crime reporter Matt Naham, in an article published on February 10, explains, “The latter volume has never seen the outside of Cannon’s chambers. Cannon, the Trump-appointed judge who threw out Trump’s Espionage Act prosecution and ruled that Smith was unlawfully appointed as special counsel, has, for a calendar year, kept Volume 2 of Smith’s report out of public view. At first, the rationale behind the injunction was preventing ‘prejudice’ to valet Waltine Nauta and Carlos de Oliveira Mar-a-Lago property manager, former Trump co-defendants who had active appeals. But those appeals went away when the Trump Administration dropped the cases, following Trump’s inauguration, and so did the prospect that Nauta and de Oliveira will face charges in the future, according to the Knight Institute.”

In a legal brief filed on February 9, the Knight First Amendment Institute and American Oversight told the 11th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, “There is also no risk that a future administration will revive the charges, because the five-year statute of limitations that applies to the charges will expire in 2028, before President Trump leaves office. It is thus disingenuous for Nauta and De Oliveira to represent that they remain in ‘jeopardy’ and that as a result ‘their due process rights remain a serious concern that strongly outweighs any interest that the Government or the public might have in the release of the Special Counsel’s Report.’”

Cannon’s injunction against the release of Volume 2 expires on February 24, and American Oversight is arguing that her reason for the injunction “no longer exists.”

“Before the expiration date,” Naham notes, “Trump and his former co-defendants have each supported Cannon issuing an order which would ‘permanently’ block the DOJ from releasing ‘so-called Special Counsel’ Smith’s ‘unlawfully prepared’ report and destroy it.”

Trump was facing four criminal indictments when he won the 2024 GOP presidential nomination: Smith’s two federal cases, Fulton County, Georgia District Attorney Fani Willis’ election interference/RICO case, and then-Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg Jr.’s hush money case. Trump was convicted on 34 criminal counts in Bragg’s case, but all four cases were doomed when he won the 2024 election.In an official statement, American Oversight Executive Director Chioma Chukwu said the group “will not allow the president and his guard dogs to bury information that belongs to the American people.”

Chukwu stated, “For more than a year, Judge Cannon has kept the Special Counsel’s final report under seal, long after any legitimate claims by Trump’s co-conspirators expired. By blocking our effort to challenge her gag order, the court handed President Trump a roadmap for burying the report through delay and procedural gamesmanship.”

Our Analysis:

A Judicial Farce: The Cannon Conundrum and Trump’s Teflon Presidency

In the ever-unfolding saga of Donald Trump’s legal escapades, a new chapter has been written, one that reeks of judicial bias and a flagrant disregard for the principles of transparency and accountability. The focal point of this chapter is the unreleased Volume 2 of Special Counsel Jack Smith’s report on Trump’s Mar-a-Lago documents case. This document, shrouded in secrecy thanks to Judge Aileen Cannon, a Trump appointee, remains an enigma, a testament to the lengths to which Trump’s judicial appointees will go to protect him.

Volume 1: A Prelude to Obstruction

Before diving into the murky waters of Volume 2, let’s not gloss over the release of Volume 1. Assigned to Judge Tanya Chutkan, the election interference case was dismissed “without prejudice” at Smith’s request, citing the DOJ’s policy against prosecuting a sitting president. This move, made after Trump’s eyebrow-raising defeat of Kamala Harris in the 2024 election, sets a dangerous precedent. It essentially grants Trump a shield against legal accountability, courtesy of his own administration’s policies. The irony? It’s as thick as the indictments that seem to slide off Trump’s Teflon-coated presidency.

Volume 2: Cannon’s Court of Concealment

The real travesty, however, lies with Volume 2. Judge Aileen Cannon, in a display of judicial partisanship that would make even the most stoic of legal scholars wince, has kept this volume out of public view for over a year. Initially, Cannon’s rationale was to prevent “prejudice” against Trump’s co-defendants. However, this reason evaporated faster than Trump’s credibility when the Trump Administration dropped the cases post-inauguration. Yet, Cannon persists in her blockade.

The efforts by American Oversight and Columbia University’s Knight First Amendment Institute to bring this document to light underscore a fundamental truth: the public has a right to know. Their legal brief poignantly highlights the lack of jeopardy faced by Trump’s co-defendants, noting the expiration of the statute of limitations before Trump leaves office. Cannon’s injunction, therefore, stands as nothing more than a smokescreen, a disingenuous attempt to shield Trump from further scrutiny.

A Roadmap for Obstruction

Perhaps the most audacious aspect of this saga is Trump’s attempt to “permanently” block the DOJ from releasing the report and to obliterate it altogether. This move, supported by Trump and his former co-defendants, is a blatant effort to erase any evidence of wrongdoing, to rewrite history in Trump’s favor. American Oversight Executive Director Chioma Chukwu’s statement captures the essence of this struggle. By hindering the release of the Special Counsel’s final report, Cannon has effectively handed Trump a “roadmap for burying the report through delay and procedural gamesmanship.”

The Verdict: A System in Disarray

This entire debacle is a glaring symptom of a deeper malaise afflicting the American judicial system: a crisis of accountability. When judges like Cannon act less as impartial arbiters of justice and more as guardians of Trump’s legacy, they erode the public’s trust in the judiciary. The saga of the unreleased Volume 2 is more than just a legal battle; it’s a stark illustration of how the scales of justice can be tipped, not by evidence or ethics, but by allegiance and appointment.

In the end, the American people are left in the dark, their right to transparency trampled under the weight of judicial bias and political maneuvering. As this story unfolds, one can only hope for a reclamation of judicial integrity and a reaffirmation of the principle that no one, not even a president, is above the law. But as long as figures like Cannon preside over the courts, that hope remains a distant dream.
