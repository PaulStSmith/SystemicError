Asked if he supported repealing ObamaCare, Johnson told reporters that he believed the health care program “was created to implode upon itself,” and has “failed the American people.”

“It was promised to be a great success, to make healthcare more affordable. It’s done exactly the opposite.”

He claimed that the “Republican Party is the party that has the plans to fix it,” and that it’s “a very, very complicated series of measures and steps that you have to take to fix it.”

Republicans have “lots of plans on the table,” he said, including, probably, “a hundred different ideas on how to fix it.”

“Now, it takes a long time to build consensus in a legislative body to do those kinds of things. And we have lots of plans on the table. We have whole caucuses who work on this around the clock all the time.”

Johnson has kept Republican House members in their home districts for weeks since the shutdown began.

He also pointed to the “reforms” Republicans made to Medicare as an example of how they might fix the Affordable Care Act, or ACA.

NBC News reported that “Johnson’s comments escalate the battle one day before the Senate is slated to return to Washington, albeit with no clear path to end the shutdown. It will test the patience and resolve of both parties as federal employees — including members of law enforcement, air traffic controllers and TSA staff — are slated to miss paychecks.”

U.S. Sen. Patty Murray, the vice chair of the Appropriations Committee, blasted the House Speaker.

“5 minutes of mealy-mouthed lies,” she wrote in response to the Speaker’s claims. “Republicans wanted to repeal the ACA with no replacement—they failed. This summer, they cut $1 trillion from health care, gutting Medicaid & sabotaging the ACA.”

“They have no health care plan and they want to let your premiums more than double,” she said.

Democrats have demanded Republicans continue the Affordable Care Act subsidies they struck down in the so-called “Big Beautiful Bill,” that will, they say, skyrocket premiums to possibly double or even triple current levels.

Our Analysis:

Speaker Mike Johnson’s ACA Repeal Rhetoric: A Closer Look

In a recent exhibition of political theater, Speaker of the House Mike Johnson has catapulted himself into the limelight with claims that could only be described as both audacious and dangerously misleading. His statements regarding the Affordable Care Act (ACA), colloquially known as Obamacare, and his avowed intent to repeal it, merit a thorough examination—not just for their factual inaccuracies but for the underlying intent they mask.

The Myth of ACA’s Failure

Johnson’s assertion that the ACA “was created to implode upon itself” and has “failed the American people” is a narrative as flawed as it is fantastical. This rhetoric, while appealing to a certain ideological faction, flies in the face of evidence and data. The ACA, contrary to Johnson’s claims, has significantly reduced the uninsured rate, provided protections for individuals with pre-existing conditions, and attempted to curb the runaway costs of healthcare in the United States.

The Irony of Republican Plans

The Speaker’s vague allusion to “a very, very complicated series of measures and steps” and “probably, a hundred different ideas on how to fix it” from the Republican Party is, at best, a nebulous commitment to healthcare reform. The lack of specificity and the absence of a concrete plan lay bare the truth: the GOP, after years of attempting to dismantle Obamacare, has yet to present a viable alternative that offers the same level of coverage and protections.

Misleading Claims and Misguided Solutions

Johnson’s praise for Republican-led “reforms” to Medicare as a blueprint for fixing the ACA is a red herring. These reforms, often cloaked in the language of efficiency and sustainability, have raised serious concerns about eroding the safety net for the most vulnerable populations, including the elderly and the poor.

The Real Impact on Americans

The Speaker’s rhetoric and the actions of his party stand in stark contrast to the lived realities of millions of Americans. As U.S. Sen. Patty Murray pointedly highlights, the Republican efforts have aimed at gutting Medicaid and sabotaging the ACA—actions that directly threaten to increase premiums and dismantle critical healthcare protections.

The Underlying Political Theater

Amidst a backdrop of a government shutdown, with federal employees, including those critical to national security and safety, missing paychecks, Johnson’s escalation of the battle over the ACA seems particularly ill-timed and tone-deaf. This political maneuvering does little to address the urgent needs of the American people and instead, serves to deepen divisions and exacerbate the healthcare crisis.

Conclusion

Speaker Mike Johnson’s claims about the ACA and his party’s purported efforts to “fix” healthcare are not only misleading but dangerously so. They neglect the substantive gains made under the ACA and ignore the critical need for comprehensive, accessible, and affordable healthcare. As the debate rages on, one thing is clear: political rhetoric should not come at the expense of the American people’s health and well-being. The time for actual solutions, not baseless attacks, is now.
