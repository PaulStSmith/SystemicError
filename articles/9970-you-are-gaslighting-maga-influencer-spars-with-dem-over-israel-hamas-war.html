'You are gaslighting': MAGA influencer spars with Dem over Israel-Hamas war

MAGA influencer Laura Loomer sparred with Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) on Sunday over the Israel-Hamas war after Loomer posted on social media about one of Khanna’s recent speaking engagements.

Khanna spoke on Saturday at the 45th ArabCon, a convention organized by the American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee in Dearborn, Michigan. Loomer said in a post on X that Khanna should either “apologize or resign” for speaking at the conference, which she said was attended by “pro-jihadists.”

“Why did you speak at a pro-jihad conference in Dearborn, Michigan, where supporters of Islamic terror gathered to celebrate October 7th and promote HAMAS?” Loomer posted on X.

Khanna responded in a post on X by telling Loomer that he “called the Hamas Oct 7 terrorist attacks horrific & called for the unconditional release of hostages.”

“I vehemently disagree with anyone who excuses or minimizes that. Now do you condemn Netanyahu’s ongoing killing of women and children in Gaza @LauraLoomer?” Khanna posted.

Loomer argued that she doesn’t think Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu responded forcefully enough to the attack on October 7, 2023, and that she does not feel sorry for Palestinian civilians killed by Israeli forces.

“So you believe...that it is fine to kill women and children in Gaza who are not part of Hamas’ brigades and battalions?” Khanna responded.

Loomer accused Khanna of “gaslighting” to excuse terrorism.

“You are gaslighting the American people to give an excuse to Islamic terror,” Loomer posted. “Yes, women and children can be terrorists. And yes, women and children in Gaza who support HAMAS are terrorists unworthy of my sympathy.”

Our Analysis:

The Loomer-Khanna Spar: A Glimpse into the Fractured Discourse on Israel and Gaza

In a recent exchange that can only be described as a political firestorm, MAGA influencer Laura Loomer and Representative Ro Khanna (D-CA) found themselves at the heart of a contentious debate over the Israel-Hamas conflict. Their spat, unfolding on social media, provides a stark illustration of the polarized and often incendiary rhetoric surrounding this issue.

The Spark of Controversy

Loomer’s attack was launched following Khanna’s engagement at the 45th ArabCon, a convention organized by the American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee in Dearborn, Michigan. Her accusation that Khanna was pandering to “pro-jihadists” by speaking at the event is a bold claim, starkly illustrating the ease with which political discourse can veer into the realm of wild accusation. It’s crucial to dissect the implications of labeling a civil rights organization’s conference as a gathering of “pro-jihadists,” a term loaded with connotations that do little to foster meaningful dialogue.

Khanna’s response, emphasizing his condemnation of the Hamas October 7 terrorist attacks and the call for the unconditional release of hostages, was a clear attempt to set the record straight. His challenge to Loomer to condemn the killing of women and children in Gaza by Israeli forces further illuminates the complex moral landscape of the conflict, where condemnation of violence often seems selectively applied.

The Unforgivable Stance

Loomer’s assertion that Palestinian civilians killed by Israeli forces, including women and children, are “unworthy of my sympathy” because they might support Hamas is a chilling dehumanization of individuals caught in a war zone. The implication that any support, presumed or real, for Hamas renders civilians legitimate targets is a dangerous precedent. It’s a stance that strips away the nuance of human conflict, reducing individuals to mere extensions of political entities.

Accusations of Gaslighting

The term “gaslighting” is thrown around with increasing frequency in political discourse, but its use by Loomer to describe Khanna’s defense is a misapplication. Pointing out the civilian toll of military actions is not gaslighting; it’s highlighting a fundamental concern in the ethics of war. To equate this with providing an “excuse to Islamic terror” is not just a leap in logic; it’s a deliberate obfuscation of the issue at hand.

Conclusion: A Discourse in Disarray

What this exchange lays bare is the deep fracture in how the Israel-Hamas conflict is discussed and understood in American political dialogue. The willingness to engage in hyperbole, to dehumanize, and to dismiss any call for a nuanced understanding of the conflict’s toll on civilians is indicative of a broader systemic failure.

The discourse is not just polarized; it’s become toxically adversarial, where the complexities of a decades-long conflict are reduced to soundbites and accusations. Meaningful dialogue—the kind that acknowledges the humanity on both sides and seeks solutions rather than scoring political points—is conspicuously absent.

As observers and participants in this conversation, it’s incumbent upon us to demand better from our influencers and our elected officials. The stakes are too high, and the consequences too grave, for anything less.
