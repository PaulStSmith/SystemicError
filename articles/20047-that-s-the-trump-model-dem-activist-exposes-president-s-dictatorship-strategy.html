On Monday, Trump claimed that "a lot of people are saying, 'Maybe we'd like a dictator?'" during a press conference in the Oval Office.

Trump came close to describing himself as a dictator during the press conference, but never crossed the line.

"I’m a man with great common sense, and I’m a smart person," Trump said. "And when I see what’s happened to our cities, and then you send in troops. Instead of being praised, they’re saying, ‘You’re trying to take over the republic.’ These people are sick.”

Democratic activist Cameron Kasky, who co-founded the gun safety group Never Again MSD, said Trump's comments are similar to his strategy for flirting with women, during a new episode of The Bulwark's "FYPod."

"I'm normally too insecure to say anything that's directly a flirt, because I'm afraid that somebody doesn't like me," Kasky said. "So, what I like to do is I like to make any flirtatious comment that I'm making funny enough that if someone isn't interested, they sort of have the out of laughing it off."

"And it seems like that's the Trump model," Kasky added. "It starts as a joke, and then the people who choose to take it as a joke will do so."

"Trump's dictatorship gambit is similar to how you flirt with actresses," he added.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Dictatorship Flirtation: A Dangerous Joke?

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as both bizarre and deeply unsettling, President Donald Trump let slip a comment that has sparked outrage and concern across the political spectrum. During a press conference in the Oval Office, Trump mused, "a lot of people are saying, 'Maybe we'd like a dictator?'" This remark, while not outright declaring himself as a dictator, dangerously toes the line between authoritarian jest and a veiled threat to the democratic foundations of the United States.

Trump's "Joke": A Thinly Veiled Threat?

Trump's attempt to downplay his authoritarian inclinations by framing them as a response to unrest in American cities is nothing short of alarming. He stated, "I’m a man with great common sense, and I’m a smart person. And when I see what’s happened to our cities, and then you send in troops. Instead of being praised, they’re saying, ‘You’re trying to take over the republic.’ These people are sick.” This rhetoric not only dismisses legitimate concerns about his approach to governance but also insidiously suggests that his critics are the irrational ones.

Cameron Kasky's Insightful Comparison

Democratic activist Cameron Kasky, co-founder of the gun safety group Never Again MSD, offers a striking analogy to Trump's approach, comparing it to a timid flirtation strategy. According to Kasky, Trump's comments start as a joke, allowing those inclined to support him an out to dismiss criticism as overreaction. This tactic, likened to a flirtatious comment made in jest to avoid outright rejection, serves a dual purpose: it tests the waters of public opinion on authoritarianism while maintaining plausible deniability.

The Danger of Normalizing Authoritarian "Humor"

Kasky's comparison sheds light on a grave concern: the normalization of authoritarian ideas under the guise of humor or offhand remarks. This method enables Trump to introduce radical and dangerous concepts into public discourse, retreating under the cover of jest if the reaction proves unfavorable. It's a calculated move to desensitize the public and gauge the possibility of extending his power unchallenged.

Conclusion: No Laughing Matter

Trump's flirtation with dictatorship, whether in jest or earnest, is a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy. It's imperative to recognize these comments not as harmless banter but as a potential prelude to further erosion of democratic norms and institutions. The comparison drawn by Cameron Kasky is not just an amusing analogy but a cautionary tale of how authoritarianism can creep into a society under the guise of humor and hypotheticals.

In a functioning republic, the notion of desiring a dictator should be met with unequivocal rejection, not entertained as a subject of debate. Let us not be the audience that laughs off the joke until it becomes our reality.
