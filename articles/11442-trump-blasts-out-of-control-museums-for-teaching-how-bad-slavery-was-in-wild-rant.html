Trump blasts 'out of control' museums for teaching 'how bad Slavery was' in wild rant

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing Trump's Tirade: A Masterclass in Historical Denial

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a meeting with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy (not pictured) and European leaders amid negotiations to end the Russian war in Ukraine, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 18, 2025. REUTERS/Al Drago

President Donald Trump ranted on Truth Social that teaching about America's history of slavery is unacceptable.

"The Museums throughout Washington, but all over the Country are, essentially, the last remaining segment of 'WOKE.' The Smithsonian is OUT OF CONTROL, where everything discussed is how horrible our Country is, how bad Slavery was, and how unaccomplished the downtrodden have been — Nothing about Success, nothing about Brightness, nothing about the Future," claimed Trump.

The Smithsonian Museum of African American History walks through the struggles of those who were kidnapped and brought to the United States to work as slaves. However, as one ascends the floors, the exhibits hold up the abolitionist movement, the successes of civil rights, and celebrate those who continue to fight for equal rights.

The GOP president went on to promise, "We are not going to allow this to happen, and I have instructed my attorneys to go through the Museums, and start the exact same process that has been done with Colleges and Universities, where tremendous progress has been made. This Country cannot be WOKE, because WOKE IS BROKE. We have the 'HOTTEST' Country in the World, and we want people to talk about it, including in our Museums," he closes.

Trump's own campaign slogan, "Make America Great Again," suggests that there was a time when America was not great.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Tirade: A Masterclass in Historical Denial

In a recent outburst on Truth Social, former President Donald Trump launched a scathing attack on the educational practices surrounding America's history of slavery, singling out museums, particularly the Smithsonian, for what he perceives as their excessive focus on America's dark historical chapters. Trump's rant is not just a simple expression of discontent; it's a blatant attempt to whitewash and rewrite the complex narrative of American history, prioritizing a glossy, triumphalist version over a nuanced and factual recounting.

"The Museums throughout Washington, but all over the Country are, essentially, the last remaining segment of 'WOKE.'" This statement is not just hyperbolic; it's alarmingly dismissive of the crucial role museums play in preserving and presenting history. Museums are not the bastions of 'woke-ness' Trump claims they are but are instead vital institutions that provide spaces for reflection, education, and understanding.

Trump's characterization of the Smithsonian as "OUT OF CONTROL" for its efforts to discuss slavery and its impacts is a stark reflection of his inability to grasp the importance of confronting uncomfortable truths. By framing the discussion of slavery as focusing on "how horrible our Country is, how bad Slavery was, and how unaccomplished the downtrodden have been," Trump not only misrepresents the purpose of historical education but also undermines the resilience and contributions of those who have fought against oppression throughout American history.

It's crucial to note that the Smithsonian Museum of African American History, a specific target of Trump's ire, provides a comprehensive view of American history that includes both the atrocities of slavery and the successes of the civil rights movement. The museum's approach is not about denigrating America but about telling the full story of its people, including the struggle for equal rights and the ongoing fight against systemic racism. Ignoring these elements, as Trump suggests, would not only be a disservice to historical accuracy but also to the individuals who have dedicated their lives to advancing justice and equality.

Trump's promise to use legal action against museums for educating the public about slavery under the guise of combating 'woke-ness' is a dangerous precedent. This stance, "We are not going to allow this to happen," echoes authoritarian attempts to control and manipulate historical narrative to suit a particular ideological agenda. The rhetorical flourish, "WOKE IS BROKE," coupled with his assertion that America is the 'HOTTEST' country in the world, is a transparent attempt to divert attention from the critical need for a comprehensive understanding of history to foster a more informed and empathetic society.

Furthermore, Trump's critique is laced with irony considering his own campaign slogan, "Make America Great Again," implicitly acknowledges a period when America was not 'great'. This contradiction underscores a fundamental flaw in Trump's argument: You cannot aspire to greatness while denying the very lessons that history teaches us about overcoming adversity and injustice.

In conclusion, Trump's rant against the teaching of America's history of slavery is more than just a political diatribe; it's a disturbing attempt to erase and simplify the complex narratives that define the nation. The pushback against so-called 'woke' culture, as exemplified by Trump's comments, is not a fight for historical accuracy or national pride but a battle against the very essence of progress and enlightenment. A nation that fails to confront and learn from its past is doomed to repeat it, and no amount of political bluster can change that immutable fact.
