'Claptrap-laden diatribes': JD Vance leveled by legal scholar over his outbursts on X

Vice President J.D. Vance was taken to task by the conservative National Review's Andrew McCarthy for his constant running to X to post disingenuous attacks on opponents of the Donald Trump administration's immigration policies.

In a column posted Saturday morning, the legal analyst claimed the VP, a Yale Law School graduate, knows fully well the administration is denying immigrants their right to due process, and that Vance is making his claims more as a performance for MAGA devotees.

Labeling Donald Trump's second-in-command as "infuriating," he added the VP is "too smart not to know that the nonsense he spouts is nonsense. Well-framed nonsense, to be sure."

He then added, "Vice President Vance issued one of his claptrap-laden diatribes on social media Wednesday, slamming 'the media and the far left' who are 'weeping over the lack of due process' in the Trump administration’s illegal deportations of people it alleges — probably correctly in most instances — are members of criminal gangs."

Using that as a springboard, he noted that Trump's goal is to "illustrate that he has amassed uncheckable power," and for Vance that's "the point of the vice president’s post: Due process is for whiners, and if you’re 'weeping' over its sudden death, you’re the problem."

"Adopting the habit of Trump officials of making up their own facts as well as their own law, Vance argues that all the administration is doing is returning an illegal alien to his home country," he accused. "This is so mendacious it’s hard to decide where to begin — even accepting for argument’s sake Vance’s blithe claim that Abrego Garcia is an MS-13 member, which, again, Trump officials chant as a mantra but no one has actually proved."

"He says we who want the law enforced don’t have a plan for deporting 20 million people — and that’s true, for there is no such four-year plan; there’s just 'do the best you can to materially reduce the illegal population, get on a trajectory for bigger reductions over time, and then manage illegal immigration like we manage other ordinary crime.'" he wrote before pointing out, "But to flip it around, Vice President Vance does not tell us what his plan is for rapidly deporting 20 million people. That’s because such a plan cannot include faithfully executing the law. And he knows it."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Disguise: Vance's Performance in the Political Theater

In a recent column by Andrew McCarthy for the conservative National Review, Vice President J.D. Vance's affinity for the dramatic and disingenuous takes center stage. McCarthy's critique exposes a troubling performance where Vance, a Yale Law School graduate, knowingly distorts the truth to curry favor with MAGA enthusiasts. This act, as McCarthy articulates, is not just a simple dalliance with falsehoods but a calculated attempt to undermine the principles of due process under the guise of enforcing immigration policies.

The Charade of Competence

Vance's approach, branded as "infuriating" by McCarthy, is emblematic of a broader trend within the Trump administration: the deliberate manipulation of facts and law to serve a political agenda. It's particularly galling that Vance, with his legal education and understanding, chooses to engage in what McCarthy describes as "well-framed nonsense." This isn't a matter of ignorance but a deliberate choice to mislead.

The Illusion of Due Process

At the heart of McCarthy's critique is the accusation that the Trump administration, with Vance as its vocal cheerleader, is trampling over the fundamental right to due process. Vance's social media tirades, as highlighted by McCarthy, paint a picture of a Vice President more interested in stoking the fires of division and rallying the MAGA base than in safeguarding constitutional rights. The characterization of due process as a concern for "whiners" is not just dismissive but dangerous, signaling a willingness to discard legal protections when they become inconvenient.

The Mendacity of Vance's Arguments

McCarthy points out the mendacity in Vance's arguments, particularly in his oversimplification of complex immigration issues. The claim that the administration is merely "returning an illegal alien to his home country" sidesteps the nuanced realities of immigration law and the very real consequences of denying due process. Furthermore, the assertion, largely unchallenged and unproven, that individuals targeted for deportation are members of criminal gangs, underscores a troubling reliance on unverified narratives to justify aggressive policies.

The Absence of a Plan

Perhaps most striking in McCarthy's critique is the acknowledgment of the impracticality and inhumanity of Vance's implied stance on mass deportations. The absence of a coherent plan to address illegal immigration, beyond the simplistic and punitive measures favored by Vance, reveals a lack of genuine commitment to resolving the issue in a manner consistent with American legal and ethical standards.

Conclusion: The Disingenuous Spectacle

Andrew McCarthy's column does not just challenge J.D. Vance's statements; it exposes a broader, more concerning trend of political posturing at the expense of legal integrity and human rights. Vance's performances may play well to a certain audience, but they erode trust in public institutions and undermine the principles upon which the American legal system is built. It's a dangerous game of smoke and mirrors, where the stakes are the very foundations of democracy and the rule of law.
