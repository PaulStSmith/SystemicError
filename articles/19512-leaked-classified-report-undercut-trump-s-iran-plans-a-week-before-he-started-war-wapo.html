Leaked classified report undercut Trump's Iran plans a week before he started war: WaPo

Three members of the Donald Trump administration have confirmed to the Washington Post that a classified document created by the National Intelligence Council poured cold water on the president’s hopes about regime change in Iran following his war launch.

According to the Post, the warning was completed a week before the president gave the go-ahead for the Department of Defense, under Secretary Pete Hegseth to proceed with the bombings on February 28th.

The classified report examined succession scenarios under two potential military scenarios: a targeted campaign against Iran’s leadership or a broader assault on its government institutions. In both cases, intelligence analysts concluded that “Iran’s clerical and military establishment would respond to the killing of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei by following protocols designed to preserve continuity of power,” according to those familiar with the findings.

The National Intelligence Council comprises veteran intelligence analysts tasked with producing classified assessments representing the combined judgment of Washington’s 18 intelligence agencies.

White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly dismissed the implications in a statement to the Post, asserting: “President Trump and the administration have clearly outlined their goals with regard to Operation Epic Fury: destroy Iran’s ballistic missiles and production capacity, demolish their navy, end their ability to arm proxies, and prevent them from ever obtaining a nuclear weapon. The Iranian regime is being absolutely crushed.”

Suzanne Maloney, an Iran scholar and vice president at the Brookings Institution, characterized the NIC assessment as credible, stating: “It sounds like a deeply informed assessment of the Iranian system and the institutions and processes that have been established for many years.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Follies of Wishful Thinking: Trump’s Iran Fantasy

In a revelation that should surprise exactly no one, members of the Trump administration have confirmed an inconvenient truth: a classified document from the National Intelligence Council (NIC) essentially threw cold water on then-President Donald Trump’s dreams of regime change in Iran. This report, completed a mere week before the green light was given for the Department of Defense, under Secretary Pete Hegseth, to bomb Iran on February 28th, underscores a theme as old as time itself in American foreign policy: wishful thinking in lieu of strategic foresight.

The Illusion of Easy Victory

The NIC’s assessment, drawing on the expertise of veteran intelligence analysts and representing the consensus of not one, not two, but eighteen intelligence agencies, examined succession scenarios under two military options aimed at Iran. Whether through a targeted campaign against Iran’s leadership or a broader assault on its government institutions, the conclusion was stark. Intelligence analysts unanimously agreed that “Iran’s clerical and military establishment would respond to the killing of Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei by following protocols designed to preserve continuity of power.”

This flies in the face of the simplistic fantasy that a decisive military strike could lead to a capitulation or a collapse of the Iranian regime. It’s a narrative as threadbare as it is dangerous, ignoring the resilience of Iran’s political structure and the likely rallying effect such attacks would have among its populace.

The White House’s Dismissive Tune

In what can only be described as a masterpiece of obfuscation, White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly waved away the implications of the NIC’s findings. Her statement, a marvel of distortion, claimed that “President Trump and the administration have clearly outlined their goals with regard to Operation Epic Fury: destroy Iran’s ballistic missiles and production capacity, demolish their navy, end their ability to arm proxies, and prevent them from ever obtaining a nuclear weapon. The Iranian regime is being absolutely crushed.”

This bald assertion stands in stark contrast to the realities on the ground and the NIC’s analysis. It’s a classic case of the administration’s modus operandi: declare victory and hope nobody looks too closely at the details.

A Credible Warning Ignored

Suzanne Maloney, an Iran scholar and vice president at the Brookings Institution, offered a dose of reality, characterizing the NIC assessment as “a deeply informed assessment of the Iranian system and the institutions and processes that have been established for many years.” Her endorsement highlights a critical point: the analysts involved in the preparation of this report are not armchair commentators but experts deeply versed in the complexities of the Iranian state.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error of Judgment

The decision to proceed with Operation Epic Fury, in direct contradiction to the NIC’s assessment, is emblematic of a systemic error in judgment that plagued the Trump administration. It’s a stark reminder of the dangers inherent in dismissing the expertise of intelligence agencies in favor of politically expedient narratives.

In the end, the saga of Trump’s Iran policy is a cautionary tale about the pitfalls of hubris and the perils of ignoring the lessons of history. As we sift through the aftermath, one can only hope that future administrations heed these warnings, rather than consign them to the same oblivion Trump attempted for the NIC’s inconvenient truths.
