Trump officials point fingers as they obey his 'master class' in deflection

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

The Buck Stops Nowhere in Trump’s White House

Power Without Accountability

The point of this story is not that Donald Trump is surrounded by confused subordinates. It is that the person at the top has built a government where power is real, but responsibility is optional. Trump holds the institutional authority. He sets the tone, rewards the excuses, and teaches everyone below him that blame must never travel upward.

That is not a communication problem. It is the operating system.

Scapegoating as Policy

The source reporting shows a pattern that should be named plainly: deliberate deflection. When the administration fails, the story gets rerouted toward underlings, Democrats, generic enemies, or whichever internal target is most convenient. Trump’s own line about being angry at Republicans, but not at him, is the clearest version of this fraud. He wants the benefits of command without the burden of ownership.

That is how authoritarian politics protects itself. It does not deny failure. It disperses it.

Hegseth Learns the Lesson

Pete Hegseth is not portrayed as a rogue actor here. He is a student. The report describes him taking credit when things looked triumphant, then dodging criticism when the war in Iran dragged on and munitions stockpiles were reportedly depleted. He asked Congress for money to refill the arsenal while denying the shortage and blaming Biden for years of underinvestment.

That is not candor. It is political self-preservation dressed up as management.

Blame Flowing Sideways

The article gets closest to the truth when it describes blame moving laterally among equals, downward to subordinates, or backward into the past, but never up to the people making the calls. That is exactly how dysfunctional power protects itself: the staff absorbs the damage, the principals keep their hands clean, and the public is told failure came from somewhere vague and lower-status.

This is why the usual press habit of treating chaos as if it were mere disorder misses the point. A lot of this is not chaos. It is discipline for everyone except the powerful.

Consequences, Not Theater

The cost of this structure is straightforward. Without accountability, mistakes do not get fixed. They metastasize. Leaders who can never be blamed have no reason to correct course, and officials trained to survive by deflection have no incentive to tell the truth when it matters. The result is a government that protects egos while degrading its own capacity to govern.

Trump has not merely normalized excuse-making. He has turned it into a governing principle.

The Larger Pattern

This is the deeper political story: when a movement is organized around domination rather than administration, truth becomes a liability and responsibility becomes something to dump on the nearest expendable person. Trump’s White House is not failing despite that logic. It is functioning according to it.

The result is a system where power concentrates at the top and blame evaporates on contact. That is not leadership. It is impunity with a press office.
