A collapsed sewage pipe in January has resulted in over 250 million gallons of raw sewage being poured into the Potomac River. While the Army Corps of Engineers are at work building a new wall to hold back the increase of water in the era of climate change, that same department hasn’t even heard from Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth on the toxic sludge floating by the nation’s memorials and monuments.

Former CNN Pentagon reporter Barbara Starr commented on X, “Why has @SecWar not called in Army Corps of Engineers for what may be biggest spill in history of US? This is a huge story and a national security imperative!”

Indeed, the infrastructure of Washington D.C. can be a serious issue, given that national and world leaders rely on the river as a water source. Thus far, testing has found E. coli levels 12,000 times above safe limits, as well as dangerous levels of Staph bacteria and MRSA, reported WUSA9.

“Officials say the river remains partially frozen, but with highs climbing into the 50s this week, bacteria trapped in the ice could soon be released into the water, posing risks to people who live, work, or recreate along the river,” reported WUSA9 in another report.

President Donald Trump entered the White House complaining that Washington D.C. was a dump and he was willing to deploy the National Guard and spend millions to clean up city parks and squares.

Aside from a security issue, Trump is planning a huge 250th-anniversary celebration for the country, with events all over the city. Officials are telling residents not even to touch the water, much less fish in it.

It will take another nine months for DC Water to fix the sewer pipe, ABC7News reported. It means almost all of Trump’s events for 2026 will be surrounded by sewage.

“You can visibly see all this raw sewage and toilet paper,” said Dean Naujoks with the Potomac Riverkeeper Network.

Ironically, the department that runs the Army Corps of Engineers doesn’t appear to want engineers anymore, quipped digital consultant Andy Barr.

A Friday CNN report revealed that they will no longer provide tuition assistance for some colleges and universities, Hegseth said, that have “troublesome partnerships with foreign adversaries.”

Our Analysis:

The Potomac Pollution Catastrophe: A Scathing Analysis

In an era where environmental crises seem to be on the daily menu, the disaster unfolding in the Potomac River has etched a new low in the annals of governmental apathy and incompetence. Over 250 million gallons of raw sewage have been unceremoniously dumped into the river, transforming it from a historic waterway into a festering conduit of disease and pollution. This situation is not merely an environmental debacle; it’s a glaring testament to the failure of leadership and the erosion of responsibility among those at the helm of our nation’s infrastructure and environmental stewardship.

The Sound of Silence from Secretary Hegseth

The deafening silence from Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth is as toxic as the sludge that now flows past the nation’s memorials. The Army Corps of Engineers, a beacon of hope in times of infrastructural crisis, has been left in the dark, with no directives to combat what may well be the largest spill in U.S. history. Barbara Starr’s outcry on X (formerly Twitter) encapsulates the bewilderment and frustration shared by many. The question arises: How can such a critical issue be met with such indifference?

A National Security Imperative Ignored

The implications of this disaster stretch beyond environmental damage; it’s a glaring national security risk. Washington D.C.’s infrastructure is not just any infrastructure—it underpins the very heart of the nation, serving world leaders and being emblematic of the United States. The discovery of E. coli levels 12,000 times above safe limits, along with dangerous concentrations of Staph bacteria and MRSA, underscores the gravity of the situation. Yet, the response from those in power has been lackluster at best, and criminally negligent at worst.

Trump’s Grand Plans Amidst a Sewage Crisis

The irony of President Donald Trump’s grandiose plans for a 250th-anniversary celebration amidst this sewage debacle cannot be overstated. Trump’s ambitions to beautify and celebrate the nation’s capital are juxtaposed against a backdrop of environmental negligence that threatens to mar any celebration with the stench of untreated sewage. The promise of nine more months before the sewer pipe is fixed is not just a technical timeline; it’s a sentence to prolonged exposure to health risks for the residents of D.C. and a blight on the city’s image.

The Army Corps of Engineers: Engineers No More?

Andy Barr’s quip about the department running the Army Corps of Engineers no longer wanting engineers is a poignant, albeit bitter, reflection of the current state of affairs. The decision to cut tuition assistance for courses at institutions deemed to have “troublesome partnerships with foreign adversaries” might have its merits in a vacuum. However, juxtaposed against the monumental failure to address a domestic crisis of epic proportions, it reeks of misplaced priorities and a baffling allocation of resources.

Conclusion

The Potomac River sewage spill is not just an environmental catastrophe; it’s a litmus test for the competency, integrity, and priorities of those in power. The silence and inaction from Secretary Hegseth, coupled with the federal government’s glaring mismanagement, paint a bleak picture of leadership at a time when decisive action is not just expected—it’s imperative. As the river continues to carry this toxic burden past the nation’s most cherished monuments, one cannot help but wonder: If the government cannot safeguard the health of its river and its people, what can it safeguard?
