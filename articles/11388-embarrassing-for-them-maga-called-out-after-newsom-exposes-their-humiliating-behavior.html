'Embarrassing for them': MAGA called out after Newsom 'exposes their humiliating behavior'

A progressive analyst fired back at President Donald Trump's supporters, who are calling on a prominent Democratic lawmaker to shape up his online behavior.

For the last several days, California Gov. Gavin Newsom has been trolling Trump online by imitating his syntax, diction, and capitalization style in several posts. Trump supporters on Fox News, News Nation, and social media have come to the president's defense, telling Newsom that he needs to cut it out.

Progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen mocked the outrage from Trump supporters on his show "No Lie" on Tuesday. Cohen argued that Newsom was simply providing a "mirror" for Trump supporters.

"The reality is that the reason these Republicans are melting down is because they don't know how to handle the fact that Newsom is exposing the humiliating behavior that they themselves have supported and normalized," Cohen said.

"All Newsom's doing is acting as a mirror," Cohen continued. "And it's embarrassing for them. They would love for him to stop because then the stuff that they approve of that they've been approving of for years wouldn't be so obvious to the rest of the country."

Cohen then played multiple clips of TV hosts defending Trump against Newsom's posts.

"Just so I'm understanding here, Newsom, the governor of California, is directly imitating the president of the United States," Cohen said. "But his imitating the president is somehow disqualifying him from being president? Please, someone, make it make sense."

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy Unveiled: Newsom vs. Trump Supporters

In an era where political discourse frequently descends into the theater of the absurd, the latest saga involving California Governor Gavin Newsom and his online trolling of former President Donald Trump unfolds as a masterclass in exposing hypocrisy. The outcry from Trump supporters over Newsom's mimicry of Trump's distinctive online communication style is as ironic as it is revealing.

The Ironic Outrage

Let's dissect the heart of this comedy. Trump supporters, a group notoriously indifferent (if not supportive) of the former president's often inflammatory and rule-defying social media behavior, have suddenly found their sensibilities offended. Newsom, by adopting Trump's own syntax, diction, and capitalization, has held up a mirror to the very tactics they've defended or celebrated. The outrage from these quarters, as highlighted by progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen, is nothing short of a farcical display of selective sensitivity.

A Mirror to the Madness

Cohen's analysis cuts to the core of the issue. By imitating Trump, Newsom isn't just poking fun; he's strategically highlighting the absurdity and often the toxicity of the rhetoric Trump's supporters have normalized. Cohen argues, and rightly so, that the meltdown from Trump's camp over Newsom's actions underscores an inability to confront or even recognize their complicity in degrading political discourse. The discomfort isn't with Newsom's behavior per se but with the sudden, stark visibility of their champion's conduct reflected back at them.

The Disqualification Double Standard

Cohen's incredulity at the argument that Newsom's mimicry somehow disqualifies him from presidential consideration is the cherry on top of this illogical outrage sundae. The suggestion that imitating Trump's online behavior is disqualifying, while Trump's original actions are not only acceptable but defensible, is an exercise in mental gymnastics worthy of Olympic recognition. It's an absurdity that Cohen skewers with precision, highlighting the double standards and the cognitive dissonance rampant among Trump's defenders.

The Bottom Line

The saga of Newsom trolling Trump is more than just a petty political squabble; it's a revealing case study in the hypocrisy and selective outrage that plagues American political discourse. Cohen's commentary, sharp and saturated with incredulity, underscores the necessity of holding a mirror up to the political landscape. When the reflection is met with outrage, it reveals not just a discomfort with the image presented but a deeper malaise concerning the state of political integrity and the selective blindness of partisan support.

In the grand scheme, Newsom's trolling and the subsequent fallout is a reminder of the importance of consistency in our moral and ethical judgments. If Trump's style of communication was never the problem for his supporters, their outrage at Newsom's imitation is not just hypocritical; it's emblematically indicative of a larger systemic error in political engagement and accountability.
