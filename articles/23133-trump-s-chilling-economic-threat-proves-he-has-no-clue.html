Trump's chilling economic threat proves he has no clue

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20263 min read

Trump Is Not Managing the Economy. He Is Distorting It.

The Job Report Is Not the Story

The Labor Department says the U.S. added 162,000 jobs in August. That number matters less than the conditions behind it: a labor market already battered by Trump’s mass deportations, an aging workforce, and policy choices that make the data harder to read and the economy harder to trust.

The real story is not whether one monthly report beat expectations. It is that the people with power are breaking the machinery that is supposed to measure, stabilize, and explain the economy.

The Power Is at the Top

Actual institutional power sits with Trump and the Republican apparatus around him. He is the one driving the economy through tariffs, threats, and escalation. He is the one who can turn trade policy into punishment and monetary policy into a political demand. The Bureau of Labor Statistics does not control these conditions. It is trying to estimate a labor market that has been deliberately disrupted.

That matters because confusion is useful to power. When the labor market is damaged by policy, the damage can be disguised as statistical fog. That is not an innocent complication. It is political cover.

Wages Tell the Truth

The cleanest fact in the source is the ugliest one: wages are rising more slowly than prices. Average hourly earnings rose 0.3 percent for the month, 3.1 percent over the year, while prices rose 3.4 percent. That means most hourly workers are getting poorer.

This is the central economic fact Trump and Republicans want to talk around. A jobs number can be positive while working people still lose ground. Low-wage job creation is easy. Raising living standards is hard, and it requires policy choices Trump is not making.

Stagnation Is a Policy Outcome

The labor market described here is not healthy just because it is not collapsing. A low-hire, low-fire economy is a stalled economy. It blocks advancement, locks young people out, and keeps workers stuck.

That is not some natural phase of the business cycle. It is what happens when the economy is managed for price pressure, political theater, and elite convenience instead of broad wage growth and real mobility. Stability without opportunity is not stability. It is managed stagnation.

The Real Misdirection

The article is right to dismiss the idea that the jobs report can be treated like a clean, decisive snapshot. But the deeper problem is not just statistical uncertainty. It is political misdirection.

Trump’s tariffs are pushing up prices. His war in Iran is costly. His trade threats are absurd. His demand that the Fed cut rates so he can juice the economy before the midterms is nakedly political. Then he threatens embargoes if he does not get his way, as if the world economy were a leverage game for a president with no discipline and no grasp of consequence.

The blame should not drift toward analysts, technicians, or the abstraction of “the economy.” The decisions are coming from Trump. The damage is coming from Trump. The confusion is part of the method.

A Familiar Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in economic form: break systems, create uncertainty, punish expertise, and then demand credit for the ruins. The public gets told to squint at lagging indicators while the people in power scramble wages, prices, trade, and institutions.

The lesson is not that the labor market is mysterious. The lesson is that when power is reckless enough, even basic economic reality gets harder to measure because the damage is being made on purpose.
