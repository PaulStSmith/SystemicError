Pentagon’s recruitment boast unravels as ‘strikingly beautiful’ soldiers revealed as AI

AI images of women in the US military. (Photos: Screen capture with emphasis added by RawStory.com)

The Department of Defense claimed on social media that its recruitment of women into the armed forces is going great. The problem, however, is that when they bragged about it in an interview, artificial intelligence photos were used.

AI-generated images were part of an effort to "make it seem like the government’s recruitment efforts are actually working," The New Republic reported Friday.

Edith Olmsted wrote that a "propaganda segment" appeared on Matt Gaetz's show on Thursday, where DOD press secretary Kingsly Wilson cheered "incredible success" bringing women to the DOD.

“These numbers are fantastic. Under the previous administration, we had about 16,000 female recruits last year. Now we’ve got upwards of 24,000,” Wilson said.

Images showed "photographs" of what Olmsted called "strikingly beautiful and notably diverse female officers." However, a watermark appeared in the corner of each image showing that they were images created by Elon Musk's AI named Grok. The writer called the recruits "nothing but ghosts in the machine."

The DOD distanced itself from the photos, saying that they didn't make them. Later in the show, Gaetz issued a statement, confessing, “The DOD didn’t give us these images; Grok did. And we’ll use better judgment going forward.

The new numbers from DOD are curious, as a 2024 report said that the government saw a "surge" of female recruitment of nearly 10,000. It was an 18% increase at the time. There has been a recruitment slump among male enlistment over the years, Military.com remarked in January. The Trump DOD appears to be editing those numbers to 16,000.

The data has not been fully released publicly, so it cannot be fact-checked for accuracy.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth attacked the idea of "diversity, equity, and inclusion" in the military, alleging standards have declined and, as a result, men have been driven away from the military, The Associated Press reported in January.

"I’m straight up just saying we should not have women in combat roles. It hasn’t made us more effective. Hasn’t made us more lethal. Has made fighting more complicated," he told podcaster Shawn Ryan on Nov. 7, 2024, the AP recalled.

In his book, Hegseth wrote, “women cannot physically meet the same standards as men.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Mirage of Military Recruitment Success

In a recent revelation that could only be characterized as a farce wrapped in a digital illusion, the Department of Defense (DOD) has found itself embroiled in a controversy that reads like a script from a dystopian novel. The claim of a triumphant surge in female recruitment into the armed forces, as lauded on social media and a subsequent interview, was not just exaggerated but seemingly manufactured using artificial intelligence-generated images. This debacle was reported by The New Republic, uncovering a layer of deceit that deserves nothing short of a scathing critique.

The Spectacle of Success

During an appearance on Matt Gaetz's show, DOD press secretary Kingsly Wilson boasted about an "incredible success" in recruiting women, citing a jump from 16,000 female recruits under the previous administration to a staggering 24,000. Yet, the visual evidence supporting these claims turned out to be as real as a mirage. The images, described as showcasing "strikingly beautiful and notably diverse female officers," bore watermarks from Elon Musk's AI, Grok, effectively rendering these recruits as "nothing but ghosts in the machine."

A Disturbing Trend of Deception

What's particularly alarming here is the casual reliance on AI to fabricate the illusion of success. The DOD's distancing from the photos, claiming ignorance, does little to alleviate concerns. It rather highlights a disturbing trend of deception within governmental communication strategies. When truth becomes a casualty in the quest for positive PR, accountability is thrown out the window.

The Issue with Data

The murky waters of the DOD's recruitment data only thicken the plot. With no public release of the data to back the claimed surge in female recruitment, we're left in the dark, forced to take their word for it. This lack of transparency not only erodes trust but also begs the question: What are they hiding?

A Divisive Narrative

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's comments further fan the flames of a divisive narrative within the military ranks. By outrightly stating women should not be in combat roles and alleging that standards have declined due to diversity, equity, and inclusion efforts, Hegseth not only undermines the contributions of countless women but also ignites a dangerous debate that distracts from the core issue at hand: the integrity of military recruitment and representation.

Conclusion: A Call for Authenticity and Accountability

This entire episode serves as a stark reminder of the perils of prioritizing propaganda over truth. As the lines between reality and AI-generated content blur, the need for authenticity and accountability becomes paramount. The DOD's misstep is not just a PR blunder; it's a breach of public trust. In the pursuit of showcasing diversity and success, let's not resort to digital deception. Our armed forces, and indeed our society, deserve a narrative grounded in truth, not in the pixels of AI-generated fantasies.
