'Wow': MSNBC host dumbfounded after 'bat poop crazy' Sunday Trump press conference

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Rambling: A Masterclass by Donald TrumpIgnoring Gaza and Misrepresenting RwandaThe Ballroom FantasyConclusion: Beyond the Absurd

Donald Trump, Ali Velshi (MSNBC screenshot)

Immediately after Donald Trump concluded a press availability with Ursula von der Leyen, president of the European Commission, MSNBC host Ali Velshi admitted he was stunned by the president's rambling and blustering answers to reporters' questions.

During the approximately 30-minute presser, the president praised the new ballroom at his Scotland golf resort, went on another extended rant about windmills, blew off questions about Gaza and pulled numbers out of the air about genocide in Rwanda.

After the president shut down any more questions, the camera cut to an open-mouthed Velshi who remarked, "Wow. That was bat poop crazy."

"That was crazy," he repeated while holding up a sheaf of papers. "I took lots of notes–– this is in no particular order: windmills are killing us. 7 million people died in Rwanda, 'But I ended that.' The Rwanda genocide ended in 1994. 'Energy needs no subsidy.' America subsidizes energy to a great degree."

"Windmills are, yeah, yeah 'windmills are killing us –– they're the most expensive form of energy.' He's going to have a deal with the EU in an hour. He's 'not in a good mood,' he said that? He said there's not going to be a deal really with the EU, he said very specifically, he said mostly I'm just going to charge tariffs. It's not really a deal per se, people are just going to pay tariffs," he continued.

Velshi pointed out, "He went on for a little while about a ballroom and how they don't have a ballroom in the White House because they've never had a developer in charge of the White House. But now he just built a beautiful ballroom and he could just drop it into the White House and that's going to be fantastic. Wow."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Rambling: A Masterclass by Donald Trump

In a spectacle that left MSNBC host Ali Velshi visibly shaken, Donald Trump delivered what can only be described as a masterclass in political rambling and blustering, a performance so bewildering it compelled Velshi to declare it "bat poop crazy." This incident unfolded after a press availability with Ursula von der Leyen, president of the European Commission, showcasing not just Trump's penchant for digression but also his remarkable ability to veer off into realms of utter absurdity.

The Scotland Golf Resort Ballroom and Windmill Rants

Let's break this down: Trump somehow managed to praise the new ballroom at his Scotland golf resort in the midst of what should have been a focused political discourse. This detour into self-promotion is a classic Trump maneuver, blurring the lines between presidential duties and personal business interests. But why stop at self-aggrandizement when you can also wage war on windmills? Yes, windmills. Trump's extended rant on these harbingers of renewable energy, which he baselessly claims are "killing us," reveals not just a stark anti-environmental stance but a bewildering disconnection from reality.

Ignoring Gaza and Misrepresenting Rwanda

Brushing off questions about Gaza and pulling numbers "out of the air" about the genocide in Rwanda, where Trump inflated the tragic death toll to 7 million and bizarrely claimed credit for ending it, underscores a troubling disregard for factual integrity and sensitivity. The Rwandan genocide, a dark chapter in human history that ended in 1994, is diminished by such flippant and incorrect references.

Energy Subsidies and EU Tariffs

Trump's assertion that "Energy needs no subsidy" flies in the face of the substantial subsidies the U.S. government provides to the energy sector, particularly fossil fuels. This statement not only showcases a profound ignorance (or deliberate misrepresentation) of how energy markets operate but also a refusal to acknowledge the role of subsidies in shaping energy policy and environmental impacts.

Moreover, Trump's comments on striking a deal with the EU morphed into a vague threat to primarily impose tariffs, revealing a transactional view of international relations that prioritizes punitive measures over genuine negotiation.

The Ballroom Fantasy

Perhaps the most bizarre of all was Trump’s lamentation over the lack of a ballroom in the White House and his boast about the "beautiful ballroom" he could "just drop" into it. This remark, seemingly trivial, encapsulates the former president's preoccupation with grandeur and luxury—a preoccupation that starkly contrasts with the responsibilities of presidential office.

Conclusion: Beyond the Absurd

Trump's performance, as recounted by a stunned Ali Velshi, was not just "bat poop crazy"; it was a glaring exhibition of the former president's disregard for accuracy, diplomacy, and the gravity of the office he once held. The rambling about golf resorts, windmills, and ballrooms, coupled with the dismissal of critical global issues and the mishandling of facts, paints a picture of a leader more consumed by personal grievances and fantasies than by the pressing realities facing the world.

In the end, this episode serves as a stark reminder of the chaos and distraction that often dominated Trump's tenure—a tenure marked by a relentless assault on truth, decorum, and the very principles of governance. As observers and as citizens, we are left to ponder the implications of such leadership and the importance of vigilance in holding our leaders accountable.
