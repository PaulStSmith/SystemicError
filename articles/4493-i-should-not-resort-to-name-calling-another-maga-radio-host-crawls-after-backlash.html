'I should not resort to name-calling': Another MAGA radio host crawls after backlash

The Gothamist reports a new right-wing radio host is apologizing after lobbing over-the-top insults at a Democrat.

Joining the ranks of other conservative radio jockeys who have also had to recant statements and apologize to their audience, WABC radio host Sid Rosenberg apologized to New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Wednesday after calling him an “America-hating, Jew-hating, Radical Islam cockroach” and a “jihadist” on X this week.

Rosenberg unleashed his attack after Mamdani called U.S. airstrikes on Iran “a catastrophic escalation in an illegal war of aggression.” But as criticism rolled in over Tuesday, Rosenberg claimed on social media, “No one can force me to apologize. I won’t do it. I did nothing wrong!”

One day dater — the same day WABC CEO John Catsimatidis said the station would not tolerate “personal attacks” — a more humble Rosenberg released a video saying his comments were “a bit over the top” and claiming he had apologized to Mamdani.

“I should not resort to name-calling,” Rosenberg said on his show, “Sid and Friends in the Morning” on Wednesday. “I’ve already apologized to the mayor and it was heartfelt.”

Rosenberg is no stranger to racist or incendiary comments, however, having referred to tennis legend Venus Williams as an “animal” and once calling the U.S. Women’s Olympic soccer team “juiced-up dykes,” according to The Gothamist.

The social media platform known has X been ramping up apologies from vociferous right-wing entertainers. Rosenberg is the second MAGA-curious radio personality who caught President Donald Trump’s war-fever and said something regrettable this week. The Milwaukee Journal Sentinel reported inflammatory right-wing radio host Dan O’Donnell also recently apologized on X a few days after calling for someone to “take out” Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz, Trump-style, after Trump killed Iranian supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei with joint air strikes.

O’Donnell posted a March 1 request on X to: “Now take out the Supreme Leader of Minnesota,” and claimed “We will be greeted as liberators,” followed with the tweet of an AI photo of Gov. Walz wearing a black turban with the phrase “Death to fraud investigations!” in quotes.

Like Rosenberg, O’Donnell deleted his inflammatory post and apologized, writing on X that “I want to take a moment to offer my sincerest apologies for a post I made about Minnesota’s Governor that, quite frankly, I am deeply ashamed of.”

“Time will tell how sincere of an apology it is,” Mamdani said, reports the Gothamist.

Our Analysis:

The Apology Tour: A Right-Wing Radio Rhetoric Rendezvous

In the latest episode of the Right-Wing Radio Meltdown, we witness Sid Rosenberg, a WABC radio host, issue what can only be described as a half-hearted apology for his vitriolic comments directed at New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani. Rosenberg’s initial tirade, where he labeled Mamdani an “America-hating, Jew-hating, Radical Islam cockroach” and a “jihadist,” is a stark reminder of the inflammatory rhetoric that has become all too common in certain media circles.

The Art of the Non-Apology

Rosenberg’s backtrack is a classic example of the non-apology apology. After boldly declaring on social media that no one could force him to apologize and insisting he did nothing wrong, he suddenly shifts gears. Under the watchful eye of WABC CEO John Catsimatidis, who publicly condemned personal attacks, Rosenberg decides his comments were “a bit over the top.” This sudden change of heart seems less about genuine remorse and more about salvaging a career teetering on the edge of acceptability.

A Pattern of Provocation

This isn’t Rosenberg’s first rodeo with racist and incendiary comments. His history of derogatory remarks, including referring to Venus Williams as an “animal” and calling the U.S. Women’s Olympic soccer team “juiced-up dykes,” paints a picture of a man who thrives on controversy and disparagement. The pattern is undeniable and disturbing, suggesting a deep-seated belief in the shock value of his words rather than a momentary lapse in judgment.

The Echo Chamber Effect

Rosenberg is not alone in his rhetorical recklessness. The article mentions another right-wing radio host, Dan O’Donnell, who also found himself in hot water over a post calling for someone to “take out” Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz. These incidents are symptomatic of a larger problem within certain segments of the media: an echo chamber that amplifies and rewards extreme statements, only to feign surprise and regret when called to account.

Apologies: Genuine Remorse or Strategic Retreat?

The apologies from Rosenberg and O’Donnell raise questions about the sincerity behind their words. Is the regret genuine, or are these apologies merely strategic retreats in the face of backlash? The pattern of behavior suggests the latter. True remorse is accompanied by change, something yet to be seen in a meaningful way from figures like Rosenberg.

The Costs of Incendiary Commentary

The real victims of this kind of media behavior are the individuals and communities targeted by these attacks. Mamdani’s response, questioning the sincerity of the apology, highlights the damage done by such baseless accusations. It’s not just about hurt feelings; it’s about the perpetuation of harmful stereotypes and the fanning of the flames of division.

Conclusion: The Need for Accountability

The cycle of inflammatory commentary followed by forced apologies will continue until there are real consequences for those who engage in this toxic behavior. Media organizations, advertisers, and audiences must hold figures like Rosenberg and O’Donnell accountable, demanding more than just empty apologies. Without meaningful action, the apologies are nothing more than words, as hollow as the promises of change that inevitably follow.

In a media landscape that increasingly rewards sensationalism over substance, it’s time to demand better from those who command the airwaves. Only then can we hope to see a shift away from the divisiveness that plagues our public discourse.
