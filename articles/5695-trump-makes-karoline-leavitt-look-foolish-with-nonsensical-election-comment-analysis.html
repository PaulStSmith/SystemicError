Trump makes Karoline Leavitt look foolish with 'nonsensical' election comment: analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 6, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt during a press briefing on March 17, 2025 (Andrew Leyden / Shutterstock.com)

An attempt by Karoline Leavitt to explain away an outlandish Donald Trump comment has backfired and made her look foolish, a political commentator has suggested.

The White House Press Secretary had been asked to explain what the president meant when he called for the Republican Party to “nationalize the voting” in 15 states. The specific states were not mentioned by Trump, who made the comment in a speech broadcast on the former United States Deputy Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation Dan Bongino’s podcast.

Trump’s claim that the Republican Party should “nationalize” the elections had been walked back by Leavitt, who suggested it is the president’s way of referring to Congress passing the SAVE Act. This was then rebuked by Trump, who confirmed he had been talking about voting in specific states, rather than across the country.

CNN analyst Aaron Blake wrote, “She [Leavitt] claimed Trump was instead referring to Congress passing the SAVE Act, a bill that aims to combat noncitizen voting in federal elections – something that is already illegal and that experts say rarely happens.

“That was nonsensical, of course. The SAVE Act would add federal requirements to register to vote, sure, but Trump was talking about taking over the voting in a specific number of places (15) – not passing a law that would apply to the whole country.

“And sure enough, Trump on Tuesday made clear that he meant what he said. Asked by CNN’s Kaitlan Collins what he meant by nationalize the election, he made no mention of the SAVE Act and doubled down on the idea of the federal government asserting a more expansive form of control.”

Trump would pass comment again on the 15 states, saying that if a state cannot run an election it should be up to “the people behind me” to do something about it, referring to the Republicans standing behind him in the Oval Office at the time.

Blake added, “It’s the kind of contradiction that would be a scandal in any other administration. Trump’s top spokesperson said he meant one thing, and that turned out not to be true. If nothing else, it’s a huge mark against a spokesperson’s credibility. After all, their job is to quite literally speak for the president.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Political Double-Speak: The Trump-Leavitt Fiasco

In a world where political rhetoric often dances on the edge of absurdity, the recent debacle involving Karoline Leavitt, the White House Press Secretary, and Donald Trump, the former U.S. President, stands out as a prime example of the chaotic communication that has come to define Trump’s political saga. The incident, dissected with surgical precision by CNN analyst Aaron Blake, offers a stark glimpse into the convoluted world of political messaging and the dire consequences of miscommunication.

At the heart of this debacle is Trump’s contentious call to “nationalize the voting” in 15 unspecified states—a statement that, even by Trumpian standards, raises eyebrows for its blatant disregard for federalism and the decentralized nature of U.S. elections. Leavitt’s attempt to spin this outrageous demand as a misinterpreted endorsement of the SAVE Act—a legislative proposal aimed at combating the virtually nonexistent issue of noncitizen voting—was nothing short of political gymnastics.

The SAVE Act Smokescreen

Leavitt’s redirection towards the SAVE Act is a classic case of political obfuscation. By linking Trump’s comments to a piece of legislation that ostensibly aims to secure elections, Leavitt sought to sanitize a fundamentally undemocratic suggestion—federal takeover of state elections—under the guise of election security. This maneuver, however, crumbles under the slightest scrutiny, as Blake adeptly points out. The SAVE Act, with its focus on federal elections and noncitizen voting, bears no relevance to Trump’s explicit call for the nationalization of voting processes in specific states.

Trump’s Unwavering Stance

The unraveling of Leavitt’s narrative was hastened by Trump himself, who, in a characteristic display of bluntness, doubled down on his initial claim without so much as a nod to the SAVE Act. This contradiction between Trump’s unabashed admission and Leavitt’s strained reinterpretation lays bare the inherent challenge of serving as a spokesperson for a figure whose statements often defy conventional political logic.

The Credibility Conundrum

Blake’s analysis underscores a critical point: the significant toll such contradictions take on a spokesperson’s credibility. In a role that hinges upon the accurate and faithful conveyance of the principal’s views, Leavitt’s misrepresentation—intentional or otherwise—casts a long shadow over her ability to effectively serve as the president’s mouthpiece. This incident not only exposes the precarious nature of speaking for Trump but also serves as a cautionary tale about the perils of attempting to rationalize the irrational.

Conclusion: The Scandal of the Status Quo

What stands out in this entire episode is the normalization of scandal within the Trump administration. In any other political context, a spokesperson’s failure to accurately represent the president’s views on such a fundamental issue would be met with widespread outrage and calls for accountability. Yet, as Blake articulately notes, this kind of contradiction has become par for the course under Trump’s tenure—a disheartening reflection of the lowered standards to which we have collectively acquiesced.

In a political era marked by misinformation and strategic ambiguity, the Trump-Leavitt fiasco is a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance, critical analysis, and the refusal to accept the unacceptable as the new normal.
