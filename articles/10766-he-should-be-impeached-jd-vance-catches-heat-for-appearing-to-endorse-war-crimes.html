Vance over the weekend took to X, where he wrote, "Killing cartel members who poison our fellow citizens is the highest and best use of our military."

"Democrats: let’s send your kids to die in Russia," he added. "Republicans: actually let’s protect our people from the scum of the earth."

Liberal commentator Brian Krassenstein responded to Vance's comments, saying, "Killing the citizens of another nation who are civilians without any due process is called a war crime."

Vance then dropped a profane reply:

"I don’t give a s--- what you call it," the V.P. said.

Krassenstein replied, "Killing 11 people in this way could be classified as an extrajudicial killing under international human rights law, potentially exposing U.S. officials to responsibility. Good luck in 2029. Any idea what the penalty for such a crime would be?"

In a separate post, Krassenstein said, "I think this thread tells you everything you need to know about JD Vance and the way he views law and order."

"Pathetic," the influencer added. "He should be impeached for this tweet."

The interaction also saw responses from other big names who lobbed allegations at the V.P.

Ex-GOP strategist Cheri Jacobus said, "You seem hell bent on getting yourself convicted of war crimes one day, Skippy."

PatriotTakes asked, "Are you sure there were no human trafficking victims on that boat?"

Law professor Anthony Michael Kreis asked, "What’s the point of the Constitution and the rule of law or did you skip that day at YLS?"

Our Analysis:

The Vance Controversy: A Manifestation of Reckless Rhetoric

In a political climate that increasingly resembles a battlefield, U.S. Vice President JD Vance's recent social media outburst not only exemplifies the degradation of discourse but also flirts dangerously with advocating for war crimes. The statement made by Vance, as reported, "Killing cartel members who poison our fellow citizens is the highest and best use of our military," followed by a divisive comparison between Democratic and Republican priorities, brings to light not just a polarized viewpoint but a gross misunderstanding or disregard for international law and human rights.

The War Crime Allegation

Brian Krassenstein's response to Vance’s post is not an overreaction but a necessary highlight of the gravity of Vance's words. When a figure of such high political stature openly advocates for the killing of individuals—labeling them broadly as "cartel members" without due process or trial—it's not just a slip of the tongue; it's a dangerous precedent. Krassenstein's reminder that killing civilians without due process could be classified as an extrajudicial killing under international human rights law is a sobering reality check for those who may dismiss Vance’s words as mere rhetoric.

Vance's Dismissive Rebuttal

Vance's profane dismissal of Krassenstein's valid criticism not only underscores a concerning disdain for international law and human rights but also reflects a broader issue within the current political landscape: the erosion of accountability. When public officials believe they can speak without consequence, or worse, without consideration for the weight their words carry, it paves the way for a dangerous normalization of extreme views.

The Broader Implications

The outcry and criticism from various quarters, including former GOP strategist Cheri Jacobus, PatriotTakes, and law professor Anthony Michael Kreis, are indicative of the broader implications of Vance’s statement. Jacobus’s warning of potential war crimes, PatriotTakes’s concern over the fate of human trafficking victims, and Kreis’s invocation of the Constitution and the rule of law are all critical perspectives that highlight the multifaceted dangers of Vance’s approach to governance and diplomacy.

A Call for Accountability

The call for Vance’s impeachment over this tweet, while seemingly drastic to some, is not without merit. In an era where the lines between political rhetoric and actionable policy are increasingly blurred, holding public officials accountable for statements that could incite violence or advocate for international law violations is paramount. This is not merely about punishing a controversial statement but about safeguarding the principles of due process, human rights, and international law that form the bedrock of democratic societies.

In conclusion, Vice President JD Vance’s comments do not exist in a vacuum. They reflect a broader, more troubling trend of political figures using their platforms to espouse views that undermine the very foundations of law, order, and human decency. As such, this incident should not be brushed aside as another example of political hyperbole but should be met with the full weight of scrutiny and accountability it deserves. The implications of Vance’s words are far-reaching, affecting not just the immediate political landscape but the international perception and moral standing of the United States.
