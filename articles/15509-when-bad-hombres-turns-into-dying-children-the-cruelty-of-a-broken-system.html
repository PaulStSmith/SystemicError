When "Bad Hombres" Turns Into Dying Children: The Cruelty of a Broken System

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 29, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

AI generated illustration.

In 2016, the American public was sold a simple idea: immigration enforcement would target only the "bad hombres" — violent criminals, gang members, traffickers. Families, children, law-abiding immigrants were, we were told, not the targets. America, we were promised, had no quarrel with the innocent.

It was a comforting narrative. It allowed those unconcerned with immigration issues to turn away, to trust that the system would be fair and compassionate, that the line between good and evil would be clear.

Today, nearly a decade later, that comforting fiction lies in ruins.

In just the past week, news has surfaced that at least three children — one battling cancer — have been deported or threatened with deportation. Three children whose only "crime" was needing medical treatment, security, and a future. Three children who were promised a chance at life, only to find themselves ensnared by a system that treats paperwork as more important than human lives.

This is not an isolated anomaly. It is the inevitable result of an immigration enforcement system that values documents over decency, status over survival. A machine set in motion with promises of "only the bad guys" that, once built, could not — or would not — distinguish between a cartel member and a kid with chemotherapy ports in his chest.

Under Trump's administration, cruelty was not a byproduct — it was the point. While early in his term institutional guardrails still constrained the worst impulses, over time those guardrails were weakened, dismantled, or destroyed. The immigration system became increasingly hostile not just to criminals, but to the innocent, the vulnerable, the dying.

The tragedy is that they have built a system where such "mistakes" are not aberrations but entirely predictable outcomes. Where the deportation of a child with cancer is not viewed as a scandal within the system, but as business as usual — unless public outrage happens to catch the right headlines at the right time.

We must be clear: the problem is not simply which party holds power. The problem is the system itself — a system that speaks of "law and order" while meting out death sentences to the defenseless, a system that has forgotten the human beings at its center.

America cannot claim to be a beacon of hope while it deports children to their deaths. We cannot speak of values while our immigration practices betray them at every turn.

It is not enough to elect better leaders. It is not enough to hope for better headlines. We must demand a different system — one that holds human dignity as the highest priority, that treats vulnerable children not as "cases" but as lives worth saving.

Anything less is a betrayal — not just of the immigrants who trusted us, but of the nation we pretend to be.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
