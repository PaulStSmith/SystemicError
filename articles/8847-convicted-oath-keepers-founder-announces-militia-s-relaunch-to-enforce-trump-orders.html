Rhodes was released from prison earlier this year when Trump commuted his 18-year sentence for seditious conspiracy, and Media Matters reported that he was planning to reform his paramilitary organization to help enforce federal immigration law.

“Right now, under federal statutes, President Trump can call us up as the militia if he sees it necessary, especially for three purposes: to repel invasions, to suppress insurrections, and to execute the laws of the union, and right now, we see all three of those in play,” Rhodes told the right-wing Gateway Pundit outlet.

“We have — we’re facing an ongoing invasion of this country, it has not stopped. We’re facing an insurrection by the left, and we’re also facing a direct blocking and resistance against enforcing federal law — federal immigration laws, attacking ICE agents, you know, trying to ram them and run them off the road, throwing bricks to their windows. All of that is going on, and so under those three purposes, he can call up the militia.”

“The militia is all of us, so the National Guard is part of the militia, which is why it’s completely lawful for him to use the National Guard as he has, and he should do more of that, I think, across the country,” Rhodes added.

Rhodes was convicted in 2023 for his role in organizing and carrying out the Jan. 6 insurrection at the U.S. Capitol, but he was among nearly 1,500 participants who Trump pardoned or commuted the sentences of shortly after returning to the White House.

“He could just say given the circumstances of an invasion, insurrection, and resistance against federal law being being enforced, I’m calling up the militia, ordering all the men to come together [in] every county, put the veterans in charge of training and organizing all the other men and just have them stand too and await his orders,” Rhodes said.

“He can do that and I think he should do that, and so that’s what I’ll be advocating for from him. So from the bottom, from down the rank and file, we can let President Trump know that we’re ready to serve, encourage him to do that, call us up as a militia, order us all to come together in our counties under his command, which gives you complete legal sanction to do what you’re doing.

Our Analysis:

The Rekindling of a Militia: A Dangerous Precedent

In an audacious move that reeks of both desperation and defiance, Stewart Rhodes, the founder of the right-wing militia group, the Oath Keepers, has declared his intention to resurrect the organization. This announcement comes on the heels of his release from prison, a freedom granted by President Donald Trump, who commuted Rhodes’ 18-year sentence for seditious conspiracy. According to reports from Media Matters, Rhodes is not only planning to reform this paramilitary outfit but is also positioning it as a tool for Trump to enforce federal immigration laws. The gall of this proposition cannot be overstated.

Rhodes’ rhetoric, as reported by the Gateway Pundit, dangerously blurs the lines between lawful enforcement and vigilantism. He claims, without a shred of irony, that the United States is facing an “ongoing invasion,” an “insurrection by the left,” and resistance against federal law enforcement, particularly in the context of immigration. His solution? For Trump to call upon the militia—which he erroneously equates to include every citizen and the National Guard—for the purposes of repelling invasions, suppressing insurrections, and enforcing laws.

The Militia Misconception

First and foremost, Rhodes’ assertion that “the militia is all of us” and that this includes the National Guard is a gross misinterpretation of United States law and a dangerous conflation of concepts. The National Guard operates under both federal and state jurisdictions and cannot be whimsically commandeered by a militia leader’s call to arms, nor can civilians be lumped into this category with legal legitimacy.

The Seditious Convict’s Call to Arms

It’s particularly alarming that Rhodes, convicted for his role in the January 6 insurrection—an event that shook the foundations of American democracy—is the voice advocating for such actions. His conviction speaks volumes about his intentions and the lengths he’s willing to go to undermine the rule of law. Yet, here he is, unfettered and unabashed, proposing to organize and mobilize a force under the guise of legality and patriotism.

Trump’s Role in This Dangerous Game

The fact that President Trump commuted Rhodes’ sentence, along with nearly 1,500 others involved in the January 6 insurrection, sets a harrowing precedent. It signals a willingness to overlook, even endorse, actions that directly threaten the democratic institutions of the United States. Rhodes’ subsequent call to arms, under the banner of supporting Trump’s agenda, further illustrates the perilous path this administration seems willing to tread—empowering and legitimizing extremist groups under the guise of law enforcement and national security.

A Call for Rationality and the Rule of Law

Rhodes’ proposal for Trump to “call up the militia” and his plan to advocate for such a mobilization is not only legally dubious but is also a clear and present danger to the fabric of American society. It undermines the legitimate functions of law enforcement agencies and the National Guard, sows discord and fear, and emboldens extremist elements.

The United States finds itself at a crossroads, where the rule of law and the whims of those who seek to subvert it for their own ends are in direct conflict. The rhetoric espoused by Rhodes and his ilk must not be normalized or entertained as a viable form of governance or law enforcement. The dangers of empowering a convicted insurrectionist to lead a civilian militia, under the endorsement of a president, cannot be overstated. It is a path fraught with peril, one that leads away from democracy and toward authoritarianism.

As we stand witness to these developments, it is incumbent upon every citizen to critically assess the implications of such actions and rhetoric. The sanctity of the rule of law, the principles of democracy, and the very fabric of our society depend on it.
