Max Miller's bizarre claim on Trump's 'nasty criminal misconduct' stuns Newsmax host

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosAugust 15, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Impunity Is the Template

Rep. Max Miller is not being asked to answer for a misunderstanding. He is facing public accusations of physical abuse, a court filing from his ex-wife, a restraining order sought by Stephanie Grisham, and a House Ethics Committee review. His response was not accountability but imitation: he invoked Donald Trump’s history of surviving allegations as proof that he should stay in the race. That is the whole political move in miniature. Not innocence. Not explanation. Just borrowed impunity.

Power Chooses the Frame

The key fact is not Miller’s denial. It is that the institutions around him are still treating his survival as a political calculation. Republicans set a Wednesday deadline to replace him on the Ohio ballot. Trump privately told Miller his reelection was in trouble. The House Ethics Committee is now reviewing the allegations. In other words, actual institutional power sits with party leaders, ballot access rules, and congressional discipline mechanisms. Miller is not defending a mandate; he is trying to outlast consequences.

Resilience Is Not a Defense

Miller’s core argument on Newsmax was that Trump “stayed in the fight,” so he should too. That is not a defense of conduct. It is a confession of the party’s moral collapse. He is borrowing Trump’s brand of shameless endurance to turn repeated allegations into a badge of toughness. But the comparison is not even honest. The source text notes that Trump was found liable for sexual abuse in 2023, and the 2016 scandal Miller referenced was the Access Hollywood tape, not some flood of “nasty criminal misconduct” claims. He is laundering abuse into political mythology.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The framing here tries to shift attention from the alleged conduct to the optics of accusation. That is deliberate misdirection. Finnerty, to his credit, pushed back: physical abuse allegations are not the same as nasty campaign chatter. Miller still tried to flatten the difference, as if “where’s the proof?” were a sufficient answer to a pattern alleged by multiple women in different settings. This is how political damage control works now: deny, blur, compare, and wait for the audience to get tired.

Republican Standards Are Optional

The party’s internal response tells the real story. Bernie Moreno, Miller’s ex-father-in-law, said Miller “fails” basic standards of character and should not serve in the House. That is not a tiny family dispute. It is a Republican senator publicly acknowledging that the threshold for holding office exists, even if his party usually ignores it. When a party can recognize the standard and still keep bargaining over whether the candidate stays on the ballot, it is not enforcing norms. It is rationing shame.

What This Reveals

This is not just about one embattled congressman. It is about a political culture trained by Trump to treat allegations as branding opportunities and accountability as weakness. Miller’s example shows how authoritarian habits spread: first among the powerful, then through the media channels that normalize them, then into the rank-and-file assumption that survival is the same thing as legitimacy. The problem is not that a few bad men lie. The problem is that a whole political machine keeps rewarding them when they do.
