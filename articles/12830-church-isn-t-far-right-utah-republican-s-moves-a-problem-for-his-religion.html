'Church isn't far-right': Utah Republican's moves a 'problem' for his religion

U.S. Senator Mike Lee (R-UT) is reportedly causing "severe repercussions" to his own religion due to his rabid embrace of Donald Trump and his tactics.

Lee, a MAGA lawmaker who backs Trump every chance he gets, was in hot water when he posted insensitive statements about a shooting in which a Democratic lawmaker was killed. His religion, the Church of Latter-Day Saints, has been harmed by those comments and others, according to a Politico report.

Lee's "online persona has further damaged his already frayed relationship with The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints," whose members are often referred to as Mormons, according to Politico.

"It wasn’t just Washington that was buzzing over Lee’s response to the tragedy. It also set off a debate in Salt Lake City. At the global headquarters of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, a discussion over how to respond — if at all — began in earnest, according to two people granted anonymity to discuss private conversations," the report states. "Several of the church’s senior leaders saw the posts and were concerned. Lee is a prominent Latter-day Saint, arguably the best-known elected Church member in the nation. His insensitive words were reflecting poorly on the faith at large. One church official estimated the backlash from Lee’s posts would have severe repercussions for the church’s public image, the first person said."

The report notes that, "for Lee’s church, that bellicose online alter ego poses a problem."

"Even as some Christian denominations have delved into Christian nationalism or partisan politics during the Trump era, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (commonly referred to as the Mormon Church) has consistently called on members to do the opposite: to be 'peacemakers' in the civic square," according to the report.

After its analysis, "The church, however, made the decision to stay quiet."

"Top-down, public rebukes of individual members are very rare in the faith, much less when action could be seen as a partisan exercise and further exacerbate tensions. The church, as an institution, has a strict political neutrality policy; its members, while encouraged to be Christ-like, are to act as they wish," according to the report. "But the incident brought to a boil something that had been simmering in Salt Lake City for some time. 'Mike Lee is far-right,' said one emeritus general authority, a former high-ranking church official, granted anonymity to speak openly. 'The church is not far-right.'"

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Alliance: Senator Mike Lee's Collision Course with His Own Faith

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of cognitive dissonance, U.S. Senator Mike Lee (R-UT) has managed to embroil himself in a controversy that not only tarnishes his own reputation but also casts a long, dark shadow over the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church). His rabid embrace of former President Donald Trump and the MAGA movement has, according to a Politico report, resulted in "severe repercussions" for his own religion—a faith that has long prided itself on being above the political fray and focused on the principles of peace and neutrality.

The Incident That Sparked Outrage

Lee's insensitivity came to a head following a tragic shooting in which a Democratic lawmaker was killed. His response, deemed insensitive by many, was not just a topic of heated discussion in Washington, D.C., but also set off alarms within the global headquarters of the LDS Church in Salt Lake City. The concern was not limited to the lay members; senior church leaders were reportedly worried about how Lee's comments and overall online behavior were reflecting on the LDS faith.

It's one thing to have a political opinion; it's another to air such views in a manner that directly contradicts the teachings and ethos of one's faith. Lee, as the "arguably the best-known elected Church member in the nation," according to Politico, should be acutely aware of the weight his words carry and the reflection they cast on the LDS community.

The LDS Church's Delicate Dance

The LDS Church's response, or lack thereof, to Lee's antics is a study in institutional caution. The church, which has a "strict political neutrality policy," found itself in a precarious position. While the church's leaders were concerned about Lee's behavior, they ultimately chose silence. This decision, while in line with the church's stance of not publicly rebuking individual members, especially in politically charged situations, raises questions about the limits of such neutrality.

The Politico report suggests that there was a significant internal debate within the church about how to handle the situation. This debate underscores a larger issue: how religious institutions navigate the choppy waters of modern politics, especially when their members, particularly those in positions of power, engage in behavior that is at odds with the institution's values.

A Reflection on the Church's Political Stance

The LDS Church's historical commitment to being "peacemakers" in the civic square is commendable. In an era where some Christian denominations have dived headfirst into the murky waters of Christian nationalism and partisan politics, the LDS Church's call for its members to embody Christ-like neutrality is a beacon of hope. However, incidents like the one involving Senator Lee test the strength and integrity of such stances.

The report's mention of an emeritus general authority labeling Lee as "far-right" and distancing the church from such political extremities is telling. It highlights a rift, not just between Lee and the LDS Church, but potentially within the church itself—a rift between its core values and the actions of some of its most prominent members.

The Broader Implications

Senator Mike Lee's actions and the subsequent fallout are more than just a personal or institutional crisis; they are symptomatic of a larger problem facing many religious communities today. The entanglement of faith and politics, especially in the context of the polarizing Trump era, poses significant challenges to religious institutions striving to remain neutral and focused on their spiritual missions.

As the LDS Church grapples with the repercussions of Lee's behavior, the incident serves as a cautionary tale for other religious organizations. The delicate balance between encouraging members to engage civically and maintaining an apolitical stance is more precarious than ever.

In conclusion, while Senator Lee's allegiance to Trump and the MAGA movement might have won him political points in certain circles, it has simultaneously exposed the vulnerabilities of religious institutions in political whirlwinds. The LDS Church's struggle to navigate this storm is a stark reminder of the complexities at the intersection of faith and politics—a reminder that, in the end, silence might not always be golden.
