Revealed: The real for a Republican’s last minute withdrawal from Montana’s Senate race

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 8, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Calculated Cowardice of Steve Daines

Sen. Steve Daines of Montana with U.S. Army Gen. Frank Grass in 2015 (Creative Commons)

CNN’s Manu Raju reported that two sources told him that Sen. Steve Daines (R-MT) waited until the last minute to withdraw from his Senate race for a key reason.

Writing Thursday, Raju said that Daines was scared that a popular Democrat would announce they’d run for the open seat and win.

On Wednesday, Daines’ announcement was made just eight minutes before the candidate filing deadline closed in Montana. It means there was only a tiny moment for well-known Democrats like former Sen. Jon Tester or former Govs. Brian Schweitzer and Steve Bullock to declare their intentions to run. With such a Democrat on the ballot in a year that is expected to be a blue wave, it would have put the Montana Senate seat in play.

The following day, Thursday, NBC News reported that Daines is among two possible candidates being considered to replace Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

The President is reportedly furious with her after her testimony this week before the House and Senate Judiciary Committees.

The White House has already contacted Daines’ office, the two sources told NBC News. The report didn’t indicate if anyone else was contacted by the White House.

Independent candidate Seth Bodnar said in a statement that Daines “has so little respect for Montana Republicans that he withdrew at the last minute to coronate his handpicked successor instead of giving them a voice at the ballot box.”

Our Analysis:

The Calculated Cowardice of Steve Daines

In a revelation that can only be described as a masterclass in political cowardice, Sen. Steve Daines (R-MT) employed a strategy that underscores the lengths to which some politicians will go to cling to power. According to CNN’s Manu Raju, Daines waited until the metaphorical eleventh hour to announce his withdrawal from the Senate race, a move so calculated it reeks of desperation. The reason? Fear—a palpable terror that a popular Democrat might throw their hat into the ring and, heaven forbid, win.

Let’s dissect this, shall we? Daines’ announcement came a mere eight minutes before the candidate filing deadline in Montana. This eleventh-hour retreat was not a spur-of-the-moment decision spurred by personal epiphany but a calculated maneuver designed to hamstring the Democratic opposition. This act deprived well-known Democrats—former Sen. Jon Tester, former Govs. Brian Schweitzer, and Steve Bullock—of the time needed to mobilize a campaign. In a year anticipated to be a “blue wave,” the presence of a heavyweight Democrat on the ballot could have significantly altered the political landscape in Montana.

The Audacity of Manipulation

What Daines’ maneuver reveals is a level of audacity that should be insulting to the democratic process. The implication that the electoral field must be manipulated to secure a win is a damning indictment of Daines’ confidence in his party’s policies and appeal. Seth Bodnar, an independent candidate, hit the nail on the head, stating that Daines “has so little respect for Montana Republicans that he withdrew at the last minute to coronate his handpicked successor instead of giving them a voice at the ballot box.”

This isn’t just about disrespecting the opposition; it’s a brazen disregard for the electorate’s right to choose from a full spectrum of candidates. It’s a tacit admission that when faced with a formidable opponent, the strategy of choice is to limit the options available to voters. This maneuver is antithetical to the principles of democracy and should be recognized as such.

A New Chapter or More of the Same?

Adding a layer of intrigue to this political drama, NBC News reported that Daines is under consideration to replace Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, with the President allegedly furious at Noem following her recent testimony. The White House’s swift outreach to Daines’ office post-announcement raises questions about political chess games played at the highest levels. Yet, it doesn’t absolve Daines of the disdainful tactics employed to secure his position.

The underlying issue here is not just about one senator’s strategic retreat. It’s about a pervasive mindset within certain political circles that the end justifies the means, ethics be damned. It’s about a willingness to undermine the electoral process for personal or party gain. And most disturbingly, it’s about the normalization of such tactics by those who wield significant power.

Conclusion

Steve Daines’ last-minute withdrawal from the Senate race is a stark reminder of the cynical strategies employed in the political arena. It’s a maneuver that should not be applauded but condemned for what it is: a blatant attempt to manipulate electoral outcomes. As the dust settles, one can only hope that Montana’s voters see this act for what it truly is—a desperate ploy by a politician more concerned with maintaining power than respecting the democratic process.
