An explosive new piece in Salon claims that recruits signing up to become ICE agents under President Donald Trump are motivated more by power and aggression rather than "a sense of noble duty."

Dr. Geoffrey Grammer, a retired U.S. Army colonel and psychiatrist, wrote that before Trump, "ICE agents were typically motivated by integrity, courage, resilience, and a strong sense of duty and allegiance to the Constitution."

Today, however, Grammer claims the emphasis on very public raids and heavy-handed tactics attracts recruits with "authoritarian and punitive traits" who relish the "satisfaction from the suffering they cause" to vulnerable populations.

Grammer traces the change back some 10 years to the moment Trump made his way down his namesake Tower's golden escalator.

"Trump has spent the last decade maligning immigrants in the United States, often with fabricated stories to create fear within communities and foster xenophobic hatred among his supporters," Grammer writes. "This paved the way for harsh, and sometimes sadistic, policies involving aggressive raids, warrantless detentions, family separations and imprisonment under conditions condemned by human rights groups."

What's more, Grammer claims that the huge allotment for ICE funding in Trump's "Big Beautiful Bill Act" will inevitably lead to a surge in hiring more aggressive personality types.

"With the change in the type of recruit expected, their loyalty is more likely to be rooted in Trump himself than in the principles of the Constitution," Grammer writes. "New agents may not view themselves as ethical public servants, but rather as followers driven by anger and retribution, which can result in unchecked sadism."

This mindset of this new kind of ICE agent, Grammer argues, has created "serious risks to human rights and constitutional values." It also highlights how close America is to "a new era of fascism" made possible -- and enforceable -- by "the evolution of ICE under Donald Trump."

Our Analysis:

The Trumpian Transformation of ICE: A Dive into Authoritarian Recruitment

In an incendiary piece by Salon, the transformation of the U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) under the Trump administration is laid bare, revealing a disturbing shift in the motivations and characteristics of its recruits. Dr. Geoffrey Grammer's analysis, steeped in his experience as a retired U.S. Army colonel and psychiatrist, posits a chilling evolution from a force motivated by "integrity, courage, resilience, and a strong sense of duty and allegiance to the Constitution" to one that attracts individuals drawn to power, aggression, and the infliction of suffering.

From Duty to Sadism: A Transformation Rooted in Trump’s Rhetoric

The essence of this transformation, according to Grammer, is not a natural progression but a calculated manipulation of public sentiment and agency culture. The article suggests that Donald Trump's decade-long campaign of demonizing immigrants, coupled with the implementation of "harsh, and sometimes sadistic, policies" such as aggressive raids, warrantless detentions, and family separations, has fostered an environment ripe for attracting recruits with "authoritarian and punitive traits."

The Allurement of Authoritarianism

It's important to dissect the claim that today's ICE recruits are driven more by "satisfaction from the suffering they cause" than by a sense of noble duty. This assertion, if true, marks a dangerous pivot towards authoritarianism within a federal agency tasked with significant power over vulnerable populations. The Salon article draws a straight line from Trump’s rhetoric to the type of individual now swelling the ranks of ICE, suggesting that these individuals are more loyal to Trump the persona than to the principles enshrined in the U.S. Constitution.

Funding Fascism?

The "Big Beautiful Bill Act," with its "huge allotment for ICE funding," is highlighted as a mechanism likely to exacerbate this shift. By pouring money into the agency, the Trump administration didn't just expand its operational capacity but also its potential to hire individuals aligned with Trump's punitive philosophy towards immigration. This influx of funding, paired with an aggressive recruitment drive, could create an enforcement arm not just for current policies but for an ideology that stands in stark contrast to constitutional values.

A Constitutional Crisis in the Making?

Grammer's analysis raises an alarm not just about the treatment of immigrants but about the very foundations of American democracy. The suggestion that new ICE agents may view themselves as "followers driven by anger and retribution" rather than as ethical public servants poses a "serious risk to human rights and constitutional values." This evolution, or rather devolution, of ICE under Trump's stewardship is framed as a stepping stone towards "a new era of fascism."

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Reform

The Salon article, through Grammer's lens, serves as a stark reminder of the dangers inherent in politicizing law enforcement agencies and utilizing them as tools for authoritarian purposes. The recruitment of individuals motivated by power and aggression, over a commitment to duty and the Constitution, not only undermines the agency's integrity but also poses a grave threat to the principles of justice and human rights.

The shift in ICE's recruitment and operational ethos as described in the Salon piece is not just a matter of policy change but a fundamental challenge to American values and democracy. It's a call to action for all who cherish those values to reflect and to demand reform. As we dissect this analysis, it's crucial to remember that the implications extend far beyond immigration policy to the very heart of what America stands for.
