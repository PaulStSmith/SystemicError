Trump announces Iran strikes will continue 'uninterrupted' until 'peace' is achieved

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a visit to a Whataburger in Corpus Christi, Texas, U.S., February 27, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

President Donald Trump announced on Saturday that the strikes the U.S. and Israel coordinated against Iran will continue throughout the week until peace is achieved.

Early Saturday morning, the U.S. and Israel struck multiple sites across Iran. Israel struck sites where the country’s military and political leadership were located, which killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, the dictator who has ruled the country since 1989. The U.S. struck Iran’s nuclear and ballistic missile facilities, even though Trump previously claimed those sites were “totally obliterated” during a similar strike last year.

“The heavy and pinpoint bombing ... will continue, uninterrupted throughout the week or, as long as necessary to achieve our objective of PEACE THROUGHOUT THE MIDDLE EAST AND, INDEED, THE WORLD!” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

Trump also claimed that he expects the Iranian people to begin taking steps to form a new government.

“That process should soon be starting in that, not only the death of Khamenei but the Country has been, in only one day, very much destroyed and, even, obliterated,” Trump wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Promise of Peace Through Destruction

In a recent announcement that has sent ripples through the international community, President Donald Trump has declared that the coordinated strikes by the U.S. and Israel against Iran will persist until an elusive “peace” is achieved. This strategy, as outlined by Trump, involves a relentless campaign of bombings aimed at crippling Iran’s military capabilities and, ostensibly, its government’s grip on power. The audacity of this approach, however, is matched only by its dangerous naiveté.

The Fatal Strike on Leadership

The operation’s most startling outcome thus far has been the assassination of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, a figure who, notwithstanding his contentious rule, stood as a symbol of Iran’s sovereignty. The killing of a nation’s leader, especially under the ambiguous banner of promoting peace, is a move that reeks of imperial hubris. It not only destabilizes an already volatile region but also sets a chilling precedent for international conduct.

The Mirage of Obliterated Threats

Trump’s assertion that the U.S. has targeted Iran’s nuclear and ballistic missile facilities—despite previous claims of their total destruction—betrays a troubling inconsistency in the administration’s narrative. It begs the question: were the initial assessments of damage grossly overstated, or is this a tacit admission of the inefficacy of such strikes in deterring Iran’s alleged nuclear ambitions? The contradiction here is not just rhetorical but reveals a deeper misjudgment of the situation’s complexity.

Peace Through Uninterrupted Bombing?

The notion that peace can be achieved through “heavy and pinpoint bombing” is a fallacy that history has repeatedly debunked. The strategy outlined by Trump, far from fostering peace, is likely to sow the seeds of enduring conflict. The idea that such aggression will lead to the formation of a new, presumably more compliant, government in Iran is speculative at best and dangerously misguided at worst. It underestimates the Iranian people’s resilience and their potential to rally against foreign aggression.

The Illusion of a Swift Resolution

Trump’s confidence in a quick resolution to the crisis, evidenced by his anticipation of a new governmental process in Iran, is both baseless and naive. The destruction of a nation’s infrastructure and the assassination of its leader do not pave the way for democracy or peace. They create power vacuums, unleash chaos, and foster the very extremism such actions purport to eliminate.

In conclusion, the strategy employed by the Trump administration is a stark reminder of the folly of attempting to impose peace through military might. The simplistic notion that uninterrupted bombing campaigns will lead to a stable and peaceful Middle East ignores the complex realities on the ground. It is a dangerous gamble, one that risks not only the lives of countless innocents but also the fragile balance of international peace. The pursuit of peace through such means is not just flawed; it is fundamentally contradictory.
