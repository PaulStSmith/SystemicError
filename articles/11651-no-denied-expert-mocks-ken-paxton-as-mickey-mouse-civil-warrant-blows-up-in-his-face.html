'No, denied!' Expert mocks Ken Paxton as 'Mickey Mouse civil warrant' blows up in his face

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Folly of Legal Desperation: Analyzing Ken Paxton's Court Loss

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton speaks during a news conference in Washington, U.S., April 26, 2022. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz/File Photo

A former federal prosecutor mocked the Texas Attorney General on Wednesday after he suffered his latest loss in court.

Glenn Kirschner, a former Deputy U.S. Attorney, joined progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen on the show "Legal Breakdown" to discuss the event. Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has sued state Democrats to force them to return from Illinois, which would allow Texas Republicans to pass new election maps that eliminate four districts held by Democrats of color.

Kirschner described Paxton's legal filing as a "Mickey Mouse civil warrant" because of how the judge reacted to it.

"Judge Larson looked at this filing and said, 'First of all, I don't have subject matter jurisdiction over this civil warrant that was issued by the Texas House of Representatives,'" Kirschner said.

"And then he went on to say in a four-page ruling, 'By the way, even if I did have subject matter jurisdiction, I think there may be a venue problem,'" he continued. "I think there may be a problem on the underlying merits of this claim. And that's just for openers."

"So basically, no, denied. Go away," Kirschner added.

Paxton and Governor Greg Abbott have made numerous threats against the Democrats since they left. For instance, Abbott has threatened to remove Democrats from their elected seats if they do not return. Abbot has also pledged to call a special session every 30 days until the Republicans pass their new election maps.

However, it seems those efforts could hit more "roadblocks," as Kirschner described them, because of Paxton's lawyering.

"This is beyond laughable, and it's really beyond poor lawyering," Kirschner said. "I think desperation and folly are perhaps the best ways to characterize this."

Our Analysis:

The Folly of Legal Desperation: Analyzing Ken Paxton's Court Loss

In a spectacle that could only unfold in the current political theater, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has once again found himself at the center of legal mockery and judicial rebuke. This time, his attempt to coerce state Democrats back from Illinois—a maneuver aimed at enabling Texas Republicans to redraw election maps to their advantage—has been spectacularly thwarted.

Glenn Kirschner, a former Deputy U.S. Attorney, dissected Paxton's latest legal fiasco with a level of scorn that befits the situation, during an appearance on Brian Tyler Cohen's "Legal Breakdown." Paxton's legal strategy, described derisively as a "Mickey Mouse civil warrant," not only failed to impress but was outright dismissed by Judge Larson for lacking subject matter jurisdiction. But the judge didn't stop there; he went further to question the venue and the underlying merits of the claim. In essence, Larson's response was a polite judicial version of “try again, but actually don’t.”

Paxton's legal blundering, as dissected by Kirschner, is not merely an isolated incident of incompetence. It's emblematic of a broader, more troubling trend: the weaponization of the legal system to serve partisan ends. This is not about the rule of law; it's about the rule by law, where legal mechanisms are manipulated to secure political victories.

Governor Greg Abbott's threats to oust Democrats from their elected positions if they fail to return, coupled with his pledge to incessantly call special sessions to pass the controversial election maps, reeks of desperation and authoritarianism. It's a stark reminder of how low some politicians are willing to stoop to cling onto power, even at the expense of democratic principles and governance norms.

Kirschner's critique of Paxton's "lawyering" as "beyond laughable" and indicative of "desperation and folly" is not just a scathing rebuke of a failed legal strategy; it's a damning indictment of the current state of political leadership in Texas. The attempt to force Democrats back to pass partisan election maps—maps that would disenfranchise voters, particularly those in districts held by Democrats of color—is not just legally dubious; it's morally bankrupt.

The judicial slapdown Paxton received is a temporary reprieve in the ongoing battle over voting rights and election integrity in Texas and across the United States. However, it's a poignant reminder of the resilience of the judiciary in the face of political pressure and manipulation.

But let's be clear: while this episode may provide a moment of schadenfreude for critics of Paxton and Abbott, the underlying issues at play are no laughing matter. The relentless push to redraw election maps, the overt threats against democratically elected officials, and the abuse of the legal system are symptoms of a democracy in distress.

As observers, critics, and participants in this democratic experiment, we must remain vigilant. The battle for the soul of American democracy is fought in courtrooms, at ballot boxes, and in the court of public opinion. Paxton's latest legal misadventure is a stark reminder of the lengths to which some will go to undermine that democracy. It's up to all of us to ensure they don't succeed.
