Maddow warns Trump’s military moves backfiring: 'Americans don’t like being threatened'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Maddow Warning: A Closer Look

(Screengrab via MSNBC)

MSNBC's Rachel Maddow warned Monday night that President Donald Trump's insistence on using physical force to try to "cow and hurt people" is about to backfire spectacularly.

Maddow began her show Monday night, pointing out that mass protests have erupted in Israel, with calls for a nationwide work stoppage.

"People marched all over Israel against their own government, demanding an end to the war in Gaza," she said, noting that some protesters set fires and blocked highways. Half a million people protested in Tel Aviv on Sunday night, she noted, the equivalent of 17 million Americans.

Trump, she said, has already had more than 5 million protesters in the United States. And more are coming, she warned.

"As Trump continues to arrogate all government power to himself, as his popularity drops further and further with the American people, as he increasingly uses physical force to try to cow and hurt people in this country — and the American public really don’t like that — the pace of protest against Trump appears to be picking up here again," she said.

Maddow pointed to a USA Today report finding anti-Trump protest numbers have hit an "all-time high."

"Higher than anything he saw at any point in his first term. Higher than anything since the huge outbreaks of thousands of protests we had in the summer of 2020 over the police killing of George Floyd," said Maddow, adding that a professor who studies protests and mass mobilization said this may just be the beginning, as Trump sent soldiers into U.S. cities.

“I would say that using the military for civil control is a real wild card here," she quoted the professor as saying. "In many cases, the move to authoritarian kinds of tactics will actually bring out more protesters.”

Maddow added, "Yes, I can see how that might work. And as of right now, Washington, D.C. is certainly showing that."

Protesters in the nation's capital unfurled a massive banner along an overpass that said, "Be brave. Demand Democracy." And thousands of soccer fans at a National Women’s Soccer League game in Washington, D.C., chanted "Free D.C.! Free D.C!"

"Day after day after day after day, the president’s military takeover of D.C. has been met with direct action, in these really big protests like the one we saw on Saturday, also in smaller ones as well," said Maddow.

She added: "You know, I don’t know if it’s related or not, but I think Americans just really don’t like being threatened with their own military. And maybe that’s the reason why two of the other U.S. cities that had really big protests against Trump this weekend were two other U.S. cities where Trump has threatened that he’s going to send the troops next."

Our Analysis:

The Maddow Warning: A Closer Look

MSNBC's Rachel Maddow has once again illuminated the dark corridors of American politics with her Monday night broadcast, drawing stark parallels between the mass protests erupting in Israel and the burgeoning dissent against President Donald Trump's increasingly draconian measures. Maddow's piercing analysis sheds light on a critical junction in American democracy, where the use of physical force to quash opposition is not only evident but alarmingly escalating.

Trump's Strategy: Intimidation and Force

The core of Trump's approach, as Maddow astutely points out, is the reliance on physical force to "cow and hurt people." This tactic, far from being a novel invention of the Trump administration, is a tried and tested method of authoritarian regimes. Yet, in the context of a country that prides itself on democratic ideals and civil liberties, such tactics are not only reprehensible but fundamentally counterproductive. The American populace, as Maddow suggests, has a visceral aversion to being bullied by its government, especially through the utilization of the military against its citizens.

The Israeli Parallel

Maddow's comparison to the mass protests in Israel is particularly telling. These protests, ignited by governmental actions deemed unjust by a significant portion of the populace, offer a mirror to the potential trajectory of dissent in the United States. The massive turnout in Tel Aviv, equated to an American equivalent of 17 million people, underscores a universal truth: when governments overreach, the people push back.

The Rise in Anti-Trump Protests

The surge in anti-Trump protests, hitting an "all-time high" according to a USA Today report, is a testament to the growing unrest. Maddow’s reference to the summer of 2020's protests following George Floyd's murder by police underscores a significant pattern of increased public mobilization in response to perceived injustices. The deployment of soldiers into U.S. cities, a move Maddow rightly identifies as a "real wild card," is not just a step towards authoritarianism but a potential catalyst for even greater public outcry.

The Role of Public Sentiment

The unfolding scenario in Washington, D.C., as detailed by Maddow, with protesters unveiling banners that demand democracy and chant for freedom, is emblematic of a broader national sentiment. Americans, it seems, are not just resistant to being threatened with military force by their own president; they are actively repelled by it. The significant protests in cities threatened with military deployment corroborate Maddow's assertion that such tactics are likely to backfire.

Conclusion: A Precarious Moment

Maddow's analysis, grounded in the uncomfortable reality of current events, highlights a precarious moment in American history. The increasing militarization of responses to civil dissent is not only alarming but a clear indication of a democracy in distress. This strategy of intimidation, far from quelling dissent, appears to be fueling it, serving as a rallying cry for an increasingly mobilized and motivated populace.

In essence, Maddow's report is not just a commentary on the present but a warning for the future. As the Trump administration continues to escalate its use of force, the response from the American public is likely to be equally forceful. The true spectacle, however, will not be the administration's might, but the resilience and resolve of the American people in the face of authoritarian encroachment. The ultimate outcome of this confrontation remains to be seen, but one thing is clear: democracy, in its truest sense, is at stake.
