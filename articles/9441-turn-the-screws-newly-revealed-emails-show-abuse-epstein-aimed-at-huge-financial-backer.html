The chairman of the board of Apollo Global Management had been backing away from paying Epstein $40 million a year for various advisory work after Epstein’s first Florida indictment related to charges of underage sex.

“So Mr. Epstein threw a tantrum,” the Times reported. “The typo-strewn tirade was one of dozens of previously unreported emails … in which Mr. Epstein hectored Mr. Black, at times demanding tens of millions of dollars beyond the $150 million he had already been paid.”

And the pressure campaign appeared to work. Black continued to fork over tens of millions of dollars in fees and loans, according to the report. After Epstein served jail time for soliciting prostitution from a minor, many of his contacts backed away. But not Black, who “kept Epstein afloat for years,” according to the Times. Eventually, Black was pushed out of the private equity firm he co-founded over his ties to Epstein.

“The two men had been personally entwined for more than two decades. When a former girlfriend accused Mr. Black of sexual assault, he turned to Mr. Epstein for advice about paying her millions of dollars to keep it quiet,” said the Times, according to court records. “Another woman said in a lawsuit that Mr. Black had raped her at Mr. Epstein’s Manhattan townhouse. She eventually dropped the lawsuit.”

“And, for reasons that are unknown, Mr. Black wired hundreds of thousands of dollars to at least three women who were associated with Mr. Epstein,” the Times added, also according to court documents and notes taken by congressional investigators that were shared with The Times.

In one email, a frustrated Epstein boasted of his protective services to Black: “If you reflect on your financial life, you have been kept safe, had remarkable results and no disasters.”

Their intimate tangle of machinations might explain how Epstein could berate Black so abusively in letters that sometimes referred to his children as “r-------” for supposedly making a mess of his estate.

“The emails … sent in 2015 and 2016 to Mr. Black through his personal assistant, as well as to a handful of his advisers, show another dimension of Mr. Epstein’s cruelty,” the Times reports. “While he was known for ingratiating himself with the rich and powerful, he could also veer into nastiness and was willing to turn the screws on his biggest client.”

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Alliance: Epstein and Black’s Tangled Web

The recent exposé by the New York Times on the tumultuous relationship between Jeffrey Epstein and billionaire Leon Black reads like a Shakespearean tragedy, if Shakespeare had been obsessed with the corrupt underbelly of billionaire advisory services and sex crimes. At the heart of this saga is a narrative that reveals much more than just a personal feud; it uncovers a disturbing picture of power, manipulation, and the dark currency of silence and compliance.

Epstein’s Tantrum Over Money: The Tip of the Iceberg

The article begins with a seemingly trivial anecdote about Epstein throwing a “tantrum” over not being paid his usual $40 million a year by Black, his biggest financial supporter. This episode, however, is emblematic of the deeper, more sinister dynamics at play. Epstein, despite his first Florida indictment related to charges of underage sex, felt entitled to demand tens of millions of dollars beyond the $150 million he had already pocketed. The audacity of his demands, post-incarceration for soliciting prostitution from a minor, speaks volumes about the impunity enjoyed by individuals like Epstein. They operate in a realm where legal boundaries are seemingly negotiable, and financial entitlements know no moral limits.

Leon Black: The Enabler?

Leon Black’s role in this sordid affair cannot be understated. Despite Epstein’s notorious reputation and criminal conviction, Black continued to provide financial lifelines, effectively keeping Epstein “afloat for years.” This relationship did not merely extend to business advisories; it spilled over into more personal and morally questionable territories. When faced with accusations of sexual assault, Black reportedly sought Epstein’s counsel on handling the fallout, even going as far as to pay off accusers.

Additionally, Black’s mysterious transactions, wiring hundreds of thousands of dollars to at least three women associated with Epstein, raise alarming questions. These actions do not just hint at a deep-seated complicity; they scream of a partnership rooted in exploiting power dynamics and silencing dissent.

Epstein’s Cruelty and Manipulation

The article sheds light on another facet of Epstein’s personality: his cruelty and willingness to manipulate his biggest client. Epstein’s berating of Black, including derogatory remarks about his children, underscores a relationship not based on mutual respect but on coercion and control. Epstein’s self-vaunted “protective services” to Black, boasting of keeping him “safe” and ensuring “remarkable results,” resemble less the assurances of a financial advisor and more the threats of a mobster ensuring their mark remains under their thumb.

Conclusion: A Tale of Moral Bankruptcy

The intertwining of Epstein and Black’s lives is more than just a story of financial transactions and personal vendettas. It is a stark illustration of how the wealthy and powerful can create their own ecosystems of complicity, where money silences morality and legality becomes a mere inconvenience. The relationship between Epstein and Black is not an anomaly; it is a systemic symptom of a broader societal malaise where wealth and power shield the elite from the consequences of their actions.

In the end, this saga serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked power and the corrosive influence of money in obfuscating justice and decency. It’s a narrative that demands not just our attention but our outrage and action.
