'Have we no shame?' Trump castigated by judge sickened by 'appalling' ruling

A federal judge smacked down the Trump administration's effort to drastically cut grants to the National Institutes of Health, delivering a scathing rebuke and calling out the government's clear case of racial and LGBTQ discrimination.

The Trump administration cut NIH grants as part of a sweeping effort to combat waste, fraud, and abuse in federal spending. The administration has targeted non-essential and programs related to diversity, equity and inclusion programs, with Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. testifying that officials aimed to “eliminate DEI funding and redirect resources toward real poverty reduction."

"We will fund cutting-edge research at the NIH while cutting risky or non-essential services," he said.

On Monday afternoon, Politico's Kyle Cheney reported that Judge William Young, an appointee of Ronald Reagan, declared the cuts "illegal" and "void," and ordered many restored.

"I am hesitant to draw this conclusion, but I have an unflinching obligation to draw it – that this represents racial discrimination. And discrimination against America’s LGBTQ community. That’s what this is. I would be blind not to call it out. My duty is to call it out."

Young called it "palpably clear" that the directives and the specific terminated grants were "designed to frustrate, to stop, research that may bear on the health – we’re talking about health here — the health of Americans, of our LGBTQ community. That’s appalling."

Young said he'd "never seen a record where racial discrimination was so palpable."

"I’ve sat on this bench now for 40 years. I’ve never seen government racial discrimination like this ... I ask myself, how can this be?" he railed, according to Cheney.

Young said he has a duty to speak the truth in every case, and questioned if he hadn't had Constitutional protections to do so, would he have stood up "against all this?"

"Would I have said, 'you can’t do this?' You are bearing down on people of color because of their color. The constitution will not permit that," he said.

Young added: "Have we fallen so low? Have we no shame?"

Our Analysis:

A Federal Rebuke: Unveiling Discrimination in Policy

In a recent ruling that can only be described as a judicial thunderclap, Judge William Young handed down a decision that starkly exposes the Trump administration's efforts to cut grants to the National Institutes of Health (NIH) as not just misguided, but as actions permeated with racial and LGBTQ discrimination. This ruling is more than a simple legal setback; it's a scathing indictment of the administration's purported war on waste, fraud, and abuse in federal spending. Let's dissect the layers of this decision and its implications.

The Facade of Fiscal Responsibility

Under the guise of combating "waste, fraud, and abuse," the Trump administration took a scalpel—or more accurately, a sledgehammer—to NIH grants, specifically targeting programs related to diversity, equity, and inclusion. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. articulated this approach as an effort to "eliminate DEI funding and redirect resources toward real poverty reduction." However, this reasoning crumbles under scrutiny. Labeling initiatives aimed at supporting marginalized communities as "non-essential" or not directly related to poverty reduction reveals a fundamental misunderstanding—or deliberate ignorance—of the systemic issues that exacerbate poverty and health disparities.

Judicial Integrity vs. Administrative Inequity

Judge Young's ruling is a beacon of judicial integrity and a damning critique of the Trump administration's policies. Appointed by Ronald Reagan, Judge Young's conservative credentials underscore the depth of his condemnation, making it clear that the issue at hand transcends partisan politics—it's about basic human rights. His declaration that the cuts were "illegal" and "void" is a powerful affirmation of the law's role in protecting those rights.

Judge Young did not mince words, stating it was "palpably clear" that the administration's directives were designed to frustrate and halt research impacting the health of Americans, particularly those in the LGBTQ community. His admission of never having seen such blatant government racial discrimination in his four decades on the bench is a sobering reminder of how egregious these actions were.

The Constitutional and Moral High Ground

The judge's reflections on constitutional protections and moral obligations are particularly poignant. His hypothetical questioning of whether he would have had the courage to stand up against these policies without constitutional protections underscores the gravity of the situation. "Have we no shame?" he asks, echoing the sentiment of many who view these policies as not only legally indefensible but morally reprehensible.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Action

This ruling is a stark reminder of the importance of judicial oversight in safeguarding the rights and well-being of all Americans, especially the most vulnerable. It also serves as a call to action for policymakers to reflect on the real-world impact of their decisions on marginalized communities. The Trump administration's attempt to cut NIH grants under the banner of fiscal responsibility was unmasked for what it truly was: a discriminatory policy that targeted racial and LGBTQ Americans under a thinly veiled guise of budgetary concern.

In the end, Judge Young's decision is not just about restoring funding; it's about affirming the principle that government policies must promote equality and inclusion, not exacerbate division and discrimination. It's a ruling that should be celebrated, studied, and remembered as a pivotal moment in the ongoing struggle for justice and equity in America.
