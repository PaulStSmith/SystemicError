'No one voted to deport moms': Pro-Trump town regrets its choice

A Missouri town that voted overwhelmingly for Donald Trump in the 2024 election has come to regret its choice after a beloved neighbor was detained by ICE, according to The New York Times.

In the farming town of Kennett, MO, population 10,000, Times reporter Jack Healy met with residents who "supported in theory" Trump's tough talk on immigration. Now they're rallying around a Hong Kong immigrant named Ming Li Hui, who went by "Carol" in her adopted hometown.

"In the 20 years since she arrived from Hong Kong, she had built a life and family in Kennett, working two waitressing jobs and cleaning houses on the side," Healy wrote. He quoted a Kennett city councilwoman who said, “Everyone knows Carol."

But Hui has since been arrested and detained by the Department of Homeland Security as she awaits deportation back to her birth country.

“I voted for Donald Trump, and so did practically everyone here,” one woman told Healy. “But no one voted to deport moms. We were all under the impression we were just getting rid of the gangs, the people who came here in droves.”

According to Hui's attorney, she hoped to gain permanent resident status in the U.S. after paying "an American citizen $2,000 to enter into a sham marriage."

"Ms. Hui was never criminally charged for the fake marriage, which ended in divorce in 2009," Healy wrote. "Court papers indicate that she has no criminal record." In the meantime, Hui stayed on in the U.S. even as her tourist visa expired.

One resident told Healy, "She’s exactly the sort of person you’d want to come to the country. I don’t know how this fits into the deportation problem with Trump.” Another declared, "I can’t believe they’re doing this to her."

Some of Hui's neighbors "said they had implored state and national Republican lawmakers representing the area to intervene to stop Ms. Hui’s deportation, but had gotten mostly cursory responses," Healy wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Regret: Kennett's Harsh Awakening

The narrative unfolding in Kennett, Missouri, serves as a stark illustration of the chasm between political rhetoric and the harsh realities of policy implementation. The town, which overwhelmingly supported Donald Trump in the 2024 election, is now steeped in regret as they rally around Ming Li Hui, affectionately known as "Carol," a Hong Kong immigrant facing deportation by ICE.

A Reality Check for Kennett

The tale of Kennett is rife with ironies that are as predictable as they are tragic. Residents who were once buoyed by Trump's "tough talk on immigration" are now confronted with the personal cost of such policies. The distinction they attempted to draw between the "bad immigrants" (gangs and droves of others) and Carol, the hardworking, community-integrating immigrant, underscores a profound misunderstanding of the indiscriminate nature of sweeping immigration crackdowns.

Carol's Story: A Mirror to America's Soul

Carol's journey is emblematic of many immigrants in America: a quest for better opportunities, the pursuit of the American Dream, and the hope of legal residency. Her attempt to gain permanent resident status through what was described as a "sham marriage" highlights the desperate and often precarious paths immigrants navigate, driven by the desire for a better life.

It's crucial to note that Carol was never criminally charged for the fake marriage, and court papers indicate she has no criminal record—facts that should ostensibly mitigate the harshness of her treatment. And yet, here we are, witnessing the potential expulsion of a person who, by many accounts, embodies the qualities desired in an immigrant: hardworking, community-oriented, and law-abiding.

The Echo Chamber of Political Rhetoric

The residents of Kennett are now experiencing the repercussions of subscribing to and voting based on a narrow, idealized view of immigration policy. Their shock at Carol's deportation proceedings reveals a disconnect between their perception of Trump's policies and the reality of those policies' application. "But no one voted to deport moms," one resident lamented, a statement that reeks of naïveté. The harsh truth is, they voted for exactly that: a blanket approach to immigration that makes no distinction between a "mom" and a "gang member."

A Callous Response from Leadership

The response—or lack thereof—from state and national Republican lawmakers is indicative of a broader issue within the political establishment: a disconnection from the constituents' needs and a failure to address the unintended consequences of their endorsed policies. The cursory responses received by those who implored their representatives to intervene on Carol's behalf highlight a systemic failure to engage with the complexities of immigration in a humane and nuanced manner.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Political Accountability

The situation in Kennett serves as a cautionary tale about the importance of understanding the full implications of political rhetoric and policies. It's a bitter pill for the town to swallow, but one that is necessary. The regret and disbelief expressed by Carol's neighbors are not enough to reverse the damage done. This story should serve as a wake-up call for voters everywhere to demand more from their elected officials and to hold them accountable for the real-world outcomes of their policies.

In the end, Kennett's regret is a reflection of a larger American dilemma: the struggle to reconcile the ideals of liberty and opportunity with the realities of policy and governance. As Carol's story unfolds, it remains to be seen whether it will catalyze a broader reevaluation of immigration policies or if it will simply be dismissed as collateral damage in the pursuit of a misguided interpretation of "security."
