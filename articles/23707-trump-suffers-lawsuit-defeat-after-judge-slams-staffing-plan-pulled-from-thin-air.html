Trump suffers lawsuit defeat after judge slams staffing plan 'pulled from thin air'

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

FEMA Was Ordered to Invent a Reason for Its Own Destruction

The Number Came First

The Trump administration’s Department of Homeland Security ordered FEMA to plan for cutting its workforce from roughly 23,000 employees to 11,500 by the end of fiscal year 2026. Agency specialists recommended maintaining or increasing staffing. Their expertise did not shape the decision. According to the court record, then–senior FEMA official Karen Evans supplied the 50 percent target first, and staff were told to manufacture a strategy around it.

That is not workforce planning. It is institutional sabotage disguised as arithmetic.

Power Sat Above FEMA

The institutional hierarchy is straightforward: DHS controlled the mandate, while FEMA personnel were expected to rationalize it. The agency’s supervisors and human-capital chief could assess operational needs, but they could not protect FEMA from political superiors determined to impose a predetermined result.

Coverage that says the administration merely “cut FEMA staffing” risks flattening this chain of command. The central fact is that officials stripped the emergency agency of authority over its own workforce. Those with relevant knowledge were overruled by those with greater power.

Expertise Was Reduced to Paperwork

U.S. District Judge Susan Illston found that the staffing figure appeared to have been “pulled from thin air.” The record described Evans tasking an employee with developing a plan to reach the already chosen reduction.

The distinction matters. Officials did not study FEMA’s responsibilities and conclude that half its workers were unnecessary. They selected an outcome and worked backward to produce bureaucratic cover. This is how deliberate damage is routinely laundered into administrative procedure: political leadership dictates the conclusion, and career staff are left to decorate it with spreadsheets.

The Storm Exposed the Fraud

FEMA paused a wave of non-renewals on January 22 because a severe winter storm was approaching. Illston noted the obvious implication: had the storm arrived on another date, a different group of employees would have lost their jobs.

That episode demolished any claim that the cuts reflected a serious assessment of need. The workers suddenly became indispensable when disaster was imminent, then disposable once the calendar permitted it. Emergency capacity was treated not as public infrastructure but as a variable to be reduced until reality became too visible to ignore.

Deleted Messages Were Part of the Governance

The ruling also found that officials used self-set auto-delete timers on Signal and destroyed relevant messages. Illston concluded that Evans intended to deprive the plaintiffs of those communications, and the court will presume the missing texts would have harmed the government’s case.

This was not merely sloppy recordkeeping. On the court’s findings, the same leadership imposing an unsupported staffing target also obstructed scrutiny of how it acted. Arbitrary power and disappearing evidence are complementary practices: make the decision without a defensible basis, then erase the communications that might reveal its actual basis.

Destruction by Target, Justification by Retrospect

The larger pattern is government by predetermined injury. Institutional capacity is marked for reduction, experts are ordered to reverse-engineer an explanation, immediate danger produces a temporary retreat, and records vanish when accountability approaches.

FEMA exists because disasters do not accommodate ideological staffing targets. Cutting its workforce in half would transfer the consequences of federal political choices onto employees, local governments, and communities confronting emergencies. Calling that process confusion or bureaucratic disorder would be misdirection. The record described by the court points to something more coherent: political superiors chose institutional weakening, compelled subordinates to justify it, and tried to control what evidence survived.
