No More Pardons: It's Time to Hold Every Enabler of Fascism Accountable

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 2, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

AI generated illustration

There’s a rot at the heart of American history, and it’s not just the crimes—we’ve had plenty of those. No, the real rot is the refusal to reckon with them. Again and again, we’ve watched as the perpetrators of national trauma walked free in the name of "healing." And every time, we paid for it with blood, disenfranchisement, and the corrosion of public trust. Forgiveness without justice doesn’t heal a nation. It sabotages it.

In 1865, the Confederacy surrendered—but their leaders were never punished. Jefferson Davis didn’t hang. Robert E. Lee became a folk hero. The South was "welcomed back" into the Union as if it hadn’t committed treason to preserve slavery. That mercy gave us the KKK, lynchings, Jim Crow, and a century of institutionalized white supremacy. Entire generations were born under the shadow of a rebellion that was never truly extinguished, because we were too cowardly—or too conciliatory—to make accountability non-negotiable.

In 1974, Richard Nixon resigned in disgrace, only to be pardoned by Gerald Ford. No trial. No jail time. No reckoning for subverting democracy. And what did we get? The message that presidents are above the law, and an accelerating erosion of trust in our institutions. The seeds of executive impunity were sown there, and from them grew an imperial presidency, unchecked surveillance, and a culture of secrecy.

In 2008, Barack Obama inherited a country wrecked by illegal wars and CIA torture programs. Instead of holding Bush-era officials accountable, he looked forward. Instead of justice, we got impunity—and the intelligence-security complex grew even more bloated and unaccountable. The architects of black sites and drone assassinations retired to lucrative consulting gigs and academic posts. Meanwhile, whistleblowers like Chelsea Manning were punished with solitary confinement.

And now, we stand at the edge of another historic moment. The Trump regime has not just bent the machinery of government—it has brutalized it. Armed agents storming homes in the dead of night. Citizens terrorized because of bureaucratic incompetence or political mandate. Children torn from their parents and caged. Constitutional protections treated as optional. This is not theoretical. This is not distant. It is happening now, to mothers and daughters, in American cities.

Consider the recent case out of Oklahoma City, where a mother and her daughters—all U.S. citizens—were dragged out of their home in the rain by heavily armed agents with no accountability, no identification, no recourse. The agents tore through their home, confiscated their phones, their laptops, even their life savings in cash. No charges were filed. No explanations were given. And no agency would admit they were even involved. This is the government we now live under—opaque, authoritarian, and proudly indifferent to your rights.

The only thing more dangerous than this fascist descent is what will happen if, once again, we choose to forgive and forget. No. Not this time. Every ICE agent who broke the law must be investigated. Every DHS official who enabled this reign of terror must be named. Every judge, bureaucrat, strategist, and billionaire who supported this regime should face real consequences—legal, financial, professional. No more book deals. No soft landings. No reputational rehab. They do not deserve a page-turning memoir or a second act at a think tank. They deserve subpoenas and trials.

This is not a call for vengeance. It is a demand for structural justice. Accountability is not revenge—it is the foundation of democracy. Without it, we are telling every future demagogue: do your worst, and we’ll let you walk away. You’ll get a pension. You’ll get a platform. You’ll get to rebrand.

We’ve done that too many times. We let the slavers return to the halls of power. We let the war criminals write op-eds. We let the insurrectionists retire in peace. And every single time, we embolden the next wave of authoritarianism.

This time, we burn the playbook. This time, we name names. This time, we hold the line.

No more pardons. No more mercy for the architects of cruelty. No more appeals to unity that demand silence in the face of state violence.

Justice demands a reckoning—and history is watching. And if we fail to rise to this moment, it won’t be because we didn’t know. It will be because we chose cowardice over courage once again.
