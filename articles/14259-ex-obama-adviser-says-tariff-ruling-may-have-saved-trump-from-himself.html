Ex-Obama adviser says tariff ruling may have saved Trump 'from himself'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Irony of Judicial Intervention: Trump's Tariffs and the Unintended Shield

(Screengrab via CNN)

A federal court’s decision to strike down Donald Trump’s sweeping tariff plan may be a political “black eye” for the administration – but could ultimately help the president if it effectively causes him to be “stymied in doing something that's going to raise costs for people,” according to Democratic strategist David Axelrod.

“It may be a black eye, but it may also be a break for him,” Axelrod claimed.

Speaking to CNN’s Anderson Cooper just after the unanimous ruling dealt a major blow to Trump’s economic agenda, Axelrod argued that the decision may have spared Trump from pushing forward with economic policies that could backfire on American consumers.

“In a weird way, I mean, in the short run, I think it creates a lot of havoc,” Axelrod said. But, the former Obama adviser added, “In the long run, it could save him from himself because these tariffs are bad economic policy, and these tariffs could raise costs on Americans.”

ALSO READ: 'Second biggest scandal': Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

The remarks came after the U.S. Court of International Trade ruled that Trump’s so-called “Liberation Day” tariffs went beyond his legal authority. The court ordered the tariffs vacated in a sweeping decision Wednesday that CNN legal analyst Elie Honig called “a huge setback” for the administration.

“As big a deal as the tariffs were, this is as big a deal in the opposite direction,” Honig said. “It essentially pauses for now, rules illegal and unconstitutional, almost all of the tariffs that have been in place.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Judicial Intervention: Trump's Tariffs and the Unintended Shield

In what can only be described as a dramatic twist of fate, the federal court has delivered what some might call a political "black eye" to Donald Trump by striking down his grandiose tariff plan. This decision, as Democratic strategist David Axelrod astutely points out, might paradoxically serve as a hidden boon for Trump, potentially rescuing him from the political fallout of his own misguided economic policies. Axelrod's commentary, ripe with a blend of irony and insight, underscores a pivotal moment where judicial intervention might just save a president from his own disastrous inclinations.

The Court's Ruling: A Major Setback or a Saving Grace?

The U.S. Court of International Trade's unanimous ruling against Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs is not just a slap on the wrist; it's a full-fledged knockout punch to a significant chunk of his economic agenda. CNN legal analyst Elie Honig didn't mince words when he called it "a huge setback" for the administration. Yet, the blow dealt by the court carries with it an ironic twist of fate. By halting the implementation of these tariffs, the court may have inadvertently spared American consumers from the brunt of "bad economic policy" that would have inevitably led to increased costs.

Axelrod's Analysis: A Silver Lining in the Clouds of Economic Folly

David Axelrod's analysis presents a fascinating dichotomy. On one hand, the court's decision undeniably represents a political defeat for Trump, challenging his authority and undermining a key piece of his economic strategy. On the other hand, Axelrod suggests that this setback could ironically benefit Trump in the long run. By being "stymied in doing something that's going to raise costs for people," Trump may avoid the direct blame for the economic hardships that his tariffs would have caused. This perspective is a testament to the complex and often counterintuitive nature of political strategy and economic policy.

The Larger Implication: When Judicial Checks Meet Economic Balances

This episode serves as a poignant reminder of the critical role that judicial checks play in the governance of the United States. The court's decision to vacate Trump's tariffs on the grounds of exceeding legal authority is a stark demonstration of the constitutional balances that protect the country from potentially harmful unilateral actions by any branch of government. Moreover, it highlights the intricate interplay between legal decisions and their economic ramifications, underscoring the courts' indirect yet profound impact on the livelihoods of everyday Americans.

Conclusion: A Political Paradox Wrapped in Judicial Prudence

In conclusion, the federal court's ruling against Trump's tariff plan is a multifaceted event that encapsulates the complexities of American governance. While it may appear as a straightforward legal defeat for the Trump administration, the layers of political and economic implications suggest a more nuanced outcome. David Axelrod's commentary sheds light on the unintended positive consequences that such judicial interventions can have, even for those whom the decisions ostensibly oppose. It's a stark reminder that in the realm of politics and policy, victories and setbacks are often two sides of the same coin, and what initially seems like a "black eye" could, in fact, be a hidden blessing in disguise.
