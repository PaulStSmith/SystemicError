Trump isn't the disease — he’s just a boil on the American system

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20263 min read

Jamie Dimon Is Not the Exception. He Is the Operating System.

The Source Material

The piece uses Jamie Dimon as its central example: the head of JPMorganChase, former Business Roundtable chair, a lifelong Democrat, and a banker who can talk fluently about inequality while still presiding over the machinery that extracts wealth and power upward. That is the setup. The real argument is not about one executive’s personality. It is about who controls the rules.

Follow the Power, Not the Theater

The article gets one thing right: power sits with a small class of financiers, CEOs, donors, and institutional gatekeepers who can shape legislation, influence the Fed and the courts, and set the agenda before voters ever enter the room. Dimon is not a commentator outside that structure. He is one of its managers. JPMorganChase is not just a bank in the story. It is an instrument of political-economic power.

That is why the usual moral language is too weak. “Sincere.” “Patriot.” “Useful reforms.” These are comforting labels for a system that keeps transferring gains upward and calling the result leadership. When someone helps concentrate wealth, buys influence, and normalizes inequality as the price of expertise, the issue is not whether he means well. It is the damage his role produces.

The Article’s Half-Truth

The strongest part of the source is its refusal to blame Trump for everything. Trump is a consequence, not the root. But the piece still softens the people who made the conditions for him possible by treating elite self-awareness as a meaningful substitute for accountability. It is not.

That is the familiar trick of American establishment politics: let the powerful describe themselves as troubled insiders, then ask the public to reward the candor. A banker says he sees the problems. A donor says he cares. A CEO says he wants reform. Meanwhile the structure that made them wealthy stays intact. The confession becomes camouflage.

Enshittification Is a Class Project

The article’s list of broken systems is not random bad luck. Healthcare, education, justice, public services, wages, unions, monopoly pricing, finance as casino logic, AI investment, tax avoidance: these are connected. The people at the top have spent decades weakening labor, blocking political power, and redirecting national wealth into private hands. Then they act shocked when the country feels cheaper, harsher, and more arbitrary.

That is the political meaning of “enshittification.” It is not just declining quality. It is the lived experience of an economy organized to serve owners first and everyone else later, if at all. The bureaucratic cruelty ordinary people face is not an accident of complexity. It is what a captured system looks like when it is allowed to run long enough.

Why This Matters for Democrats

This is where the article should be read carefully, not reverently. If Democrats are going to be the institutional vehicle against authoritarian politics, they cannot keep treating the finance class as a partner to be managed gently. A democracy cannot be defended by the same donor network that profits from hollowing it out.

That does not mean false equivalence with the GOP. It means the democratic coalition has to decide whether it is organizing around working people and public power, or around a polished version of the same extraction machine. You cannot fight authoritarian politics while excusing the oligarchy that feeds on democratic failure.

The Real Lesson

The story is ultimately about a ruling class that has convinced itself it is a civic class. That delusion is the problem. Not because Jamie Dimon is uniquely evil, but because he is ordinary for the system he represents: powerful enough to shape outcomes, insulated enough to deny responsibility, and rewarded for calling the damage reform.

The deeper pattern is simple. American democracy weakens when wealth becomes power and power becomes self-justifying. Until that link is broken, the country will keep producing more Dimons, more excuses, and more wreckage.
