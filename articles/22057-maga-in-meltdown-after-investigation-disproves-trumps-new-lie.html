MAGA in meltdown after investigation disproves Trump’s new lie

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Lie Was the Assignment

Power, Not Evidence

The basic fact here is not complicated: the Trump administration hired a cybersecurity firm, the firm found no evidence of vote tampering, and Trump-aligned figures responded by attacking the firm rather than the fantasy they wanted confirmed. That is the whole story in miniature. The machinery of state was used to rummage for proof of a stolen election, and when the search failed, loyalists treated the failure as a problem to be punished.

The Real Decision-Makers

Mojave Research was not the power center. ODNI was. Trump allies were. The people with leverage were the ones who could issue contracts, circulate suspicion, and freeze work with a stop-work order. Wareham’s team was hired to provide analysis; the political actors around Trump wanted a verdict. When they did not get one, they tried to destroy the messenger and recast the absence of evidence as evidence of incompetence.

That is not confusion. It is coercion.

The Soros Smear Is the Point

The smear that Mojave was funded by George Soros is not a side detail. It is the mechanism. The accusation taps a long-running antisemitic conspiracy theory about hidden liberal power, and it was used here exactly as these theories are used in practice: to poison a finding before it can become inconvenient.

Wareham, a Republican, had to submit a declaration under penalty of perjury and financial statements to prove he was not part of some imaginary “deep plant.” That is how far the lie had to be carried to protect the bigger lie. The burden was not on the people insisting on fraud. The burden was shifted onto the people who failed to manufacture it.

Election Security as Political Theater

CNN’s reporting makes clear that the administration’s revisiting of the 2020 election has continued into Arizona, including the FBI obtaining records of an audit that confirmed Biden won the state. The same pattern keeps repeating: call the infrastructure suspicious, force institutions to inspect it, then reject the result unless it flatters Trump.

Mojave found “extensive and largely unacceptable weaknesses,” but not evidence that votes were altered. That distinction matters. Weaknesses in systems deserve repair. They do not magically validate a stolen-election narrative. The Trump ecosystem needed those distinctions erased because its political project depends on keeping fraud as a permanent accusation, never a proven fact.

The Bureaucracy That Serves the Myth

The article’s weakest framing is any suggestion that this is a mere clash of “competing factions” inside government. That softens the reality. One faction wanted election security analysis. Another faction wanted a boogeyman. The latter had Trump’s ear and the power to derail the work.

This is what institutional cowardice looks like when it dresses itself up as scrutiny. Agencies do not just fail under pressure; they can become instruments for laundering paranoia into official process. Once that happens, “review” becomes a weapon and “security” becomes cover for political revenge.

The Pattern Is Authoritarian

The larger pattern is simple: demand an investigation, reject the answer, smear the investigator, and keep the myth alive so it can be used again. That is how authoritarian politics feeds itself. Facts are not discovered; they are permitted only when they serve the leader’s story.

The lesson is not that the system was briefly misled. The lesson is that Trump’s circle treats state power as a farm team for propaganda. When institutions fail to confront that directly, they become part of the con.
