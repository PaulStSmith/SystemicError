‘January’s going to be a big month’: GOP lawmakers look to reclaim power from Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Irony of Sudden Republican Concern over Executive Overreach

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures upon returning to the White House from Dover Air Force Base in Washington, D.C., December 17, 2025. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

A growing number of Republican lawmakers are growing dissatisfied with the ever-increasing power of the executive branch under President Donald Trump and are hoping to reclaim some of Congress’ authority starting next month, The Hill reported Saturday.

“I’ve been concerned for ten years about that,” said Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) on the growing power of the executive branch, and the corresponding relinquishing of power by the legislative branch, speaking with The Hill. “I don’t know that it’s getting any better. Seems to be getting worse.”

Trump has strengthened the power of the executive branch of government significantly during his first year back in the White House, clawing back spending already appropriated by lawmakers, declaring national emergencies to circumvent Congress, and asserting control over independent agencies, and in some cases, with the backing of the Supreme Court.

Democratic lawmakers have been nearly unanimous in opposing the Trump administration’s consolidation of power, but as Trump’s second term approaches its first year, a growing number of Republicans are also saying they want to reclaim Congress’s authority as well.

“It’s really important. It’s about the power of the purse. It’s about us determining what gets funded and what doesn’t get funded. It’s setting priorities,” said Sen. John Hoeven (R-ND), speaking with The Hill. “January’s going to be a big month.”

Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-CA) also called for Congress to reclaim its power, warning that Americans’ view of the legislative branch would only continue to sour the longer lawmakers cede their power to the White House.

“I think this is why people are so frustrated with Congress and what Congress has, what, a 15% approval rating?” Kiley said, speaking with The Hill. “I think it’s gonna go down if we don’t get something done here.”

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Sudden Republican Concern over Executive Overreach

In an almost comical twist that could only be scripted in the convoluted narrative of American politics, we find ourselves witnessing a growing cabal of Republican lawmakers who have suddenly awoken to the perils of a bloated executive branch under President Donald Trump. According to a report by The Hill, these politicians, who have long been silent partners in the aggrandizement of presidential power, now seem to be clamoring for a return to the good ol’ days of legislative authority. Let’s dissect this newfound enlightenment with the skepticism it so richly deserves.

The Late Awakening of Sen. Rand Paul and Co.

Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY), who has oscillated between moments of libertarian defiance and baffling Trump loyalty, now claims a ten-year concern over the growth of executive power. Yet, this concern seemingly ebbed and flowed with the political tides, reaching a crescendo now that Trump’s unilateral flexes have become too blatant to ignore. Paul’s lamentation that the situation “seems to be getting worse” rings hollow when one considers his own complicity during moments when he could have stood firm but chose political convenience instead.

Trump’s Unabashed Power Grab

Donald Trump’s tenure has been marked by a relentless push to consolidate power within the Oval Office. From declaring national emergencies to sidestep congressional funding decisions, to manipulating spending, and exerting influence over supposedly independent agencies, Trump has stretched the fabric of executive authority to its breaking points. And let’s not forget the Supreme Court’s complicit nods that have bolstered these moves. The silence from the Republican ranks during these episodes was deafening, with opposition from Democrats painted as partisan rather than principled.

A Sudden Call to Arms from the GOP

Now, as Trump’s second term looms, figures like Sen. John Hoeven (R-ND) and Rep. Kevin Kiley (R-CA) speak of reclaiming Congress’s authority with the zeal of revolutionaries, albeit ones late to their own cause. Hoeven’s invocation of the “power of the purse” and Kiley’s concern over Congress’s dismal approval ratings highlight a sudden, convenient realization: that perhaps there is merit in the legislative branch after all.

The Hypocrisy and Hopes for January

What we’re observing is a classic case of political opportunism wrapped in a thin veil of constitutional concern. These lawmakers’ sudden desire to curb executive overreach is as transparent as it is self-serving. Their warnings that January will be a “big month” for reclaiming power smack of a last-minute scramble to restore a semblance of balance and integrity to a government that has been lopsided for far too long.

In conclusion, while any effort to restore congressional authority should be welcomed, one cannot help but smirk at the irony of these late-to-the-party Republicans. The question remains: Are these calls for action a genuine attempt to restore balance, or merely a political maneuver to distance themselves from the unchecked excesses of a Trump presidency? Only time will tell, but history will not forget the years of acquiescence and the sudden, convenient awakening.
