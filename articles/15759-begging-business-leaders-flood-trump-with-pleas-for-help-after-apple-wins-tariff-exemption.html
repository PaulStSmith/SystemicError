Begging business leaders flood Trump with pleas for help after Apple wins tariff exemption

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Closer Look

An Apple store. (Shutterstock.)

President Donald Trump granted Apple and many other tech businesses an exemption from his "Liberation Day" tariff regime so their supply chains in China would not be disrupted — and now a bunch more companies are coming to beg for similar arrangements, The New York Times reported on Tuesday.

"Almost immediately, top aides to Mr. Trump insisted they had not strayed from their promise to apply import taxes across the economy with minimal, if any, exceptions," reported Tony Romm. Nevertheless, Trump's policy "still caught the attention of many businesses nationwide, igniting a fresh scramble for similar help in the throes of a global trade war."

Per the report, Trump has been inundated with pleas from lobbyists "for the agriculture, construction, manufacturing, retail and technology industries" to make further carveouts from his tariffs, "with many arguing that there are some products they must import simply because they are too expensive or impractical to produce in the United States."

Home Depot, Target, and Walmart are among the retail giants telling Trump they cannot maintain their current prices if they don't get some kind of relief from tariffs.

Trump's policy imposed 10 to 49 percent "reciprocal" import duties on virtually every other country, with rates set according not to actual trade barriers or foreign tariffs, but to the size of the U.S. trade deficits with each country — an idea that seems to have originated with Trump's longtime trade adviser Peter Navarro.

After markets plunged in response to the policy, and amid rising discontent even from a number of Republican lawmakers, Trump announced a three-month grace period in which all countries except China would be subject to the minimum rate of 10 percent.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Closer Look

President Donald Trump's approach to trade policy, specifically his decision to grant exemptions from his "Liberation Day" tariff regime to Apple and other tech giants, is a glaring example of the inconsistency and chaos that has characterized his administration's economic policies. While Trump and his aides insist they are sticking to their guns on applying import taxes across the board, the reality tells a different story—one of preferential treatment and policy backpedaling in the face of mounting pressure from various industry sectors.

Selective Exemptions: The Apple of Trump's Eye

First and foremost, the exemption granted to Apple and similar tech companies starkly contrasts with the administration's proclaimed hardline stance on trade. This move not only undermines the policy's integrity but also sets a dangerous precedent, signaling to other corporations that with enough lobbying power, they too can secure a get-out-of-tariff-free card. The New York Times report highlights this burgeoning trend, with industries ranging from agriculture to retail clamoring for their own exemptions. This scenario reeks of crony capitalism, where government favors are doled out to the well-connected few, leaving smaller businesses and the broader economy to bear the brunt of these erratic trade policies.

A Policy Built on Quicksand

Trump's tariff strategy, largely influenced by trade adviser Peter Navarro, is fundamentally flawed. Pegging reciprocal import duties not to actual trade barriers or foreign tariffs but to the size of U.S. trade deficits with each country is a misguided approach. It oversimplifies complex trade dynamics and ignores the multifaceted reasons behind trade imbalances, such as the nature of global supply chains and domestic consumption patterns.

Moreover, the administration's hasty announcement of a three-month grace period following market backlash is indicative of policy made on the fly, without due consideration of its economic impacts. Such unpredictability not only destabilizes markets but also erodes the United States' credibility on the global stage.

Retail Giants and the Tariff Burden

The plea from retail behemoths like Home Depot, Target, and Walmart for tariff relief is a testament to the policy's far-reaching consequences. These companies argue that without exemptions, they cannot maintain current prices, underscoring the broader inflationary impact of tariffs on American consumers. The administration's tariff regime, thus, acts as a tax on the very people it purports to protect—the American public.

Conclusion: A Misguided Gamble

In essence, Trump's tariff policy is a misguided gamble, predicated on the false notion that trade wars are easy to win and that tariffs are a one-size-fits-all solution to complex trade imbalances. The exemptions saga, marked by a lack of coherence and fairness, only serves to highlight the policy's inherent flaws. As the administration continues to navigate these tumultuous waters, one thing is clear: the real losers in this trade war will be American businesses and consumers, left to navigate an increasingly uncertain economic landscape.
