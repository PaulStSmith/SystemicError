Revealed: Trump’s Board of Peace plots 'sweeping immunity' for members

Trump’s Peace Board Is a Sovereignty Laundering Scheme

Power, Not Peace

The reporting describes a “Board of Peace” built around Gaza that has drifted from reconstruction language into something much uglier: a body seeking sweeping legal immunity for itself, its affiliates, contractors, and military forces. Trump would stay chairman even after leaving the White House, while the board’s reach could extend to property in Gaza “free of charge” and to acts shielded from arrest, detention, and court proceedings.

Who Actually Holds Power

This is not a neutral peace mechanism. The power sits with Trump, who keeps the chairmanship, and with the states and personnel invited into his orbit. The fact that several participants are authoritarian regimes or dictatorships, while prominent democratic U.S. allies refused to join, tells you more than any branding exercise. The people closest to this structure are the ones least interested in public accountability.

Immunity Is the Point

The immunity proposal is not a side detail. It is the architecture. The draft resolution reportedly extends protections to the board, its office of the high representative, Palestinian technocrats, international military forces, and contractors. That is not oversight. It is preemptive insulation from consequences. Six lawyers told The Guardian that if shootings, accidents, or land disputes arise, it is unclear who can be held accountable. That uncertainty is the design flaw and the political intention at once.

The Frame Is Doing Cover Work

The board’s representative insists concerns about lawlessness are “wrong” and “misleading.” That is the standard evasion: deny the obvious while preserving the structure that makes abuse possible. The issue is not whether the board likes the word “impunity.” The issue is that the resolution appears to create it. Calling that a peace framework is political laundering.

Gaza as an Exception Zone

The proposal treats Gaza less like a place with law and more like a managed zone where outside actors can operate above it. Property “free of charge,” broad immunity, and unclear liability are not reconstruction virtues. They are the language of occupation-adjacent administration without responsibility. If a plan requires legal shielding before it can even begin, it is advertising what it expects to do.

The Larger Pattern

This is the familiar pattern of contemporary strongman governance dressed as international order: centralize authority, recruit compliant or authoritarian partners, bypass institutions that constrain you, and call the result peace. The public-facing rhetoric promises rebuilding. The private machinery prepares impunity. That gap is not a bug in the project. It is the project.
