Trump's now thinking about his own death — and that's petrifying

Based on an article by Ray Richmond

Trump’s Saudi Nuclear Deal Is Not Diplomacy. It’s Monetized Risk.

The Transaction

According to the source, Trump approved a 30-year deal giving Saudi Arabia access to a U.S.-run civilian nuclear program without International Atomic Energy Agency monitoring or inspections. That is the core fact, and it is enough to make the rest of the pageant look like what it is: a bribery ecosystem dressed up as statecraft.

This is not a hard bargain made in the national interest. It is a transfer of strategic leverage to a monarchy whose leadership has already said it wants the bomb if Iran gets one, with U.S. firms positioned to profit either way.

Who Actually Holds Power

The real decision-makers here are Trump, Mohammed bin Salman, and the money networks around them. Trump controls the signature; MBS controls the cash; Jared Kushner’s private equity ties show how the family channel runs alongside the formal channel. The source says Saudi Arabia’s Public Investment Fund put $2 billion into Kushner’s firm and that Senate investigators later estimated roughly $87 million in fees had accrued to Affinity Partners.

That is the power structure. Not abstract “relations.” Not “mutual understanding.” A president who treats a foreign sovereign wealth fund like a personal ATM is not conducting foreign policy. He is cashing in on it.

The Misdirection

The article uses 9/11 and Khashoggi as reminders, but the real point is more brutal: the United States keeps pretending the problem is uncertainty when the problem is permission. The source notes that 15 of the 19 9/11 hijackers were Saudi citizens, that the 9/11 Commission found no evidence the Saudi government knowingly funded the attacks, and that Jamal Khashoggi was murdered and dismembered after entering the Saudi consulate in Istanbul.

Those are not random historical stains. They are evidence of what the U.S. elite is willing to overlook once enough money changes hands. The issue is not that Trump “looks past” brutality by accident. It is that brutality has no price high enough to exceed the payoff.

Nonproliferation as Theater

The source is right to say this pact effectively pushes nuclear nonproliferation “out the window” in the Middle East. What matters is who gets to decide that risk is acceptable. It is not voters. It is not inspectors. It is not the public. It is a president who treats safeguards as obstacles and a crown prince who openly wants deterrent parity without the discipline that comes with oversight.

That is the pattern: rules for the weak, exemptions for the rich, and catastrophic consequences for everyone else. When the only “assurance” against enrichment is Saudi Arabia’s word, the system has already admitted it no longer means what it says.

The Family Business State

The source keeps circling back to the same fact because it is the only one that matters: this is about cash. U.S. nuclear firms get contracts. Saudi capital gets access. Trump gets the appearance of a foreign-policy triumph. Kushner gets fees and future investor conversations. MBS gets another step toward nuclear leverage and another proof that the U.S. presidency can be bought by the right dynasty.

This is not corruption at the margins. It is corruption as operating system. Foreign policy becomes a family business, national security becomes a branding exercise, and the world is left to absorb the consequences of decisions made by people insulated from them.

The Pattern Beneath It

The larger political story is simple: authoritarian power and oligarchic greed reinforce each other because each makes the other harder to punish. Trump rewards MBS’s cruelty because cruelty does not disqualify someone from partnership if the money is right. The Saudis reward Trump’s corruption because corruption buys access, cover, and silence.

That is the real scandal in the source material. Not just that a reckless nuclear deal is being pushed. It is that the machinery of U.S. power is being used to normalize it, and the people steering that machinery expect to profit from the fallout.
