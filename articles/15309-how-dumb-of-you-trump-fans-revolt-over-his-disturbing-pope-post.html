'How dumb of you': Trump fans revolt over his 'disturbing' pope post

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

A decision by either Donald Trump, or whomever adds content to his Truth Social account to share a picture of him as the pope, set off a firestorm with some of his religious MAGA supporters who questioned whether its was appropriate while Catholics are still grieving.

On Friday afternoon, Trump's Truth Social account was updated with a picture (which can be seen here) of the president seated and dressed as the pope with no comment.

That, in turn, led to some furious supporters of the president letting him know they were not amused, with one calling it "blasphemous," and another adding, "that picture was disgustingly disturbing!!"

Rita Sellers, who ranted at President Joe Biden as a "jackal" who makes "her skin crawl," wrote, "I'm not a Catholic, but I'm a Christian and Jesus Christ is my Lord and Savior! However, I do not think it was a good idea to have President Trump look like the pope. He is no pope, he is the President of the USA period! This was disrespectful to the Catholic faith! This is nothing to laugh at! You all, whoever did this, really messed up on this one!"

ALOLO73 lamented, "Why did I ever believe you actually loved America over your own hubris? You only love yourself and I’ve NEVER regretted voting for you more than I do today. NEVER AGAIN"

Rebecca Woodruff Williamson, who is a big supporter of Donald Trump's tariff war, added, "I’m an evangelical Christian, never been catholic but I really wish you hadn’t shared this (your interview suggestion at being Pope was a bit cringy in itself) but this is borderline sacrilegious no matter what your beliefs. Why must we fuel the fake news fires?"

"Trump, I voted for you and I admire what you’re doing for us, but this is a mistake! Stop doing those mistakes, messing with the almighty may cause our demise! It’s Christ who instated the papacy upon the first Pope Saint Peter, and here you are messing around with what’s sacred! How dumb of you!" Chadi wrote.

Sherry Methling, whose Truth Social account is full of biblical references, wrote, "Who is advising you to post this photo (you as pope) and past video (Trump Gaza)?!? Their (sic) fired!!"

KrisInCleveland who has railed at "old school Republicans," warned, "This is a bridge too far for me."

"I love Trump but this post isnt funny. Pretty scary actually," wrote FrankieNDallasTX.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Papal Parody: A Misguided Misstep or Calculated Chaos?

In a move that can only be described as bewildering at best and deeply offensive at worst, Donald Trump, or perhaps a rogue social media manager with a peculiar sense of humor, decided it would be a sterling idea to share an image of Trump dressed as the pope on his Truth Social account. This decision, unsurprisingly, ignited a firestorm among his devout MAGA followers, particularly those of the Catholic faith—or really, anyone with a modicum of respect for religious institutions.

The Blasphemous Display

The image, which lacks any comment or context, depicts Trump seated in papal regalia, an audacious comparison that has left some of his staunchest supporters aghast. The backlash was swift, with comments ranging from calling the image "blasphemous" to "disgustingly disturbing." Such reactions underscore the gravity of Trump's misjudgment, or at the very least, a startling lack of foresight by his content team.

Supporter Outrage: A Spectrum of Disbelief

Rita Sellers, despite not being Catholic, expressed her disdain, highlighting the disrespect towards the Catholic faith. It's a sentiment that resonates with many, regardless of their religious affiliations. Rita's words, "This is nothing to laugh at! You all, whoever did this, really messed up on this one!" encapsulate the shock and disappointment felt by many.

ALOLO73's comment injects a more personal note of betrayal, stating, "You only love yourself and I’ve NEVER regretted voting for you more than I do today. NEVER AGAIN." This reflects a broader sense of disillusionment that goes beyond religious disrespect to question Trump's loyalty to his country over his ego.

Rebecca Woodruff Williamson and Chadi both express concerns of sacrilege, with Williamson calling the move "borderline sacrilegious" and Chadi condemning Trump's audacity to mess with what's sacred. Such comments highlight a shared belief in the sanctity of religious figures and institutions, a line Trump has unapologetically crossed.

KrisInCleveland's remark, "This is a bridge too far for me," succinctly captures the sentiment of many who have tolerated or supported Trump's myriad controversies but find this particular act indefensible.

Analysis: A Misstep or a Tactic?

One must ponder whether this was a mere misstep by Trump or a calculated move to stir the pot. Given Trump's history of provocative actions, it's tempting to view this as another attempt to dominate the news cycle. However, the backlash from his own base suggests a miscalculation, perhaps underestimating the reverence his supporters hold for religious figures.

The reaction of Sherry Methling, who demanded, "Who is advising you to post this photo... Their (sic) fired!!" points to the internal conflict this image has sparked among Trump's followers, who are forced to reconcile their loyalty to Trump with their religious convictions.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

This incident is emblematic of the dangerous game Trump plays with the boundaries of respect and reverence. While his supporters have often lauded his disregard for political correctness, this move has clearly crossed a line for many, highlighting a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard for the sacredness of the papacy and, by extension, the religious beliefs of his followers.

In attempting to be irreverent or humorous, Trump has alienated a segment of his base, revealing the risks of his often-unfiltered approach to communication. Whether this will have lasting effects on his support base remains to be seen, but it undeniably serves as a reminder of the volatile nature of Trump's brand of politics—a brand that, in its quest to shock and awe, sometimes bites the hand that feeds it.
