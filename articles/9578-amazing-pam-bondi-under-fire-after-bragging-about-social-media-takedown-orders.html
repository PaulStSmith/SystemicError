'Amazing': Pam Bondi under fire after 'bragging' about social media takedown orders

Bondi claimed that a Facebook group posts public photos of federal agents, including U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents, that identify them.

“Today following outreach from @thejusticedept, Facebook removed a large group page that was being used to dox and target @ICEgov agents in Chicago,” Bondi said, noting that ICE agents are being targeted.

It prompted a lot of questions from those who recalled Republicans being furious when former President Joe Biden’s administration requested that false information about COVID be removed.

“Amazing. When the Biden adm reached out to Facebook to take down pages filled with Covid disinformation, it caused Zuckerberg to decry a full assault on free speech in America. Now, the Trump adm reaches out to remove ICE information and Facebook complies,” wrote The Bulwark’s Sam Stein on X.

First Amendment and defamation lawyer Ari Cohen commented, “Discussion of where ICE has been spotted operating, and even the identities of agents, is protected by the First Amendment. If there’s a true threat, you prosecute that. But you cannot silence lawful speech, or pressure platforms to, because you’re worried it might be ‘misused.’”

Zeteo News founder and CEO Mehdi Hasan pointed to Matt Taibbi and Bari Weiss, recalling hysteria over the so-called “Twitter Files.”

“I’m sure @mtaibbi and @bariweiss and the rest of the ‘Twitter Files’ crew will be all over this, right? Right??” he asked. Bondi, he said, is “bragging about their social media censorship efforts.”

American Immigration Council senior fellow Aaron Reichlin-Melnick agreed, recalling, “I remember when it was a massive scandal on the right when government officials asked some platforms to take down posts they felt were spreading dangerous COVID misinformation that might endanger the lives of Americans.”

Reporter Billy Binion wrote on X that Republicans even went to the Supreme Court, alleging social media companies were being pressured by the government.

Nu Wexler of Four Corners Public Affairs also remembered all of the congressional hearings about the issue.

National security expert Marcy Wheeler pointed to cases being ignored that include the far right doxing people on social media by not only posting photos but also their addresses and other information.

“Does this mean you’ll arrest Laura Loomer for doxing and targeting FBI agents and AUSAs? Or, on top of violating the First Amendment, are you doing it selectively, protecting just some LE?” Wheeler asked.

She also asked, “Bondi [is] allowing TPUSA to dangerously dox college professors but censoring the speech of those she favors. Also, she’s lying about there even BEING a wave of violence.”

Chicago Tribune senior journalist Laura N. Rodríguez Presa explained that the page isn’t about identifying and doxing ICE agents.

“The page removed practically served as a way for people find out when their loved ones had been taken. Most people shared videos and pictures after ICE agents had already left the area,” she wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Censorship: Unpacking Attorney General Pam Bondi’s Controversial Move

In a move that has sparked considerable controversy, Attorney General Pam Bondi has come under fire for her request to Meta, the parent company of Facebook, to take down content related to federal agents, particularly those from U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). Bondi’s action, ostensibly aimed at preventing the doxxing of ICE agents, has unleashed a storm of criticism and raised serious questions about free speech, government censorship, and the inconsistent application of these principles across political lines.

The Core of the Controversy

Bondi’s request to Meta led to the removal of a Facebook group that she claimed was being used to dox and target ICE agents in Chicago. This action has not only drawn criticism for its implications on free speech but also for the glaring hypocrisy it represents when juxtaposed with the Republican outcry against similar actions by the Biden administration. The administration’s attempts to remove COVID misinformation were met with vehement accusations of an assault on free speech. Yet, when the tables are turned, the same level of outrage from these quarters is conspicuously absent.

Selective Outrage and the First Amendment

The reaction—or lack thereof—from figures who previously decried censorship highlights a troubling inconsistency. As pointed out by Ari Cohen, a First Amendment and defamation lawyer, discussions about the operations of ICE and the identities of its agents fall under the protection of the First Amendment. The government’s role should not extend to silencing lawful speech or pressuring platforms to do so based on potential misuse. This selective outrage underscores a deeper issue: the willingness to sidestep foundational principles of free speech when it serves a particular political agenda.

The Misrepresentation of Intent

Further complicating this issue is the mischaracterization of the Facebook group’s purpose. Contrary to Bondi’s claims of doxxing, Laura N. Rodríguez Presa of the Chicago Tribune clarifies that the group served as a resource for individuals to find out when their loved ones had been detained by ICE, often sharing information after agents had left the area. This revelation not only challenges the justification for the group’s removal but also raises concerns about the suppression of community support networks under the guise of protecting law enforcement.

Double Standards and the Path Forward

The incident brings to light the selective enforcement of what constitutes harmful or dangerous speech, depending on who is being protected or targeted. As Marcy Wheeler, a national security expert, points out, similar actions of doxxing by far-right individuals, which include posting addresses and other sensitive information, have not received the same level of attention or action. This selective application of censorship principles erodes trust in the impartiality of government actions and highlights the need for a consistent and principled approach to handling speech on social media.

In conclusion, the actions of Attorney General Pam Bondi, and the subsequent reactions, lay bare the complexities and contradictions inherent in the current discourse on free speech and government censorship. It is imperative that we strive for consistency in our principles, regardless of the political winds, to preserve the integrity of our public discourse. The path forward must be grounded in a commitment to uphold the First Amendment, ensuring that lawful speech is protected, even when it is politically inconvenient.
