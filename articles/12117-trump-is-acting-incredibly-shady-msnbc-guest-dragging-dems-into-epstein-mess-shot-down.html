'Trump is acting incredibly shady': MSNBC guest dragging Dems into Epstein mess shot down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by Lindsey Williams Drath

Sam Stein, Lindsey Williams Drath (MSNBC screenshot)

An attempt by an MSNBC guest to pin blame for the growing public distrust with both parties on how Jeffrey Epstein partner Ghislaine Maxwell is being treated was swiftly shot down on Saturday morning.

During an appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend," Lindsey Williams Drath, CEO of Andrew Yang's Forward Party, was asked about the battle to get convicted human trafficker Maxwell to testify before members of Congress and she used the opportunity to bash both parties.

Drath was prompted by co-host Jonathan Capehart about the movement of Maxwell to a "Club Fed," as he asked, "How are the American people supposed to believe anything that's coming out of the administration, let alone the president's mouth?"

'Well, I think, Jonathan, this isn't just this administration," Drath, a former RNC official, replied. "This is government in general right now. Earlier in the year, you know, we saw polls saying that 70 percent of Americans believed that government was fundamentally corrupt and what happens here is that, when you see selective accountability, that's when trust erodes in the American people."

Going on to detail plans by both parties to depose Maxwell she made the point, "When you see when Congress does exactly what it's supposed to do on behalf of the American people, and there's ambiguity about the sequencing, that's when trust is eroding. That's why you're seeing 10,000 people every day leaving the Republican and Democratic parties. This isn't a partisan issue; this is a fundamental issue. This is a fundamental issue of trust in our institutions of government. and that's why at the Forward Party, we're calling for full transparency and accountability here."

Occasional MSNBC co-host Sam Stein, who grimaced while she talked, wasn't having it and pointed out the elephant in the room as far as voters are concerned.

"Yeah. Well, I mean, I think trump's acting incredibly shady around this stuff, to be honest with you," he shot back. "And I think that's part of the problem. So while I do take your point about trust in parties, the fact is this man ran on, you know, producing documents exposing these lies and now he's acting in a way that suggests that he has something to hide."

"I don't know how he gets out from under this without full transparency," he added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by Lindsey Williams Drath

In what can only be described as a tour de force of political gymnastics, Lindsey Williams Drath, CEO of Andrew Yang's Forward Party and former RNC official, attempted to pivot a conversation about Ghislaine Maxwell's treatment into a sweeping indictment of the entire U.S. government's trustworthiness. This maneuver, executed with the finesse of a seasoned politician, occurred during an appearance on MSNBC's "The Weekend." However, the real story here is not Drath's deft deflection, but the underlying issues it attempts to obscure and the boldness of such an attempt to rewrite the narrative.

Selective Accountability or Selective Outrage?

Drath's argument hinges on the premise of "selective accountability," suggesting that the handling of Maxwell, a convicted human trafficker, is emblematic of a broader governmental failure. Yet, this argument, while not without merit, conveniently glosses over the specifics of the Maxwell case in favor of a generalized critique of political institutions. It's an impressive leap from discussing the treatment of a specific high-profile criminal to decrying the erosion of trust in government at large. The subtlety of this shift cannot be understated—it's a classic bait-and-switch, designed to redirect focus away from uncomfortable specifics to more nebulous, and therefore less actionable, concerns.

The Role of the Forward Party: Revolution or Rebranding?

Drath's pitch for the Forward Party as a beacon of "full transparency and accountability" further complicates the narrative. On the surface, this call to action sounds laudable, even necessary. However, it raises questions about the practicality of such promises and the feasibility of their implementation. Is the Forward Party genuinely offering a revolutionary approach to governance, or is this simply another case of political rebranding, leveraging public dissatisfaction for political gain? The cynical might argue that it's the latter, noting that promises of transparency and accountability are a staple of political discourse, rarely fulfilled in practice.

The Elephant, The Shadiness, and The Room

Sam Stein's interjection about Trump's "shady" behavior adds another layer of complexity to the discussion. Stein points out the glaring omission in Drath's argument: the specific actions and inactions of individual political figures, including Trump, significantly contribute to the erosion of trust she laments. By focusing on institutional failure without addressing the roles individuals play within those institutions, Drath's argument feels incomplete at best and disingenuous at worst.

Concluding Thoughts: Navigating the Morass

The conversation between Drath and Stein exemplifies the difficulty of discussing corruption and accountability in today's political landscape. On one hand, Drath's broad critique of governmental trustworthiness reflects a genuine and widespread concern among the American populace. On the other hand, Stein's pushback underscores the importance of not allowing such critiques to become so generalized that they lose sight of specific, addressable issues.

In the final analysis, this exchange serves as a microcosm of the larger challenges facing political discourse in the United States. The quest for accountability and transparency is undoubtedly noble, but it must be pursued with a clear-eyed recognition of the specificities of each situation. Otherwise, we risk being caught in an endless cycle of deflection and disillusionment, where genuine issues are obscured by the very rhetoric that claims to expose them.
