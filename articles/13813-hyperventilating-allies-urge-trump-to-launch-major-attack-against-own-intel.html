'Hyperventilating' allies urge Trump to launch major attack — against own intel

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 11, 20255 min read

Some influential conservatives — possibly including media mogul Rupert Murdoch — have been pushing President Donald Trump behind the scenes to give the go-ahead for Israel to attack Iran.

Trump doesn't seem inclined to go along with their scheme, but the campaign has prompted the president's allies to push back in private and online to keep the administration's work on track to reach a nuclear deal with Tehran, reported Politico.

"During a private lunch with the president at the White House last Wednesday, conservative talk show host Mark Levin told Trump that Iran was days away from building a nuclear weapon, an argument Trump’s own intelligence team has told the president is not accurate, according to an intelligence official as well as another Trump ally familiar with the matter," the website reported.

"Levin urged Trump to allow the Israeli government to strike Iranian nuclear sites, which Trump has told Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu would torpedo the diplomacy."

Levin has been attacking Steve Witkoff, the president's longtime friend and special envoy leading those talks, from his talk radio perch, and Murdoch's papers, particularly the New York Post, have been suggesting Witkoff works for Qatar, and a source told Politico the conservative media titan has been privately complaining about the envoy's diplomatic work.

“They’re trying to push the president to make a decision that’s not what he wants,” a senior administration official told Politico. “There’s clearly a lobby for war with Iran vs. those who are more aligned with the president, that know he is the one that has been able to bring them to the negotiating table.”

Someone was alarmed enough by the lobbying by Levin, who also hosts a Fox News program that Trump regularly promotes, to alert former Fox News host Tucker Carlson, who then accused Levin of trying to push the U.S. into war.

“There is zero credible intelligence that suggests Iran is anywhere near building a bomb, or has plans to. None,” Carlson tweeted. “So why is Mark Levin once again hyperventilating about weapons of mass destruction? To distract you from the real goal, which is regime change — young Americans heading back to the Middle East to topple yet another government.”

Some allies believe Trump, who has campaigned on pulling the U.S. out of foreign entanglements, will resist the pressure, and some of them believe the efforts will even annoy him.

“Levin and Murdoch are all over Trump all the time — I actually think they hurt their case because I know Trump,” said one longtime Trump confidant. “Once he’s kind of made his mind up, you can come at it later from a different angle, but you keep pressing, he digs in.”

Trump sounded skeptical Monday about the possibility of reaching a deal with Iran on nuclear enrichment, saying Tehran was asking for more than he's willing to give, but White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt told Politico said he was "ultimately ... final decision maker."

“With respect to Iran, President Trump has made his own opinion clear: he would like to pursue diplomacy and make a deal, but IF Iran makes a deal impossible, President Trump has other options on the table,” Leavitt said.

Our Analysis:

The Shadow Puppeteers of War and Diplomacy

In a brazen display of backroom power plays, the latest Politico article reveals how the strings of potential conflict are being tugged not from the secure confines of policy meetings but rather from the plush seats of media moguls and conservative talk show hosts. The narrative here isn't just about the potential for an Israeli attack on Iran but the unsettling reality of influential conservatives, potentially including Rupert Murdoch, engaging in a shadow campaign to sway President Donald Trump towards a decision that could recalibrate the geopolitics of the Middle East.

The Unholy Alliance of Media and War Advocacy

At the heart of this narrative is the unsettling confluence of media power and advocacy for military intervention. The article underscores how Mark Levin, a conservative talk show host with direct access to Trump, has been leveraging his platform to peddle inaccuracies about Iran's nuclear capabilities. Levin's crusade, backed by the dubious support of Murdoch's media empire, exemplifies how media personalities have crossed over from reporting and commentary into active lobbying for war. The suggestion that Murdoch's publications have been casting aspersions on Steve Witkoff, a key figure in the diplomatic talks, adds another layer of intrigue, pointing to a coordinated effort to derail diplomacy in favor of conflict.

The Misinformation Machine in Full Swing

What's particularly alarming is the boldness with which misinformation is being weaponized. According to Politico, Levin's assertions about Iran's nuclear program directly contradict the assessments of Trump's own intelligence team. This discrepancy highlights a dangerous game of selective hearing and deliberate misinformation, where the opinions of talk show hosts are valued over the assessments of intelligence professionals. Tucker Carlson's intervention, calling out Levin's rhetoric, underscores the internal conservative conflict between warmongering and a more cautious approach to foreign policy.

Trump's Skepticism and the Diplomacy Dilemma

Despite the pressure, Trump appears to remain skeptical about the feasibility of a deal with Iran, hinting at the complexity of negotiations and the high stakes involved. The article suggests that Trump's resistance to the push for military action may stem from a broader reluctance to entangle the U.S. further in Middle Eastern conflicts, a stance that has been a cornerstone of his political identity. Yet, the whispers of war and the lobbying efforts of individuals like Levin and Murdoch present a significant test of Trump's resolve and his administration's diplomatic strategy.

Conclusion: The War Lobby vs. Diplomatic Prudence

The Politico article paints a vivid picture of a tug-of-war between the advocates of military intervention and the proponents of diplomatic engagement. The behind-the-scenes lobbying by influential conservatives highlights a troubling trend where personal agendas and media power can potentially dictate matters of war and peace. As the Trump administration navigates these treacherous waters, the ultimate question remains: Will diplomacy prevail, or will the voices clamoring for conflict shift the course of U.S. foreign policy? In this high-stakes game, the implications reach far beyond the halls of power in Washington, echoing across the volatile landscapes of the Middle East and the world at large.
