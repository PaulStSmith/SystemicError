'Just nonsense': Economist dismantles core Trump claim

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 30, 20255 min read

Economist Justin Wolfers appears on MS Now, Nov. 29, 2025. (Screengrab / MS NOW)

President Donald Trump repeatedly claims that he inherited a troubled, dysfunctional economy from his Democratic predecessor, Joe Biden, and quickly turned it around. In fact, the United States enjoyed record-low unemployment during Biden’s presidency, but widespread frustration over inflation helped Trump pull off a narrow victory of roughly 1.5 percent on Election Night 2024.

Economist Justin Wolfers, who teaches at the University of Michigan but is originally from Sydney, Australia, tore apart Trump’s claims about the economy — from tariffs to the stock market — during a Saturday, November 29 appearance on MS NOW’s “The Weekend.”

Trump claimed that his tariffs will bring in so much revenue that the U.S. will be able to “almost completely” eliminate federal income tax.

But Wolfers told “The Weekend” hosts Eugene Daniels, Jackie Alemany and Jonathan Capehart, “This is just nonsense. Let me start with one important fact-check. The president appears not to understand the difference between millions, billions, and trillions. That’s actually one of the most important points in all of economics; they’re massively different. We are not taking in trillions of dollars in tariff revenue.”

Wolfers added, “If we were, we could afford his $2000 tariff checks. We aren’t. So therefore, we can’t.”

The University of Michigan economist also pushed back against Trump’s claims about the stock market, which, he stressed, is performing better in other countries than it is in the United States.

Wolfers told Daniels, Alemany and Capehart, “The other thing that he talks about a lot is the stock market. Now, here’s a funny thing: If you look at the stock market returns from, say, 25 of the biggest countries around the world, the United States ranks around about 22nd right now. So yes, American stocks are up. But guess what? They’re up even more everywhere else.”

U.S. consumers, according to Wolfers, are pessimistic — not optimistic — about the state of the economy.

Wolfers told Daniels, Alemany and Capehart, “So, consumer confidence right now — it’s quite striking just how bad it is. So, consumer confidence right now is very close to being an all-time low. And this has been measured back to the 1970s. So consumers say that they’re feeling worse than they were during the Great Recession, than they were during the pandemic, than they were during the early ‘80s recession. It’s really quite striking numbers. If you dig into that a little bit, you ask them things like: What do you think about the quality of us economic policy?”

The economist continued, “The number of people who think that the quality of economic policy is poor is at an all-time high. It’s roughly three-fifths of the American people. What you have, I think, is that the president has fundamentally lost the battle of ideas. What you normally do with an economic program is you come up with some ideas, and you try and convince people of the virtues of them. And he has fundamentally failed. There’s a deep question as to how long people are going to keep spending.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Economic Illusions Cast by President Trump

In a recent spectacle of economic misunderstanding, President Donald Trump has been fervently spinning tales of a dismal economy left by Joe Biden, only to be miraculously salvaged by his administration. However, the fabric of reality, woven with actual facts and figures, tells a starkly different story. Economist Justin Wolfers, in his appearance on MS NOW’s “The Weekend,” systematically dismantles Trump’s economic fantasies with the precision of a seasoned academic. Let’s dissect the fallacies and present the unvarnished truth.

The Tariff Delusion

Trump’s grandiose claim that tariffs will fill the U.S. coffers to the brim, enabling the elimination of federal income tax, is a classic example of his economic illiteracy. Wolfers astutely points out the monumental gap in Trump’s understanding of basic economic scales - millions, billions, and trillions. The reality is as straightforward as it is brutal: the U.S. is not swimming in a sea of tariff revenue. The idea of funding $2000 tariff checks is, therefore, not just impractical but patently absurd.

The Stock Market Mirage

Moving on to the stock market, Trump’s narrative of American dominance crumbles under global scrutiny. As Wolfers highlights, when compared to stock market returns from the top 25 countries, the U.S. languishes at an embarrassing 22nd place. This not only refutes Trump’s claims of unparalleled American financial prowess but also underscores a broader global economic context that the President either ignores or fails to comprehend.

The Consumer Confidence Crisis

Perhaps the most telling indicator of Trump’s economic mismanagement is the current state of consumer confidence. Contrary to the optimistic picture painted by the administration, Wolfers reveals a starkly different reality. Consumer confidence is not just low; it’s nearing historic lows, with Americans feeling more pessimistic about the economy than during the Great Recession, the pandemic, or the early ‘80s recession. Moreover, the overwhelming majority of Americans view the quality of economic policy as poor, signaling a profound disillusionment with Trump’s economic strategies.

The Battle of Ideas: Lost

Wolfers’ analysis culminates in a damning conclusion: Trump has lost the battle of ideas. Economic policy, at its core, is about crafting innovative solutions and persuading the public of their efficacy. Trump’s failure to convince the American people of his economic vision’s merits speaks volumes about his administration’s disconnect from the economic realities faced by ordinary citizens.

Conclusion

The economic narrative spun by President Trump is not just flawed; it’s a masterclass in misinformation. As Wolfers’ incisive critique demonstrates, the reality of Trump’s economic policies is one of missed marks, misguided priorities, and misunderstood principles. It’s high time we look beyond the bluster to the cold, hard numbers, which tell a story of an administration out of touch with the economic pulse of the nation. As we move forward, let us ground our economic discussions in facts, not fantasies, and demand the same from our leaders.
