'Stanning for Trump': CBS deletes post critical of No Kings protesters after call out

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Demonstrators gather during a “No Kings” protest against U.S. President Donald Trump’s policies, in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 18, 2025. (REUTERS/Kylie Cooper)

A local CBS News affiliate in Tennessee deleted its social media post this weekend after being called out by the progressive news outlet The Tennessee Holler for its “totally not biased” coverage of the “No Kings” protests, which the progressive outlet argued was “stanning for Trump.”

Attended by millions, the No Kings protests saw more than 2,700 separate rallies held across the nation Saturday, motivated largely by opposition to President Donald Trump and his second administration that continues to test the limits of the executive branch’s power.

In its coverage of the protests in Chattanooga, Tennessee, however, WDEF News 12 – a CBS-affiliated network – wrote the following: “Even after President Trump secured the cease-fire deal in the Middle East, demonstrators continued to protest his actions and policies.”

The Tennessee Holler flagged the post immediately, alleging WDEF News 12 to have capitulated to the new editor-in-chief for CBS News Bari Weiss, who’s cozied up to billionaires in her assent to CBS’ top leadership role and once said she considered it her “job as an outspoken Jewish Zionist” to be “defending Israel,” according to the Jewish Journal.

“Wow – local CBS affiliate WDEF News 12 stanning for Trump in their ‘totally not biased’ tweet about the protests today,” The Tennessee Holler wrote in a social media post on X Saturday. “Is this that CBS News-Bari Weiss-approved framing?”

The local CBS outlet would ultimately delete the post, and replace it with another post using more neutral language, and alongside an editor’s note communicating that the new post replaced “an earlier post that was deleted.”

Justin Kanew – film producer, political activist and founder of The Tennessee Holler – told Raw Story that the local CBS News outlet’s language in covering the protests was “just another reminder of how we ended up descending into authoritarianism,” and that it was vital to call out new organizations when they’ve “so clearly dropped the ball.”

“It’s disturbing how many reporters and editors are unwilling to directly challenge those in power, and how easily they cave to propaganda narratives and GOP spin,” Kanew told Raw Story Sunday in a text message. “It seems the pro-Trump pro-Israel Bari Weiss effect is already taking hold throughout CBS.”

Our Analysis:

The “Unbiased” Bias: A Deep Dive into WDEF News 12’s Coverage Gaffe

In what can only be described as a masterclass in media bias, a local CBS News affiliate in Tennessee, WDEF News 12, has showcased the increasingly blurred lines between unbiased reporting and political partisanship. The incident in question? A social media post regarding the “No Kings” protests, a large-scale demonstration against President Donald Trump’s administration, which managed to simultaneously downplay the significance of the protests and highlight a cease-fire deal as if to counterbalance the protestors’ grievances.

“Stanning for Trump” or Journalism?

Let’s break down the post that sparked the controversy. WDEF News 12’s initial coverage declared, “Even after President Trump secured the cease-fire deal in the Middle East, demonstrators continued to protest his actions and policies.” This statement, dripping with an almost satirical level of bias, insinuates that the protestors should perhaps reconsider their stance in light of Trump’s foreign policy achievement. It’s the journalistic equivalent of saying, “But look over here, not there!”

The Tennessee Holler, a progressive news outlet, was quick to flag this biased representation, accusing WDEF News 12 of essentially cheering on Trump, an action seemingly influenced by the recent ascent of Bari Weiss to CBS News’ top leadership. Weiss, with her history of controversial statements and clear political leanings, seems to be the ghost in the machine, her editorial influence looming large over what was presented as unbiased news coverage.

The Swift Backtrack

Following the backlash, WDEF News 12 deleted the controversial post, opting for a more neutral approach in their subsequent coverage. This knee-jerk reaction to criticism highlights a disturbing trend within modern journalism: the propensity to bend under pressure and the lack of steadfast commitment to impartial reporting. It’s a band-aid solution to a systemic problem, a superficial attempt at damage control rather than a sincere effort to address underlying biases.

The Bari Weiss Effect

The article mentions the “pro-Trump pro-Israel Bari Weiss effect” as taking hold throughout CBS, a statement that should alarm anyone concerned with journalistic integrity. Weiss’s own admission of her mission to defend Israel as part of her journalistic duty further muddies the waters of impartiality. When personal agendas begin to seep into news coverage, we venture into dangerous territory, where facts are overshadowed by ideology.

Authoritarianism’s Subtle Creep

Justin Kanew’s comments to Raw Story underscore the broader implications of such biased reporting. The descent into authoritarianism doesn’t happen overnight; it’s a gradual erosion of democratic norms, facilitated by a media landscape that prefers complacency over confrontation. When news outlets fail to hold those in power accountable, opting instead to parrot their narratives, they become complicit in the erosion of democratic values.

Conclusion

The incident with WDEF News 12 is not just a one-off gaffe but a symptom of a larger disease plaguing the media industry. As consumers of news, it’s imperative to remain vigilant, question narratives, and demand journalism that doesn’t just echo power but challenges it. In an era where the truth is more valuable than ever, we must not allow biased reporting to muddy the waters of our collective understanding.
