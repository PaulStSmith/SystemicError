Jesse Kelly, who in 2021 openly speculated that Republicans may soon get tired of "following the rules" and "pick a fascist" to lead the party, has also been criticized for his prior social media comments.

On Saturday, he turned his attention toward the nation's top court after it temporarily halted the Trump administration's deportation of alleged Venezuelan gang members under an 18th-century law.

"Ignore the Supreme Court. Arrest anyone who tries to enforce this. Dissolve the Supreme Court entirely if they push. You can deport foreigners or you don’t have a country anymore. There are no good choices now," Kelly said. "Donald Trump is the last thing before we get a dictator. I wonder if he knows this. Not a left wing dictator either. The Right will demand a dictator if Trump is not allowed to implement the will of the voters. And they will get one. A terrible place to be as a country."

This prompted some conservatives to criticize Kelly's position.

Veteran and Texas conservative Mack Latimer, for instance, said, "This is not conservative."

"This is authoritarian," he added on Saturday.

Libertarian Chase Oliver said, "Go, and I can't stress this enough, f--- yourself."

Political analyst Rachel Bitecofer also weighed in, saying, "But don’t call them fascists."

Civil rights litigator Patrick Jaicomo chimed in with, "What a stupid post."

"Get a grip," Jaicomo then added.

Our Analysis:

Anatomy of Anti-Democratic Rhetoric: Jesse Kelly's Authoritarian Wish

In a recent tirade that has sent shockwaves through the political spectrum, conservative talk show host Jesse Kelly has openly advocated for a move that strikes at the very heart of American democracy. By suggesting that Donald Trump and his administration should "ignore the Supreme Court" and even "dissolve" it, Kelly has not just crossed a line; he's obliterated it. This is not a mere flirtation with authoritarianism; it's a full-blown love affair.

The Dangerous Precedent of Ignoring the Supreme Court

Kelly's comments came in the wake of the Supreme Court's temporary halt on the Trump administration's deportation of alleged Venezuelan gang members. By advising to "Ignore the Supreme Court. Arrest anyone who tries to enforce this. Dissolve the Supreme Court entirely if they push," Kelly essentially advocates for the dismantling of the checks and balances that are foundational to the United States' constitutional republic.

It's crucial to understand the gravity of this suggestion. The Supreme Court serves as the ultimate arbiter of constitutional disputes in the country, ensuring that no branch of the government exceeds its power. To suggest its dissolution or to ignore its rulings is to advocate for a tyranny of the executive, a path that leads away from democracy and towards dictatorship.

Fascist Undertones and the Danger of Authoritarian Leadership

Kelly's remarks are particularly alarming in light of his 2021 speculation that Republicans might "pick a fascist" to lead the party. His latest comments do not merely echo this sentiment; they amplify it. By stating, "Donald Trump is the last thing before we get a dictator," Kelly not only misconstrues the nature of democratic leadership but also inadvertently acknowledges a willingness—perhaps even a desire—among some factions to embrace authoritarianism if it serves their political objectives.

This rhetoric is dangerous. It undermines democratic institutions and norms, sowing seeds of division and mistrust. It's a call for the suspension of democracy in favor of an autocratic regime that would bypass the judiciary and, by extension, the Constitution itself.

Conservative Criticism and the Defense of Democracy

Notably, Kelly's position has drawn criticism from within conservative circles. Veteran and Texas conservative Mack Latimer's response—"This is not conservative. This is authoritarian"—captures the essence of the concern. Conservatism, traditionally, upholds the values of limited government, the rule of law, and the preservation of established institutions. Kelly's stance is antithetical to these principles, veering instead towards a dictatorial modus operandi.

Libertarian Chase Oliver's succinct advice for Kelly to "Go, and I can't stress this enough, f--- yourself," alongside political analyst Rachel Bitecofer's sarcastic "But don’t call them fascists," and civil rights litigator Patrick Jaicomo's dismissal of Kelly's post as "stupid," collectively underscore a bipartisan rejection of Kelly's anti-democratic impulses.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Jesse Kelly's comments are a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy. They underscore the need for vigilance against the rise of authoritarian sentiments, even in societies that pride themselves on their democratic traditions. The reaction from various political figures highlights a consensus that democracy, with all its imperfections, must be defended against those who seek to undermine it for political gain or out of misguided ideology.

In a time when democratic institutions are under strain from various quarters, Kelly's rhetoric is not just irresponsible; it's a clarion call for those who value democracy to stand up against the tide of authoritarianism. Let's be clear: advocating for the dissolution of the Supreme Court and the dismissal of its rulings is a step towards despotism. It is a systemic error that must be corrected, not through the dissolution of checks and balances, but through the robust defense of democratic principles.
