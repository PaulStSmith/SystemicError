The Emperor's Echo Chamber: How the Press Became Trump's Favorite Accessory

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 9, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In an era when democracy hinges precariously on truth, the White House press corps has opted for decorum over duty, access over accountability. Lawrence O'Donnell's searing monologue dissecting Donald Trump's grotesque manipulation of the media and his blatant falsehoods about tariffs is not just a media critique—it's an indictment.

We are not merely spectators to a buffoonish presidency; we are witnesses to the complicity of a fourth estate that has failed to act as a check on power. O'Donnell lays bare the painful irony: Donald Trump, the most aggressively ignorant president in American history, has been gifted with a press corps too cowardly, too career-conscious, or too incompetent to challenge even his most easily disprovable lies.

The Big Lie: Tariffs as a Revenue Stream

Trump has repeatedly asserted that tariffs are bringing in "tremendous amounts of money" to the U.S. treasury. This is an economic lie of staggering audacity. Tariffs are taxes paid by American importers, not foreign governments. The burden is passed on to consumers through higher prices. This isn't an arcane economic theory—it's first-year macroeconomics. Yet not one voice in the press pool raised an objection.

Not one reporter asked, "Mr. President, why are you lying to the American people about tariffs?"

And that's the rot at the core: the press has normalized deceit through silence. They have allowed the White House to weaponize their presence, turning legitimate journalism into ornamental farce.

Accessories, Both Legal and Decorative

O'Donnell draws a brutal parallel between the legal concept of an "accessory to a crime" and the fashion term "accessory." In Trump-world, the press corps fulfills both roles. They are accomplices after the fact, aiding and abetting misinformation by failing to confront it. Simultaneously, they are reduced to decorative props, human wallpaper that lends an illusion of transparency to a deeply opaque regime.

When Trump deflected a serious question about arms shipments to Ukraine with a smug, "I don’t know. Why don’t you tell me?" the reporter who asked the question meekly accepted it. No pushback. No insistence on truth. Worse still, another reporter jumped in to change the subject—rescuing Trump from even the mildest scrutiny. That exchange wasn’t just embarrassing; it was journalistic malpractice.

A Press Corps That Doesn't Know What It Doesn't Know

O'Donnell goes further, eviscerating the press corps not only for its cowardice but for its ignorance. He argues that most of them don't even understand the basics of how tariffs work. If true, this is catastrophic. The press's job is not just to ask questions but to know what questions matter. If they can't grasp a fundamental economic issue like tariffs, how can they hold any administration accountable?

Worse still, O'Donnell contrasts their passivity with their aggressive treatment of President Joe Biden. A reporter known for being "very aggressive" with Biden barely mumbled a follow-up to Trump's dodge. This isn't journalism; it's auditioning for a second term of White House access.

Public Smarter Than the Press

Perhaps the most damning data point: 81% of Americans report being concerned about the personal financial impact of tariffs. This suggests the public, derided so often as ill-informed, understands more about economic cause and effect than the journalists covering the presidency. The people get it. The press doesn't.

And let’s not pretend this is mere oversight. This is systemic. Trump has stacked the press room with sycophants and enablers, people who confuse propaganda with journalism. And the so-called serious outlets have chosen not to fight—but to survive.

The Danger of Normalizing Stupidity

O'Donnell’s opening salvo is worth quoting in full: "The possibility of a relentlessly stupid person becoming president... has been underexplored by screenwriters." That line should terrify us. Not just because it mocks Trump’s intellect, but because it highlights how unprepared our institutions are for someone who breaks all norms and pays no price.

We often say the system held. But did it? A president who doesn't understand his own policies, who lies without consequence, who uses the press like stage dressing, has revealed just how hollow those systems are when no one dares speak the truth.

Final Notes from the Abyss

This isn’t about liberal vs conservative. It’s about truth vs fiction, cowardice vs courage. The White House press corps had one job: to stand between the people and the power. They chose proximity to power instead.

They have become cufflinks on the emperor's empty suit—shiny, spineless, and ultimately useless.

History will not remember them kindly.
