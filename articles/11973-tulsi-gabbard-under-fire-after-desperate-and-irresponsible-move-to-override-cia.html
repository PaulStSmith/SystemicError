Tulsi Gabbard under fire after 'desperate and irresponsible' move to override CIA

Two former CIA officials are concerned about Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard and her eagerness to release top secret information that could compromise sources and methods for agents.

The Washington Post reported on Wednesday that Gabbard's indiscriminate way of releasing classified documents regarding Russian interference in the 2016 election angered many career intelligence experts. Gabbard published the information with minimal redactions.

"Gabbard, with the blessing of President Donald Trump, overrode arguments from the CIA and other intelligence agencies that more of the document should remain classified to obscure U.S. spy agencies’ sources and methods, the people said, speaking on the condition of anonymity, like others interviewed for this report, because of the matter’s sensitivity," said the report.

While CIA Director John Ratcliffe has been supportive of the release of information, those who actually work in intelligence have concerns about the release, but also that the characterization of the evidence is false. Gabbard and Trump claimed that their evidence proved that Russia did not hack the 2016 election votes. However, that was never part of the allegations. Russians hacked the Democratic and Republican Party servers and leaked information from the former. Russian-funded content farms generated scores of memes and posts spreading conspiracies about Hillary Clinton while being supportive of Trump.

"Multiple independent reviews, including an exhaustive bipartisan probe by the Senate Intelligence Committee, have found that Putin intervened in part to help Trump," characterized the Post.

The investigation documents contain multiple references to human sources that detail Putin's plans. Those sources are among the "most closely guarded secrets," the report said. "After the report was completed in 2020, it was considered so sensitive that it remained in storage at the CIA rather than on Capitol Hill."

Larry Pfeiffer, a former senior CIA and White House official, revealed on an episode of the “SpyTalk" podcast that the report had "sources and methods" that "could easily" be "inferred in almost every instance."

"I don’t know if I’ve seen a document of that sensitivity so lightly redacted," he said of the release from Gabbard.

The report released went through multiple reviews, but the once-secret document was "circulating" among Trump administration staff. Gabbard was the one who wanted to release as much as possible.

Gabbard “has greater declassification authority than all other intelligence elements and is not required to get their approval prior to release," said one person familiar with the process when speaking to the Post.

Trump then decided there would be "minimal redactions and no edits," the person added.

Sen. Mark. Warner (D-VA) called Gabbard's release of the report "desperate and irresponsible," and said it threatens some of the Intelligence community's "most sensitive sources and methods" it uses to spy on Russia and keep Americans safe.

“And in doing so, Director Gabbard is sending a chilling message to our allies and assets around the world: the United States can no longer be trusted to protect the intelligence you share with us," he warned.

Our Analysis:

The Reckless Transparency of Tulsi Gabbard

In a startling revelation that reads more like a script for a political thriller than a sober account of intelligence handling, Tulsi Gabbard, the Director of National Intelligence, and by implication, President Donald Trump, have ignited a firestorm of controversy with their decision to release top secret information related to Russian interference in the 2016 election. According to a report by The Washington Post, their actions have not just ruffled feathers but potentially endangered critical intelligence sources and methods.

A Dangerous Game

At the heart of this debacle is Gabbard's seemingly indiscriminate approach to declassifying documents that, until recently, were considered too sensitive to be made public. The insistence on minimal redactions, ostensibly to lay bare the truth about Russian interference, flies in the face of established protocols designed to protect the very backbone of intelligence operations: its sources and methods.

The narrative spun by Gabbard and Trump—that their evidence refutes the hacking of the 2016 election votes by Russia—is a masterclass in misdirection. The allegations, as repeatedly confirmed by multiple independent reviews, including an exhaustive bipartisan probe by the Senate Intelligence Committee, never centered on vote hacking. Instead, the consensus points to a systematic campaign by Russia to undermine the electoral process through hacking Democratic and Republican Party servers and spreading disinformation supportive of Trump and harmful to Hillary Clinton.

Misplaced Priorities and Mischaracterizations

The release of these documents, with what has been described as shockingly light redactions, not only puts at risk the "most closely guarded secrets" but also stands as a testament to a dangerous prioritization of political objectives over national security. Larry Pfeiffer's observation on the “SpyTalk" podcast about the unprecedented sensitivity of the lightly redacted document underscores the recklessness of this move.

It’s essential to decipher the motivations behind this drastic transparency. While Gabbard and Trump claim a noble quest for truth, the real picture emerging is one of political expediency, potentially at the cost of compromising the United States' intelligence gathering capabilities. The assertion, as reported, that Gabbard, backed by Trump, could bypass the collective wisdom of intelligence agencies speaks volumes about the current administration's disregard for established safeguards.

A Chilling Message to Allies

Senator Mark Warner’s comments encapsulate the broader implications of this saga. By prioritizing a misguided and potentially self-serving narrative over the protection of sources and methods, Gabbard is broadcasting a "chilling message" to allies and intelligence assets worldwide. The trust painstakingly built over decades, essential for the global intelligence-sharing ecosystem, is now at risk of being irreparably damaged.

Conclusion: A Precarious Path

The unfolding story of Tulsi Gabbard’s handling of top secret information is more than a case of questionable judgment. It’s a vivid illustration of how political motivations, when allowed to overshadow the meticulous and often invisible work of intelligence agencies, can endanger national security. The repercussions of this decision will likely reverberate far beyond the immediate political cycle, potentially altering the landscape of intelligence sharing and cooperation for years to come.

In the final analysis, the saga is a stark reminder of the delicate balance between transparency and security—a balance that, once upset, is not easily restored.
