The ascendancy of Cardinal Robert Prevost — now Pope Leo XIV – to become the global head of the Catholic Church is an inherent threat to the worldview of the American religious right, according to one Christian pastor.

In a recent essay for Religion News Service (RNS), the Rev. Jennifer Butler — who is the senior pastor at First Congregational United Church of Christ in Corvallis, Oregon — argued that the far right is railing against Leo XIV specifically because of the dilemma he presents to their belief system. Butler wrote that the MAGA faithful is not opposed to the new pontiff because he's a liberal like his predecessor Pope Francis, but because he's a traditional conservative Catholic who nonetheless holds radically different views than Christian nationalists.

"Leo has become a MAGA flashpoint not due to any dramatic break from Catholic doctrine, but because his worldview directly threatens the Christian nationalist engine behind the rising authoritarianism of our time," Butler wrote. "He threatens to become a transnational counterweight to a rapidly expanding authoritarian religious network."

Not long after Prevost was elected to the papacy, Vice President JD Vance traveled to the Holy See to meet the first American pope in history. But Butler pointed out that Leo XIV and Vance are in direct contradiction with one another when it comes to Christian nationalism. While Vance has argued that Americans should put their country first before the rest of the world, Leo XIV — who was a longtime priest in Peru — has said that "Jesus doesn't ask us to rank our love for others."

"This was no minor theological dustup over the proper 'order of love' Christians should obey," Butler observed. "It was a direct rebuttal to the ideological foundation of Christian nationalism: that faith should serve state nativist power and preserve national sovereignty at the expense of human rights."

The United Church of Christ pastor called the Christian right a "transnational" movement that has also found footing in authoritarian regimes like Vladimir Putin's Russia and Viktor Orbán's Hungary. And she characterized its goal as one in which its proponents impose a "rigid moral order" on the rest of the world that serves to "polarize the public and erode democratic institutions." However, she said Pope Leo XIV's tenure could be one in which the Christian right's use of religion "to justify authoritarian consolidation and frame dissent as sacrilege" is upended.

"The election of Leo XIV signals that not all global religious institutions are capitulating," she wrote. "... MAGA influencers understand that when Leo speaks, people listen — not just Catholics, but others looking for spiritual leadership grounded in clarity. His critics know how powerful that can be."

The Ascendancy of Pope Leo XIV: A Threat to the Far Right's Worldview

The recent article detailing Cardinal Robert Prevost's rise to the papacy as Pope Leo XIV and its implications for the American religious right highlights a fascinating and complex dynamic at play within global religious and political spheres. The analysis by Rev. Jennifer Butler, as reported by Religion News Service (RNS), paints a vivid picture of the ideological clash between traditional conservative Catholicism and the far-right Christian nationalist movement in the United States.

The Far Right's Dilemma: Pope Leo XIV, unlike his predecessor Pope Francis, isn't labeled a liberal by his critics. Instead, his traditional conservative stance, coupled with a distinctly anti-nationalist worldview, sets him apart. This positioning creates a substantial ideological rift with the MAGA movement, which thrives on nationalism and a distorted fusion of state power with religious authority. Butler's assertion that Leo XIV’s rise is a direct challenge to the "Christian nationalist engine behind the rising authoritarianism of our time" is both bold and insightful. It’s not about theological nuances but a clash of worldviews — one that respects global human rights and Christian love without borders versus one that seeks to prioritize national sovereignty and a rigid moral order at any cost.

Vance vs. Leo XIV: The meeting between Vice President JD Vance and Pope Leo XIV, as highlighted by Butler, symbolizes the evident contradiction in their ideologies. Vance's "America First" mantra starkly contrasts with Leo XIV's inclusive, global perspective on Christian love and compassion. This ideological divide is not merely a "theological dustup" but a fundamental disagreement on the role of faith in public and political life. The Pope's rebuttal to Christian nationalism's agenda is a clear indication of his broader vision for the Catholic Church's role in confronting modern authoritarian movements, emphasizing love and human rights over nationalistic fervor.

The Global Context: Butler's characterization of the Christian right as a "transnational" movement with allies in authoritarian regimes like Russia and Hungary is a critical observation. It underscores the global nature of this ideological battle, where authoritarian leaders exploit religious fervor to consolidate power, often at the expense of democratic institutions and human rights. Pope Leo XIV's tenure represents a beacon of hope against such trends, challenging the use of religion to justify authoritarianism and framing dissent as sacrilege.

A Powerful Counterweight: The fear among MAGA influencers and their ilk regarding Pope Leo XIV's influence is telling. It acknowledges the power of religious leadership to sway public opinion and mobilize global sentiment against authoritarianism and nationalism disguised as patriotism. Butler's essay suggests that Leo XIV's papacy could mark a pivotal moment in the struggle against the manipulation of religious belief for authoritarian ends.

In conclusion, the article effectively highlights the significant ideological and theological battle lines being drawn with Pope Leo XIV's ascendancy. His papacy, characterized by a return to traditional conservative Catholic values coupled with a firm stance against nationalism and authoritarianism, poses a direct challenge to the Christian nationalist movement. This clash of visions for the future of global Christianity and its role in society is not just a matter of religious debate but a critical battleground in the fight for democracy and human rights worldwide. Pope Leo XIV's leadership, grounded in clarity and a commitment to universal values, may indeed upend the far right's authoritarian ambitions, offering a new direction for those seeking spiritual guidance in turbulent times.
