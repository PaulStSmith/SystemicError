'Like a rogue cult leader': Ex-GOP lawmaker skewers party over fealty to Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump points a finger during a meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu (not pictured) in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, U.S., April 7, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

A former state GOP lawmaker slammed his party for giving up traditional conservative values and adopting Trumpism.

Geoff Duncan, the former Lt. Governor of Georgia and a lifelong Republican, told the panel on CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip" that President Donald Trump's "complete control" of the party poses an existential threat.

"The only example I can give is like a rogue cult leader coming into a church and taking it over," Duncan said. "He's brainwashing people, and you don't have to look any further than the zombie-esque reaction to the last few weeks. DOGE has imploded. He's made promises, yet the base doesn't hold him accountable for it."

Duncan's comments come just hours after former vice president Mike Pence told CNN that MAGA is a "minority voice" within the GOP.

"[ Trump] has spent trillions of dollars that he promised that he wouldn't," Duncan continued. "We still have war in Ukraine, and he's taken the side of Vladimir Putin until the last few hours. This is not the Republican Party that I grew up in, and I don't think it's ever coming back. I think it's gone for good."

"They're in the ditch and they just don't know it yet," Duncan added.

GOP strategist Scott Jennings replied that the party just won the national popular vote in the 2024 election, and mentioned that gas prices and grocery prices appear to be coming down.

"How are we in the ditch?" Jennings asked pointedly.

Phillip then questioned why Jennings chose to begin supporting Trump, given that he was an outspoken skeptic when Trump first ran for office.

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Existential Crisis: A Reflection on Duncan's Dire Warning

In a recent CNN's "NewsNight with Abby Phillip", Geoff Duncan, the former Lt. Governor of Georgia, delivered a scathing critique of the current state of the Republican Party, likening its capitulation to Donald Trump to a "rogue cult leader" taking over a church. This analogy, while stark, encapsulates the gravity of the situation as seen from the perspective of a lifelong Republican disillusioned by his party's departure from its traditional conservative values.

Trump's Alleged "Complete Control" and the GOP's Identity Crisis

Duncan's assertion that Donald Trump has "complete control" over the Republican Party is a bold claim, yet it's one that resonates deeply within the context of recent political dynamics. The imagery of a "rogue cult leader" and his followers exhibiting "zombie-esque reactions" is not just a dramatic flourish; it's a pointed criticism of the blind allegiance that seems to pervade the party's base, even in the face of failed promises and controversial decisions. The mention of DOGE imploding—a likely metaphor for the broader consequences of Trump's leadership—highlights the tangible impacts of this unwavering support.

The Contrast with Pence's Assessment

Interestingly, Duncan's comments arrive on the heels of remarks by former Vice President Mike Pence, who characterized the MAGA movement as a "minority voice" within the GOP. This juxtaposition paints a picture of a party at war with itself, torn between its traditional roots and the allure of Trumpism. Duncan's lament that "this is not the Republican Party that I grew up in" echoes a sentiment likely shared by other conservatives who feel alienated by the party's current trajectory.

Spending, War in Ukraine, and Putin: Where Traditional Conservatism Clashes with Trumpism

Duncan's critique extends beyond the cult of personality, touching on substantive policy issues where Trump's actions starkly deviate from conservative principles. The accusation that Trump "spent trillions of dollars that he promised he wouldn't" challenges the fiscal responsibility traditionally championed by conservatives. Moreover, the handling of the war in Ukraine and the apparent siding with Vladimir Putin represent significant departures from the GOP's historical stance on foreign policy and national security.

The Ditch Metaphor and the GOP's Future

Duncan's metaphor of the GOP being "in the ditch" suggests a party that has lost its way, blinded perhaps by short-term gains or the magnetic pull of Trump's base. This notion is sharply contested by GOP strategist Scott Jennings, who points to the party's recent success in the national popular vote and improving economic indicators like gas and grocery prices. Jennings' question, "How are we in the ditch?", reflects a fundamental divide in how success and progress are measured within the party.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The exchange on CNN underscores a critical juncture for the Republican Party. On one side, figures like Geoff Duncan mourn the loss of the GOP's traditional values and warn of the long-term consequences of its current path. On the other, proponents of Trumpism celebrate recent successes and question the premise that the party is in distress.

What is clear from this dialogue is that the Republican Party is grappling with an identity crisis, caught between the allure of Trump's base and the principles of traditional conservatism. The resolution of this conflict will not only determine the future direction of the GOP but could also have profound implications for the broader political landscape in the United States.
