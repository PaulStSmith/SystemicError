Our Analysis:

Analyzing "Protests, Polling, and Pushback": A Closer Look

In the recent episode of The New Abnormal podcast, hosts Jesse Cannon and Danielle Moodie delve into what they perceive as the crumbling of the Trump administration's stronghold, citing "protests, polling, and pushback" as the triad chipping away at the president's power base. Their analysis, while imbued with a sense of hopeful resistance, warrants a deeper, more critical examination to sift through the optimism and extract the grounded realities.

The Mythical "Trump Dam"

Cannon's metaphor of the "Trump dam breaking" is compelling but oversimplified. The notion that small victories, such as Trump's historically low 100-day approval rating, signify a fundamental weakening of his base is optimistic at best. Such metrics, while symbolically significant, overlook the entrenched systemic and structural supports that buoy Trump's presidency. Approval ratings are but the surface sheen, hardly indicative of the deep-seated political and social currents that drive Trump's base.

The Achilles’ Heel Fallacy

Describing these events as strikes against Trump's "Achilles’ heel" is to misinterpret the nature of his support. Trump’s resilience isn’t rooted in high approval ratings or universal acclaim but in a fervent, unwavering core support base that seems impervious to scandals, gaffes, or low approval ratings. The metaphor, while poetic, is misapplied—suggesting that minor victories can topple a presidency built on systemic and ideological fortifications.

Polling and Perspective

Moodie's reference to polling data comparing Trump, Biden, and Bush serves to highlight Trump's unpopularity but also inadvertently underscores a more disturbing trend: the stark polarization of American politics. Trump's 41% approval rating, while low, is alarmingly high considering the controversies surrounding his presidency. This statistic shouldn't just be a note of triumph for his detractors but a sobering reminder of the deep divisions within the American electorate.

The Fascist State Speculation

The podcast's foray into theorizing about life under a Trump-led fascist state and the possibility of him "canceling the mid-term elections" ventures into the territory of speculative fear-mongering. While it's important to be vigilant against authoritarian tendencies, such discussions risk shifting the narrative from actionable resistance to doomsday prophesizing. It’s crucial to ground our fears in reality, focusing on the tangible, present dangers rather than hypothetical extremes.

The Role of Pushback and Protests

Cannon’s encouragement of continued protests and pushback as means to "weaken" Trump underscores a fundamental truth about democratic resilience: active, engaged citizenship is crucial. However, the efficacy of these actions must not be overestimated nor romanticized. Protests are a vital expression of dissent, yet their impact is often gradual, indirect, and requires sustained momentum alongside other forms of political engagement and pressure.

Conclusion: A Call for Nuanced Resistance

In their zeal to portray a crumbling Trump administration, Cannon and Moodie offer an analysis that is as passionate as it is problematically simplistic. The fight against the regressive policies and ideologies represented by Trump requires more than just celebrating low approval ratings or engaging in speculative fears. It demands a strategic, multifaceted approach that addresses the systemic roots of his support and the broader societal issues that allowed for his rise to power.

In essence, while "Protests, Polling, and Pushback" provides a rallying cry for those opposed to Trump, it also serves as a reminder of the need for deeper, more critical engagement with the realities of American politics. The path to meaningful change is complex and requires more than symbolic victories—it necessitates a sustained, strategic, and informed resistance that is prepared for the long haul.
