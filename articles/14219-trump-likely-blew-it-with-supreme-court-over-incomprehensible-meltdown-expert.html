Trump likely blew it with Supreme Court over 'incomprehensible' meltdown: expert

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

A decision by Donald Trump to flip out on Truth Social and unload on a close associate of multiple Supreme Court justices will likely come back to haunt him.

That is according to Politico's Senior Legal Affairs correspondent Josh Gerstein who told the hosts on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" that the president may have obliterated a great deal of SCOTUS good will he could count on when he called conservative judicial activist Leonard Leo a "sleazebag."

After Trump tariff war was put on a temporary pause on Thursday, he turned to his Truth Social account to post an extraordinarily long and vicious attack on Leo as well as the Federalist Society which is home to a wide array of conservative attorneys and judges.

EXCLUSIVE: Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

In part, the president wrote, "I was new to Washington, and it was suggested that I use The Federalist Society as a recommending source on Judges. I did so, openly and freely, but then realized that they were under the thumb of a real 'sleazebag' named Leonard Leo, a bad person who, in his own way, probably hates America, and obviously has his own separate ambitions. He openly brags how he controls Judges, and even Justices of the United States Supreme Court — I hope that is not so, and don’t believe it is! In any event, Leo left The Federalist Society to do his own 'thing.'"

According to Gerstein, Trump will come to regret the attack.

"Leo is somebody who's very close to those justices and we're already seeing signs, right, that the three Trump appointees are not so firmly in Trump's camp as some would have expected," the legal expert told the panel.

"And to have that kind of unhinged attack calling Leonard Leo a sleazebag, the word the president used, I mean, it's almost incomprehensible," he added before recalling, "The last time I saw Leonard Leo was last fall at the Federalist Society conference, and he was not schmoozing with other, you know, wealthy lawyers who were there or other conservative legal activists who were there. He was sitting in the lobby of the Hilton Hotel that I know you all are very familiar with, chatting with a Catholic priest and a Catholic nun, okay, so the guy is not a sleazebag."

"There are people that will have disagreements and criticisms, will say he's anti-majoritarian, those are all things that I think one can debate as reasonable criticism," he pointed out. "But to call him a sleazebag is just sort of bizarre lashing out at someone because you didn't get your way and I don't think that's going to sit well with his friends on the Supreme Court."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Trump's Tirade Against Leonard Leo and Its Potential Backlash

In a recent spectacle of political theatre, Donald Trump unleashed a tirade against Leonard Leo, a figure with deep ties to the Supreme Court's conservative justices, on his platform Truth Social. Labeling Leo a "sleazebag" and launching a broadside against the Federalist Society, Trump's outburst is not just a simple case of political sour grapes but a strategic blunder of potentially epic proportions.

The Unraveling of Trump's Judicial Safety Net

Trump's presidency was notable for its aggressive reshaping of the federal judiciary, with the Federalist Society playing a pivotal role in recommending conservative judges and justices. The irony here is palpable. Trump, who once benefited from the guidance and influence of the Federalist Society, now finds himself in a self-inflicted feud with one of its key architects, Leonard Leo. This move is akin to a chess player knocking over their own queen in a fit of pique.

Josh Gerstein, Politico's Senior Legal Affairs correspondent, suggests that this outburst might sever the unspoken bond of goodwill between Trump and the conservative justices he appointed. It's a classic case of cutting off one's nose to spite one's face. The three Trump appointees, already showing signs of judicial independence, may now feel even less beholden to their benefactor's whims and wishes.

The Misguided Attack on Leonard Leo

The attack on Leo, described as "extraordinarily long and vicious," is not just a personal vendetta but a strategic misstep. Gerstein recounts a seemingly innocuous sighting of Leo, engaging in humble conversation with religious figures, to counter Trump's "sleazebag" allegation. This anecdote, though seemingly trivial, serves to humanize Leo and, by extension, paints Trump's assault as not just unfounded but petty.

Trump's characterization of Leo as someone who "probably hates America" and has ambitions to control judges and justices reads like a conspiracy theory rather than a reasoned critique. It's a narrative that might resonate with Trump's base but risks alienating the broader conservative legal community that respects Leo's contributions.

The Real Loser in Trump's Unhinged Attack

Trump's tirade against Leo and by extension, the Federalist Society, is symptomatic of his broader political strategy: loyalty above all else, and retribution against those who are perceived to deviate from this loyalty. However, in attacking a key figure in the conservative legal establishment, Trump is not just burning bridges; he's detonating them.

The fallout from this attack is not limited to Trump's relationship with the Supreme Court. It signals to the broader conservative movement that Trump's allegiance is fickle, and his support is contingent upon unflinching loyalty. This is a dangerous precedent for someone who still harbors political ambitions.

Conclusion

In his quest to assert dominance and demand loyalty, Trump may have inadvertently isolated himself from a critical source of support. The Supreme Court, with its lifetime appointments, operates beyond the reach of political vendettas. Trump's inability to recognize this, choosing instead to engage in a public spat, reflects a profound misunderstanding of the judiciary's independence.

Trump's attack on Leonard Leo is not just a blip in the 24-hour news cycle; it's a strategic error that could have lasting implications on his political legacy and future ambitions. In his pursuit of perceived enemies, Trump risks alienating allies, undermining his own agenda, and, most critically, eroding the very foundation of his political support.
