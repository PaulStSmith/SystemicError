Trump flees reporters asking about peace plan: 'Not really appropriate to take questions'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Evasion: The Trump-Netanyahu Press Charade

Members of the media raise their hands to ask questions as U.S. President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu hold a joint press conference in the State Dining Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 29, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu held a joint announcement on Monday afternoon to discuss their proposed peace deal to end the Israeli occupation of Gaza — a deal that drew immediate criticism from observers for not even including leaders of the terror group Hamas at the table, which as one of the parties fighting in the conflict would be essential for the agreement to work in the first place.

The president, seemingly trying to avoid any difficult questions, simply ended the event without letting reporters ask anything.

“So I think while we wait for these documents to be signed and get everybody in line, I think it maybe is not really appropriate to take questions,” Trump told the crowd of reporters. “I’m meeting with, as you know, a couple of Democrats in a little while about the country, about keeping our country open up. They’re going to have to do some things because their ideas are not very good ones. They’re very bad for our country. So we’ll see how that works out. But this is something that was really historic today. This was something that was amazing.”

Trump turned to Netanyahu. “I think maybe I mean, depending on you, I think while we’re waiting for signatures and waiting for approvals from a lot of different countries that are involved in this, we probably shouldn’t take questions. Or would you like to take a question or two from perhaps a friendly Israeli reporter, if there’s such a thing?”

“That’s a very, very tough proposition,” said Netanyahu as some reporters in the crowd laughed. “But I would think, Mr. President, that I would go by your instinct that this is a — we’ll have enough time for questions. Let’s settle the issue first, I think.” The two leaders then exited as reporters tried to shout questions after them.

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Evasion: The Trump-Netanyahu Press Charade

In a scene that could only be described as an Orwellian twist on diplomatic transparency, President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu presented their so-called peace deal to end the Israeli occupation of Gaza. This announcement, steeped in irony and evasion, was nothing short of a theatrical display of how not to broker peace.

The Elephant Not in the Room

It is an unequivocal truth that any peace deal concerning Gaza that fails to include Hamas at the negotiation table is fundamentally flawed. Hamas, whether one labels them as a terror group or a de facto governing body, is an integral player in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict. Ignoring their role is akin to preparing a banquet and forgetting to invite the guests of honor. The Trump-Netanyahu alliance, in their infinite wisdom, decided that the path to peace could be paved by the very powers that have a vested interest in maintaining the status quo, sidelining those who are essential for any genuine reconciliation.

A Press Conference Without Press

The event quickly turned farcical when President Trump, in a move that would make any authoritarian leader blush with envy, decided to end the announcement without entertaining questions from the press. This deliberate avoidance of accountability is emblematic of Trump’s tenure: when faced with potential scrutiny, retreat is preferable to engagement. His excuse? A vague reference to awaiting documents and approvals, as if the mere possibility of signing a piece of paper in the future could somehow absolve him from addressing the present concerns of the international community and the press.

The Art of Deflection

Trump’s attempt to deflect with a non sequitur about meeting Democrats to discuss “keeping our country open” was a transparent effort to shift focus from the glaring inadequacies of the proposed peace deal. His disparagement of Democratic ideas as “very bad for our country” was not only irrelevant to the topic at hand but also a stark reminder of his penchant for polarizing rhetoric over constructive dialogue.

Netanyahu’s Silent Complicity

Prime Minister Netanyahu, for his part, played his role in this charade to perfection. His response to Trump’s suggestion of possibly taking questions from “a friendly Israeli reporter” was a tacit endorsement of the avoidance strategy. His remark about the proposition being “very, very tough” and deferring to Trump’s “instinct” was nothing short of a diplomatic sidestep, ensuring that no substantive questions could puncture the bubble of their manufactured reality.

Conclusion: A Historic Missed Opportunity

Trump heralded the event as “historic” and “amazing,” words that, in the context of their actions, ring hollow. History will indeed remember this moment, not for the achievement of peace, but for the glaring omission of critical stakeholders and the refusal to engage in transparent discourse. The Trump-Netanyahu exit, as reporters’ questions went unanswered, was symbolic of their entire approach: when the going gets tough, the tough get going—away from accountability, away from tough questions, and, most tragically, away from any real chance at peace.
