Trump's DOJ sues red state for defying order to hand over voter rolls

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 27, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Irony of “Election Integrity”

FILE PHOTO: An election worker prepares absentee ballots for the upcoming general election before they are mailed to voters, at Wake County Board of Elections headquarters in Raleigh, North Carolina, U.S. September 5, 2024. REUTERS/Jonathan Drake/File Photo

President Donald Trump’s Justice Department is suing the state of Oklahoma for refusing to turn over confidential information from its voter rolls.

According to KOSU, “Earlier this month, State Election Board Secretary Paul Ziriax wrote in a letter first obtained by Oklahoma News 4 that he could not legally share voter data with the DOJ. The Trump administration disagrees with Ziriax’s interpretation. Oklahoma was among five states sued Thursday by the DOJ.”

Among the information the DOJ demanded from the Oklahoma State Election Board is the driver’s license and Social Security numbers of people registered to vote — a demand they have made in other states as well.

“Accurate, well-maintained voter rolls are a requisite for the election integrity that the American people deserve,” said Attorney General Pam Bondi in a statement announcing the lawsuit. “This latest series of litigation underscores that This (sic) Department of Justice is fulfilling its duty to ensure transparency, voter roll maintenance, and secure elections across the country.”

A number of other states have also refused to hand over their voter rolls, and Oklahoma is not alone among Republican-controlled states. Georgia’s Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger, who famously rebuffed Trump’s demands to “find” extra votes in 2020 and is currently running for governor, also did not comply with the order.

This comes as Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt, who was elected as a hard-right candidate, has grown bolder in speaking out against the Trump administration’s more controversial policies in recent months. In an interview this week, he criticized Trump for rolling back renewable energy projects and pursuing a no-tolerance mass deportation program.

Our Analysis:

The Irony of “Election Integrity”

In a move that reeks of irony so thick you could spread it on toast, President Donald Trump’s Justice Department has launched a lawsuit against the state of Oklahoma. The crime? Refusing to hand over confidential information from its voter rolls. This scenario, as reported by KOSU, unfolds a narrative that is as alarming as it is absurd.

The Trump administration, in its infinite wisdom, disputes State Election Board Secretary Paul Ziriax’s legal stance on sharing voter data with the Department of Justice (DOJ). Here, we have the federal government demanding sensitive information—driver’s license and Social Security numbers—from registered voters. The same administration that has, time and again, cried wolf about privacy overreach and federal overstepping, now sees no issue in a sweeping collection of personal data.

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s statement on the matter is a masterpiece of doublespeak. “Accurate, well-maintained voter rolls are a requisite for the election integrity that the American people deserve,” she claims, as if the integrity of an election hinges on the federal government’s ability to stockpile private information. This litigation, according to Bondi, is all in the name of “transparency, voter roll maintenance, and secure elections.” If this is transparency, one shudders to think what opacity might look like.

Let’s not gloss over the fact that Oklahoma is not the lone star in this invasive night sky. Georgia’s Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger, a Republican, also defied similar demands. This is the same Raffensperger who stood firm against Trump’s infamous plea to “find” extra votes in 2020. The thread here is clear: even within the GOP, there’s recognition of the overreach and potential for misuse these demands represent.

Amidst this, Oklahoma Gov. Kevin Stitt—a hard-right figure—finds his voice against Trump’s controversial policies, including the rollback of renewable energy projects and the pursuit of a harsh deportation program. It’s intriguing to witness Stitt’s evolution from a Trump-endorsed candidate to a critic of the administration’s more extreme policies. This development hints at a broader discomfort within the Republican party, as members grapple with the administration’s polarizing actions and demands.

In essence, the DOJ’s lawsuit against Oklahoma is not just a legal battle over voter data; it’s a stark illustration of the Trump administration’s hypocrisy. The demand for sensitive voter information under the guise of ensuring “election integrity” is a thinly veiled attempt to exert control and instill fear. It’s a tactic that flies in the face of privacy rights and state sovereignty, ironically coming from an administration that once championed both.

The unfolding drama signals a troubling trend towards heightened federal intrusion into personal data, under the pretense of securing elections. It begs the question: At what point does the quest for “election integrity” infringe upon the very freedoms it purports to protect?

In this circus of contradictions and power plays, one thing is clear: the administration’s actions are more about consolidating power than protecting the sanctity of the vote. As the legal battles wage and states like Oklahoma and Georgia stand their ground, the narrative of “election integrity” is being co-opted into a tool for political maneuvering, rather than a genuine effort to secure and improve the electoral process.
