'It's a dictatorship': Lawmaker fears future Dems may never be sworn in under Mike Johnson

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 18, 20255 min read

Mike Johnson (Photo via Reuters)

WASHINGTON — Former House Leader Steney Hoyer (D-MD) warned this week that Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) could be slow-walking Congress into an era where he refuses to swear in more members of the Democratic caucus.

Speaking to Raw Story on Thursday about Johnson’s refusal to swear in Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ), Hoyer said that if Johnson hadn’t already sworn in two Republicans, he might have a valid argument.

“It is a dictatorship,” said Hoyer. “You cannot do that. The people of Arizona elected her by a very substantial vote. And he won’t swear her in!”

“This business of not swearing in a member of Congress. What if he decided in the next Congress, he’s an elected Speaker, and then he decides not to swear in 25% of our people?”

Raw Story also spoke with Sens. Rick Scott (R-FL), Lindsey Graham (R-SC), and Jim Banks (R-IN), all of whom dismissed the issue as either unimportant or claimed they don’t know who she is.

“Nope,” Graham said simply when asked if he’d heard about Grijalva.

“Who?” asked Scott when Raw Story mentioned her name. “They’re not in session.”

Raw Story mentioned the two lawmakers from Florida who were also sworn in during a pro-forma session, which is how Grijalva would be sworn in if Johnson chose to.

“I don’t know. I can’t imagine there’s any politics involved,” said Scott.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA) added her name to the list of furious Democrats.

“The people of her district are being denied representation,” Warren said, harkening back to the rallying cry from American colonists protesting British taxes. “The speaker of the House is too frightened to swear her in. That’s not how democracy works. There was an election. The election has been certified. She needs to be sworn in.

Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) has been one of the outspoken voices from the Arizona delegation demanding that Grijalva be seated. He and Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) confronted Johnson in the Capitol last week, demanding answers.

Kelly recalled that Johnson had previously said he would swear in the winner of the race as soon as they wished. Now, however, he’s changed his story.

He also agreed it was related to the discharge petition that would be approved if Grijalva were sworn in. A discharge petition would mandate a vote on a measure that would require the Justice Department to release the investigation files of sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. As it stands, the petition requires one additional vote to bring the legislation to the House floor for a vote.

“He should embrace the term rip off the bandaid,” quipped Kelly, noting that Johnson will eventually be forced to swear Grijalva into her seat and that the discharge petition is coming even if he wants to stop it. “Just get it done and over with.”

“But there are a lot of women out there who deserve for there to be some accountability,” said Kelly.

Our Analysis:

A Brazen Assault on Democratic Norms

In a move that can only be described as a blatant disregard for democratic principles, Speaker Mike Johnson’s refusal to swear in Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva (D-AZ) marks a perilous slide into partisan pettiness at the expense of the American democratic process. This isn’t merely a procedural hiccup; it’s a crude power play with dangerous implications for the future of congressional operations.

The Mockery of Democracy

Imagine, if you will, the audacity of a Speaker who decides, on a whim, which duly elected members get to participate in the governance of their country. Former House Leader Steney Hoyer’s comments to Raw Story illuminate the gravity of the situation. By refusing to swear in Grijalva, Johnson is not only sidelining a singular Democratic voice but is threatening the very notion of representative democracy. Hoyer’s invocation of a potential future where a Speaker might refuse to swear in a significant portion of the opposition is not as far-fetched as it might once have seemed.

The Partisan Echo Chamber

The responses from Senators Rick Scott, Lindsey Graham, and Jim Banks are emblematic of a broader issue within American politics: a wilful ignorance or, worse, a tacit endorsement of anti-democratic maneuvers, so long as they serve partisan ends. Graham’s terse “Nope” and Scott’s feigned ignorance speak volumes about the Republican Party’s collective shrug towards the erosion of democratic norms.

Accountability and Representation Denied

At the heart of this debacle is the undeniable fact that the people of Arizona’s congressional district are being denied their rightful representation. Senator Elizabeth Warren’s comparison to the rallying cry of American colonists protesting British taxes - “no taxation without representation” - rings eerily relevant. The Speaker’s actions, or lack thereof, are not just a slap in the face to Grijalva but to every voter who believed their voice would be heard in the halls of Congress.

The Epstein Connection: A Disturbing Undercurrent

The refusal to swear in Grijalva gains an additional, sinister dimension with the revelation that her seating could pave the way for a discharge petition related to the Jeffrey Epstein investigation. This isn’t just about partisan politics; it’s about obstructing justice and shielding the powerful. Senator Mark Kelly’s remarks underscore the urgency and necessity of transparency and accountability, values that are seemingly anathema to Speaker Johnson.

Conclusion: A Democracy at Risk

Speaker Mike Johnson’s actions, bolstered by a complicit silence from his party, represent a direct assault on the foundational principles of American democracy. This isn’t merely a question of political strategy; it’s a question of how far we’re willing to let our leaders erode the institutions and norms we hold dear. If the refusal to swear in a duly elected representative is allowed to stand without significant outcry and consequence, we must ask ourselves: What comes next?

In an era where democratic norms are under constant threat, it’s incumbent upon every citizen to remain vigilant, to demand accountability, and to ensure that no individual, regardless of position or power, can undermine the collective will of the people. The situation with Rep.-elect Adelita Grijalva is a stark reminder of the battles ahead in the defense of democracy.
