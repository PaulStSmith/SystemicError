GOP rep confirms Trump DOJ spying on members of Congress

President Donald Trump’s Department of Justice (DOJ) is apparently keeping close tabs on members of Congress who are reading unredacted Jeffrey Epstein documents. That apparently even includes Republicans.

On Wednesday, Rep. Nancy Mace (R-S.C.) – who is not running for another term this fall — posted to her official X account that she “will confirm” the DOJ is actively surveilling members of Congress perusing the Epstein files.

“I was able to navigate the system today and I won’t disclose how or the nature of how; but confirmed the DOJ is tagging ALL DOCUMENTS Members of Congress search, open and review,” Mace tweeted. “Based on how I confirmed this, there are timestamps associated with this tracking.”

“I’m probably the last person you want in your software,” she added.

Mace’s post comes on the same day Attorney General Pam Bondi sat for several hours of questions from the House Judiciary Committee. CNBC reported that during the hearing, a photographer captured an image of notes Bondi had at her desk. One document Bondi had in a folder read “Jayapal Pramila Search History,” suggesting the DOJ was keeping track of what documents Rep. Pramila Jayapal (D-Wash.) was reading when she looked through the Epstein files this week. Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-Md.), who is the ranking member of the Judiciary Committee, told journalist Pablo Manriquez that he “had reason to believe [DOJ surveillance] was happening to everyone.”

“It is a violation of the separation of powers for them to essentially be monitoring our computer searches on the Epstein files,” he said. “It’s outrageous, Orwellian, and we’re going to have more to say about it soon.”

Members of Congress were able to start reading through unredacted Epstein documents earlier this week. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.), who co-authored the Epstein Files Transparency Act, accused the DOJ of protecting Epstein’s alleged co-conspirators. He later named six men who may have committed crimes with Epstein whose names were redacted in the DOJ latest batch of files.

Our Analysis:

The Orwellian Oversight: DOJ’s Surveillance of Congressional Epstein Inquiry

In a revelation that seems ripped straight from the pages of a dystopian novel, the Department of Justice (DOJ), under the Trump administration, has been caught with its hands far too deep in the cookie jar of surveillance, monitoring the very individuals meant to hold them accountable. The recent uncovering that the DOJ has been keeping a watchful eye on members of Congress accessing unredacted Jeffrey Epstein documents is not just a breach of privacy but a flagrant violation of the separation of powers—a cornerstone of American democracy.

The Unsettling Revelation

Rep. Nancy Mace (R-S.C.) threw the first stone, revealing on her official X account a practice so Orwellian, it sends shivers down the spine. According to Mace, the DOJ isn’t just passively observing; it’s actively “tagging” all interactions with the Epstein documents by members of Congress, including search histories, document access, and review times. This isn’t just oversight; it’s overreach.

The timing of Mace’s disclosure, coinciding with Attorney General Pam Bondi’s marathon questioning by the House Judiciary Committee, couldn’t be more telling. A captured note, suggesting the DOJ was keeping tabs on Rep. Pramila Jayapal’s (D-Wash.) search history regarding the Epstein files, adds a layer of concrete evidence to these Orwellian tactics.

A Violation of Trust and Law

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-Md.), ranking member of the Judiciary Committee, minced no words in calling out this surveillance as a “violation of the separation of powers.” The sentiment echoes across the aisle, transcending partisan boundaries in recognizing the gravity of this breach. This isn’t about politics; it’s about power, and the unchecked surveillance of lawmakers delving into the Epstein saga is a testament to the lengths the DOJ will go to protect certain interests.

The Epstein Files Transparency Act

At the heart of this controversy is the Epstein Files Transparency Act, co-authored by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.), which aimed to shed light on the murky dealings of Jeffrey Epstein and his alleged co-conspirators. The DOJ’s latest batch of files, with redactions still hiding the names of six men accused of crimes alongside Epstein, only fuels the fire of suspicion. Massie’s accusation that the DOJ is protecting these individuals is not just a claim; it’s a chilling reminder of the potential complicity within the highest levels of law enforcement.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This Orwellian surveillance saga is more than just a privacy issue; it’s a direct challenge to the democratic principles upon which the United States is built. The DOJ’s actions, as described by members of Congress, represent a dangerous encroachment on legislative independence and a blatant disregard for the checks and balances designed to prevent the abuse of power.

The implications of this surveillance extend beyond the Epstein case, hinting at a broader pattern of governmental overreach. If members of Congress can be surveilled this brazenly, what does that say about the privacy and freedom of the average citizen?

It is imperative that this incident not be swept under the rug. There must be a thorough investigation, transparency, and, most importantly, accountability. Anything less would be a disservice to the principles of justice and a betrayal of the American people’s trust. The DOJ’s Orwellian antics must be met with unwavering scrutiny and swift action to preserve the sanctity of our democratic institutions.
