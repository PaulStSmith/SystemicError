MAGA melts down after conservative paper tries to get men back to work

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Paulo SantosAugust 23, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Right Is Furious at the Wrong Class

The Setup

A Wall Street Journal video asked why young men are leaving the workforce and offered the usual managerial answer: nudge men toward education, health care, administration, training, and apprenticeships. That is the level of the source material. The reaction from MAGA was more revealing than the video itself, because it exposed what they are determined not to discuss: power, ownership, and the labor market that has been deliberately remade around them.

Who Actually Holds Power

Mary Julia Koch is not a random commentator. She is an associate editor at the Wall Street Journal and the daughter of David Koch, the late Koch Industries executive and Republican megadonor. That matters because the outrage machine wants to turn her into a symbol of elite condescension while ignoring the institutions she comes from and the economic order her family helped finance.

But the bigger power is not her. It is the corporate and political system that has spent decades automating work, shipping jobs overseas, and narrowing the terms of employment until “choice” is mostly a slogan. The source itself points to male-dominated industries moving into automation and offshoring. That is the real story. Everything else is theater.

The Favorite Lie: Blame Women, Blame DEI

Rep. Brandon Gill and Benny Johnson both did what the right always does when faced with structural decline: they searched for a scapegoat with a face. Gill blamed “institutions” for purging young white men from entry-level jobs. Johnson blamed “DEI policies,” “HR Karens,” and “untalented and unqualified women and minorities.”

That is not analysis. It is grievance laundering. It converts labor-market restructuring into a morality play about supposedly undeserving women and nonwhite workers. It gives angry men a target that is easier to hate than the executives, investors, and political donors who actually own the machinery of displacement.

The Useful Condescension

Koch’s suggestion is not some radical answer. It is a technocratic patch for a political injury. Encourage men into sectors where women already work. Expand apprenticeships. Rebrand the problem as a skills mismatch. That is the standard elite reflex: when the economy is broken, rearrange the résumé, not the system.

The weakness in that framing is obvious. Men are not drifting away from work because they forgot the right career advice. The source points to automation, offshoring, and more men staying home. Those are consequences of an economy organized for capital accumulation, not social stability. Vocational pep talks do not reverse that. They merely dress up surrender as guidance.

The Real Enemy They Won’t Name

The rage in the MAGA response is misdirected on purpose. It is easier to attack a billionaire heiress than to attack billionaires as a class. It is easier to rail against “women in the workforce” than to confront the fact that men’s jobs were stripped, outsourced, mechanized, and cheapened by decisions made at the top.

This is the right’s preferred fraud: it takes a real collapse in work, status, and expectation, then routes the blame downward toward women, minorities, and public institutions while sparing corporate power. It is reactionary politics with a labor-market vocabulary. It sounds like economic complaint, but it serves hierarchy.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is simple. Elites produce instability. Reactionaries weaponize the fallout against vulnerable people. And the conversation never returns to the actual mechanisms of power: ownership, offshoring, automation, and the political economy that treats workers as disposable inputs.

The men leaving the workforce are not a mystery. They are evidence. The scandal is not that Mary Julia Koch suggested a few career pivots. The scandal is that a right-wing movement can look at decades of economic damage and still decide the problem is women getting jobs. That is not confusion. It is the maintenance of a system that needs scapegoats more than solutions.
