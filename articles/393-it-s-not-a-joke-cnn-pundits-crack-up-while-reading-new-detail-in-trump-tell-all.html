'It's not a joke': CNN pundits crack up while reading new detail in Trump tell-all

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20262 min read

A President Who Needed a Caddy to Forge His Own Myth

The Power Was Real

The central fact here is not that Trump compared himself to Attila, Genghis Khan, Napoleon, Stalin, Mao, and Hitler. It is that he did so while sitting inside the office that controls the federal state. That is the institutional reality the laughter can’t erase: a president does not need to be historically literate to wield power, only willing to use it.

The Lie Was the Point

Trump reportedly presented the document as if it came from a presidential historian. It did not. According to the reporting, it came from Gary Player’s longtime caddy. That detail matters because it shows the familiar Trump method: dress vanity up as authority, then dare everyone else to notice the costume after the performance has already landed.

Who Enabled the Scene

Trump made the claim, but he did not build the whole illusion alone. Someone in his orbit produced or passed along a document meant to flatter him with grotesque historical comparisons. That is not confusion. It is enablement. The machinery around him did what loyal political machinery often does: convert insecurity into propaganda and feed the boss a script that flatters his appetite for domination.

What the Media Found Funny

CNN’s panel laughed because the story is absurd on its face. But the absurdity is exactly why it should be treated seriously. The focus on the punchline risks muting the harder truth: this is a president using mythmaking, false attribution, and strongman comparison as self-advertising. When the press treats that as pure eccentricity, it helps launder authoritarian performance into late-night banter.

The Misdirection Problem

The weaker actors in this story are not the ones shaping events. The weak actors are the commentators left laughing at a stunt that only works because the office itself carries force. The article’s own framing leans toward spectacle, but the actual consequence is straightforward: a powerful man lied about the source of his own praise while presenting himself in the language of dictatorship. That is not harmless cringe. It is political theater built to normalize contempt for restraint.

The System Beneath the Joke

This is what modern authoritarian branding looks like when it is functioning properly: the leader inflates himself, the entourage supplies the props, and the media is invited to giggle at the ridiculousness instead of tracking the power arrangement underneath. The real story is not that Trump is absurd. It is that absurdity and power are perfectly compatible, and the institutions around him keep proving they will often describe the spectacle before they name the damage.
