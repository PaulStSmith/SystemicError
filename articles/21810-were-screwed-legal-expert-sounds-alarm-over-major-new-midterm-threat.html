‘We’re screwed’: Legal expert sounds alarm over major new midterm threat

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Election Machinery Runs Through Cowardice

Ty Cobb’s warning is simple: the next democratic breach may not look like 2020, because the personnel and the institutions around Trump are already more compliant. He pointed to Todd Blanche’s confirmation as attorney general, House Speaker Mike Johnson’s role, and the prospect of ICE being used around polling places as the ingredients of a new intimidation campaign.

Power, Not Process

This is not a story about abstract election “concerns.” It is about who holds the levers. Trump has the presidency. Blanche now sits atop the Justice Department. Johnson controls the House floor and the machinery of seating members. That is the power map. Everything else is decoration.

The relevant question is not whether Trump might “pressure” officials. He will. The real issue is whether the institutions meant to resist him have already been staffed with people willing to comply, stall, or pretend they are powerless. Cobb’s answer is that they have.

The Enabler Is the Office

Blanche’s confirmation matters because the attorney general is not some neutral referee. The office can bless fraud narratives, weaponize law enforcement, and give authoritarian fantasy the veneer of procedure. That is what made Bill Barr so dangerous in 2020: not that he invented Trump’s lies, but that he delayed, narrowed, and managed them just enough to make them usable.

Cobb is describing a worse version of that problem. If the Justice Department is already wired to serve the president, then the barrier to election interference is no longer law. It is only embarrassment. That is a paper wall.

ICE at the Polls Is Not “Security”

The ICE detail is the ugliest part because it exposes the tactic plainly: intimidation dressed up as enforcement. If ICE is being discussed as a visible presence near polling places, then the goal is not public safety. It is fear. That is ballot suppression with a badge.

There is no democratic fix for that by way of “better training” or “clearer guidance.” ICE should be abolished. An agency built to terrorize immigrant communities cannot be repurposed into neutral election infrastructure because its political function is already coercive. Putting ICE near polls is not a mistake to be corrected. It is the point.

Johnson’s Gatekeeping Role

Cobb’s mention of Mike Johnson is not incidental. If a legitimately elected Democratic Congress is refused seating, delayed, or otherwise obstructed, the crisis will not begin with a courtroom flourish. It will begin with a Republican-controlled House acting like loyalty to Trump outranks certification of voters.

That is the slow-motion version of authoritarianism: not one dramatic coup, but a series of “contemptuous acts,” each small enough for commentators to sanitize and large enough to corrupt the outcome. The institution does the damage and then asks to be praised for preserving order.

The Pattern

The pattern is now familiar. The right does not merely threaten democracy; it normalizes the tools needed to break it, then hands those tools to obedient officials and calls it governance. The press, meanwhile, keeps translating deliberate sabotage into procedural anxiety.

That framing is too soft. This is not confusion. It is preparation. Trump’s project depends on officials willing to make misconduct look bureaucratic and coercion look routine. Cobb is not warning about a hypothetical collapse. He is describing a state that has already bent toward the man who wants to own it.
