'They are sick!' Raging Trump demands criminal probes into reports of his bad poll numbers

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media on board Air Force One on the way to Miami, Florida, U.S., April 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump highlighted findings by a Republican pollster to attack media organizations that found his approval rating was lagging.

A survey conducted by ABCNews and the Washington Postfound just 39 percent of Americans approve of his performance as president while 55 percent disapprove, while a New York Times poll found 44 percent approval and 53 percent disapproval. But Trump pointed to claims by GOP pollster John McLaughlin that the polls were rigged against him.

"Great Pollster John McLaughlin, one of the most highly respected in the industry, has just stated that The Failing New York Times Poll, and the ABC/Washington Post Poll, about a person named DONALD J. TRUMP, ME, are FAKE POLLS FROM FAKE NEWS ORGANIZATIONS," Trump posted Monday on Truth Social at 5:28 a.m. EST. "The New York Times has only 37 percent Trump 2024 voters, and the ABC/Washington Post Poll has only 34 percent Trump Voters, unheard of numbers unless looking for a negative result, which they are."

The president suggested their polling methods might have violated election laws and targeted Fox News, as well, after the conservative network's recent poll showed similar findings, with 44 percent approval and 55 percent disapproval.

"These people should be investigated for ELECTION FRAUD, and add in the FoxNews Pollster while you’re at it," Trump posted. "They are Negative Criminals who apologize to their subscribers and readers after I WIN ELECTIONS BIG, much bigger than their polls showed I would win, loose [sic] a lot of credibility, and then go on cheating and lying for the next cycle, only worse. They suffer from Trump Derangement Syndrome, and there is nothing that anyone, or anything, can do about it."

"THEY ARE SICK, almost only write negative stories about me no matter how well I am doing (99.9 percent at the Border, BEST NUMBER EVER!), AND ARE TRULY THE ENEMY OF THE PEOPLE!" Trump added. "I wish them well, but will continue to fight to, MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!"

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Art of Distraction and Disinformation

In a recent spectacle of outrage and defiance, President Donald Trump has taken to Truth Social to launch an attack against a series of unfavorable polls, utilizing a strategy that is as transparent as it is tiresome. It's a classic Trump maneuver: when the news doesn't favor him, discredit the messengers and claim victory regardless of the facts. The essence of his recent tirade can be distilled into a few key points, each more ludicrous than the last.

The Attack on Polls: A Tale as Old as Time

The article outlines Trump's assault on various polls conducted by ABCNews, the Washington Post, and the New York Times, which depict his approval ratings in a less-than-favorable light. According to these reputable sources, his approval ratings are underwater, with a significant portion of the American populace disapproving of his performance as president. Yet, Trump, in an attempt to deflect these unfavorable optics, points to John McLaughlin, a GOP pollster, claiming these polls are "rigged" against him.

The Irony of Crying Wolf

Trump's allegations of "fake polls from fake news organizations" and his subsequent suggestion that these organizations should be investigated for "election fraud" would be laughable if they weren't so dangerously misleading. His use of capitals and bold assertions serves not to strengthen his argument but to highlight the fragility of his position. It's a classic case of crying wolf – except this time, the wolf accuses the villagers of conspiracy for pointing out his failure to protect the flock.

Election Law Violations: A Baseless Claim

The claim that these polling methods might have violated election laws is as baseless as it is desperate. Trump's attempt to lump in Fox News, a network that has historically been favorable to him, with his accusations, only underscores his willingness to attack any entity that does not align with his narrative, regardless of their past allegiance.

The Blame Game and Trump Derangement Syndrome

Trump's declaration that these media organizations "suffer from Trump Derangement Syndrome" is a fascinating example of psychological projection. By accusing his critics of being irrational in their disapproval, Trump deflects from the substantive criticisms of his presidency. His statement that these organizations are "sick" and "the enemy of the people" for reporting unfavorably on his performance — particularly his dubious claim of achieving "99.9 percent at the Border, BEST NUMBER EVER!" — is not only a gross exaggeration but a dangerous attack on the free press.

Conclusion: The Real Enemy of the People

Trump's final wish for the well-being of these organizations, paired with his pledge to continue fighting to "MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN," rings hollow in the shadow of his preceding attacks. It's a thinly veiled attempt to play the victim while actively undermining the institutions that hold power to account.

In this latest outburst, Trump has again shown a flagrant disregard for the truth and a profound misunderstanding of the role of a free press in a democratic society. The real "enemy of the people" is not the organizations that strive to report the truth, but those who seek to discredit it for their personal gain.
