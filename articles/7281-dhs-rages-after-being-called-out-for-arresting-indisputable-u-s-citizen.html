DHS rages after being called out for arresting 'indisputable' U.S. citizen

Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agents wait outside a courtroom to make targeted detainments at U.S. immigration court in Manhattan, New York City, U.S., December 18, 2025. REUTERS/David ‘Dee’ Delgado

A Homeland Security spokesperson erupted this week after attorney Victoria Slatton accused Immigration and Customs Enforcement of unlawfully arresting her client, who Slatton says is an American-born U.S. citizen.

Dulce Consuelo Diaz Morale, a 22-year-old woman whose lawyer says was born in Maryland, was arrested last Sunday by ICE officers and held at a detention facility in Baltimore, Maryland, CBS News reported this week.

In a series of now-viral videos, Slatton has said that she’s been unable to contact Morale, who, despite a Maryland District Court judge ruling that Morale could not be deported from the United States, was later transferred to an ICE detention facility in Louisiana, and later, to an ICE detention facility in Texas. Slatton said she only learned of her client’s transfers through public records.

Ticia McLaughlin, DHS assistant secretary, would later lash out at Slatton’s claims in a statement to CBS News, and insisted that Morale was “not a U.S. citizen.”

“She did not provide a valid U.S. birth certificate or any evidence in support of her claim that she is a U.S. citizen,” McLaughlin told CBS News. “[And], any allegation that ICE does not allow detainees to contact legal assistance is false. All detainees have access to phones to communicate with lawyers.”

Slatton openly refuted both claims from DHS.

“It is an indisputable fact that she was born inside the United States. I’ve seen her birth certificate. We have immunization records. We have multiple affidavits from people who were there at her birth,” she said, CBS News reported.

“I have not been able to talk to her. I was supposed to be able to speak to her in a confidential meeting...when we connected to that meeting, we were told that she was transferred. Her family was told she’s being deported, thank goodness that was not the case.”

Our Analysis:

The Alleged Unlawful Detainment: A Critical Analysis

In an era where trust in governmental institutions is teetering on a precipice, the recent debacle involving Dulce Consuelo Diaz Morale, a 22-year-old woman claimed to be an American-born U.S. citizen, and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), specifically Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), adds fuel to the fire of systemic distrust. The incident, as reported, spirals into a convoluted narrative of accusations, denials, and the apparent obfuscation of justice.

The Heart of the Matter

Morale’s attorney, Victoria Slatton, accuses ICE of unlawfully arresting her client, alleging Morale to be a U.S. citizen by birth. The narrative that unfolds is one of bureaucratic mishandling and alleged denial of rights, with Morale being shuffled between detention facilities across states, all the while her attorney struggles to establish contact—a fundamental right purportedly guaranteed to detainees.

DHS’s Retort: A Study in Deflection?

Ticia McLaughlin, DHS assistant secretary, vehemently denies Slatton’s claims, insisting Morale is not a U.S. citizen and alleging a lack of valid documentation to prove otherwise. This rebuttal, however, raises eyebrows. Slatton’s counterclaim of having seen Morale’s birth certificate and other supporting documents casts a shadow on DHS’s assertion. The situation begs a critical question: Is the issue at hand truly about the lack of documentation, or is there a deeper, more systemic issue within DHS and ICE’s operational frameworks?

The Right to Legal Assistance: A Hollow Promise?

McLaughlin’s statement that “all detainees have access to phones to communicate with lawyers” flies in the face of Slatton’s firsthand account. If an attorney of record struggles to maintain contact with their client, what does this say about the state of detainees’ access to legal assistance? This discrepancy is not just troubling; it’s alarming. It suggests a potential breach of the basic rights afforded to individuals within the U.S. legal system, citizen or not.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection on Systemic Flaws

This incident, while isolated, is symptomatic of a broader issue within the United States’ immigration enforcement agencies. The alleged mishandling of Morale’s case is indicative of a systemic error—a lack of transparency, accountability, and respect for basic human rights. It underscores the urgent need for comprehensive reform within DHS and ICE, where the principles of justice and fairness are upheld, not as mere ideals, but as concrete practices.

In Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Reform

The case of Dulce Consuelo Diaz Morale is more than a legal anomaly; it’s a litmus test for the state of America’s immigration enforcement policies and practices. It challenges us to question, critique, and demand better from institutions built to uphold the law. For if the law does not protect the most vulnerable among us, who does it protect?

In the pursuit of justice and transparency, it’s imperative that DHS and ICE are held accountable for their actions and that a thorough, unbiased investigation is conducted. Only then can trust in these institutions be restored, and only then can we begin to address the systemic errors that plague our system.
