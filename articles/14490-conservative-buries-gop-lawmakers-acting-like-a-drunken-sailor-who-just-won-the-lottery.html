Conservative buries GOP lawmakers acting like 'a drunken sailor who just won the lottery'

Longtime conservative columnist Matt Lewis took a dim view of the budget deal that passed this week with a one-vote margin, lamenting it will explode the national debt to historic levels.

Appearing on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," the columnist and MSNBC contributor was asked by host Jonathan Lemire to weigh in on the bill that was barely approved by the GOP caucus with a bullying assist from Donald Trump.

"Gve us your sense as to what this bill actually is and what its future might be," host Lemire pressed his guest.

"Yeah, look, it takes a lot of chutzpah to, you know, pass a bill that raises the deficit by trillions of dollars and, you know, call it conservative," Lewis began before dryly noting, "It seems like their definition of limited government is spending like a drunken sailor who just won the lottery, so it will be interesting to see."

"The House Freedom Caucus mostly went along with this, and we'll see if some of the, you know, deficit and budget hawks in the Senate do as well," he added.

"Let me say though, I am impressed with the Speaker of the House Mike Johnson," he remarked. "This is a guy who, I mean, this is like finding out the guy who volunteers to run the church potluck somehow rewrote the tax code. I mean, it's stunning to me what the what he has pulled off. But, of course, it's not Mike Johnson pulling it off –– it's Donald Trump and to me, that's what the big story here is."

He later pointed out, "At the end of the day, this is a party completely beholden to Donald Trump. I don't think he really cares that much about the details about Medicaid, about spending. I think he likes getting things done and having a deal and so this will happen."

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Fiscal Conservatism: A Critical Analysis of the Budget Deal

In an era where political theater often overshadows the substance of policy, the recent budget deal passed by a razor-thin margin serves as a perfect illustration of the glaring inconsistencies that riddle our political landscape. Longtime conservative columnist Matt Lewis's appearance on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" provides a necessary, albeit bleak, commentary on the state of fiscal conservatism within the GOP, especially under the looming shadow of Donald Trump.

The Budget Deal: A Mockery of Fiscal Responsibility

Matt Lewis's critique begins with a scathing indictment of the bill's financial imprudence, which is poised to "explode the national debt to historic levels." This observation is not just a mere critique but a glaring alarm over the hypocritical pivot of the GOP towards fiscal extravagance, under the guise of conservatism. Lewis's comparison of the GOP's spending habits to "a drunken sailor who just won the lottery" is not just a colorful metaphor but a pointed critique of the reckless abandon with which the party, once the bastion of fiscal restraint, now operates.

The Irony of Conservative Support

The support from the House Freedom Caucus, a group purportedly dedicated to limiting government spending and preserving fiscal responsibility, for this bill is ironic, to say the least. It highlights a disturbing trend within the party: the principles of conservatism are easily sidelined when they conflict with the prevailing political winds, especially those blown by Donald Trump.

The Trump Factor

Lewis astutely points out the central role Donald Trump plays in this legislative process, overshadowing figures like Speaker of the House Mike Johnson, who Lewis sarcastically remarks, is like someone "who volunteers to run the church potluck [but] somehow rewrote the tax code." This observation speaks volumes about the real power dynamics within the GOP, where Trump, despite not holding a formal office, continues to exert an outsized influence over the party's direction and policy decisions.

A Party Beholden to One Man

Lewis's most damning critique, however, is reserved for the party's unwavering loyalty to Trump, a man he suggests is more interested in the spectacle of deal-making than the substantive details of governance, such as the implications of Medicaid spending. This observation is a stark reminder of the transactional nature of Trump's political ethos, where the ends invariably justify the means, irrespective of the long-term consequences on the nation's fiscal health or the ideological coherence of the party.

Conclusion: A Call for Genuine Conservatism

The passage of this budget deal, facilitated by Trump's influence and supported by a party that has seemingly abandoned its principles, is a stark reminder of the need for a return to genuine fiscal conservatism. The GOP must reconcile its actions with its stated values, lest it risks becoming a party of convenience, unmoored from any foundational principles besides the pursuit of power. The critique by Matt Lewis, therefore, is not just a commentary on a single budget deal but a clarion call for introspection and realignment within the Republican Party.
