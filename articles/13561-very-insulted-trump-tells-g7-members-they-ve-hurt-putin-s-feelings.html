'Very insulted': Trump tells G7 members they've hurt Putin's feelings

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's G7 Rhetoric: A Masterclass in Misdirection and Revisionism

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a meeting with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney (not pictured) at the G7 summit in Kananaskis, Alberta, Canada, June 16, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

U.S. President Donald Trump used a press conference with G7 chair Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney to air Russia's grievances about being kicked out of what was then the G8 over the annexation of Crimea.

Carney had just publicly welcomed Trump to the summit when the president launched into his claim that there would never have been a full-scale invasion of Ukraine had he been president.

"The G7 used to be the G8," Trump began before attacking former Canadian Prime Minister Jusin Trudeau in a rambling statement.

" Barack Obama and a person named Trudeau didn't want to have Russia in. And I would say that that was a mistake, because I think you wouldn't have a war right now if you had Russia in, and you wouldn't have a war right now if Trump were president four years ago.

"But it didn't work out that way. But it used to be the G8, and now it's, I guess, what's that? Nine years ago, eight years ago, it switched over. They threw Russia out, which I claimed was a very big mistake, even though I wasn't in politics. And I was very loud about it. It was a mistake in that you spent so much time talking about Russia, and he's no longer at the table, so it makes life more complicated. But you wouldn't have had the war. And other than that, I think we're going to accomplish a lot."

After asking about trade with Canada and whether the U.S. would get involved in the Israel-Iran conflict, a reporter steered Trump back to the topic of Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Trump added that he wasn't saying Russia should be let back in to the global policy summit because "too much water has gone over the dam, maybe."

Trump then mocked Trudeau again, saying, "the head of your country, the proud head of your country, didn't want [Putin]."

After another question about Israel, Trump claimed that Putin now talks only to him.

"Putin speaks to me; he doesn't speak to anybody else. He doesn't want to talk because he was very insulted when he got thrown out of the G8, as I would be, as you would be, as anybody would be. He was very insulted.

"And, I mean, he was thrown out by Trudeau, who convinced one or two people along with Obama. He was thrown out. And he's not a happy person about it, I can tell you that. He basically doesn't even speak to the people that threw him out. And I agree with him."

Our Analysis:

Trump's G7 Rhetoric: A Masterclass in Misdirection and Revisionism

In a display of unparalleled historical revisionism and a blatant disregard for the facts, former U.S. President Donald Trump used a press conference with G7 chair Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney to air what can only be described as a distorted version of reality. His statements, riddled with inaccuracies and a peculiar nostalgia for Russia's participation in the G8, serve as a stark reminder of his tenure's often chaotic foreign policy approach.

Trump's Misplaced Nostalgia for the G8

Trump's lamentation over Russia's expulsion from the G8 (now G7) over its annexation of Crimea is a bewildering attempt to rewrite a clear narrative: Russia was expelled because it flagrantly violated international law. Trump's assertion that "there would never have been a full-scale invasion of Ukraine" under his presidency is both unprovable and irrelevant, as it ignores the underlying issue of Russia's aggression towards its neighbors. His criticism of former Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau—misidentified in his ramble as "Jusin Trudeau"—and former U.S. President Barack Obama for their stance on Russia is not just misplaced but a glaring attempt to absolve Russia of its actions.

The Faulty Logic of Inclusion

Trump's argument that including Russia in the G8 would have prevented the war in Ukraine is a dangerous simplification of complex international relations. His claim hinges on the assumption that mere dialogue at a high-profile summit could deter a nation from pursuing aggressive territorial expansion. This notion is not only naive but dangerously misleading. It suggests that the international community should reward aggressive behavior with a seat at the table, thereby undermining the very principles the G7 stands for.

Trump and Putin: A Dubious Relationship

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Trump's press conference was his boast about his exclusive communication with Russian President Vladimir Putin. "Putin speaks to me; he doesn't speak to anybody else," Trump claimed, painting a picture of a personal relationship that somehow transcends international diplomacy. This assertion raises serious questions about the nature of Trump's relationship with Putin, especially given the backdrop of Russia's continued aggression and interference in democratic processes around the world. It's a chilling reminder of Trump's often criticized approach to Putin, one marked by seeming deference and alarming camaraderie.

Mocking Allies and Misrepresenting Facts

Trump's mockery of Trudeau, referring to him as "the head of your country, the proud head of your country, didn't want [Putin]," is both disrespectful and indicative of his broader disdain for traditional allies. His personal attacks on leaders who stand up for international norms reflect a fundamental misunderstanding of global leadership and diplomacy.

Conclusion: A Disturbing Display of Revisionism

Trump's statements at the G7 press conference are more than just ramblings of a former president; they are a deliberate attempt to rewrite history, misrepresent the facts, and undermine the international order. His misplaced nostalgia for the G8, faulty logic on international relations, cozy relationship with Putin, and disrespect for allies are not just troubling on their own but paint a picture of a leader unmoored from the realities of global diplomacy and the principles of democratic governance.

In a world fraught with complex challenges, Trump's rhetoric is not just a harmless diversion; it's a dangerous distortion that we must continually challenge and correct. The stakes are too high to let such narratives go unchallenged.
