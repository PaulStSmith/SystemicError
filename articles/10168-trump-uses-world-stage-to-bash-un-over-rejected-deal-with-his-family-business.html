Trump uses world stage to bash UN over rejected deal with his family business

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 24, 20254 min read

President Donald Trump complained for minutes on end during his address to the United Nations General Assembly Tuesday about what he perceived as a real estate snub in the early 2000s to renovate the U.N. building in New York City, New York.

“Many years ago, a very successful real estate developer in New York known as Donald J. Trump bid on the renovation and rebuilding of this very United Nations complex, I remember it so well,” Trump said in his address to close to 150 world leaders.

“I said at the time that I would do it for $500 million, rebuilding everything. It’d be beautiful, I used to talk about ‘I’m going to give you the best of everything; you’re going to have mahogany walls, they’re going to give you plastic.’”

Trump did, in fact, make a bid in the early 2000s to renovate the U.N. building, though his offer was ultimately rejected, a rejection that reportedly enraged Trump, who went on to appeal the rejection, and even make an offer to waive his fee should he be awarded the contract.

Trump went on to complain about his rejected offer before the several dozen world leaders, alleging that the company that was ultimately awarded the project “did not know what they were doing when it came to construction,” and that he had accurately predicted that the project would see “massive cost overruns.

“I turned out to be right, they had massive cost overruns and spent between $2 trillion and $4 billion on the building, and did not even get the marble floors that I promised them!” Trump continued.

“You walk on terrazzo, do you notice that? As far as I’m concerned, frankly, looking at the building andgetting stuck on the escalator... they still haven’t finished the job!”

Our Analysis:

The Art of the Grudge: Trump’s U.N. Tirade

In what can only be described as a surreal blend of personal vendetta and international diplomacy, former President Donald Trump transformed the United Nations General Assembly—a forum designed for global cooperation and addressing the world’s most pressing issues—into a stage for airing decades-old grievances over a real estate deal gone sour.

Trump’s lament over his rejected bid to renovate the U.N. building is not just a bizarre footnote in a speech to world leaders; it’s a stark illustration of his inability to distinguish between personal business interests and the duties of public service. His fixation on this rejection, to the extent of complaining about it in a global forum years later, underscores a pettiness and self-obsession that is both astonishing and alarming.

Mahogany Walls vs. Diplomatic Halls

Trump’s promise of mahogany walls as a symbol of opulence he could bring to the U.N. is emblematic of his approach to governance—valuing appearance over substance, showmanship over diplomacy. It’s a clear reflection of his priorities, where aesthetic improvements trump (no pun intended) the real and hard work of international relations and peacekeeping.

The Cost Overrun Conundrum

Trump’s claim that the company which eventually took on the renovation “did not know what they were doing” and caused “massive cost overruns” is rich with irony. Not only does this complaint seem petty in the context of a U.N. address, but it also mirrors criticisms often levied against Trump’s own projects. The assertion that the renovation cost “between $2 trillion and $4 billion” is a bafflingly wide range that seems to betray a lack of understanding of numbers—or perhaps a dramatic flair for hyperbole.

Moreover, Trump’s fixation on the absence of marble floors—lamenting the presence of terrazzo instead—serves as a metaphor for his tenure in office: a focus on superficial details over the foundational issues facing the world.

The Escalator to Nowhere

The mention of getting “stuck on the escalator” is perhaps the most metaphorically rich part of Trump’s complaint, symbolizing his presidency’s frequent inability to ascend to the heights of effective leadership and often finding itself mired in controversies of its own making. It’s a fitting encapsulation of a tenure marked by unfulfilled promises and stalled initiatives.

Conclusion: The Divider in Chief

Trump’s address to the U.N. was a missed opportunity to engage with global leaders on the critical issues facing the world today. Instead, it was hijacked by personal anecdotes that highlight his enduring preoccupation with perceived slights and an insatiable need for validation and acknowledgment of his business acumen.

This episode at the U.N. serves as a stark reminder of Trump’s tenure: a period characterized by a profound misunderstanding of the presidency’s purpose. It wasn’t about serving the public or fostering global cooperation but about nursing personal grievances and promoting the Trump brand, regardless of the stage or the stakes involved.
