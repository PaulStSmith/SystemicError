'Can we stop pretending?' Ex-RNC official snaps at report on Trump negotiations

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Illusion: Trump's "Diplomacy" in Ukraine

Michael Steele (MSNBC screenshot)

Following a report that Donald Trump is claiming he is making headway in negotiations with Ukraine President Volodymyr Zelenskyy after they met in Rome where they are both attending the burial of Pope Francis, former RNC chair Michael Steele wasn't having it.

On MSNBC's "The Weekend," Steele, as co-host, noted the report that Trump "expressed optimism about ending the war in Ukraine. Posting online that negotiations are, quote, 'going smoothly and that success seems to be in the future,'" before adding, "Color me skeptical."

"You know, Trump doesn't engage in diplomacy, alright?" he exclaimed, "So can we stop pretending he does? He doesn't. This, you know, the social media posts on all of that is just because –– the bottom line is his design has been drawn up by Russia. The plan he's pushing is a Russian plan."

"The reality of it is, and I appreciate President Zelenskyy's optimism of a good meeting, what what else is he going to say? It sucked?" he added. "But the reality of it is that you're dealing with someone who's not a good faith partner and someone who does not want to see you survive this."

"So why are we sitting down and pretending that this is all now going to shift in a direction in which a fair deal, in which the sovereignty of Ukraine remains intact and Russia backs the hell up and gets out," he asked.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Illusion: Trump's "Diplomacy" in Ukraine

In a recent spectacle that could only be described as the apex of political theater, Donald Trump has claimed to make significant strides in negotiations with Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The stage for this dubious declaration? None other than Rome, amidst the somber occasion of Pope Francis' burial. This claim, best taken with a grain of salt—or perhaps the entire salt shaker—was scrutinized by Michael Steele, the former RNC chair, on MSNBC's "The Weekend."

Steele's skepticism is not just warranted; it's imperative. Trump, whose diplomatic finesse is as subtle as a sledgehammer, has "expressed optimism about ending the war in Ukraine." Yet, Steele cuts through this smokescreen with the precision of a scalpel: "Trump doesn't engage in diplomacy," he asserts. And how could we argue? Trump's tenure was hardly a masterclass in the art of negotiation, leaning more towards unilateral decisions and Twitter diplomacy.

The critique doesn't end with Trump's questionable negotiation skills. Steele sharply points out that Trump's plan seems less like a roadmap to peace and more like a leaflet out of the Kremlin's playbook. The plan he's pushing is a Russian plan. Here, Steele not only questions the authenticity of Trump's intentions but also underscores a dangerous undercurrent of foreign influence.

Moreover, Steele empathizes with Zelenskyy's predicament, acknowledging the Ukrainian president's need to maintain a facade of optimism. "But the reality of it is that you're dealing with someone who's not a good faith partner," Steele says, highlighting Trump's lack of commitment to Ukraine's sovereignty and survival.

Steele's finishing blow is a question that slices to the heart of the matter: "So why are we sitting down and pretending that this is all now going to shift in a direction in which a fair deal, in which the sovereignty of Ukraine remains intact and Russia backs the hell up and gets out?" This rhetorical question isn't just a critique of Trump's alleged negotiation efforts; it's a scathing indictment of the entire charade being passed off as diplomacy.

In dissecting this narrative, we're forced to confront a sobering reality: the erosion of genuine diplomatic engagement. Trump's claims of progress, set against the backdrop of a global leader's funeral, not only smack of opportunism but also highlight a concerning departure from the norms of international diplomacy. Steele's commentary isn't just a critique; it's a clarion call to scrutinize the substance over spectacle, to differentiate between genuine diplomatic endeavors and self-serving political maneuvers cloaked in the guise of negotiation.

As we navigate these murky waters, the importance of critical analysis and skepticism has never been more crucial. In an era where political narratives are increasingly muddled by disinformation and grandstanding, Steele's insights remind us of the imperative to remain vigilant, questioning, and, above all, skeptical of the motives behind the headlines.
