'Bad news for the White House': Dems now tied with the GOP on key Trump issue

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Sparky's Analysis:Trump's Tarnished Economic Halo: A Deep Dive into the April Polls

U.S. President Donald Trump arrives on the South Lawn of the White House in Washington, U.S., April 13, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

An April poll shows President Donald Trump is losing his biggest polling advantage over Democrats.

“Bad news for the White House per @EchelonInsights,” wrote Snapchat host Peter Hamby. “Dems are now tied with Republicans on the question of who would do a better job on inflation and the cost of living.”

Digital monitoring company Echelon Insights' April 2025 Voter Omnibus package revealed that "on the issue of inflation and cost of living," Trump and Democrats now share 43% approval. It also showed Trump’s overall approval going negative with 51% disapproving and 47% approving, only four months into his term, which is commonly considered still within the White House “honeymoon phase” for many presidents.

Additionally, 48% of respondents say Donald Trump “does not have a thorough plan and end-goal for tariffs, compared with 42% who say he does.”

This marks a change from February, when the Pew Research Center reported that about three-quarters of Republicans and Republican-leaning independents (73%) expected the economy to be better a year into President Donald Trump’s second term. Last year, Gallup reported the economy to be the “most important” issue in the 2024 election vote, which promises problems for Republicans leading the US House in the midterms if the economy remains voters' highest priority.

Former Vice President Kamala Harris lost the November election mere weeks after the same poll reported that “voters view Donald Trump as better able than Kamala Harris to handle the economy, 54% versus 45%."

House Majority PAC Communication Director CJ Warnke posted on X that Echelon Insights information mirrors other polling over the last two weeks “consistently show[ing] Trump's Econ approval sinking lower and lower. Echelon: -8% CBS/YouGov: -12% Fabrizio Ward: -8% Navigator Research: -13% Morning Consult: -3% Economist/YouGov: -10%.”

Sparky's Analysis:

Trump's Tarnished Economic Halo: A Deep Dive into the April Polls

In the latest twist of political theater, President Donald Trump finds himself in an unfamiliar position—losing his grip on what was once his unassailable stronghold: the economy. According to an April poll by Echelon Insights, the once-clear divide between Republicans and Democrats over who could best manage inflation and the cost of living has now dissolved into a dead heat, with both camps locked at 43% approval. This development is not just a blip on the radar but a glaring red flag for the Trump administration, signaling a potential shift in the political landscape as we inch closer to the midterms.

The Honeymoon is Over The so-called "honeymoon phase" for presidents, a period marked by typically high approval ratings and goodwill, appears to have been cut short for Trump. Only four months into his term, a stark 51% disapprove of his performance, overshadowing the 47% who still stand by him. This dip into negative approval territory is a significant departure from the norm and suggests a growing disenchantment among the electorate.

Tariffs Without a Plan? A particularly damning finding from the poll is that 48% of respondents believe Trump lacks a "thorough plan and end-goal for tariffs," compared to 42% who think otherwise. This perception undermines not only Trump's economic policies but also his image as a decisive leader who gets things done. It's one thing to wage trade wars; it's another to do so without a clear strategy or objective, leaving the public and markets in a state of uncertainty.

Economic Optimism Wanes Among Republicans The shift in sentiment is even more pronounced among Republicans and Republican-leaning independents. Just a few months ago, in February, a Pew Research Center report showed that 73% were optimistic about the economy improving in the second year of Trump's term. This optimism was in stark contrast to the current climate of skepticism and concern. If the economy remains the paramount issue for voters, as it was in the 2024 election according to Gallup, this erosion of confidence could spell trouble for Republicans aiming to maintain control of the US House.

The Competition Heats Up It wasn't long ago that Trump was seen as the more capable economic steward compared to former Vice President Kamala Harris, with a 54% to 45% advantage in voter perception. However, the tide seems to be turning. The Echelon Insights poll, along with other recent polls by CBS/YouGov, Fabrizio Ward, Navigator Research, Morning Consult, and Economist/YouGov, consistently show Trump's economic approval rating plummeting.

Conclusion: An Economic Achilles' Heel? The narrative that once buoyed Trump to electoral victory—the promise of economic prosperity and adept management of the nation's finances—is unraveling. As the economic approval ratings continue to sink, it's clear that Trump's once impenetrable armor has been compromised. This isn't just a series of unfortunate polls; it's a wake-up call to the administration that resting on past economic laurels isn't enough. Voters demand not just promises but tangible plans and outcomes.

In the political arena, where perceptions often hold more weight than reality, the administration's failure to articulate a clear and convincing economic strategy could be its undoing. As we move closer to the midterms, it's imperative for Trump and his team to recalibrate their approach if they wish to avoid the economic issue becoming their Achilles' heel.
