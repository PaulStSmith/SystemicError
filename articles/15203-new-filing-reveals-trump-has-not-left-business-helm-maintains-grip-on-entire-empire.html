New filing reveals Trump has not left business helm: 'Maintains grip on entire empire'

The U.S. president has long insisted he has little to do with Trump Organization operations while in the White House, but the document, titled “notice of individual person with significant control,” identifies him as an individual with influence over Golf Recreation Scotland Limited, through which he controls the Trump Turnberry golf course, reported Forbes.

"A more detailed section of the filing, labeled 'nature of control,' specifies that 'the person has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, significant influence or control over the activities of a trust,'" the publication reported. "The Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust holds Golf Recreation Scotland Limited and, via a web of additional entities, virtually every other asset in Donald Trump’s empire. By stating that Trump has control over the trust, his business appears to admit that Trump maintains a grip on his entire empire."

The "nature of control" section describes how, during his first term in office, Donald Trump Jr. and Trump Organization executive Allen Weisselberg, with Eric Trump serving as an adviser, served as the trust's designated trustees or trustee, Forbes reported, but the arrangement makes clear that Trump delegated authority while retaining ultimate power over the company.

Trump promised from the start of his first term that he wouldn't talk businesses with his sons, but Eric Trump told Trump in early 2017 that his father would receive financial updates “probably quarterly," while documents showed he could demand money from the trust at any point, and he frequently visited his properties with Secret Service agents in tow.

Another document, filed in December 2024 with the Securities and Exchange Commission, also stated that Trump remains the sole beneficiary of his trust, and as recently as last month, the president urged his business should fire ethics attorney William Burck over his work for Harvard University – and Eric Trump confirmed a short time later they were parting ways.

A spokesperson for the Trump Organization admitted the structure of the business has not changed, which Forbes took to mean Trump has been in charge all along, but the White House said last month that he had little control over the company's operations.

“The president’s assets are in a trust managed by his children while he is working overtime to lead the country to economic prosperity," a spokesperson said last month, but White House representatives did not respond to questions about the new filing.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Business Empire: A Cloak of Transparency or A Veil of Deception?

In an era where political transparency is more of a buzzword than a practice, documents filed with British regulators have thrown a glaring spotlight on President Donald Trump’s claims of dissociation from his sprawling business empire. Despite Trump's assertions of having "little to do with Trump Organization operations while in the White House," a document, strikingly titled “notice of individual person with significant control,” begs to differ. It explicitly identifies Trump as wielding influence over Golf Recreation Scotland Limited, a cog in the wheel of his Trump Turnberry golf course operations, as reported by Forbes.

The Illusion of Separation

Trump’s verbal gymnastics around his business involvement have been a contentious issue since his inauguration. He has maintained a narrative of detachment, asserting that his sons, primarily Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump, along with Trump Organization executive Allen Weisselberg, were at the helm. However, the nature of control detailed in the filings paints a starkly different picture. It's not just a matter of Trump having his cake and eating it too; it's about him having the entire bakery at his beck and call.

The document's "nature of control" section is particularly damning. It delineates that Trump, through the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust, which holds Golf Recreation Scotland Limited among other assets, "has the right to exercise, or actually exercises, significant influence or control." This admission is akin to opening Pandora's box, revealing that Trump's grip on his empire is as tight as ever.

Promises Made, Promises Broken

Trump's promise to abstain from business discussions with his sons now seems more like a performative gesture rather than a binding commitment. Eric Trump’s admission that his father would receive financial updates “probably quarterly” is the tip of the iceberg. The reality that Trump could demand money from the trust "at any point," coupled with his frequent visits to his properties, underlines a blatant contradiction between his public declarations and his actions.

Moreover, the recent push to fire ethics attorney William Burck over his work for Harvard University, which was quickly acted upon, further underscores the centralization of power. The Trump Organization’s spokesperson’s tacit admission that the business structure “has not changed” is the nail in the coffin, unequivocally indicating that Trump has been at the driver's seat all along.

The Verdict

The White House’s attempt to brush off these revelations with platitudes about Trump leading the country to "economic prosperity" while his assets are in a trust "managed by his children" does little to mitigate the concerns these documents raise. The lack of responsiveness to questions about the new filing only adds to the opacity surrounding Trump's business dealings.

In essence, these documents filed with British regulators do not just challenge Trump’s claims of non-involvement with his business empire; they shatter the illusion of separation he has tried to maintain. It's a stark reminder of the complexities and potential conflicts of interest that arise when a business mogul ascends to the presidency without fully divesting from his empire. Trump's tightrope walk between his roles as president and business magnate is fraught with ethical questions, leaving the public to wonder where his loyalties truly lie.
