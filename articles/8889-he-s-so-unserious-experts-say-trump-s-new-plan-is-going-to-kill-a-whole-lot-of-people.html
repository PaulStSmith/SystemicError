'He's so unserious': Experts say Trump's new plan is 'going to kill a whole lot of people'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Healthcare Gambit: A Mirage of Magnanimity

U.S. President Donald Trump listens to remarks during a swearing-in ceremony for Administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Mehmet Oz in the Oval Office in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 18, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Donald Trump over the weekend was met with ridicule after he unleashed a major plan that he said will provide a multibillion dollar payout directly to Americans as an alternative to funding government health care subsidies that have divided lawmakers amid the ongoing government shutdown.

“I am recommending to Senate Republicans that the Hundreds of Billions of Dollars currently being sent to money sucking Insurance Companies in order to save the bad Healthcare provided by ObamaCare, BE SENT DIRECTLY TO THE PEOPLE SO THAT THEY CAN PURCHASE THEIR OWN, MUCH BETTER, HEALTHCARE, and have money left over,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

But the plan didn’t go over well with some experts, including economist Justin Wolfers, who chimed in with, “Econ 101 midterm question: Based on what you know about risk pools and adverse selection in healthcare markets, will giving people money instead of giving them money to buy health insurance lead these markets to function more effectively, or will it just lead more people to be uninsured?”

Democratic Senator Chris Murphy said, “This is, unsurprisingly, nonsensical.”

“Is he suggesting eliminating health insurance and giving people a few thousand dollars instead?” the senator then asked. “And then when they get a cancer diagnosis they just go bankrupt? He is so unserious. That’s why we are shut down and Americans know it.”

Combat veteran Matthew Spira added, “Because Americans themselves have shown they do such an excellent job at choosing the optimized Medicare Advantage plans for themselves, and do not get fleeced by companies into bloated profit-optimized packages for the companies.”

“And do a wonderful job of investing for themselves,” he wrote on X. “Which is why the financial advisor industry is just withering away.”

Melanie D’Arrigo, executive director of Campaign for New York Health, also weighed in:

“The average cost of giving birth without insurance is $18,865. The average cancer patient will spend at least $150k. Trump’s approach would bankrupt and kill even more Americans when they can’t afford care. Our taxes should be funding universal healthcare with Medicare for All.”

She added that the plan “is going to kill a whole lot of people.”

“This benefits the for profit health industry, not us,” she said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Healthcare Gambit: A Mirage of Magnanimity

In a theatrical display of what can only be described as fiscal folly, Donald Trump has proposed a plan that seems to be more of a mirage than a meaningful solution to America’s healthcare crisis. His grand solution? To funnel what he claims to be “Hundreds of Billions of Dollars” directly into the pockets of Americans, ostensibly for them to purchase their own healthcare. This plan, unveiled amidst the cacophony of a government shutdown, not only misdiagnoses the ailment but prescribes a remedy that could exacerbate the patient’s condition—leaving them worse off than before.

The Premise: A Misguided Missile

Trump’s proposition, as articulated on his social media platform Truth Social, promises a utopian scenario where Americans are handed a wad of cash to buy “MUCH BETTER, HEALTHCARE” while presumably having money to spare. However, this notion is fundamentally flawed. As economist Justin Wolfers aptly points out, it ignores the basic principles of risk pools and adverse selection in healthcare markets. By dismantling the structured payment system that spreads risk among a larger group and opting for direct payouts, Trump’s plan could inevitably lead to a significant increase in the uninsured population. Wolfers’s critique isn’t just academic pedantry; it’s Economics 101, highlighting how Trump’s plan could destabilize the healthcare market further.

Democratic Senator Chris Murphy and Combat Veteran Matthew Spira: Voices of Reason

Senator Chris Murphy and combat veteran Matthew Spira offer scathing, yet wholly rational, critiques of Trump’s plan. Murphy’s rhetorical question sheds light on the absurdity of expecting individuals to manage catastrophic health events with a “few thousand dollars.” Spira, on the other hand, draws on the sarcasm-laden truth that Americans often struggle with making optimized healthcare decisions in an already convoluted system, let alone if they were thrown into the deep end with a lump sum of cash expected to cover their healthcare needs comprehensively.

Melanie D’Arrigo’s Stark Warning

Melanie D’Arrigo, the executive director of Campaign for New York Health, provides a chilling perspective on the real-world implications of Trump’s proposal. By highlighting the exorbitant costs associated with major health events like childbirth and cancer treatment, D’Arrigo underscores the sheer inadequacy of Trump’s plan. Her assertion that such a plan would not only bankrupt individuals but also potentially lead to loss of lives is a dire prediction that cannot be ignored. D’Arrigo’s analysis cuts to the heart of the issue—Trump’s plan, far from being a panacea, could very well be a death sentence for those unable to afford care.

Conclusion: The Mirage of Beneficence

In essence, Trump’s healthcare proposal is a mirage of magnanimity—a plan that at first glance seems to promise relief but upon closer inspection reveals itself to be fraught with peril. The critiques leveled by economists, politicians, and healthcare advocates alike paint a picture of a plan that is not only impractical but potentially lethal. It plays into the hands of the for-profit health industry while leaving the average American exposed and vulnerable.

As the nation grapples with the complexities of healthcare reform, it becomes increasingly clear that what is needed is not a band-aid solution but a thoughtful, comprehensive approach that ensures affordable and accessible healthcare for all Americans. Trump’s proposal, unfortunately, is a far cry from this ideal, serving as a stark reminder of the need for solutions grounded in reality, not rhetoric.
