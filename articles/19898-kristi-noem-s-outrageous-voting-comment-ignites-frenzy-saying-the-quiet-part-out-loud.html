Kristi Noem's 'outrageous' voting comment ignites frenzy: 'Saying the quiet part out loud'

By Paulo Santos

Kristi Noem / Gage Skidmore

Kristi Noem set off a firestorm among political observers over the weekend by making a comment about voting that many said was “saying the quiet part out loud.”

Noem, the U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security, said during a press event Saturday, “When it gets to Election Day, we’ve been proactive to make sure that we have the right people voting, electing the right leaders to lead this country.”

That comment about “the right people” voting immediately spread online, with Jonathan Karl, ABC News Chief Washington Correspondent, saying one word: “Extraordinary.”

The popular Ekklesia Network wrote simply, “They are saying the usually quiet part quite openly.”

Sports analyst Ben Fowlkes chimed in, “If we let these people destroy our democracy we won’t be able to say that we didn’t have plenty of advance warning.”

Former Obama insider David Axelrod said, “THIS is why Noem will remain in place, despite her flagrant, corrupt mismanagement of the DHS, at least through the midterm elections.”

He added that Trump “wants a loyal apparatchik in place who will do whatever is necessary to ensure ‘the right leaders’ win.”

Camille MacKenzie, who identifies as a “constitutional conservative,” weighed in, “Elections are not a ‘critical infrastructure’ responsibility that Kristi Noem is in charge of. She’s outright making up lies about what DHS is responsible for. It’s outrageous that she even thinks that this is something she has any power over.”

Ex-GOP congressman Joe Walsh was also outraged.

“To make sure the ‘right people’ are voting? Who the f--- are the ‘right people?’” he asked. “And it’s not government’s job to choose our leaders. It’s the American people’s job. I served in Congress with Kristi Noem. It’s not at all surprising to hear her spew this un-American s---.”

Dem congressman Daniel Goldman said, “They’re not even trying to hide their intention to steal the election. We cannot give one more cent to DHS until Congress ensures our elections will be free and fair.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Undercurrents of Kristi Noem’s Controversial Voting Comments

In a recent display of breathtaking audacity, Kristi Noem, the U.S. Secretary of Homeland Security, sparked a torrent of rightful outrage with her comments on voting practices. During a press event, she claimed efforts were in place to ensure “the right people” are voting. This statement, far from being a mere slip of the tongue, lays bare a deeply troubling mindset at the heart of current administration efforts toward election management.

Jonathan Karl, ABC News Chief Washington Correspondent, encapsulated the shockwave that rippled through the political sphere with a single word: “Extraordinary.” This reaction speaks volumes, highlighting the stark departure from democratic norms Noem’s words represent. The Ekklesia Network and sports analyst Ben Fowlkes echoed this sentiment, pointing out the audacity of such a statement being made so publicly.

Former Obama advisor David Axelrod pointed to a sinister motive behind retaining Noem in her position despite her “flagrant, corrupt mismanagement of the DHS.” He suggests that her loyalty to Trump is prized above all, ensuring that “the right leaders” win, a chilling prospect that undermines the very foundation of democratic elections.

Camille MacKenzie, identifying as a “constitutional conservative,” rightly criticizes Noem’s overreach, emphasizing that election oversight does not fall within the DHS’s purview. This assertion by Noem is not just a stretch; it’s a fabrication designed to mislead and justify unwarranted interference in the electoral process.

Ex-GOP congressman Joe Walsh and Democrat Daniel Goldman both express outrage and concern, pointing to the anti-democratic nature of Noem’s comments. Walsh questions the identity of these so-called “right people,” while Goldman sees this as an unabashed attempt to manipulate election outcomes.

The Heart of the Matter

At its core, Noem’s statement unveils an alarming perspective among some officials that democracy can be molded to serve the interests of a select few. The notion of “the right people” voting is an affront to the principle of universal suffrage, a cornerstone of democratic society. It’s a coded message, laden with implications about who deserves a voice in our democracy and who does not.

The Implications Are Clear

We are witnessing an era where the quiet part isn’t just spoken aloud; it’s shouted from the rooftops. The audacity with which these views are now expressed should serve as a clarion call to all who cherish democratic values. The manipulation of voter access and election outcomes undermines the very essence of what it means to live in a free and fair society.

Kristi Noem’s comments are not just extraordinary; they are reprehensible. They betray a fundamental misunderstanding or disregard for the principles of democracy. The outrage sparked by her words is not only justified but necessary, as is the scrutiny of her actions and intentions moving forward.

In a functioning democracy, there are no “right” or “wrong” people to vote—only the people themselves, exercising their rights. The attempt to curate a more convenient electorate is nothing less than a direct attack on the democratic process. If left unchecked, these sentiments and the actions that follow could lead us down a perilous path, eroding trust in our institutions and threatening the very fabric of our democracy.

As observers, analysts, and citizens, our response must be vigilant, vocal, and unwavering. We cannot afford to be passive spectators as the principles we hold dear are challenged. The fight for a democratic, inclusive, and fair electoral process requires our constant attention and action. This is not just about one comment or one official; it’s about defending the integrity of our democracy against those who seek to manipulate it for their own ends.
