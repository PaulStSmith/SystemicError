'This is a huge one': New Trump move is reportedly 'making MAGA Republicans uncomfortable'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Vengeful Politics of Donald Trump: A Critical Analysis

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump’s increasingly vengeful attack on his perceived enemies has reached a point where some of his most avid supporters are becoming hesitant that he is going too far.

That was a message Axios founder Mike Allen passed along on Monday morning during an appearance on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe.”

With the entire panel alarmed by Trump's positioning of troops in Washington, D.C., and threatening other major cities in blue states with the same, combined with the raid on former Trump official John Bolton’s home and office, the segment started off with co-host Mika Brzezinski reading from a Wall Street Journal editorial.

According to the editors, “It’s hard to see the raid as anything other than vindictive. Mr. Bolton fell out of Mr. Trump’s favor in the first term and then wrote a book about his experience in the White House while Mr. Trump was still President. Mr. Trump tried and failed to block publication.”

They then went a step further and concluded, “The real offender here is a President who seems to think he can use the powers of his office to run vendettas. We said this was one of the risks of a second Trump term, and it’s turning out to be worse than we imagined.”

That set up Allen from Axios to tell the panel, “I talked to people in MAGA world, and these are not like Paul Ryan Republicans. these are Donald Trump Republicans who said that raid which unfolded live on your air on Friday, made them uncomfortable, that this seemed like even for President Trump, this seemed like new territory.”

“Now, important to note that we don't yet know the reason for that search,” he cautioned. “We have not seen the documents that were behind it. Axios’ Marc Caputo has reporting that part of what they are looking for here had to do with the sharing of classified information and so there may be more to this than we know. There's reporting out there that it goes beyond the book. So we just don't know. But I can tell you that even MAGA Republicans are uncomfortable about it.”

“Second, the command economy, the involvement of the American government in business, such a change, and it's the deal-making mindset of the president,” he added. “And we've seen this time and again, we saw this in the U.S. getting a cut of Nvidia chips that are sold in China. We see the golden share that the US has that gives control as part of a steel deal and then we see here 10 percent of Intel, such a change. We talk again and again about how the Republican party has changed and we talk about Russia. We talk about trade, this is –– talk about the deficit, this is a huge one.”

The Vengeful Politics of Donald Trump: A Critical Analysis

In a recent development that has even the staunchest supporters of Donald Trump scratching their heads in discomfort, the former President's actions have escalated to a level that can only be described as dangerously vindictive. According to a report shared by Mike Allen of Axios on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," Trump's aggressive tactics, including the positioning of troops in Washington, D.C., and threats to do the same in other major cities, coupled with a raid on John Bolton's home and office, signify a disturbing trend that could have far-reaching consequences for American democracy.

The Wall Street Journal editorial board, hardly a bastion of anti-Trump sentiment, has openly criticized the raid on Bolton as "vindictive," highlighting a glaring misuse of presidential power. This incident is not an isolated one but a part of a broader pattern of behavior from Trump, who appears to believe that the powers of his office are tools for personal vendettas. The editorial's conclusion that Trump's actions in his second term are "worse than we imagined" is a stark warning of the dangers of unchecked executive power.

Mike Allen's conversations with individuals within "MAGA world" reveal a growing unease among Trump's base. The raid on Bolton's premises, described as "new territory" even for Trump, has evidently crossed a line for many. While the full details and motivations behind the raid remain unclear, the discomfort among Trump's supporters is telling. It suggests that there may be a limit to what even the most ardent followers will tolerate in terms of authoritarian overreach.

Furthermore, Allen touches upon the Trump administration's interventionist economic policies, such as the involvement in business deals and trade negotiations, which represent a significant departure from traditional Republican values. These actions, including securing a portion of profits from Nvidia chip sales in China and gaining control in a steel deal through a "golden share," highlight a shift towards a command economy. This is a radical change for a party that has historically championed free-market principles.

The analysis underscores a broader transformation within the Republican Party under Trump's leadership, marked by shifts in stance on issues ranging from Russia and trade to fiscal responsibility. The party's embrace of Trump's deal-making mindset and tolerance for his retaliatory tactics against perceived enemies poses serious questions about its future direction and the impact on American political norms.

In Conclusion, the events and reactions detailed by Mike Allen paint a concerning picture of a presidency and a political movement increasingly characterized by vindictiveness and a disregard for democratic principles. The raid on John Bolton's home and office, seen in conjunction with the militarization of response to civil unrest and the aggressive manipulation of economic policies, signals a dangerous escalation in Trump's authoritarian tendencies. As some of his most loyal followers begin to express unease, it is imperative for all stakeholders in the American political system to critically assess the long-term implications of these actions. The discomfort within MAGA circles may just be the tip of the iceberg, signaling deeper fractures and a potential awakening to the systemic dangers posed by such a presidency.
