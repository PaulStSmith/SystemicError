Trump's 'insane' AI image posting spree alarms onlookers: 'Pure delusion'

Presidential Theater, Real Power

The source issue is not confusion

Donald Trump spent hours on Truth Social posting more than 40 AI-generated images, including fantasies about an Iranian oil tanker, cheap shots at late-night comedians, and the usual Rosie O'Donnell fixation. The reporting treats this as a moment of alarm, and it is. But the real political fact is simpler: the president is using the machinery of attention like a private humiliation engine while still occupying the most powerful office in the country.

Power is not being misread here

Trump holds the institutional power. He is the elected president, the owner of the account, and the person with the authority to make the spectacle matter. That is the point. The photos were not random internet noise generated by an outsider. They were a presidential performance, posted while reporters said he was out golfing and inspecting White House renovations. That combination matters: leisure, control, and degradation packaged as leadership.

The real misdirection is the diagnosis game

A lot of the reaction in the source turns immediately to mental health language and 25th Amendment fantasy. That may satisfy outrage, but it can also become a dodge. The central issue is not whether Trump is “joking,” “delusional,” or “unhinged” in some clinical sense. The issue is that deliberate humiliation has become a mode of governance, and the office still functions around it.

Commentators are reacting from weakness

The people quoted in the source are not making decisions. They are trying to name a reality they do not control. Their panic may be understandable, but it is not power. The stronger political question is why the system keeps translating open contempt into content, then into chatter, then into nothing. Trump does not need his posts to be coherent. He only needs them to dominate the frame.

This is not an anomaly

The AI images are useful because they strip away the pretense. They show a president who treats public office as a feed, enemies as props, and aggression as entertainment. The tariffs of spectacle are paid by everyone else: the press, the opposition, and a public forced to live inside a degraded information environment while the office continues to function as if this were normal.

The pattern underneath

The deeper pattern is authoritarian style without shame: constant baiting, personal grievance turned into state atmosphere, and institutional passivity dressed up as concern. If Democratic politics is going to mean anything, it has to treat this as a power problem, not a personality problem. Trump is not merely embarrassing the office. He is demonstrating how much damage one man can do when the country mistakes spectacle for consequence.
