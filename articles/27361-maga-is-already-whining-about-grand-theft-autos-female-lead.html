MAGA is already whining about Grand Theft Auto’s female lead

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The verdict came before the gameAn invented takeover meets Rockstar’s workforceThe audience did not follow the outrage merchants

Grand Theft Auto’s first female lead has provoked a familiar grievance campaign from right-wing commentators who decided, before playing the game, that putting Lucia Caminos behind the wheel would make the franchise “woke.” Their complaints arrived as the new installment reportedly posted enormous early sales and strong reviews, making the backlash less a consumer revolt than an attempt to police who is permitted to occupy the center of a mass-market story.

The verdict came before the game

Markus Meechan, the Scottish YouTuber known as Count Dankula and a former UK Independence Party election candidate, told The Times there was “definitely” a risk that Grand Theft Auto would become too woke because of its female protagonist. He predicted that the male character would be portrayed as incompetent while Lucia repeatedly rescued him.

That was speculation presented as diagnosis. Meechan, who has more than a million YouTube subscribers and was fined in 2018 over a video showing his girlfriend’s dog performing a Nazi salute, did not identify a played scene establishing this supposed dynamic in the account reported by The Times.

Other commentators dispensed with even that thin pretense. Before the release, AndyPantsGaming called the game “girl boss slop” and claimed Rockstar Games was staffed by “a bunch of fat white women.” Sargon of Akkad complained that players would have to “deal with” a female protagonist in what he predicted would be “Grand Theft Auto for women.”

The point was not criticism of the game’s mechanics, writing or design. The objection was the woman herself. A franchise built around theft, armed robbery and indiscriminate violence apparently crossed a line when it gave a female character narrative authority.

An invented takeover meets Rockstar’s workforce

The suggestion that women have captured Rockstar is especially strained by the company’s reported employment figures. Women represented 18.4 percent of its workforce in April 2025, according to The Times, up from 15.7 percent in 2019. That is an increase of 2.7 percentage points, but it still leaves women a small minority inside the company.

This is how the grievance economy manufactures dispossession: modest representation becomes domination, and one female lead after decades of male-centered storytelling becomes proof that men are being displaced. The language of consumer preference disguises a demand for cultural control. Women may appear, but they are not supposed to drive—literally in this case, and narratively in every other sense.

Game writer Rhianna Pratchett put Rockstar’s decision in its less hysterical context. “Lara Croft has been around for 30 years so a game having a female protagonist is hardly new,” she told The Times, adding that Rockstar was “a little behind the curve” in placing women at the center of its stories.

Developer Gisela Vaquero described what distinguishes Lucia’s role: “She drives both the car and the narrative.” Lucia is not decorative representation attached to someone else’s adventure. She helps shape the story. That agency, more than her mere presence, is what the backlash treats as an intrusion.

The audience did not follow the outrage merchants

The campaign appears to have failed at its most basic commercial task. The Times reported that the game was on course to break records after going on sale at midnight following a 13-year wait. Sensor Tower estimated that 19.1 million copies had sold by Thursday morning, producing roughly $1.84 billion in revenue. The reported total was more than twice the $800 million earned by the previous installment on its opening day in 2013.

Those figures do not establish what every buyer thinks about Lucia, but they do show that the loud predictions of a market poisoned by female leadership did not translate into the collapse these commentators implied. Critics were also enthusiastic, according to the report.

AlterNet widens the story into a claim about women’s growing disillusionment with MAGA politics, citing Intelligencer reporting on young women encountering the far right’s “valorized” sexism. The connection is plausible but should not be overstated: one video-game release does not explain women’s political or religious behavior. What the episode does expose is the underlying political habit. Influencers convert the diminished cultural monopoly of men into a story of male persecution, then monetize the resentment.

Lucia Caminos did not seize an institution from men. She became the first woman to lead one installment of a nearly three-decade-old franchise. The people calling that intolerable were not uncovering a hostile takeover. They were demonstrating how little change is required before a movement organized around male entitlement begins to howl about its own imagined exclusion.
