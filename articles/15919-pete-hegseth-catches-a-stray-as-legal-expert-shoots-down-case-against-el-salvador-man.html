Pete Hegseth catches a stray as legal expert shoots down case against El Salvador man

CNN

A legal expert ripped apart the Trump administration's case against Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia, who the government has admitted was removed illegally from the U.S. and sent to a notorious El Salvador prison.

Attorney general Pam Bondi insisted that Garcia, a Salvadoran national who was protected from deportation by an immigration judge in 2019, was "not coming back" to the U.S. despite court orders to facilitate his return and claimed the Maryland resident was a gang member, but legal expert Roger Parloff disputed her allegations on "CNN This Morning."

"What it really is, is a double hearsay, as we would say," said Parloff, a senior editor at Lawfare. "It's a detective who wasn't brought to court, never testified, saying what? Somebody allegedly told him, and that person is a confidential informant and we don't know anything about him. So it's double hearsay as to him [being a gang member]."

"The only thing that the detective observed himself was he was wearing a Chicago Bulls hat and some sort of hoodie," Parloff added. "The hoodie had some sort of insignia that one of the officers called indicative of Hispanic gang culture, not even a particular gang, and so it all came down to the detective also said he spoke to some confidential informant who had been reliable in the past, and that person identified him as a high-ranking member of a MS-13, a clique called the Westerns. Just, that's right there in Brentwood, Long Island, and he's never lived in New York."

Abrego Garcia's attorney tried to track down the officer in 2019 case but was told he had been suspended, and the New Republic's Greg Sargent discovered that officer had actually been indicted and later pleaded guilty to giving confidential information about an unrelated case to a sex worker.

"So, in a way, like our whole portrait of him as described by the White House is, in fact, informed by somebody who was indicted for misconduct," said CNN's Audie Cornish.

The Department of Homeland Security also released court filings showing that Abergo Garcia's wife filed for a protective order in 2021 claiming her husband had punched and scratched her during a domestic dispute, which she told Newsweek had been done in an abundance of caution after being victimized by abuse in a previous relationship, but Parloff said those documents had never been provided to an immigration court.

"It had no bearing on any of his immigration proceedings, so far as I know," Parloff said. "It wasn't brought in at any of them. Obviously, you know, there's a lot of people in Congress that have these things like this out against them or, you know, [defense secretary] Pete Hegseth. He has no criminal record, not in this country, not in any country, so far as we know."

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Misdirection: The Trump Administration's Dubious Case Against Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia

In a recent spectacle of legal contortionism, the Trump administration's efforts to vilify Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia, a Salvadoran national previously shielded from deportation, have been laid bare and methodically dismantled by legal expert Roger Parloff on CNN. The administration, through Attorney General Pam Bondi, has unabashedly declared that Garcia, despite a judge's protection and court orders for his return to the U.S., was a gang member and unwelcome in the country. This claim, as Parloff elucidates, is not just thin; it's transparently flimsy, resting on the precarious foundation of double hearsay and questionable law enforcement credibility.

The Fragility of Evidence

The government's case against Garcia seemingly crumbles under the mildest scrutiny. The accusation of gang affiliation is based on nothing more solid than a detective's word—an individual who notably did not testify—about an alleged confession from a confidential informant of dubious reliability. Parloff's breakdown of the situation highlights the absurdity of using such hearsay as a linchpin for a deportation case, especially when the only observed evidence is Garcia's choice of clothing, a Chicago Bulls hat, and a hoodie with an unidentified insignia. This is not evidence; it's a caricature of investigative diligence.

The Specter of Misconduct

Adding a layer of irony (and incompetence) to the narrative, the detective in question, who contributed to the flimsy portrayal of Garcia as a gang member, was later indicted and found guilty of providing confidential information to a sex worker in an unrelated case. This revelation not only undermines the credibility of the accusations against Garcia but also casts a shadow over the administration's vetting and oversight of its sources.

Ignoring the Courts and Due Process

The Trump administration's disregard for court orders mandating Garcia's return is a stark reminder of its often cavalier attitude toward legal norms and due process. Attorney General Pam Bondi's blunt assertion that Garcia was "not coming back" to the U.S. flies in the face of the judiciary's directive and underscores a troubling willingness to override legal protections based on flimsy pretexts.

The Misuse of Domestic Dispute Allegations

Perhaps most egregiously, the administration attempted to bolster its case with allegations of domestic violence—claims that were never presented in immigration court and, according to Parloff, had "no bearing" on Garcia's immigration proceedings. This attempt to leverage sensitive and unrelated allegations to further tarnish Garcia's reputation is not only ethically questionable but also highlights a desperate grab at straws in the absence of legitimate evidence.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's case against Kilmar Armando Abrego Garcia is emblematic of a broader pattern of using immigration enforcement as a blunt instrument of political rhetoric, often at the expense of truth, fairness, and the principles of justice. The reliance on hearsay, the employment of discredited law enforcement personnel, and the disregard for legal processes reveal a systemic error in the approach to immigration and law enforcement—a failure rooted not in the protection of American citizens but in the perpetuation of fear and misinformation.

As this case unravels in the public eye, it serves as a damning indictment of the methods and motivations behind the Trump administration's immigration policies. It is a stark reminder of the necessity for rigorous scrutiny, accountability, and, above all, a commitment to justice that transcends political expediency.
