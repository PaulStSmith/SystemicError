Trump administration's new warrants come back to bite them

In recent months, President Donald Trump’s administration has tried to bypass parts of the law to nab those it believes are in the United States illegally. In some cases, they have used “administrative warrants,“ which are not the same as judicial warrants, which require a judge’s sign-off. However, in one recent case, they didn’t even try that.

That strategy is coming back to bite them as a Trump-appointed federal judge in Indiana demanded that Immigration and Customs Enforcement immediately release a Brazilian man he said was “unlawfully detained in violation of the laws or Constitution of the United States.”

Law&Crime reported Wednesday that U.S. District Judge Damon R. Leichty ordered the release of Robert Mendes Barbosa, saying that the “detention is unlawful under the applicable statutory scheme.” The statute he’s referring to is federal law.

“It seems no warrant was issued here,” the judge wrote. “Mr. Mendes Barbosa is unaware of an arrest warrant. The respondents usually will tell the court when there has been a warrant, but here they do not argue there was one and they do not produce one.”

The judge explained that Mendes Barbosa was given a notice to appear in immigration court, but that was very different than an arrest warrant.

“Warrantless arrests are permitted by statute only when certain conditions are met, such as when an ICE official personally witnesses a noncitizen attempting to enter the United States, or when an official has reason to believe a noncitizen has no right to be in the United States and is ‘likely to escape before a warrant can be obtained,’” the judge said, citing the law.

The report stated that Mendes Barbosa came into the U.S. in 2024 near San Diego, was detained and then “released on his own recognizance.” In Jan. 2026, he was then snatched by ICE agents in Boston and taken to a detention facility in Miami.

“He argues that his detention violates applicable statutes, the Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, and the Administrative Procedures Act,” the order said. “He seeks immediate release from custody or alternatively an individualized bond hearing.”

The report noted that in July 2025, ICE invented a new policy, in which all agents were to deny bond to anyone who entered the U.S. without an “inspection.” As Law&Crime characterized, it “opened the floodgates of litigation.”

Those individuals are not to be detained “for the duration of their removal proceedings” unless a judge steps in. “The Trump administration has made clear such detentions are intended to be indefinite,” the report said.

“Mr. Mendes Barbosa has met his burden of showing a ‘deprivation of rights leading to unlawful detention,’” the judge wrote.

Our Analysis:

A Systemic Subversion of Justice

In a striking revelation that underscores the Trump administration’s relentless pursuit to clamp down on immigration through any means necessary, a recent court ruling has brought to light a particularly egregious example of the administration’s willingness to bypass legal safeguards in its quest to detain individuals it deems unlawfully present in the United States. The case of Robert Mendes Barbosa, a Brazilian man ensnared in the immigration enforcement machinery, epitomizes the administration’s flagrant disregard for the rule of law and the constitutional rights of individuals.

Judicial Scrutiny Reveals Unlawful Detention

U.S. District Judge Damon R. Leichty’s ruling is a damning indictment of the administration’s tactics. Appointed by President Trump himself, Judge Leichty’s decision to order the immediate release of Barbosa is a remarkable rebuke of the administration’s immigration enforcement strategy. The judge’s assertion that Barbosa was “unlawfully detained in violation of the laws or Constitution of the United States” is a clear denunciation of the administration’s reliance on “administrative warrants” and, in some cases, no warrants at all.

The Illusion of Legality

The Trump administration’s use of “administrative warrants,” which lack the requisite approval from a judge, already skirts dangerously close to the edge of legal propriety. However, in Barbosa’s case, the administration did not even attempt this charade of legality. Judge Leichty’s observation that “it seems no warrant was issued here” and that the respondents (ICE) failed to produce a warrant when challenged, lays bare the administration’s brazen willingness to flout legal requirements and due process.

Statutory and Constitutional Violations

Judge Leichty’s ruling highlights two critical frameworks that the detention of Barbosa violated: the applicable statutory scheme and the Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause. The statutory scheme refers to federal laws that dictate the conditions under which warrantless arrests can be made, conditions that were clearly not met in Barbosa’s case. The Fifth Amendment’s Due Process Clause, a cornerstone of American constitutional rights, guarantees individuals the right to fair treatment through the judicial process—a right that was trampled upon by ICE’s actions.

A Policy of Indefinite Detention

The introduction of a new ICE policy in July 2025, denying bond to anyone who entered the U.S. without an “inspection,” is particularly troubling. This policy, as reported, seems designed to facilitate the indefinite detention of certain immigrants, a notion that stands in stark contrast to the principles of justice and liberty. Judge Leichty’s acknowledgment that the Trump administration intends such detentions to be indefinite is a chilling reminder of the administration’s autocratic approach to immigration policy.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This case is a microcosm of a larger, more systemic issue within the Trump administration’s approach to immigration. The administration’s actions, as laid bare by Judge Leichty’s ruling, demonstrate a disturbing willingness to circumvent legal standards and constitutional protections in pursuit of its immigration enforcement agenda. It is imperative that such practices are not only brought to light but are met with the full force of judicial scrutiny and public accountability. The case of Robert Mendes Barbosa should serve as a clarion call for all who value the rule of law and the rights enshrined in the Constitution. The administration’s systematic subversion of justice must not go unchallenged.
