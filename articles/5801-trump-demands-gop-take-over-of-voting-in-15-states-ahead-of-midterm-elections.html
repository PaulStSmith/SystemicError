Trump demands GOP 'take over' of voting in 15 states ahead of midterm elections

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 4, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Call for GOP Control Over Voting: A Dangerous Precedent

Conservative reveals how GOP may abandon Trump if they lose big in the midterms

Donald Trump has asked Republican Party officials to “take over” voting procedures in 15 states.

During an interview with ex-deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino, the president made a speech on immigration, and directly called for the election process to be taken into Republican control. Trump’s call is cause for concern after previous comments from the president saw him suggest canceling the midterms entirely.

Earlier this year, Trump said, ““I won’t say cancel the election, they should cancel the election because the fake news will say ‘he wants the elections canceled. He’s a dictator.’ They always call me a dictator.”

During his comments on Bongino’s podcast, Trump called for GOP members to “take over” the procedures in 15 unnamed states.

He said, “The Republicans should say, ‘We want to take over. We should take over the voting, the voting in at least many — 15 places. The Republicans ought to nationalize the voting.” Trump’s call for a political party to control the election cycle comes not long after administration officials made moves to exert more control on the process.

Trump went on to suggest the GOP must take over the elections process now or it would face a complete wipeout in the future.

He said, “If Republicans don’t get them out, you will never win another election as a Republican. It’s crazy how you can get these people to vote. If we don’t get them out, look, Republicans will never win another election.”

The New York Times reported FBI agents had seized ballots from the 2020 election in Fulton County, where Trump allies have falsely claimed election fraud took place.

Trump has also been fretting over midterm results according to analysis from Stephen Collinson, with the commentator claiming the president is worried about being hindered in the second half of his presidency.

Collinson wrote, “He showed yet again Monday he’s obsessing about the midterm elections — two days after a Democratic upset in a reliably Republican state Senate district in Texas offered another ominous sign for the GOP in November.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Call for GOP Control Over Voting: A Dangerous Precedent

Donald Trump’s recent request for the Republican Party to “take over” voting procedures in 15 states is not just another headline in the cacophony of the political discourse. It’s a glaring red flag that demands our undivided attention. In an interview with ex-deputy FBI Director Dan Bongino, Trump not only made sweeping statements on immigration but also directly solicited for the election process to fall under Republican control. This maneuver, when unpacked, reveals a disturbing inclination toward undermining democratic processes.

Trump’s History of Questionable Comments on Elections

It’s critical to note that Trump’s latest comments are not emerging in a vacuum. They are part of a disturbing pattern where he has previously hinted at canceling the midterms entirely. Earlier this year, Trump’s words, “I won’t say cancel the election, they should cancel the election because the fake news will say ‘he wants the elections canceled. He’s a dictator.’ They always call me a dictator,” were a jarring prelude to his current demands. This rhetoric is not just inflammatory; it’s perilously close to autocratic musings.

The Dangerous Implications of a Political Party Controlling Elections

Trump’s assertion that the GOP should “take over” the voting in at least 15 states is a stark affront to the very essence of democratic elections. The idea of “nationalizing the voting” by a single party is antithetical to the principles of fairness, transparency, and independence that form the bedrock of democratic electoral processes. Such a move would not only compromise the integrity of the elections but also significantly erode public trust in the outcomes.

Moreover, Trump’s rationale that this takeover is necessary to prevent a Republican wipeout in future elections is both self-serving and undemocratic. It suggests a willingness to manipulate and control electoral processes for partisan gain, disregarding the fundamental democratic principle that elections should be free, fair, and open to scrutiny, not controlled or influenced by one party.

The Alarming Precedent

This call for control over the election cycle is reminiscent of actions taken by authoritarian regimes that seek to consolidate power by undermining democratic institutions. It’s a clear signal that Trump and his allies are willing to push the boundaries of democratic norms to cling to power. Such propositions threaten the very foundation of democracy and should be met with rigorous scrutiny and resistance from all quarters of society, regardless of political affiliation.

The Role of Misinformation

The backdrop to Trump’s demands includes the seizure of ballots from the 2020 election in Fulton County, where Trump allies have propagated baseless claims of election fraud. This underscores the role of misinformation in fueling distrust in the electoral process and justifying attempts to undermine it. It’s crucial to confront and debunk these falsehoods with facts to protect the integrity of democratic elections.

Conclusion

Trump’s call for the Republican Party to take over voting procedures in 15 states is more than a political strategy; it’s a direct threat to the democratic process. It’s imperative that citizens and institutions alike remain vigilant, pushing back against any attempts to undermine electoral integrity. Democracy thrives on transparency, fairness, and accountability, principles that must be defended at all costs against any attempts to subvert them for political gain. The path Trump suggests is not just undemocratic; it’s dangerous.
