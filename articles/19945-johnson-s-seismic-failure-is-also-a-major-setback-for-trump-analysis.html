Johnson's 'seismic' failure is also a 'major setback for Trump': analysis

U.S. House of Representatives Speaker Mike Johnson shakes hands with U.S. President Donald Trump after Trump signed the sweeping spending and tax legislation, known as the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act,” at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 4, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

MS NOW producer Steve Benen says House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) is racking up the kind of failures his predecessors never had — including his most recent one, who was ousted by his own party.

On Tuesday, Johnson and House GOP leaders tried to propose a rule change that would prevent any lawmaker from threatening President Donald Trump’s tariffs until August. Johnson’s effort failed 214-217 after three Republicans joined all Democrats to destroy it.

The vote is historic for its rarity, but also historic for its frequency under Johnson, said Benen.

“During Democratic Rep. Nancy Pelosi’s tenure as House speaker, she literally never lost a rule vote. During former Republican Reps. John Boehner’s and Paul Ryan’s tenures, they also never lost such a vote,” said Benen. “In the last Congress, however, then-Speaker Kevin McCarthy lost three of these votes. And after he was stripped of his gavel, his Republican successor, Mike Johnson, lost four. In the current Congress, Johnson lost one rule vote last year, and then another Tuesday night.”

But the vote is more than just another failure for what is racking up to be one of the House’s least successful speakers. The vote itself was a warning to a president who has — at least until now — wielded complete control of the Republican Party.

Couched into Johnson’s rule was language forbidding House lawmakers from challenging Trump’s tariffs until July 31. Specifically, it stipulated that certain days no longer “constitute a calendar day” for the purpose of terminating national emergencies. This is meaningful because Trump used a national emergency to impose his controversial tariffs.

Politico described the vote as a “seismic” failure, but Benen said “it’s worth appreciating why.”

“First, this was a major setback for Trump, who expected the House chamber that he has effectively controlled for a year to back him up on his controversial trade tariffs agenda,” said Benen. “When that didn’t happen, it offered fresh evidence of an unpopular lame-duck president whose grip on Capitol Hill, even when his own party is in control, is clearly loosening.”

Secondly, Benen said this embarrassing vote “clearly didn’t do the House speaker any favors.”

“Johnson, who has long been seen as a weak figure, has now lost more rule votes than any speaker in generations, and the Louisiana representative’s troubles are only going to get worse,” Benen said. “In the wake of Tuesday night’s failure, Democrats will be able to force a series of uncomfortable votes on the White House’s tariffs, and GOP leaders, already struggling with a vanishingly small majority, won’t be able to stop them.”

Essentially, what Johnson did was ask his own party “to keep surrendering their own legal authority” to a president who is using that power to raise costs for American consumers and businesses. The failure of this vote, said Benen, was a fundamental rejection of an entire partisan mindset plaguing the Republican Party under Trump.

Our Analysis:

The Spectacular Failure of Speaker Mike Johnson: A Symptom of a Fragmenting GOP

In a political climate where the unexpected has become the norm, House Speaker Mike Johnson’s recent fiasco over a rule change aimed at shielding President Donald Trump’s tariff regime from Congressional scrutiny is not just a failure; it’s an emblematic moment of disarray within the GOP ranks. Steve Benen of MS NOW captures this debacle with a clarity that lays bare the dysfunction at the heart of the current Republican leadership.

A Historic Series of Failures

The crux of the matter lies in the numbers. The failed vote — 214-217 — against the proposed rule change is not just another tally; it’s a historic marker of Johnson’s ineptitude as a leader. To put this in perspective, Benen points out a stark contrast: “During Democratic Rep. Nancy Pelosi’s tenure as House speaker, she literally never lost a rule vote. During former Republican Reps. John Boehner’s and Paul Ryan’s tenures, they also never lost such a vote.” This comparison is not just damning; it’s a glaring spotlight on Johnson’s inability to corral his own party, marking him as potentially one of the least successful speakers in recent memory.

The Trump Tariff Shield: A Failed Gambit

Johnson’s attempt to protect Trump’s tariffs by manipulating legislative procedure was not merely a strategic misstep; it was a profound miscalculation of the political winds within his party. The inclusion of language that would forbid House lawmakers from challenging Trump’s tariffs until July 31 was nothing short of an attempt to extricate congressional authority and hand it over to a presidency marked by unilateral decision-making on trade policies. This move, rightly seen as a capitulation to Trump’s whims, was not just a failure; it was a rejection of a broader partisan alignment that has increasingly characterized the GOP under Trump.

The Ramifications of Failure

Benen astutely highlights the broader implications of this vote. Firstly, it’s a significant blow to Trump, revealing a loosening grip on a party he has dominated. The failure signals a potential shift in the power dynamics within the GOP, with lawmakers showing a rare backbone against a president’s controversial agenda. Secondly, and perhaps more crucially, it spells trouble for Johnson. His position, already tenuous, is further weakened by this public defeat. With Democrats poised to leverage this failure to force uncomfortable votes on tariffs, Johnson’s leadership — or lack thereof — will be under increasing scrutiny.

The Underlying Issue: A Partisan Mindset Rejected

Beyond the immediate political theater, the failed vote underscores a fundamental rejection of a partisan mindset that has plagued the Republican Party under Trump. Benen’s analysis suggests that Johnson’s request for his party to “keep surrendering their own legal authority” to Trump was a bridge too far for some Republicans. This moment of rejection is significant. It’s not just about tariffs or procedural losses; it’s about a growing awareness within the GOP that blind allegiance to one man’s agenda could have lasting implications for their party’s integrity and the country’s democratic processes.

Conclusion

In sum, Johnson’s failed rule change is more than a misstep; it’s a symptom of a deeper malaise within the Republican Party. The fragmentation, the loss of legislative control, and the rejection of a partisan mindset aligned too closely with Trump’s controversial policies are all coming to a head. The implications for Johnson’s leadership are dire, but more broadly, this episode is a stark reminder of the ongoing struggle within the GOP to define its identity in the post-Trump era. Benen’s analysis doesn’t just chronicle a failure; it illuminates the seismic shifts within American politics, where the battle for the soul of the Republican Party continues to rage.
