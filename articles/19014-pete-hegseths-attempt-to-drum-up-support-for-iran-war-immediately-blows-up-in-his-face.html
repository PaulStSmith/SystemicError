Pete Hegseth’s attempt to drum up support for Iran war immediately blows up in his face

U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth arrives to hold a briefing amid the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran, at the Pentagon in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 2, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Amid the ongoing Iran war, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth shared a video on social media Friday of a U.S. servicemember urging fellow servicemembers to be “combat ready” and ready for deployment, but over the weekend, the post soon backfired after critics pointed out it was five years old and featured a retired Marine who had been relieved of duty amid controversy.

Hegseth shared the video without context, leading onlookers to assume that the U.S. servicemember was speaking about the need to be “combat ready” as it relates to the Trump administration’s war against Iran. On Saturday, however, political commentator Micah Erfan revealed the context around the video, context that appeared to undercut Hegseth’s apparent effort to drum up support for war.

“This video is 5 years old and the guy in it was caught breaking into someone’s home,” Erfan wrote to his more than 57,000 followers on X.

The video shared by Hegseth features Christian Bull, a former sergeant major in the U.S. Marine Corps who enlisted in 1998. A former drill instructor in San Diego, California, Bull was relieved from duty due to “loss of confidence in his ability to lead” after being captured on video rummaging through a subordinate Marine’s home.

Bull was reportedly conducting a wellness check on his subordinate – Juan Gamez-Alzate – after he had not shown up for duty. However, Gamez-Alzate’s absence was due to an arrest over a traffic violation, which he claimed he had informed his sergeant major of.

“The Marine Corps aircraft group was notified that I was under custody, so why is [Bull] doing a welfare check when he knows that I’m in custody,” Gamez-Alzate said, the military digital media outlet Task & Purpose reported.

Hegseth having shared the video without context, however, led to an onslaught of ridicule from critics of all stripes.

“Too much alcohol has rotted your brain,” wrote Setareh Sadeqi, an Iranian professor and political commentator, in a social media post on X.

“You’re a drunkard buffoon,”wroteauthor and activist Amy Siskind to their more than 446,000 followers on X

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Crusade: Unpacking Hegseth’s Blunder

The Illusion of Support

In the whirlwind of social media, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s recent attempt to drum up support for the ongoing war with Iran through a recycled and contextually barren video has sparked not just controversy, but a critical examination of leadership and the manipulation of narratives in times of conflict. The incident, involving a video featuring Christian Bull, a former U.S. Marine relieved of duty under controversial circumstances, underscores a significant lapse in judgment and a misunderstanding of the responsibility held by those in power.

Identifying the Power Players

  1. Who Holds Institutional Power?

  2. In this scenario, the institutional power rests squarely with Pete Hegseth, the Defense Secretary, given his position within the government and influence over military and public perception regarding the war with Iran.

  3. Decision Maker Behind the Outcome

  4. The decision to share the video, devoid of context, was made by Hegseth himself. This action, intended or not, attempted to leverage his authoritative position to shape public opinion on military readiness and support for the war.

  5. Framing and Misdirection of Responsibility

  6. The article’s framing suggests a misdirection of responsibility toward Christian Bull, the servicemember in the video, despite his lack of current relevance or authority in the matter. The real focus should be on Hegseth’s choice to share an outdated and misleading representation of military readiness.

Dissecting the Narrative

The crux of the issue lies not with the serviceman long retired and out of the picture but with Hegseth’s decision to utilize a piece of history stripped of context as a modern call to arms. This action not only misleads the public but also irresponsibly wields the power of his position to sway opinion based on a false premise.

Critics like Micah Erfan and responses from figures such as Setareh Sadeqi and Amy Siskind highlight the broader implications of Hegseth’s attempt: the erosion of trust and credibility. The backlash, characterized by scathing rebukes, underscores the importance of responsible communication by those in power, especially in matters of war and peace.

Hegseth’s lapse in judgment reveals a deeper issue within the current administration’s approach to garnering support for military action. It reflects a willingness to manipulate narratives, irrespective of truth, to achieve desired ends. The fallout from this incident serves as a cautionary tale about the consequences of undermining public trust through deceptive or careless messaging.

Conclusion: The Path Forward

In a time of global instability and conflict, the need for transparent, honest, and responsible communication from our leaders cannot be overstated. The responsibility lies with figures like Hegseth not to mislead the public or exploit their positions for manipulative purposes. It is imperative to maintain a high standard of integrity and accountability, particularly when the stakes involve the potential for loss of life and global consequences.

As we move forward, let this incident serve as a reminder of the importance of scrutinizing the actions and motivations of those in power. Misplaced criticism and false equivalences do not contribute to constructive discourse. Instead, focusing on the actual decision-makers and their actions will ensure that accountability is not just a concept, but a practice.
