'This isn't what we paid for': Analyst says Trump facing more MAGA fury over dinner move

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20255 min read

Saudi Crown Prince and Prime Minister Mohammed bin Salman laughs as U.S. President Donald Trump speaks while shaking hands during a meeting in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 18, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

President Donald Trump surrendering American security for the sake of cozying up to Saudi Arabia should have even MAGA questioning their life choices, Axios CEO Jim Vandehei told MSNBC’s Joe Scarborough on Wednesday’s “Morning Joe.”

“If you’re in MAGA and you’re America First, what the hell are you thinking today?” said Vandehei. “You’re watching the fact that you have the White House and Congress. The only thing that they’ve done in the last two months is vote to release the Epstein files over the objection of the president, who doesn’t didn’t want any of the files to be released in the first place. Nothing else. Then you put next to that the fact that you have a quasi-state dinner with the Saudis.”

“Now, remember, like, it was the Saudis who were on the airplane that crashed into our Towers, killing 3000 Americans. That’s just a fact,” said Vandehei. “And it’s also a fact that the journalist that he was deriding when he was talking to the press yesterday was sawed into pieces. And the CIA, under Trump, had a report that linked it pretty directly to at least the crown prince being aware of it. And you have him talking to the Saudis about getting deals from us, where we’re going to let them have our F-35s on their soil. We’re going to give them access to our AI technology” — even as the Saudis say they could pass that tech along to China.”

It’s understandable there’s a burgeoning revolt among MAGA activists, Vandehei continued, because “this isn’t what we paid for, this isn’t what we voted for. This isn’t what we asked for in your competence.”

“If you’re a republican, you got to boil at some point because you’ve got Nirvana. You’ve got as much power as I’ve ever seen, vested in one party at one time in one city,” said Vandehei. “And yet, if you look at the number of laws actually being signed, the things that will outlast the short term deals, very few of them. And then you look at the polling and you look at that working class white voter who put you into office and they’re frustrated ... every issue that they care about, including things being really expensive and them being very worried about whether they’re going to get a job or their kid’s going to get out of college and enter the job market that we’ve seen in 5 or 10 years, not even really discussed.”

“And it does come down to competence,” he added. “If you have the right people doing the right work at the right time, you get really good results. That’s like the trick of leading anything, running a company, running a government.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Questionable Allegiance: Cozying Up to Saudi Arabia

In a striking analysis delivered by Axios CEO Jim Vandehei on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” the stark contradictions of the Trump administration’s foreign policy, particularly its unsettling coziness with Saudi Arabia, were laid bare. Vandehei’s commentary, slicing through the facade of political maneuvering, raises alarm bells for even the staunchest MAGA supporters, prompting a reevaluation of their unwavering loyalty.

“What the hell are you thinking today?” This question, posed by Vandehei, encapsulates the bewildering reality MAGA adherents must now confront. The Trump administration, under the guise of American security, has seemingly surrendered its principles, engaging in controversial dealings with Saudi Arabia—a country with a dubious record on human rights and a history intertwined with the darkest day in recent American memory.

Surrendering American Security

The juxtaposition Vandehei presents is jarring: on one side, the administration’s inaction, highlighted by its singular focus on releasing the Epstein files—a move opposed by Trump himself. On the other, a quasi-state dinner with the Saudis, a nation linked to the harrowing attack on September 11, 2001, which claimed the lives of nearly 3,000 Americans. This stark contrast underscores a disturbing prioritization of interests that seem to align more closely with foreign powers than with American security or values.

The Uncomfortable Truths

Vandehei’s analysis does not shy away from the uncomfortable truths that have emerged from the Trump administration’s dealings with Saudi Arabia. The brutal murder of journalist Jamal Khashoggi, and the CIA’s subsequent report linking the act to the Saudi crown prince, paints a grim picture of the moral compromises being made. The decision to provide Saudi Arabia with F-35 fighter jets and access to advanced AI technology—despite warnings that this tech could end up in Chinese hands—further illustrates a baffling disregard for the broader implications on global security and American interests.

MAGA’s Burgeoning Revolt

What Vandehei describes as a “burgeoning revolt” among MAGA activists signals a critical juncture. This isn’t the vision of America that was sold to them. The promise of “America First” seems hollow when juxtaposed with the administration’s actions, which appear to place Saudi interests—or perhaps more accurately, the interests of those in power—above those of the American people.

Competence in Question

The crux of the issue, as Vandehei aptly points out, boils down to competence. The ability to lead effectively, to prioritize the nation’s security, prosperity, and values, seems to be in short supply. The Trump administration, vested with significant power, has shown a perplexing inability to enact meaningful legislation or address the concerns of its base. The working-class white voter, once seen as the backbone of Trump’s support, is left grappling with economic insecurities and a future that seems increasingly uncertain.

Conclusion

Jim Vandehei’s critique on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” serves as a piercing wake-up call. The Trump administration’s coziness with Saudi Arabia is not just a foreign policy blunder; it’s a betrayal of the very principles and promises that propelled them to power. For MAGA supporters and the broader Republican base, the time has come to question not just their loyalty but the direction in which their leaders are taking them. Is this the America they envisioned, or have they been sold a bill of goods? The answer, while uncomfortable, is becoming increasingly clear.
