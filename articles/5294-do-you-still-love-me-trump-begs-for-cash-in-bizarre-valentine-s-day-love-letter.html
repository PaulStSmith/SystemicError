President Donald Trump got in on the Valentine’s Day festivities Saturday by sending a “love letter” to his supporters, one that saw the president beg for both their love and financial contributions to his political action committee, Never Surrender, Inc.

“I sent you a LOVE LETTER but I haven’t heard back,” reads a Saturday email from Trump’s team, part of a fundraising machine that has raked in hundreds of millions of dollars over the years.

“It’s Valentine’s Day! I love you, and I was pretty sure you loved me back! Is everything okay? Roses are red, violets are blue. Do you still love Trump, as I love you? Before you read my letter – do you still love me and our great movement?”

The fundraising email is one of thousands sent by Trump over the years to his supporters, many of which include odd or threatening language, such as a recent fundraising email in which donors were warned that they may be tracked down by Immigrations and Customs Enforcement unless they respond to a survey, a survey that directed supporters to a request for contributions.

Other Trump fundraising emails have been described as “creepy,” such as an email from last month in which Trump was described as sitting “alone and in the dark,” along with the usual request for contributions to Trump’s political action committee.

Historically, Trump’s fundraising email campaign has also misled supporters, sometimes suggesting contributions would be used for one purpose, only to be used for another.

“Small donors who give to Trump thinking they are financing an ‘official election defense fund’ are in fact helping pay down the Trump campaign’s debt or funding his post-presidential political operation,” said Brendan Fischer with the Campaign Legal Center, speaking with the Washington Post shortly after Trump’s loss in the 2020 election.

“The average donor who gives in response to Trump’s appeal for funds to ‘stop the fraud’ likely doesn’t realize that their money is actually retiring Trump’s debt or funding his leadership PAC.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Valentine’s Day “Love Letter”: A Masterclass in Manipulation

In an unsurprising twist that blurs the lines between affection and avarice, President Donald Trump has decided to commemorate Valentine’s Day not with chocolates or flowers, but with a love letter to his supporters. However, this is no ordinary declaration of love; it’s a blatant solicitation for financial contributions to his political action committee, Never Surrender, Inc. The audacity is as blatant as it is baffling, but then again, this is Trump’s modus operandi.

“I sent you a LOVE LETTER but I haven’t heard back,” reads the email from Trump’s team, a sentence that’s as manipulative as it is melodramatic. It’s hard not to marvel at the sheer gall of equating political donations with romantic affection, but here we are. Trump’s message utilizes a blend of guilt-tripping and flattery, a tactic that’s as transparent as it is desperate.

Roses are red, violets are blue, but the only color that truly matters in this scenario is green—the color of money. The email goes on to question the loyalty and love of its recipients, a psychological maneuver designed to prey on the emotions of supporters. It’s manipulation 101, served with a side of poetic cheese.

A History of Dubious Fundraising Tactics

This Valentine’s Day stunt is just the latest in a long line of questionable fundraising efforts by Trump. His emails have ranged from the bizarre to the downright threatening. One particularly egregious example includes a warning that donors could be tracked down by Immigrations and Customs Enforcement if they failed to respond to a survey—a survey that conveniently led to a request for contributions.

Then there’s the description of Trump sitting “alone and in the dark,” an image that’s as creepy as it is laughable, designed to evoke sympathy and, ultimately, open wallets.

Where Does the Money Really Go?

The most alarming aspect of these fundraising endeavors is the deceitful way in which they are presented. Brendan Fischer of the Campaign Legal Center highlights a particularly insidious tactic: misleading donors about the intended use of their contributions. Supporters are led to believe their money is going towards fighting election fraud or some noble cause, when in reality, it’s being funneled into paying down Trump’s campaign debt or bolstering his post-presidential political operations.

It’s a bait-and-switch of the highest order, exploiting the trust and loyalty of his base for financial gain. The so-called “official election defense fund” is, in many cases, nothing more than a slush fund for Trump’s continued political aspirations.

Conclusion: The True Color of Love?

In Trump’s universe, love is measured not by deeds or words, but by dollars and cents. His Valentine’s Day “love letter” is less a romantic gesture and more a cynical attempt to capitalize on the affections of his supporters. It’s a stark reminder of the transactional nature of his relationship with his base, where emotional manipulation is just another tool in the arsenal.

As we sift through the confetti of Trump’s latest fundraising ploy, let’s not lose sight of the real message here: in the world of politics, sometimes the most dangerous love stories are the ones that come with a price tag.
