Former GOP congressman lets loose on Trump 'cultists' in Congress

President Donald Trump’s supporters are “cultists,” according to a former Republican congressman who once voted for Trump.

“I do my damnedest not to call Trump voters cultists, because I used to be a Trump voter,” said Rep. Joe Walsh (R-Ill.) on his Substack podcast on Tuesday. “I don’t always hold to that rule, but I try my best. But when it comes to my former congressional colleagues — these Republican members of Congress, and all these conservative right-wing talk radio guys and Fox News guys, the world I used to be a part of — man, I’ll call them members of a cult till the cows come home.”

Walsh, who prefaced his statement by acknowledging that he gets “a bunch of anger” whenever he makes it, challenged Trump supporters in Congress by saying “if you don’t like me calling you a cultist, if you don’t like it when I call you a cult follower of Donald Trump, then don’t act like one.” As one example, the former legislator urged his erstwhile colleagues to not back the president in attacking the Supreme Court after they ruled his tariffs unconstitutional.

“When you stand up — like Trump did — and say, ‘S---- the Supreme Court, s---- the Constitution on these tariffs,’ and you stand with him, it’s just so easy for me to point the ‘you are a cult member’ finger at you,” Walsh said. He added that unlike the previous three Republican presidents (Ronald Reagan, George H. W. Bush and George W. Bush), Trump ruins conservatives who do not agree with him on every issue.

“You want to stay a Republican?” Walsh said. “You want to be a Republican in good standing? You want to get elected as a Republican? You want to win that Republican primary? You want to keep your conservative talk radio show? You want to keep going on Fox News two or three times a week? Well, then you’ve got to get down on your knees and bow in undevoted adoration to him. You can never criticize him. You can never go crossways with him. You can never oppose him, no matter what.”

This is not the first time Walsh has said pro-Trump Republicans are in a cult. Last week, he said the same thing on his Substack.

“This is what America looks like when one of our two major political parties has become an authoritarian-embracing cult,” Walsh wrote at the time. “And I’ll throw in for good measure — hey, MAGA, MAGA — this is what America looks like when the people who voted for Donald Trump don’t get what Donald Trump said he would give them, but they still praise him to the high heavens.”

Earlier this month, when denouncing Trump’s immigration policies, Walsh described ICE and CBP agents with the epithet “thugs.”

“What these heavily armed masked thugs lawlessly roaming our streets are doing is the utter opposite of law enforcement,” Walsh explained. “And there’s a reason, by the way — I talk to cops regularly — most cops in this country detest what ICE is doing.”

The ex-congressman has also exploded about Trump’s destruction of irreplaceable structures like the White House East Wing.

“This jack—— in the f—— White House is destroying a permanent structure that can’t come back,” Walsh said. “He can’t do this on his own! There’s a rigorous, rigorous process to mess with the restructuring, structural changes of the White House, and he just blows right through all of that!”

Even during his Tuesday monologue, Walsh brought up a wide range of anti-Trump criticisms, singling out the fact that the Justice Department inexplicably omitted 53 pages from the Jeffrey Epstein files pertaining to Trump.

He added, “Also missing are pages from documents where accusations against Epstein mentioned Trump directly.”

Our Analysis:

The Cult of Personality: Dissecting Joe Walsh’s Blistering Critique

In a world where political discourse often mirrors the fervor of religious evangelism, Joe Walsh’s tirade against the Trumpian sect within the GOP is not just a breath of fresh air; it’s a gale-force wind aimed at the sails of the Republican Party’s increasingly unmoored ship. Walsh, a former Republican congressman and one-time Trump voter, has taken to his Substack podcast to lay bare the cult-like allegiance he sees corrupting his former colleagues and the conservative media landscape. His words are not just an indictment of Trump’s most fervent supporters but a rallying cry for reason over blind loyalty.

The Cult Label: A Fitting Description?

Walsh’s assertion that Trump’s supporters, especially those in Congress and the conservative media, are akin to cultists is a bold claim. Yet, it’s one that’s increasingly difficult to dismiss out of hand. When loyalty to an individual trumps (pun intended) allegiance to constitutional principles and democratic norms, what else do you call it but cultism? The phenomenon Walsh describes is not unique to the Trump era, but the scale and intensity of the devotion Trump commands are unprecedented in modern American politics.

The Litmus Test of Loyalty

Walsh outlines a stark reality for Republicans: to survive in Trump’s GOP, one must “get down on your knees and bow in undevoted adoration”. This demand for absolute fealty is not just undemocratic; it’s antithetical to the very principles upon which the Republican Party and American democracy were founded. The examples Walsh provides, from attacking the Supreme Court to demanding unwavering support for Trump’s policies, illustrate a party transformed from a political organization into a personal loyalty cult.

The Consequence of Dissent

What happens to those who refuse to drink the Kool-Aid? Walsh himself is a prime example. Once a member of the GOP and a Trump voter, his outspoken criticism has made him a pariah within the party he once served. His experiences reflect a broader trend: dissent within Trump’s GOP is not just discouraged; it’s career-ending. This is a chilling development in American politics, where healthy debate and dissent are essential components of the democratic process.

The Irony of the MAGA Movement

Walsh’s critique extends beyond the halls of Congress to the very base of the Trump movement. He highlights the irony of MAGA supporters who continue to praise Trump “to the high heavens,” despite not receiving what Trump promised them. This, perhaps, is the most defining characteristic of a cult: unwavering devotion in the face of unfulfilled promises and evident failures.

An Authoritarian Embrace

Walsh’s commentary on ICE and CBP agents as “heavily armed masked thugs” and his condemnation of Trump’s disregard for legal processes in altering White House structures further paint a picture of an administration that operates with authoritarian impulses. The reference to the missing pages from the Jeffrey Epstein files pertaining to Trump adds another layer of concern about transparency and accountability.

Conclusion: A Call to Reason

Joe Walsh’s transformation from a Trump voter to one of his most vocal critics is a journey that encapsulates the broader struggle within the Republican Party and American politics. His critique is not just a condemnation of Trump and his most ardent supporters; it’s a warning about the dangers of cultism in politics. The fervor for Trump has morphed significant segments of the GOP into a personality cult, where loyalty to the leader supersedes policy, principle, and even the law.

Walsh’s call to reject this cult of personality and embrace reasoned debate and principled leadership is more than just a critique; it’s a plea for the soul of the Republican Party and the country. Whether this call will be heeded remains to be seen, but ignoring the signs of authoritarianism and cultism he highlights is a risk we cannot afford. The GOP, and indeed American democracy, stands at a crossroads. The path it chooses will shape not just the next election cycle, but the very fabric of the nation.
