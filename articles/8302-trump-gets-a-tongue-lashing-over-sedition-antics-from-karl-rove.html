Trump gets a tongue-lashing over 'sedition' antics from Karl Rove

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 28, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump got a tongue-lashing from the conservative Karl Rove on Wednesday, over his constant stream of social media provocations that have now escalated to death threats against members of Congress.

“We’re witnessing the rise of a new class of political performance artists,” wrote Rove. “They want to be at the center of national attention, creating chaos and conflict, shocking sensibilities, and unleashing dark passions. The hard work of legislation, of addressing Americans’ real needs, is of little interest. Instead, they compete to see who can say the most outrageous and inflammatory things. Their goal is more posts, more views, and more reactions — positive or negative, it doesn’t matter.”

This has happened in a number of spheres of the right, Rove wrote, including Nick Fuentes, the extremist “Groyper” founder who has praised Adolf Hitler and denied the Holocaust; and the now-resigning Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who suggested on social media “that satellites, possibly controlled by the Rothschilds, started California forest fires.”

But it is Trump himself, Rove wrote, who remains the master of social media fire-starting.

“Given Donald Trump’s online domination, even generally sensible Democrats and Republicans have stopped being serious and started seeking more retweets and followers. Take the six Congressional Democrats, all veterans of the military or the CIA, who released a video last week encouraging active-duty service personnel to ‘refuse illegal orders’ from Mr. Trump,” wrote Rove.

While it was provocative to do so without actually naming an illegal order from Trump, Rove argued, Trump took it up to 11 by calling the video “sedition” and “punishable by death.”

“The lack of trust Americans have in our institutions may be in part a reaction to how blaring our political discourse is and how preoccupied it is with pointless matters,” Rove concluded. “It’s likely to get louder and the topics more idiotic until someone shows a better way. That’s probably years off. Change will require a new wave of leaders with different values and sensibilities. But the sooner someone starts, the sooner we’ll be free of this. And voters will be grateful.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Distraction: A Critical Analysis

In a recent tirade that seems to be ripped straight from the pages of a satirical dystopian novel, Karl Rove, a figure synonymous with conservative politics, launched a scathing critique against President Donald Trump and a cohort of right-wing personalities and politicians. Their crime? Turning political discourse into a circus of “social media provocations” that range from the bizarre to the downright dangerous. But let’s not be fooled; this is not a mere squabble within conservative ranks. It’s a glaring symptom of a deeper malaise affecting the body politic.

The Masters of Chaos

Rove’s condemnation targets the rise of “political performance artists” whose primary aim is to shock, provoke, and divide. These are not your garden-variety politicians. They are, according to Rove, the new nihilists of the political sphere, for whom the act of governance takes a back seat to the pursuit of viral infamy. Names like Nick Fuentes and Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene are trotted out as prime examples of this trend. Fuentes, with his abhorrent praise of Hitler and Holocaust denial, and Greene, with her outlandish conspiracy theories about space lasers, are not anomalies. They are, frighteningly, becoming the norm.

But the pièce de résistance in Rove’s analysis is Donald Trump himself, described as the “master of social media fire-starting.” Trump’s penchant for stirring controversy and his unyielding grip on the digital conversation are presented as the catalysts for a broader decline in political seriousness and decorum, ensnaring even “generally sensible Democrats and Republicans.”

The Seduction of the Absurd

Rove highlights a particularly alarming incident where Congressional Democrats, veterans of the military and CIA, released a video urging active-duty service personnel to “refuse illegal orders” from Trump. Trump’s hyperbolic response, branding the video as “sedition” and punishable by death, exemplifies the extremities to which political rhetoric has plummeted.

It’s crucial to underline Rove’s point about the motivation behind these antics: attention. In a world dominated by clicks, likes, and shares, the incentive to be reasonable, thoughtful, or even factual diminishes by the tweet. This incessant clamor for social media validation is not just a game; it’s dangerously reshaping how political figures communicate, prioritize, and operate.

The Bigger Picture

Rove’s lamentation about the “lack of trust Americans have in our institutions” being a byproduct of this cacophonous political environment is a stark reminder of the stakes involved. The erosion of faith in governance, the judiciary, and other critical institutions can’t solely be laid at the feet of digital age dynamics. It’s also a consequence of how those in power choose to wield or, more aptly, waste their influence.

A Call to Arms or a Cry in the Wilderness?

The article concludes on a somewhat hopeful note, positing that a new wave of leaders with “different values and sensibilities” could eventually turn the tide. However, given the entrenched nature of the current political landscape, one has to wonder if Rove’s call for change is less a clarion call and more a wistful longing for a bygone era of political engagement.

Final Thoughts

In dissecting Rove’s critique, we must acknowledge the irony. A figure who once masterminded the very tactics of division and distraction now bemoans their escalation to absurdity. Yet, the core message should not be lost amidst this irony. The transformation of political discourse into a spectacle of the grotesque has real-world consequences. It diverts attention from pressing issues, erodes public trust, and makes a mockery of the democratic process.

As readers and citizens, we are left with a daunting question: How do we recalibrate our political compass in an age where the outrageous has become the norm? The path forward is uncertain, but one thing is clear—the need for a renaissance of sincerity, substance, and sanity in politics has never been more urgent.
