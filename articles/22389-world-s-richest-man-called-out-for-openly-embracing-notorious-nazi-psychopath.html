World's richest man called out for openly embracing notorious ‘Nazi psychopath’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Post Was the PointPower Is the StoryThe Frame Is Too PoliteReactionary Elite LogicWhat This Reveals

Musk Didn’t Accidentally Signal Fascism

Elon Musk used X to call for the release of Derek Chauvin, the police officer serving a 22-year sentence for murdering George Floyd, and amplified a similar demand from Nick Land, a far-right philosopher tied to “Dark Enlightenment” politics and “hyper-racism.” The reporting treats this as a controversy around an eccentric billionaire and an edgy intellectual. That is too soft. What is actually on display is elite normalization of anti-democratic reaction.

The Post Was the Point

Musk is not a random user throwing provocation into the void. He owns the platform, shapes the attention economy around it, and speaks from a position of structural power that ordinary users do not have. When a man like that asks for Chauvin’s freedom, the message is not about a single case. It is about which forms of violence he considers legible, defensible, or politically useful.

That matters because Chauvin’s crime is not ambiguous. The source text says Floyd was killed after Chauvin knelt on his neck for more than nine minutes. A billionaire using his own megaphone to advocate leniency for that act is not “controversial” in some abstract sense. It is a political choice that tells police violence and white grievance they still have patrons at the top.

Power Is the Story

The decisive actor here is Musk, not Land, and not the online commenters reacting to him. Land may supply the ideological varnish. Musk supplies reach, legitimacy, and institutional weight. That distinction is the whole story. Reactionary ideas do not become dangerous because a fringe thinker mutters them. They become dangerous when a billionaire with a global platform packages them as something worth repeating.

This is how anti-democratic politics moves: not always through formal party structures, but through wealthy intermediaries who can launder extremist language into respectable conversation. The source notes that Land has been linked by U.S. researchers to white nationalist terrorism and that critics describe him as openly anti-democracy. Those are not irrelevant color details. They are the point of contact between theory and consequence.

The Frame Is Too Polite

The article’s language still treats this as a bizarre alignment, a celebrity oddity, a philosophical cringe event. That framing misdirects blame toward the theater and away from the machinery. The issue is not whether Musk is “increasingly open” about his interest in Land. The issue is that he is open about it because he can be. Wealth buys insulation. Platform ownership buys amplification. Both reduce the cost of flirting with authoritarian ideas.

Calling Land a “controversial far-right figure” also sandpapers the edges off what the source itself describes. “Hyper-racism,” “superior race,” anti-democracy, white nationalist terrorism: these are not intellectual quirks. They are a political program for separating elites from the rest of humanity and justifying domination as destiny.

Reactionary Elite Logic

There is a pattern here that reaches beyond one post. Parts of the tech-rich right increasingly behave as though democracy is a constraint on competence, not a condition of legitimacy. That is the logic behind “Dark Enlightenment” aesthetics and accelerationist fantasies: abandon equality, treat hierarchy as natural, and recast cruelty as insight.

That worldview is useful to billionaires because it turns accountability into mockery. If society objects to police murder, the answer is not reform or repair in this frame. The answer is to delegitimize the moral basis of the objection itself. Free Chauvin. Sneer at the dead. Elevate the philosophers who can dress the whole thing in pseudo-theory.

What This Reveals

The larger political pattern is simple: wealthy men with massive platforms are helping normalize anti-democratic sentiment by treating racist violence as a matter of opinion and extremist ideology as intellectual fashion. That is not confusion. It is coalition work for reaction.

The danger is not that a billionaire had a bad thought online. The danger is that he has the power to make bad thoughts feel ambient, fashionable, and less shameful than they should be. That is how elite corruption operates in the open: by making brutality look like commentary and fascist longing look like thought leadership.
