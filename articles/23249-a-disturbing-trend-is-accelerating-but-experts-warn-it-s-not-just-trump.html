A disturbing trend is 'accelerating' — but experts warn it's not just Trump

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

The Real Divide Is Not Family Conflict. It Is Political Machinery.

The Source Story, Briefly

The piece describes Americans losing relationships over politics, citing a UC Irvine survey and several groups trying to help people cope with fractured families and conspiratorial politics. It leans on the language of polarization, suggests Trump is a factor but not the only cause, and centers “civil discourse” as the available remedy.

Who Has Power Here

This is the first distortion: the story talks as if political fracture is an atmosphere everyone breathes equally, when the damage is being produced by institutions with unequal power. Families do not manufacture nationwide polarization. Media systems, partisan operators, donor networks, platform algorithms, and elected Republicans who keep escalating grievance do.

The article gestures at “us versus them” tribalism as though it floated free of power. It does not. Tribalism is useful to people who need voters frightened, distracted, and emotionally isolated from any collective political project that might challenge their interests.

Misdiagnosis by Civility

Braver Angels and similar groups are doing social repair work, but the story’s framing turns repair into diagnosis. That is a mistake. “Civil discourse” is not the root problem. It is what people invoke when they want to manage the fallout without confronting the system that keeps manufacturing the fallout.

The problem is not that Americans forgot how to listen. It is that a profitable political-media ecosystem has trained people to treat opponents as enemies and conspiracy as identity. Calling that “polarization” makes it sound symmetrical and spontaneous. It is neither.

Trump Is a Catalyst, Not the Whole Structure

The article correctly notes that Trump is not the only factor. But that caution can also become cover. Trump’s role is not mystical, and it is not just personal style. He is an accelerant inside a wider authoritarian ecosystem that rewards humiliation, scapegoating, and total loyalty. He did not invent the conditions, but he exploited them ruthlessly.

That distinction matters. When reporters flatten the story into “everyone is divided,” they blur the difference between a politician using division as a weapon and ordinary people reacting to the weaponization. The damage to families is real. So is the political engineering behind it.

Grief Is Not the Same as Politics

The most revealing passage is the one from the paralegal who feels she may stop speaking to her MAGA mother. That is not just “disagreement.” It is social rupture produced by a political identity that has become morally sealed off from accountability. When people say they are ashamed of what relatives believe, they are not describing a stylistic disagreement over taxes. They are describing the collapse of a shared reality.

The article treats this mainly as interpersonal tragedy. That is too small. Once political belief becomes a container for cruelty, conspiracy, and dehumanization, family pain is not the story’s center. It is the consequence.

The Pattern Beneath the Panic

This is the larger pattern: institutions that profit from division constantly convert political violence into a problem of tone. That keeps attention away from the actors with actual leverage. It invites people to manage symptoms while leaving the machine intact.

That is why “be nicer” stories are so often politically convenient. They soothe the public while absolving the structures that turned propaganda into a daily habit. If the nation is being torn apart, the responsible question is not why ordinary people lack civility. It is who benefits from keeping them isolated, suspicious, and easier to rule.
