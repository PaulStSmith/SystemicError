'I'd bust your face!' GOP lawmaker snaps at reporter in heated exchange

Republican Tennessee state Sen. Paul Rose confronts progressive activist in Nashville, Tennessee, March 24, 2026. (Screenshot / Justin Kanew)

Republican Tennessee state Sen. Paul Rose snapped at an activist journalist on Tuesday after being pressed about a controversial bill he sponsored that would support Israel’s right to annex the West Bank, territory widely recognized as Palestinian land that has been illegally occupied by Israel since 1967.

The bill, known as the “Recognizing Judea and Samaria Act,” would mandate all Tennessee state agencies cease referring to the territory as the “West Bank,” and instead, refer to the territory as “Judea and Samaria,” a term used by some Israeli officials to assert its control over the territory. The bill would also recognize “Israel’s sovereignty in these areas” as being “consistent with international law.”

Rose was asked about the bill by former Democratic congressional candidate-turned progressive activist journalist Justin Kanew, who asked the GOP lawmaker “who gave” him “the West Bank bill?”

“You’re doing Israel’s bidding as they push us into war,” Kanew could be heard saying in a video of the encounter he shared on social media. “I say this as a Jew with family in Israel, Sen. Rose. Why are you doing Israel’s bidding?”

Rose then turned to Kanew to issue him a violent threat.

“You know, if I was at home, I’d bust your face right now!” Rose said.

Rose later said he would not actually assault Kanew, adding that his comment was conditional on “if we were home.” Kanew, the founder of the progressive news outlet The Tennessee Holler, later ridiculed Rose for getting “triggered” and having “fantasized about punching” him

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Scandal

In the swirling controversy surrounding Tennessee state Sen. Paul Rose’s heated exchange with activist journalist Justin Kanew, the root causes and implications of the proposed bill to recognize Israel’s right to annex the West Bank, referred to as Judea and Samaria in the bill, demand a thorough examination. This incident is not merely about a personal altercation but encapsulates the intertwining of local governance with international political stances, the influence of external entities on state legislation, and the responsibilities of elected officials.

Who Holds Institutional Power?

The institutional power in this situation resides with the Tennessee state legislature, specifically with Sen. Paul Rose as the sponsor of the “Recognizing Judea and Samaria Act.” The ability to propose, debate, and enact legislation that could redefine the state’s official stance on international territorial disputes places significant power in the hands of state legislators. Furthermore, the support or opposition of the Tennessee state legislature as a collective body can either empower or limit the impact of individual members like Sen. Rose.

Decision-Makers and Outcomes

Sen. Paul Rose, as the sponsor of the bill, is the primary decision-maker in this context. The outcome, should the bill pass, would be a formal shift in how Tennessee state agencies refer to and recognize the territory commonly known as the West Bank. This move would symbolically align Tennessee’s stance with that of certain Israeli officials and policies, marking a significant departure from the widely accepted international perspective that considers the territories as occupied Palestinian land.

Misdirection of Responsibility?

The article’s focus on the altercation between Rose and Kanew, while sensational, risks overshadowing the deeper political and ethical implications of the bill itself. The framing initially draws attention to the personal conflict and Rose’s aggressive response, which, though newsworthy, diverts attention from the motivations behind and the potential consequences of the bill. The responsibility for this legislative push appears to lie squarely with Rose (and potentially with any co-sponsors or supporters within the legislature), yet the article does not delve into whether external influences or lobbying efforts are at play.

Analysis

Misplaced Criticism? The criticism of Sen. Rose for his personal conduct, while valid, should not eclipse critical analysis of the bill’s content and its implications for international relations and local governance. The real issue at hand is the attempt to legislate on matters of international sovereignty and territorial disputes from a state level, which raises questions about the appropriateness and jurisdiction of such actions.

False Equivalence and Scapegoating? By focusing primarily on the heated exchange, there’s a risk of creating a false equivalence between personal misconduct and the broader, more impactful legislative actions being undertaken. Furthermore, while Rose’s reaction is indefensible, it should not serve as a scapegoat that diverts scrutiny from the legislative process and the content of the bill itself.

Conclusion

The controversy surrounding Sen. Paul Rose and the “Recognizing Judea and Samaria Act” serves as a stark reminder of the complex interplay between local politics and international affairs. It underscores the need for a nuanced analysis that distinguishes between personal conduct and institutional actions. While Sen. Rose’s behavior towards Justin Kanew warrants criticism, the broader implications of his proposed bill demand a more focused and critical examination. The real story here is not about a moment of personal vitriol but about the significant, tangible effects legislative actions can have on international perceptions, relations, and potentially, peace.
