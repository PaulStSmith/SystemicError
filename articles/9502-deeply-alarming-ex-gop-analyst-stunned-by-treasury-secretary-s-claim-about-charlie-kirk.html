'Deeply alarming': Ex-GOP analyst stunned by Treasury Secretary's claim about Charlie Kirk

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s claims about the death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk were so alarming that one former GOP spokesperson wondered if Bessent had “gone insane.”

Bessent appeared on Kirk’s podcast, “The Charlie Kirk Show,” on Wednesday, where he described Kirk’s assassination as a “domestic 9/11 moment,” referring to the terrorist attack on the Twin Towers in 2001. Tim Miller, host of “The Bulwark Podcast,” responded to Bessent’s claims on a new episode of “Bulwark Takes” on Wednesday evening.

“Our Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessett, has gone insane,” Miller said. “The dude is high on his power, high on his own supply. I don’t know what’s happening, what is going on in the Barbie house that he has in South Carolina, but Scott Bessant has lost his d--- mind.”

Miller was visibly stunned by Bessent’s argument. He said the person who killed Kirk was “sick” and “disgusting,” but added that Kirk’s murder was nothing like 9/11, where more than 3,000 Americans died following the collapse of the towers.

“The idea that we go and look back to 9/11 and say we need to take that security state spy apparatus and use it to target and root out foes, there’s no comparison,” Miller said.

The Treasury Secretary added that he was looking at ways to use his office to go after left-aligned groups.

“It is deeply alarming what the Treasury Secretary is planning as far as looking at the financing of left-wing groups here in this country,” Miller said.

Our Analysis:

The Hyperbolic Metaphor: A Blatant Misstep

In an era where the line between political hyperbole and alarming rhetoric increasingly blurs, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s recent comments on the tragic death of conservative activist Charlie Kirk cross into the realm of the absurd. Describing Kirk’s assassination as a “domestic 9/11 moment” is not only a gross mischaracterization but a dangerous distortion of historical context and the gravity of the actual events of September 11, 2001.

Bessent’s alarmist rhetoric is a stark example of how political discourse has devolved, where sensationalism trumps sensibility. The comparison to 9/11, a day that remains one of the most tragic events in American history, is not only inappropriate but insensitive to the families of the nearly 3,000 individuals who lost their lives. The assassination of Charlie Kirk, while undoubtedly tragic, does not equate to the scale, impact, or geopolitical consequences of 9/11.

The Response: A Reflection of Disbelief

Tim Miller’s reaction to Bessent’s claims, asserting that the Treasury Secretary has “gone insane,” reflects a broader disbelief and concern over the state of political rhetoric. Miller’s description of Bessent as being “high on his power, high on his own supply” is a scathing indictment of not just the comments in question but of a perceived detachment from reality within certain political echelons.

Miller rightly points out that the murder of Kirk, however reprehensible, cannot and should not be likened to 9/11. The implication that such a comparison could justify an expansion of a “security state spy apparatus” to target political foes is not only misguided but dangerous. It sets a precedent for the misuse of national tragedy to further partisan objectives, undermining the foundational principles of justice and civil liberties.

The Alarming Intentions

Even more concerning than Bessent’s hyperbolic metaphors is his indication of using his office to target left-aligned groups. This explicit politicization of the Treasury Department’s resources to pursue ideological adversaries is a red flag for the health of American democracy. It suggests a willingness to leverage government power against political opposition, a move that could have chilling implications for freedom of speech and association.

Miller’s concern about the Treasury Secretary “looking at the financing of left-wing groups” in the country is a legitimate alarm that should resonate beyond partisan lines. It hints at a potential abuse of power and a departure from the department’s mission towards a politically motivated agenda.

Conclusion: A Call for Rational Discourse

In sum, the comments made by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent regarding Charlie Kirk’s death and the subsequent response from Tim Miller unveil a disturbing trend in political communication. The use of exaggerated comparisons and the weaponization of tragic events for political gain is a dangerous game that threatens to undermine the very fabric of democratic discourse.

It is imperative for public officials to maintain a level of decorum and responsibility in their statements, especially when addressing sensitive topics such as the death of an individual or national tragedy. Sensationalist rhetoric serves only to deepen divisions and distract from the real issues at hand. As observers and participants in the political landscape, it is our responsibility to demand better, to seek truth over sensationalism, and to engage in discussions grounded in fact, not hyperbole.
