The New Republic reported Tuesday that Deutsche Bank managing director Jim Reid, who is the bank's global head of macro research and thematic strategy, is now expressing increasing worry that Trump's policies are gradually wrecking the economy. In a memo obtained by Fortune, Reid remarked that the recent decision by Moody's to downgrade the U.S. credit rating from AAA to AA1 for the first time in history could be a harbinger of tougher economic times in the near future.

“Yesterday felt like we were somewhere along the line of a ‘death by a thousand cuts’ with regards to the U.S. fiscal situation,” Reid wrote. “Hard to know where in that thousand we are but probably much nearer a thousand than at zero even as yesterday saw an initial sell-off reverse as the session went on."

“At the end of the day the loss of the final U.S. triple-A rating late on Friday night doesn’t change anything much immediately but it keeps the drip, drip, drip of poor fiscal news building up against the debt sustainability dam in the background," he added.

Moody's cited the growing U.S. national debt – which is in excess of $36.8 trillion – as a primary reason for the downgrade, and analysts opined that policy proposals currently under consideration by the Republican-controlled Congress would only worsen the United States' long-term debt outlook. The credit downgrade could jeopardize efforts by the U.S. to borrow money in the future, and could increase the cost of debt service obligations.

One major component of the Republican megabill that House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.) is attempting to pass before the Memorial Day holiday is an extension of Trump's 2017 tax cuts — which have been criticized as overwhelmingly tilted in favor of the rich. One estimate pegged a 10-year extension of those tax cuts at roughly $4.6 trillion.

The Republican-controlled House Budget Committee are scheduled to vote on the bill early Wednesday morning at 1 AM. However, its fate in the full House remains unknown, as multiple Republicans have expressed concern about the legislation's impact on the federal deficit.

Our Analysis:

The Great American Economic Illusion: Unraveling Under Trump

In a revelation that cuts through the cacophony of market optimism, Deutsche Bank's Jim Reid, casts a long shadow over the supposedly robust economic facade erected during President Donald Trump's tenure. According to The New Republic, Reid, not one to mince words, has articulated a growing concern that should have the White House and its economic policy architects on high alert. The drip, drip, drip of poor fiscal news, as Reid poetically phrases it, is not just background noise; it's the ominous sound of an approaching storm.

The Downgrade: A Symbol of Decline

Let's talk about the elephant in the room: Moody's historic downgrade of the U.S. credit rating from AAA to AA1. This isn't just a minor blip on the economic radar; it's a glaring red flag signaling that all is not well in the land of the free. The downgrade, driven by the ballooning U.S. national debt—now a staggering $36.8 trillion—is a testament to the short-sightedness of current fiscal policies. Yet, the Trump administration seems to be in a state of blissful ignorance, or perhaps willful denial, as it continues to peddle policies that exacerbate the debt crisis.

Tax Cuts: A Gift to the Wealthy, A Curse to the Economy

The extension of Trump's 2017 tax cuts, as championed by House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.), is a masterclass in fiscal irresponsibility. Criticized for disproportionately benefiting the wealthy, these tax cuts are not the boon to the economy that their proponents make them out to be. Instead, they are a ticking time bomb, with one estimate projecting a 10-year cost of $4.6 trillion. This is not just an economic issue; it's a moral one. At a time when income inequality is at historic highs, policies that further enrich the rich at the expense of the fiscal health of the nation are indefensible.

The Republican Dilemma: Deficit Hawks or Deficit Hypocrites?

The Republican-controlled House Budget Committee's decision to proceed with a vote on the tax cut extension bill, under the cover of darkness at 1 AM, is emblematic of the party's cognitive dissonance. On one hand, they claim to be champions of fiscal responsibility. On the other, they support policies that plunge the nation deeper into debt. The irony is palpable, and the hypocrisy is glaring. It's time for the Republican Party to decide what it stands for: the principles of fiscal conservatism or the expediency of political gains.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

Jim Reid's warning is a wake-up call, and it's time for the Trump administration and its allies in Congress to listen. The economic policies currently being pursued are not just unsustainable; they are dangerous. The downgrade by Moody's is a harbinger of tougher economic times ahead, and unless there is a significant shift in policy direction, the U.S. is on a trajectory towards fiscal ruin.

The facade of economic strength under Trump's leadership is just that—a facade. Behind the veneer of stock market gains and superficial metrics of success lies a shaky foundation, eroded by short-term thinking and fiscal imprudence. It's time for a course correction, lest the drip, drip, drip of poor fiscal news becomes a deluge that the U.S. economy cannot withstand.
