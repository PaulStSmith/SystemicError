'They are breaking the law': Tulsi Gabbard lashes out over Greenland spying report

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard is hitting back at the Wall Street Journal after they published an exclusive report claiming the U.S. has ordered its intelligence agencies to “step up spying on Greenland.”

In a statement to the outlet, Gabbard said, “The Wall Street Journal should be ashamed of aiding deep state actors who seek to undermine the President by politicizing and leaking classified information. They are breaking the law and undermining our nation’s security and democracy.”

The journal quoted multiple sources in their report.

They also spoke with one former intelligence official who said, “Intelligence collection resources are inherently limited,” and these resources are typically directed to pay attention to “perceived threats, not allied countries.”

Since his first term, President Donald Trump has emphasized his determination to purchase, annex, or conquer Greenland.

“We need Greenland for national security and even international security, and we’re working with everybody involved to try and get it,” Trump said in a joint address to Congress in March. “One way or the other, we’re going to get it.”

More recently, in an interview with NBC News, Trump said, “We need Greenland very badly. Greenland is a very small amount of people, which we’ll take care of, and we’ll cherish them, and all of that. But we need that for international security.”

Greenland was essential to the United States during World War II.

After Denmark was occupied by Nazi Germany, Greenland, which was a Danish colony at the time, served as a place for the U.S. to hold military operations and gave access to key shipping routes.

According to the Journal, “The island is home to substantial deposits of rare-earth minerals necessary for the production of everything from electric vehicles to wind turbines, as well as untapped oil and natural-gas reserves.”

Our Analysis:

The Greenland Gambit: A Closer Look at the Spying Scandal and Geopolitical Maneuvering

In the latest chapter of what could be mistaken for a Cold War spy novel, Tulsi Gabbard, the Director of National Intelligence, fires back at the Wall Street Journal for unveiling what seems to be a U.S. directive to intensify espionage operations in Greenland. The accusations and rebuttals flying back and forth are not merely a skirmish in the media landscape but a revelation of deeper, perhaps more insidious political and geopolitical machinations.

The Allegations: The Wall Street Journal has brought to light claims that the U.S. intelligence community has been instructed to increase its surveillance activities in Greenland. Citing multiple sources, including a former intelligence official, the report paints a picture of strategic interests and espionage that seems at odds with the relationship one would expect between allied nations.

The Rebuttal: Gabbard’s response is a classic deflection, wrapped in indignation. By attacking the messenger, the Wall Street Journal, and invoking the specter of "deep state actors," Gabbard aims to shift the focus from the substance of the allegations to the alleged breach of national security through the leaking of classified information. This tactic, while not new, is concerning in its implications for press freedom and its potential to obscure the truth.

The Underlying Geopolitical Chess Game

At the heart of this controversy is President Donald Trump's unabashed interest in Greenland. Describing it as crucial for "national security and even international security," Trump's comments, especially his assertion that "one way or the other, we’re going to get it," are eerily reminiscent of colonial-era declarations of manifest destiny. This blunt instrument approach to international diplomacy, especially concerning a territory that is part of the Kingdom of Denmark, an ally, is as bewildering as it is reckless.

Why Greenland? It's not just about the strategic military advantages that Greenland offered during World War II or its pivotal location in the Arctic. The island is sitting on a treasure trove of rare-earth minerals, untapped oil, and natural-gas reserves, vital for the green technology revolution and energy independence. However, the environmental and ethical costs of extracting these resources are immense and largely glossed over.

The Deep State Narrative and Its Dangers

Gabbard’s use of the term "deep state" is a calculated move to discredit the Wall Street Journal's report without directly addressing its contents. This narrative, which insinuates that a shadowy cabal within the government is working to undermine the president, serves to polarize and distract. It's a dangerous game that threatens to erode trust in public institutions and the media, pillars of American democracy.

Conclusion: Beyond the Smoke and Mirrors

The real issue at hand is not just about espionage or the ethical implications of spying on allies. It's about the broader strategy of U.S. engagement with the Arctic region and the global scramble for resources that are becoming increasingly scarce due to climate change. The focus should be on international cooperation and sustainable development, not a zero-sum game of conquest and acquisition.

The Wall Street Journal's report, regardless of the controversy it has stirred, sheds light on a critical aspect of U.S. foreign policy and its implications for global security. Rather than shooting the messenger, it would be prudent for the U.S. administration to engage in a transparent and constructive dialogue about its intentions and strategies in Greenland and beyond. After all, in an era of mounting global challenges, the world can ill afford another round of imperialist expansion disguised as strategic necessity.
