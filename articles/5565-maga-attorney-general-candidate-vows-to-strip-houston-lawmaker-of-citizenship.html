MAGA attorney general candidate vows to strip Houston lawmaker of citizenship

A MAGA candidate for Texas attorney general vowed Monday to strip citizenship from Houston-area state Rep. Gene Wu based partly on a circulating viral clip from an old interview being deceptively paraphrased by far-right social media accounts.

“As AG, I want to see @GeneforTexas de-naturalized,” wrote Aaron Reitz, who has worked for the Trump Justice Department and under sitting Texas AG Ken Paxton. “On what basis? He likely concealed his anti-American sentiment throughout his citizenship app process—the details of which are conspicuously absent from the public record. Wu is a subversive whose citizenship should be revoked.”

Wu, the Democratic caucus leader in the Texas House of Representatives, was born in Guangzhou, China, and immigrated to the United States with his family as a child. He played a key role in the Democratic walkout that briefly stalled GOP efforts to pass an aggressive mid-decade gerrymander that seeks to grant Republicans up to five extra seats.

The subject of Reitz’s ire was a brief clip of Wu being circulated by the pro-Trump account End Wokeness. The video, taken from a 2024 interview with Pulitzer laureate journalist Jose Antonio Vargas, captions him as saying, “Non-whites share the same oppressor and we are the majority now. We can take over this country.” Pro-Trump accounts, and even some Texas lawmakers, are sharing the clip as proof Wu is anti-white or even wants a “white genocide.”

But as Evan Mintz pointed out in the Houston Chronicle, this is a wildly misleading paraphrase of what Wu actually said.

“I always tell people the day the Latino, African American, Asian and other communities realize that they share the same oppressor is the day we start winning,” said Wu. “Because we are the majority in this country now. We have the ability to take over this country and do what is needed for everyone. And to make things fair. But the problem is our communities are divided — they’re completely divided.”

Pressed for comment, Wu explained the “oppressor” he was talking about in the 2024 clip was not white people, but Republicans: “It is undeniable that Republicans have spent the past 50 years beating down communities. It’s not just minority communities. It’s the poor, it’s religious minorities, it’s women, it’s veterans, it’s the disabled, it’s every community like that that’s oppressed.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of Aaron Reitz’s Call to Strip Citizenship from Rep. Gene Wu

Aaron Reitz, a MAGA candidate with aspirations for the Texas attorney general office, has recently made headlines with a statement that can only be described as a blatant display of political theater built on a foundation of misinformation and xenophobia. In a social media manifesto, Reitz vowed to target Houston-area State Rep. Gene Wu, aiming to strip him of his citizenship based on a grossly misconstrued clip from an old interview. This move is not just an attack on Wu but a dangerous precedent that threatens the very fabric of American democracy and the principles of free speech and fair political play.

The Misleading Battle Cry

Reitz’s argument hinges on a video clip circulated by the right-wing account End Wokeness, purportedly showing Wu harboring “anti-American sentiment” and advocating for a “takeover” by non-white communities. However, this interpretation is a distortion of Wu’s actual words. Wu’s statement, as clarified by the Houston Chronicle, emphasized unity among marginalized communities against a common oppressor, which he later identified as the Republican party’s policies, not a racial or ethnic group.

The Dangerous Implications of Reitz’s Proposal

1. Ignorance or Willful Misinterpretation?

Reitz’s proposal to de-naturalize Wu based on alleged “anti-American sentiment” reveals a concerning tendency to either misinterpret statements to fit a political agenda or a willful ignorance of the context in which those statements were made. This represents a chilling assault on political discourse, where statements can be cherry-picked, stripped of context, and used as ammunition to undermine political opponents.

2. Xenophobia Masquerading as Patriotism

The call to strip Wu of his citizenship, an immigrant who has risen to a position of influence and service in the Texas House of Representatives, reeks of xenophobia. It sends a sinister message to immigrants and naturalized citizens that their status in the U.S. is conditional, subject to revocation the moment they express views that dissent from the political right’s ideology. This is not patriotism; it’s political gatekeeping based on ethnic origin and political belief.

3. Undermining Democratic Principles

The move to target Wu’s citizenship based on his political stance and activism (notably his role in the Democratic walkout) is an affront to democratic principles. It suggests that political retaliation through personal and legal means is acceptable, undermining the very notion of free political expression and the right to dissent, which are cornerstones of American democracy.

Conclusion: A Call for Rational Discourse

Aaron Reitz’s call to de-naturalize Gene Wu is a dangerous ploy that should alarm anyone who values free speech, fair play in politics, and the contributions of immigrants to American society. It is a stark reminder of the lengths to which some politicians will go to silence opposition and control the narrative. Voters and political figures alike must recognize and reject such tactics for the threat they pose to democratic values and the integrity of political discourse.

In a country where political division is already at a breaking point, we need leaders who seek to bridge these divides, not widen them further with baseless accusations and threats against individuals’ citizenship. The case against Gene Wu is not just about one man’s right to belong to the nation he calls home; it’s about defending the principles that make America a beacon of democracy and freedom.
