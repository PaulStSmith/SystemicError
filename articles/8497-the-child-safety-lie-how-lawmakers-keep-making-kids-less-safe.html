The Child-Safety Lie: How Lawmakers Keep Making Kids Less Safe

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 21, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Every few months, another U.S. state unveils a shiny new bill wrapped in the saccharine packaging of “protecting the children.” And every time, without fail, the bow falls away to reveal the same rotten core: expanded surveillance, weakened privacy, and a digital landscape made more dangerous for the very kids lawmakers claim to defend.

It’s a pattern so predictable it borders on ritual. Legislators invoke the talisman of child safety, and suddenly any policy—no matter how technologically illiterate or authoritarian—is treated as noble by default. The press releases glow, the hearings fill with platitudes, and the public is once again told that draconian measures are necessary to shield minors from the terrors of the modern internet.

But when you peel back the rhetoric, the machinery underneath consistently exposes children to greater harm. A recent video on YouTube, analyzing Wisconsin’s proposed VPN crackdown, lays the entire mechanism bare. It’s not just a misfire. It’s a systemic failure.

Age Verification: The First Domino of Disaster

To understand the VPN panic sweeping through state legislatures, you have to start with this year’s resurgence of mandatory age-verification laws. These schemes inevitably require users—including minors—to upload government IDs, selfies, biometric data, or other sensitive personal information to third-party vendors. The sales pitch is simple: Prove your age so we can keep kids safe.

But the reality is a privacy time bomb. Children who barely understand digital hygiene are suddenly instructed to hand over identifying documents to companies they’ve never heard of. No one in power seems bothered that these vendors often lack meaningful oversight, rigorous security standards, or transparency.

The consequences have been catastrophic.

The video discusses a recent breach involving Discord’s age-verification pipeline, where children’s IDs, addresses, and other identifying details were leaked thanks to shoddy storage practices. This isn’t new or rare: other verification providers have left login credentials exposed for months. These aren’t isolated incidents. They’re the predictable outcome of forcing minors into data-harvesting funnels.

The people crafting these laws do not care. They treat children’s personal data as disposable, raw material for their moral theater.

VPNs: The New Enemy of the Week

Of course, once you compel users to upload identifying documents, people are going to reach for the nearest privacy tool to avoid being surveilled in the first place. That’s where VPNs come in.

The video points out what digital rights groups have been yelling for years: VPNs are not exotic “hacker tools.” They’re mainstream safety devices used by journalists, abuse survivors, students, businesses, international travelers, and anyone who doesn’t want their ISP monetizing their traffic like a Peeping Tom with a spreadsheet.

Wisconsin’s legislation—AB-105 and SB-130—claims it will “close loopholes” by banning the use of VPNs to access certain categories of content. This is both technically impossible and politically revealing.

Websites cannot determine whether a VPN user lives in Wisconsin, Wyoming, or Warsaw. The entire point of a VPN is to obscure that information. The only way to enforce such a law would be to require a universal system of identity verification at the website level. In other words: the state wants to graft a digital ID requirement onto the bones of the open internet.

All “to protect children.”

The Endless Parade of Collateral Damage

The lawmakers pushing VPN bans treat high-stakes digital infrastructure like toys. They’re blinded by their own performative zealotry. In their rush to score political points, they fail to see the wreckage they’re creating.

When you ban VPNs in a state, or criminalize their use for accessing arbitrary categories of content, here’s what happens:

  • Journalists lose tools that protect their sources.

  • Abuse survivors lose the ability to evade stalkers.

  • LGBTQ+ teens in hostile homes lose access to life-saving resources.

  • Businesses lose secure remote access to internal networks.

  • Students and researchers lose access to restricted academic portals.

  • Ordinary citizens lose the only line of defense against predatory ISPs.

Meanwhile, children—the supposed beneficiaries—find themselves forced into systems that vacuum up their identities, track their browsing, expose them to new risks, and hand their most sensitive information to companies that barely know how to encrypt a password.

The irony is almost artful.

The Surveillance State Hiding Behind a Lullaby

The Western world has spent decades condemning Russia and China for banning VPNs, restricting encryption, and demanding real-name registration for online activity. Yet the minute domestic lawmakers frame the same authoritarian policies in cooing language about “protecting minors,” the political class swoons.

This is how democracies sleepwalk into digital authoritarianism.

The video spells it out clearly: attacking VPNs is not a child-safety measure. It’s a privacy-erasure measure. It’s an attempt—intentional or not—to criminalize anonymity itself.

Once you establish a government’s right to know who’s accessing what online, you’ve bulldozed the foundation of free expression. And kids grow up in a world where their every click is logged, scrutinized, and archived. They inherit a culture where surveillance is normal, where privacy is suspicious, and where seeking information is treated as a privilege, not a right.

The Real Way to Protect Children

If lawmakers genuinely cared about minors’ well-being, they’d do what actual experts have been recommending for years:

  • Invest in digital literacy education.

  • Fund mental health support.

  • Strengthen parental tools without mandating ID harvesting.

  • Require platforms to minimize data collection rather than expand it.

  • Penalize companies that leak children’s information instead of rewarding them with government contracts.

You’ll notice something about this list: none of it creates mass surveillance systems. None of it requires children to surrender their identities. None of it requires banning basic cybersecurity tools.

But those measures don’t give politicians the moral high ground. They’re harder to explain in press conferences. They’re not easily weaponized in election ads.

So the cycle continues.

Ultimately, the Kids Aren’t the Target—We Are

Every time the “protect the children” refrain echoes through a legislature, adults should hear the subtext: We’re expanding the surveillance apparatus, and you’re next.

Children are the pretext. Adults are the target. Privacy is the casualty.

The video exposes a truth our political establishment refuses to acknowledge: these policies don’t fail accidentally. They fail because they’re built on a lie. The lie that surveillance is safety. The lie that identification is protection. The lie that freedom is dangerous.

Children deserve better. So do the rest of us.

Because the last thing any of us need—children included—is a government more interested in policing screens than protecting people.
