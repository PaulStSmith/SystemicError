Trump calls out 'miserable, horrible people' in Kennedy Honors speech after quoting Rocky

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 9, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump gestures while he poses for a picture at the presidential box at the Kennedy Center, in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 17, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria/File Photo

Donald Trump used part of his Kennedy Center Honors appearance to call people in the audience “miserable” after unfavorably comparing them to Rocky Balboa.

In three appearances across the night, Trump paid tribute to Hollywood star Sylvester Stallone, who he made ambassador to the film industry at the start of this year, as well as the rock band KISS, Gloria Gaynor, Michael Crawford, and George Strait. In one appearance, Trump quoted Stallone’s famous on-screen character, Rocky Balboa, and used the quote as a launchpad for criticising the audience, AP News reported.

The president said, “Some of them have had legendary setbacks, setbacks that you have to read in the papers because of their level of fame. But in the words of Rocky Balboa, they showed us that you keep moving forward, just keep moving forward.”

“I know so many of you are persistent. Many of you are miserable, horrible people. You are persistent. You never give up. Sometimes I wish you’d give up, but you don’t.” Trump would claim he had not prepared very much for the Kennedy Center Honors appearance.

He said, “I have a good memory, so I can remember things, which is very fortunate. But just, I wanted to just be myself. You have to be yourself.” The Kennedy Center has since seen ticket sales for future events slump after the president’s appearance.

According to internal sales data obtained and reviewed by CNN, ticket sales for the Kennedy Center’s performance of “The Nutcracker” topped out at around 10,000 seats across the production’s seven performances, around a 33% drop off from the around 15,000 ticket sales for the production seen in 2001 through 2024.

A person with knowledge of the internal workings of the Kennedy Center told CNN that even if the performance of “The Nutcracker” were to have sold out this year, the costs for the performance would still outweigh the revenue from ticket sales.

“Selling every ticket to ‘The Nutcracker’ is absolutely not paying your bills,” the person told CNN on the condition of anonymity. “We have 19 unions here. The production costs are huge.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Kennedy Center Honors Appearance: A Masterclass in How Not to Behave

In what could only be described as a spectacular display of tactlessness, Donald Trump managed to turn his Kennedy Center Honors appearance into yet another episode of the reality show that seems to perpetually orbit around him. His choice of words? Calling members of the audience “miserable” and “horrible people” after unfavorably comparing them to Rocky Balboa. This, ladies and gentlemen, is the kind of performance that leaves you wondering whether to laugh, cry, or simply stare in disbelief.

Trump’s Tribute to Stallone: An Irony Too Rich to Ignore

Let’s dissect the irony here. Trump paid tribute to Sylvester Stallone, a man best known for portraying the underdog who overcomes all odds with humility and hard work. Yet, in the same breath, Trump managed to insult an entire audience, comparing their setbacks to those of a fictional character. The level of disconnect here is staggering. It’s as if Trump decided to channel the spirit of Rocky but forgot that at the heart of Rocky Balboa’s appeal is his inherent goodness and resilience, not the ability to insult and belittle others.

The Art of Unpreparation

Trump’s claim of not preparing much for his appearance, relying instead on his “good memory,” is a telling admission. It reflects a man who believes his off-the-cuff remarks are as golden as scripted ones. This approach, while perhaps endearing to some, falls catastrophically short when the occasion calls for decorum and respect. Saying, “I wanted to just be myself,” Trump seems to forget that being oneself does not excuse one from the basic expectations of civility, especially in a setting as distinguished as the Kennedy Center Honors.

The Fallout: A Drop in Ticket Sales

The aftermath of Trump’s appearance is a testament to the tangible impact words can have. According to internal sales data reviewed by CNN, there was a significant drop in ticket sales for the Kennedy Center’s performance of “The Nutcracker” – a 33% decrease from previous years. This slump isn’t just a number; it’s a reflection of the public’s response to a venue being associated with negative rhetoric. It underscores the reality that hosting controversial figures can have real economic repercussions, especially when those figures use their platform to spread divisiveness.

The Economic Reality Check

The Kennedy Center, with its 19 unions and high production costs, operates on a financial tightrope. The insight provided by an anonymous source that even a sold-out performance of “The Nutcracker” wouldn’t cover the bills highlights the precarious nature of arts funding and revenue. Trump’s appearance and subsequent fallout serve as a stark reminder of the delicate balance between artistic integrity and financial viability. It’s a reminder that words matter, actions have consequences, and in the world of arts and culture, there’s little room for error.

In conclusion, Trump’s Kennedy Center Honors appearance was more than just an awkward night filled with cringe-worthy comments; it was a demonstration of how not to conduct oneself in a position of power. It served as an unfortunate example of the impact negative rhetoric can have on the arts community, both culturally and economically. One can only hope that future honorees and speakers will take note and aim for a higher standard of respect and decorum.
