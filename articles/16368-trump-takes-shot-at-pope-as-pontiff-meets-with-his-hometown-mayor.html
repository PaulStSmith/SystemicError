Trump takes shot at pope as pontiff meets with his hometown mayor

Presidential Power as a Personal Grudge Machine

The Point of the Post

Donald Trump used Truth Social to call Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson “useless” while Johnson was at the Vatican meeting with Pope Leo XIV. In the same breath, Trump pivoted to Iran’s nuclear program, signing the post with his full name and title as president. Johnson had described the meeting as a meaningful encounter during a broader multi-faith gathering. Trump was not there.

Who Actually Holds Power

This is not a feud between equals. Trump holds the presidency, the platform, and the ability to shape federal policy toward cities. Johnson is a mayor governing under the pressure of federal immigration policy and funding cuts that Trump himself has used as leverage. When the president singles out a city mayor as “useless,” he is not merely insulted. He is using the office of the presidency to degrade a subordinate political target.

What the Post Is Doing

The message is not an argument. It is a display of command. Trump’s attack on Johnson works as political theater, but the real function is misdirection: turn public attention from the administration’s own choices and onto a Democratic mayor, then bury it under a rhetorical jump to Iran. That pivot is not random. It is the familiar Trump method of collapsing unrelated issues into one stream of grievance, so responsibility disappears inside performance.

The Scapegoat Pattern

Trump has repeatedly used Truth Social to target Democratic mayors of major cities, treating urban dysfunction as proof of Democratic failure. That framing is politically useful because it launders federal power into commentary. Cities become the stage for blame while Washington keeps the leverage. If a mayor resists federal immigration policy or objects to funding cuts, the president can recast that resistance as incompetence. The stronger actor gets to define the story and then pose as the one naming the problem.

Vatican, IRL, and the Theater of Resentment

The setting matters. Johnson was at the Vatican, meeting the pope, as part of a multi-faith prayer gathering. Trump’s response was to attack the optics of that meeting and try to contaminate it with his own agenda. The effect is crude but effective: reduce diplomacy, faith, and civic ritual to another occasion for dominance politics. The fact that he was not present only sharpens the absurdity. He is not participating in the event; he is trying to own the interpretation of it.

The Larger Pattern

This is how authoritarian-adjacent politics works in practice: not only through formal policy, but through constant degradation of institutions, rituals, and local officials that stand outside direct control. The president does not need to solve urban problems if he can endlessly assign them to enemies, then declare those enemies useless. The harm is not confusion. The confusion is the method.
