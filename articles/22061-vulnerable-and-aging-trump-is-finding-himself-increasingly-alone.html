'Vulnerable' and aging Trump is finding himself 'increasingly alone'

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

The Mouthpiece Always Gets Used Up

The Job Description

According to the source, Karoline Leavitt is leaving the White House press secretary job after a punishing stretch that collided with maternity leave, nonstop demands, and the reality that the role is built to consume the person in it. The piece treats that as a personal pivot. It is also evidence of a political operation that expects its spokespeople to absorb the damage for Trump and then disappear.

Power Sits With Trump

The institutional power here is not with Leavitt. It is with Donald Trump, who, by the source’s account, did not want her to leave because he trusts her and prefers familiar people around him. That is the real relationship: Trump as the center, staff as disposable translators of his impulses. Leavitt did not shape the message. She was selected because she would defend it.

Spin Is The Point

The article’s own examples make that plain. Leavitt was not merely answering questions; she was explaining away “bizarre behavior, gaffes and missteps,” attacking reporters, and insisting that the Epstein files proved “absolutely nothing” other than Trump “did nothing wrong.” That is not communication in any democratic sense. It is institutionalized denial, a press operation built to smother accountability with aggression.

The Exhaustion Is Structural

The source frames Leavitt’s exit as fatigue, and that part matters. But the deeper fact is that this White House runs on emotional labor for Trump’s benefit: younger staff soothing him, feeding him praise, printing out supportive tweets, drafting posts for his approval. This is a court, not a functioning executive branch. The exhaustion is not incidental. It is part of the machinery.

The Real Test Is After The Departure

The piece also hints at the next phase: if Democrats take the House and Senate, Trump will need someone else to spin for him while oversight pressure rises. That is the correct political reading. The issue is not whether Trump finds a new comfort blanket. The issue is whether the democratic opposition treats a weakened White House as an opportunity for real institutional confrontation, or wastes it on cable-news choreography.

A System That Eats Its Interpreters

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics relies on loyal intermediaries, then burns through them when they can no longer carry the lie. Leavitt is not the story’s villain and not its victim in any clean sense. She is a function of the system, a talented enabler of a project that demands shamelessness on command. The scandal is not that she is leaving. The scandal is that a White House can still operate this way at all.
