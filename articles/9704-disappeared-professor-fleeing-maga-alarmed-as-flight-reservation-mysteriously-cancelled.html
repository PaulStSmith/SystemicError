'Disappeared': Professor fleeing MAGA alarmed as flight reservation mysteriously cancelled

By Paulo Santos

FILE PHOTO: United Airlines planes land and prepare to take off at Newark Liberty International Airport in Newark, New Jersey, U.S., January 27, 2025. REUTERS/Fabrizio Bensch/File Photo

A history professor who claims he was targeted by the conservative group founded by the late MAGA influencer Charlie Kirk says he was alarmed Thursday after his flight was suddenly “cancelled,” adding, “I don’t think it’s a coincidence.”

Dr. Mark Bray, a Rutgers University professor and author of “Antifa: The Anti-Fascist Handbook,” was leaving the U.S. at Newark Liberty International Airport for Spain on Wednesday night, just hours after President Donald Trump had announced a crackdown on “left-wing” groups during a roundtable, Salon reports.

“’Someone’ cancelled my family’s flight out of the country at the last second. We got our boarding passes. We checked our bags. Went through security. Then at our gate our reservation ‘disappeared,’” Bray wrote on Bluesky.

“I may sound conspiratorial, but I don’t think it is a coincidence,” he told The New York Times, adding that the airline had booked a new flight for Thursday night. “We’re at a hotel and we’re just going to try again.”

Bray said he has received multiple death threats in the wake of Kirk’s killing and planned to move his family. He says Rutgers did not provide security for him following the threats. He said he told both campus and local police, but decided it would ultimately be safer to leave and relocate with his two young children and wife, another Rutgers professor.

He plans to teach via Zoom and told his students earlier this week. Bray plans to stay in Spain for the academic year.

“My role in this is as a professor,” Bray told The Times on Wednesday, just hours before leaving for his scheduled flight. “I’ve never been part of an antifa group, and I’m not currently. There’s an effort underway to paint me as someone who is doing the things that I’ve researched, but that couldn’t be further from the truth.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Shadows: Dr. Mark Bray’s Flight Cancellation and the Echoes of Political Intimidation

In a recent episode that seems ripped straight from a Cold War-era thriller, Dr. Mark Bray, a Rutgers University professor known for his book “Antifa: The Anti-Facist Handbook,” encountered what could be construed as a chilling act of political intimidation. Bray’s experience raises dire questions about the state of academic freedom and the potential for systemic targeting of individuals based on their research or political views.

The Incident

Dr. Bray’s preparation for a seemingly routine flight to Spain was abruptly disrupted when his family’s flight was mysteriously “cancelled.” This wasn’t just a minor hiccup in travel plans; it was a sudden erasure of their reservation after they had checked in, passed security, and arrived at their gate. Bray’s response to this incident was to voice his suspicions on Bluesky, suggesting that this was no mere coincidence, especially given the timing relative to President Donald Trump’s announcement of a crackdown on “left-wing” groups.

The Context

It’s essential to place this incident within the broader political context. Hours before Bray’s scheduled departure, Trump had targeted “left-wing” groups, a category vague enough to potentially encompass a wide range of individuals and organizations, including academic researchers like Bray. The cancellation occurred in this charged atmosphere, raising the specter of politically motivated harassment or worse.

Rutgers University’s Silence

Adding to the distressing nature of these events is Rutgers University’s apparent failure to provide adequate security or support for Bray following multiple death threats. This lack of action is alarming, suggesting a potential abdication of responsibility by academic institutions to protect their faculty from threats that directly impact their safety and freedom to research.

The Bigger Picture

Bray’s ordeal is not just a personal crisis but a stark reminder of the dangers facing academics and others who dare to explore and criticize extremist movements. The fact that Bray has had to relocate his family and plan to teach remotely from Spain for an academic year underscores the severity of the threats he faces.

Conclusion

The cancellation of Dr. Mark Bray’s flight might be dismissed by some as a coincidence or a bureaucratic snafu. However, when viewed against the backdrop of recent political events and the specific threats Bray has received, it cannot be so easily explained away. This incident should alarm anyone who values academic freedom and the importance of protecting individuals from politically motivated intimidation.

In a society where the lines between political rhetoric and actionable threats become increasingly blurred, the targeting of academics like Dr. Bray serves as a canary in the coal mine. It’s a warning of the systemic erosion of freedoms that are supposed to be protected, not just for the sake of those directly affected, but for the health of democracy itself.

Rutgers University, and indeed all academic institutions, must take a stand. They need to provide unequivocal support to their faculty facing threats for their research or political views. Anything less is a failure to uphold the principles of academic freedom and a tacit endorsement of intimidation tactics. The question now is, will they rise to the occasion, or will they remain silent, complicit in the shadows that threaten to engulf the light of free inquiry and expression?
