'Trump’s presidency is effectively over': New polling spells disaster for Trump in 2026

Paulo SantosSeptember 26, 20254 min read

New polling data on swing states shows that a plurality of voters now favor President Donald Trump being impeached, a figure that one journalist suspects will reach an outright majority ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

“If Democrats win 20-40 seats, while only needing to flip three to take the majority, Trump’s presidency is effectively over,” wrote journalist Jason Easley, co-owner and editor of PoliticusUSA, a left-leaning news website, in an analysis published Thursday.

“The opposition to this president and his presidency appears to be only growing stronger. The pot is boiling, and the boil-over moment will likely manifest itself next November.”

Conducted by Lake Research Partners and commissioned by the national nonprofit Free Speech For People, the new poll shows that 49% of likely 2026 voters in swing districts supported impeaching Trump, compared to 44% who opposed. Additionally, the polling showed that 56% of those same voters disapproved of Trump during his second term compared to 40% who approved.

To the polling firm, the results were “unprecedented,” noting that such high support for impeachment so early into a president’s term were “remarkable.”

“It is remarkable and, as far as we know, unprecedented for such a large proportion of voters in swing districts to support impeachment of a president in the first year of their term,” the polling firm wrote in its memorandum alongside the polling data.

As for Easley, he suspected that given the dramatic shift toward supporting impeachment, that support would reach an outright majority ahead of the 2026 midterm elections.

“This is a surprising number of people in swing districts who want Trump impeached,” Easley wrote. “At the current pace this administration is on, the number of voters who want Trump impeached for a third time will pass 50% before the midterm election.”

Trump has been impeached twice already; first for his “perfect phone call” in which he was alleged to have attempted to extort Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy for damaging information on former President Joe Biden in 2019, and again in 2020 for attempting to overturn the results of the election.

Our Analysis:

The Cauldron of Contempt: Analyzing the Swing State Sentiment

In what can only be described as a political cauldron bubbling over with disdain, new polling data reveals a startling trend: a growing plurality of voters in swing states are not just fed up with President Donald Trump; they’re favoring the nuclear option of impeachment. Yet again. This isn’t merely a case of political fatigue; it’s the manifestation of a profound disillusionment with a presidency marred by controversy and alleged misconduct.

Unprecedented? Undoubtedly.

The polling, executed with precision by Lake Research Partners and commissioned by the indefatigable Free Speech For People, unveils numbers that should send shivers down the spine of any sitting president. With 49% of likely 2026 voters in swing districts supporting impeachment against a 44% opposition, the message is crystal clear. But it’s the 56% disapproval rating during his second term that turns the knife deeper. These aren’t just disgruntled voters; these are citizens pushed to the brink, collectively voicing their discontent in a manner that is, as the polling firm rightly notes, “remarkable and... unprecedented.”

The Easley Perspective: A Boiling Pot Ready to Spill

Jason Easley’s analysis in PoliticusUSA, while emanating from a left-leaning lens, hits the nail on the head. The opposition isn’t a simmering stew; it’s a boiling cauldron ready to spill over by next November. Easley’s projection of a potential 20-40 seat gain for the Democrats is not just optimistic; it’s a testament to a presidency dangling by a thread. If the Democrats secure this, Trump’s presidency wouldn’t just be over in the traditional sense; it would be an unequivocal repudiation of his policies, his conduct, and his legacy.

A Historical Hat-trick of Impeachments?

Trump’s historical ledger is already blotted with the ink of two impeachments: one for the so-called “perfect phone call” with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and another for his attempts to overturn the 2020 election results. The prospect of a third impeachment is not just historical; it’s Shakespearean in its tragedy.

Yet, amid the noise and fury, one must ponder: What does it say about the state of our union when a president faces the specter of impeachment thrice? It speaks to a deeper, systemic malaise—a dissonance between the elected and the electorate, a chasm of trust that widens with each controversy.

The Road to 2026: A Political Precipice

As we edge closer to the 2026 midterms, the air is thick with anticipation and anxiety. The swing state sentiment, as captured in this latest polling, is not just a gauge of political preference; it’s a barometer of national sentiment. If the trend holds, and impeachment support crosses the 50% threshold, it won’t just be a victory lap for Trump’s detractors; it will be a clarion call for a fundamental reassessment of our political landscape.

In the end, the numbers speak louder than any pundit ever could. They tell a story of disillusionment, of a presidency teetering on the brink, and of a populace clamoring for change. As we march towards November next year, one thing is certain: the pot is indeed boiling, and the spill-over could well redraw the contours of American politics.
