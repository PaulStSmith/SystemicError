'The biggest political scandal': Trump goes off in overnight Truth Social binge

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20254 min read

S Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Donald Trump was up in the wee hours of the morning and fired off three posts on Truth Social that included complaining about having to deal with China and then attacking former President Joe Biden.

After spending most of the day silent on his social media platform after having snarled at Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) for balking at the budget bill now before the Senate, and writing, "His ideas are actually crazy (losers!). The people of Kentucky can’t stand him," the president let loose at about 4am ET on Wednesday.

In the first of three brief posts he let it be known that trade negations with China are not going well, writing, "I like President XI of China, always have, and always will, but he is VERY TOUGH, AND EXTREMELY HARD TO MAKE A DEAL WITH!!!"

That led to a post where he stated, "THE UNITED STATES HAD THE BEST MAY IN 30 YEARS. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!!!" without any explanation.

He then turned to two of his favorite topics: his belief that ex-President Joe Biden was not in control before he left office, again asserted without evidence, and complaining that he was robbed in the 2020 presidential election.

"With the exception of the RIGGED PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION OF 2020, THE AUTOPEN IS THE BIGGEST POLITICAL SCANDAL IN AMERICAN HISTORY!!!" he wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Predawn Tantrum: A Glimpse into Trump's Social Media Frenzy

In what can only be described as a virtual meltdown, Donald Trump embarked on a predawn tirade across his social media platform, Truth Social. This episode is a stark reminder of the chaos that often accompanies Trump's online presence, a chaos that, unfortunately, paints a broader picture of the political landscape he occupies.

Trade Negotiations: A One-Man Show

Trump's lamentation over the difficulty of dealing with China and his peculiar fondness for President Xi Jinping highlights a troubling blend of personal admiration and professional frustration. "I like President XI of China, always have, and always will, but he is VERY TOUGH, AND EXTREMELY HARD TO MAKE A DEAL WITH!!!" This statement, devoid of any diplomatic nuance, underscores the ex-president's simplistic view of international relations—a view where personal relationships supposedly trump (pun intended) strategic complexities and national interests.

The MAGA Mirage

Following his venting session on trade, Trump boasted about the United States having "THE BEST MAY IN 30 YEARS. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN!!!"—a claim thrown into the ether without a shred of evidence or context. This habit of making unsubstantiated claims is a hallmark of Trump's communication style, one that aims to construct an alternate reality where his presidency is seen as an unmitigated success. The lack of detail invites skepticism: What metrics is he referring to? Economic growth? Job creation? As usual, Trump leaves these questions unanswered, preferring bold declarations over substantiated facts.

A Conspiracy Theorist’s Delight

Trump's predilection for conspiracy theories is no secret, and his posts transition smoothly into this familiar territory. He revisits the well-trodden path of the "RIGGED PRESIDENTIAL ELECTION OF 2020" and introduces what he considers the "BIGGEST POLITICAL SCANDAL IN AMERICAN HISTORY!!!"—the autopen. This leap from trade negotiations and economic boasts to election fraud claims and autopen scandals encapsulates the chaotic, scattergun approach Trump often employs. It's a technique designed to overwhelm, confuse, and ultimately galvanize his base, regardless of the truth.

The Bottom Line

Trump's early morning social media spree is a microcosm of his broader political strategy: keep the narrative focused on him, regardless of the truth or coherence of his statements. His posts, ranging from personal grievances to grandiose claims, are designed to keep him in the spotlight, feeding his base with a steady diet of outrage and victimhood.

However, these tactics, while attention-grabbing, distract from pressing issues facing the nation. They highlight a disturbing trend where political discourse is dominated by personalities over policies, and sensationalism over substance. As observers, we must sift through this morass of misinformation and focus on the facts, challenging the narratives that seek to distort reality for political gain.

In conclusion, Trump's Truth Social tirade is not just a series of early morning posts; it's a manifestation of a deeper problem within American politics—one where truth is malleable, and accountability is often just an afterthought.
