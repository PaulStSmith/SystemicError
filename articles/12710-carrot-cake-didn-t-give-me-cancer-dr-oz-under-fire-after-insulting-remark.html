'Carrot cake didn't give me cancer': Dr. Oz under fire after 'insulting' remark

Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Mehmet Oz speaks during a press conference to discuss health insurance reform, at the Department of Health and Human Services in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Mohatt

U.S. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz appeared on Fox Business on Monday, where he presented a carrot cake to celebrate Medicaid's 60th birthday and brushed aside concerns about the millions of Americans likely to lose their healthcare coverage under recently passed Republican legislation—by telling people to not eat carrot cake.

Oz—the multimillionaire erstwhile celebrity surgeon, purveyor of "miracle" cures, and failed U.S. Senate candidate—gave Fox Business host Stuart Varney what he called a "MAHA Medi-cake" before proceeding to extol the virtues of Medicaid, the program launched during then-President Lyndon B. Johnson's "Great Society" agenda that provides health insurance to more than 70 million lower-income Americans.

Medicaid "was a promise to the American people to take care of you if you are having problems financially or if you were having an issue because you're older and needed healthcare," Oz said. "And it changed the country in a good way for many reasons."

"But we're all in it together, Stuart," he added, "which means we'll be there for you, the American people, when you need help with Medicaid and Medicare, but you've got to stay healthy as well. Be healthy, do the most you can do to really live up to the potential, your God-given potential to live a full and healthy life, you know, don't eat carrot cake, eat real food."

Yet another said, "Um... your boss eats McDonald's every chance he gets and you are judging people eating carrot cake," a reference to Trump's legendary fondness for Big Macs, Filet-O-Fish sandwiches, and vanilla shakes.

Still another quipped, "First he came for my crudites, now my carrot cake."

Over on X, one account with over 130,000 followers said: "What an insensitive p---- as he brings a piece of carrot cake to Stu Varney during the interview. Republicans seem so gleeful to be hurting Americans. This is why millionaires and billionaires should never be in Congress or the [White House]."

The Occupy Democrats X account also weighed in, posting, "It's not enough for them to take away our healthcare, Republicans want to blame us for getting sick."

"The idea that avoiding carrot cake in favor of healthier foods will somehow render Americans immune to health problems is insulting in the extreme," Occupy Democrats continued. "Rather than 'let them eat cake,' he's telling us 'do not eat cake,' but the sentiment is every bit as out of touch as Marie Antoinette's apocryphal quote."

"MAHA stands for 'Make America Healthy Again,' an Orwellian phrase deployed by an administration that is actively making Americans sicker by stripping away their healthcare," the account added. "This is what Republicans really think of the American people. They ram through policies making our lives worse in countless ways, then they laugh at us and spit in our faces. There has never been a more gleefully spiteful political movement."

Our Analysis:

The Carrot Cake Conundrum: A Slice of Satire or a Recipe for Disaster?

In a display that could only be concocted in the surreal kitchens of American politics, Dr. Mehmet Oz, the U.S. Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services Administrator, served up a carrot cake alongside a hefty dose of obliviousness on Fox Business. Celebrating Medicaid's 60th birthday, Oz seemingly attempted to sugarcoat the bitter pill that is the largest Medicaid cuts in history, courtesy of the so-called One Big Beautiful Bill Act signed by President Donald Trump. This legislative concoction is poised to leave an estimated 17 million Americans without health insurance, a fact Oz blithely bypassed with advice as nutritionally deficient as it is politically: “don’t eat carrot cake.”

A Multimillionaire's Misguided Missive

Oz, a figure already marinated in controversy due to his promotion of "miracle" cures and a failed U.S. Senate run, now finds himself at the helm of a program that is a lifeline for over 70 million lower-income Americans. His comments, intended to be a light-hearted nod to health, instead revealed a staggering disconnect. By suggesting that the path to avoiding the fallout of his administration's policies is as simple as eschewing dessert, Oz's advice is not just tone-deaf; it's indicative of a broader malaise in political discourse that prioritizes platitudes over substantial policy discourse.

The Satirical and the Serious

It's almost as if Oz's appearance was engineered for satirical fodder, yet the implications are anything but humorous for the millions facing the loss of healthcare coverage. The social media backlash was swift and scathing, highlighting not just the absurdity of Oz's remarks but the real pain and fear that such policies exacerbate. From Senators to social media users, the message was clear: this isn't about carrot cake; it's about the callousness of suggesting that health can be distilled to individual choices amidst a crisis of access and affordability.

"Make America Healthy Again"?

The invocation of "MAHA" or "Make America Healthy Again" by Oz is an exercise in Orwellian doublespeak. It's a phrase that would be laughable if its implications weren't so dire. This administration, through actions like the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, isn't making America healthy again; it's systematically dismantling the safety nets that contribute to the health and well-being of its most vulnerable populations.

The Verdict

The article lays bare a grotesque tableau of American healthcare politics, where the offering of a carrot cake becomes a symbol of the vast chasm between those who dictate healthcare policy and those who live (or die) by its consequences. Dr. Oz’s cavalier attitude serves as a microcosm of a larger, more disturbing trend: a political elite so ensconced in their privilege that the struggles of the millions without healthcare are as foreign to them as the idea that a piece of cake could be anything more than a punchline.

In the end, the carrot cake debacle is more than just a misguided PR stunt; it's a stark reminder of the systemic rot that plagues American healthcare policy—a policy shaped by those who have the luxury of treating it as an abstract debate rather than a matter of life and death. The reality is, no amount of sugary rhetoric can mask the bitter truth of a healthcare system in crisis, a system that requires substantial, compassionate reform, not flippant remarks and pastry analogies.
