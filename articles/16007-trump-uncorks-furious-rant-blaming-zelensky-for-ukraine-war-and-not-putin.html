Trump uncorks furious rant blaming Zelensky for Ukraine war -- and not Putin

U.S. President Donald Trump meets with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 28, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump on Monday published a long tirade blaming former President Joe Biden and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky for Russia's invasion of Ukraine more than three years ago.

Writing on his Truth Social page, the president tried to brush off responsibility for the failure of his diplomatic efforts to stop the war by attacking both Biden and Zelensky.

"The War between Russia and Ukraine is Biden’s war, not mine," Trump wrote. "I just got here, and for four years during my term, had no problem in preventing it from happening. President Putin, and everyone else, respected your President! I HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH THIS WAR, BUT AM WORKING DILIGENTLY TO GET THE DEATH AND DESTRUCTION TO STOP. If the 2020 Presidential Election was not RIGGED, and it was, in so many ways, that horrible War would never have happened. President Zelenskyy and Crooked Joe Biden did an absolutely horrible job in allowing this travesty to begin. There were so many ways of preventing it from ever starting. But that is the past. Now we have to get it to STOP, AND FAST. SO SAD!"

In his rant, Trump never placed any blame on Russian President Vladimir Putin, who launched the invasion of Ukraine in 2022 and who has blown off Trump's demands to halt offensive operations inside Ukraine.

During the 2024 presidential campaign, Trump claimed that he could end the Russian invasion of Ukraine in a single day, but so far no such resolution to the conflict appears to be in sight.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Tirade on Ukraine

President Donald Trump's latest outburst on his Truth Social page is a masterclass in deflection and distortion, a testament to his unyielding commitment to rewriting the narrative of significant global events to suit his personal and political agenda. His attempt to absolve himself of any responsibility for Russia's invasion of Ukraine and to lay the blame squarely at the feet of former President Joe Biden and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is as bold as it is baseless.

A Convenient Omission of Putin's Agency

It's startling, yet unsurprisingly, that Trump's lengthy diatribe fails to assign even a sliver of blame to the actual architect of the invasion, Russian President Vladimir Putin. This glaring omission speaks volumes about Trump's skewed perspective on international diplomacy and his unsettling deference to authoritarian figures. The refusal to acknowledge Putin's role in initiating and sustaining the conflict in Ukraine is a glaring testament to Trump's skewed worldview, where accountability is as malleable as clay, to be shaped to fit his narrative.

The Blame Game

Trump's assertion that "The War between Russia and Ukraine is Biden’s war, not mine," is a simplistic and misleading claim that ignores the complex web of international relations, historical tensions, and geopolitical strategies that predate his presidency. His boast of having prevented the war during his term by virtue of respect from Putin and others is a fanciful revision of history that neglects the multifaceted nature of international diplomacy. It's a classic Trump maneuver: inflate his own successes (real or imagined) and deflect any semblance of failure or responsibility.

The Rigged Election Narrative

Once again, Trump weaves the baseless narrative of a rigged 2020 Presidential Election into his commentary, suggesting that had the election not been "RIGGED," the invasion "would never have happened." This claim not only perpetuates the unfounded and dangerous myth of widespread election fraud but also irresponsibly suggests a simple, singular cause to a complex international crisis. It's a hallmark of Trump's rhetoric: blame shifting coupled with the fantastical notion that his presence alone could reshape global geopolitics.

A Dubious Claim of Resolution

Trump's claim during the 2024 presidential campaign that he could end the Russian invasion of Ukraine in a single day is as audacious as it is unsubstantiated. It underscores a recurring theme in Trump's approach to policymaking and diplomacy: an overreliance on his personal brand and supposed deal-making prowess, often at the expense of nuanced, strategic planning and execution. The absence of any concrete plan or diplomatic breakthrough to date only serves to highlight the gap between his grandiose promises and the complex reality on the ground.

Conclusion

Trump's latest statements on the Ukraine conflict are a stark reminder of his penchant for revisionism, his selective allocation of blame, and his unwavering belief in the power of his personal mythos to reshape the world. It's a dangerous cocktail of narcissism and naivety, one that distorts the past and muddies the waters for the future. As the situation in Ukraine continues to unfold, the international community would do well to base its assessments and actions on the grounded realities of international law, diplomacy, and respect for sovereignty, rather than the fanciful narratives spun by those more concerned with personal legacy than global stability. It's not just sad; it's a systemic failure of accountability and leadership.
