'The math doesn't math': Dem scorches GOP's $7 trillion tax cuts at oversight hearing

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Republicans' Tax Cut Proposal: A Masterclass in Fiscal Irresponsibility

House Oversight Committee/screen grab

Rep. Melanie Stansbury (D-NM) blasted Republicans for a plan to partially pay for tax cuts for the wealthy by forcing federal workers to either receive less generous retirement benefits or lose their civil service protections.

During a House Oversight Committee hearing on Wednesday, members considered the proposal that would create a new pension surcharge for workers who did not choose to serve on an at-will basis.

"So first of all, what's important to understand is that over the last couple of months, the president has transmitted to the Hill a list of asks and they really focus on making permanent tax breaks that were originally put into place in 2017 that are about to sunset," Stansbury explained. "So these tax breaks are for billionaires, millionaires, mega corporations, and it's gonna cost $7 trillion."

"And to pay for it, they wanna try to offset some of those costs because our friends across the aisle are trying to claim that they're being fiscally responsible," she continued. "But let's be clear, the math doesn't math because the offsets that they're seeking only add up to about 1.5 to $2 trillion."

"And when you add it all up over 30 years, we're talking over $30 trillion in new spending for the United States government."

Stansbury argued that Republicans were trying to pay for the tax cuts "on our federal workers' backs."

"What that means is that they wanna strip away the protections for our federal workers so they can be fired at will at any moment, okay?" she said. "And if you do not, as a federal employee, elect to be at-will, if this passes, if this piece of legislation passes, you're gonna have your retirement taxed, essentially, an additional 5%. So that means your pay is gonna get cut unless you elect to be fireable."

"So basically what this package is doing is extortion," the lawmaker added. "This is called extortion."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Republicans' Tax Cut Proposal: A Masterclass in Fiscal Irresponsibility

In a bold move that reeks of fiscal hypocrisy, Republicans have laid out a plan that epitomizes their penchant for padding the pockets of the wealthy at the expense of the working class. The latest spectacle, as highlighted by Rep. Melanie Stansbury (D-NM) during a recent House Oversight Committee hearing, is a scheme to finance tax cuts for the affluent by strong-arming federal workers into a corner where they face the loss of either their retirement benefits or their job security. Let's dissect this proposal with the rigor it deserves.

The Proposal: A Closer Look

At its core, the plan involves making permanent the tax breaks introduced in 2017, primarily benefiting billionaires, millionaires, and mega corporations. The price tag? A cool $7 trillion. The Republicans' strategy to counterbalance this hefty sum involves imposing a new pension surcharge on federal workers, pushing them towards an "at-will" employment status—stripped of their civil service protections and susceptible to being fired on a whim.

The Fiscal Fallacy

Rep. Stansbury's critique unveils the fundamental flaw in the Republicans' fiscal logic. Proclaiming fiscal responsibility while plotting to add over $30 trillion in new spending over 30 years is akin to claiming sobriety while chugging a bottle of whiskey. The math, as Stansbury rightly points out, simply does not add up. The proposed offsets, totalling a mere 1.5 to $2 trillion, are but a drop in the ocean compared to the gargantuan costs of the tax cuts.

Extortion in Disguise?

The stark choice presented to federal workers—sacrifice your job security or watch your retirement benefits dwindle—smacks of extortion. This isn't just an assault on the financial welfare of these employees; it's an attack on the very principles of fair labor practices. By holding workers' livelihoods hostage, the proposal sets a dangerous precedent for labor rights erosions under the guise of fiscal prudence.

Conclusion: The True Cost of Tax Cuts

The Republicans' tax cut proposal lays bare a troubling priority: safeguarding the wealth of the elite over the well-being of the workforce. It's a stark reminder of the systemic flaws that prioritize profit over people. This isn't just a policy debate; it's a moral quandary that challenges the essence of American values.

As this plan moves through legislative scrutiny, one hopes that reason and justice will prevail over greed and exploitation. However, given the track record, one must prepare for a continued fight against policies that seek to enrich the few at the expense of the many. It's high time for a fiscal strategy that genuinely embodies responsibility—not just to the ledger, but to the people it's supposed to serve.
