Red state governor rips GOP for 'not getting anything done'

By Paulo Santos

Although Oklahoma’s conservative two-term Republican governor, Kevin Stitt, is far from a Never Trumper, tensions between him and President Donald Trump have been increasing lately. Trump was furious when Stitt, who heads the National Governors Association, politely disagreed with his decision to exclude two Democratic governors, Maryland’s Wes Moore and Colorado’s Jared Polis, from a White House event. And he is attacking Stitt as a RINO (Republican In Name Only) on his Truth Social platform.

During an interview with Politico’s Adam Wren posted on X n February 19, the outgoing governor — whose second term ends in early January 2027 — spoke candidly about the 2026 midterms. And he acknowledged that, according to polls, his party could be in trouble.

Stitt told Wren, “I think the American people want a president to give us real solutions. Let’s stop the fighting. What are the real solutions? I tell people — some of my friends when I’m back home — it feels like we’re a car racing towards a cliff. And right now, my party’s driving, and the Democratic Party’s in the passenger seat. And we’re fighting over the control of the radio. And our car is racing towards the cliff here.”

The Oklahoma governor continued, “And then, you know, in three years, you may have a Democrat driving, and the Republicans will be in the passenger seat. We’ll still be fighting over the radio.”

Stitt lamented that “Congress is not getting anything done.”

Stitt told Wren, “We haven’t passed a balanced budget in Congress since the ‘90s. (President) Bill Clinton was the last time we had a balanced budget…. We literally have a crisis that’s facing us, and nobody seems to be talking about it.”

Our Analysis:

The Political Irony of Kevin Stitt and the GOP’s Direction

The recent revelations from Oklahoma Governor Kevin Stitt provide a stark glimpse into the internal strife and philosophical discord within the Republican Party, particularly as it pertains to its relationship with former President Donald Trump. Stitt, a conservative Republican and by no stretch a critic of Trump’s overarching policies, has found himself in the crosshairs of Trump’s ire, not for policy disagreements but for a perceived lack of loyalty. This situation underscores the current political theatrics more akin to high school cliques than the serious business of governing a divided nation.

Trump’s Ire and the RINO Branding

Trump’s frustration with Stitt stems from the latter’s disagreement over excluding Democratic governors from a White House event, an act that Trump apparently perceived as sacrilege. This has led to Trump branding Stitt a RINO (Republican In Name Only) on his Truth Social platform, a term that has become Trump’s favorite pejorative for Republicans who dare to cross him or, in this case, simply advocate for bipartisan inclusion. This episode is emblematic of Trump’s continued stranglehold on the GOP, where loyalty to Trump often seems to supersede policy alignment or even common sense.

Stitt’s Candid Reflections

In his interview with Politico, Stitt offers a metaphor of American politics that’s both vivid and troubling. He compares the nation to a car racing toward a cliff with Republicans and Democrats fighting over control of the radio rather than addressing the imminent danger. This analogy not only highlights the petty squabbles that dominate political discourse but also accurately depicts the existential crises facing the nation, from climate change to fiscal irresponsibility, which are being ignored in favor of partisan bickering.

Stitt’s lamentation of Congress’s inability to pass a balanced budget since the Clinton era further emphasizes the fiscal irresponsibility that has become a hallmark of both parties, albeit with the Republicans often positioning themselves as the party of fiscal conservatism. This claim seems increasingly hollow as the national debt continues to balloon, with little to no action taken to address it substantively.

The Bigger Picture

The article paints a picture of a Republican Party at a crossroads, torn between allegiance to Trump’s divisive brand of politics and a need to address the real issues facing Americans. Stitt’s criticisms, while pointed, seem to fall short of calling for a comprehensive reevaluation of the party’s direction, instead reflecting a sense of resignation to the status quo.

This episode raises critical questions about the GOP’s future and its ability to govern effectively. Can the party move beyond personality cults and infighting to address the pressing issues Stitt outlines? Or will it continue down the path of internal division and external alienation, racing towards the cliff with the radio blaring?

As the 2026 midterms approach, Stitt’s reflections serve as a cautionary tale. The American people are indeed looking for real solutions, not just from the GOP but from all political leaders. The question remains: will those in power heed the warning, or is the car doomed to go over the cliff? Only time will tell, but the current trajectory is decidedly worrying.
