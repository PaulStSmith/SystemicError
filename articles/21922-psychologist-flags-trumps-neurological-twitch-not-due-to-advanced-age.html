Psychologist flags Trump’s 'neurological twitch' not due to 'advanced age'

A Drowsy Strongman Is Still a Strongman

The Body Is Not the Story

The source centers on a single Oval Office signing where Donald Trump allegedly slurred, drifted off, lost his place, and repeatedly fell asleep while Robert F. Kennedy Jr. spoke. A psychologist quoted in the piece reads those moments as signs of possible neurological impairment rather than simple age. That is the setup. The real story is what power does with a visibly deteriorating man in the Oval Office.

Who Holds Power Here

Trump is still the institutional center. That is the point. The presidency is not a medical exam; it is an office that hands one person enormous authority even when he is visibly unsteady. The relevant question is not whether Trump looked alert enough for cable news. It is who continues to vest executive power in him, stage the optics, and ask the country to pretend this is normal governance.

The answer is not hidden. His aides, his party, his Cabinet, and the political media ecosystem all help keep the machine moving while the man at the top appears increasingly unable to hold the moment together. That is not a tragic accident. It is the operating system.

The Misdirection

The source tries to make the issue feel like a private medical puzzle. That framing is convenient because it shifts attention away from political responsibility. “Is it age or something neurological?” is a narrow question. “Why is this man still exercising presidential power, and who is enabling it?” is the one that matters.

This is where political coverage often gets weak. It treats deliberate institutional maintenance as if it were confusion. It treats obvious decay as an edge case to be medically debated instead of a governance failure to be confronted. The result is a theater of concern that leaves the underlying power structure untouched.

Enablers, Not Spectators

RFK Jr. is in the room. So are the people managing the event, the messaging, and the choreography around a president who, according to the source, keeps drifting off in the middle of a formal duty. That matters because authoritarian politics does not require a perfectly coherent leader. It requires a network willing to absorb his incoherence and convert it into authority anyway.

The cruelty here is bureaucratic. Everyone around the center knows the center is compromised, and the system keeps functioning by pretending that concern ends at observation. It does not. The enabling is the story.

The Pattern Behind the Scene

This is not just about one man looking unwell. It is about a political order that rewards spectacle, loyalty, and denial over capacity. A presidency built around personal power can survive all kinds of obvious dysfunction so long as the surrounding institution refuses to name the problem in political terms.

That is the larger pattern: decay at the top, compliance beneath it, and a press cycle that often prefers symptom-tracking to responsibility-tracking. Trump’s condition, whatever it may be, is not the democratic lesson. The lesson is that American institutions will keep staging authority long after they stop being able to guarantee it.

The Real Collapse

The source wants the reader to look at Trump’s body. The more important object is the regime around it. A democracy does not fail only when a dangerous man takes power. It fails when the people and institutions around him keep treating deterioration as a side issue, and governance as a backdrop to manage optics. That is not stability. It is institutional cowardice with a flag on it.
