It's about 'to get a lot worse' for GOP as Trump preps self-destructive plan: Nobel winner

A “retribution tour” of the United States for Donald Trump will hit the GOP hard, a Nobel Prize-winning economist has warned.

The president seems set on touring the country as a way of explaining how things have never been better for people economically, and this could cause ripples of doubt through the Republican supporter base. Trump’s trip around the country was confirmed by the president earlier this week, though writer Paul Krugman explained how talking about the economy to those in financial hardship will hinder both Trump’s administration and the work of the GOP.

Krugman, writing in his Substack, explained, “Trump is handling this reversal with his usual style and grace: in the past few days he has repeatedly called affordability a ‘hoax’ and a ‘con job.’ According to Axios, he’s planning a nationwide retribution tour to convince voters that things are going great and that they’re wrong to be so down on the economy.”

“Democratic strategists must be rubbing their hands with glee. And if you are one of those Republicans reconsidering your future career options, know that things are going to get worse. A lot worse.”

Krugman would go on to suggest the “health care earthquake” on the horizon is going to hit soon and will be damaging for Republican representatives. He wrote, “Which brings us to the health care earthquake that’s soon to hit — an earthquake that, based on my read of Mettler, is going to inflict significant political damage on the Republicans.”

“So let’s think about the politics of what’s about to happen: Millions of Americans are about to see a sudden rise in health care costs — not a hypothetical future rise, but a sudden jump on January 1.

“This ACA premium shock will hit as other forces are exacerbating the sense of crisis over affordability. Businesses are starting to fully pass onto consumers the cost of Trump’s tariffs. Electricity prices are soaring as data centers inflict the cost of their enormous power demands on consumers. In addition, Trump’s deportation policies are increasing the cost of food.”

Krugman then added, “Trump may believe that affordability is a con job, but it isn’t. It’s going to hit him and his allies hard. And it couldn’t happen to a more deserving group of people.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s “Retribution Tour”: A Misguided Victory Lap?

In what can only be described as a bizarre twist on victory laps, Donald Trump’s so-called “retribution tour” across the United States is shaping up to be a masterclass in political self-sabotage. As Nobel Prize-winning economist Paul Krugman insightfully points out, Trump’s insistence on touting an economic golden age, in stark contrast to the lived realities of those in financial hardship, is not just tone-deaf; it’s a recipe for fracturing his own base.

An Economic Disconnect

Trump’s Disconnect with Economic Reality is not just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring chasm. By branding affordability concerns as a “hoax” and a “con job,” Trump is not only alienating those directly impacted by economic policies under his administration but also signaling a broader disregard for the tangible struggles faced by many Americans. This approach seems not only misguided but recklessly indifferent.

The Ripple Effects Within the GOP

The implications of Trump’s nationwide tour are far-reaching, particularly for the GOP. Krugman’s analysis suggests a looming “health care earthquake” that could further exacerbate the party’s challenges. As health care costs are set to rise sharply, the Republican representatives might find themselves in an increasingly precarious position, defending policies that directly contribute to the financial strain on their constituents.

The Tariffs and Their Toll

One cannot overlook the irony in Trump’s policies, such as tariffs and deportation strategies, backfiring spectacularly. These policies, purportedly designed to strengthen the economy and protect American jobs, are inadvertently driving up the cost of living for the very people they were meant to help. Electricity prices soar and food costs increase, yet Trump’s administration appears blindsided by the consequences of their own actions.

The Verdict

Krugman’s commentary, although scathing, is grounded in a grim reality that the Trump administration seems hell-bent on ignoring. The “retribution tour” is less a celebration of economic success and more a testament to a profound disconnect from the American populace. The impending “health care earthquake” and the backlash from policies that have tangibly harmed rather than helped Americans are set to be a reckoning for the GOP.

In this context, Krugman’s closing remark that this couldn’t happen to a “more deserving group of people” is not just a biting critique; it’s a reflection of a broader sentiment that the political and economic policies championed by Trump and his allies have been not just ineffective, but actively detrimental to the well-being of many Americans. As the GOP braces for the fallout, one thing is clear: the retribution tour might just be the prelude to a much larger political earthquake.
