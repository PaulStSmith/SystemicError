'Did not appear to be routine': Experts question White House over Trump's hospital visit

The White House raised some analysts’ eyebrows when it announced Trump would be going to Walter Reed for an “annual” visit, despite the fact that Trump had an annual visit in April. The White House declared Trump to be in excellent health, but didn’t release all the details.

Now, medical professionals are raising a red flag about the information provided after the visit.

The Washington Post quoted a medical doctor for Dick Cheney.

“Jonathan Reiner, a longtime cardiologist for former vice president Dick Cheney, said the White House should be more forthcoming about the results of the latest visit as some of the tests did not appear to be routine — and as he and other medical experts argue that the job of determining a president’s fitness is too important to entrust to a handpicked physician,” according to the report.

Reiner is further quoted as asking, “Are they following up on concerns from when it was noted during the summer that his legs were very swollen, or are they following up on new symptoms?”

Jeffrey Kuhlman, who served as a White House physician to three presidents, also had some questions. He reportedly said “that presidents typically visit Walter Reed to receive advanced imaging or undergo medical procedures. The military hospital also has space to perform procedures in the privacy of a unit dedicated to the president’s care.”

“I could move anything I needed to the White House to provide diagnostics,” said Kuhlman, who served as a physician to former presidents Bill Clinton, George W. Bush and Barack Obama, the outlet reports. “Can’t move an MRI or a CAT scan, but I can move any scope, any person, any medication, and most of that is already pre-positioned there.”

“For a 79-year-old, best practice is to also use advanced imaging, like a CT of coronary arteries to evaluate plaque buildup and extent of atherosclerosis blockage,” Kuhlman added. “The White House states he went there for advanced imaging. They chose not to release those results.”

Conservative George Conway also weighed in on Trump’s hospital visit on Sunday, saying on MSNBC early in the morning that he has questions about it.

“Why aren’t we talking about that?” Conway asked this weekend. “Why did he make that trip up there? What’s going on with that?”

Our Analysis:

The Opaque Veil of Trump’s “Routine” Hospital Visit

In a spectacle that feels more at par with Cold War-era secrecy than the expected transparency of a modern democracy, the recent hospital visit of former President Donald Trump has morphed into a quagmire of questions, half-truths, and, let’s be honest, outright evasions. The White House’s attempt to brand this jaunt to Walter Reed as an “annual” event is not just suspect; it’s borderline farcical. Given that Trump’s last so-called annual visit was in April, the math doesn’t just seem fuzzy—it’s blatantly incorrect.

The narrative pushed by the White House, proclaiming Trump in “excellent health,” crumbles under the mildest scrutiny when faced with the refusal to disclose full details of the visit. This lack of transparency is not a mere oversight; it’s a deliberate obfuscation of the facts. When medical professionals, those whose life’s work revolves around the parsing of complex health data, raise a red flag, it’s not just a suggestion of something amiss—it’s a blaring siren.

Experts Demand Transparency

Dr. Jonathan Reiner, a cardiologist with enough stripes to have served Dick Cheney—a man whose heart history reads like a medical thriller—questions the routine nature of these tests. His skepticism is not without merit. The job of determining a president’s fitness, both mental and physical, is of paramount importance. It’s not something to be left to the whims of a “handpicked physician” whose loyalty may lie more with their employer than the truth.

Reiner’s pointed question about whether this visit was a follow-up on previously noted health concerns or the investigation of new symptoms is not just medically relevant; it’s a matter of national security. The fitness of the commander-in-chief is not a trivial matter. It’s a crucial pillar upon which the stability of the global order might rest.

Jeffrey Kuhlman, a physician with a pedigree of serving three presidents, further dismantles the White House’s narrative by highlighting the capabilities of White House medical facilities. His assertion that almost any diagnostic tool, except for large-scale imaging machines, can be brought to the White House, begs the question: Why the need for a secretive hospital visit?

Kuhlman’s advocacy for advanced imaging for someone of Trump’s age is a standard medical practice, not a revolutionary idea. The refusal to release the results of such imaging, however, is not standard. It’s a deviation from the norm that screams of a desire to keep the public in the dark for reasons unknown.

Political Figures Weigh In

Even conservative voices, like George Conway, are puzzled by the clandestine nature of this visit. His questions echo the concerns of medical and public observers alike. The lack of clarity, the deviation from established protocols, and the silence from the White House form a pattern that is deeply troubling.

Conclusion: A Call for Clarity

In a healthy democracy, transparency, especially regarding the health of its highest official, is not optional; it’s fundamental. The evasion displayed by the White House not only undermines public trust but also poses a troubling question: What are they hiding?

The silence is not just a refusal to share medical details; it’s a loud, resonant note of disregard for the public’s right to know. It’s a systemic error that needs correction, not tomorrow, but today. The health of a president should never be shrouded in mystery. It’s a matter of public record, public concern, and, ultimately, public safety.
