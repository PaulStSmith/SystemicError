'Congress kept in the dark': Bombshell report reveals covert operation greenlit by Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump “may have violated the law” according to a bombshell report Thursday that revealed the president had personally greenlit a covert military operation targeting North Korea.

According to two dozen government officials and military personnel who spoke with the New York Times, Trump had signed off in 2019 on an operation to deploy SEAL Team 6’s Red Squadron – the same unit that carried out the 2011 assassination of Osama Bin Laden – to the shores of North Korea to plant an electronic device to intercept the communications of its Supreme Leader Kim Jong Un.

The operation was ultimately a failure, with the operation being compromised by a small boat approaching the team’s mini-subs. The team ended up firing upon and killing the boat’s two to three passengers, later learned to likely be civilians that had been “diving for shellfish,” according to New York Times reporters Dave Philipps and Matthew Cole, who spoke with the officials under the condition of anonymity.

And, while the team’s actions likely constituted a violation of international law – both by the violation of North Korea’s sovereignty by crossing into its borders, and the extrajudicial execution of civilians – Trump’s signing off on the operation may itself have violated the law as well.

“The Trump administration did not notify key members of Congress who oversee intelligence operations, before or after the mission,” the report reads. “The lack of notification may have violated the law. The White House declined to comment.”

The operation took place amid Trump’s attempts to broker peace with the East Asian nation, a long-time adversary to the United States, in large part for rejecting American influence and instead cozying up to the Soviet Union. In June of that year, Trump became the first United States president to set foot in North Korea in what was a widely-covered spectacle at the time.

As to Trump’s criminal culpability for greenlighting the operation, Matthew Waxman, a professor at Columbia University, told the New York Times that while some gray area existed for presidents to order covert operations without notifying Congress, in this instance, it was more likely than not that Trump’s decision ran afoul of standard protocol.

“The point is to ensure that Congress isn’t kept in the dark when major stuff is going on,” Waxman said. “This is exactly the kind of thing that would normally be briefed to the committees and something the committees would expect to be told about.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Covert North Korea Operation: A Tangled Web of Legal and Ethical Violations

In a revelation that reads more like a plot from a Cold War spy novel than a chapter from recent history, President Donald Trump's decision to authorize a covert military operation targeting North Korea has surfaced, casting a long shadow over his presidency and the United States' legal and ethical standing. According to a "bombshell report" by the New York Times, Trump's green light for SEAL Team 6's Red Squadron to infiltrate North Korea and plant surveillance equipment not only possibly breached U.S. law but also international law, highlighting a dangerous disregard for both diplomatic norms and human life.

Covert Operation Gone Awry

The operation, aimed at intercepting communications from North Korea's Supreme Leader, Kim Jong Un, turned fatal when the team reportedly engaged and killed two to three civilians mistaking them for a threat. These individuals, purportedly diving for shellfish, became unintended casualties in a mission that appears to have been as ill-conceived as it was executed. The tragic error underscores a grave violation of international law, both in terms of breaching North Korea's sovereignty and the extrajudicial killing of civilians.

Trump's Rogue Decision-Making

At the heart of this debacle is Trump's unilateral decision to authorize such a high-stakes operation without notifying key Congressional leaders, a move that not only deviates from standard protocol but "may have violated the law." This lack of notification raises serious concerns about the transparency and accountability of presidential powers, especially in matters of national security and covert operations. Matthew Waxman's insights to the New York Times crystallize the issue: the imperative to keep Congress informed about significant military undertakings is not just a matter of courtesy but a legal and democratic necessity.

A Spectacle of Diplomacy?

Ironically, this covert operation unfolded against the backdrop of Trump's highly publicized attempts to broker peace with North Korea—a nation with which the U.S. shares a tumultuous history. Trump's overtures towards diplomacy, including his unprecedented visit to North Korea, now seem at odds with his behind-the-scenes authorization of a military operation on North Korean soil. This dissonance between public diplomacy and covert aggression raises questions about the true intentions and strategies of the Trump administration regarding North Korea.

Legal and Ethical Implications

The report sheds light on a concerning pattern of behavior that skirts the edges of legal and ethical boundaries. The decision to proceed with a covert operation without Congressional notification, coupled with the resultant loss of civilian life, paints a picture of a presidency willing to bypass essential checks and balances. Moreover, it highlights the perils of a foreign policy approach that prioritizes secrecy and unilateral action over collaboration and transparency.

Conclusion

The fallout from this botched operation extends beyond the immediate legal and diplomatic repercussions; it serves as a stark reminder of the importance of adhering to both domestic law and international norms. As the United States navigates its complex relationship with North Korea and other global adversaries, this incident underscores the need for a foreign policy grounded in respect for human rights, legal standards, and the principles of democratic oversight. The bombshell revelations about Trump's covert operation in North Korea should prompt a thorough reevaluation of how such critical decisions are made and the mechanisms in place to ensure they are lawful, ethical, and in the nation's best interest.
