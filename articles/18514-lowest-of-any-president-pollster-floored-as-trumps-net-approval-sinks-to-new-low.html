‘Lowest of any president’: Pollster floored as Trump’s net approval sinks to new low

The Real Story Behind Trump’s Plummeting Poll Numbers

Data journalist and pollster Elliott Morris recently highlighted a striking downturn in President Donald Trump’s approval ratings, marking them as the lowest of any president at a similar stage in their term since the 1940s. According to the latest figures from FiftyPlusOne, Trump’s net approval has nosedived to -21.4, with only 37.2% of respondents approving of his job performance against a significant 58.6% disapproving.

Contextualizing Trump’s Unpopularity

It’s essential to dissect the roots of these figures to understand the broader implications. Morris points out that Trump’s historically low ratings aren’t the result of a singular scandal, unlike former President Richard Nixon during the Watergate scandal. Instead, Trump’s declining favorability stems from a series of presidential actions and external events that have collectively impacted public perception. These include the fallout from his tariffs, mass deportations, the government shutdown in October 2025, and an unpopular war.

The Cumulative Effect of Policy Decisions

Trump’s approach to governance, characterized by decisions that have often been perceived as unilateral and divisive, appears to be a significant factor in his waning public support. The tariffs, for instance, likely raised consumer prices and hurt American businesses, contributing to economic discontent. Similarly, the mass deportations and the government shutdown would have instilled a sense of instability and governmental dysfunction, further eroding trust in his leadership.

Morris’s analysis underscores a critical point: Trump’s plummeting approval ratings reflect more than just transient political woes; they signal a profound disconnection and dissatisfaction among the electorate. This isn’t merely about one poor decision; it’s about a series of decisions that cumulatively undermine public confidence and economic stability.

Institutional Responsibility

As we analyze these figures, it’s crucial to pinpoint who holds the reins of power. In this scenario, Trump, as the President, directly influences policies like tariffs and immigration enforcement. Therefore, the accountability for the resulting public sentiment squarely falls on his administration. It’s not just about the actions themselves but also about the perceived intent and responsiveness of the President to the ensuing public backlash.

Misdirected Blame and Scapegoating

Often in political discourse, there’s a tendency to attribute a leader’s low approval ratings to external factors or isolated incidents. However, Morris’s analysis clarifies that Trump’s unpopularity is not a sudden occurrence but a predictable outcome of sustained policy directions and leadership style. This ongoing situation reflects a deeper, systemic issue rather than mere episodic setbacks.

Conclusion: A Presidency Defined by Discontent?

Trump’s second term appears to be increasingly defined by a lack of economic security and a feeling among voters of not being heard. His consistent inability—or unwillingness—to address these concerns and adjust his policies accordingly not only shapes his presidency but also sets a precedent about the consequences of ignoring the electorate’s voice. The lesson here is clear: leadership that consistently disregards public sentiment and economic impact invites not just temporary disapproval but a legacy of discontent and disconnection.
