Trump will prompt 'historic reversal' of rights for all veterans if he wins Mark Kelly case

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 10, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ) looks on at a press conference calling for the release of the Epstein files, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 18, 2025. Picture taken November 18, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

Sen. Mark Kelly’s (D-Ariz.) lawsuit against President Donald Trump’s administration has far greater implications then the retired U.S. Navy captain’s military pension.

That’s according to a Monday essay by the Atlantic’s Missy Ryan, who wrote that all retired military personnel are likely following Kelly’s lawsuit very closely given what’s at stake. Attorneys representing the Department of Defense (DoD) are attempting to justify the Pentagon’s decision to cut Kelly’s retirement pay by claiming his video — which he made with with five other Democrats who served in the military or in intelligence agencies — by saying he undermined the chain of command and harmed military discipline.

Kelly has countered that he is protected by the U.S. Constitution’s Speech and Debate Clause, which prohibits members of Congress from being held liable for statements made as part of their duties as elected officials by any institution outside of Congress. And U.S. District Judge Richard Leon — an appointee of former President George W. Bush – has suggested that he’ll rule in Kelly’s favor. However, if he rules against Kelly, or if a ruling favorable to Kelly is eventually overturned on appeal, Ryan suggested it could have grave consequences for all veterans in the future.

“As someone who’s covered 10 secretaries of defense, I’ve never seen anything like what [Defense Secretary Pete] Hegseth is doing — not even close,” Ryan wrote. “If Hegseth gets his way, he won’t just be punishing Kelly; he will succeed in dramatically curbing the First Amendment rights of all military retirees, some of the very people who have fought to defend such freedoms.”

In the video itself, Kelly and others urged all members of the U.S. military and intelligence agencies to remember their duty to disobey illegal orders. The video was released prior to Hegseth overseeing an operation in which survivors of a boat strike in the Caribbean Sea clinging to debris from their wrecked vessel were ultimately blown apart in the water by a subsequent strike – which Maj. Gen. Steven J. Lepper (Ret.) said was a war crime.

Ryan wrote that the Trump administration’s attempts to punish Kelly and chill his speech are “straightforwardly outrageous and without merit,” according to several legal experts she interviewed for her article. She argued that regardless of how Judge Leon rules, the case represents a tipping point in the Trump administration’s quest for drastically expanded executive power.

“Expanding the military’s ability to control veterans’ speech would be a major and historic reversal of basic freedoms,” she wrote. “Retired service members turned public officials have a long history of criticizing Pentagon leaders in ways those leaders might find objectionable.”

“If the government prevails, the silencing of longtime service members would be like barring retired doctors from opining on health policy,” she added, referencing a comment from former U.S. Army lawyer and combat engineer Dan Maurer. “When debating matters of war and peace, he said, it serves the public to hear from the very people who have served on the front lines — and lived through the consequences.”

Our Analysis:

The Unwarranted Assault on Free Speech: The Case of Sen. Mark Kelly

In an era where the boundaries of free speech are being tested, the lawsuit involving Sen. Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) against President Donald Trump’s administration stands as a stark reminder of the lengths to which power will go to silence dissent, particularly from those with firsthand experience in military and defense. The implications of this legal battle extend far beyond Kelly’s military pension; they strike at the very core of First Amendment rights for all retired military personnel.

Missy Ryan of the Atlantic brings to light a deeply concerning narrative: the Department of Defense’s (DoD) attempt to justify the reduction of Kelly’s retirement pay. The justification? A video made by Kelly and five other Democrats, all of whom served in the military or intelligence agencies, that allegedly undermined the chain of command and harmed military discipline. However, this argument is not just flimsy; it’s a direct assault on the constitutional protections afforded to every American, particularly those who’ve served.

Kelly’s defense hinges on the U.S. Constitution’s Speech and Debate Clause, a provision designed to protect the independence and integrity of legislative discourse. This clause is not a mere technicality; it’s a fundamental pillar of democratic governance. U.S. District Judge Richard Leon’s inclination to rule in Kelly’s favor is a glimmer of hope in this otherwise bleak scenario. Yet, the potential for an unfavorable ruling—or an overturn on appeal—looms large, with catastrophic implications for the free speech rights of veterans.

Missy Ryan’s assertion that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s actions are unprecedented in their audacity is no understatement. Hegseth’s crusade against Kelly is not an isolated incident but a symptomatic manifestation of the Trump administration’s broader quest for expanded executive power. This quest is not only alarming but fundamentally antithetical to the principles upon which the United States was founded.

The video at the center of this controversy, wherein Kelly and his colleagues urge military and intelligence personnel to disobey illegal orders, is especially poignant. Given the subsequent operation overseen by Hegseth, which Maj. Gen. Steven J. Lepper (Ret.) labeled a war crime, the message of the video is not only justified but necessary. The Trump administration’s attempts to penalize Kelly for this act of conscience are, as Ryan rightly points out, “straightforwardly outrageous and without merit.”

The broader implications of this case cannot be overstated. To allow the military—or any branch of government—to control the speech of veterans is to undermine the very freedoms these individuals have fought to protect. These are not merely retired professionals; they are individuals with unique insights into the most pressing issues of national security and defense. Silencing them is akin to blinding society to the realities of war and peace, a move that benefits no one but those in power seeking to evade accountability.

In conclusion, the case of Sen. Mark Kelly is a litmus test for the state of free speech and democratic governance in the United States. It’s a reminder that the fight for freedom doesn’t end on the battlefield; sometimes, it begins anew at home, in the halls of justice and the court of public opinion. The outcome of this legal battle will have far-reaching consequences for all Americans, but particularly for those who have served. The silencing of these voices would not only be a travesty of justice but a national shame, signaling the erosion of the very liberties that define America.
