Trump racing to beat 'alarming trend' as MAGA base collapses: 'Potential wipeout'

President Donald Trump has little time to reverse course after shocking new polling results have revealed his base was slipping away from him ahead of midterms, according to an Axios report on Thursday.

Three new polls have revealed that Americans think the president is “so politically toxic” that they preferred former President Joe Biden‘s leadership more, putting Republicans further on edge.

“One year in, Trump has squandered virtually every advantage that won him the presidency. The White House has nine months to turn the ship around before a potential midterm wipeout for Republicans,” Axios reported.

Trump has been vocal critic of Biden throughout his campaign and throughout his first year in office. Despite his view, voters have different thoughts.

“Three national surveys point to the same alarming trend for a president who’s done everything in his power to erase his predecessor’s legacy,” according to Axios.

The new polls include Harvard CAPS/Harris, Rasmussen Reports and YouGov/Economist.

The Harvard CAPS/Harris poll was conducted from Jan. 28 to 29 and “found that 51% of registered voters say Trump is doing a worse job than Biden, compared with 49% who say he’s doing better.”

Trump-friendly pollster Rasmussen cited criticism from MAGA and discovered that 48% of potential voters believe Biden did a better job while in office compared to the 40% who said Trump has performed better during their polling from Feb. 2 to 4. And another 8% said they think both presidents have done “about the same.”

And a YouGov/Economist poll conducted from Feb. 6 to 9 revealed that 46% of American adults say Trump is doing worse than Biden, while 40% believe Trump is doing better than Biden, and 7% who think it’s “about the same.”

CNN polling analyst Harry Enten has pointed to Trump’s net approval rating on the economy (-18), which is 26 points lower than where he was at this time during his first term. It’s now 53 points lower among independents.

“If you know anything about Donald Trump, you know that he built his two presidential victories on winning voters [who are] without a college degree,” Enten said. “Well, Donald Trump’s base with non-college voters is absolutely collapsing.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tumultuous Polling Plunge: A Deep Dive

In a recent Axios report, we’ve been graced with the kind of polling data that reads more like a political thriller than a dry statistical analysis. President Donald Trump, the master of the political spectacle, appears to be facing an erosion of support so significant that it threatens not just his personal political future but that of the Republican Party as a whole. Let’s dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned critic.

The Stark Reality of Polling Data

The numbers, as they say, don’t lie. Three separate polls from Harvard CAPS/Harris, Rasmussen Reports, and YouGov/Economist have painted a picture so grim for Trump that it might as well have been drawn in charcoal. According to these polls, a majority of Americans now view Trump as “so politically toxic” that they harbor a nostalgic yearning for the days of Biden’s leadership. The irony should not be lost on us—Trump, who has dedicated an inordinate amount of energy to dismantling Biden’s legacy, is now less favorable in the eyes of the public.

The Harvard CAPS/Harris poll is particularly telling, with 51% of registered voters asserting that Trump is faring worse than Biden. Even more damning, Rasmussen Reports—often cited by conservatives as the gold standard of polling due to its perceived right-leaning bias—found that 48% of potential voters preferred Biden’s performance over Trump’s. Let that sink in.

The Crumbling Foundation

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of these polls for Trump is the revelation by CNN’s Harry Enten regarding Trump’s base. Trump’s victories were built on the backs of non-college-educated voters, a demographic that once seemed unshakeably loyal. Yet, according to Enten, this base is “absolutely collapsing.” This isn’t just a leak in the dam; it’s a full-scale structural failure.

Trump’s net approval rating on the economy, a cornerstone of his previous electoral successes, has plummeted to -18, a staggering 26 points lower than at a comparable point in his first term. Among independents, this figure is an astonishing 53 points lower. These numbers are not merely indicators of dissatisfaction; they are harbingers of a political reckoning.

The Bigger Picture

The Axios report frames this scenario as a dire warning for the Republican Party, suggesting that Trump has “squandered virtually every advantage that won him the presidency.” With only nine months to reverse this trend before the midterms, the GOP finds itself in a precarious position. The implications are clear: continue to tether the party’s fortunes to Trump’s polarizing brand, or undertake a painful but necessary recalibration.

Trump’s vocal criticism of Biden, which has been a constant refrain throughout his campaign and first year in office, now seems almost quixotic in light of these polling figures. Voters, it appears, are not buying what Trump is selling. This isn’t just a failure of messaging; it’s a fundamental misreading of the public’s priorities and concerns.

Conclusion: A Call to Action or a Swan Song?

The data speaks volumes about the shifting sands of American political allegiance. For Trump and the GOP, these polls could serve as a much-needed wake-up call, prompting a strategic pivot that might salvage their midterm prospects. Alternatively, they could represent the early verses of a swan song for Trump’s political relevance.

As we analyze these developments, let’s not lose sight of the broader implications. This isn’t merely a story about polling data; it’s a narrative about the fragility of political fortunes and the ever-present need for leaders to remain attuned to the will of the people. Trump’s apparent decline is a stark reminder that in politics, there are no guarantees, only opportunities to better serve and understand the electorate.

In the final analysis, these polls should not be viewed as just another political setback for Trump but as a clarion call for introspection and recalibration within the Republican Party. The road to the midterms, and beyond, promises to be fraught with challenges. How Trump and his party respond to this wake-up call could very well determine their place in the annals of American political history.
