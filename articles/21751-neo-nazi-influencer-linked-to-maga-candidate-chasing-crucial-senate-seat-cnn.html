Mike Collins and the Open Door Between Power and Hate

Power Is the Point

The story is not that a Republican Senate candidate knows a man with Nazi-friendly politics. The story is that Mike Collins has enough power, proximity, and institutional protection that this kind of association is treated as background noise instead of disqualifying evidence. Collins is a sitting congressman, Trump-endorsed, and now running for the Senate against Jon Ossoff. That is not a fringe position. It is a power position.

The reporting on David Alan Scheer II, Collins’ son-in-law, matters because it shows how far-right politics travels when it is allowed to move through family ties, campaign work, and partisan lenience. Scheer is not some anonymous troll on the internet. He appears close to Collins in the intimate, practical ways that matter: at the primary night party, helping with promotional work, living at a Collins-owned property. That is access. That is normalization.

Not A Mystery, A Network

Scheer’s posts are not ambiguous. The reporting describes white supremacist and neo-Nazi content, praise for Patriot Front, Holocaust inversion, antisemitic conspiracies, and genocidal logic dressed up as “questions.” That is not confusion. It is ideological garbage, and it is being circulated by someone inside Collins’ orbit.

The important fact is not merely that these ideas exist online. It is that a political household can absorb them without immediate collapse. That tells you what the surrounding ecosystem permits. When the barrier to entry for proximity to elected power is this low, extremism does not need a march through institutions. It just needs a family connection and a tolerant coalition.

The Candidate’s Own Record

Collins is not being dragged into this story by accident. He has already “come under fire” for sharing racist content, including a video of Ole Miss students taunting a Black protester with monkey noises. That matters because it changes the frame from guilt by association to pattern recognition.

A politician who shares racist material is not a passive victim of bad company. He is selecting and amplifying material that fits his politics. That does not prove every person around him shares the same views, but it does prove the boundary between “mainstream Republican” and overt racialized contempt is much thinner than the party would like to admit.

Misdirection For The Weak

What gets hidden in stories like this is the distribution of responsibility. The weaker actors are often the easiest targets: an influencer son-in-law, a viral clip, a social media trail. But the institutional actor is Collins, and the enabling structure is the Republican brand that still finds people like him viable.

That is where the real misdirection lives. The problem is not a random extremist drifting near power. The problem is power making room for extremism when it is useful, familiar, or politically convenient. Trump’s endorsement is not a footnote. It is the seal that says these politics remain acceptable inside the party’s governing machinery.

The Pattern Beneath The Scandal

This is not just about one congressman, one family, or one ugly set of posts. It is a picture of modern Republican normalization: racial resentment, antisemitic conspiracy, white grievance, and performative deniability all sitting inside the same political vehicle. The party does not need every officeholder to say the quiet part out loud. It only needs enough of them to keep moving the line.

Democrats are not the issue here in any symmetrical sense. The relevant fact is that the Republican side keeps rewarding figures who traffic in racist and authoritarian-adjacent politics while pretending the problem is tone, not power. That is how degradation becomes routine.

The System Works This Way

Collins’ circle is the point. Scheer’s posts are the symptom. Trump’s endorsement is the permission structure. Together they show a system that does not merely tolerate hate at the margins; it absorbs it when it can be folded into ambition, family loyalty, and partisan advantage.

That is the story. Not an accident. Not a misunderstanding. A political machine revealing what it will live with in order to keep winning.
