Trump’s trade war has seen nations fire back at the United States in a number of ways. India, for instance, was hit with a 50% tariff and responded by halting previously planned purchases of American-made weapons. Trump’s ongoing trade feud with China, however, has produced arguably the most impactful response.

China recently announced a full boycott of soybeans from the United States, an announcement that has left farmers enraged and Trump reportedly panicked.

“Not only is this absurd, it’s also crazy that no one in the Trump administration could have seen this coming,” said Shephard, a staff writer at The New Republic.

“Now they’re clearly scrambling. Trump is promising to rub some pennies together from the tariffs that he’s bringing in, but I think this is yet another sign that this trade war is devastating for a lot of people, many of whom are Trump voters.”

Soybeans make up a considerable share of all food products exported from the United States, with soybeans making up 14% of all agricultural exports. China has long been the top buyer of American soybeans, purchasing more than $12.5 billion’s worth in 2024, 50% of all American exports of the crop that year. However, China has not purchased a shipment since May, leaving farmers on the hook for 104 million bushels of soybeans and without their largest customer.

Trump has pledged to use some of the United States’ tariff revenue to offer a temporary lifeline to soybean farmers, but for Sargent, also a staff writer at The New Republic, this plan was a tepid admission of his own policies’ failures.

“This is a real calamity for those farmers. China bought over $12 billion worth of soybeans last year, and this year it’s nothing!” Sargent said.

“And when Trump says he will pay off the farmers with money from tariffs, well, that money was actually collected from importers who passed on higher costs to consumers, who also happen to be Trump voters, or many of them are anyway. He’s basically admitting he’s really screwing his own people here; he’s taking money from some of his own people and he’s going to give it to others to mask the deep failure of his policies.”

Our Analysis:

The Trade War Blunder: Trump’s Admission Through Desperation

In a recent podcast aired by The New Republic, journalists Greg Sargent and Alex Shephard dissected what appears to be a glaringly obvious misstep by President Donald Trump in the ongoing trade war, particularly highlighting the dire consequences for American farmers. The analysis presented isn’t just a critique; it’s a stark revelation of the self-inflicted wounds the Trump administration has managed to inflict upon its own voter base, through a policy that can only be described as shortsighted.

Trump’s Folly: A Self-Inflicted Wound***

Trump’s trade war, marked by a tit-for-tat tariff exchange, has undeniably backfired in spectacular fashion. The most striking retaliation comes from China, the erstwhile largest buyer of American soybeans, now imposing a complete boycott. This isn’t just a minor hiccup; it’s a devastating blow to American agriculture, particularly soybean farmers, who have been left stranded with a surplus of 104 million bushels. The fallout from this is not just economic—it’s political, with many of those affected being Trump’s own supporters.

The Absurdity of Unforeseen Consequences

Shephard’s observation that “no one in the Trump administration could have seen this coming” drips with sarcasm so thick it’s nearly palpable. But it’s more than just sarcasm; it’s a critical analysis of a fundamental lack of foresight. The administration’s failure to anticipate China’s response is not just absurd; it’s a failure of basic strategic planning. To describe the scramble to mitigate this disaster as rubbing “some pennies together” is to aptly highlight the inadequacy and desperation of Trump’s solution.

A Tepid Admission of Failure

The plan to use tariff revenue to support soybean farmers is, as Sargent points out, a “tepid admission” of Trump’s policy failure. This isn’t just robbing Peter to pay Paul; it’s robbing Peter, who is also Paul. The tariff revenue, drawn from importers who then pass the cost onto consumers, essentially means Trump is indirectly taxing American consumers to fix a problem he created. This cycle of taking money from one group of his supporters to pay another group, while failing to address the root cause of the issue, is emblematic of the administration’s shortsighted and self-harming economic policies.

The Real Calamity: Trump’s Economic Policy

The real calamity here, as both journalists make clear, is not just the immediate financial impact on farmers. It’s the broader implication of Trump’s economic policy, which seems to operate on a whim rather than strategic foresight. The administration’s approach to trade has not only alienated international partners but has also started to eat away at its own foundation, affecting those who had placed their trust in Trump’s ability to protect their interests.

Conclusion: A Self-Inflicted Economic Quagmire

The situation that Trump has maneuvered himself into is not just a quagmire but a self-inflicted one. The administration’s policies have not only failed to achieve their intended goals but have also managed to alienate and harm the very people they purported to protect. The trade war, particularly the skirmish with China over soybeans, serves as a stark reminder of the consequences of impulsive and ill-conceived policy decisions. It’s a cautionary tale of how not to engage in international trade, with Trump’s panicked responses only further highlighting the deep failures of his administration’s approach.
