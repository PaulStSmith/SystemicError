‘Stinks to high heaven’: Trump’s favorite newspaper sick of his family's corruption

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosJuly 2, 20263 min read

The Post Found the Rot It Helped Normalize

A Familiar Scandal, Only Closer to the Throne

The narrow news hook is simple: the New York Post, long one of Donald Trump’s most useful media shields, published an editorial blasting the Trump family’s enrichment after Trump returned to power. The paper pointed to a reported Kazakhstan mining deal, crypto profits tied to Trump family interests, and overlapping business with Howard Lutnick’s sons. It also invoked the Biden family scandal it once helped inflate, because hypocrisy is the only language this ecosystem speaks fluently.

Power Was Never in the Commentary Booth

The relevant power here is not in the editorial page. It sits in the White House and in the cabinet-level apparatus shaping policy. Trump controls the administration. His sons are not random relatives freelancing on the margins; they are family members operating in the shadow of presidential authority, cashing in where access, policy, and influence intersect. Lutnick’s sons matter here for the same reason: they are attached to government power through their father, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

That is the structure. Public office creates leverage. Family and associates monetize the leverage. Then friendly media pretends the problem is a lapse in standards rather than the business model.

The Post Is Angry About the Crime It Previously Armed

The Post’s complaint is not false because corruption is absent. It is compromised because the paper spent years helping manufacture the moral panic used to weaponize the Biden family story. That campaign leaned hard on insinuation, guilt by association, and the fantasy that Hunter Biden’s misconduct proved a criminal syndicate at the top of the Democratic Party. The source text notes that Republican investigations found no evidence Joe Biden profited from Hunter’s foreign dealings, yet the accusation lived on because accusation was the point.

Now the same machine stares at Trump-world self-dealing and discovers a principle it previously ignored: if family enrichment around a president is scandalous, then it was scandalous when Trump’s allies did it too. The paper is not suddenly principled. It is reacting to a scandal too blatant to fully bury.

The Real Story Is the Template

The most important detail in the source is not the Kazakhstan deal by itself, but the pattern the Daily Beast description identifies: a deal involving a UAE-linked entity purchasing a stake in World Liberty Financial, routing millions to Trump-family interests and to entities linked to Steve Witkoff. The source says that arrangement set a template that later helped produce an estimated $2.3 billion in digital-asset gains for the first family.

That is not incidental grift. That is an operating system. It suggests a political family using proximity to state power to create a private revenue stream, with crypto, foreign capital, and policy access all feeding one another. The administration does not merely tolerate the conflict. It becomes the condition for the conflict to flourish.

The Blame Shuffle Is the Point

Notice how these stories are always narrated downward. Corruption becomes “controversy.” Family profiteering becomes “deal-making.” Foreign money becomes “connections.” The article’s weakest framing is the idea that the scandal might simply “swamp” Trump’s final two years unless he “acts transparently.” That is institutional cowardice dressed as advice.

Transparency does not repair a system built to reward concealment. Once the president’s family and cabinet-linked relatives are profiting from policy-adjacent deals, the damage is already structural. The question is not whether the administration can clean it up. The question is whether the institutions around it have any remaining will to treat elite enrichment as corruption when the beneficiaries are politically protected.

Legacy Is Not the Issue

The Post warns that this could define Trump’s legacy. That is too polite. The legacy is the method itself: normalize the fusion of office and enrichment, weaponize scandal against enemies, then plead surprise when your own circle is caught doing the same thing at larger scale.

The broader political pattern is simple. In this system, power is not abused despite the branding. The branding is part of the abuse.
