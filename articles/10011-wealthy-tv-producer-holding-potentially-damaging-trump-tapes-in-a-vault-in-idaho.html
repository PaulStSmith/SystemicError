​Wealthy TV producer holding potentially damaging Trump tapes 'in a vault in Idaho'

Radio and television personality Billy Bush, who in 2005 was featured alongside President Donald Trump in the infamous Access Hollywood tape that saw Trump brag about grabbing women “by the p----,” said that a trove of still unseen videos exist but are currently in the possession of a wealthy television producer.

Speaking on the Jim Acosta Show Thursday, Bush provided details as to how and why the Access Hollywood tape was initially obtained, and how evidence exists that far more yet-unseen videos still exist.

“When [the Access Hollywood tape] came out, it was because a contestant in a Miss Universe pageant had said ‘Donald Trump has said terrible things about me,’ and he said ‘I never talk like that at all,’ and then NBC sent out to all their divisions ‘look through your tapes,’” Bush said. “Now, the ones they wanted belonged to Mark Burnett and are in a vault somewhere in Idaho, and those are all the outtakes from The Apprentice set.”

Burnett, a thirteen-time Emmy Award winner, has a long personal relationship with Trump, with Trump reportedly once calling Burnett a “special special friend.” Burnett also produced Trump’s popular reality television series “The Apprentice,” which ran from 2004 to 2017, hundreds of tapes of outtakes of which still exist and are in the possession of Burnett, Bush claimed.

Whether any of the unseen tapes will ever come to light, remains unclear. Burnett has denied being a supporter of Trump’s presidency, declaring himself in 2016 “I am not pro-Trump,” and that he and his wife “reject the hatred, division and misogyny that has been a very unfortunate part of his campaign.”

However, despite Burnett’s past claims, he was tapped by Trump as the special envoy to the United Kingdom, suggesting that the two may very well have made amends, and further suggesting that the television producer may be hesitant to cave to demands to release his trove of Trump tapes, which likely include well over a decade of outtakes and behind the scenes footage shot on the set of The Apprentice.

Our Analysis:

The Unseen Trump Tapes: A Vault of Controversy

The latest revelation by Billy Bush about a hidden trove of unseen videos featuring former President Donald Trump, brought to light on the Jim Acosta Show, is a tantalizing piece of information that begs for scrutiny. It’s a story that intertwines the complex web of celebrity, politics, and media manipulation, presenting a narrative ripe for examination.

The Source of the Scandal: Billy Bush’s recounting of the origin story behind the infamous Access Hollywood tape’s emergence paints a picture of reactive media excavation. Triggered by allegations of Trump’s derogatory comments, NBC’s scramble to find evidence corroborates a known pattern where media entities only dive deep when controversy knocks. It’s a reactive, rather than proactive, stance on uncovering the truth.

Mark Burnett’s Vault: The assertion that a cache of Trump outtakes, courtesy of his time on The Apprentice, is locked away in some Idaho vault under the watchful eye of Mark Burnett, is both intriguing and frustrating. Burnett, an acclaimed television producer and a “special special friend” to Trump, holds the key to potentially explosive content that could further illuminate Trump’s character—or lack thereof.

Burnett’s Dichotomy: Here lies the crux of the issue. Burnett’s public disavowal of Trump’s divisive campaign rhetoric contrasts sharply with his actions—namely, his appointment by Trump as the special envoy to the United Kingdom. This dual-faced stance is emblematic of the broader issue of powerful figures navigating the treacherous waters of political allegiance and public persona. Burnett’s refusal to release the tapes, despite his stated opposition to Trump’s “hatred, division and misogyny,” speaks volumes about the complexities of personal relationships and political expediency in the realm of celebrity and power.

The Unseen Evidence: The existence of potentially hundreds of hours of Trump’s unfiltered moments on The Apprentice set raises critical questions about accountability and the public’s right to know. If these tapes contain material that could have swayed public opinion or revealed more about Trump’s character before his presidency, their suppression is a disservice to the democratic process. However, the specter of these tapes also raises ethical questions about privacy, editing, and the manipulation of reality TV content for political purposes.

Conclusion: The saga of the unseen Trump tapes, as revealed by Billy Bush, lays bare the intricate dance between celebrity culture, political power, and media responsibility. While the public remains in the dark about the contents of Burnett’s vault, the mere knowledge of its existence is a reminder of the unseen forces shaping our perceptions of leaders. The intersection of media production, personal loyalty, and political influence is a fertile ground for systemic error, where the truth becomes a casualty of convenience and relationships. The potential impact of these tapes on historical and political discourse remains an open question, one that challenges our collective appetite for truth in the age of reality TV politics.
