The Blood Trail Doesn’t Lie: Right-Wing Violence and the Collapse of the “Both Sides” Myth

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 17, 20254 min read

For years, American political discourse has been held hostage by a poisonous false equivalence. Like a mantra for the cowardly and the complicit, "both sides" has become the go-to phrase for deflecting blame, avoiding accountability, and camouflaging one side's descent into fanaticism. But the blood trail is clear, and it runs in one direction.

A post on Quora doesn't pull punches, and for good reason. Its central claim? That political violence in the United States overwhelmingly comes from the right. Not from "both sides." Not from nameless radicals. From the American right. And now, after reviewing government data, terrorism databases, and public incident reports, it's time we cut through the rhetorical fog and face the documented facts.

A Body Count Drenched in Red

Right-wing violence isn’t a bug in the system. It is the system—the operating mechanism for a radicalized base, sustained by fear, hate, and deliberate disinformation.

Let’s start with the numbers:

  • The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) found that right-wing extremists were responsible for 57% of terrorist attacks in the U.S. between 1994 and 2020. In 2020 alone, over 90% of terrorist attacks came from right-wing actors.

  • The Anti-Defamation League (ADL) reported that 96% of extremist-related murders in the U.S. in the past decade were committed by right-wing individuals.

  • The FBI and DHS have repeatedly warned that white supremacist and anti-government extremists are the most persistent and lethal threats to American national security.

This isn’t conjecture. It’s the statistical footprint of a violent ideology, confirmed by every credible investigative agency in the United States.

Assassinations: A Reactionary Reflex

When Abraham Lincoln was shot in 1865 by Confederate actor and white supremacist John Wilkes Booth, the intent was clear: punish the president who had threatened the Southern slave economy. That wasn't the last time right-wing hate pulled a trigger.

  • Martin Luther King Jr. (1968) was assassinated by James Earl Ray, a segregationist incensed by the Civil Rights Movement.

  • Robert F. Kennedy (1968) was gunned down by Sirhan Sirhan, a Palestinian nationalist — not leftist, not Antifa, not BLM.

  • Gabby Giffords (2011) survived a gunshot to the head in an attack fueled by far-right anti-government paranoia.

  • Paul Pelosi (2022) was assaulted by a QAnon conspiracist echoing Trump’s rhetoric.

Each one of these incidents flowed from an ideology hostile to equality, progress, and democracy.

Mass Shootings: A Right-Wing Calling Card

If assassinations are the scalpel of political terror, mass shootings are the shotgun blast—brutal, theatrical, and devastating.

  • Oklahoma City (1995): Timothy McVeigh, a far-right militia extremist, murdered 168 people in the bombing of a federal building.

  • Charleston Church (2015): Dylann Roof killed 9 Black worshippers to ignite a race war.

  • Pittsburgh Synagogue (2018): Robert Bowers executed 11 Jews, blaming them for immigration. He cited right-wing conspiracy theories.

  • Buffalo Grocery Store (2022): The shooter livestreamed his anti-Black rampage, inspired by the racist "Great Replacement" theory.

The pattern is grotesque in its consistency: right-wing radicalization, often online, leads to manifestos, and then bullets.

The Culture War’s Casualties

Right-wing extremism doesn’t only target politicians. It spills into cultural spaces, punishing those who dare to be free, different, or proud.

  • John Lennon (1980) was assassinated by a man enraged by Lennon's criticism of Christianity and capitalism.

  • LGBTQ+ nightclubs, drag shows, and Pride events have become frequent targets of shooters indoctrinated by conservative outrage and religious moral panic.

These are not isolated events. They are calculated acts of violence designed to police culture through fear.

And the Left?

Here’s what right-wing propagandists don’t want to admit:

  • No mass shootings by Antifa.

  • No assassinations by Black Lives Matter.

  • Broken windows during protests? Sure. But that’s not terrorism. That’s not mass murder. That’s not church massacres or synagogue executions.

There is no equivalence. One side demands justice with placards. The other side answers with AR-15s.

The JFK Illusion

Some point to Lee Harvey Oswald, JFK’s assassin, as a leftist outlier. But Oswald’s self-described Marxism was never rooted in equity or liberation. He defected to the USSR, not for peace, but to embrace a surveillance state drenched in authoritarianism—not unlike the vision Trumpists peddle today:

  • State-run media

  • Anti-LGBTQ laws

  • Ultrareligious nationalism

  • Obsession with enemies and control

Oswald was no leftist. He was a proto-fascist in the making, and his actions mirrored a far-right worldview that cloaked itself in Cold War labels.

Final Verdict: The Lie of Symmetry

Let’s stop pretending. This isn’t about "disagreement." It’s about a side that has normalized political violence. A side that cheers on Kyle Rittenhouse, excuses January 6, and finds heroes in domestic terrorists.

They shoot. They bomb. They assassinate. They radicalize. And then they lie.

This is not "both sides." This is the American right’s war on democracy.

It’s time to name it. And fight it.
