'Dangerous': NY Times accused of causing 'great harm' with latest Trump headline

The Times has earned the ire of Trump for its numerous stories breaking news about his administrations, but readers have also accused the outlet of "normalizing" the only president with a criminal record boasting numerous felonies.

That criticism came to a head over the weekend, when the Times published a headline about Trump's recent address to cadets at West Point.

The Times' headline, Trump Gives Commencement Address at West Point, Stressing a New Era, took center stage on social media as readers accused the outlet of burying the most important aspects of the president's unconventional speech.

Climate scientist David Ho, for instance, put the Times headline juxtaposed with one from the Independent. The latter reads, "Trump gives rambling speech about trophy wives, golf and the ‘great late’ Al Capone in politically-charged West Point address."

"Hard to believe they're talking about the same event," Ho wrote.

Historian Kevin M. Kruse also chimed in on the comparison, asking, "Which news outlet is actually informing its readers about what happened?"

Conservative attorney and anti-Trump activist George Conway also weighed in, writing, "We make jokes about this sometimes because it’s laughable. But when journalists try to make sense of the nonsensical, rational out of the irrational, and coherence out of the incoherence, they affirmatively do the public interest great harm."

Political scientist and scholar Norman Ornstein agreed with Conway, saying, "Normalizing the abnormal is sadly normal for so much of the mainstream press."

"The NYT has done some terrific reporting and had some great opeds on Trump. But this framing, all too typical, is deeply dangerous," the scholar wrote on social media. "Imagine if Biden had given this kind of speech how they would’ve handled it. The double standard is journalistic malpractice."

Our Analysis:

The New York Times' Editorial Misstep: A Case of Dubious Normalization

The New York Times, a publication no stranger to controversy, has once again ignited the ire of its readership. This time, the spark was a headline that seemed to veer dangerously close to normalizing the behavior of Donald Trump, a president whose tenure and post-presidency have been anything but normal. The headline in question, "Trump Gives Commencement Address at West Point, Stressing a New Era," stands accused of glossing over the bizarre and politically charged nature of Trump's speech. This is not just a matter of editorial oversight; it's a symptom of a larger, more concerning trend within mainstream media.

The Headline That Stirred the Hornet's Nest

At the heart of the controversy is the stark contrast between The New York Times' headline and the content of Trump's speech, as well as how other news outlets reported on the same event. When compared to The Independent's headline, "Trump gives rambling speech about trophy wives, golf and the ‘great late’ Al Capone in politically-charged West Point address," it's hard not to see The Times' version as anything but a whitewash. This isn't just about differing editorial decisions; it's about what happens when a major news outlet appears to downplay the eccentricities of a figure like Trump.

Voices of Concern

The chorus of disapproval from various quarters only underscores the gravity of The Times' misstep. Climate scientist David Ho, historian Kevin M. Kruse, conservative attorney George Conway, and political scientist Norman Ornstein have all voiced their concerns, pointing out the dangerous precedent such editorial choices set. When journalists attempt to "make sense of the nonsensical" and present the irrational as coherent, they're not just failing in their duty to inform; they're actively participating in the erosion of public discourse.

The Danger of Normalizing the Abnormal

Ornstein's accusation of "journalistic malpractice" hits the nail on the head. The New York Times, for all its commendable reporting and insightful op-eds on Trump, falls into a dangerous trap with headlines like these. It's not merely about bias or a lack of objectivity; it's about the failure to adequately convey the gravity and peculiarity of the moment. When the abnormal is presented as routine, it desensitizes the public to behaviors and rhetoric that should, under no circumstances, be considered normal.

A Double Standard?

The hypothetical scenario posed by critics, imagining how The Times might have treated a similar speech given by Joe Biden, serves to highlight the potential double standard at play. It's a thought experiment that begs the question: Are we witnessing a form of journalistic malpractice grounded in a misguided attempt at neutrality? Or is it something more insidious—a reluctance to fully scrutinize figures like Trump for fear of backlash or accusations of bias?

Conclusion: A Call for Editorial Vigilance

The controversy surrounding The New York Times' headline is more than just a tempest in a teapot. It's a stark reminder of the responsibilities that come with editorial decision-making. In an era where the lines between the sensational and the factual are increasingly blurred, publications like The Times must exercise greater caution not to contribute to the normalization of behaviors and rhetoric that undermine the very fabric of democratic discourse. The readers deserve better, democracy demands more, and the press must rise to the occasion.
