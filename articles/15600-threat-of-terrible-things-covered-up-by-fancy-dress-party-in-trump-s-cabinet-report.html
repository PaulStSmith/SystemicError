And it’s covering up a grossly disturbing ineptitude for their jobs, she added.

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem — dubbed “ICE Barbie” — has been seen in firefighting gear, bulletproof ICE vests, cowboy hats and shouldering rifles.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth posts pictures of himself deadlifting and doing push-ups in the snow with troops. Dan Bongino, the deputy director of the FBI, spars on wrestling mats

Trump’s “underlings perform near-daily tone poems to a certain type of MAGA masculinity, publicly pantomiming their professional responsibilities,” wrote Ashley Parker.

And it’s all in a desperate attempt to catch their boss's attention and persuade the public they’re doing a job that they're actually unqualified to do, she added.

The dress-up parties are even get push back from conservatives. Right-wing radio host Erick Erickson told the Atlantic that Trump is an “image guy” who wants to surround himself with people out of“central casting.”

But, he said, it “distracts the voters from: Is stuff actually going well behind the scenes?”

“It’s like hiring the guy who plays a doctor on Grey’s Anatomy. You don’t actually want that guy to do your heart surgery. He’s an actor. You hire the people who sound competent because they use the polysyllabic words. But can they actually do the job?”

The message comes from Trump himself who, during his most recent campaign, posed as a McDonald worker and as a garbage truck driver. Much of his business persona was sold to the public in his role as high-flying mogul on The Apprentice.

The same trick has been adopted by his underlings who “sometimes give the impression that they view government work more as a game than as true public service,” wrote Parker.

“It looks like a lot of them are sort of showing up at a government costume party in which they get to wear the costume of being the secretary of defense or the costume of being the director of national intelligence, but they don’t have the qualification for those roles,” Rhode Island Democrat Senator Sheldon Whitehouse told the Atlantic.

“Part of it is they know the point of entry to the costume party is you have to suck up ferociously to Trump every minute, and to get on his radar, images help. He likes the fake macho imagery, and so that’s just part of the deal.”

But he warned,”If you’re not a serious person, and you’re in a serious job, there’s this enormous gap of competence through which terrible things can happen.”

Our Analysis:

The Masquerade of Competence: Trump's Cabinet Dress-Up Fiasco

In an era where image often trumps substance, the theatrics employed by Donald Trump's senior Cabinet members take this adage to a ludicrous extreme, morphing governance into a grotesque costume party. The article in discussion paints a vivid picture of this farcical reality, where the quest for the president's approval and public adoration supersedes the solemn duties attached to their high offices. Let's dissect this tragicomedy with the precision of a scalpel, cutting through the layers of pretense to reveal the disturbing ineptitude lurking beneath.

The Charade of MAGA Masculinity

The spectacle of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, dubbed "ICE Barbie," donning firefighting gear and cowboy hats, or Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth showcasing his physical prowess alongside troops, is not just absurd; it's an insult to the intelligence of the American public. These are not isolated incidents of patriotic display but a concerted effort to embody a "certain type of MAGA masculinity." This act, as pointed out by the Atlantic columnist Ashley Parker, is a desperate bid for attention from their boss and an attempt to dupe the public into believing they are fit for their roles.

The Illusion of Competence: The comparison to hiring an actor from "Grey’s Anatomy" to perform heart surgery, as Erick Erickson aptly puts it, underscores the dangerous gamble of prioritizing image over ability. The message is clear: this administration is more concerned with the optics of power than the responsible exercise of it. The mimicry extends to the top, with Trump himself posing as a McDonald worker and a garbage truck driver, perpetuating the facade that he is a man of the people, all while his underlings play dress-up in roles they are woefully unqualified for.

The Consequences of the Farce

Senator Sheldon Whitehouse's remarks encapsulate the peril of this situation. The "gap of competence" he speaks of is not just a void; it's a chasm where "terrible things can happen." When those at the helm of national security, defense, and intelligence view their positions as roles in a government-themed masquerade ball, the stakes are alarmingly high. This is not merely about unqualified individuals occupying positions of power; it's about the systematic erosion of the very fabric of governance, where loyalty to Trump and adherence to his cult of personality trump qualifications and competence.

A Disturbing Reflection

What does this say about the state of American politics? When did the solemn duty of public service devolve into a desperate scramble for the affections of a leader obsessed with appearances? This administration's embrace of spectacle over substance is a troubling indicator of its priorities, where the real work of governance is secondary to the theatrics of power.

The obsession with dressing up and the resultant incompetence is not just a quirky footnote in the annals of this administration; it's a glaring symptom of a deeper malaise. It speaks to a culture where the superficial is celebrated, where the semblance of action is preferred over genuine progress, and where, ultimately, the American people are the ones who suffer the consequences.

In this grotesque theater of the absurd, the Trump administration has laid bare the vulnerabilities of American democracy, where the unqualified are elevated, and the qualified are sidelined, all in the service of vanity. As the curtain falls on each act of this farcical display, one can't help but wonder: when will the audience demand a return to reality?
