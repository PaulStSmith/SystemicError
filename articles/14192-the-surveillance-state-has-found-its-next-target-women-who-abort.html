The Surveillance State Has Found Its Next Target: Women Who Abort

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The dystopia isn’t coming. It’s here, and it has a badge.

Across Texas, law enforcement agencies are deploying license plate readers (ALPRs) not just to track stolen vehicles or hunt fugitives, but to identify and pursue individuals suspected of obtaining abortions. A recent and deeply troubling allegation from Johnson County paints a picture of local police spending a month hunting down a woman who they merely suspected had terminated a pregnancy. No warrant. No court order. Just a hunch and a uterus.

The excuse? “For her safety.”

Let’s be crystal clear: this has nothing to do with safety. This is about control.

Since the fall of Roe v. Wade, Texas has become a testbed for a chilling new era of reproductive surveillance. Empowered by SB8 and subsequent legislative extremism, law enforcement is now emboldened to use invasive surveillance tools against private citizens on the mere suspicion of engaging in constitutionally contested behavior. The barrier between authoritarian overreach and lawful policing has never been thinner.

What once would have been dismissed as fear-mongering fiction is now plausible reality: ALPRs can scan millions of plates a day. Cross-reference those scans with data on visits to abortion clinics out-of-state, and suddenly you have a dragnet targeting pregnant people exercising bodily autonomy.

Let that sink in: the same tools used to find child abductors and dangerous criminals are now allegedly being aimed at women leaving medical clinics. And in Texas, that alone is enough to spark a manhunt.

What is perhaps most grotesque is the ease with which this apparatus can be turned on the public. Unlike wiretaps or home searches, license plate data doesn't require a warrant in most jurisdictions. ALPR logs are often shared across departments, stored for months or years, and run through AI filters that can flag "suspicious patterns."

In other words, suspicion isn’t based on evidence—it’s based on data trails. Combine that with bounty laws that reward citizens for ratting out abortion seekers, and what you get is a 21st-century witch hunt powered by silicon and state violence.

To those who say "if you’ve done nothing wrong, you have nothing to fear," let's flip the script: would you be fine with cops tracking your car because you went to a therapist? A gun shop? A political rally? Once the infrastructure for reproductive surveillance is in place, it won’t stay confined to abortions.

You can already hear the justifications being preloaded: "It's for public health." "It's about saving lives." "It's only used in extreme cases." But if Johnson County's alleged month-long dragnet is any indication, the truth is far more sinister: no oversight, no accountability, and no limit to how far the state will go when the target is a pregnant woman daring to choose.

And make no mistake—this is not just about Texas. Other red states are watching. The same lawmakers who cried about mask mandates and vaccine requirements are now fine with militarized surveillance of wombs. This isn’t hypocrisy. This is hierarchy. It's about enforcing a rigid, patriarchal order where women are not citizens with rights but vessels to be regulated.

Where does it end?

With data brokers selling your fertility status to police departments? With prosecutors demanding geolocation data from rideshare apps? With vigilantes using ALPR info to stalk and harass women across state lines?

If this sounds paranoid, it shouldn't. We've seen time and again how technology built for one purpose gets repurposed for oppression. The PATRIOT Act wasn't meant to justify spying on protesters. Facial recognition wasn't sold as a tool to identify immigrants. But here we are.

Abortion is now the frontline of the American surveillance state.

The right to privacy—a concept conservatives once championed when it came to gun ownership and corporate deregulation—is now conveniently forgotten when it comes to women's health. The same political movement that rails against "big government overreach" is gleefully turning law enforcement into reproductive bounty hunters.

We are not talking about a rogue cop or a one-time excess. We are talking about a system that is being designed, funded, and normalized to criminalize half the population.

If we do not raise hell now, we’ll wake up in a future where the question isn't whether abortion is legal but whether you can exist as a free woman at all.

Texas cops aren’t just enforcing the law. They are defining a new one—one where your uterus puts you on a watchlist.

And that should terrify everyone.
