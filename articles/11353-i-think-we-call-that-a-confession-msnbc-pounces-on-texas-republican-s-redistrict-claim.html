'I think we call that a confession': MSNBC pounces on Texas Republican's redistrict claim

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unvarnished Truth Behind Texas Redistricting

Texas State Rep. Todd Hunter (R) — (MSNBC screenshot)

A comment made by a hectoring Republican lawmaker as a justification for redistricting the state drew the attention of a panel on MSNBC on Thursday morning.

Hours after the Texas legislature shoved through their plan to set themselves up for flipping a projected five seats in the U.S. House of Representatives in the 2026 midterms, the co-hosts of MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” shared a clip of State Rep. Todd Hunter (R), who represents Nueces County, lecturing Democrats who objected to the power play ordered by Donald Trump.

Citing the Supreme Court, Hunter, a former Democrat, stated, “You want transparency? Here's the U.S. Supreme Court legal transparency. The underlying goal of this plan is straightforward: improve Republican political performance.”

“I mean, he's just very transparent about that, what the plan is. They would now like to know what the plan is to wear a black tie on a black shirt in Texas in August,” co-host Joe Scarborough joked about Hunter's clothes before adding, “I don't, I just don't know many people in the south to do that. But, anyway...”

“Claire, I think we call that a confession,” co-host Willie Geist interjected.

Former U.S. Senator Claire McCaskill (D-MO), who also served as a prosecutor, replied, “Yeah! I expected Perry Mason to pop up because he always gets the confessions, right?”

‘And, you know, and the sad thing is part of me has to acknowledge the guy's right about this,” she added. “The legacy of the [Chief Justice John] Roberts court is going to end up being not what Roberts wanted it to be, because the decisions they made in Shelby and other cases that basically, this couldn't have happened before until the Supreme Court said, ‘No, we're kind of tired of that old idea that you can't bust up Black districts to deny Black representation.’ The Supreme Court said, ‘Yeah, now you can bust up Black districts and try to, you know –– basically that's what they're doing around the country.”

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth Behind Texas Redistricting

In an era where political subtlety is often traded for the blunt force of power plays, a recent comment by Texas State Rep. Todd Hunter (R) has stripped the facade off the Republican strategy for redistricting in the state. During an MSNBC segment, the "Morning Joe" co-hosts played a clip that should be ringing alarm bells for anyone with a vested interest in the integrity of the democratic process.

Hunter's remark, "The underlying goal of this plan is straightforward: improve Republican political performance," is so brazen, it's almost admirable. Almost. Here we have, in crystal-clear high definition, a lawmaker unabashedly admitting that the purpose of redistricting is not to ensure fair representation or to correct demographic shifts, but to cement Republican power. This isn't a dog whistle; it's a foghorn.

The co-hosts' reaction, particularly the banter about Hunter's fashion choices, might seem frivolous, but it underscores the surreal nature of the admission. It's as if the audacity of the statement temporarily short-circuited their political commentary instincts, reducing them to commenting on sartorial choices. Yet, Willie Geist's remark, "I think we call that a confession," and former U.S. Senator Claire McCaskill's agreement, turn the conversation back to the gravity of the situation.

McCaskill's observation about the legacy of the Chief Justice John Roberts-led Supreme Court is especially poignant. The decisions mentioned, notably Shelby County v. Holder, have effectively dismantled key protections under the Voting Rights Act, enabling the very gerrymandering tactics Hunter so candidly described. The Court's rulings have laid the groundwork for the fragmentation of districts along racial lines, undermining the representation of Black communities and others of color.

This isn't just a Texas issue; it's a stark illustration of a nationwide strategy to manipulate electoral boundaries for partisan gain. The audacity of Hunter's statement should not be lost in the noise of political discourse. It's a rare moment of honesty, revealing the cynical calculations at work behind the scenes. The implications of such tactics are profound, affecting the foundational principle of equal representation in a democracy.

The MSNBC panel's discussion, while momentarily sidetracked by lighter commentary, ultimately highlights a critical issue: the erosion of democratic norms and the Supreme Court's complicity in that process. The laughter about Hunter's attire fades quickly in the face of the realization that the joke, ultimately, is on the American electorate.

In sum, Hunter's admission and the subsequent analysis on "Morning Joe" provide a damning insight into the current state of American politics, where power is pursued at the expense of principle. The Texas redistricting plan, as laid bare by Hunter, is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a broader, systemic issue that threatens the very core of democratic representation. As observers, commentators, and participants in this democracy, the response to such revelations should be one of concern, action, and a steadfast commitment to restoring integrity to the electoral process.
