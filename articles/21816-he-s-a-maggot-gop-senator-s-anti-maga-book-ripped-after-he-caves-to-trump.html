'He's a maggot': GOP senator's anti-MAGA book ripped after he caves to Trump

The Book Deal Is Not Resistance

The real power was never missing

The story here is not that Thom Tillis may eventually publish a book. The story is that Republicans are still confirming Trump-aligned officials while pretending later memoirs count as accountability. Power sits with the senators who vote, the officials who execute, and the institutions that keep functioning after they are bent toward abuse. A future tell-all does nothing to stop a present-day machinery that is already in motion.

Blanche is the point

Steve Schmidt’s sharpest claim is the one that matters: Todd Blanche is not some neutral technocrat dropped into the Justice Department by accident. In the source, Schmidt ties Blanche to a broader pattern of weaponized federal law enforcement, alongside Pam Bondi and Kristi Noem, aimed at harassing, prosecuting, and trying to lock up innocent people. That is not administrative drift. It is deliberate state aggression dressed up as normal governance.

The Senate is enabling, not observing

Tillis is not outside the system criticizing it from the safety of a kitchen table. He is inside it, participating in it, and then auditioning for post hoc moral clarity. That is the ugliest bipartisan habit in Washington: vote for the harm first, market your regret later. Schmidt is right to treat that as hollow. A book written after the damage is done is not courage. It is branding.

Cowardice with a spine of law

What makes this worse is not only the Trump operation itself, but how easily Republican senators normalize it. The source says it is “inexcusable” that any U.S. senator would tolerate this conduct, and that is exactly the scale of the problem. The abuse is not hidden. It is being processed through official channels by people who know better and proceed anyway. That is not confusion. It is institutional cowardice with a procedural veneer.

The article is useful because it names the rot

The source framing is strongest when it refuses the comforting fiction that everyone is merely trapped by events. Schmidt’s comparison to a Nazi general is not there to flatter anyone; it is there to distinguish between participation, refusal, and belated denunciation. Some people do not become heroes. Fine. But at minimum, they can stop helping. Tillis, as described here, has not done even that.

The larger pattern

This is what democratic decay looks like in practice: elected Republicans keep the machinery running, then look for future language to distance themselves from the consequences. The book, the interview, the late condemnation all serve the same function if they leave current power untouched. Moral theater is not resistance. The system does not change when the people inside it reserve their conscience for the afterlife of a scandal. It changes when institutional enablers stop laundering authoritarian harm as hesitation, nuance, or regret.
