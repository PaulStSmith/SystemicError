Trump targets progressive Dem’s wife amid online posting frenzy

Trump Uses the Presidency Like a Megaphone for Cruelty

A Target Chosen for Signal, Not Substance

Trump’s Saturday posting spree did two things at once: it singled out the wife of Democratic Senate nominee Abdul El-Sayed with an Islamophobic comparison image, and it pushed a stale, evidence-free claim about vandalism at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool that his own handpicked prosecutor has said was false. He also mixed in vanity content about a proposed “Drone Port,” his golf clubs, and statue renovations. The point was not information. The point was dominance.

The Real Power Is in the Hands That Post

The institutional power here belongs to Trump. He is not some outside troll shouting into the void. He is the president, using a platform he controls to set the terms of attention, target political enemies, and launder prejudice into public spectacle. When a sitting president chooses to circulate a candidate’s family and religious identity as a political weapon, that is not random online ugliness. It is executive power behaving like private spite.

The Lie Is the Method

The Reflecting Pool claim matters because it shows how this operation works. Trump repeats an allegation without evidence. A prosecutor he chose says it is not true. The falsehood remains in circulation anyway, because the purpose is not verification. The purpose is to keep a hostile story alive long enough to harden into ambient suspicion. That is how propaganda works when it does not need to persuade everyone, only to contaminate the field.

The Target Is Democracy’s Edge, Not Its Center

El-Sayed is running as a Democratic Senate nominee, which means the target is not just one family but a coalition trying to defend democratic institutions from authoritarian politics. Trump’s image choice was not subtle. The use of a traditional Islamic headscarf, paired with a contrast against the Trumps’ formal attire, is a familiar tactic: racialize, religiously mark, and imply foreignness without having to say the slur out loud. The cruelty is deliberate because the deterrent effect is the point.

Noise Is the Cover for Control

The volume matters. Seventeen posts in under two hours is not a lapse in judgment; it is a strategy of saturation. The Financial Times and Daily Beast figures cited in the source show the pattern clearly: sustained daily posting, intensified bursts, and late-night surges. This is what political attention looks like when it has been industrialized. Flood the channel, crowd out scrutiny, and make every other institution react to the president’s latest provocation instead of governing on its own terms.

The Pattern Is the Story

This is not a glitch in the system. It is the system revealing itself. A president with direct access to mass communication can use public power to humiliate opponents, circulate falsehoods, and convert prejudice into political theater while pretending it is just another post. The larger pattern is not chaos for its own sake. It is governance by degradation: weaken truth, personalize power, and make cruelty feel routine.
