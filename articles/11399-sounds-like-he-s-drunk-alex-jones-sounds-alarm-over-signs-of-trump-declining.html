'Sounds like he's drunk': Alex Jones sounds alarm over 'signs of Trump declining'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Unhinged: Alex Jones on Trump's Health

The Alex Jones Show/screen grab

Pro-MAGA conspiracy theorist Alex Jones told his viewers that President Donald Trump had "serious" health problems after the commander-in-chief expressed concerns about getting into heaven.

A day after Trump told Fox News that he was "not doing well" and wanted to "get to heaven if possible," Jones insisted he had a duty to sound the alarm "even if it could be used to overall hurt our pro-human liberty agenda."

"I've seen a lot of signs of Trump declining," the right-wing host admitted. "And so he's on a lot of the time, but like a light bulb is starting to go out. It gets brighter. It gets dim. It goes in and out."

Jones pointed to the president's swollen ankles as a sign of his poor health.

"That's not a good sign for the heart and the rest of the body, because the rest of him, he's lost weight, he's not that fat," he insisted. "For everybody that you've known that's having heart problems, that's not good right there. Okay? And they're getting bigger and bigger. I would say, I don't say, I know, we have a crisis."

According to Jones, "Trump is unconscious, basically, for periods of the day now."

"But you can watch the decline into the evening, and then also these morning interviews," he explained. "So it's late at night, he sounds like he's drunk. He doesn't drink, obviously. He's exhausted. And then it is early in the morning, he doesn't sound too hot."

"And this is just one of many things I've been hearing and seeing him do that send up red flags."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Unhinged: Alex Jones on Trump's Health

In a recent twist that could only make sense in the bizarro world of fringe conspiracy theories, Alex Jones, a name synonymous with far-right lunacy and unfounded claims, has turned his sights on none other than former President Donald Trump. Trump, a figure often idolized to messianic levels by the same cohorts that hang on Jones' every word, has apparently exhibited signs of declining health, or so Jones claims. This proclamation is as rich in irony as it is in hypocrisy, coming from a man whose career is built on the shaky ground of misinformation and sensationalism.

Jones' Alarmist Health Diagnosis

Let's dissect the layers of absurdity here. Jones, with the medical expertise of a garden gnome, asserts that Trump's health is in serious jeopardy, citing swollen ankles as a red flag for heart issues. This leap from a common symptom, which can arise from a myriad of less sinister causes, to a dire health crisis is emblematic of Jones' approach to information: see a spark, scream fire.

The description of Trump's condition, with fluctuating energy levels and periods where he is "basically unconscious," suggests a narrative more befitting a Victorian novel than a serious discourse on someone's health. Jones' dramatic flair for the morose would almost be entertaining if it weren't so recklessly misleading.

The Irony of Concern

The irony of Alex Jones, of all people, expressing genuine concern for Trump's health under the guise of it potentially "hurting our pro-human liberty agenda" is palpable. This is, after all, the same Alex Jones who has promoted some of the most dangerous and unfounded conspiracy theories in recent history, often with a blatant disregard for the truth and the well-being of those he targets.

It's almost as if Jones has momentarily forgotten his usual role as a purveyor of paranoia to play doctor—albeit, one based more in the realm of fiction than fact. The mention of Trump's "decline" and erratic interview performances as evidence of his failing health ignores a simpler explanation: that perhaps the pressures of leadership and the weight of numerous controversies have taken their toll on Trump, in the same way they would on any mortal man.

Conclusion: The Bigger Picture

This entire episode is a fascinating study in how conspiracy theorists like Jones operate. They latch onto any thread, no matter how tenuous, and weave it into their larger tapestry of deceit and distortion. The real crisis here isn't Trump's health—about which we should remain skeptical of any diagnosis from the likes of Jones—but the continued influence of conspiracy theorists who manage to keep their audience in a perpetual state of fear and agitation.

In the end, Jones' sudden concern for Trump's well-being serves as a reminder of the strange bedfellows politics can create. It also highlights the lengths to which figures like Jones will go to remain relevant and in the spotlight, even if it means biting the hand that feeds them. As always, when it comes to the world of Alex Jones, it's wise to approach with a healthy dose of skepticism—and perhaps a pair of ankle weights, just in case.
