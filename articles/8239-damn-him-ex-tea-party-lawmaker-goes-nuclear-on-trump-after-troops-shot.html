Former Rep. Joe Walsh (R-IL) blasted Donald Trump on Friday over the death of Sarah Beckstrom, the West Virginia National Guard member who was fatally shot near the White House earlier this week.

Walsh – once a Tea Party congressman and now an outspoken Trump critic – said the 20-year-old soldier should never have been stationed in the nation’s capital to begin with.

“Army Spc. Sarah Beckstrom should be alive. Air Force Staff Sgt. Andrew Wolfe should not be in a hospital fighting for his life,” Walsh wrote in a searing post on X. “There’s no f-----g reason for National Guard troops to be in DC. There’s no f-----g reason at all. There never has been.”

He added: “Damn Trump. Damn him.”

Beckstrom was one of two National Guard members shot in a Nov. 26 ambush-style shooting in Washington, D.C., according to media reports. Both members had been serving in D.C. since August as a part of Trump’s so-called crackdown on crime.

Walsh and other Trump critics argue the continued presence of national guard troops is unnecessary and places young servicemembers in harm’s way

Our Analysis:

The Misguided Militarization of American Streets: A Tragedy Under Trump’s Watch

Former Representative Joe Walsh, a figure who has journeyed from a Tea Party enthusiast to an ardent critic of Donald Trump, has unleashed a scathing indictment of the former president following the tragic death of Sarah Beckstrom, a young National Guard member. Beckstrom’s death, resulting from an ambush-style shooting near the White House, has reignited the debate over the deployment of military personnel on American soil—a policy aggressively championed by Trump under the guise of a crackdown on crime.

The Unnecessary Sacrifice of Sarah Beckstrom

Walsh’s outrage, articulated through a raw and unfiltered post on X, underscores a critical and painful reality: Sarah Beckstrom should be alive today. The deployment of National Guard troops in Washington, D.C., initially framed as a temporary measure to address civil unrest, has morphed into a seemingly indefinite occupation. Walsh’s condemnation is not just a critique of Trump’s policy but a lament for the young lives put at risk by what he sees as a pointless and politically motivated show of force.

The Flawed Premise of Trump’s “Crackdown on Crime”

The so-called crackdown on crime, as touted by Trump, has led to the continued presence of National Guard troops in the nation’s capital, a decision that now appears more as a tactic of fearmongering than a genuine attempt at ensuring public safety. The incident that claimed Beckstrom’s life and left Air Force Staff Sgt. Andrew Wolfe fighting for his in a hospital bed is a grim testament to the dangers of militarizing public spaces. It begs the question: What crime wave necessitated such a prolonged military presence? Critics, including Walsh, argue that the justification for this deployment is tenuous at best and disastrous at worst.

A Reckoning for Reckless Decisions

Walsh’s unequivocal message—”Damn Trump. Damn him.”—is more than a personal rebuke; it’s a call for a broader reckoning with the consequences of Trump’s tenure. The decision to station National Guard troops in D.C. has transformed the capital into a battleground, not against crime, but against the very citizens these measures purport to protect.

The tragedy of Sarah Beckstrom is not just the loss of a young soldier; it’s a stark reminder of the perils of political leaders using the military as pawns in their games of power. The continued militarization of American cities under the pretext of law and order does not just undermine civil liberties—it endangers lives.

In the wake of this needless loss, one must question the rationale behind such deployments and demand accountability for the decisions that lead to such outcomes. The narrative spun by Trump and his adherents about the necessity of a strong military presence on American streets has been pierced by the harsh reality of Beckstrom’s death. It’s a narrative that must be challenged, dissected, and ultimately, rejected.

The debate over the role of the military in civilian life is far from over, but let the loss of Sarah Beckstrom serve as a sobering reminder of the stakes involved. It’s time to reassess not just the efficacy but the morality of domestic military deployments. If anything, Walsh’s fiery condemnation should ignite a broader dialogue about the values and visions that guide our nation’s policies and priorities. The cost of silence and inaction, as we’ve tragically seen, is far too high.
