A decision by Donald Trump’s administration to ice out Minnesota authorities from having access to evidence in the shooting of 37-year-old nurse Alex Pretti in late January by Department of Homeland Security agents was thoroughly trashed on MS NOW Tuesday.

On Monday, Minnesota’s Bureau of Criminal Apprehension (BCA) issued a statement saying the FBI made a formal notification that it would not share any of the accumulated information or evidence related to Pretti’s shooting.

That led Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz (D) to respond, “Trump’s left hand cannot investigate his right hand. The families of the deceased deserve better.”

On MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” there was unanimous agreement among the panelists that there is a cover-up that stretches from the FBI to DHS Secretary Kristi Noem to the White House itself.

As co-host Mika Brzezinski put it, “In one case, Stephen Miller called Alex [Pretti] a ‘would-be assassin.’ This seems even more brazenly corrupt with the appearance of zero interest in accountability. Zero. Absolutely none.”

“These renegade agency heads who have been lying through their teeth from the very beginning, have these agents who behaved like thugs, behaved improperly. And that, of course, we heard that actually at a Senate hearing earlier this week from people in the department themselves that they acted in an extraordinarily unprofessional way,” co-host Joe Scarborough contributed.

Co-host Willie Geist suggested, “They’re saying inside [state officials] that this is hampering their own investigation, because the FBI is holding so much of the evidence that they can’t actually get. They think they know what happened, they’ve seen the videos, but they don’t have all the evidence. They need to bring it to trial. So this is clearly the federal government saying we have something to hide here. Therefore, we’re not going to share all of our evidence with the state.”

According to Scarborough, Minnesota officials need to take the next step, assemble what evidence they have, and approach a Minnesota judge to put the federal government on the spot.

“I think the state of Minnesota, if they believe there are charges, they need the information that they have, and they have a lot of information, they have a lot of eyewitnesses, they have a lot of video,” he advised. “I think they need to go ahead and bring the charges and then go to a judge and have the judge, through the discovery process, make the feds give up the evidence that they have because they do not have a legitimate cause that will stand up in court for covering up these two killings.“

“In fact, that’s really the only justification they’re going to have to go in front of a Minnesota judge and say, ‘Your honor, we at this time cannot turn the evidence of these two killings over to the state because we, the federal government, are actively involved in a cover-up of their killings’,’” he predicted.

Our Analysis:

The Unveiling of a Cover-Up: The Case of Alex Pretti

In a brazen display of obfuscation and power play, the Trump administration’s decision to withhold crucial evidence from Minnesota authorities regarding the shooting of Alex Pretti, a 37-year-old nurse, by Department of Homeland Security agents, exposes a disturbing narrative of governmental overreach and a flagrant disregard for accountability. This case, dissected with fervor on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” reveals layers of a potential cover-up that seemingly permeates from the corridors of the FBI through to the DHS, and shamelessly up to the White House.

The Stonewalling of Justice

The FBI’s formal notification to Minnesota’s Bureau of Criminal Apprehension (BCA) that it would not share any information or evidence related to Pretti’s shooting is not just a procedural hiccup; it’s a calculated move. Governor Tim Walz’s poignant critique, “Trump’s left hand cannot investigate his right hand,” underscores the inherent conflict of interest and the deep-rooted issues within the administration’s operations. The denial of evidence to state authorities not only hampers the investigation but reeks of a desperate attempt to shield agency actions from public scrutiny.

The Theater of the Absurd: White House Edition

The narrative takes a darker turn with Stephen Miller’s branding of Alex Pretti as a ‘would-be assassin.’ This unfounded allegation by a senior advisor to the President is not merely inflammatory; it’s a deliberate diversion. It’s a tactic ripped straight from the playbook of authoritarian regimes, where truth is malleable and accountability is a foreign concept. Co-host Mika Brzezinski’s outrage over this “brazenly corrupt” maneuver reflects a broader sentiment of disbelief and anger towards this blatant manipulation of facts.

A Call to Arms

The panelists on “Morning Joe,” including Joe Scarborough and Willie Geist, highlight not just the moral bankruptcy of withholding evidence, but also the practical impediments it creates for state officials striving to pursue justice. The federal government’s stranglehold on the evidence underlines a sinister message: We have something to hide. Scarborough’s advice to Minnesota officials to leverage the judicial system to compel the release of evidence is more than strategic counsel; it’s a rallying cry for state sovereignty and the rule of law.

The Bigger Picture

This episode transcends the tragic shooting of Alex Pretti. It’s a glaring symptom of a much larger disease – a systemic erosion of democratic principles and a flagrant abuse of power. When federal agencies act as rogue entities, answerable to no one but themselves, the fabric of American democracy is torn asunder. The Trump administration’s actions, as dissected by the “Morning Joe” panel, are not just administrative decisions; they are affronts to justice and an insult to the memory of Alex Pretti and the rights of the citizens of Minnesota.

Conclusion

The refusal of the Trump administration to share crucial evidence with Minnesota authorities is not an isolated incident but a disturbing pattern of behavior that threatens the very pillars of democracy. The case of Alex Pretti is a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance, transparency, and accountability in governance. As the “Morning Joe” panel rightly points out, it’s up to the state of Minnesota and its judiciary to challenge this cover-up and demand justice, not just for Alex Pretti, but for the sanctity of American democracy itself.
