The Vasectomy Hypocrisy: When Men Discover the Government Shouldn’t Control Bodies

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 4, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Performative OutrageBodily Autonomy, but Only for MenThe Real Purpose of the BillConclusion: The Mirror Doesn’t Lie

It started, as these things often do in the circus of American politics, with a tweet. Senator Ted Cruz, never one to miss a cheap applause line, took to social media to rage against a satirical bill introduced by an Alabama Democrat. The bill would mandate vasectomies for men at age 50 or after the birth of their third child—whichever came first.

"Yikes. A government big enough to give you everything is big enough to take everything...literally!"

He clutched his ideological pearls with gusto, framing the bill as a dystopian overreach by big government. But what Cruz failed to acknowledge—or perhaps hoped everyone else would ignore—is that the bill wasn’t meant to pass. It was political satire. A provocation designed to highlight the deep, gaping hypocrisy at the heart of conservative reproductive policy.

Enter historian Kevin M. Kruse, who calmly quote-tweeted Cruz with a scalpel:

"Yes, the government shouldn’t be involved in private reproductive health choices, yes, that’s a great point you made, yes."

And just like that, Cruz’s entire argument collapsed in on itself. Kruse wasn’t mocking the concern over government overreach—he was exposing the selective application of that concern. Because when the government mandates forced pregnancy for women, the GOP cheers. When the hypothetical mandates a vasectomy for men? Suddenly, it’s Orwellian.

Let’s be clear: the vasectomy bill was a mirror. A rhetorical device. A way to say, “If it’s tyranny to control men’s reproductive systems, what the hell do you think you’re doing to women?”

It’s not just about the bill, or about Cruz, or even about Alabama. It’s about a conservative movement that has weaponized government power to control one half of the population while pretending it’s defending liberty. That’s not liberty. That’s patriarchy with a PR team.

The Performative Outrage

Republicans love to shriek about government tyranny when it’s their hypothetical genitals on the line. Ted Cruz didn’t post a fraction of this outrage when Roe v. Wade was gutted. He didn’t weep for the women denied life-saving abortions in states with trigger bans. He didn’t pen op-eds about liberty when a 10-year-old rape victim had to cross state lines to terminate a pregnancy.

But suggest that maybe, just maybe, men shoulder a fraction of the reproductive burden? Now that’s tyranny. Now we’re in Handmaid’s Tale territory. Now the Constitution matters again.

It’s not just hypocrisy—it’s strategy. The GOP has spent decades turning “small government” into a Trojan horse for big, moralistic, authoritarian policy. They scream about freedom while regulating uteruses with sadistic precision. They cry “liberty” while banning abortion pills, contraception access, and even sex education.

Bodily Autonomy, but Only for Men

The irony is painful. For all their talk of “my body, my choice” when it came to masks and vaccines, those same voices fall silent when women demand the same respect for their reproductive organs. Because deep down, the conservative movement isn’t about freedom. It’s about control. And control flows in one direction—from men, over women.

There are no mandatory paternity laws that match the ferocity of anti-abortion statutes. No tracking of men’s sexual activity. No legislative efforts to penalize sperm donors for creating too many offspring. The male reproductive role is treated as invisible, untouchable, sacred. Women, on the other hand, are public property the moment a fertilized egg exists inside them.

The Real Purpose of the Bill

The Alabama vasectomy bill was never going to become law. Its author, Representative Rolanda Hollis, introduced it in 2020 (and resurfaced the idea again) as a form of protest. A way to challenge her colleagues to think about what it means to legislate someone’s body. It was a rhetorical scalpel meant to carve open the smug moral certainty that drips from the anti-abortion right.

And predictably, it worked. Cruz’s outrage confirmed that the very people most eager to control others recoil when the same control is even imagined in their direction.

Kruse’s reply nailed the point with brutal efficiency. If you believe government shouldn’t mandate vasectomies, congratulations: you’ve just made the argument for bodily autonomy. You’ve just endorsed the pro-choice position.

Conclusion: The Mirror Doesn’t Lie

The conservative movement’s reaction to the vasectomy bill revealed something they usually try to hide: their ideology isn’t about limited government, it’s about selective authoritarianism. It’s about whose rights matter. It’s about who gets to control whom.

And when forced to imagine a world where their own bodies are legislated with the same cold indifference they inflict on others, they can’t handle it.

The vasectomy bill was never a threat. It was a test. And they failed it spectacularly.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
