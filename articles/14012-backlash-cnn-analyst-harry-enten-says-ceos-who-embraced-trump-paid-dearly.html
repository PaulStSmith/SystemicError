Donald Trump might have found untapped ways to monetize the presidency for his personal fortune — but wealthy businessmen who have hitched their wagons to the president tend to find themselves losing huge amounts of money, CNN data analyst Harry Enten told anchor Erin Burnett on Tuesday evening.

No better example of this exists, he said, than Mike Lindell, the CEO of MyPillow, who admitted to CNN's Donie O'Sullivan this week that his business has been essentially ruined by his promotion of pro-Trump election conspiracy theories.

"He got Mike Lindell to reveal something about himself and what he said — which is why Donie is so amazing, okay — the hit though, when he said to Donie, I've lost everything, I've lost — I had $40 million saved, it's all gone. It's all gone," said Burnett. "The rise and fall of Mike Lindell has been pretty spectacular.

"It's been very spectacular," agreed Enten. "You know, according to Mike Lindell, get this, by 2023, the number of sales or the percentage drop in sales for MyPillow, get this, dropped 95 percent. This used to be a $100 million company, well north of $100 million in terms of sales. He now claims that by 2023, that sales had actually dropped to, get this, just $5 million. Now, again, that's according to him. We can't independently verify it. But according to Mike Lindell, this whole election conspiracy stuff really made him take a hit in his wallet and his company's wallet as well."

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Trump's Midas Touch: A Closer Look

The recent revelation from a CNN discussion has laid bare a stark paradox in the world of business and politics. Donald Trump, the former U.S. President, is often touted for his business acumen and the supposed Midas touch that turns everything he handles into financial success. Yet, the reality, as highlighted by CNN data analyst Harry Enten, paints a markedly different picture, especially for those wealthy businessmen who have aligned themselves too closely with Trump.

The Spectacular Fall of Mike Lindell

Mike Lindell, the CEO of MyPillow, serves as the poster child for this phenomenon. Once a fervent promoter of pro-Trump election conspiracy theories, Lindell has witnessed his business empire crumble. His confession to CNN's Donie O'Sullivan—that he had lost everything, including a $40 million savings, to a staggering 95% drop in MyPillow sales—is not just a personal tragedy but a cautionary tale.

Lindell's journey from a company boasting sales well over $100 million to a mere $5 million is nothing short of "spectacular," as Enten and Burnett describe. Yet, this spectacle is not of success but a harrowing descent fueled by unwavering allegiance to debunked conspiracy theories. It's a stark reminder of the cost of misinformation and the volatile intersection of business and political fervor.

The Irony of Trump's Prosperity

The irony becomes even more pronounced when juxtaposed with Trump's own financial trajectory. As noted by Dan Alexander of Forbes, Trump's net worth has surged, with significant earnings from ventures like cryptocurrency. This disparity underscores a critical point: Trump's prosperity seems not only untouched but bolstered by the very controversies that obliterate the fortunes of his staunchest supporters.

A Broader Pattern of Loss

Lindell is not an isolated case. The article mentions other high-profile figures, like Elon Musk and the CEO of Goya, who faced backlash and potential financial repercussions due to their political stances. Even Jeff Bezos saw a decline in The Washington Post's revenue following attempts to alter its editorial direction. This pattern suggests a broader, systemic issue where business leaders suffer tangible losses for their political affiliations, particularly when those affiliations become highly controversial or divisive.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

This narrative serves as a profound cautionary tale about the dangers of intertwining personal political beliefs too closely with business strategy. The allure of aligning with powerful figures like Trump may seem tempting for business leaders seeking to capitalize on political fervor. However, as the cases of Lindell and others illustrate, such alignments can lead to significant financial and reputational damage.

In the end, the saga of Mike Lindell and his peers is not just a story of financial loss but a larger commentary on the current state of political discourse and its impact on the business world. It's a sharp reminder that the consequences of political entanglements can be both swift and severe, leaving behind a trail of cautionary tales for future businessmen to ponder.
