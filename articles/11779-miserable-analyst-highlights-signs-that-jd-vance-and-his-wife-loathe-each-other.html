'Miserable': Analyst highlights signs that JD Vance and his wife 'loathe each other'

Vice President J.D. Vance's marriage appears to be falling apart behind the scenes, Amanda Marcotte wrote for Salon — and the two barely seem to be capable of hiding how much they "loathe each other" at this point.

The tension between the vice president and Usha Vance, who has already been dispatched for some of President Donald Trump's geopolitical side ambitions, has already gotten to the point that comedians are making fun of it. But those jokes are coming from a real place, Marcotte continued.

"Last month, Megan McCain hosted Usha Vance for what should have been the softest of softball interviews, focused on what life as second lady is like," wrote Marcotte. "Vance clearly understood her assignment was to make it all seem like a great time — that leaving her career as a high-powered attorney to spend her days doing sub-Melania Trump public appearances about dull topics like the importance of reading for kids. But her acting chops weren’t up to it. Instead, audiences were treated to a one-hour hostage video, as the increasingly desperate McCain tried to get Vance to feign interest in her own life."

In particular, she showed no interest in her role as second lady, and talked on and on about how much she missed her old life, saying her husband doesn't cook for her anymore, and reflecting, “In a dream world, eventually, I’ll be able to live in my home and continue my career” — all while offering McCain no details about her romantic life no matter how much she was pressed.

The vice president himself doesn't appear to get any more joy out of the marriage, wrote Marcotte.

" J.D. Vance has spent years now dropping similarly miserable hints that he is not enjoying married life ... In March, he 'joked' that when 'the cameras are all on; anything I say, no matter how crazy, my wife Usha has to smile, laugh, and celebrate it,'" And during his speech at the GOP convention last year, he boasted about how “when we allow newcomers into our American family, we allow them on our terms,” and that his marriage proposal included being buried in the family's seven-generation Eastern Kentucky cemetery plot. "The implication wasn’t subtle: Usha Vance is expected to subsume her South Asian identity to his family’s whiteness, as well as her husband’s blood-and-soil idea of what makes someone an American."

Beyond that, Marcotte noted, Vance is constantly talking about how married couples have a duty to stay together no matter how rough their marriage is.

"Despite his claims that 'childless cat ladies' are 'miserable,' it’s hard not to notice the protest-too-much quality to his obsession with single or childless people," she wrote. "He keeps insisting the childless are 'sociopathic,' painting them as selfish hedonists, all in a tone of barely concealed envy — as if he senses they are getting away with something." At the same time, she noted, he is constantly distancing himself from his family in rhetoric, "joking" that she has the kids, not himself, and agreeing with a radio host that free child care is “the whole purpose of the post-menopausal female.”

"I don’t know what goes on in private behind the gates of the Naval Observatory," Marcotte concluded — but "if that’s what the right thinks marriage is all about, J.D. Vance shouldn’t be so surprised that ever larger numbers of women are saying, 'Thanks, I’ll choose cats instead.'"

Our Analysis:

The Disintegrating Facade of Vice President J.D. Vance's Marriage

In an article that reads more like a script for a tragicomedy than a piece on political spousal dynamics, Amanda Marcotte for Salon delves into the crumbling marriage of Vice President J.D. Vance and Usha Vance. The narrative is not just about the apparent disdain brewing between the couple but also serves as a stark reflection of the warped values Vance seems to champion. Here, we dissect Marcotte's observations, layer by layer, revealing not just a marriage in turmoil but a symptomatic representation of deeper societal and political malaise.

1. The Unconvincing Performance of Usha Vance

In what Marcotte describes as a "one-hour hostage video," Usha Vance's appearance on Megan McCain's show was less an endorsement of her life as the Second Lady and more a distress signal of her dissatisfaction. Her lament about missing her previous life as a "high-powered attorney" and the absence of domestic support from her husband not only shatters the illusion of political marital bliss but underscores a regressive expectation of women sacrificing their ambitions for their husband's careers. This isn't just about a failing marriage; it's about a failing system.

2. J.D. Vance's Miserable Hints and Racial Implications

J.D. Vance's own expressions of marital dissatisfaction, coupled with his "blood-and-soil" remarks on American identity, reveal a man caught in the web of his own ideological contradictions. His comment on Usha having to "subsume her South Asian identity to his family’s whiteness" is not just appalling but highlights a dangerous intersection of personal prejudices with political ideologies. Vance isn't just struggling with marital discord; he's emblematic of a broader political discourse that seeks to homogenize American identity, erasing the rich tapestry of multiculturalism that truly defines the nation.

3. The Ominous Obsession with Childlessness

Marcotte's insight into Vance's fixation on "childless cat ladies" and the supposed misery of the childless is revealing. His branding of the childless as "sociopathic" and "selfish hedonists" is not just a gross misrepresentation but a dangerous narrative that seeks to valorize certain life choices over others. Vance's envy-tinged rhetoric is a thinly veiled attack on personal freedom, autonomy, and the right to choose one's own path to happiness.

4. The Distancing Rhetoric

The Vice President's comments on family dynamics and child care responsibilities are as regressive as they are revealing. Joking about free child care being "the whole purpose of the post-menopausal female" is not only deeply misogynistic but also dismissive of the genuine challenges facing families today. Vance's rhetoric is a testament to a broader societal ailment: the undervaluing of women's roles and contributions beyond their reproductive capabilities.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Broader Ills

Marcotte's article, while focused on the personal dynamics between J.D. and Usha Vance, serves as a microcosm for examining the broader socio-political landscape. The disintegration of the Vance marriage is not just a matter of tabloid intrigue but a window into the troubling ideologies that underpin much of today's political discourse. If the Vances' marriage is a barometer for the state of our political and societal health, then the prognosis is dire. The narrative woven by Marcotte is not just about the choice between cats and companionship but a call to critically examine the values and beliefs we champion in our leaders and ourselves.
