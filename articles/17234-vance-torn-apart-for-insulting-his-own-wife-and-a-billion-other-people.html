Disingenuous Advocacy: The Misdirection of Religious Freedom and the Imperialist Agenda

Power Dynamics in Religious Discourse

When Vice President JD Vance publicly expressed a desire for his Hindu wife to convert to Catholicism, he not only cast a spotlight on his personal religious preferences but also inadvertently ignited a broader discussion about the power dynamics inherent in religious conversion. Avatans Kumar, an anti-woke columnist, critiqued this incident, pointing out the systemic neglect of non-proselytizing religions like Hinduism under the guise of promoting religious freedom. This critique, however, reveals a deeper layer of selective advocacy and historical amnesia about the role of colonialism in religious conversion.

Selective Religious Freedom

Kumar’s argument that the religious freedom movement in the U.S. disproportionately favors proselytizing religions such as Christianity and Islam over non-proselytizing ones strikes at the heart of a significant imbalance. This isn’t merely an oversight but a reflection of historical and cultural dominance where conversion-focused faiths often receive more legitimacy and support. Kumar is correct in highlighting this disparity, yet his analysis stops short of examining why this preference exists, rooted deeply in the West’s imperialist past and ongoing cultural hegemony.

The Imperial Legacy in Religious Conversion

The linkage between colonialism and religious conversion is critical yet often glossed over in discussions about religious freedom. British missionaries in India, motivated by a belief in the superiority of their religion, embarked on widespread conversion campaigns among Hindus. This historical context is essential to understanding current religious dynamics and power structures, yet it’s frequently absent from mainstream discourse, which prefers to treat religious freedom as a neutral, universally applied principle.

Contradictions and Political Convenience

The complexity of Kumar’s position is further muddled by his own political stances. Despite advocating for Hindu representation, Kumar has critiqued “wokeism,” aligning himself with a faction that often opposes the kind of cultural sensitivity required to genuinely understand and support non-proselytizing religions. This contradiction suggests a selective engagement with issues of religious freedom and representation, contingent more upon political convenience than a consistent philosophical stance.

Reinforcing Conservative Norms

The political manipulation of religious and cultural narratives extends into the personal domain of the Vances as well, with the Second Lady engaging in content creation that subtly reinforces conservative gender roles. This move aligns with a broader conservative strategy of creating parallel cultural institutions to propagate specific ideological values under the guise of combating liberal dominance. The intertwining of personal, religious, and cultural narratives within political frameworks highlights a strategic use of cultural power to sustain specific political ends.

Conclusion: The Broader Political Pattern

The discussion around JD Vance’s comments and Kumar’s response exemplifies how religious freedom is often co-opted as a political tool, selectively applied and intertwined with broader imperialist legacies and cultural battles. True advocacy for religious freedom would require dismantling the hierarchies that elevate some religions over others and confronting the historical contexts that shape these biases. Until then, discussions about religious freedom will remain incomplete, serving more as political rhetoric than as a genuine commitment to equitable treatment of all faiths.
