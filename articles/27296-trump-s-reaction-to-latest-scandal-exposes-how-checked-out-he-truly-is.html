Trump's reaction to latest scandal exposes how checked out he truly is

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosNovember 16, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump responded to a nationwide aviation shutdown by saying nothing about stranded passengers and promoting his plan to revive American film production. As the Federal Aviation Administration imposed what The i Paper described as only the third complete ground stop at every U.S. airport, the president offered no public reassurance, explanation or sympathy. At 5:52 p.m. Friday, with the disruption still unfolding, his social-media account instead demanded that the country “MAKE OUR ENTERTAINMENT INDUSTRY GREAT AGAIN!”

That was the reaction the headline gestures toward. But calling the episode merely a “scandal” obscures the more concrete failure: the federal government had taken an extraordinary action affecting millions of travelers, and the official with the largest platform in the country declined to address it.

A president absent from his own government’s crisis

The FAA’s order paralyzed commercial travel and left passengers facing fragmented information at airports, according to The i Paper’s Simon Marks. Trump did not call a television network, contact favored reporters or use Truth Social to tell travelers what his administration was doing. Marks reported that the president made no visible effort to stabilize public confidence in air safety.

Presidents do not personally operate the aviation system, and a televised appearance would not have moved an aircraft. But public communication during a national transportation emergency is part of governing, particularly when the administration’s own agency has halted flights nationwide. Trump possessed the authority, staff and communications machinery to explain the disruption. His silence was therefore not evidence that nobody could speak. It showed that the person at the center of the executive branch chose not to.

The contrast with his post about film production was especially revealing. Trump asserted that actors and producers wanted to return from Canada, Britain, Australia and elsewhere. At that moment, the federal aviation shutdown meant travelers across the United States were struggling to get home at all. The administration’s immediate responsibility was functioning transportation; the president’s chosen subject was an entertainment-industry slogan.

Electoral defeat and executive withdrawal

The aviation episode followed what AlterNet described as a major Republican defeat in the 2026 midterms. Trump, normally relentless in public, had largely stopped discussing the election and the political damage confronting his party.

Earlier in November, New York Times reporter Peter Baker portrayed him as subdued and seemingly detached while Republicans assessed their losses. Trump signed a steel beam for a planned White House ballroom and traveled to Florida for a space-program speech. Those activities did not answer the political setback, just as the film-industry post did not answer the aviation crisis.

It is impossible to establish Trump’s private state of mind from this reporting. Descriptions such as “in denial,” “dazed” or “checked out” remain interpretations of his conduct, not independently measurable facts. What the reported record does establish is a pattern of avoidance: faced first with an electoral repudiation and then with a highly visible failure in national transportation, Trump directed public attention elsewhere.

That matters because presidential communications are an exercise of power, not simply a personality test. Trump has repeatedly used his platform to define enemies, elevate preferred controversies and command news coverage. When he withholds that machinery during a crisis managed by his own government, the vacuum protects the administration from immediate presidential accountability while leaving passengers to absorb the consequences.

The election proved harder to seize than to denounce

AlterNet also reported that Trump had repeatedly suggested before Election Day that he might nationalize elections or otherwise interfere if the results went against Republicans. Yet no comparable effort succeeded after the midterm losses.

Beauregard Patterson, election and voting-rights counsel at the Democracy Defenders Fund, told AlterNet that the structure of a midterm election made reversal exceptionally difficult. Because voters in multiple states were choosing separate House and Senate delegations, the administration would have needed to overturn outcomes across several states at once.

Patterson also pointed to the failure of earlier litigation and attempts to federalize election administration, including an effort involving the Postal Service and rules governing whether ballots would be accepted. In Patterson’s account, those defeats left the administration with few effective tools to take control of the results.

That explanation is more useful than portraying Trump’s post-election quiet as simple bewilderment. The relevant constraint was institutional: dispersed state elections, multiple decisive contests and failed legal efforts limited what the White House could accomplish. The results were not protected by Trump’s restraint. According to Patterson, they survived because the scale and structure of the defeat made subversion far harder.

The same distinction applies to the flight shutdown. Trump’s silence did not remove presidential power from the situation. It removed presidential responsibility from view. Millions of passengers still faced the consequences of an extraordinary federal order while the man running the executive branch advertised a cinematic “Golden Age” and left his own government’s emergency unexplained.
