President Donald Trump’s Justice Department is expected to announce the indictment of former FBI Director James Comey this week, MSNBC’s Ken Dilanian and Carol Leonnig reported Wednesday.

“Former FBI Director James Comey, for years the target of President Trump’s ire, is expected to be indicted in the coming days in the Eastern District of Virginia, where a prosecutor who opposed bringing the case was recently fired, three sources familiar with the matter told MSNBC,” wrote Dilanian on X.

“The full extent of the charges being prepared against Comey is unclear, but the sources believe that at least one element of the indictment—if it goes forward--will accuse him of lying to Congress during his testimony on September 30, 2020, about whether he authorized a leak of information. The five-year statute of limitations on that charge would lapse on Tuesday,” Oct. 1, 2025, he added.

“We can’t delay any longer, it’s killing our reputation and credibility,” Trump wrote in a Truth Social post that was addressed to “Pam,” presumably meaning Attorney General Pam Bondi, earlier this week, urging action against several foes including Comey.

Comey was fired by Trump in 2017 during the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election. The abrupt termination raised significant questions about potential obstruction of justice. Trump has attacked him multiple times since.

“They impeached me twice, and indicted me (5 times!), OVER NOTHING. JUSTICE MUST BE SERVED, NOW!!!” Trump also said.

Trump also demanded that New York Attorney General Letitia James and Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) also be indicted, though he has yet to cite which crimes they committed.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Brewing Storm of Political Vendettas

In a move that reads like a page ripped from a political thriller, President Donald Trump’s Justice Department is gearing up to announce an indictment against former FBI Director James Comey. According to reports from MSNBC’s Ken Dilanian and Carol Leonnig, this development emerges amidst a backdrop of long-standing animosity from Trump towards Comey, a saga that unfolds with the subtlety of a sledgehammer in the hands of a toddler.

The Charges and The Context

The expected charges against Comey, while not fully disclosed, hinge on an alleged lie to Congress about authorizing a leak of information. This charge, with its looming statute of limitations, suggests a last-minute scramble to pin something—anything—on Comey. What stands out is not just the potential indictment itself but the theatrical timing and the personal vendetta it seems to serve.

The Theater of Justice

“We can’t delay any longer, it’s killing our reputation and credibility,” Trump reportedly exclaimed in a post directed at Attorney General Pam Bondi. This statement, dripping with urgency and desperation, reads less like a call for justice and more like a plea for political revenge. It’s an echo of Trump’s broader strategy: when in doubt, attack. The fact that Comey was axed by Trump during the Russian interference investigation only adds layers to this narrative, suggesting a retaliatory motive rather than a pursuit of truth.

The Question of Obstruction

Comey’s abrupt firing in 2017, amidst the Russian interference probe, already raised eyebrows and questions about obstruction of justice. This impending indictment seems to be an extension of that saga, potentially serving as a smokescreen to distract from the controversies surrounding Trump himself. It’s worth noting Trump’s own lamentations about being impeached twice and indicted five times, as he clamors for “justice” against his perceived enemies. This doesn’t just reek of hypocrisy—it’s a blazing inferno of it.

Demanding Indictments Left and Right

Trump’s demands for indictments against not just Comey but also New York Attorney General Letitia James and Senator Adam Schiff (D-CA) underscore a troubling pattern: a seeming attempt to weaponize the justice system against political adversaries without clear allegations of crimes. This isn’t justice; it’s a witch hunt dressed in legal garb.

Conclusion

In this unfolding drama, one thing is crystal clear: the line between justice and political vendetta is not just blurred but obliterated. The expected indictment of James Comey appears less about holding individuals accountable and more about settling scores, with Trump as the conductor of this unsettling symphony. As this story develops, one must ask: where does justice end, and revenge begin?

In a landscape where political animosity overshadows legal principles, it’s crucial to remain vigilant, questioning, and critical of the motivations behind such legal actions. The integrity of the justice system and the very fabric of democracy depend on it.
