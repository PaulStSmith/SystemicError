Former speechwriter to President George W. Bush, David Frum, on Sunday flagged a comment from Trump in which the president threatened military action against Nigeria based on claims that have been rejected by the allied nation. For the conservative writer, the response to Trump’s comment set of alarm bells.

“Yesterday afternoon, Trump threatened to launch a military attack on the most populous country in sub-Saharan Africa,” he wrote. “The fact that few express much upset at this bluster is a marker of how little respect this blowhard president commands at home or abroad.”

He went on to elaborate, “If any previous president issued a personal statement, ‘I’m considering massive military strikes in sub-Saharan Africa,’ there would be tumult - even panic.”

“But with Trump, everybody shrugs, including his own supporters. ‘That’s Trump. He says things. He’s full of s---. Next?’” he concluded this weekend.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Accidental Revelation: A Commentary on Global Respect—or the Lack Thereof

In a recent, rather telling episode, Donald Trump, the erstwhile commander-in-chief, managed to inadvertently showcase the scant respect he garners on the global stage, as pointed out by David Frum, a former adviser to George W. Bush. Trump’s off-the-cuff remark about launching military action against Nigeria, based on claims that have been widely dismissed, not only rings alarm bells but also serves as a stark indicator of his diminished stature both domestically and internationally.

The Bluster That Barely Blinked an Eye

Frum’s observations draw attention to a rather bizarre reality: Trump’s threat to initiate military aggression against Nigeria, the most populous country in sub-Saharan Africa, barely caused a ripple of concern. This indifference, as Frum astutely notes, is not a testament to the seriousness with which the world takes Trump, but rather the opposite. It signals a collective desensitization to the former president’s bombastic rhetoric—a rhetoric so disengaged from reality that it fails to elicit more than a shrug, even in the face of potential military conflict.

A Comparative Lens on Presidential Authority

The lack of response to Trump’s threat is not just alarming but also deeply revealing. Frum contrasts this with a hypothetical scenario where any of Trump’s predecessors might have issued a similar threat. The difference in potential public and global reaction is stark. Had a previous president so much as hinted at “massive military strikes in sub-Saharan Africa,” the ensuing response would likely have been one of tumult, if not outright panic. This hypothetical scenario underscores the gravity with which presidential words were once treated—a gravity that seems conspicuously absent from the current discourse surrounding Trump.

The Shrugging Shoulders of Supporters and Detractors Alike

What is perhaps most concerning, and indeed indicative of Trump’s eroded authority, is the reaction (or lack thereof) from his own base. Frum’s conclusion—”That’s Trump. He says things. He’s full of s---. Next?”—encapsulates the dismissive attitude that has become all too common. This nonchalance towards potentially catastrophic pronouncements from a former president not only highlights a troubling normalization of reckless rhetoric but also underscores a broader issue of accountability, or the lack thereof.

In Conclusion: A Diminished Figure on the World Stage

The incident flagged by Frum is emblematic of a larger, more systemic problem. It’s not just about Trump’s penchant for making inflammatory and often baseless statements; it’s about what these moments reveal regarding his standing in the global hierarchy of respect and influence. When threats of military action elicit nothing more than collective indifference, it speaks volumes to the diminished figure Trump has become.

In a world fraught with real and pressing challenges, the ability of a leader—current or former—to command respect and serious consideration is paramount. Trump’s latest faux pas, as highlighted by Frum, serves as a sobering reminder of the perilous consequences when such respect is conspicuously absent. As we navigate these turbulent political waters, one can only hope for a future where leadership and rhetoric are once again taken seriously, both at home and on the world stage.
