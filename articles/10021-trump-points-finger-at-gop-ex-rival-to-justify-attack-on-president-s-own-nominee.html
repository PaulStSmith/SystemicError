Trump points finger at GOP ex-rival to justify attack on president's own nominee

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Donald Trump on Saturday blamed a former ally for recommending someone who he would then nominate for a key role.

Trump earlier on Saturday pledged to target his own former FBI director, Christopher Wray, in a fiery social media post regarding what he dubbed the “January 6th hoax.”

“It was just revealed that the FBI had secretly placed, against all Rules, Regulations, Protocols, and Standards, 274 FBI Agents into the Crowd just prior to, and during, the January 6th Hoax,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social.

Trump then created a separate post later on, mentioning Wray and another Republican who used to be the president’s key ally.

“For those who are interested, and there aren’t many of you, Christopher Wray was recommended to me by Sloppy Chris Christie when Chris was in my ‘good graces’ — which was a very long time ago!” Trump wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Blame Game: A Dissection

Donald Trump’s recent tirade on social media is a vivid illustration of his enduring strategy: deflect, distort, and blame. In a series of posts, Trump managed to not only perpetuate the myth of the “January 6th hoax” but also to cast aspersions on former allies and officials, showcasing a blend of vindictiveness and revisionism that has become all too familiar.

The “January 6th Hoax” Narrative

Trump’s assertion that the FBI “secretly placed, against all Rules, Regulations, Protocols, and Standards, 274 FBI Agents into the Crowd” prior to and during the events of January 6 is a claim fraught with implications yet devoid of proof. This narrative serves a dual purpose: it attempts to delegitimize the investigations surrounding the Capitol riot while painting Trump and his supporters as victims of a deep-state conspiracy.

The use of the term “hoax” to describe the January 6 insurrection is particularly insidious. It suggests an attempt to rewrite history, to erase the violence and chaos of that day, and to undermine the gravity of an attack on the democratic process. This is not just a matter of political spin; it’s a dangerous manipulation of reality that threatens the foundation of democratic discourse.

The Art of the Scapegoat

Trump’s mention of Christopher Wray and Chris Christie in his posts is a classic example of his scapegoating technique. By blaming Christie for recommending Wray, Trump seeks to absolve himself of any responsibility for the actions of his administration and to fracture the unity among Republicans by highlighting divisions and betrayals.

This tactic is not new. Throughout his presidency and subsequent political endeavors, Trump has consistently deployed blame-shifting as a means to distance himself from failure and controversy. However, this move against Christie and Wray is particularly telling. It reveals Trump’s long memory for slights and his willingness to use his platform to settle scores, irrespective of the truth or the consequences.

Conclusion: The Trumpian Universe

In Trump’s universe, loyalty is paramount, facts are malleable, and history is what he says it is. His recent posts are a reminder of the alternate reality he perpetuates—a reality where he is perpetually the hero or the victim, never the villain. This is not merely a matter of political rhetoric; it’s a deliberate strategy to reshape the narrative, to sow division, and to mobilize his base.

The danger lies not just in what Trump says, but in the fact that millions believe him. As we dissect his words, we must remember that the battle for truth is not just about countering lies; it’s about reaffirming a shared reality where facts matter, history is not rewritten, and democracy is valued. Let’s not get lost in Trump’s illusion; let’s anchor ourselves in the truth and hold fast to the principles that define us.
