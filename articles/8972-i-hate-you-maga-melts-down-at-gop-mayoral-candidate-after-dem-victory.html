'I hate you!' MAGA melts down at GOP mayoral candidate after Dem victory

Democratic nominee Zohran Mamdani scored a decisive victory Tuesday night in the New York City mayoral election, and MAGA is taking its rage out not on the progressive candidate, but rather, his Republican opponent.

“F— you Curtis Sliwa!’ wrote former Republican Congressman George Santos, who was recently released from prison after being pardoned by President Donald Trump, in a social media post on X. “I HATE YOU, your dumb wife, that stupid Beret of yours and all your f------ cats!”

Mamdani faced off Tuesday night against former New York Gov. Andrew Cuomo – who secured 41.6% of the vote to Mamdani’s 50.4%, according to unofficial election results – and Sliwa, the lone GOP candidate who ended up receiving 7.1% of the vote.

The rage directed at Sliwa, many MAGA enthusiasts say, is over allegations that he acted as a spoiler in the race by directing votes away from Cuomo, who polls indicated had the best chance to overcome Mamdani. However, based on the unofficial election results, even if every Sliwa voter had voted for Cuomo, Mamdani still would have come out on top.

Nevertheless, MAGA loyalists couldn’t hold their contempt for Sliwa after Mamdani’s victory, with many shunning him from the Republican party for life.

“Never want to hear from Curtis Sliwa again,” wrote X user “Johnny MAGA,” who’s amassed more than 260,000 followers.

Charly Arnolt, a pro-Trump sports broadcaster with a net worth estimated to be around $2 million, went as far as to hope that Sliwa would be forever disgraced in his home city.

“I truly hope Curtis Sliwa is shunned in NYC,” Arnolt wrote in a social media post on X. “He is a disgrace, a coward and a traitor and should no longer be welcome to walk the streets he claimed to support.”

Chaos had even ensued at Cuomo’s election watch party Tuesday night, where one attendee was captured on video shouting profanity directed at Sliwa.

“Curtis Sliwa threw this election, shame on you, you’re a scumbag, you will never be loved in New York City ever again!” the attendee could be heard shouting.

Our Analysis:

The Fallout of Factionalism: An Analysis of the NYC Mayoral Election

In the wake of Zohran Mamdani’s decisive victory in the New York City mayoral race, the response from the MAGA corner is not just astonishing but a vivid illustration of political factionalism run amok. Rather than direct their ire towards the Democratic nominee, Mamdani, MAGA loyalists have launched a vitriolic attack against Curtis Sliwa, the Republican candidate who trailed significantly in the polls.

The Misdirected Rage

The venomous language used by figures such as George Santos, a former Republican Congressman recently pardoned by Donald Trump, is not just appalling but ironically misguided. Santos’s social media tirade against Sliwa, involving personal attacks and expletives, underscores a disturbing trend of internal party conflict overshadowing broader political discourse. Sliwa, who received a mere 7.1% of the vote, has been accused of being a spoiler in the race—a claim that holds little water when considering the mathematics of the election results.

The Spoiler Myth Debunked

The charge against Sliwa as a spoiler is not just baseless but demonstrably false. Even if every single vote for Sliwa had instead gone to Andrew Cuomo, the former New York Governor and runner-up, Mamdani would still have emerged victorious. This fact seems to elude MAGA loyalists, who prefer scapegoating over acknowledging the strategic shortcomings and unpopularity of their preferred candidates.

A Divided Opposition

What we’re witnessing is a faction within the Republican Party, aligned with MAGA ideologies, that is more interested in settling scores and ostracizing members than in understanding the electoral dynamics at play. The call by figures such as Charly Arnolt, a pro-Trump sports broadcaster, for Sliwa to be “shunned” in NYC, speaks volumes about the toxicity permeating certain segments of the party. It’s a classic case of shooting the messenger rather than addressing the message.

The Bigger Picture

In their blind rage against Sliwa, MAGA enthusiasts are missing the forest for the trees. The real story here is not about Sliwa’s alleged betrayal or the inflammatory remarks by MAGA figures; it’s about the shifting political landscape of New York City and the electorate’s clear preference for progressive policies as represented by Mamdani. The insistence on blaming Sliwa is a smokescreen, distracting from the deeper analysis required to understand why the GOP’s message failed to resonate with NYC voters.

Conclusion: The Road Ahead

The aftermath of the NYC mayoral election is a lesson in what happens when political discourse descends into personal vendettas and baseless accusations. For the Republican Party, and especially the MAGA faction within, there’s a critical need for introspection and strategy recalibration. Instead of ostracizing those like Sliwa, who dare to challenge the status quo, the focus should be on building a cohesive, forward-looking platform that addresses the needs and concerns of all voters. Until then, the cycle of blame and internal strife is likely to continue, to the detriment of not just the party, but the broader political ecosystem.
