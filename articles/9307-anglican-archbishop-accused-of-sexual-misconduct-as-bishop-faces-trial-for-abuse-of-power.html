Anglican archbishop accused of sexual misconduct as bishop faces trial for abuse of power

Archbishop Stephen Wood, the senior-most official of the Anglican Church in North America, has been accused of sexual misconduct, while another top official has been accused of allowing men with troubling backgrounds to serve as church leaders.

A former children’s ministry director accused the 62-year-old Wood of putting his hand against the back of her head and trying to kiss her in his office in April 2024, two months before he was elected to his current role, according to a new church presentment obtained by the Washington Post.

Stewart Ruch III, a 58-year-old bishop who heads a diocese in the Midwest, is facing an ecclesiastical trial on charges that he allowed men with histories of violence or sexual misconduct to worship or hold staff or leadership roles in his diocese.

“This is a painful and very personal process for all involved,” wrote Mike Hughes, the church’s senior warden, in an email to congregants alerting them to the Post’s reporting.

A complaint was filed Monday by four presbyters and seven lay members of the Anglican Church. Wood denied the allegations and declined to answer specific questions about the woman’s accusation.

“I do not believe these allegations have any merit,” Wood said in a statement. “I place my faith and trust in the process outlined in our canons to bring clarity and truth in these matters and respectfully decline to comment further at this time.”

The woman gave an interview to the Post and said Wood, a married father of four sons, gave her thousands of dollars in unexpected payments from church coffers before making the alleged advance.

“I was in shock,” said Claire Buxton, a 42-year-old divorced mother of three sons. “It’s just bizarre to me how far we — the Anglican Church in North America and its leadership — have gotten away from basic morals and principles.”

Wood remains the rector of St. Andrew’s Church near Charleston, South Carolina, and a bishop overseeing a diocese that includes more than 40 churches across the South, but if the presentment sets in motion an ecclesiastical trial, he could be defrocked and forced to step down.

“Unfortunately, the problems at the highest levels of the ACNA are deeper, wider and more entrenched than many of its own parishioners realize,” said Andrew Gross, an Anglican priest who previously served as the Anglican Church’s communications director. “The ACNA has never before had to deal with serious allegations of misconduct by the archbishop. This is a crisis without precedent, and how these concerns are handled will determine the future trajectory of the denomination and its credibility.”

Testimony in Ruch’s trial on abuse of power allegations ended earlier this month, and a verdict from the court’s seven-member panel of judges is expected later this year.

Our Analysis:

The Scandal at the Pinnacle of the Anglican Church in North America: A Closer Look

In a revelation that casts a long, dark shadow over the Anglican Church in North America (ACNA), Archbishop Stephen Wood and Bishop Stewart Ruch III find themselves embroiled in allegations that strike at the very heart of the institution’s moral and ethical framework. The accusations, as reported by the Washington Post, paint a picture of a church hierarchy potentially rotten to its core, challenging the trust and faith of its followers.

The Allegations Against Archbishop Stephen Wood

Archbishop Wood, a figure who should epitomize the highest standards of moral integrity given his position, stands accused of sexual misconduct. The specifics of the accusation—that he attempted to kiss a former children’s ministry director in his office—are disturbing enough. However, the added detail that he allegedly made unexpected payments to the accuser, Claire Buxton, from church funds prior to the incident, suggests a premeditation that is profoundly troubling. Wood’s response, a flat denial devoid of engagement with the specifics of Buxton’s claims, does little to reassure. His reliance on the church’s internal process, while perhaps procedurally necessary, feels like a deflection in the face of serious accusations.

Bishop Stewart Ruch III’s Questionable Leadership

Bishop Ruch’s situation, though different in nature, is equally concerning. The charge that he allowed individuals with histories of violence or sexual misconduct to assume roles within the church not only poses a direct risk to the safety and well-being of congregants but also represents a catastrophic failure of judgment. This alleged negligence undermines the very essence of what a church community should be—a safe haven.

The Church’s Response

The response from the church’s senior warden, Mike Hughes, emphasizes the personal pain of the process but fails to address the systemic issues at play. It is not merely a painful moment but a critical test of the church’s commitment to justice and moral leadership. The implication that this scandal is an unfortunate but isolated incident misses the mark; instead, it points to a potentially systemic failure to uphold the values the church professes to stand for.

The Larger Implications

Andrew Gross’s commentary—that the allegations represent a crisis without precedent and will determine the future trajectory and credibility of the ACNA—is spot on. This isn’t just about the alleged actions of two men; it’s about the integrity of an entire institution. How the church handles these allegations will speak volumes about its ethical foundations or lack thereof.

The Verdict on Bishop Ruch and the Future of Archbishop Wood

As the church awaits the verdict on Bishop Ruch, the wider community watches closely. The outcome and the handling of Archbishop Wood’s accusations will be a litmus test for the church’s moral compass. Should the church fail to act decisively and transparently, it risks not just the ire of its followers but a fundamental break in trust that could be irreparable.

In Conclusion:

The allegations against Archbishop Wood and Bishop Ruch are a stark reminder of the power dynamics and potential for abuse within religious institutions. The ACNA finds itself at a crossroads, with the actions it takes next potentially reshaping its future. For an institution that stands on the principles of faith, integrity, and community, the path forward must be one of rigorous self-examination, accountability, and reform. Anything less would not only be a disservice to the victims but a profound betrayal of the values it purports to uphold.
