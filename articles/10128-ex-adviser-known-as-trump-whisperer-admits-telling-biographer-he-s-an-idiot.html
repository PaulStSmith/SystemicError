Ex-adviser known as 'Trump whisperer' admits telling biographer 'he's an idiot!'

Even some of President Donald Trump’s own close advisers, from the beginning, have had no confidence in his intelligence or sense, biographer Michael Wolff told the Daily Beast’s “Inside Trump’s Head” podcast Wednesday.

Specifically, Wolff said, Trump’s medically unsupported announcement that the administration blames Tylenol use during pregnancy for autism — something even his own Medicaid administrator, Dr. Mehmet Oz, doesn’t seem to believe — reminded Wolff of a conversation he had right after the 2016 election with Trump’s then-adviser, Sam Nunberg.

Wolff said he believed at the time Trump might be an “interesting president” and could end up doing “the unexpected in a good way.” But when he suggested this to Nunberg, known as the “Trump whisperer,” Nunberg replied, “You don’t get it, do you? He’s an idiot!”

“And at that moment, it all came clear to me because Trump is, in very classic terms, an idiot,” Wolff continued.

Asked for comment by The Beast, Nunberg admitted the comment but said he no longer feels this way about Trump, because, “That was a long time ago and President Trump has certainly proved me wrong by getting [re-elected] in 2024.”

Meanwhile, Trump’s infamous foul-mouthed communications director Steven Cheung gave his stock response to the Beast, “Michael Wolff is a lying sack of s--- and has been proven to be a fraud. He routinely fabricates stories originating from his sick and warped imagination, only possible because he has a severe and debilitating case of Trump Derangement Syndrome that has rotted his peanut-sized brain.”

Nunberg, who was fired by the Trump campaign over unearthed racist comments on Facebook, became widely known after a bizarre rant on TV in 2018 in which he refused to cooperate with the Russia special counsel investigation, before shortly backing down and doing so. He was widely speculated by observers to be drunk during that interview, although he adamantly denies this.

Our Analysis:

The Unfiltered Reality of Trump’s Inner Circle

The Daily Beast’s recent episode of “Inside Trump’s Head” podcast peeled back another layer of the chaotic onion that is Donald Trump’s presidency, as recounted by biographer Michael Wolff. Wolff, who has made a career dissecting the tumultuous Trump administration, didn’t hold back in his latest revelations. Here lies the crux of our analysis: even those who have navigated the stormy waters of Trump’s inner circle harbor profound doubts about his capabilities and intentions.

The Unfounded Tylenol Claim

At the heart of Wolff’s narrative is Trump’s bizarre and medically unsound proclamation linking Tylenol use during pregnancy to autism. This claim, devoid of scientific backing, not only undermines the credibility of the presidency but also sows confusion and fear among the public. Dr. Mehmet Oz, Trump’s own Medicaid administrator, doesn’t subscribe to this theory, highlighting a distressing disconnect within Trump’s administration. This isn’t just about a singular unfounded claim; it’s emblematic of a broader pattern of misinformation that has plagued Trump’s tenure.

Nunberg’s Realization

Sam Nunberg’s initial description of Trump as an “idiot” to Wolff post-2016 election is telling. It speaks volumes about the internal perceptions of Trump’s competency, or lack thereof. Though Nunberg has since retracted this stance, citing Trump’s supposed re-election in 2024 as evidence of his proven worth, this flip-flop is more indicative of political expediency than a genuine change of heart. Nunberg’s past, peppered with controversy and erratic behavior, including a bizarre rant on national television and subsequent compliance with the Russia special counsel investigation, casts a shadow on his credibility and motivations.

Cheung’s Defense: A Familiar Tune

Steven Cheung’s response to Wolff’s claims is a textbook example of Trumpian defense mechanisms: attack, discredit, and belittle. Cheung’s vitriolic characterization of Wolff as a “lying sack of s---” suffering from “Trump Derangement Syndrome” is not only unprofessional but also devoid of substantive rebuttal. This kind of ad hominem attack has become a hallmark of Trump’s team when confronted with criticism, reflecting a concerning aversion to accountability and truth.

Conclusion: A Pattern of Unreliability

The revelations shared by Wolff, coupled with the reactions from Trump’s advisers, paint a picture of an administration mired in incompetence, denial, and internal dissent. The unwarranted attack on Tylenol and the autism link, Nunberg’s initial assessment of Trump’s intelligence, and Cheung’s aggressive dismissal of Wolff’s claims all serve to underscore a systemic issue within Trump’s presidency: a disturbing disregard for facts, science, and decorum.

In sum, the insights from Wolff’s conversation and the ensuing responses from Trump’s inner circle are not merely isolated incidents but rather symptomatic of a deeper, more systemic dysfunction. This dysfunction, characterized by misinformation, internal discord, and a penchant for attacking critics, raises crucial questions about the integrity and efficacy of Trump’s leadership. As observers and critics, it’s imperative that we continue to scrutinize and challenge these patterns of behavior, for the health of the political discourse and the nation.
