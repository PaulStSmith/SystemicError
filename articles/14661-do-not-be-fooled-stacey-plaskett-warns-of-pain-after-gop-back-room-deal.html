'Do not be fooled': Stacey Plaskett warns of pain after GOP 'back room' deal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 20, 20255 min read

Stacey Plaskett, congresswoman from the Virgin Islands. (Screengrab via C-SPAN)

Republicans pushed their massive reconciliation bill through the House Budget Committee late Sunday after striking a deal with GOP hardliners who tanked a vote on the package late last week, complaining that the measure's proposed cuts to Medicaid and other programs were not sufficiently aggressive.

The final vote on Sunday was 17-16, with the four Republicans who voted against the bill on Friday switching their votes to "present," allowing the legislation to clear the committee.

Rep. Ralph Norman of South Carolina, one of the Republicans who switched his vote, said during Sunday's hearing that he is "excited about the changes we've made"—prompting Democratic committee members to ask, "What changes?"

"Do not be fooled," Democratic Rep. Stacey Plaskett of the U.S. Virgin Islands said Sunday. "The 'no' votes from certain Republicans on Friday were because the cuts were not fast or deep enough. In the back room, Republicans agreed to deeper and especially faster cuts to programs."

Rep. Chip Roy (R-Texas) wrote in a social media post after joining Norman in voting "present" that "after a great deal of work and engagement over the weekend," the legislation "now will move Medicaid work requirements forward and reduces the availability of future subsidies under the green new scam"—a reference to clean energy tax credits established by the Inflation Reduction Act.

Roy and other Republican hardliners are also reportedly pursuing changes that could force states to end their Medicaid expansions, which would strip coverage from millions and potentially kill tens of thousands of people per year.

In its current form, the Republican reconciliation bill would inflict the largest cuts to Medicaid and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) in U.S. history, slashing or eliminating benefits for millions by implementing strict work requirements and forcing many Medicaid recipients to pay more for coverage, among other changes—all while giving major tax breaks to the wealthy.

The legislation's Medicaid work requirements, which policy experts have condemned as cruel and ineffective, were slated to begin in 2029, but GOP hardliners want them to start immediately.

The changes sought by Roy, Norman, and other far-right Republicans must get through the House Rules Committee before the bill can reach the House floor. The GOP controls the panel, and both Roy and Norman are members.

Rep. Brendan Boyle (D-Pa.), the top Democrat on the House Budget Committee, said following Sunday's vote that "Republicans have spent months lying about their plan to make the largest cuts to healthcare and food assistance in American history."

"Kicking 13.7 million people off their healthcare apparently wasn't enough for House Republicans," Boyle added. "The only reason this vote passed tonight is because they've plotted behind closed doors to hurt even more families while refusing to share this backroom deal with the American people. This fight isn't over, and we're going to make sure every American knows exactly how they've been betrayed by Donald Trump and the Republican Party."

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Ruthless Ambition: A Deep Dive into Medicaid Cuts

The latest maneuverings within the House Budget Committee serve as a stark reminder of the lengths to which the Republican Party is willing to go to dismantle social safety nets, all the while cloaking their intentions in the guise of fiscal responsibility. Late Sunday, in what can only be described as political theatre at its most Machiavellian, Republicans managed to push their mammoth reconciliation bill through the House Budget Committee. This was achieved not through transparent debate or public scrutiny, but through backroom deals and a sudden, albeit calculated, alignment with GOP hardliners who deemed the initial proposed cuts to Medicaid and other programs as too lenient.

The Deal with the Devil

The so-called compromise reached to appease the hardliners was nothing short of a capitulation to the most extreme elements within the party. Rep. Ralph Norman of South Carolina, one of the Republicans who initially voted against the bill, mysteriously switched his vote to "present," following undisclosed "changes" that evidently made the bill more palatable to those demanding more aggressive austerity measures. What changes? This question, posed by Democratic committee members, resonates as a chilling reminder of the opacity and the underhanded tactics increasingly defining Republican strategy.

The Cruelty of Medicaid Work Requirements

The centerpiece of this legislative onslaught is the imposition of draconian Medicaid work requirements and the slashing of clean energy tax credits, derisively referred to as the "green new scam" by Rep. Chip Roy (R-Texas). These work requirements, previously slated to begin in 2029, are now being pushed to take immediate effect—a move that can only be described as cruel and ineffective, according to numerous policy experts. The implications are nothing short of catastrophic, potentially stripping coverage from millions and, by some estimates, leading to tens of thousands of deaths annually.

A Symphony of Hypocrisy

The bill, in its relentless pursuit to gut Medicaid and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), stands as a testament to the GOP's commitment to enriching the wealthy at the expense of the vulnerable. Major tax breaks for the affluent are preserved and expanded, all while millions are set to lose vital benefits. The audacity of this legislative gambit is matched only by its hypocrisy, with Republicans continuing to peddle the false narrative of fiscal prudence while simultaneously engineering the largest cuts to healthcare and food assistance in American history.

The Call to Action

Rep. Brendan Boyle's (D-Pa.) remarks encapsulate the gravity of the situation and the deceit at play. The GOP's strategy, marked by secrecy and lies, aims to disenfranchise millions of Americans by kicking them off their healthcare and stripping away essential food assistance. This fight, as Boyle rightly points out, is far from over. It is imperative for every American to understand the full extent of the betrayal at hand—a betrayal orchestrated by Donald Trump and the Republican Party, who have shown time and again their willingness to sacrifice human lives on the altar of wealth and power.

In conclusion, the Republican reconciliation bill is not just a policy proposal; it is a declaration of war against the American people. It is a clear signal that for the GOP, the well-being of the wealthy few trumps the basic needs of the many. As this bill progresses, it is crucial to remain vigilant, informed, and ready to hold those responsible accountable. The health, indeed the very lives, of millions depend on it.
