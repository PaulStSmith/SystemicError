'MAGA is a cult': Observers slam Charlie Kirk memorial as 'weird and embarrassing'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Politicization of a Tragedy: The Charlie Kirk Memorial Service

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio speaks during a memorial service for slain conservative commentator Charlie Kirk at State Farm Stadium, in Glendale, Arizona, U.S., September 21, 2025. REUTERS/Daniel Cole

Political commentators and analysts on Sunday slammed the MAGA-led memorial service for slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk.

Kirk was killed almost two weeks ago when he was struck by a single bullet while speaking at a Utah university campus, while giving a speech. His death has become a political flashpoint, with conservatives seeking to lionize Kirk while some Democrats have criticized some of Kirk's ideas.

A memorial service for Kirk was held in his hometown of Glendale, Arizona, on Sunday. It was attended by multiple high-profile MAGA members like President Donald Trump, his son Donald Jr., and Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

Several left-wing commentators and analysts responded to the memorial service on social media.

"MAGA is a cult," left-wing analyst Wajahat Ali posted on Bluesky. "Normies around the world, the majority, rightfully look at them as unhinged. We have the numbers. Don't bow down or be silenced. Also, don't make Charlie Kirk into a saint. Use your free speech to accurately call out his life that was lived in hate."

"Thing about this is that Charlie Kirk *was* a racist transphobic neonazi creep," podcaster Andrew Hickey posted on Bluesky. "Also misogynist and antisemitic. He was pure distilled hatred and evil. It's bad that he was shot, but that doesn't mean he was any less of a monster."

"All I can say is, this whole Charlie Kirk shit is weird and embarrassing," Josh Sternberg, executive editor of Morning Brew, posted on Bluesky.

"Charlie Kirk referred to slain civil rights leader Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. as 'an awful' person," USA Today opinion columnist Michael Stern posted on Bluesky. "And MAGA are going crazy if liberals do the same?"

"I will not whitewash the white supremacist that was Charlie Kirk," writer Mona Eltahawy posted on Bluesky. "For someone so young, what an embarrassment of hatreds he left us to excoriate him with."

Our Analysis:

The Politicization of a Tragedy: The Charlie Kirk Memorial Service

In the wake of Charlie Kirk's tragic death, the political machinery didn't pause to mourn but rather, seized the moment to further entrench the divisive polarities that define our modern political landscape. At the helm of this charge was the memorial service in Glendale, Arizona, which rather than serving as a solemn remembrance, morphed into a political rally for the MAGA movement. Attended by high-profile figures such as President Donald Trump, Donald Jr., and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, the event was less about the man and more about the martyrdom of a conservative ideology.

The Left's Response: Between Condemnation and Reflection

Left-wing commentators and analysts took to social media to voice their criticisms, not just of the event, but of Kirk's legacy itself. Wajahat Ali's denouncement of the MAGA movement as a "cult" and his call for the majority to "not bow down or be silenced" underscores the deep-seated division and the battle over narrative control. Similarly, Andrew Hickey's unapologetically blunt labeling of Kirk as a "racist transphobic neonazi creep" reflects a refusal to sanitize one's public record in death.

However, these reactions, while grounded in the critics' truths, risk further entrenching the "us vs. them" mentality that plagues American politics. The sharpened language, though cathartic and rallying for some, may only serve to alienate and embolden the opposition.

The Danger of Political Canonization

The memorial service for Charlie Kirk and the subsequent outcry highlight a worrying trend: the canonization of political figures who, in life, were highly controversial. This sanctification process, engaged by both ends of the political spectrum, transforms individuals into symbols, erasing the nuances of their actions and beliefs. It's a maneuver that simplifies complex legacies into digestible, polarizing narratives that serve the interests of political factions rather than the truth.

Mourning in the Age of Polarization

What is lost in this cacophony of political posturing and ideological warfare is the human element. A life was tragically cut short. Yet, instead of a moment of collective mourning and reflection, we find ourselves dissecting the deceased's legacy through the lens of political expediency.

The tragedy of Charlie Kirk's death and its aftermath is not just the loss of life, but the missed opportunity for a moment of national introspection. In a healthier democratic society, such a moment could serve as a catalyst for dialogue and understanding. Instead, we find ourselves mired in the muck of political opportunism and ideological rigidity.

Conclusion: The Need for a Return to Civility

The response to Charlie Kirk's memorial service underscores a pressing need for a return to civility and a reevaluation of how we engage with those we disagree with. The vilification and sanctification of individuals based on their political beliefs only serve to deepen the divides within our society. As we navigate these turbulent waters, it's imperative that we strive for a balance between holding individuals accountable for their actions and beliefs and recognizing our shared humanity.

In an era where every event is politicized, perhaps the greatest act of defiance is to remember the person behind the politics, flawed though they may be, and to engage with each other's humanity first and foremost.
