Trump creating 'nonstop chaos' at law firms — and embarrassing them in the process

President Donald Trump’s administration has humbled some of the largest law firms and highest-paid individuals in the legal profession. And other businesses and individuals that potentially need his grace on their activities have to be watching and wondering if they are next.

That’s the conclusion of a New York Times opinion piece by contributor Jeffrey Toobin, which notes that bullies (i.e., the Trump administration) “are never satisfied with just a single capitulation.”

The lesson to be drawn is that government pressure is effective even when its actions are clearly unconstitutional.

What the opinion piece terms “nonstop chaos” caused by the Trump administration began last year with executive orders targeting progressive law firms that worked for Trump opponents and causes he doesn’t favor. The orders would have created insurmountable barriers to the law firms’ corporate activities.

Some of the firms in the cross hairs of those orders opted to settle by offering the administration millions in pro-Bono legal work on favored causes.

Last year, four judges ruled that the executive orders targeting the law firms were unconstitutional. The Department of Justice appealed, but then decided earlier this month that it was dropping the plans.

The next day, the appeals were placed back on.

“The about-face was embarrassing, but it obscured a larger truth of this lamentable episode: President Trump had already won this fight months ago, when the American legal profession — especially its largest and richest law firms — lost. And that’s not funny at all.”

While the legal profession was initially targeted, there’s a broader application underway. The Trump administration has presented the same “extortionate choices” to a variety of targets, including universities and companies.

The implication of the administration’s message is clear: “Agree to our unconstitutional demands and sacrifice your principles, or fight back and suffer our wrath in the form of lost patronage and dollars.”

In all of those cases, Toobin argues, “surrender looks — and perhaps even is — the path of least resistance.” But giving in may embolden further actions, “and you don’t have to be a lawyer to see that was a foolish choice indeed.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Strong-Arm Tactics of the Trump Administration

The New York Times opinion piece by Jeffrey Toobin presents a chilling narrative of power misuse by President Donald Trump’s administration, targeting the very pillars of legal advocacy and corporate America. This analysis isn’t just about the legal profession’s capitulation; it’s a broader warning of the administration’s coercive tactics against any entity daring to oppose its agenda.

The Bully at the Pulpit

Toobin’s use of the term “bullies” to describe the Trump administration isn’t hyperbole; it’s a factual representation based on the administration’s actions. The essence of bullying is to intimidate or coerce someone into doing what one wants, and that’s exactly what’s happening here. The administration has weaponized executive orders to punish Trump’s opponents and those who support causes he disfavors. This isn’t governance; it’s tyranny masquerading as executive action.

The Legal Profession’s Capitulation

The fact that some of the largest law firms, with their vast resources and legal expertise, felt compelled to offer millions in pro-Bono legal work to appease the administration is both astonishing and alarming. This isn’t just about law firms bending the knee; it’s a testament to the chilling effect of the administration’s tactics on legal advocacy and free speech. The courts may have ruled the executive orders unconstitutional, but the damage was already done. The administration’s message was clear: comply or face our wrath.

The Broader Implications

What’s even more concerning is the “broader application underway,” as Toobin points out. Universities, companies, and other entities are faced with the same “extortionate choices.” This strategy of coercion over conversation undermines the very foundations of America’s democratic principles and the rule of law. Agreeing to unconstitutional demands out of fear or for convenience is a slippery slope that could lead to further erosions of freedoms and rights.

The Foolish Choice of Surrender

Toobin argues that surrender may seem like the path of least resistance but warns of the dangers of such capitulation. Giving in to such demands not only emboldens further unconstitutional actions but also sets a precedent that could be hard to reverse. This isn’t just a legal issue; it’s a moral crisis. The administration’s tactics are not just an affront to the legal profession but to all who believe in the principles of justice, freedom, and democracy.

Conclusion

Jeffrey Toobin’s piece isn’t merely an opinion; it’s a call to action. The Trump administration’s actions represent a clear and present danger to the fundamental values that underpin American society. It’s imperative that all sectors of society, not just the legal profession, stand up against such bullying tactics. Surrendering to such demands, however tempting in the short term, only invites further aggression. We must resist, not because it’s easy, but because our principles and our democracy demand it.
