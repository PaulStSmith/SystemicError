'The president's full of it': Trump's finger-pointing fails under expert scrutiny

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20262 min read

Trump Is Lying About Who’s Dragging Whom Down

The Polls Tell the Opposite Story

Trump told interviewers voters were angry at Republicans, not him. The polling picture in the source points the other way: House Republicans are running ahead of Trump on the generic congressional ballot, voters prefer Republicans to “stand up to Trump,” and even Republican voters are giving the party more room than they give him.

That is the basic fact pattern. The rest is blame management.

Power Sits With Trump

Trump is not some outside commentator being unfairly attached to the party. He is the president. He sets the tone, dominates the agenda, and imposes the brand problem Republicans are trying to survive.

That matters because his attempt to separate himself from the party is not confusion. It is self-protection. He is trying to make the damage look collective so the accountability becomes nobody’s.

Republicans Are Choosing Submission

The article’s most revealing detail is not that Trump is unpopular. It is that congressional Republicans are outperforming him while remaining tethered to him.

That is an enabling relationship, not a hostage situation. If House Republicans were serious about preserving their own political future, they would use their institutional leverage to distance themselves. Instead, they keep feeding the same machine that is dragging them down. Voters can see the difference: 56 percent want Republicans to stand up to Trump, while only 35 percent want them to work more closely with him.

Misdirection Is the Point

Trump’s claim is meant to invert responsibility. He wants Republicans to absorb the blame for his brand while he keeps the authority of the presidency. That is a familiar authoritarian move: concentrate power at the top, then scatter blame downward when the consequences land.

The framing also tries to flatten a real hierarchy. Republicans in Congress are not the source of his power. They are his extension. Their weakness is political choice, not structural innocence.

The Party Is Still Carrying Him

The source notes that even inside the Republican base, Trump trails his own party. That is important because it shows the GOP is not simply an echo of his personal approval rating. It is a broader ballot brand that remains more durable than he is.

But durability is not independence. Republicans are still betting that voters will separate down-ballot races from Trump’s liabilities. That is not a strategy for governing. It is a strategy for surviving while refusing to break with the man causing the wreck.

The Larger Pattern

This is what institutional cowardice looks like in a party system: a president inflates his own role, congressional allies absorb the fallout, and everyone pretends the damage is an accident. It is not an accident. It is a deliberate arrangement built to keep power centralized and accountability dispersed.

The deeper lesson is simple. When a political movement depends on one man’s domination, its members stop acting like representatives and start acting like excuses.
