NYT sues Pentagon for kicking out press corps and replacing them with MAGA sycophants

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Pete Hegseth (Reuters)

The New York Times filed a lawsuit alleging that President Donald Trump’s Department of Defense violated the First Amendment by imposing new press access rules that drove out nearly the entire news corps — including conservative outlets — and replaced them with a group of MAGA bloggers and activists loyal to the regime.

“[T]he policy — which vests Department officials with unbridled discretion to immediately suspend and ultimately revoke a reporter’s [Pentagon Facility Alternate Credentials] for engaging in lawful newsgathering, both on and off Pentagon grounds, or for reporting any information Department officials have not approved — is neither reasonable nor viewpoint-neutral,” stated the lawsuit filed in the District Court for D.C. “It is exactly the type of speech- and press-restrictive scheme that the Supreme Court and D.C. Circuit have recognized violates the First Amendment.”

Furthermore, the suit continues, the Pentagon has responded to the exodus of reporters by bringing in a new contingent of Trump loyalists willing to comply with the demands.

“While Plaintiffs and many other journalists and news organizations no longer possess PFACs because they refused to accede to a Policy that would restrict independent reporting, the Department has welcomed what it calls the ‘next generation of the Pentagon press corps’ — individuals and media outlets strongly supportive of the Trump administration and whose viewpoints the Department favors,” said the suit.

This group, the filing continued, includes “Mike Lindell, the chief executive of MyPillow, who has promised to ‘make [the Administration] proud’ with his Pentagon coverage; Laura Loomer, an influential pro-Trump activist; and Raheem Kassam, editor in chief of the National Pulse, who described his publication as ‘basically an industry mag/site for MAGA world.’”

All of this is coming as Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth faces sharp bipartisan questions over an order to kill survivors of wrecked vessels the administration deemed to be running drugs for cartels — which experts have broadly deemed a war crime, and which Hegseth has repeatedly changed his story on.

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Indictment of Press Freedom Erosion Under Trump’s Defense Department

In what can only be described as a flagrant assault on the First Amendment, the New York Times has taken legal action against President Donald Trump’s Department of Defense. The lawsuit alleges a deliberate and systematic effort to undermine press freedom through the imposition of draconian press access rules. These rules, it appears, have not only marginalized a broad swath of the traditional news media but have also facilitated the creation of a propaganda echo chamber within the Pentagon itself.

The Erosion of Press Neutrality

At the heart of the lawsuit is a policy that grants Department officials “unbridled discretion” to suspend or revoke the Pentagon Facility Alternate Credentials (PFACs) of reporters. This policy is not only unreasonable but patently antithetical to the principles of a free press. It’s a mechanism that could only be dreamed up by an administration with a distaste for accountability and an appetite for authoritarian control.

What’s more egregious is the claim that the Department of Defense has effectively purged the Pentagon press corps of critical voices. In their place, a cadre of MAGA bloggers and activists, known more for their loyalty to Trump than any journalistic rigor, have been installed. This move isn’t just a restructuring; it’s a wholesale replacement of critical oversight with sycophantic cheerleading. Figures like Mike Lindell, Laura Loomer, and Raheem Kassam are hardly the paragons of unbiased reporting. Their inclusion in what’s now dubbed the “next generation of the Pentagon press corps” signals a disturbing shift towards state media in a country that prides itself on press freedom.

Beyond the Press: A Question of International Law

As if the assault on press freedom wasn’t concerning enough, the Department of Defense, under Secretary Pete Hegseth, is embroiled in another controversy of potentially grave international consequence. The order to kill survivors of wrecked vessels—branded as drug runners—has not only raised bipartisan eyebrows but has also drawn accusations of war crimes. Hegseth’s shifting explanations do nothing to assuage concerns but rather underscore a troubling lack of transparency and accountability at the highest levels of defense.

The Bigger Picture

This lawsuit isn’t merely a legal battle over press credentials; it’s a canary in the coal mine for American democracy. When a government begins to cherry-pick its chroniclers, truth becomes malleable, and accountability becomes an anachronism. The actions of Trump’s Department of Defense—as outlined in this lawsuit—represent a clear and present danger to the foundational ideals of the United States.

In the end, this isn’t just about the New York Times or the Pentagon press corps. It’s about whether the United States will continue to champion the freedoms it has long promoted or whether it will succumb to the ease of authoritarianism, where the press is nothing more than a mouthpiece for those in power. The implications of this lawsuit extend far beyond the courtroom; they reach into the very soul of American democracy.
