'Grotesque': Furor over Trump's opulent party grows hours before America goes to polls

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio attend a Halloween party at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., October 31, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Sam Stein, managing editor of “The Bulwark,” bashed President Donald Trump’s Gatsby-themed Halloween party, saying it was an example of the “incredible and sad dichotomy” of the impact of the Trump administration.

On Friday, Trump held a Gilded Age party at his estate in southern Florida, Mar-a-Lago. Reports indicate the event was attended by administration officials including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, as well as high-profile Republican donors and other wealthy individuals.

Stein argued in a new episode of “Bulwark Takes” on Sunday that the party was “surprising,” given that communities across the country were reeling from continued immigration raids and the expiration of food assistance benefits.

“They are living it up like they’re in the Gatsby’s,” Stein said. “Like this is Moulin Rouge. They’re all having nice drinks and stuff, and this is all during a government shutdown...when the president is demolishing the East Wing to build his ostentatious ballroom, where he’s posting about this new marble bathroom he’s constructed in the White House, mocking the old bathroom because it had old plumbing.”

“The level of out-of-touchness that is exhibited here is surprising,” Stein added. “Honestly, it suggests a president and administration that just doesn’t give a [stuff] because I don’t really understand why they would do this.”

Stein added that he doesn’t think images of opulence like Trump’s Halloween party can continue to coexist with the administration’s efforts to take away social benefits.

“It is grotesque, honestly, and it’s obviously problematic, too, for this country where communities are being torn apart and the people who are top of the government get to have a good old Great Gatsby ball on Halloween,” Stein said. “So, that is a microcosm of the current place we are in. It’s a sad microcosm, but it is what it is.”

The party came just days before the nation votes in a series of key elections, including several tightly contested races for governor and a New York City mayoral contest in which a democratic socialist leads in polls.

Our Analysis:

The Gaudy Gatsby Gala: A Stark Emblem of Trump’s Disdain

Sam Stein’s commentary on President Donald Trump’s latest escapade—a Gilded Age-themed Halloween party at his southern Florida estate, Mar-a-Lago, serves as a chilling reminder of the administration’s flagrant disregard for the pressing issues facing ordinary Americans. Amid a backdrop of immigration raids, the expiration of food assistance benefits, and a government shutdown, Trump’s decision to live it up in a fashion reminiscent of Jay Gatsby’s extravagant soirees is nothing short of appalling.

The Dichotomy of Decadence

The party, attended by high-profile Republican donors, administration officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and other wealthy individuals, represents the “incredible and sad dichotomy” of the Trump administration’s impact, as Stein rightly puts it. While communities across the nation grapple with the dire consequences of the administration’s policies, Trump and his entourage are busy indulging in opulence and grandeur.

A Mockery of the Marginalized

Stein’s observation that the event occurred “during a government shutdown...when the president is demolishing the East Wing to build his ostentatious ballroom” is particularly damning. It’s a grotesque display of wealth and power at a time when many Americans are suffering. Trump’s boasting about constructing a new marble bathroom in the White House, while mocking the old one for its outdated plumbing, further underscores the administration’s callous indifference to the plight of those not born into affluence.

The Problem with Opulence

Stein questions the sustainability of such displays of wealth, especially as the administration simultaneously works to strip away social benefits from the very people it purports to serve. The grotesqueness of hosting a “Great Gatsby ball on Halloween” while the country is “being torn apart” is not just tone-deaf; it’s a stark manifestation of the administration’s priorities—or lack thereof.

A Microcosm of Misplaced Priorities

As Stein concludes, the party is a “microcosm of the current place we are in.” It’s not just sad; it’s a damning indictment of an administration that seems more interested in flaunting its wealth and power than in addressing the urgent needs of its citizens. The timing of the event, just days before key elections, including several tightly contested races for governor and a New York City mayoral contest, adds insult to injury.

In summary, the Gatsby-themed Halloween party at Mar-a-Lago is more than just a social event; it’s a glaring symbol of an administration deeply out of touch with the American people. Stein’s analysis sheds light on the profound disconnect between the lived experiences of everyday citizens and the frivolous, self-indulgent antics of their supposed leaders. As the nation stands at a crossroads, the choice between continued opulence for the few and genuine progress for the many has never been clearer.
