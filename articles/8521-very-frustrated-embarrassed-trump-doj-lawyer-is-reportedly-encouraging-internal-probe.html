'Very frustrated': 'Embarrassed' Trump DOJ lawyer is reportedly encouraging internal probe

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: Todd Blanche, then attorney for President-elect Donald Trump, leaves the New York State Appellate Division of the Supreme Court in New York City, U.S., January 7, 2025. REUTERS/Adam Gray/File Photo

United States Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche is “very frustrated” and “embarrassed” by the latest failures of the DOJ, so much so that he is reportedly encouraging an internal investigation of Trump admin officials.

MS Now investigative reporter Carol Leonnig appeared on the network on Thursday to further discuss a bombshell report that two Trump officials are being investigated by the FBI.

The host asked of Leonnig, “I’m curious though because this is a new development that I think I was not expecting today. This is the DOJ investigating two Trump officials. And how did that come about? Because so far what we’ve seen is DOJ has done the investigating that Donald Trump wants in terms of politics, how did the DOJ open an investigation and then get one approved into two of Donald Trump’s own officials?”

“So great question,” Leonnig began. “Our sources say that the deputy attorney general, Todd Blanche, is well aware of this probe, not technically running this investigation, but aware of it and familiar with it and not in any way interfering with it, but possibly even encouraging it.”

She added, “You know, there is a claim by some of our sources that Blanche and some of the people around him at the top layers of the Department of Justice have been very frustrated with the methods used by Ed Martin and Bill Pulte and their frustration about the possibility that these cases are not solid, the ones that both men have been pushing despite them being Trump allies and doing what Trump really would like, which is going after his perceived enemies, his critics.”

“In this case, it is a problem, according to our sources, for Todd Blanche, to have cases that aren’t being handled very properly. And as we saw, there were some unforced errors,” she noted on Thursday. “ In another case in the eastern district of Virginia yesterday. We’re told Blanche is a little bit embarrassed about all of that.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Tangled Webs of Justice and Politics

In the latest chapter of the seemingly never-ending saga of the Department of Justice (DOJ) and its encounters with political figures, we find Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche caught in a whirlwind of frustration and embarrassment. The reason? The DOJ’s recent failures, particularly concerning the investigations into two officials from the Trump administration. Let’s dissect this bombshell with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

Firstly, the irony of the DOJ, historically a beacon of impartial justice, now being seen as a puppet manipulated by political strings, is as rich as it is distressing. The mention of the DOJ conducting investigations that Donald Trump desires, as opposed to those warranted by law and evidence, paints a grim picture of a justice system under siege by the very political biases it seeks to stand above.

Todd Blanche’s Embarrassment: The Deputy Attorney General’s reported embarrassment and frustration are noteworthy. It’s not every day that high-ranking officials in the DOJ air their grievances so publicly. Blanche’s push for an internal investigation into Trump admin officials signals a desperate attempt to claw back some semblance of integrity and autonomy for the department. But let’s not pat him on the back just yet. This move, while seemingly in the right direction, also reeks of too little, too late. Where was this righteous indignation when the seeds of partisanship were being sown within the DOJ?

The FBI’s Investigation: The involvement of the FBI in investigating two Trump officials is a development that should raise eyebrows. Not because the FBI is investigating, but because of the context. If the DOJ has been, as reported, a tool for Trump’s political vendettas, then the independence of this investigation is crucial. The fact that Blanche is “not technically running this investigation” but is “aware of it and familiar with it” is a thin line to tread. It’s a balancing act between oversight and interference, and only time will tell on which side of that line Blanche and the DOJ will land.

The Crux of the Matter: The heart of this issue lies in the claim that the DOJ is frustrated with the methods used by Ed Martin and Bill Pulte, and the lack of solidity in their cases. This is where the systemic error in the justice system becomes glaringly obvious. When the pursuit of justice is overshadowed by political maneuvering, the very foundation of the legal system is compromised. The “unforced errors” mentioned are symptoms of a much larger disease: a justice system that is being manipulated to serve the ends of political agendas.

In conclusion, while Todd Blanche’s frustrations and embarrassments are valid, they only scratch the surface of the systemic issues plaguing the DOJ. The investigation into Trump officials, under the shadow of political influence, is a story as old as time, yet it continues to erode the public’s trust in the institutions designed to protect them. It’s high time for a thorough examination not just of individual cases, but of the systemic biases that allow politics to infiltrate the halls of justice. Only then can we begin to untangle these webs and restore faith in the rule of law.
