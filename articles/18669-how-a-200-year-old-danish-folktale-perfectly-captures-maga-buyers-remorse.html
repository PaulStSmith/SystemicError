How a 200-year-old Danish folktale perfectly captures MAGA buyers' remorse

The True Threads of Deception: Understanding Trump Through Andersen’s Lens

In the quaint setting of Rutland County, Vermont, protesters recently donned theatrical costumes to voice their dissent against former President Donald Trump, invoking the imagery of Hans Christian Andersen’s tale, The Emperor’s New Clothes. This protest, led by Marsha Cassel who portrayed a naked Trump crowned and sceptered, wasn’t merely a satirical jab but a pointed critique deeply rooted in literary history.

Who Was Hans Christian Andersen?

Born in 1805 in Odense, Denmark, Andersen’s humble beginnings as the son of a cobbler and an illiterate washerwoman starkly contrasted with the noble pretensions handed down by his grandfather. His tales, emerging from socio-economic hardship, began to take shape after his education, funded by the theatre director Jonas Collin. Among these, The Emperor’s New Clothes (1837) stands out as a scathing commentary on vanity, deception, and the dangerous complicity of sycophantic courts and publics.

The Tale’s Timeless Echo

Andersen’s story is simple yet profound. A vain emperor, obsessed with his appearance, is duped by two conmen posing as weavers. They promise him garments visible only to those fit for their roles or exceptionally intelligent. Out of fear of appearing unfit or foolish, the emperor, his aides, and eventually the townspeople, play along with the obvious lie until a child’s innocent truth-telling breaks the collective delusion.

This narrative has been wielded against political figures frequently, but its use against Donald Trump taps into deeper layers of Andersen’s intent and the historical context of the tale. Literary scholar Hollis Robbins points to how the tale mirrored a shift in societal values from legitimacy and heritage to competence and skill, a shift uncomfortably parallel to contemporary political anxieties about legitimacy and capability. Andersen, with his proletarian background, masterfully critiqued the social dynamics that allowed such absurdities to thrive unchallenged.

Why Andersen, Why Now?

The choice of Andersen’s tale to critique Trump is laden with significance. Trump’s presidency was marked by a contentious relationship with truth and an embattled media landscape that often struggled to pierce the veil of falsehoods. The tale’s relevance is further amplified by scholars like Naomi Wood, who highlight a post-9/11 shift towards accepting narratives that, while blatantly false, are forcibly embedded into public consciousness.

The Rutland protest, therefore, isn’t just a theatrical stunt. It’s a calculated invocation of a narrative that questions not just the truthfulness of leaders but the complicity of the societies over which they govern. The protesters, through the imagery of Andersen’s tale, aren’t merely mocking a political figure but are critiquing an entire political and cultural milieu that allows, and often encourages, the parade of falsehoods to continue unchallenged.

Conclusion: The Power of Fairy Tales

Andersen’s works, particularly The Emperor’s New Clothes, survive not merely as children’s stories but as potent socio-political critiques that continue to offer insights into modern governance and public accountability. They remind us that the power of truth often lies with those perceived to have the least influence – the innocents or the outsiders who see beyond the fabric of the political spectacle.

The Rutland County protest, and its vivid portrayal of Trump as Andersen’s deluded emperor, serves as a stark reminder of the enduring power of literary parallels in political commentary. It’s a call to recognize and challenge the cloaks of deception woven by modern-day emperors, urging us to listen more attentively to the “voices of innocence” that might save us from the farcical parades of our times.
