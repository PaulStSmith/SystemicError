‘We have to do something about it!’: Trump unleashes bizarre underwear rant at rally

Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump attends a rally in Coachella, California, U.S., October 12, 2024. REUTERS/Mike Blake

President Donald Trump once again dove into familiar grievances during a rally-style speech on Friday in which he relitigated the 2020 election – and complained at length about the FBI search of Mar-a-Lago, including what he said happened to First Lady Melania Trump’s underwear drawer.

As he has repeatedly done, Trump returned to his false, five-year-old claims that the 2020 election was “rigged,” telling the crowd that Americans went to bed believing he had won, only to wake up to a different result. He again suggested the outcome was fraudulent, adding: “By the way, you have not heard the last of that.”

Trump then pivoted to the 2022 FBI search of his Mar-a-Lago beach resort, framing it as a personal and political attack. He told the audience he was not home when agents arrived to conduct “a raid of a popular president, a former president,” before launching into an unusual digression about his wife.

Describing Melanie Trump as “a very meticulous person,” Trump told the crowd of rallygoers that the First Lady makes sure “everything is perfect” with her belongings.

“Her undergarments – always nice, sometimes referred to as panties – are folded perfect, wrapped. They’re like, so perfect. I think that she steams them, makes sure they’re just sitting.”

According to the president, that order was disrupted during the search. “She opened the drawers, and it was not that way,” he said. “They were a mess. It was all over the place.” He claimed agents also went into his son’s room, declaring, “These thugs are disgusting, and we cannot let them get away with this stuff.”

“We have all the evidence now,” Trump added. “We have to do something about it! It’s illegal, and it’s disgusting.”

As Trump’s speech passed the one-hour mark, people in the crowd began to walk out, The New York Times reported Friday. Some rallygoers on stage behind the MAGA leader could also be seen on a live feed leaving the stage as Trump continued to speak for nearly 90 minutes.

“If I didn’t get elected, our country would be ruined; we would’ve not have had a country,” he told the crowd. “I’m not bragging about myself; I’m saying we would not have had a country. And now we have the greatest country in the world. I’m telling you – we’re the hottest.”

Trump made no mention of the release of records related to Jeffrey Epstein during his Friday night remarks in Rocky Mount, North Carolina.

Our Analysis:

A Dive into the Abyss of Trump’s Rally Rhetoric

In a performance that can only be described as a theatrical display of grievance politics, President Donald Trump once again took to the stage, this time in Rocky Mount, North Carolina, to rehash and reframe narratives that have long been debunked and dismissed by both the courts and the court of public opinion. His speech, a blend of personal vendetta and political theatre, ventured into territories that ranged from the absurd to the alarming.

The Unyielding Specter of the 2020 Election

Trump’s insistence on revisiting the 2020 election is akin to a broken record, stuck on replay, refusing to acknowledge the music has stopped. His claims, described as false and five-year-old, that the election was “rigged,” not only undermine the democratic process but also dangerously inflame tensions among the American populace. It’s critical to understand the implications of such rhetoric; it’s not merely about a lost election but about an ongoing assault on the very foundations of democracy. The fact that he ominously hinted, “By the way, you have not heard the last of that,” suggests a troubling future narrative arc that could further destabilize political discourse.

The Mar-a-Lago Search: A Personal Affront?

The segue into the FBI search of Mar-a-Lago was both jarring and revealing. Portraying the legal search as a “raid” on a “popular president, a former president,” Trump attempted to cast himself as the victim of a political witch hunt. The diversion into a detailed account of Melania Trump’s underwear drawer is not only bizarre but disturbingly trivializes the gravity of the situation. The FBI’s search, conducted under legal warrants, is part of a broader investigation into potential mishandling of classified documents, a matter of national security. Yet, Trump’s narrative focused more on the alleged disarray of lingerie than on the legal and ethical implications of the search itself.

The Cult of Personality and the Disillusioned Crowd

As Trump’s monologue breached the one-hour mark, the response from the crowd was telling. The New York Times reported that people began to walk out, a visual and literal representation of dwindling patience and perhaps, dwindling support. This moment encapsulates the broader issue at hand: the cult of personality that Trump has cultivated is not infallible. The spectacle of rallygoers, once hanging on his every word, now turning their backs and walking away, speaks volumes.

The Omission of Epstein

Trump’s failure to address the release of records related to Jeffrey Epstein during his remarks is a glaring omission, given the current climate of accountability and transparency. This silence may speak louder than any of his bombastic claims or grievances aired during the speech. It raises questions about selective outrage and the strategic avoidance of topics that might not serve his narrative or personal agenda.

Conclusion

In sum, Trump’s latest foray into public speaking was a masterclass in diversion, deflection, and the politics of personal grievance. The re-litigation of the 2020 election, the peculiar fixation on personal anecdotes during a federal investigation, and the selective silence on critical issues paint a picture of a political figure more concerned with personal vendettas than with the pressing issues facing the nation.

As observers and analysts, we must sift through the noise, challenge the falsehoods, and demand accountability, regardless of political affiliation. The health of our democracy depends on it.
