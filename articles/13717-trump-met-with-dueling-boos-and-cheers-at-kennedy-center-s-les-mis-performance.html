Trump met with dueling boos and cheers at Kennedy Center's 'Les Mis' performance

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump attend “Les Miserables” opening night at the Kennedy Center in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 11, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A chorus of loud boos and cheers greeted President Donald Trump on Wednesday night at the newly MAGAfied John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, where Trump was set to watch "Les Misérables."

Video posted on X by Reuters' White House Correspondent Jeff Mason showed Trump take his seat in the direct center of the second level of the theater. As Trump waved to fellow attendees, loud cheers erupted, followed by dueling — and equally loud — boos.

The crowd broke out into chants of "U.S.A." as the president took his seat.

Trump dismissed the entire board of trustees for the Kennedy Center, including its long-serving chair. He then appointed himself as chairman in February and installed Richard Grenell as interim executive director and later president.

Several actors from the touring production of "Les Misérables" planned to boycott the performance at the Kennedy Center over Trump and Vice President JD Vance's appearance. Wednesday marked Trump's first visit to the center since he took control of the institution and vowed to do away with so-called "woke" programming, such as drag shows.

Our Analysis:

The Theatrical Politics of the MAGAfied Kennedy Center

In a scene more reminiscent of a Shakespearean drama than a night out at the theater, President Donald Trump's recent visit to the "MAGAfied" John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts became a spectacle of division, emblematic of the polarized state of our nation. Trump, whose presidency has been a series of contentious and often controversial decisions, decided to grace "Les Misérables" with his presence, only to be met with a cacophony of boos and cheers, a vivid reflection of the discord that runs through the veins of America.

Bold Moves and Boisterous Crowds

The atmosphere was charged, to say the least. Trump, taking his seat at the epicenter of the theater, was greeted with a symphony of reaction. The crowd's response—a mix of cheers, boos, and chants of "U.S.A."—was not just a spontaneous outburst but a mirror to the deeply entrenched divisions that have come to define the Trump era. The very act of attending the theater, particularly a production as charged with themes of rebellion and justice as "Les Misérables," became a political statement in itself.

A Self-Appointed Chair in a Self-Centered Move

In what can only be described as a bold power grab, Trump dismissed the entire board of trustees for the Kennedy Center, including its long-serving chair, and appointed himself as chairman. This maneuver is not just a testament to Trump's authoritarian inclinations but also a stark illustration of his desire to reshape the cultural landscape in his own image. Richard Grenell's appointment as interim executive director and later president further cements this, signaling a shift towards a MAGA-centric cultural policy.

The Boycott and the Battle Against "Woke" Programming

Several actors from "Les Misérables" took a stand by planning to boycott the performance, a move that signals the growing resistance against the Trump administration's efforts to politicize and monopolize cultural spaces. Trump's vow to eliminate "woke" programming, such as drag shows, from the Kennedy Center is not just an attack on diversity and inclusivity but a clear indication of his administration's broader cultural war agenda. This isn't about preserving tradition; it's about enforcing conformity.

The Kennedy Center as a Cultural Battlefield

The Kennedy Center, once a beacon of artistic freedom and expression, has now been thrust into the center of a cultural battlefield. Trump's intervention is a direct challenge to the autonomy of artistic institutions and their role in society. By imposing his own vision and stifling dissent, Trump is not just remaking the Kennedy Center in his image; he's attempting to rewrite the cultural narrative of the nation.

In Conclusion

Trump's appearance at the Kennedy Center, amidst a divided crowd, is not merely an isolated incident but a manifestation of the broader cultural and political divide engulfing the nation. His takeover of the Kennedy Center is a microcosm of his presidency—divisive, authoritarian, and always courting controversy. As the boos and cheers echo through the halls of the Kennedy Center, they remind us that the battle for the soul of our cultural institutions is far from over. It's a stark reminder that in Trump's America, even the arts are a battleground.
