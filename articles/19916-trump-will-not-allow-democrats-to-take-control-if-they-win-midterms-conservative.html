Trump will 'not allow Democrats to take control' if they win midterms: conservative

Robert Kagan, a conservative historian who has written extensively about American foreign policy, warned CNN’s Christiane Amanpour that President Donald Trump is likely to meddle in the 2026 midterm elections and thereby take “one big step into dictatorship.”

“The U.S. Congress — both parties — are unwilling to really fight Trump,” Kagan told Amanpour after noting that only ordinary people have been standing up to the president.

“The Republicans have become the party of dictatorship,” he added, arguing that Democrats have so far been inadequate in standing up to the president.

“I am worried, as I have said and others have been pointing out, about whether we will even have free and fair elections in 2026, let alone in 2028,” Kagan said. “I think Trump has a plan to disrupt those elections, and I don’t think he’s willing to allow Democrats to take control of one or both houses as could happen in a free election.”

When Amanpour suggested Republican support for Trump might be cracking because he is pulling ICE from Minnesota and six Republican legislators refused to vote with him on tariffs against Canada.

“I don’t agree with that,” Kagan replied. “I mean, it’s always one step forward and two steps back with Donald Trump.”

Speaking to NPR earlier this month, Kagan pointed out that Trump has repeatedly said he would like to be a dictator. Furthermore, Trump has said that if Democrats claim the House of Representatives in the 2026 midterm elections, he will be impeached, so he cannot allow that to happen.

“It’s clear that he has no intention of allowing the elections to play out and allow a Democratic victory,” Kagan said. “And I think it’s important to understand his motives here. He knows perfectly well that, in effect, his presidency will be greatly diminished once the Democrats take either one or both of the Houses.”

He added, “He himself is saying right now that he’ll be impeached, and that is why he wants to prevent the Democrats from taking power.”

In January Kagan wrote in The Atlantic that Trump’s foreign policy will also consolidate his authoritarianism by isolating America from the rest of the world.

“Americans are entering the most dangerous world they have known since World War II, one that will make the Cold War look like child’s play and the post-Cold War world like paradise,” Kagan wrote. “In fact, this new world will look a lot like the world prior to 1945, with multiple great powers and metastasizing competition and conflict. The U.S. will have no reliable friends or allies and will have to depend entirely on its own strength to survive and prosper.”

Because this will require increased military spending and America will have lost much of its access to world markets, global privileges Americans previously took for granted “will no longer come as a benefit of the country’s alliances. Instead, they will have to be contested and defended against other great powers.”

Kagan is not alone among Republicans warning that Trump plans on stealing the midterm elections. Speaking to David Frum of The Atlantic, former Republican county recorder of Maricopa County Stephen Richer (who held office when Trump tried to overturn Arizona’s 2020 election) said: “This is the first instance in which I could begin to believe that something truly spectacular is going to happen in which our 2026 midterm elections are not administered like past elections have been.”

Conservatives also warned that Trump’s unpopular economic policies could cost him support in the midterm elections.

“Voters are rarely able to connect policy to outcomes, but they have done so in the case of tariffs,” wrote conservative columnist Mona Charen for The Bulwark on Thursday. “Back in 2024, Americans were about equally divided on the question of trade, with some favoring higher tariffs and roughly similar numbers opting for lower tariffs. Experience has changed their views.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Alleged Road to Dictatorship: A Scathing Analysis

In a recent interview with CNN’s Christiane Amanpour, Robert Kagan, a conservative historian known for his extensive works on American foreign policy, delivered a dire warning that reads like a script from a dystopian playbook. Kagan asserted that President Donald Trump is gearing up to meddle in the 2026 midterm elections, a move he describes as a “one big step into dictatorship.” This isn’t a light accusation, especially coming from someone within the conservative camp. Kagan’s analysis, steeped in a mixture of historical insight and contemporary observation, paints a grim picture of the current political landscape in the United States.

The Complicit Silence of Congress

Kagan’s criticism doesn’t spare the legislative branch. According to him, the U.S. Congress, across both parties, has shown a disturbing unwillingness to stand up to Trump’s antics. This isn’t just about partisan politics; it’s about the very essence of democracy and the checks and balances that are supposed to prevent any slide into authoritarianism. When he says, “The Republicans have become the party of dictatorship,” it’s a scorching indictment of how far the GOP has strayed from its principles in the Trump era. The Democrats aren’t let off the hook either, criticized for their inadequacy in mounting a formidable opposition.

The Fear of a Rigged Election

Kagan’s worries extend beyond mere political squabbles, diving into the very integrity of the electoral process. His fear that the 2026 and 2028 elections might not be “free and fair” is a chilling reminder of how fragile democracy can be when faced with a leader determined to cling to power at any cost. Trump’s alleged plan to disrupt the elections and prevent a Democratic victory is, as Kagan suggests, a clear sign of his dictatorial ambitions. This isn’t democracy; it’s despotism draped in the flag.

Ignoring the Signs at Our Peril

Kagan isn’t a lone voice in the wilderness. His concerns are echoed by others, including Stephen Richer, a former Republican county recorder who witnessed Trump’s attempts to overturn Arizona’s 2020 election results firsthand. When figures with insider knowledge and historical context start ringing alarm bells, it’s time to listen. Yet, the reaction from many quarters has been lukewarm at best, with a dangerous tendency to dismiss these warnings as hyperbole.

Trump’s Dictatorial Ambitions

Perhaps the most damning aspect of Kagan’s critique is the acknowledgment of Trump’s own words. Trump has not been shy about his disdain for democratic norms, openly expressing his admiration for dictatorial powers. The idea that he would see impeachment as a certainty if the Democrats win power isn’t just political strategy; it’s an admission of guilt. Kagan’s analysis suggests that Trump’s motives aren’t just about maintaining power but about escaping accountability.

Isolationism as Authoritarian Enabler

Kagan’s January piece in The Atlantic offers an even broader context for Trump’s authoritarian tendencies. By pushing America into isolationism, Trump isn’t just altering foreign policy; he’s setting the stage for a more militarized, authoritarian state. This isn’t just about losing allies or market access; it’s about fundamentally changing what America stands for on the global stage and at home.

The Economic Angle

Even on the economic front, Trump’s policies, particularly tariffs, have become a point of contention. Conservative columnist Mona Charen points out that American voters are connecting the dots between Trump’s policies and their outcomes, particularly in trade. This rare moment of voter clarity should be a wake-up call for Trump, but it’s more likely to be another reason for him to double down on his efforts to undermine the electoral process.

Conclusion

Robert Kagan’s warning is a siren call to all who cherish democracy. The signs of a potential slide into dictatorship under Trump are not just apparent; they’re flashing in neon lights. The silence and complicity of Congress, the alarming rhetoric from Trump himself, and the ominous shifts in foreign policy all point to a dangerous path ahead. Ignoring these warnings, or dismissing them as partisan fear-mongering, could have devastating consequences for the United States and the world. The time to act is now, before the predictions of historians like Kagan become our irreversible reality.
