During a wide-ranging Oval Office question-and-answer session on Friday, President Donald Trump was asked about former President Joe Biden, who is now battling an aggressive form of cancer. Trump told reporters he does not feel sorry for his predecessor.

“He’s been a sort of a moderate person over his lifetime,” President Trump said, “not a smart person, but a somewhat vicious person, I will say.”

“If you feel sorry for him, don’t feel so sorry ’cause he’s vicious, what he did with his political, all of the people that he hurt, he hurt a lot of people by and so I really don’t feel sorry for him.”

Earlier this month aboard Air Force One, Trump told Fox News’ Sean Hannity, “Biden is a vicious person. Biden’s a stupid person. He’s a low IQ person, but he’s vicious and that’s a bad combination,” according to a transcript from Roll Call.

President Joe Biden beat Donald Trump in the 2020 election by over seven million votes. Biden won 51.3% of the vote, Trump received only 46.8%. Trump has baselessly maintained the election was “rigged.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Comments on Biden: A Display of Political Vindictiveness

In a recent display of what can only be described as unbridled vindictiveness, President Donald Trump has launched a scathing attack on former President Joe Biden, amidst Biden's battle with an aggressive form of cancer. Trump's lack of empathy is not just shocking; it's a revealing testament to his character, or the lack thereof. His remarks, “He’s been a sort of a moderate person over his lifetime,” followed by, “not a smart person, but a somewhat vicious person, I will say,” are not just distasteful but deeply troubling.

The Irony of Accusations

Trump's choice to label Biden as “vicious” and “not a smart person” is not just ironic but blatantly hypocritical. This is coming from a figure whose tenure in office was marked by relentless attacks on political adversaries, often veering into personal territory. Trump's assertion that one should not feel sorry for Biden because he is “vicious” and has “hurt a lot of people” could very well be a reflection of his own political playbook—one he executed with gusto during his presidency.

The Disregard for Human Decency

Trump's remarks about Biden, especially in light of Biden's current health challenges, underscore a complete and utter disregard for basic human decency. The fact that these comments were made during an Oval Office question-and-answer session adds a layer of official sanction to his vitriol, making them all the more reprehensible. It's one thing to engage in political rivalry; it's another to kick someone when they're down, battling a life-threatening illness.

The Election Resentment

Perhaps Trump's inability to show even a modicum of empathy stems from his continued resentment over the 2020 election results. His baseless claims that the election was “rigged” have been debunked time and again. Yet, he clings to these falsehoods, possibly as a mechanism to justify his loss to Biden—who won the election by over seven million votes, securing 51.3% of the vote to Trump's 46.8%.

Conclusion: The True Measure of Viciousness

Trump's comments are not just an attack on Biden; they are an attack on the very principles of compassion and empathy. To take aim at someone at their most vulnerable is, by every metric, the true measure of viciousness. Trump's inability to rise above personal vendettas—even in the face of someone's serious illness—reveals a deep-seated malice that should concern us all.

In a political landscape already fraught with division, Trump's comments do nothing but deepen the chasm of civility. It's a sobering reminder of the toxic political culture that has been fostered by figures like Trump, who prioritize personal grievances over basic human kindness. As we reflect on these comments, let us not forget the importance of empathy, decency, and the collective human experience—values that seem to be in short supply in certain political quarters.
