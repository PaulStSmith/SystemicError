Trump admin 'caught in a lie' about massive IRS layoffs: reporter

By Paulo Santos

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks before departing for Scotland, on the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 25, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump's administration has been "caught in a lie" about its massive cuts to government agencies, including the IRS, CNN senior reporter Marshall Cohen reported Tuesday.

Cohen cited the release of a report from the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration, which found that probationary IRS employees were terminated "without following internal procedures and considering individual performance."

"The Trump administration fired 7,300+ probationary IRS employees in February, with a termination letter stating: 'taking into account your performance... your continued employment... is not in the public interest,'" said Cohen.

Of those, 51% of newer probationary employees "didn't even have a performance evaluation on record yet. And for the other employees who had been evaluated, 99% (!) were deemed 'fully successful' at their jobs, or better," Cohen added.

Being on probation doesn't mean that the employee did something wrong; rather, it means they're new and there is a probationary period before

The New York Times reported in April that before Trump took office, there were about 100,000 IRS employees. Since, however, 22,000 employees took resignation offers and 7,000 probationary employees were let go. The report said it means "the agency would be on track to lose about a third of its work force this year."

The firings fell under Trump's effort to significantly reduce the workforce in the federal government under the guise that it would be more efficient. He tasked tech billionaire Elon Musk with the job.

Our Analysis:

The Trump Administration's IRS Firings: A Cloak of Efficiency Hides a Dagger of Deception

In a revelation that reeks of hypocrisy and mismanagement, the Trump administration has once again been caught with its hand in the proverbial cookie jar. This time, the jar is filled with the careers of over 7,000 probationary IRS employees, unceremoniously dumped in the name of "public interest". CNN's Marshall Cohen brings this to light, backed by a damning report from the Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration. Let's dissect this debacle, where efficiency appears to be a euphemism for evisceration.

The Massacre of Probationary Employees

The Trump administration, in a move that was as brazen as it was callous, terminated over 7,300 probationary IRS employees in February. The justification? A nebulous "public interest". Yet, the Treasury Inspector General's report uncovers a disturbing lack of due process. A staggering 51% of these employees hadn't even had the chance to prove their worth through a performance evaluation. Of the ones who did, 99% were deemed 'fully successful' or better. This isn't just a failure to follow internal procedures; it's a blatant disregard for fairness and evidence-based decision-making.

The Illusion of Efficiency

Let's address the elephant in the room: the Trump administration's obsession with "reducing the workforce for efficiency". Handing Elon Musk the reins to this charade only adds a layer of Silicon Valley sheen to a deeply flawed policy. Efficiency is critical, but when it becomes a smokescreen for indiscriminate cuts, it's a problem. The narrative that a leaner IRS would somehow be more effective is laughable when considering the complexity and volume of work the agency handles. This isn't trimming the fat; it's hacking off limbs and expecting the body to function better.

A Pattern of Deception

This incident is not an anomaly but a symptom of a larger disease plaguing the Trump administration: a pattern of deception and mismanagement. Time and again, we've seen policies and decisions cloaked in the guise of public interest, only to reveal a lack of foresight, empathy, and sometimes, basic competence. The firing of 7,000+ IRS employees under dubious circumstances is a stark reminder of this administration's willingness to sacrifice individuals and integrity on the altar of so-called efficiency.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

The IRS firings are more than just a failure to follow procedure; they are indicative of a systemic error in how the Trump administration views governance. It's not about making government work better for the people; it's about slashing and burning without regard for the consequences. Efficiency at the cost of effectiveness is no virtue. The public deserves better, and the dismissed IRS employees deserved a fair evaluation. In the end, the only thing this debacle has efficiently achieved is exposing the administration's disregard for due process and its penchant for punitive measures over pragmatic governance.

In a world where actions should speak louder than words, this administration's actions are a cacophony of chaos. As we sift through the wreckage of policies like these, one can only hope that future administrations will learn from these mistakes. But until then, we're left to ponder the real cost of "efficiency" — and who's paying the price.
