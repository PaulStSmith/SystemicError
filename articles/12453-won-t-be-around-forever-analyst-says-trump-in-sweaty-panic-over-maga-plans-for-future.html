'Won't be around forever': Analyst says Trump in 'sweaty panic' over MAGA plans for future

U.S. President Donald Trump wears a ‘Make America Great Again’ (MAGA) hat as he attends the commencement ceremony at West Point Military Academy in West Point, New York, U.S., May 24, 2025. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

Donald Trump's latest verbal fumble has revealed more than just his declining mental state—it's exposed a growing tension within MAGA about life after their aging leader, a columnist wrote Wednesday,

The president’s desperate—and so far unsuccessful—scramble to kill the Jeffrey Epstein story—has brought into sharp focus that MAGA is very aware that he can’t be around for long, Amanda Marcotte wrote for Salon.

And it’s refusing to give up on a core conspiracy that fuels its base just to let their aging figurehead off the hook.

“This is also a story about his mortality,” Marcotte wrote.

“Every day, Trump gets older in ways his base can’t completely ignore. If they want their movement to continue after him, MAGA leaders and influencers need to build brands and power separate from the president and what serves his interests.

“The right-wing media mill needs content, and speculating about what’s in the Epstein files provides it. Giving that up is going to be hard for MAGA — especially as they look at the aging Trump and realize he won’t be around forever.”

During a Pittsburgh roundtable, the 79-year-old president bizarrely confused Jeffrey Epstein with his uncle John Trump, spinning a false tale about the Unabomber,” Marcotte wrote.

“Trump claimed his MIT professor uncle taught Ted Kaczynski and called him a "seriously good" student. The problem? John Trump died in 1985, eleven years before Kaczynski was identified as the Unabomber, and Kaczynski attended Harvard, not MIT.

The story bears striking resemblance to a fabricated tale Epstein used to tell. According to Stuart Pivar, an Epstein acquaintance who spoke to Mother Jones in 2019, "Jeffrey told me that he studied math at UCLA with the Unabomber." That story was also false—Epstein never attended UCLA.

Trump's physical decline is becoming impossible to ignore. Despite his "elaborate combover, heavy orange bronzer and poorly-applied makeup," his advanced age shows, Marcotte wrote.

The White House recently admitted Trump has been diagnosed with a vein condition after his legs visibly swelled. His vocabulary has shrunk dramatically—when denying he sent a nude doodle to Epstein, Trump said, "I never wrote a picture in my life."

This deterioration terrifies MAGA influencers who've built empires around Trump worship, Marcotte wrote.

Figures like Charlie Kirk and Candace Owens face an impossible balancing act: maintaining absolute loyalty while preparing for a post-Trump future. Their solution? Conspiracy theories, particularly around Epstein.

The Epstein files controversy perfectly illustrates this tension, the columnist wrote. While Trump desperately wants the story buried, MAGA media needs the content to survive without him.

Trump's panic is evident in his frantic distraction attempts—threatening to arrest Obama, strip Rosie O'Donnell's citizenship, and demanding sports teams revert to racist names, Marcotte wrote.

Trump's "sweaty panic" isn't just about potential embarrassment—it's about his mortality and MAGA's existential crisis as they confront a future without their aging godhead, Marcotte finished.

Our Analysis:

The Mortality of MAGA and the Desperation of Donald Trump: A Scathing Analysis

Amanda Marcotte's recent column in Salon serves not just as a critique of Donald Trump's latest verbal misstep but as a piercing analysis of the existential crisis gripping the MAGA movement. Through a blend of factual recounting and sharp commentary, Marcotte dissects Trump's bumbling attempt to distance himself from the Jeffrey Epstein scandal, using it as a lens to explore the broader anxieties within his base about the sustainability of their movement post-Trump. Let's break down the key points and add our own Systemic Error flavor to this analysis.

Trump's Mental and Physical Decline: The Unignorable Elephant in the Room

Trump's recent confusion between Jeffrey Epstein and his uncle John Trump during a Pittsburgh roundtable is not just a "verbal fumble"; it's a glaring spotlight on his declining mental state. Marcotte's recounting of this bizarre episode, where Trump fabricates a connection between his uncle and the Unabomber, Ted Kaczynski, is emblematic of a man struggling to tether his narratives to reality. This isn't just about age; it's about the sustainability of a movement hitched to the wagon of a leader whose faculties are visibly waning.

Marcotte notes Trump's physical decline, marked by a reliance on "elaborate combover, heavy orange bronzer, and poorly-applied makeup" to mask the evident toll of age. The recent admission from the White House regarding Trump's vein condition only adds to the mounting evidence of his physical deterioration. When Trump bizarrely claims, "I never wrote a picture in my life," in an attempt to deny sending a nude doodle to Epstein, it's not just a reduction in vocabulary we're witnessing; it's a desperate grasp at coherence. MAGA's figurehead is crumbling, and with him, the facade of invincibility that has buoyed the movement.

The MAGA Media Machine: Conspiracy as Lifeblood

Marcotte deftly points out that MAGA influencers are caught in a Catch-22. Figures like Charlie Kirk and Candace Owens have built lucrative empires on the back of Trump worship, yet they must now navigate the treacherous waters of a future without their central figure. The solution, as Marcotte outlines, is a deeper dive into the world of conspiracy theories, with the Epstein files controversy serving as a perfect storm.

Conspiracy theories have always been a cornerstone of the MAGA movement, providing a seemingly endless well of content to rally the base and distract from the more unpalatable realities of their leader's failings. But as Trump's attempts to bury the Epstein story reveal, there's a palpable sense of desperation at play. Threatening to arrest Obama, strip Rosie O'Donnell of her citizenship, and demand sports teams revert to racist names are not the actions of a confident leader; they are the flailings of a man in "sweaty panic," terrified of the potential fallout from his associations and increasingly aware of his own mortality.

The Existential Crisis of MAGA: Looking Beyond Trump

Marcotte's analysis concludes on a note of existential dread for the MAGA movement, a sentiment we share. The realization that Trump "can't be around for long" has laid bare the fractures within MAGA, as leaders and influencers scramble to build brands and power bases that can survive the inevitable transition. This is not just about finding a new figurehead; it's about the sustainability of a movement built on a foundation of idolatry, misinformation, and divisiveness.

The MAGA movement is at a crossroads, faced with the daunting task of envisioning a future without its aging, faltering leader. The reliance on conspiracy theories and the refusal to disengage from debunked narratives reveal a movement in crisis, unsure of its direction in a post-Trump world.

In conclusion, Marcotte's column is a sobering reminder of the fragility of movements built around singular, charismatic leaders. As Trump's physical and mental state continue to draw concern, the MAGA movement must confront the reality of its own mortality. The question remains: Can MAGA evolve beyond its godhead, or will it crumble under the weight of its own contradictions and the declining faculties of its leader? Only time will tell, but the signs are not promising.
