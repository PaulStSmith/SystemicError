'Just walking around': Even National Guard troops don't know why they're in DC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 30, 20255 min read

National Guard soldiers were deployed to Washington, D.C., earlier this month, but since arriving, they haven't done much. Two reporters at The Atlantic found that even the soldiers have no idea what their mission is.

Photos captured them standing around in extreme heat and humidity. Last week, they were seen grabbing ice cream from a food truck and guarding the Krispy Kreme. In the past few days, it seems to have evolved into Guard soldiers picking up trash and spreading mulch around the landscaping in the city.

Writing on Friday, Ashley Parker and Nancy A. Youssef described some of the answers that they got when asking the soldiers why they're there and what their specific mission is.

“We’re the president’s patrol, ma’am,” said a small group of South Carolina Guard soldiers standing along the waterfront.

Another group characterized their mission as, "Just walkin’ around."

A third group walking along the National Mall told reporters that they were from West Virginia. When asked what they were doing, they confessed it was nothing more than “Smiling and waving."

Even in areas where the Guard has a big presence, it isn't necessarily helping reduce crime. The report described a stabbing that happened at a major intersection along the "H" Street corridor at 3 p.m. on a Wednesday. Guard soldiers had spent the day walking back and forth through the area, and a group of three were having lunch on the patio nearby when it happened. The suspect escaped.

"So safe,” a neighborhood resident texted the reporters sarcastically.

The report noted that this was the first week that the Guard soldiers were standing around while heavily armed. Earlier this month, they were unarmed. The city was in strong opposition to the takeover before the soldiers were armed, but now, citizens are beginning additional measures.

"The occupation has chilled life in the city, especially in neighborhoods with large immigrant populations: quiet playgrounds, empty restaurants, fewer street vendors, fewer food-delivery scooters," the report said. "Nannies have stayed home, and house cleaners have canceled. Some mixed-status families are keeping their children home from school or skipping work until the federal focus moves on, or they’re only leaving home when absolutely necessary."

The report also noted that as students came back to school this week, "local parent-teacher groups organized impromptu 'walking buses'—volunteers willing to help walk to and from school kids whose parents don’t feel safe doing so."

The report explained that while the National Guard has become the face of the occupation, the reality is that the federal agents are the ones who are tackling delivery drivers to the ground and chasing people with tail lights out.

"For those who feel afraid, it’s in many respects the least of their worries. The Guardsmen themselves have generally behaved more like a notional guard than a national one," the report explained.

While the mayor acknowledged that there has been a decrease in crime in the city, there has also been a significant decline in overall activity. Local reports revealed that even during the famed summer "Restaurant Week," there was a 31% decrease in restaurant reservations. Those empty playgrounds and fewer street vendors aren't sustainable in the long term. Once the federal agents are gone and the crowds return, crime is likely to follow.

Our Analysis:

A Misguided Mission: The National Guard's D.C. Deployment

The recent deployment of National Guard soldiers in Washington, D.C., as vividly described by Ashley Parker and Nancy A. Youssef of The Atlantic, paints a picture that is both absurd and alarming. The soldiers, seemingly without a clear mission, have been relegated to tasks ranging from guarding doughnut shops to spreading mulch. This scenario prompts a critical examination of not just the deployment's effectiveness but also its impact on the community and the broader implications for democracy.

The Aimless Guardians

The heart of the issue lies in the ambiguous nature of the soldiers' mission. Descriptions of their daily activities - “We’re the president’s patrol, ma’am,” “Just walkin’ around,” and “Smiling and waving" - reflect not just a lack of direction but a profound misunderstanding of military deployment's seriousness. It's ludicrous to think that in a city as politically significant as D.C., the best use of the National Guard is to have them perform as glorified greeters and landscapers.

A False Sense of Security?

The claim that the presence of these soldiers isn’t necessarily helping reduce crime adds insult to injury. A stabbing incident in broad daylight, in proximity to the Guard, underscores the inefficacy of this strategy. Deploying soldiers heavily armed in a civilian context, especially without a clear mission, does not equate to increased safety. Instead, it introduces a militarized tension into daily life, which is counterproductive and potentially dangerous.

The Chilling Effect

The broader social implications of this deployment are deeply concerning. The article outlines a city where life has been "chilled" - playgrounds quiet, restaurants empty, and daily routines disrupted, particularly in immigrant communities. This isn't just about the inconvenience of decreased business or the eeriness of empty streets; it's about a community living in fear. The fact that mixed-status families feel compelled to keep their children home from school speaks volumes about the perceived threat posed by this federal presence.

Misplaced Priorities

While the mayor points to a decrease in crime, one must question the cost at which this comes. A 31% decrease in restaurant reservations during a key economic period like "Restaurant Week" is indicative of a deeper malaise. The temporary suppression of crime, achieved by creating an atmosphere of fear and uncertainty, is no victory. As the report rightly suggests, once the federal agents and National Guard leave, the underlying issues will remain, potentially exacerbated by the interruption of community life and economic activity.

Conclusion

The deployment of the National Guard in Washington, D.C., as detailed by The Atlantic, is a stark reminder of the dangers of employing military forces without clear objectives or understanding of the consequences. The impact on the community, characterized by fear, economic downturn, and a general atmosphere of unease, is a price too high for the purported benefits. This misguided mission serves as a cautionary tale of the misuse of power and the essential need for clear, justified, and community-informed objectives in any military deployment within the United States. In the absence of such principles, we risk not only the erosion of trust in our military and government but also the very fabric of our communities.
