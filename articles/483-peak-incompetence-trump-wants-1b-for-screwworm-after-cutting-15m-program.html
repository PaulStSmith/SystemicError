'Peak incompetence': Trump wants $1B for screwworm after cutting $15M program

Based on an article by Stephen Prager

The Price of Pretending Cuts Are Policy

The People Actually In Charge

This story is not about an abstract bureaucracy failing. It is about power doing what power always does when it can dress itself up as thrift: cut the safeguards, break the system, then bill the public for the repair.

The actors with actual authority were Elon Musk’s DOGE operation and the Trump administration. DOGE terminated a $15 million annual USDA effort to monitor screwworm spread and cut related global animal-disease funding in March 2025. Then, when the infestation moved toward Texas and New Mexico, the administration arrived at the obvious emergency with a price tag of at least $1 billion.

How the Damage Was Made

The mechanism here is familiar: attack the prevention layer, then act shocked when the problem becomes expensive. The source describes paused plans for a Mexico sterile-fly facility, mass layoffs at USDA, and nearly 1,900 APHIS employees gone in Trump’s first year back in office. That is not misfortune. That is administrative self-sabotage.

APhIS inspectors were the first line of defense against infected cattle imports from Mexico. Removing those people and delaying containment infrastructure does not “streamline” government. It disables it.

The Blame Game Is the Point

Brooke Rollins tried to push the outbreak backward onto the Biden administration while presenting herself and Trump as bold responders. That framing is convenient because it converts a self-inflicted failure into a partisan weather event.

But the facts in the source cut against that story. Biden authorized a $165 million emergency package and a Mexico facility in 2024. DOGE then paused the project. Funding did not reappear until May 2025. If an administration freezes the machinery of response and then complains that the machine is not working, that is not leadership. It is laundering responsibility through delay.

Efficiency as A Cover Story

DOGE sold itself as a purge of “waste.” In practice, it appears to have treated expertise, monitoring, and foreign containment capacity as disposable overhead. The result was predictable: a small annual prevention budget vanished, and the government now plans to spend at least $1 billion on a crisis it helped worsen.

That is the real logic of “efficiency” as practiced here. It is not about making government cheaper. It is about making public functions easier to break, then shifting the cost of failure onto everyone else.

The Beef Price Tax

The outbreak is not only a veterinary problem. It is a political-economic one. The source notes beef prices had already risen more than 20 percent since Trump returned to office, and the screwworm spread threatens to push them higher. So the public gets hit twice: first through the policy damage, then through the inflationary consequences.

This is the quiet brutality of bad governance. Cuts are sold as discipline. The bill arrives as scarcity, delay, and higher prices.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

The larger pattern is straightforward: performative anti-government politics tends to produce more expensive government. It strips institutions of capacity, mocks the people who know how to prevent disasters, and then demands emergency spending after the disaster lands.

The screwworm episode is not a fluke or a messaging problem. It is a case study in institutional vandalism followed by blame management. The administration broke the first line of defense, delayed the fix, and now wants credit for reacting to a crisis it helped manufacture.
