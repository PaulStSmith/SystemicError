The Cult of Applause: When Truth Dies and the Cheer Goes On

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 23, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The image is searing in its simplicity: an audience that cries for forgiveness, then cheers for hatred moments later. Erika Kirk, speaking of forgiving her enemy, elicited tears, claps, and standing ovations. Moments later, Donald Trump declared that he disagreed and that he hated his enemies. The same audience roared again, rising to their feet with the same fervor.

This is not a contradiction that bothers them. It is not hypocrisy, not even irony. It is the whole point. In the MAGA circus, applause is not about the message. It is about the man delivering it. Forgiveness or hate—either can be holy if Trump sanctifies it. The substance is irrelevant; the performance is everything.

The Logic of the Mob

What we are seeing here is a collapse of critical thought. When a crowd can cheer two opposite positions back-to-back, the message doesn’t matter. The leader matters. They have ceded their agency, outsourced their values, and reduced themselves to reflexive applause machines.

This is how cults function. The content of the sermon is secondary to the fact that the sermon is delivered by the anointed one. Cult members are not asked to think but to feel, to surrender to the emotional current provided by the leader. To cheer forgiveness one moment and hatred the next is not a failure of logic—it is proof of loyalty. It demonstrates that the leader’s words, not independent judgment, define truth.

And that’s the real danger: the obliteration of consistency as a democratic value. A functioning democracy relies on a shared understanding of reality, of basic cause-and-effect, of accountability to words and deeds. A cult obliterates this, replacing it with obedience.

The Anatomy of Fascist Applause

This scene crystallizes something fascists have always understood: truth doesn’t matter when spectacle can replace it. What matters is control of the crowd’s emotions. Benito Mussolini once said, “It is faith that moves mountains, not reason.” Trump has taken this lesson to heart. The goal is not to be consistent, but to be adored. Consistency is for weak men; charisma is for strongmen.

Notice how the audience didn’t hesitate. No pause to reconcile forgiveness with hatred. No reflection on moral contradiction. They switched gears instantly, like a dog responding to two different commands from the same master. That is not the behavior of free citizens; that is the behavior of conditioned subjects.

The political project here is naked: to transform the American public into an obedient mass, ready to cheer on command, regardless of what is said. When applause is detached from meaning, the leader is free to reshape reality at will. Today, forgiveness is holy; tomorrow, vengeance is divine. Both elicit the same roar, because the roar is not for ideas—it’s for power.

The Fraud of "Values Voters"

For decades, Republicans marketed themselves as the party of “values”—Christian values, family values, American values. Yet here lies the truth: the crowd will cheer both love and hate, both forgiveness and vengeance, as long as Trump is the one speaking. That’s not values. That’s nihilism wearing a red hat.

The so-called “values voters” have revealed themselves as frauds. Forgiveness, compassion, and love of enemy are central tenets of the Christianity they claim to uphold. Trump trashes them openly, and the crowd cheers. What does that say about their faith? It says their real religion isn’t Christianity—it’s Trumpism. Jesus preached forgiveness on the cross. Trump preaches hate at a rally. Guess who gets the standing ovation.

This is why so many evangelicals defend him no matter what he does. Their religion has been hollowed out and replaced with an idol. The cross has been swapped for a flag, the Bible for a MAGA hat, and Christ for a conman. It’s not Christianity. It’s personality cult masquerading as faith.

The Psychology of the Cheer

Why does this work? Why can a crowd so easily switch between two contradictory moral positions? The answer lies in the psychology of group identity. When people are swept up in a movement, belonging becomes more important than truth. The roar of the crowd, the sense of unity, the shared purpose—it feels good. It is intoxicating.

Add to this the charisma of a demagogue, and rational thought evaporates. The audience doesn’t ask whether forgiveness or hate is right. They ask whether Trump approves. His approval becomes the moral compass, his words the scripture. To cheer him is to affirm identity, to belong to something bigger than themselves. To hesitate would be to risk exclusion, to stand outside the mob.

This is why authoritarian leaders thrive on rallies. It’s not just about speeches; it’s about training. Each cheer is reinforcement, each ovation a rehearsal in obedience. The audience learns to cheer on command, to discard their own judgment, and to sync their emotions with the leader’s words. By the end, they’re not thinking citizens—they’re Pavlov’s dogs, salivating to the bell of demagoguery.

Democracy vs. Demagoguery

This is why Trump is such a threat to democracy. Democracy requires accountability. Demagoguery erases it. When crowds cheer both forgiveness and hate without question, the leader can say anything and get away with everything. Facts dissolve. Lies metastasize. Accountability vanishes.

This is not new in history. We’ve seen it in the rallies of fascist Italy, in the torchlit parades of Nazi Germany, in the staged ovations of Stalinist Russia. The details differ, but the mechanism is the same: cultivate loyalty so blind that contradiction itself becomes irrelevant. The leader says X today and Y tomorrow, and both are true, because truth is what the leader says it is.

That’s not democracy. That’s dictatorship with better marketing.

The American Disease

The scene also reveals a deeper American sickness: the obsession with spectacle. In a country where politics is consumed like entertainment, the rally becomes theater, and the audience becomes an audience in the literal sense—spectators, not participants. They are not engaged in deliberation. They are not grappling with the hard contradictions of morality and governance. They are watching a show and cheering their star.

This is why fact-checking Trump never worked. His audience isn’t listening for facts. They’re listening for vibes, for affirmation, for the thrill of transgression. He could contradict himself in the same breath, and it wouldn’t matter, because the content is irrelevant. The emotion is the product.

America has been primed for this by decades of culture that values entertainment over truth. From reality TV to cable news to social media, the line between politics and performance has been obliterated. Trump didn’t invent this disease—he simply weaponized it.

Forgiveness and Hatred in the Balance

The tragedy is that forgiveness and hatred are not morally equal. Forgiveness builds societies. Hatred tears them apart. To see the same crowd cheer both, back-to-back, is to watch the moral foundation of a nation crumble in real time.

It is not just hypocrisy. It is nihilism. It is proof that America’s problem is not merely political polarization, but something deeper: a people who no longer believe in consistent values at all, only in the ecstasy of cheering.

Forgiveness requires courage, empathy, and strength. Hatred requires only fear and resentment. By cheering both, the audience reveals it doesn’t care which wins—so long as they get the high of belonging. That is how democracies die: not with arguments about policy, but with mobs addicted to applause.

Conclusion: The Death of Truth

This image, this moment, is not trivial. It is not just another funny anecdote about Trump rallies. It is the canary in the coal mine. When the same audience cheers forgiveness and hatred in the same breath, we are witnessing the death of truth itself.

Without truth, democracy cannot function. Without consistency, morality collapses. All that remains is the leader, the mob, and the cheer. That is fascism in embryo. And that is America’s disease today.

The question is no longer whether Trump is consistent—he never was. The question is whether Americans can still recognize that consistency matters. If they cannot, then democracy is already gone, replaced by a cult of applause that will cheer itself into ruin.
