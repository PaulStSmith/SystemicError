'Going to slap us in the pocketbook': Trump alarms supply chain analyst with latest move

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 17, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump’s latest move in the war in Iran alarmed a supply chain analyst on Sunday.

Over the weekend, Trump called on other countries to help the U.S. clear the Strait of Hormuz, which the Iranian regime has fortified in response to the coordinated bombing campaign conducted by the U.S. and Israel that began in late February. About 20% of global liquefied natural gas and oil trade passes through the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran has said U.S. and Israeli ships can no longer transit the waterway.

That call for reinforcements came as U.S. forces struck Kharg Island, a main oil export hub for the Iranian regime. Iranian forces have also attacked multiple ships traveling through the Strait of Hormuz, according to reports.

CNBC Supply Chain Analyst Lori Ann LaRocco warned in an interview on Sunday that the Trump administration’s inability to make the Strait of Hormuz safe for international trade could have long-lasting implications.

“A lot of people that I’ve talked to, a lot of tanker owners, they’re very afraid of being a target,” LaRocco told MS NOW’s Alex Witt on Sunday. “We’ve already seen a tanker on fire. This is not a very easy remedy.”

LaRocco mentioned that prices for goods ranging from fertilizer to semiconductors have skyrocketed due to disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz.

“There’s so much that we’re not even thinking about that’s going to slap us in the pocketbook, not only now, but in the months to come,” LaRocco said.

Our Analysis:

The Strait of Hormuz Crisis: Dissecting the Blame Game

The recent escalations in the Strait of Hormuz under the Trump administration have sparked significant concern, not just politically, but also across global supply chains. The situation as described paints a picture of a geopolitical chess game with high stakes, involving the safety of international waters, the flow of a substantial percentage of the world’s oil and gas, and the economic implications of these tensions. However, a closer examination is required to untangle the web of responsibility and to challenge any potential misdirection in the narrative.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Who Holds the Power?

In this scenario, the primary actors with institutional power are the governments of the United States, Israel, and Iran. Each holds significant sway over their respective military actions and policies in the region. The United States and Israel, as allies coordinating a bombing campaign, and Iran, responding by fortifying the Strait of Hormuz, are directly involved in the escalation of tensions.

Decision Makers

The decision to conduct a coordinated bombing campaign was made by the leadership of the U.S. and Israel. President Donald Trump, representing the U.S., and his counterparts in the Israeli government, bear direct responsibility for initiating the actions that led to the current state of affairs. The Iranian regime’s decision to fortify the Strait of Hormuz and target ships is a direct countermeasure to these actions.

Misdirection and Framing

The article presents the situation as an alarming consequence of President Trump’s “latest move” in the conflict, focusing on the call for international help to secure the Strait of Hormuz. However, this framing potentially misdirects the responsibility towards Trump’s request for reinforcements, rather than the coordinated bombing campaign that precipitated Iran’s aggressive stance. While Trump’s call for assistance is indeed a reaction to the situation, it is not the root cause of the crisis.

Economic Implications

Supply chain analyst Lori Ann LaRocco’s insights highlight the broader economic ramifications of the conflict, beyond the immediate military and geopolitical consequences. The disruption in the Strait of Hormuz affects global trade, with tangible impacts on prices for goods ranging from fertilizer to semiconductors. This aspect underscores the far-reaching implications of decisions made by those in power, extending to everyday citizens worldwide.

Conclusion: The Real Culprits

The narrative surrounding the Strait of Hormuz crisis requires careful scrutiny to avoid misplaced blame. While President Trump’s call for international support to secure the Strait is a significant aspect of the situation, it is crucial to recognize it as a response to the escalated conflict, not the initial cause. The coordinated bombing campaign by the U.S. and Israel is the critical action that led to the current state of affairs. This decision, and the subsequent Iranian response, are where the analysis should focus.

In holding those with institutional power accountable, it is essential to direct criticism accurately and resist the allure of simplistic narratives. The situation in the Strait of Hormuz is a complex interplay of military strategy, international diplomacy, and economic interests, with no easy solutions. However, understanding the root causes and the decisions that led to the crisis is the first step towards addressing the broader implications for global security and economic stability.
