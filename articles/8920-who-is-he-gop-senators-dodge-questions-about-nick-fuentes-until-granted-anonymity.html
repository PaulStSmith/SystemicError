'Who is he?' GOP Senators dodge questions about Nick Fuentes — until granted anonymity

WASHINGTON — In the Republican universe, there is an ongoing debate about former Fox News host Tucker Carlson embracing white supremacist and Holocaust denier Nick Fuentes. It was such a crisis that even the far-right Heritage Foundation, which crafted “Project 2025“ is in full crisis mode, CNN described.

While Speaker Mike Johnson is refusing to bring his members back to Washington, U.S. Senators are working on Capitol Hill. Raw Story caught up with a few to ask what they think of when they hear Nick Fuentes’ name.

Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) was headed into the Capitol for a Thursday evening vote when Raw Story caught him outside. His answer was simple: “Nazi.”

Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT) called balderdash when Raw Story told him that Republicans on the Hill have suddenly forgotten who Fuentes is.

“Come on!” Murphy shouted as he walked past a Capitol Police security checkpoint on his way to vote.

“He’s one of the most celebrated figures in the Republican movement. I mean, come on,” he added.

In fact, when Raw Story faced off against Republicans, some found creative ways to, metaphorically, race in the opposite direction

“You can call my Comms. Director,” Sen. Ashley Moody (R-FL) said when Raw Story questioned her about the far-right activist and friend of rapper Kanye West, who dined with President Donald Trump at Mar-a-Lago last November.

Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) sought help from his staff.

“I don’t know, why?” he asked when Raw Story asked what he thought of Fuentes.

“He’s up in the news because Tucker Carlson had him on,” Raw Story explained.

“Who is he?” the senator asked the nearby aide.

“Some Nazi Hitler apologist,” the young, white, male staffer said without hesitation.

“Where do I know him?” Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-MO) asked Raw Story later Thursday. “I don’t know him.”

Raw Story pressed, noting that he seemed not to be part of the Republican Party.

Suddenly, Schmitt remembered Fuentes enough to agree, “That is true. That is true.”

One Republican Senator didn’t want to answer on the record, saying, “Oh, I’m not going to answer that one.”

When Raw Story assured them anonymity, they replied, “I don’t get every answer right, but [I’m] gonna get that one right!”

Our Analysis:

The Unbearable Lightness of Political Amnesia: The GOP’s Selective Memory on Nick Fuentes

In a political climate where the line between the far-right fringes and the mainstream Republican Party is increasingly blurred, the recent debacle involving Tucker Carlson and Nick Fuentes serves as a glaring spotlight on the GOP’s struggle with its identity. The apparent embrace of Fuentes, a figure whose notoriety is rooted in white supremacist and Holocaust-denying rhetoric, by Carlson, a former Fox News titan, has thrown the Republican universe into a state of disarray. Even the far-right Heritage Foundation finds itself in a tailspin, according to CNN. But as this circus unfolds, the reactions—or lack thereof—from GOP senators offer a masterclass in political evasion and selective amnesia.

Dancing Around the Question

When confronted directly about Fuentes, the spectrum of responses from Republican senators ranged from feigned ignorance to outright refusal to engage. Sen. Ashley Moody (R-FL) and Sen. Markwayne Mullin (R-OK) demonstrated a near-physical aversion to addressing the topic, with Moody deferring to her communications director and Mullin seemingly bewildered about Fuentes’ identity, despite his recent notoriety.

Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-MO) initially claimed ignorance before conceding that Fuentes might, indeed, not align with the Republican Party—perhaps the understatement of the year. Meanwhile, another unnamed Republican senator chose the path of anonymity over potentially aligning with or denouncing Fuentes publicly, suggesting a deep-seated fear of the political repercussions of either stance.

The GOP’s Convenient Forgetfulness

What stands out in these interactions is not just the individual responses but the collective amnesia that seems to afflict the Republican senators when confronted with the far-right’s most unsavory characters. The act of forgetting, or claiming to, serves as a convenient political tool, allowing these senators to sidestep the critical issue of their party’s flirtation with extremist elements. This selective memory is particularly ironic when contrasted with the forthright acknowledgment of Fuentes’ character by Democrats like Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-AZ), who succinctly labeled Fuentes a “Nazi,” and Sen. Chris Murphy (D-CT), who called out the Republican party’s willful ignorance as “balderdash.”

The Implications of Silence

The GOP’s reluctance to directly address or disavow figures like Fuentes is emblematic of a broader issue: the party’s ongoing complicity in the normalization of extremist views. By refusing to take a definitive stand, these senators contribute to a political environment where white supremacist and Holocaust-denying rhetoric is not only tolerated but potentially welcomed. This silence speaks volumes, signaling an unsettling comfort with the ideologies espoused by individuals like Fuentes, as long as direct association can be plausibly denied.

Conclusion: The Cost of Ambiguity

The Republican Party’s handling of the Tucker Carlson-Nick Fuentes debacle is a case study in political evasion. In their attempts to navigate the murky waters of far-right extremism without alienating a base that might sympathize with such views, GOP senators are playing a dangerous game. This strategic amnesia and calculated silence come at a profound cost to the moral clarity and ethical integrity expected of public servants. As the GOP continues to grapple with its identity in the post-Trump era, its leaders would do well to remember that in the face of extremism, silence is not just complicity—it’s endorsement.
