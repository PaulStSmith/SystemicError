Trade war set to 'get really ugly' as 'Xi won't back down': Ex-Trump official

US tariffs on Chinese goods are now at 145 percent. This is up 135 percentage points since February 1, when there was just a 10 percent tariff.

Over the weekend, administration officials were defending President Donald Trump’s plan to “'carve-out' a tariff hike on consumer electronics from the astronomical 145 percent tariffs it levied on China.”

The move is a notable policy rollback as technology products are also subject to a 20 percent tariff from China and "some electronic components could face sector-specific tariffs in the future.”

According to the outlet, Trump's exceptions are “indicative of the relatively weak position the administration.”

They also noted a second problem with the tariffs: “The U.S. is imposing new tariffs on Chinese goods in an attempt to move manufacturing back to the U.S., but those tariffs are particularly painful for U.S. manufacturers because they are currently so dependent on Chinese parts.”

One former Trump administration official told Politico that the trade war the president launched will not be easy to deescalate.

“Xi Jinping will not back down [because] the [Chinese Communist Party] will lose confidence in him," they said. "This is going to get really ugly."

As Politico noted, “Beijing’s prompt countermeasures underscore that readiness: a cross-domain mixture of tariffs, export restrictions on critical minerals essential to U.S. industry and targeting of American firms with official probes or sanctions that press multiple economic pain points.”

The pending tariffs exceptions are expected to only affect semiconductors. However, the White House could expand them to encompass the broader “electronics supply chain.”

Trump announced he would announce the tariff rate for semiconductors within the next week. On Monday, he did not offer any more specific information.

Sparky's Analysis:

Unraveling the Trade War Mirage: A Critical Analysis

As the White House parades its supposed dominance in the ongoing trade war with China, a recent POLITICO report pierces through the administration's triumphant facade, unveiling a scenario far from victorious. The claim of having the 'upper hand' clashes starkly with the realities outlined, revealing a situation fraught with missteps and misjudgments.

The Tariff Tangle

Firstly, the escalation of tariffs on Chinese goods to a staggering 145 percent - up from a mere 10 percent at the start of February - is a testament not to strength, but to the spiraling costs of this economic confrontation. This maneuver, ostensibly aimed at penalizing China, inadvertently ensnares U.S. manufacturers who rely on Chinese parts, showcasing a blatant disregard for domestic economic vulnerabilities.

The Illusion of Strategy

The administration's attempt to “carve-out” a tariff hike on consumer electronics from the astronomical rates is a clear policy backtrack, indicative of a deeper strategic confusion. This exemption, limited as it is to semiconductors, with a vague promise to possibly include more of the electronics supply chain, signals not tactical finesse but rather a floundering amidst the consequences of its own actions.

A Weak Stance Masquerading as Strength

The portrayal of these tariff exemptions as indicative of the administration's "relatively weak position" is an understatement. It reflects a chaotic approach to trade policy, where decisions seem more reactive than part of a coherent strategy. The assertion that "this is going to get really ugly" from a former Trump administration official speaks volumes of the internal skepticism and acknowledgment of a misdirected battle.

The Counterpunch

China's swift countermeasures - a blend of tariffs, export restrictions, and sanctions - underscore a readiness and resilience that was evidently underestimated. Beijing's strategy, targeting critical minerals and American firms, reveals a nuanced understanding of economic pressure points, in stark contrast to the blunt instrument of tariffs employed by the U.S.

The Real Victims

The narrative that the tariffs will coerce manufacturing back to U.S. shores is a simplistic fantasy. The real impact falls on U.S. manufacturers caught in the crossfire, suffering under the weight of tariffs on essential components. This strategy, far from bolstering domestic manufacturing, threatens to cripple it under the guise of nationalistic fervor.

Conclusion: A Misguided Gambit

The unfolding saga of the U.S.-China trade war, as dissected by POLITICO, lays bare the fallacies of the current administration's approach. Far from wielding the upper hand, the U.S. finds itself in a precarious dance, one misstep away from grave economic repercussions. The policy reversals and tariff exemptions are not signs of strategic flexibility but of a deep-seated miscalculation of the adversary's resolve and capabilities.

In this high-stakes game, it's imperative to recognize the difference between assertive rhetoric and effective strategy. The administration's current path is a gamble with the nation's economic future at stake, underscoring a need for a reassessment of tactics if true victory is to be achieved.
