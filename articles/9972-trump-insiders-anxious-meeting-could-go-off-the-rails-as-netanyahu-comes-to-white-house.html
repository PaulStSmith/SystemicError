President Donald Trump is poised to meet with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu sometime on Monday in an effort to bring about an end to Israel’s military siege and aid blockade on Gaza, though one senior White House official is warning that the meeting could very well end up going “off the rails.”

“Bibi is on his own island,” the senior White House official told Politico in a report published Monday, speaking with the outlet on the condition of anonymity. “Not just from us, from his own government.”

Netanyahu is becoming increasingly isolated on the world stage amid both Israel’s ongoing siege of Gaza and the prime minister’s own fight in battling corruption charges. The Israeli leader has also been indicted for war crimes by the International Criminal Court, and faces continued blowback for the Israeli strike on U.S.-ally Qatar earlier this month, a strike that reportedly left Trump “infuriated.”

On Friday, however, Trump said that he was “very close” to a deal that would bring about the end of the war and a return of all remaining Israeli hostages currently held captive by Hamas. The only piece remaining to actually achieve that deal, however, is getting Netanyahu on board, which the White House official said is still very much an open question.

“[Trump is] being very firm in public while leaving some room for negotiations in private,” they said. “[Trump] is going to try to get him to agree to some terms that then they could take back to [Hamas] and finally get something done… [but] it could also go off the rails.”

It’s unknown what time Trump and Netanyahu’s meeting will take place, but reports confirm its set to take place sometime Monday in the Oval Office. The meeting will also follow Netanyahu’s defiant speech at the United Nations General Assembly in New York City, New York on Friday, where he condemned western nations for recognizing the state of Palestine, and was hit by walkouts from dozens of world leaders.

As of Sunday, Netanyahu said he believes there to be20 hostages still alivein Gaza, out of 48 total who remain in Hamas custody. The discussion on seeing a return of the hostages betweenIsraeland the United States has largely ignored the estimated9,500 Palestinianscurrently in Israeli custody, around 3,660 of them held without criminal charge.

Our Analysis:

The Trump-Netanyahu Conundrum: A Masterclass in Political Theater and Isolation

In the latest episode of what could only be described as the real-life adaptation of a political thriller, President Donald Trump is set to meet with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in a bid to untangle the Gordian knot that is Israel’s military siege and aid blockade on Gaza. However, the prelude to this high-stakes meeting reads more like a cautionary tale of isolation and desperation rather than a hopeful precursor to peace.

The Isolation of Netanyahu

Netanyahu, a figure already mired in controversy, finds himself increasingly cornered not just on the global stage but within his own governmental corridors. The Israeli leader’s persistent siege of Gaza, coupled with his own legal woes — ranging from corruption charges to being indicted for war crimes by the International Criminal Court — sketches a portrait of a leader at odds with the world, and crucially, with morality itself. His recent actions, including the Israeli strike on U.S. ally Qatar, have not only left Trump “infuriated” but have also further deepened Netanyahu’s international isolation.

Trump’s Diplomatic Tightrope

On the other side of this diplomatic chess game sits President Trump, who claims to be on the cusp of brokering a deal that could potentially end the war and see the return of Israeli hostages held by Hamas. Yet, this optimism is shadowed by skepticism from within his own administration, with senior White House officials hinting at the precarious nature of getting Netanyahu to agree to any deal. This scenario paints a vivid picture of the complexities inherent in negotiations that are as much about managing personalities as they are about resolving geopolitical crises.

A Defiant Stance Against Peace?

Adding fuel to this fire is Netanyahu’s recent performance at the United Nations General Assembly, where his condemnation of Western nations for recognizing the state of Palestine was met with walkouts. This act of defiance, emblematic of Netanyahu’s broader stance, not only underscores his isolation but also raises questions about his commitment to peace.

The Forgotten Victims

Amidst this tangled web of negotiations, military strategies, and political maneuverings, the human cost of this prolonged conflict remains alarmingly high. While the discussion between Israel and the United States has focused on the return of Israeli hostages, the plight of the estimated 9,500 Palestinians currently in Israeli custody — approximately 3,660 of whom are held without criminal charge — seems to be conspicuously absent from the dialogue. This glaring omission highlights a disturbing imbalance in the valuation of human lives, based solely on nationality.

Conclusion

As the world watches the Trump-Netanyahu meeting with bated breath, the underlying issues of isolation, defiance, and a blatant disregard for human suffering loom large. Whether this meeting will go down in history as a pivotal moment for peace or simply another chapter in the saga of missed opportunities remains to be seen. However, one thing is clear: the path to peace requires more than just negotiations between leaders; it demands a fundamental shift in how we value and fight for human rights and dignity for all.
