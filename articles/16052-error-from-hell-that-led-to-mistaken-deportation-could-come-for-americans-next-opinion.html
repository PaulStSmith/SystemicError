An opinion piece in The Washington Post written by columnist Monica Hesse warned that the "administrative error from hell" that doomed Kilmar Abrego García to a notorious Salvadoran prison could easily condemn American citizens next.

Garcia, originally from El Salvador, was granted a form of asylum to remain in the U.S. to protect him from gang violence in his home country. He was living in Maryland with his wife, an American citizen, when federal agents accused him of being involved with the violent gang MS-13 and shipped him back to Central America. Garcia has denied any connection to MS-13.

At first, the White House admitted it had "made a mistake" when it deported Garcia, but domestic policy adviser Stephen Miller walked that back on Monday, saying, “This was the right person sent to the right place.” And, although the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the administration must "facilitate" Garcia's return to the U.S., neither President Donald Trump nor El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele claimed they had the power to do so.

WaPo opinion writer Monica Hesse wrote Tuesday that American citizens who believe this scenario can't happen to them should reconsider.

"On Monday, Trump said that he had no problems with the idea of sending American citizens to be imprisoned in El Salvador," Hesse wrote.

The U.S. has already paid El Salvador $6 million to imprison deported immigrants, and Trump told Bukele to build "about five more places" to imprison what he called, "homegrown terrorists."

Trump said that he would be happy to send American citizens to El Salvador who commit crimes like pushing "people into subways," hitting "people over the back of the head" and purposely running "people over in cars."

"If he would take them," Trump said of Bukele, "I would be honored to give them."

Hesse, however, doesn't think that this president has earned the benefit of the doubt.

”What we have learned from this case is that whoopsies happen," she contended. "And according to the U.S. government, once you’ve cleared U.S. airspace, once your head has been shaved and you’ve been issued some shower sandals and a fetid cell, once your wife is standing in front of cameras begging anyone who will listen to return her husband and the father of her children — once all of that has happened, there’s really nothing they can do to facilitate your release. They won’t even try."

Sparky's Analysis:

The Kafkaesque Nightmare of Administrative Error: A Closer Look at the Kilmar Abrego García Case

In a recent opinion piece for The Washington Post, columnist Monica Hesse brings to light the harrowing tale of Kilmar Abrego García, a man ensnared in the bureaucratic nightmare of wrongful deportation, and uses it as a chilling warning for American citizens. Hesse's commentary, laden with the kind of dystopian reality that one would hope is relegated to fiction, underscores a systemic failure that is both grotesque and frighteningly plausible.

The Facts: A Summary of Injustice

Kilmar Abrego García's story is nothing short of a tragedy born from what can only be described as an administrative error from hell. Granted asylum to protect him from gang violence in El Salvador, García found himself a victim of mistaken identity or, at the very least, a hasty and reckless decision by federal agents. Accused of being involved with the notorious gang MS-13, he was deported back to Central America, despite his vehement denials of any such connections.

The White House's initial admission of a mistake, subsequently countermanded by domestic policy adviser Stephen Miller's chilling declaration that García was "the right person sent to the right place," encapsulates a terrifying flippancy towards human life and rights. It's a statement that reeks of callous disregard and serves as a stark reminder of the power wielded by those in the corridors of governance.

The Supreme Court's Ruling: A Hollow Victory?

While the U.S. Supreme Court's decision that the administration must "facilitate" García's return may appear as a beacon of hope, it quickly dims upon the realization that neither President Donald Trump nor El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele have taken steps to repatriate García. It's a classic case of bureaucratic buck-passing, leaving a man's life in limbo.

Trump's Disturbing Comments: A Slippery Slope

President Trump's comments, as highlighted by Hesse, on sending American citizens to be imprisoned in El Salvador for crimes committed on U.S. soil are nothing short of dystopian. The idea that a leader of the free world would entertain, let alone vocalize, such thoughts is alarming. It signals a potential willingness to outsource the country's criminal justice system to foreign nations, a prospect fraught with ethical and legal quandaries.

Hesse's Warning: A Dire Premonition

Monica Hesse's warning to American citizens—that they are not immune from falling into a similar abyss as García—is not just a cautionary tale; it's a dire premonition. The "whoopsies happen" attitude towards a man's life, as she puts it, is a grim reflection of a system where accountability is as elusive as justice itself.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

The case of Kilmar Abrego García, as dissected by Monica Hesse, is a stark reminder of the fragility of rights and freedoms in the face of administrative bungling and political machinations. It serves as a call to action for vigilance, advocacy, and reform. In a world where errors can have life-altering consequences, it is incumbent upon us to demand better, to hold those in power accountable, and to ensure that no one else becomes a victim of such systemic error.
