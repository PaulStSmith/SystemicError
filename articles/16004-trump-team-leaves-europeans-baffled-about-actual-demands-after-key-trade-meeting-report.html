Trump team leaves Europeans baffled about actual demands after key trade meeting: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Tariffs: A Misguided Strategy?

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

There is a 90-day pause on President Donald Trump's "reciprocal" tariffs, but the across-the-board 10% tariff on all international goods remains until something different is renegotiated by the White House team.

The European Union trade commissioner, Maroš Šefčovič, met Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Monday as part of the negotiations, but according to the Financial Times and Bloomberg News, there wasn't much talk about the demands the U.S. is making.

“We need to hear more from the Americans. We need to have a clearer idea of what their preferred outcomes are in these negotiations,” said E.U. trade spokesman Olof Gill on Tuesday.

ALSO READ: 'We’ve made a mistake': Trump’s trade war sends GOP into frenzy

FT reported that Šefčovič and Gill proposed dropping all tariffs on industrial goods like steel, aluminum, and car levies. However, as Bloomberg News reported, the U.S. rejected that proposal.

“The E.U. is doing its part. Now, it is necessary for the US to define its position. As with every negotiation, this must be a two-way street . . . with both sides bringing something,” Gill continued.

When Trump announced the "reciprocal tariffs" on the E.U., he used a formula with "calculations were based on the trade surplus in goods between them and the US. The EU was given 20 per cent, based on the $235 billion goods surplus it had in 2024," said FT.

The U.S. also had a list of demands, such as Trump's desire for the E.U. to buy more liquefied natural gas. FT said that there is an agreement "in principle" on that. Another complaint from Trump is that the E.U. doesn't buy enough American vehicles.

Trump has also attacked the E.U. for its standards on beef and chicken imports. The E.U. doesn't import U.S. chicken because it has been rinsed with chlorine-based chemicals, and beef from cattle given hormones, the reports explained. The E.U. also has stricter rules about the use of antibiotics in livestock, particularly when it comes to any preventive use in otherwise healthy animals.

Gill said the meeting with Lutnick a “focused scoping” session. He added that the E.U. food safety standards were “sacrosanct."

While there are complaints from Trump, his team hasn't come up with a list of specific demands from the E.U.

The bloc of countries is currently the U.S.'s largest trading partner, said Bloomberg. The report also noted that Trump has attacked the E.U. for coming together as a bloc, telling his Cabinet in February it was done to "screw" the U.S. Trump thinks that the trade-in-goods surplus is proof that the relationship is unfair, the report continued.

"The EU’s trade-weighted average tariff rate was 2.7% in 2023, according to World Trade Organization data," cited Bloomberg.

“The EU will continue to approach these talks in a constructive manner, with a view to identify areas of common interest. It is clear that significant joint efforts will be needed to achieve a successful outcome within the 90-day window," Gill said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariffs: A Misguided Strategy?

In the latest episode of what can only be described as the theater of the absurd, President Donald Trump has once again showcased his unique approach to international trade policy. With a 90-day pause on his "reciprocal" tariffs, the imposition of a blanket 10% tariff on all international goods looms large—a policy that reads more like a shotgun blast in the dark, hoping to hit a target by sheer chance rather than through strategic aim.

The interaction between the European Union's trade commissioner, Maroš Šefčovič, and U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, as reported by reputable sources such as the Financial Times and Bloomberg News, paints a picture of a negotiation marred by confusion and lack of direction. The EU seems to be playing the role of the reasonable adult in the room, proposing a sensible solution to drop all tariffs on industrial goods including steel, aluminum, and car levies. Yet, the U.S. response—or lack thereof—signals a troubling disconnect from rational trade practices.

The EU's Frustration: A Call for Clarity

The European Union, in a bid to navigate these tumultuous waters, has made its stance clear: tariffs on industrial goods should be abolished. This is not only logical but necessary for fostering a healthier trade environment. The U.S. rejection of this proposal, as noted, is bewildering and indicative of a larger problem at play: a fundamental misunderstanding of how trade negotiations work.

E.U. trade spokesman Olof Gill's comments highlight a growing frustration with the U.S.'s approach. Negotiations are a two-way street, yet it seems only one side is willing to compromise. The demands from the U.S., including Trump's desire for the E.U. to purchase more liquefied natural gas and American vehicles, come across as both heavy-handed and slightly desperate. Moreover, Trump's attack on the E.U. for its food safety standards, particularly regarding beef and chicken imports, is a misguided attempt to prioritize trade over public health.

Trump's Misconceptions: A Bloc to "Screw" the U.S.?

Trump's assertion that the E.U. formed as a bloc to disadvantage the U.S. is not only historically inaccurate but also dangerously simplistic. His fixation on the trade-in-goods surplus as evidence of an unfair relationship ignores the complexities of international trade. Furthermore, his criticism of the E.U.'s trade-weighted average tariff rate, which stood at a modest 2.7% in 2023 according to World Trade Organization data, is unfounded.

In the grand scheme of things, Trump's trade strategy, if one can call it that, seems more like a gamble than a thought-out plan. The imposition of across-the-board tariffs is a blunt instrument that risks more than it can possibly gain. It not only jeopardizes the U.S.'s relationship with its largest trading partner but also undermines the global trade system at large.

The 90-Day Window: A Race Against Reason

As the clock ticks on the 90-day pause, one can't help but wonder if reason will prevail. The E.U.'s commitment to a constructive approach, as voiced by Gill, offers a glimmer of hope. However, it's increasingly clear that significant joint efforts will be required to navigate this impasse.

In conclusion, the current U.S. trade policy under Trump is a case study in how not to conduct international negotiations. It's a mix of bluster, misunderstanding, and missed opportunities. As the world watches, one can only hope for a shift towards a more rational and effective strategy. Until then, we remain spectators at a show where the stakes are alarmingly high, and the script seems to be written on the fly.
