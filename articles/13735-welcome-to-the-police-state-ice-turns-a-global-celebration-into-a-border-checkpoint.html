Welcome to the Police State: ICE Turns a Global Celebration into a Border Checkpoint

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The FIFA Club World Cup is supposed to be a celebration of sport, culture, and unity. It’s one of the few events on Earth where the world gathers not to divide, but to share. It is, in short, the antithesis of nationalism. And yet, the United States—never missing an opportunity to project fear where joy should be—has decided to deploy ICE and CBP to patrol the games at Miami’s Hard Rock Stadium.

Let that sink in. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, the same agency that terrorized communities with dawn raids, family separations, and indefinite detentions under the Trump administration, is now being given front-row access to what should be a beacon of international goodwill. And not content with just a security presence, ICE added this Orwellian touch: a warning that “all non-American citizens need to carry proof of their legal status.” Because nothing says “Welcome to the land of the free” like being threatened to “show your papers.”

This isn’t a policy; it’s a statement. A message. It says to every international visitor: You are not safe here unless you can prove you deserve to exist within our borders. It’s a betrayal of everything the world of sport—and America—is meant to stand for.

A Fascist Specter in the Bleachers

Let’s be brutally clear: this isn’t about safety. It’s not about stopping a credible threat. It’s about intimidation. ICE’s reminder to carry documents is not standard practice at public events, let alone international ones hosted on American soil. It echoes the practices of authoritarian states, where legality is assumed to be conditional and where public spaces are weaponized to police the presence of the foreign, the different, the vulnerable.

This is ICE leveraging its badge not to secure, but to instill fear. When a mother from Mexico or a student from Nigeria or a fan from Germany walks into the stadium, they’re not being welcomed. They’re being watched. They’re being assessed. Their legitimacy as a guest is no longer presumed—it must be proven on demand.

And what if someone forgets their documents? What if they get hassled in line? What if they miss the game they traveled thousands of miles to see? Too bad. In this America, you’re guilty until proven paperworked.

Not even the Nazis, at the height of their militarized authoritarianism during the 1936 Berlin Olympics, dared to be this blatantly stupid. They polished their image, masked their repression, and pretended hospitality to the world. But here? The U.S. isn’t even pretending. It is weaponizing bureaucracy in plain sight, proud of its paranoia.

Sport as Soft Power — Now Weaponized

International sporting events are about diplomacy by other means. When Brazil hosts a World Cup, it’s inviting the world into its house. When France hosts the Olympics, it opens its arms to the globe. But what does America offer? Security theatre. Cold suspicion. ICE agents in aviators making sure the global audience knows exactly who’s boss.

This isn’t just a moral failure. It’s a geopolitical catastrophe. The U.S. has already lost immense global goodwill through wars, climate inaction, and grotesque inequality. Sporting events offer a rare reprieve, a chance to heal through shared joy and camaraderie. But ICE’s presence and message are a spit in the face of that potential.

And let’s not kid ourselves—this didn’t happen in a vacuum. The policies that normalized CBP and ICE involvement in everyday life came out of the Trump era, where immigration enforcement became a bludgeon to serve white grievance politics.

Miami Isn’t a Border Town. It’s a Global City.

ICE’s justification—that this is merely part of “security”—is as hollow as it is dishonest. There are ways to secure an event without turning it into a checkpoint. Local police. Private security. Even FBI coordination. But ICE and CBP are not normal event security. They are tools of federal immigration enforcement. Their very presence chills, surveils, and alienates.

And in Miami, of all places? A city built by immigrants, where Spanish is heard more often than English, and where the melting pot actually simmers with vitality? This is an insult. This is not security; this is domination. This is an assertion that even in spaces meant for the world, American paranoia and xenophobia will set the terms.

A Warning to the World

To those attending the games: be vigilant. Not of each other, but of the surveillance state watching you. If you are a non-citizen, even a legal tourist, even someone with proper documentation, understand that your presence is being scrutinized in ways no American would tolerate abroad.

To the organizers: shame on you. Shame for allowing the joyful purpose of this event to be hijacked by security theater. For prioritizing optics over ethics. For rolling out the red carpet with one hand while aiming a scanner with the other.

And to the administration: if this is what you call hospitality, expect fewer visitors. No one travels to feel like a suspect.

This Is Not Normal. Do Not Normalize It.

This cannot stand. ICE’s overreach into public cultural spaces must be resisted. If we allow a soccer match to become a de facto immigration checkpoint, what’s next? Concerts? Parks? Museums?

Public spaces belong to the people—to the public, not to the surveillance state. And global events belong to the world, not just to American security forces looking for their next headline.

The world is watching. And it sees a nation so afraid of the Other that it cannot even celebrate a sport without pulling out the badge. What it sees is not greatness. It sees a small, scared, and shrinking country.

And it deserves better. We all deserve better.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
