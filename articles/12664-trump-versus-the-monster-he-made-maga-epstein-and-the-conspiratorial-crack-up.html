Trump Versus the Monster He Made: MAGA, Epstein, and the Conspiratorial Crack-Up

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 17, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Cult Turns on the ProphetEpstein: A Myth Made for the MovementThe Addictive Power of Conspiratorial ThinkingThe Narcissist and His NarcoticA Movement Without a Brake PedalThe Rest of Us Should Pay Attention

Donald Trump is discovering what every arsonist eventually does: once the fire is raging, you don't get to tell it where to burn. For nearly a decade, Trump has stoked a bonfire of conspiracy theories, anti-institutional rage, and reactionary grievance politics—an inferno whose heat has shaped the entire modern Republican identity. But now, in a bizarre and unprecedented twist, that inferno is turning on him. Over the past few weeks, we have witnessed the spectacle of Trump’s own loyal MAGA base publicly rebelling against him over a case that has become mythic in their worldview: the death of Jeffrey Epstein.

It is difficult to overstate how stunning this turn of events is. For years, Trump has enjoyed near-total domination over the MAGA world, his pronouncements treated as gospel, his lies adopted as creed. But his base’s belief in his invincibility is facing a severe stress test. Trump, now aligned with the "official narrative" on Epstein—that the disgraced financier committed suicide and there was no credible evidence of a "client list" or deep-state conspiracy—has crossed a sacred line in the conspiracy-laced mythology of the movement he birthed. And for the first time in years, his most fervent followers are rejecting him.

The Cult Turns on the Prophet

The inciting incident? A mundane memo from the Trump-era Justice Department reiterating the conclusion that Epstein's death was a suicide, and that no evidence of a global blackmail operation involving prominent individuals was ever found. For the reality-based world, this was little more than an official yawn. But for the MAGA world, it was a betrayal of apocalyptic proportions. This wasn’t just about Epstein. This was about the foundation of their movement: a belief in a hidden cabal of elites—liberal, globalist, satanic—who manipulate the world from behind the curtain.

That Trump would echo that narrative when it suited him—and now reject it—was too much for many of his followers. At a recent MAGA rally, the discontent was palpable. Epstein wasn’t a side issue; it had become the issue. And Trump’s public rebuke of his base for falling for what he called the “Jeffrey Epstein Hoax” only poured gasoline on the flames.

Natalie Winters, correspondent for Steve Bannon’s War Room podcast, gave voice to the outrage, telling The New York Times that Epstein is “foundational” to MAGA ideology. The betrayal, in her view, was existential. Either Trump lied, is incompetent, or has been compromised—and none of those options offer his supporters a satisfying answer. In the MAGA cosmology, Trump was supposed to be the slayer of dragons, the wrecker of the deep state, the man who would tear the veil off the pedophilic elite cabal. And here he is, sounding like a spokesperson for the very institutions his movement exists to destroy.

Epstein: A Myth Made for the Movement

It’s important to understand what Epstein means to this movement. In the rational world, Epstein was a vile man, a predator enabled by wealth and a vast network of complicity. His death in custody raised legitimate questions, especially given the security lapses at the jail. But in the MAGAverse, Epstein is more than a man. He is myth. His name is code for everything the movement believes: corruption at the highest levels, untouchable elites abusing children, and a government too compromised or cowardly to act.

Steve Bannon put it succinctly: “Epstein is a key that picks the lock on so many things.” The idea of an Epstein-led blackmail ring connected to intelligence agencies and foreign governments is irresistible for a movement addicted to narrative over nuance, certainty over complexity. It reinforces every dark suspicion they hold about the world—and about why their lives haven’t turned out the way they hoped.

So when Trump dared to declare the myth false, he wasn’t simply disagreeing with them—he was desecrating a holy relic. And worse, by defending the system’s conclusions, he became, in their eyes, an agent of the very deep state he once promised to dismantle. The irony is almost too rich: the man who built his brand on never backing down, on destroying the establishment, is now being cast as part of the cover-up. It’s a narrative collapse worthy of Greek tragedy.

The Addictive Power of Conspiratorial Thinking

This rupture is not just about politics. It’s about psychology. The MAGA movement is steeped in conspiratorial thinking because conspiracy theories do something that real explanations often fail to do: they provide meaning. They simplify the world into heroes and villains, good and evil, light and darkness. They offer control, coherence, and catharsis in a world that feels increasingly chaotic.

For many in the movement, these beliefs are not loosely held opinions—they are identity. Conspiracies are addictive. They offer dopamine spikes of certainty and significance. To abandon them would be to fall into an abyss of confusion and powerlessness. That’s why Trump’s deviation from the script is being interpreted not just as a political error, but as heresy.

And therein lies the danger. Trump may have believed he could ride the conspiracy tiger forever. But tigers don’t carry passengers. Eventually, they eat them.

The Narcissist and His Narcotic

Trump’s entire political rise was built on grievance, projection, and performance. He didn’t lead a movement so much as mirror one. And what he mirrored was a set of cultural, racial, and social anxieties that had metastasized into rage. The conspiratorial thinking was just the vessel—what mattered was the identity it produced: Trump as warrior-king of the forgotten, the wronged, the righteous.

But as with all cults of personality, there comes a moment when the personality no longer fits the narrative. Trump, ever the narcissist, believed he was the narrative. Now, as the movement radicalizes further without him, he’s learning that the beast has grown beyond his control. He poured gasoline on the fire, but now the fire is demanding more fuel—and it doesn’t care if that fuel is Trump himself.

What’s more, Trump has always relied on the belief that he was uniquely in on the con. He could lie shamelessly, contradict himself daily, and gaslight his followers because he assumed they needed him more than he needed them. But conspiracy logic doesn’t work like that. Once you’re cast as a traitor, there’s no redemption. There’s no negotiation. You’re either with the movement, or you’re against it.

And increasingly, parts of the MAGAverse are concluding that Trump is now against it.

A Movement Without a Brake Pedal

This is the core problem with building a political identity around destruction: you cannot stop. There is no pause button. Once you’ve taught your followers to distrust everything, to hate compromise, to believe that every institution is lying—it becomes impossible to govern, impossible to adapt, and impossible to stop the cycle of escalation.

The Epstein debacle is not an isolated episode. It’s a symptom of a movement that has lost the ability to distinguish between truth and narrative, between allies and enemies. The moment Trump failed to deliver on their sacred mythology, he became suspect. The revolution is devouring its father.

The terrifying part? There may be no clear successor at all. Trump, in his egomania, never cultivated an heir—only acolytes and imitators, none of whom can replicate his singular grip on the MAGA psyche. When he falters, what follows won’t be a tidy transfer of power, but a vacuum filled with infighting, fragmentation, and ideological freefall. The movement may become even more erratic, radicalized not by leadership but by disillusionment. There won’t be a new Trump—only echoes, each more desperate and unhinged than the last.

The Rest of Us Should Pay Attention

To those outside the MAGA bubble, it’s tempting to laugh. Trump being eaten by his own cult? Delicious irony. Poetic justice. But this isn’t just schadenfreude. It’s a preview. The conspiratorial logic Trump unleashed will outlive him. It will mutate. It already has.

The Republican Party is now shot through with this logic. The line between governance and paranoia has blurred. And once conspiracy becomes the central organizing principle of political identity, you don’t get democracy. You get chaos. You get purity tests. You get violence.

Trump’s mistake was believing he could control the chaos. But chaos doesn’t take orders. It spreads. It consumes. And now it’s coming for him.

So yes, we should recognize the moment. Not because it marks Trump’s fall—he’s wriggled out of worse—but because it reveals a deeper truth: the MAGA movement no longer belongs to him. He built the monster. But he no longer holds the leash.
