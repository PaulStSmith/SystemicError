Trump doesn't want the GOP to win in November — and his actions prove it

Trump’s Real Constituency Is Trump

The Man in Charge

This story is about the simplest fact Washington keeps trying to decorate: Donald Trump has the power. He is president, he controls the executive branch, he sets the tone for the party, and he uses that office as a personal instrument. The groceries, the tariffs, the war, the midterms, even the ballroom spectacle all orbit that reality.

Blame as Cover

The source gives the country’s economic pain in plain numbers: $80 for a bag of groceries, Americans cutting back on food, confidence collapsing, blame landing on Trump. His response is not policy correction but denial and theater. He waves away affordability, blames Democrats for the word itself, and acts as if the public’s hardship is an optical problem rather than a governing failure.

Power Without Responsibility

Trump’s damage is not accidental drift. The source describes him keeping tariffs in place, prolonging war, and mocking the consequences while asking taxpayers to bankroll vanity projects and rewards for January 6 loyalists. That is not confusion. It is a governing style built on extraction, spectacle, and contempt for anyone who is not inside the circle of benefit.

The GOP as Hostage

Republicans are not described here as partners with leverage. They are hostages to a leader who is openly undermining their electoral prospects. He is backing primary challengers against incumbents, ignoring pleas to take the midterms seriously, and making the party more dependent on him by making it weaker everywhere else. If the point were coalition maintenance, his behavior makes no sense. If the point is domination, it makes perfect sense.

War as Political Fuel

The source is sharpest when it refuses the comforting assumption that Trump wants victory for the country or even for his party. It suggests a harder truth: prolonged conflict may serve him better than resolution because it keeps the system centered on him. A war that drains the GOP and deepens dependency on his persona is not a foreign-policy failure in his mind; it is leverage.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is not just corruption or incompetence. It is authoritarian self-preservation through managed decline. Trump treats institutions as props, crises as branding opportunities, and his own party as disposable as long as he remains the indispensable figure. That is why the story matters: it is not about one bad week or one bad poll. It is about a presidency that measures success by the damage it can impose while keeping everyone else busy explaining why the damage must mean something else.
