‘Hogwash’: Legal experts torch GOP senator's 'entirely unhinged' FBI stunt against Dems

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Theater of the Absurd: Cornyn's Crusade

U.S. Senator John Cornyn (R-TX) speaks to reporters as he walks to the office of the Majority Leader, Sen. John Thune (R-SD), on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 28, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

"Entirely unhinged" was how one constitutional law expert described a letter U.S. Sen. John Cornyn sent to the FBI Tuesday, demanding that the top federal law enforcement agency intervene "to locate or arrest potential lawbreakers who have fled" Texas—meaning the Democratic state legislators who left the state this week to prevent Republicans from advancing a congressional map that would likely net the GOP five more U.S. House seats.

Cornyn (R-Texas) didn't mention the Republican Party's redistricting effort, which has been backed by the Trump administration and is aimed at changing district lines that formed districts with Black and Latino majorities, in his letter to FBI Director Kash Patel. Instead, he claimed the dozens of Democratic state lawmakers who left Texas on Sunday were stopping the state House from addressing proposed disaster relief following deadly floods last month.

Republicans had added the redistricting efforts to the legislative agenda of a special session, making the flood relief a lesser priority—and angering state Democrats who have been in states including Illinois and New York since leaving Sunday, with Democratic Govs. JB Pritzker and Kathy Hochul providing protection against GOP Texas Gov. Greg Abbott's threat to arrest them.

Cornyn claimed in his letter to Patel that the Texas Department of Public Safety may need the FBI's help in locating and arresting the "fleeing lawmakers," and accused the state Democrats of potentially running afoul of anti-bribery laws by accepting the support of Prtizker, Hochul, and other out-of-state officials who have helped them since they left Texas.

"I am concerned that legislators who solicited or accepted funds to aid in their efforts to avoid their legislative duties may be guilty of bribery or other public corruption offenses," wrote Cornyn. "These legislators have committed potential criminal acts in their rush to avoid their constitutional responsibilities and must be fully investigated and held accountable."

Anthony Michael Kreis, a law professor at Georgia State University, said Cornyn's plea for help from Patel amounted to "police state hogwash from a guy who should know better."

"There is no reasonable basis that arresting Texas legislators will prevent the commission of a federal crime," he said. "This is simply John Cornyn asking for unconstitutional, lawless, and arbitrary federal power."

As Common Dreams reported Monday, Texas state House Speaker Dustin Burrows (R-83) signed civil arrest warrants for the more than 50 Democrats who left the state to deny the chamber a quorum, but one expert said the warrants would not be enforceable outside Texas.

Under legislative rules the state House members face $500 daily fines for each day they miss of the session, and they could be formally reprimanded, censured, and expelled if two-thirds of the chamber vote in support of those measures—but legal experts have said Abbott and other Republican leaders in Texas would have a difficult time proving the lawmakers have committed any civil or criminal offenses.

"They have not committed a crime. They are not fugitives," said Kreis. "There's no offense against the United States."

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Cornyn's Crusade

In a move that can only be described as a leap into the surreal, U.S. Senator John Cornyn has taken a daring plunge into what appears to be a political black comedy. However, the implications of his actions are far from humorous. They are a stark reflection of the dire state of American politics, where partisanship has not only clouded judgment but has seemingly obliterated any semblance of rational thought.

Cornyn's Letter: A Masterclass in Misdirection

Cornyn's letter to the FBI Director demands the federal law enforcement's intervention in what is essentially a state political maneuver—tracking down Democratic state legislators who left Texas to stall a GOP-favored redistricting plan. Notably, this plan is designed to disadvantage Black and Latino majorities, highlighting a sinister undercurrent of voter suppression.

However, Cornyn conveniently omits this critical context in his communication. Instead, he frames the Democratic lawmakers' departure as an obstruction to addressing disaster relief following deadly floods. This maneuver is a classic political sleight of hand: distract the audience with one hand while the other undermines democratic processes.

The Absurdity of Federal Involvement

Cornyn's suggestion that the FBI should aid in locating and potentially arresting these lawmakers not only stretches the bounds of credibility but leaps into the realm of authoritarianism. His baseless accusation that these lawmakers may be guilty of bribery or public corruption offenses for accepting support from out-of-state officials is as unfounded as it is reckless. It's a dangerous precedent to suggest that federal power should be mobilized to resolve what is unequivocally a state legislative conflict.

Police State Hogwash

Anthony Michael Kreis's characterization of Cornyn's plea as "police state hogwash" is not just apt; it's a necessary condemnation of an alarming trend towards normalizing extreme partisan intervention. The invocation of federal power to arrest lawmakers for fulfilling what they perceive as their constitutional duty to protect the rights of their constituents is a gross misuse of federal resources and a disturbing echo of authoritarian regimes, not a democratic republic.

The Real Issue at Hand

The heart of the matter, which Cornyn so deftly sidesteps, is the Republican party's relentless pursuit of redistricting efforts that would disenfranchise minority voters. This is not a new strategy, but it is one that continues to threaten the very foundation of American democracy. The Democratic lawmakers' decision to leave Texas was a desperate measure in response to a desperate situation: the systematic dismantling of fair electoral representation.

Conclusion: A Call to Reason

Cornyn's call to the FBI is not just "entirely unhinged"; it is a glaring symptom of a political system in crisis. It represents a dangerous escalation in the weaponization of federal agencies against political opponents, a tactic that should alarm all Americans, regardless of party affiliation.

The real issue at stake is not the whereabouts of the Democratic lawmakers but the integrity of the democratic process itself. As this saga unfolds, one can only hope that reason will prevail over partisan zealotry, and that the principles of democracy will be upheld over the convenience of political maneuvering. But, as Cornyn's letter vividly illustrates, in today's political climate, that hope seems more like a distant dream.
