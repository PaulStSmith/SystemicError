Kristi Noem's 'dirty little secret' on border security exposed by conservative outlet

President Donald Trump and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem have repeatedly bragged that the Trump administration is allowing “zero” unauthorized immigrants to cross the border into the United States. But a new scoop from the conservative Washington Examiner reveals that’s not true — and some immigrants are indeed being released into the country after being apprehended.

“Although the number of border crossers let into the interior of the country is significantly smaller than it was during the Biden administration, that some illegal immigrants have been released into the U.S. at all appears to contradict President Donald Trump’s claim during the State of the Union address, as well as numerous statements by Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, that no one had been let in since early last summer,” reported Anna Giaritelli.

Under federal law, Border Patrol can only hold detainees for up to 72 hours, after which they must be handed over to Immigration and Customs Enforcement for further action. The issue is that ICE is, in fact, then releasing an unspecified number of people that Border Patrol turns over to them, according to the report.

According to one person who texted the Examiner, “Dirty little secret that the [Trump administration] does not want getting out for obvious reasons. There are in fact folks who are being released who recently crossed the border. … [Border Patrol] is not releasing anyone, but ICE is.”

For years prior to the Trump administration, ICE has placed low-risk detainees in “Alternatives to Detention,” where they essentially are released into the local community with a court date and must check in with authorities. It has been proven to work, but Trump has long attacked the idea. Despite this, per the report, Alternatives to Detention continue to be used.

This revelation comes amid other damning reporting about Noem, including that she buried an inspector general report on a security flaw in her highly-touted airport security reforms.

Our Analysis:

Unveiling the Mirage: Trump’s Border Policy Exposed

In the labyrinth of political rhetoric, President Donald Trump and Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem have constructed an elaborate facade, boasting a zero-tolerance policy towards unauthorized immigrants crossing into the United States. This claim, intended to project an image of an impenetrable fortress, has been punctured by a recent Washington Examiner report, revealing cracks in the administration’s narrative that are too significant to ignore.

Despite the administration’s relentless assertion that not a single unauthorized immigrant has been allowed entry since last summer, the reality on the ground paints a starkly different picture. The report, authored by Anna Giaritelli, uncovers that, contrary to Trump and Noem’s claims, some immigrants are indeed being released into the country after being apprehended. This revelation not only undermines the administration’s public statements but also highlights a glaring discrepancy between rhetoric and reality.

Under the constraints of federal law, the Border Patrol can detain individuals for a maximum of 72 hours before they must be transferred to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for further action. Herein lies the crux of the issue: ICE has been releasing an unspecified number of these individuals into the United States, a fact that the Trump administration has conveniently omitted from their narrative. An anonymous source candidly reveals this as a “Dirty little secret“ that the administration desperately wanted to keep under wraps.

For years, ICE has implemented “Alternatives to Detention” for low-risk detainees, a strategy that has proven effective and efficient. Detainees are released into the community with a set court date and are required to check in with authorities. Despite its success, Trump has vehemently criticized this approach, favoring rhetoric that amplifies fear and division over practical and humane solutions.

The exposure of this policy discrepancy is not an isolated incident. It coincides with other troubling reports concerning Noem, including allegations of suppressing an inspector general report on a security loophole in her much-vaunted airport security reforms. Together, these revelations paint a picture of an administration more concerned with the optics of infallibility and toughness on immigration than with the nuanced realities of policy execution and human dignity.

The Washington Examiner’s report is a stark reminder that the truth often lurks beneath the surface of political declarations. It challenges the public and policymakers alike to question the veracity of the administration’s claims and to demand transparency and accountability. In the realm of immigration policy, as in all areas of governance, integrity and truth must prevail over deception and political posturing. The Trump administration’s handling of this issue not only contradicts its own stated policies but also undermines the fundamental principles upon which the United States prides itself. It’s time for the administration to confront this reality head-on, rather than hiding behind a facade of impenetrable borders and zero-tolerance rhetoric.
