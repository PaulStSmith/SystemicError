Expert reveals stunning reason Biden admin didn't prosecute John Bolton's security breach

By Paulo Santos

FILE PHOTO: Former White House national security adviser John Bolton arrives at his house following its search by the FBI in Bethesda, Maryland, U.S., August 22, 2025. REUTERS/Tasos Katopodis/File Photo

The Trump administration's raid on the home of his former National Security Adviser John Bolton has been roundly criticized as a weaponization of the justice system — but there may be a bit more to the story, analyst Ken Dilanian told MSNBC's Katy Tur on Friday.

Specifically, he said, the Biden administration began the original investigation into whether Bolton exposed classified information — and their decision not to pursue it was not due to a lack of evidence.

"The search warrant into John Bolton's home has just been released," said Tur. "A lot of it is pretty heavily redacted. I mean, just pages of redactions ... so I imagine that you don't have much of a handle on the full text of it, but what can you tell us in these preliminary moments?"

"I do have some context on this, Katy, and in part because The New York Times reported something significant that I've confirmed," said Dilanian. "There's a whole section of that affidavit. I think it's the most important section that's blacked out under the heading 'A hack of John Bolton's AOL account by a foreign government.'"

"What I understand from two sources, and what The New York Times has reported, is that the United States government obtained John Bolton's emails, some of those emails, from an adversarial foreign government, unnamed. And in that dump contained evidence that led to some concerns about his handling of classified information. And this happened during the Biden administration. Katy, the Biden Justice Department reviewed this. They opted not to bring charges. They didn't conduct a search. In part, I'm told there were concerns about the sensitivity of how they acquired that information and that, in fact, it's quite damaging that the public has been told that they acquired these emails from that foreign government, because that foreign government now knows that the United States had access to their systems."

"Nonetheless, it's out and we're reporting it," he added. "Again, it's blacked out in the affidavit, but — and we don't know what the evidence is, but it was enough to convince two separate federal judges that there was probable cause to believe a crime had been committed and that the evidence was in John Bolton's home. And so the FBI has carried out those searches. We haven't heard anything since about potential charges or whether they uncovered significant evidence, but that's sort of where that stands."

The Curious Case of the Bolton Raid: Unpacking the Narrative

The recent narrative surrounding the Trump administration's raid on John Bolton's home has been a whirlwind of controversy, confusion, and criticism. As the dust refuses to settle, the devil, as they say, is in the details—details that have been meticulously dissected by Ken Dilanian on MSNBC. But let's dive deeper and peel back the layers of this onion with a bit more skepticism and a lot less reverence for the establishment's talking points.

The Biden Administration's Role

First and foremost, it's crucial to underline a point that seems to have been glossed over with too fine a brush: The Biden administration initiated the original investigation. This isn't just a footnote in the grand scheme of things; it's the foundation of the entire ordeal. The narrative being peddled—that this is a Trump-led weaponization of the justice system—misses a critical beat. The Biden administration wasn't playing 4D chess; they were playing the same game, just with a quieter board.

The decision not to pursue Bolton, as Dilanian points out, wasn't for a lack of evidence but seemingly due to concerns over the sensitivity of how the information was acquired. This is where the plot thickens. It's not the lack of evidence but the source of it that caused hesitation. This revelation isn't just a minor hiccup; it's a glaring spotlight on the complex web of international espionage, ethical quandaries, and the precarious balance of justice.

The Redacted Affidavit and the Foreign Hack

Ah, the redactions. Pages upon pages of blacked-out text that scream secrecy louder than any whistleblower could. The most tantalizing part of this cloak-and-dagger tale? The "hack of John Bolton's AOL account by a foreign government." This isn't just a subplot in a spy novel; it's a stark reminder of the digital vulnerabilities and international intrigue at play in the highest echelons of power.

Dilanian's mention of the United States obtaining Bolton's emails from an adversarial foreign government opens a Pandora's box of questions. It's not just about the handling of classified information; it's about the geopolitical chess game of intelligence and counterintelligence. The fact that this acquisition is now public knowledge is a double-edged sword; it simultaneously exposes a breach and signals to the foreign government that their infiltration has been infiltrated.

The Judicial Green Light

The decision by two separate federal judges to authorize the raid on Bolton's home shouldn't be brushed aside lightly. Probable cause isn't a low bar; it's a significant threshold that requires convincing evidence. The judges' agreement suggests that the shadows of doubt were substantial enough to warrant this dramatic action.

However, the silence post-raid is deafening. The lack of updates on potential charges or findings from the FBI adds an air of mystery and, frankly, suspicion to the proceedings. It's a cliffhanger in a saga that has more questions than answers.

Conclusion: Between the Lines

As we dissect the layers of this story, a narrative emerges that is far more complex and nuanced than initially presented. It's a tale of international espionage, judicial decisions, and the ever-present shadow of political maneuvering. The raid on John Bolton's home isn't just about the weaponization of the justice system; it's a window into the murky waters of national security, international relations, and the delicate dance of democracy.
