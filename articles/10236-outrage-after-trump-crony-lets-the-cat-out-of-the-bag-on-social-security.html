“I think everything’s being considered, will be, will be considered,” Commissioner Frank Bisignano told Fox Business on Friday when asked.

Bisignano insisted that the White House “is completely committed to protect and preserve” Social Security, but that “the generations that are coming in will probably have a difference set of rules than we had.”

Frank Bisignano is a former CEO of a U.S. multinational financial technology company, and appeared on a list of the highest-paid CEOs in the country, according to the AFL-CIO.

During the 2024 presidential campaign, Donald Trump vowed, “I will not cut one penny from Social Security or Medicare and I will not raise the retirement age by one day. Not for one day.”

Critics blasted the development.

“Republicans gave away trillions In tax cuts for the wealthy. Now they are asking Americans to work longer. We won’t stand for it,” wrote U.S. Senator Ed Markey (D-MA).

“Trump’s cronies let the cat out of the bag. They’re ready to raise the retirement age. NOT ON MY WATCH! They want to cut your benefits and hand them over to Wall Street. We’re in a fight to save Social Security, and I’m not backing down. That’s a promise,” declared U.S. Rep. John Larson (D-CT).

Warren Gunnels, Minority Staff Director for the Senate’s Health, Education, Labor and Pensions (HELP) Committee Ranking Member, Bernie Sanders (I-VT), wrote: “If Bernie’s Social Security bill was signed into law, Social Security would be solvent for the next 75 years, benefits would go up by $2,400 & the bottom 91% of Americans would not pay a penny more in taxes. Instead, Republicans want you to work until you drop dead.”

Former Secretary of Labor, Professor Robert Reich, added: “A worker making $50,000 a year contributes to Social Security on 100% of their income. A CEO making $20 million a year contributes to Social Security on less than 1% of their income. Instead of raising the retirement age, we should scrap the Social Security tax cap.”

The Social Security tax cap, the amount that is subject to the Social Security tax, is $176,100.

According to Data for Progress, a progressive policy and polling organization, more Americans support lowering the Social Security retirement age than raising it or keeping it the same.

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Irony: A Deep Dive into the Social Security Age Fiasco

In a recent revelation that should surprise exactly no one paying attention, the Trump Administration is toying with the idea of raising the retirement age for Social Security recipients. This nugget of joy was delivered by none other than Commissioner Frank Bisignano on Fox Business, where reality often goes to take a long nap. Bisignano, a man who has probably never had to worry about the stability of his retirement, assures us that the White House is "completely committed to protect and preserve" Social Security. Yet, in the same breath, he suggests the next generations might not get the same deal. Oh, the generosity.

The Rich Get Richer

Let's not gloss over the delicious detail that Bisignano, a former CEO and a star on the list of the country's highest-paid CEOs, is contemplating the future of the common worker's retirement. The irony is so thick you could cut it with a golden spatula. Under his watchful gaze, the administration is "considering" making Americans work longer before they can retire, after gifting trillions in tax cuts to the wealthy. It's like a reverse Robin Hood scenario, but instead of stealing, they're just moving the finish line further for the average Joe and Jane.

Broken Promises

Remember when Donald Trump, during the 2024 presidential campaign, vowed not to cut a penny from Social Security or Medicare or raise the retirement age "by one day"? Well, add that to the long list of political promises with the lifespan of a fruit fly. The audacity to flip-flop on such a critical issue showcases a breathtaking level of disregard for the electorate's memory and intelligence.

The Democratic Counter

Democratic lawmakers, in response, have cranked up the outrage machine, and rightly so. U.S. Senator Ed Markey and U.S. Rep. John Larson are leading the charge, painting a vivid picture of an administration ready to hand over the fruits of American labor to Wall Street. The narrative isn't new, but it's effective, tapping into the deep-seated fears of many Americans about their financial futures. Warren Gunnels throws in an alternative, citing Bernie Sanders' Social Security bill as a panacea, promising solvency, increased benefits, and no additional taxes for the bottom 91% of Americans. A utopian vision, perhaps, but a stark contrast to the current direction.

A Simple Solution Ignored

Former Secretary of Labor, Professor Robert Reich, cuts through the noise with a straightforward solution: scrap the Social Security tax cap. Currently, a worker making $50,000 a year contributes to Social Security on 100% of their income, while a CEO making $20 million pays on less than 1%. This glaring inequality in the system is the elephant in the room, yet the administration seems keen on making the average American worker carry the burden a little longer, both figuratively and literally.

Public Opinion Be Damned

Despite what seems like an overwhelming consensus from progressive policy and polling organizations like Data for Progress, which reports that more Americans support lowering the retirement age than raising it or keeping it the same, the administration appears hell-bent on pushing forward with their agenda. It's a classic case of political tone-deafness, or perhaps, a calculated indifference to public opinion.

In Conclusion

The unfolding drama around Social Security is more than a policy debate; it's a stark reminder of the widening chasm between the governing elite and the governed. As the administration "considers" its next moves, one can't help but wonder whose interests they are truly serving. If history and current sentiment are any indicators, it's not the average American worker. But then again, in this era of political paradoxes, who's really keeping score anymore?
