'I resent it': Teacher exodus hits 'Trump stronghold' due to federal budget cuts

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Stark Reality of Trump's Education Cuts in Montgomery County, North Carolina

A view of the U.S. Department of Education building in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 1, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

A county in North Carolina where nearly 70% of voters favored President Donald Trump in 2024 is facing an exodus of educators following budget cuts to the Department of Education, cuts that have left some residents feeling betrayed.

During Trump’s second term, the Education Department has seen its workforce nearly cut in half, around $900 million worth of research contracts canceled, and around $600 million gutted from teacher-training grants, and all in the name of eliminating “wasteful spending” at the agency, which Trump called a “con job.”

And for some Montgomery County educators, the cuts have not only driven them to different jobs, but to resentment.

“I resent being considered government waste,” said Katie Kimrey, who’s led a 20-year career as an educator, speaking with The Washington Post. “If my kids were grown, shoot, I’d probably be gone, too.”

Kimrey had previously received a $1,500 bonus for her service to Montgomery County schools, a bonus that was to be no more after Trump’s slashing of teacher-training grants.

Another Montgomery County educator, Rhonda Perkins, who voted for Trump, defended her support for the president to The Washington Post, while at the same time, criticizing him for some of his policies.

“I think he’s awful, but he protected my values,” Perkins said.

And other educators had outright left their jobs due to the budget cuts, including 26-year-old Kylie Blankenship, who teaches middle school science.

Speaking with the Washington Post, Blankenship said working for the Montgomery County school system was already a struggle due to its high turnover, lack of resources, and 40-minute daily commute. When it was clear that Trump’s budget cuts would see her modest bonus disappear, Blankenship said she instead sought work elsewhere.

“The fact that those grants were cut, it did push me farther,” Blankenship told The Washington Post. “It gave me that kick.”

Blankenship now works in a neighboring school district making around $42,500, and is joined by at least nine other teachers from Montgomery County that left since Trump’s budget cuts were enacted.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Reality of Trump's Education Cuts in Montgomery County, North Carolina

In a striking revelation from Montgomery County, North Carolina, the consequences of President Trump's draconian cuts to the Department of Education are manifesting in ways that even his staunchest supporters find hard to digest. Nearly 70% of this county's voters cast their ballots for Trump in 2024, yet they now grapple with the fallout from his policies, which have severely impacted the local education system. Let's dissect the layers of irony, betrayal, and the dire consequences of these budget cuts.

Trump's Assault on Education: The President's second term has been marked by a ruthless downsizing of the Education Department, slashing its workforce by half, canceling around $900 million worth of research contracts, and gutting $600 million from teacher-training grants. These actions stem from Trump's crusade against what he deems "wasteful spending," dubbing the agency a "con job." This rhetoric, while appealing to fiscal conservatives, blatantly ignores the critical role of education in societal progress and economic stability.

The Human Cost: Behind the cold numbers and policy changes are real people whose lives and careers have been upended. Katie Kimrey, a dedicated educator with 20 years of service, encapsulates the sentiment of betrayal, stating, “I resent being considered government waste.” This sentiment is echoed across the community, as educators are forced to reconsider their career paths, not out of a lack of passion for teaching, but due to the unsustainable conditions fostered by these cuts.

The Irony of Political Loyalty: Rhonda Perkins, an educator who voted for Trump, represents a perplexing dichotomy. She criticizes Trump's policies that directly harm her profession while still supporting him for protecting her values. This cognitive dissonance highlights a broader trend where voters prioritize certain ideological values over practical policies that impact their daily lives and livelihoods.

The Exodus of Educators: Kylie Blankenship's departure from Montgomery County schools to seek better opportunities elsewhere is a testament to the dire straits faced by educators under Trump's policies. The loss of modest bonuses, compounded by high turnover, a lack of resources, and challenging working conditions, has spurred an exodus of at least nine teachers from the county since the budget cuts were implemented.

The Bitter Pill of Reality: The situation in Montgomery County serves as a microcosm of the broader implications of Trump's education policies. It lays bare the stark disconnect between political rhetoric and the tangible impact of governance on individuals' lives. Voters in Montgomery County, and indeed across the country, are forced to reckon with the consequences of their electoral choices, confronting the harsh truth that the policies enacted by those they support can sometimes betray their interests and values.

In conclusion, the unfolding drama in Montgomery County is a poignant reminder of the importance of evaluating political leaders not just on their words or the values they claim to protect but on the tangible outcomes of their policies. As educators in this North Carolina county and elsewhere navigate the fallout, the broader discourse around education funding and its critical role in shaping the future of the country remains more relevant than ever. The real "con job," it seems, is convincing voters that slashing education funding will not have devastating consequences for communities and the nation at large.
