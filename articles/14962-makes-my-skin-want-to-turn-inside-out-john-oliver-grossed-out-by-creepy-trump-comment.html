'Makes my skin want to turn inside out': John Oliver grossed out by 'creepy' Trump comment

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Parsing Oliver's Critique of Trump's Comments on Young Girls

John Oliver. (Shutterstock)

HBO's John Oliver highlighted President Donald Trump's comments about young girls Sunday night — and called them skin-crawling.

The president tried to justify his tariffs last week by arguing that girls might be forced to settle for “two dolls instead of 30 dolls" because they might "cost a couple of bucks more. But the "Last Week Tonight" host pointed to one particular comment that “makes my skin want to turn inside out."

“What a very weird thing to say,” Oliver said. “It’s just another snapshot in the chaos album that is Trump having anything to do with children.”

Trump defended his comments in an interview on NBC's "Meet The Press" by describing a hypothetical 11-year-old as "a beautiful baby girl," which Oliver found bizarre.

“Don’t call an 11-year-old that," Oliver said. "First of all, it’s creepy, and second, I promise: You call an 11-year-old girl a baby, she’ll ... kill you. Also… everyone knows the breakdown of what people play with by age goes, blocks from ages 1-3, dolls from ages 3-7, and our phones from ages 8 until we die.”

Trump later told reporters on Air Force One girls of various ages up to 15 years old would be "very happy" with just a few dolls.

“Every single way this man refers to girls makes my skin want to turn inside out,” Oliver said. “That said, I also wanted to see him keep going there, just to see how many variations of ages and numbers he could cycle through.”

“Has he ever met a 15-year-old girl?" Oliver added. "That’s a dumb question. Of course he has. He was friends with Jeffrey Epstein.”

Trump has seemingly focused the impact of his tariffs on dolls because it allows him to frame his trade war as "frivolous," Oliver said.

“But the fact is, exports to the U.S. have already begun to slow, and that’ll soon be felt in the form of higher prices on lots of things," he said. “So if you’re wondering: Is Trump’s stupid trade war about to hurt a lot of people? Well, is the Pope Catholic and now also some guy named Bob from Chicago? The answer is obviously yes.”

Our Analysis:

Parsing Oliver's Critique of Trump's Comments on Young Girls

John Oliver's recent takedown of President Donald Trump's comments about young girls not only spotlights the inherent weirdness in Trump's remarks but also serves as a critical reflection on the broader implications of his presidency, particularly regarding his trade policies. Oliver's analysis, while laced with humor, underscores a disturbing pattern in Trump's rhetoric and policies that cannot be ignored.

Trump's Odd Fixation on Dolls and "Beautiful Baby Girls"

First, let's address the elephant in the room: Trump's comments about young girls, as highlighted by Oliver, are indeed skin-crawling. The specific fixation on girls having to settle for fewer dolls due to tariffs is bizarre on multiple levels. It's not just the oddity of focusing on dolls as a primary concern in the midst of a trade war; it's the unsettling way Trump discusses young girls that raises eyebrows. Describing an 11-year-old as "a beautiful baby girl" is, as Oliver rightly points out, creepy. It's a remark that doesn't just border on inappropriate; it leaps over that border with alarming indifference.

The Disturbing Pattern of Trump's Comments

Oliver's critique shines a light on a disturbing pattern in Trump's interaction with or remarks about children, especially girls. This pattern isn't just about awkward word choices; it's about a consistent demonstration of Trump's inability to understand or respect boundaries. Calling an 11-year-old a "beautiful baby girl" is not a benign slip; it's part of a larger narrative that Trump has built over the years, one that Oliver notes includes a friendship with Jeffrey Epstein, a convicted sex offender. Oliver's connection here isn't just for shock value; it's a legitimate concern about the kind of company Trump keeps and what that says about his views and treatment of young girls.

The Bigger Picture: Trump's Trade War

Beyond the immediate creepiness of Trump's comments, Oliver points to a larger, more consequential issue: the impact of Trump's tariffs. By focusing on the trivial impact on dolls, Trump attempts to trivialize the broader effects of his trade war. However, as Oliver highlights, the slowdown in exports to the U.S. and the resulting higher prices on a wide range of goods are anything but trivial. The tariffs aren't just about "two dolls instead of 30"; they're about real economic repercussions for American consumers and businesses alike.

Oliver's Scathing Conclusion

Oliver's critique culminates in a scathing conclusion about the seriousness of Trump's trade war. By likening the obviousness of the trade war's consequences to the Pope's Catholicism (with a humorous twist), Oliver emphasizes the undeniable reality of the situation. Trump's policies, and his defense of them through bizarre and inappropriate comments, will have tangible negative effects on many people.

In essence, John Oliver's analysis isn't just a comedic takedown; it's a sharp, fact-based critique of Trump's presidency. Through his examination of Trump's weird comments about girls and the broader implications of his trade policies, Oliver not only entertains but also educates his audience on the systemic issues at play. It's a powerful reminder of the role of satire in political commentary and the importance of holding leaders accountable for their words and actions.
