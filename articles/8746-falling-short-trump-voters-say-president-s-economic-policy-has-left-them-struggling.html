'Falling short': Trump voters say president's economic policy has left them 'struggling'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 14, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Economic Promises: Unfulfilled and Unraveling

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump speaks during a roundtable at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 8, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

Donald Trump’s economic policy is failing those who voted for the president in the last election.

A rise in electricity, rent, and grocery prices has affected people across the country, including those who voted for Trump in the 2024 election. Those who cast their vote for Trump are now saying his policies on the economy are failing them. One person even suggested that, despite making more money than in previous years, this year has been the most financially stressful time for them.

Some voters say Trump has simply failed to bring prices down, a promise he made on the campaign trail last year. Billy Edmonson, speaking to NBC, said he’s making more money than ever yet still struggling because of the state of the economy.

Edmonson, a construction worker, said, “Everything’s already so expensive... That’s the worst thing about his presidency so far, is he promised things coming down and prices are not coming down. I make more money than I’ve ever made in my life right now, and it has, financially, been a struggle.”

“It just doesn’t seem like there’s been a whole lot of focus on, ‘Hey, let’s get these prices down.’ It’s just been like, ‘Hey, prices are high because Biden did this.’ It’s like, well, Biden’s not in office anymore. It’s personally frustrating.” Other Trump voters are in the same boat, highlighting a stark difference between what’s actually happening for the economy, and what the president says is happening.

Retiree Susan, 66, said: “He and his advisers claim the country is going well, prices are going down, the tariffs are doing wonderfully. But when you go to the store, the prices are up.” Trump has recently been grilled on rising prices for items like beef and coffee, the latter price apparently fixable by lowering tariffs, the president says.

He told Fox News, “Coffee, we’re going to lower some tariffs. We’re going to have some coffee come in. We’re gonna take care of all this stuff very quickly, very easily.” Other voters were left worrying for the next generation, with Jeremiah, 48, saying the contrast in the quality of life with his son is clear.

He said, “I was definitely hoping to see a lot of prices come back down, like gas for your car, diesel, utilities, groceries. I was hoping to see it all come back down and level out at least, but it’s not. It just keeps getting worse. I have a 20-year-old son and a 17-year-old daughter, and they can’t live on their own.”

“My son’s a welder, and he makes $28 an hour, and he’s still not out on his own. Everything is just so overpriced. It’s ridiculous. When I graduated high school, I moved out and had a $300 a month apartment, piece of cake, 10 bucks an hour.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Promises: Unfulfilled and Unraveling

Donald Trump’s economic policy, by the accounts of those who helped hoist him into the presidency, is not only failing—it’s a glaring testament to the chasm between campaign promises and tangible policy outcomes. A sharp rise in essential living costs, including electricity, rent, and groceries, has left a bitter taste in the mouths of his very electorate. This isn’t merely about discontent; it’s a deep-seated feeling of betrayal among those who saw Trump as a beacon of economic resurgence.

The Broken Promise of Lower Prices

One of the most jarring revelations comes from the very mouths of Trump voters, who now find themselves entrapped in an economic paradox: making more money than ever, yet suffocating under the weight of escalating prices. Billy Edmonson, a construction worker, encapsulates this sentiment with a clarity that’s as damning as it is desperate. He highlights a fundamental flaw in Trump’s presidency: a blatant failure to reduce prices, despite bold promises to do so. Edmonson’s experience, marked by financial struggle despite increased earnings, is a scathing indictment of Trump’s economic stewardship.

The narrative of discontent doesn’t end with Edmonson. Other voters echo this sentiment, pointing to a stark disparity between the rosy economic picture painted by Trump and his advisers, and the grim reality of inflationary pressure that squeezes the average American household. Susan, a retiree, brings to light the disillusionment that festers within the electorate, as the promised economic prosperity remains elusive.

The Scapegoat Strategy

Trump’s approach to addressing the rising cost of living—an issue that resonates deeply with the blue-collar demographic that forms a significant portion of his base—has been, at best, a game of deflection. Blaming his predecessor for the economic woes, without presenting a coherent strategy to ameliorate them, is a tactic that resonates poorly with those who expected tangible solutions, not finger-pointing.

The Tariff Tango

Trump’s recent comments on tariffs, specifically regarding coffee, offer a glimpse into a policy approach that seems both reactive and superficial. The promise to lower tariffs to mitigate price hikes is a Band-Aid solution to a hemorrhaging economic problem. It lacks the comprehensive vision needed to tackle systemic inflation and restore economic stability for the average American.

A Generation in Economic Peril

Jeremiah, a concerned father, brings a human dimension to the economic discourse—a worrying glimpse into the future. Despite decent wages, his children are unable to afford the independent living standards that previous generations took for granted. This isn’t just an economic issue; it’s a crisis of the American Dream, where hard work no longer guarantees a decent standard of living.

In conclusion, the dissonance between Trump’s economic promises and the harsh reality faced by his voters is not just a political conundrum—it’s a systemic failure that threatens the very fabric of American society. The voices of discontent, from Edmonson to Jeremiah, underscore a profound disillusionment that, if unaddressed, may redefine the political landscape in years to come. Trump’s economic policies, as they stand, are not just failing those who voted for him; they’re undermining the trust in government’s ability to effectuate positive change in the lives of its citizens.
