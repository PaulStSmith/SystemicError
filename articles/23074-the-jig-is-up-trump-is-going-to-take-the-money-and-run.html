The jig is up: Trump is going to take the money and run

Based on an article by John Stoehr

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Is Not “Holding Back” Money. He Is Holding Hostage Power.

The Story Beneath the Story

The source report says Trump is sitting on more than $400 million in super PAC cash while Republican leaders grow anxious that he will not deploy it for the midterms. That is the polite version. The plain version is simpler: he controls money he raised through access to power, and he is deciding whether to spend it on the party or keep it for himself.

Who Actually Has the Power

The important fact is not that Republicans are worried. It is that Trump personally approves the spending. That makes the cash not a party asset in any meaningful sense, but a private leverage pool attached to the presidency. The White House, the donor stream, the inauguration fund, the ballroom, the “Freedom 250” events: these are all nodes in the same machine. Corporate money flows in because people want favor, and Trump decides where the favor goes.

Call It What It Is

The article circles the truth without quite naming it: this is theft-shaped politics. Not necessarily a criminal charge, but a structure built to extract money from institutions and then redirect it to Trump’s future, his legal bills, his travel, his projects, or whatever else keeps the racket alive. “Holding on” to donor money sounds strategic only if you pretend the donors are participants instead of marks.

Republicans Built This Trap

There is no mystery here and no innocence to recover. Republicans chose him because he gave them control over women, minorities, immigrants, workers, religion, and free expression, and because they believed they could use him without being used by him. That was always fantasy. A movement that courts authoritarian power eventually discovers that authoritarian power does not remain polite, optional, or shared.

The Real Misdirection

What these stories often soften is agency. They speak in the language of “theories,” “suspicion,” and “some people around Trump,” as if a man with total control over the money were an atmospheric condition. He is not confusion. He is the decision-maker. His donors are not confused either. They are buying access in a system where access is the product.

The Pattern

This is what plutocratic authoritarianism looks like in practice: money raised through office, loyalty enforced through fear, and institutions reduced to personal property. If Trump is now hoarding the cash because he senses the spell may be breaking, that does not make him weaker in any moral sense. It makes him what he has always been: a collector of tribute who understands that the grift ends when the protection fails. The lesson is not that Republicans may be left stranded. The lesson is that they mistook a con man for a vehicle and ended up as inventory.
