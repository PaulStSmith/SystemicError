'Driving people crazy': GOP ex-lawmaker says Trump using 'deflection' to avoid big issue

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 18, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Distraction Dance: Trump's Defiance and the Deportation Diversion

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the media on board Air Force One on the way to West Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., April 13, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

A former Republican congressman speculated that president Donald Trump was fighting the courts to keep a Maryland man imprisoned in El Salvador as a distraction from an even more unpopular move.

U.S. District judge Paula Xinis ordered sworn testimony from Trump administration officials to determine whether they complied with her orders to facilitate the return of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, who was mistakenly deported to a notorious high-security prison overseas, and former GOP congressman Charlie Dent told "CNN This Morning" why he thinks the president was picking that fight.

"Well, I have a kind of cynical view about this whole issue," Dent said. "I tend to think that Donald Trump would like nothing more than to talk about this issue ad nauseam, about whether or not a person who entered the United States unlawfully should be returned from his native country."

Dent argued that the president was defying the courts to turn attention away from his sweeping tariffs against virtually all U.S. trading partners that has rattled the stock market and lopped nearly 10 percent off the dollar.

"Setting aside all the facts in the court orders, I think he's doing it because he'd rather be talking about this than the stock market tumbling, the bond market being volatile," Dent said. "Farmers can't find export markets for their their product, consumers are worried about the price of everything from cars to to sneakers, so I think this is a great deflection from the issues that are really driving a lot of people in this country crazy right now."

The president's approval rating sunk to 44 percent in a new UMass poll, compared to 51 percent who disapprove – including 44 percent who "strongly disapprove" of his tenure so far – although he still enjoys a 50 percent-46 percent advantage on his handling of immigration issues.

However, nearly two-thirds of respondents, or 62 percent, don't think Trump is doing enough to fight inflation, and 58 percent told pollsters he was mishandling his trade policy.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Distraction Dance: Trump's Defiance and the Deportation Diversion

In an era where political maneuvers often resemble a circus more than a solemn duty to govern, former Republican congressman Charlie Dent's speculation on Donald Trump's latest escapade adds another act to the show. According to Dent, Trump's obstinate battle with the courts over the wrongful deportation of Kilmar Abrego Garcia isn't just a matter of legal wrangling; it's a calculated distraction. A smokescreen to divert attention from his administration's economically damaging policies, particularly the sweeping tariffs that have sent shockwaves through the stock market and slashed the value of the dollar by nearly 10 percent.

The Master of Misdirection

Let's dissect this with the precision it deserves. Trump, a president whose tenure has been marked by controversy and chaos, allegedly finds solace in the uproar over immigration issues. With an approval rating hanging by a thread at 44 percent, as per a new UMass poll, one might argue that Trump is clutching at straws, trying to maintain a semblance of favorability where he can. Interestingly, despite the overall disapproval, he somehow manages to hold a 50 percent-46 percent advantage when it comes to handling immigration issues. The question arises: Is this the reason behind his alleged choice of battle? To keep the public's eyes fixated on the one area where he seems to have an edge?

Economic Turmoil as a Backdrop

Dent's assertion that Trump would rather fuel the fire of immigration debates than address the economic turmoil at home is telling. The tariffs imposed by his administration have not only unsettled the stock market but have also made the bond market volatile. Farmers are in distress, unable to find export markets for their products, while consumers are grappling with the rising costs of goods, from automobiles to sneakers. This paints a grim picture of a nation under strain, a strain that the president seemingly chooses to ignore or, worse, obscure with other controversies.

A Cynical Strategy?

The cynicism in Dent's perspective is hard to miss and harder to dismiss. If this speculation holds any truth, it reveals a disturbing strategy of governance where the well-being of citizens and the stability of the economy are sacrificed at the altar of political expediency. The deliberate choice to engage in a legal battle over the deportation of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, who was mistakenly deported to a high-security prison in El Salvador, underscores a willingness to exploit human suffering for political gain.

Conclusion: The Real Issues Demand Attention

As the former GOP congressman points out, the president's apparent preference for distraction over discussion, for deflection over action, does a disservice to the American people. The real issues—economic instability, the volatility of the market, and the distress of the agricultural sector—demand urgent attention and thoughtful policy responses, not the continual churn of controversy and chaos.

In the end, if Dent's speculations are accurate, this strategy might be more damaging to Trump's legacy than any economic policy. The art of distraction, while momentarily effective, can only sustain public attention for so long before the harsh realities of governance demand to be faced. It's high time the administration tackles the systemic errors crippling the nation, rather than perpetuating them through calculated diversions.
