In an opinion piece for MSNBC, Graves-Fitzsimmons wrote Monday evening that DHS’s "bible-quoting" recruitment videos are "a confession of moral bankruptcy."

He pointed to the Trump administration defending its draconian mass deportation operation by using Bible verses. That includes a pair of videos in the last month, including one that "bastardized" Isaiah 6, he said.

"As images of helicopters and tactical agents ominously scroll, the narrator says: 'Then I heard the voice of the Lord saying, ‘Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?’ And I said, ‘Here am I; send me.'"

In another video, DHS shares "dark images" of militaristic immigration enforcement actions, "paired with Proverbs 28: 'The wicked flee when no man pursueth; but the righteous are bold as a lion.'"

In a post on social media, meanwhile, DHS shared the painting "American Progress" — a famous Manifest Destiny image — with the caption, "A Heritage to be proud of, a Homeland worth Defending."

Graves-Fitzsimmons questioned why the administration has resorted to using Bible references while it militarizes an immigration crackdown and "creates a concentration camp in Florida." He suggested that the Trump administration may be feeling it needs to resort to "religious fervor" to get the rank and file behind its "brutal cruelty and injustice."

"A government confident that its policies are just wouldn’t need to borrow God’s authority to sell them. By wrapping itself in scripture, the Trump administration has only confirmed what its critics, including many devout believers, have been saying all along: This deportation crackdown isn’t a divine mandate, it’s a national disgrace, and faith communities will not be fooled into blessing it," he concluded.

Our Analysis:

The Divine Disgrace: Unpacking Kristi Noem's DHS Recruitment Tactics

In the latest turn of events that could only be described as startlingly dystopian, Kristi Noem's Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has decided that the best way to recruit for its immigration enforcement ranks is to invoke the divine. According to Guthrie Graves-Fitzsimmons in an opinion piece for MSNBC, this move reeks of desperation and moral bankruptcy. Let's dissect this unholy alliance of scripture and state-sanctioned violence.

The Bastardization of Biblical Texts

The DHS has released recruitment videos that could only be described as blasphemous in their context. One video shamelessly manipulates the words from Isaiah 6, juxtaposing images of helicopters and tactical agents with the question, "Whom shall I send? And who will go for us?" To which they audaciously answer, "Here am I; send me." Another video darkens the narrative further by pairing militaristic imagery with Proverbs 28, implying that their immigration enforcement actions are the work of the "righteous" against the "wicked." This perverse manipulation of sacred texts to justify draconian policies is not just a theological misstep; it's a full-blown moral catastrophe.

A Heritage of Hubris

The DHS didn't stop at misquoting scripture; they also shared an image of "American Progress" — a painting emblematic of Manifest Destiny — with a caption that reads, "A Heritage to be proud of, a Homeland worth Defending." This not only glorifies a past of colonial conquest and indigenous displacement but also signals a troubling endorsement of American exceptionalism as justification for current immigration policies. The symbolism here is as subtle as a sledgehammer, equating the expansionist policies of the past with the militarization of today's immigration enforcement.

The Crux of the Critique

Graves-Fitzsimmons strikes at the heart of the matter when he suggests that the Trump administration's resort to religious fervor to sell its immigration crackdown is a sign of desperation. A government confident in the justice of its policies wouldn't need to cloak its actions in divine mandate. This strategy isn't just a cynical ploy to galvanize the rank and file; it's an admission of the inherent inhumanity of their approach. The administration's use of biblical references and American iconography to justify and normalize its immigration policies is nothing short of propaganda.

The Verdict

By wrapping its policies in scripture, the administration confirms what critics and many devout believers have been saying all along: this deportation crackdown is not a divine mandate; it’s a national disgrace. The attempt to co-opt faith communities into supporting these policies is not just doomed to fail; it's an insult to the very moral principles these traditions uphold. The administration's actions are a stark reminder that the invocation of religious texts and patriotic imagery can often serve as a veneer for moral vacuity.

In conclusion, the DHS's recruitment campaign is more than just a series of questionable marketing choices. It's a window into the soul of an administration willing to manipulate faith and patriotism to serve its ends. This isn't just about poor taste or insensitivity; it's about the dangerous precedent it sets for the intersection of religion and policy. It's a stark reminder that when governments resort to invoking divine authority to justify their actions, it's often because earthly justifications are painfully lacking.
