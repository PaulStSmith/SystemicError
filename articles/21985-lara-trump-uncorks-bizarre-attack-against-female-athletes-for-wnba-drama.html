Lara Trump uncorks bizarre attack against female athletes for WNBA drama

Based on an article by Nicole Charky-Chami

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Trump Family Wants Women’s Sports as a Culture-War Prop

What This Story Actually Is

The source material is not really about the WNBA. It is about Lara Trump using a podcast platform to turn a women’s league into a conservative grievance machine, with Theo Wold helping convert that grievance into contempt. The immediate hook is Sophie Cunningham, the Indiana Fever guard who became a right-wing favorite after opposing transgender athletes in women’s sports. That is the bait. The real story is how Trump-world seizes a women’s institution, strips it down to a political prop, and then complains that the result is “too political.”

Who Has Power Here

Lara Trump is not speaking as a league stakeholder, a player, or a labor representative. She is speaking as a member of the Trump family, with all the media access and political amplification that brings. That matters. The people with actual institutional power in this story are not the WNBA players being mocked. They are the right-wing political actors with a platform large enough to launder personal contempt into public narrative.

The WNBA, for its part, is the institution being talked over. Players are being reduced to personality traits, marketing problems, and ideological targets. The league’s collective bargaining agreement is dragged into the argument not as labor policy, but as a cudgel in a culture war. That is a familiar move: strip institutions of their material context, then blame the people inside them for being insufficiently entertaining to the audience you are trying to inflame.

The Point Is Misdirection

Lara Trump’s complaint that women are “catty” and jealous is not analysis. It is gendered dismissal dressed up as commentary. It turns structural criticism into a smear against women themselves. The same goes for Theo Wold’s “root canal” line and the claim that fans do not want athletes to “lecture” them. That is not concern for the product. It is resentment toward women speaking with authority in public.

This is how the misdirection works: the league is framed as the problem, the players are framed as the irritants, and the conservative commentator gets to pose as the defender of ordinary fans. But the irritation is manufactured. The performance depends on pretending that the political project is neutral and the athletes are the ones introducing ideology. In reality, the ideology is coming from the outside, through people with larger megaphones and more power.

The Wedge Is The Product

The fixation on the CBA not defining “woman” tells you what kind of game is being played. This is not a good-faith labor or governance argument. It is an attempt to use legal ambiguity as an opening for conservative culture-war leverage. The phrase is being deployed as a wedge, not a question.

That matters because the right has learned how to turn women’s sports into an ideal target: high emotional content, identity politics on demand, and a ready-made villain in trans inclusion. Sophie Cunningham is useful here because she has voiced opposition to transgender athletes. Conservative actors can elevate her as proof that their politics are “pro-woman” while using her as a permission structure to attack the broader league. The league is praised only insofar as it can be punished.

Conservative Contempt For Women’s Institutions

The uglier pattern is the contempt underneath the whole exchange. The podcast commentary does not merely criticize the WNBA. It belittles women as a group, calls their relationships petty, and treats their public voice as inherently suspect. That is not incidental. It is the point. The same movement that claims to defend women’s sports is comfortable describing women as catty distractions the moment they stop serving the narrative.

This is where the story becomes politically revealing. Conservative outrage over women’s athletics often masks a deeper hostility to women as autonomous political actors. The league is acceptable when it can be mined for branding, grievance, or anti-trans messaging. It becomes intolerable when players sound like citizens rather than mascots.

The Larger Pattern

The broader pattern is simple: Trump-aligned media does not just comment on institutions. It colonizes them symbolically, converts them into partisan fuel, and then blames the institutions for becoming political. Women’s sports are useful because they let the right dress up domination as concern. The actual agenda is not clarity, fairness, or entertainment. It is control over who gets to speak, which identities get legitimacy, and which public spaces can be bent into service for reactionary politics.

That is the real story here. Not a league’s supposed dysfunction. A political machine looking at women’s basketball and seeing another arena to discipline.
