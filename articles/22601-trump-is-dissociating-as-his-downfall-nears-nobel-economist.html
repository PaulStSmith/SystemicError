Trump is 'dissociating' as his 'downfall' nears: Nobel economist

Trump Is Not Falling Alone. The State Is Still Carrying Him.

The Real Story Is Compliance

Paul Krugman’s warning matters less as a diagnosis of Trump’s psyche than as an indictment of the machinery around him. The source is about a president who, even while apparently deteriorating, still commands deference from the federal government. That is the core fact. Personal instability becomes political power only when institutions keep obeying.

Damage Is the Point

Trump’s behavior in the source is not random noise. It is described as a pattern of destruction, humiliation, and branding: provocations, trade wars, a hole where a ballroom should be, grotesque White House redecorating, the nation’s capital turned into a stage set for ego. Whether the impulse is spite, vanity, or panic, the consequence is the same: public assets get treated as raw material for one man’s resentment.

The Blame Shift Is the Old Trick

Trump’s reflex, as described here, is to blame Democrats, Republicans, polling, and everybody else. That is not confusion. It is a deliberate escape hatch. When power is personal, responsibility must be dispersed. The weak are always told to absorb the cost while the strong redefine sabotage as leadership. The article correctly notices the rage, but the more important pattern is the cover story: every failure is somebody else’s fault, every wrecked institution is someone else’s problem.

A Presidency Built Like a Vandal’s Contract

The reporting about Trump fixating on construction projects is revealing because it shows how he understands office: leave a mark, make it visible, make it costly to remove. The point is not governance but imprinting. He does not need a finished triumphal arch to do damage. The disruption itself is the monument. That is why the comparison to Hitler’s final sabotage, while historically loaded, works here only in one narrow sense: it is the politics of scorched earth by a man who treats the state as an extension of his grievance.

The Democratic Coalition Has to Read This Correctly

This is not a story about one bad personality drifting toward ugliness. It is a story about institutional cowardice making room for authoritarian vanity. The federal government, the Republican Party, and every elite actor still pretending this is normal are part of the mechanism that turns rage into national damage. Democrats are not being asked to admire the alarmism; they are being asked to recognize the structure. A democracy cannot survive if its institutions are built to accommodate a ruler who mistakes public power for private revenge.

The Pattern

The larger lesson is simple: authoritarian politics does not always arrive as a clean coup. Sometimes it arrives as a long, ugly conversion of government into a trash compactor for one man’s humiliation. The danger is not just what Trump wants. It is how many people and institutions continue to make room for him to get it.
