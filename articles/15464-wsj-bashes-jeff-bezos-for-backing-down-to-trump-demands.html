WSJ bashes Jeff Bezos for backing down to Trump demands

The Wall Street Journal editorial board took Amazon founder Jeff Bezos to task for backing out of a proposal to indicate the price of President Donald Trump’s tariff on each affected Amazon product.

The White House responded harshly to the proposal, declaring the move a "hostile and political act by Amazon" at a televised morning briefing, but Trump late praised Bezos for changing his mind Tuesday

“He solved the problem very quickly and he did the right thing," Trump said. "He's a good guy."

But the board wants to know why Trump would want to hide the price of his tariffs if he truly intends for them to replace the U.S. income tax.

“Why is he afraid to let Americans see what they’re paying? Instead of owning its policy, the White House has bullied Amazon to keep quiet about what it will cost,” the board wrote.

The public would have valued Amazon’s price transparency, the board argues. “Tariffs are taxes, and it’s helpful to know how policy choices affect final prices. Some shoppers might say the additional cost is worth it to support Mr. Trump’s policy. But there’s no denying that they’re paying, and everyone is better off knowing how much.”

The board went on to explain that ride-sharing platforms understand the dynamic. After each trip, riders get an itemized bill showing what portion of their fare includes tolls, fees and taxes. Hotels also list local taxes with customer bills.

“Mr. Trump likes to say foreign exporters bear all the costs of tariffs, with none passed on to customers. Economists disagree, as in a 2020 study showing that final consumers bore the burden of Mr. Trump’s first-term tariffs. Like taxes, tariffs add a certain dollar figure to an impacted product’s sticker price, and prices on Amazon, which serves as a clearinghouse for heavily tariffed Chinese item, could rise considerably.

“Voters who pay little attention to the macroeconomy will notice a direct markup on items they buy,” the board says. In fact, “consumers are already feeling the tariff pain, whether or not retailers quantify it on their websites. White House denials won’t change that, but repealing the tariffs would.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Concealment: Trump, Bezos, and the Tariff Truth

In a classic display of political theater, the Trump administration has once again showcased its adeptness at manipulating public perception, this time with Amazon and its founder, Jeff Bezos, as the unwilling co-stars. The Wall Street Journal editorial board's scathing critique of this episode lays bare the administration's efforts to obscure the true cost of its policies—specifically, the tariffs imposed on imported goods. Let's dissect the layers of this saga with the precision of a scalpel, revealing the underlying truths and the blatant attempts at deception.

The Unwilling Accomplice: Amazon's Pivot

Initially, Bezos’s proposal to include the cost of Trump’s tariffs on each affected product seemed like a stride towards transparency. It was a move that, in any rational universe, would be applauded for providing consumers with clear information about the financial implications of political decisions. However, we don't live in a rational universe; we inhabit one where the White House's response to such transparency is to label it a "hostile and political act." The swift backlash from the administration, followed by Trump's subsequent praise for Bezos's about-face, reads like a poorly scripted reality show—except the implications are all too real.

The Irony of Transparency

The editorial board’s question hits the nail on the head: Why indeed would Trump want to hide the price of his tariffs if he truly believes they serve a greater economic purpose? The administration's aggressive stance against Amazon's proposal underscores a fundamental truth about its economic policies: they are far more concerned with maintaining a facade of infallibility than with the wellbeing of the American consumer.

Tariffs as Taxes: The Unacknowledged Burden

The attempt to shield the public from the true cost of tariffs is emblematic of a broader issue—the administration's refusal to acknowledge that tariffs are, in effect, taxes paid by American consumers. This is not an opinion; it's an economic fact, supported by a plethora of studies, including the 2020 study mentioned in the article, which clearly states that consumers bear the brunt of these tariffs. The administration's narrative that foreign exporters "bear all the costs" is not just misleading; it's demonstrably false.

The Real Cost of Political Posturing

The editorial rightly points out the absurdity of the White House's position. By stifling Amazon's attempt at price transparency, the administration has chosen to keep the electorate in the dark about the real-world consequences of its trade policies. This move may serve short-term political interests, but it does a disservice to the American public, who are left to unknowingly shoulder the financial burden of these tariffs.

In Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The saga of Amazon's retracted proposal is a microcosm of a larger, more troubling trend in the current administration's approach to governance. It's a trend characterized by obfuscation, intimidation, and a blatant disregard for the economic wellbeing of the American people. The Wall Street Journal editorial board's critique is not just a condemnation of this specific incident; it's a call for transparency, accountability, and a return to policies grounded in economic reality rather than political expediency.

In the final analysis, this episode reaffirms the importance of vigilance and critical scrutiny in the face of attempts to manipulate public perception. It's a stark reminder that in the age of information, the truth is often a casualty of political warfare. But it's also a reminder that the truth, like water, has a way of finding its level—no matter how many barriers are erected in its path.
