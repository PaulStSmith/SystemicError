Ex-GOP strategist tears apart CNN Trump 'shill’s' flawed 'rationalizations'

Like Fox News’ Sean Hannity, CNN’s Scott Jennings often draws scathing criticism and mockery from Donald Trump opponents for stridently defending the president at every turn. Much of the criticism comes from Democrats, yet right-wing Never Trumpers have major disagreements with Jennings as well.

Some of those Never Trumpers are at CNN, including SE Cupp and Florida-based Ana Navarro (a Nicaraguan immigrant who, in the past, helped former Florida Gov. Jeb Bush and other Republicans with their Latino outreach).

Jennings recently attacked Never Trumpers, claiming that Trump is quite consistent with traditional GOP conservatism. But The Bulwark’s Tim Miller, a former Republican strategist, pushed back against Jennings’ pro-Trump “rationalizations” in a biting video posted in late November.

Miller told viewers, “(Jennings’) core argument for Donald Trump is that he will stand up for America and he will stand up for the West. I just think this is a fundamentally flawed argument…. There’s some idea that the left is out there and they’re in league with the Shariah-law Muslims and these other people that hate American values — so you have no choice but to support Donald Trump.”

Jennings, Miller noted, went from being a “rank-and-file part of the Bush GOP” to “Trump’s biggest cable-TV shill.” And the “problem” with Jennings’ pro-Trump arguments, according to Miller, is that “Donald Trump is a threat on American values at home and abroad.”

“And Donald Trump is a threat to the West,” Miller added. “If you care about the western world order — the post-World War 2 alliances that have allowed us to live peacefully in this world, that have created unimaginable economic success and freedom and liberty for people in Europe and here and throughout the world — then how can you say you’re for Donald Trump? Donald Trump is trying to unravel all that.”

The Never Trump conservative added, “Donald Trump is trying to dismantle the western world order. He said so. He went to the Middle East and gave a big speech about how all this talk in the past about supporting democracy abroad and all this stuff was naïve, silly, dumb.”

Miller argued that while Jennings makes light of Trump’s “bullying of Canada,” that “bullying” is having a “real impact” — as Canada is now working more “with China.”

Our Analysis:

The Perversion of Conservatism: Jennings, Trump, and the GOP Identity Crisis

In an era where political commentary often descends into the realms of the absurd and the fanatically partisan, Scott Jennings’ steadfast defense of Donald Trump as a beacon of traditional GOP conservatism not only stretches credulity but outright snaps it. Jennings, a figure who has become synonymous with the kind of unflinching allegiance to Trump often ridiculed by both sides of the political spectrum, finds himself at the heart of a debate that is less about policy than it is about the soul of the Republican Party.

The Fractured Right

The criticism Jennings faces from Democrats is as predictable as sunrise. However, the disdain from the so-called “Never Trumpers” within his own party underscores a schism that is about more than just personal animosities; it’s indicative of a profound ideological drift. Figures like SE Cupp, Ana Navarro, and notably, Tim Miller, represent a faction within the GOP that views Trump’s version of conservatism as anathema to the party’s core principles. Jennings’ attack on these Never Trumpers, therefore, is not just a defense of Trump but a testament to the ongoing battle for the identity of the Republican Party.

Jennings’ Flawed Crusade

Jennings’ assertion that Trump is the paragon of GOP values is not just flawed; it’s fundamentally misguided. Tim Miller’s critique, as presented in his “biting video,” is not merely an attack on Jennings but a lament for the perversion of conservative values. Miller articulates a vision of conservatism that transcends partisan politics, emphasizing the importance of the post-World War 2 western order, alliances that have fostered peace, economic success, and the spread of democracy and liberty.

Donald Trump as a Threat to American Values

Miller’s assertion that Trump poses a threat to American values and, by extension, the western world order, is a powerful counter-narrative to Jennings’ pro-Trump rationalizations. The argument that Trump’s policies and demeanor represent a continuity of conservative principles is dismantled by Trump’s own actions and statements, including his disparagement of democratic alliances and his unsettling affinity for authoritarian figures.

The Real Impact of Trump’s Policies

The consequences of Trump’s approach, as Miller points out, extend beyond ideological debates and have tangible effects on international relations. The “bullying of Canada,” trivialized by Jennings, is emblematic of Trump’s broader disdain for traditional allies and his misguided belief in the efficacy of unilateralism. This approach not only undermines decades of diplomatic efforts but also pushes long-standing allies towards other global powers, as evidenced by Canada’s increased engagement with China.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The debate between Scott Jennings and his critics is more than just a squabble among pundits. It’s reflective of a Republican Party at a crossroads, torn between its traditional conservative values and the populist, nationalist fervor stoked by Donald Trump. Jennings’ defense of Trump, while perhaps intended as a show of party loyalty, instead highlights the existential crisis facing the GOP. As the party grapples with its identity in the post-Trump era, figures like Tim Miller offer a reminder of the principles that once defined conservatism. The question now is not just about who gets to define Republican values, but whether the party can reconcile its past with its present, or if it will continue to be mired in the divisive, destructive politics of Donald Trump.
