White House scrambles to walk back gold bar tariff amid frenzy from traders

President Donald Trump's administration caused an uproar of panic from traders when it appeared to apply new tariffs to the importation of gold bars — and is now scrambling to clarify there will be an exemption, Bloomberg News reported Friday.

"Industry figures had understood that bars would be exempt from President Donald Trump’s so-called reciprocal tariffs, including a 39% on goods from Switzerland, a major exporter of the precious metal," reported Jennifer Dlouhy. However, U.S. Customs and Border Protection issued an agency letter "rul[ing] that one-kilogram and 100-ounce gold bars are subject to the levies," in response to queries from a gold refiner, triggering immediate confusion and panic.

In response, Trump's White House "intends to post an executive order in the near future to clarify what an official called misinformation about the tariffing of gold and other specialty products," said the report.

Trump's reciprocal tariff scheme, which has imposed billions of dollars in new taxes on importing foreign goods from virtually every country in the world, is ostensibly a bargaining chip to force other countries to make trade deals with the United States. Trump has since bragged about securing a number of such deals, but has frequently misled about the nature and terms of these agreements, or at times, even pretended deals are in the works that aren't.

Meanwhile, experts have sounded the alarm that the tariffs are already starting to put upward pressure on inflation, after years of efforts to regain price stability following the pandemic and the global disruption of supply chains.

A federal court recently heard arguments that Trump does not have the unilateral authority to impose tariffs without approval by Congress, which has sent Trump into a fury over the possibility that one of the signature policies of his second term could be blown up.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Gold Tariff Fiasco Under Trump's Administration

In a move that can only be described as a hallmark of the Trump administration's haphazard approach to governance, a recent debacle involving the imposition of tariffs on gold bars has sent shockwaves through the trading community. As Bloomberg News reported, the administration caused a stir when it seemed to apply new tariffs on the importation of gold bars, only to later backtrack and announce an exemption. This episode is emblematic of a broader pattern of policy improvisation and confusion that has characterized President Donald Trump's tenure.

The Miscommunication Meltdown The core of the issue lies in a gross miscommunication or, perhaps, a lack of foresight. Industry insiders were under the impression that gold bars would be exempt from Trump's "reciprocal tariffs," which include a staggering 39% on goods from Switzerland—a key player in the gold market. This belief was shattered when U.S. Customs and Border Protection, responding to an inquiry, ruled that gold bars were indeed subject to these levies. The ensuing panic underscores a fundamental lack of clarity and consistency in the administration's trade policies.

A Band-Aid Solution In response to the uproar, the Trump White House has promised to issue an executive order to clear up what it referred to as "misinformation" about the tariffing of gold and other specialty products. This reactionary measure is indicative of a broader pattern of governance by chaos, where policies are announced, retracted, or modified with little regard for the consequences or the stakeholders involved. The promise of an executive order to clarify the situation is a Band-Aid solution to a self-inflicted wound, doing little to assuage the concerns of traders and investors who operate on the basis of predictability and stability.

The Illusion of Trade Victories Trump's reciprocal tariff scheme has been touted as a strategic maneuver to force other countries into trade deals favorable to the United States. The President has boasted about securing numerous deals, yet there's a glaring disparity between his claims and the reality. The administration has often been found to mislead about the nature and terms of these agreements, or even fabricate the existence of deals that are nowhere near fruition. This discrepancy not only erodes trust in the administration's credibility but also raises serious questions about the efficacy of its trade policies.

Inflationary Pressures and Legal Challenges Experts have been sounding the alarm that the tariffs could exacerbate inflationary pressures, jeopardizing years of effort to regain price stability in the aftermath of the pandemic and the global disruption of supply chains. Moreover, a federal court's consideration of arguments that Trump lacks the unilateral authority to impose tariffs without Congressional approval adds a legal dimension to the controversy. The President's fury over the challenge to one of his signature policies underscores the tension between executive overreach and the constitutional checks and balances designed to curb such impulses.

Conclusion The gold tariff episode is a microcosm of the broader issues plaguing the Trump administration's approach to trade and economic policy. It reflects a penchant for impulsive decision-making, a disregard for the consequences of those decisions, and a troubling disconnect between the administration's rhetoric and the reality of its policy outcomes. As traders and the broader economy grapple with the fallout, the need for a coherent, predictable, and transparent approach to policy-making has never been more apparent.
