'Truck drivers don’t think so': Trump scorched as he insists empty ports a 'good thing'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Economic Fallacies: A Case Study in MisunderstandingThe Misguided Celebration of a SlowdownThe Real Cost of Trade CurtailmentA Dangerous Economic PhilosophyConclusion

FILE PHOTO: A few shipping containers sit in place at the Port of Los Angeles in San Pedro, California, U.S. May 5, 2025. REUTERS/David Swanson/File Photo

President Donald Trump is facing backlash after claiming that a sharp decline in port traffic—and a significant drop in goods entering the U.S.—is actually a positive development. When warned that the slowdown could cost truckers and dock workers their paychecks or even their jobs, Trump praised the downturn, arguing it means the country isn’t “losing money.”

“That means we lose less money, you know?” President Trump told reporters in the Oval Office. “When I see that, that means we lose less money. Look, China was making over a trillion, $1.1 trillion, in my opinion. You know, different numbers from $500 billion to a trillion or a trillion, I think it was 1.1 trillion. And frankly, if we didn’t do business, we would have been better off.”

“Okay, you understand that?” Trump continued. “So when you say it’s slowed down, that’s a good thing, not a bad thing.”

The President’s remarks were quickly criticized.

U.S. Senator Mark Warner (D-VA) commented, “It’s not a good thing. Dock workers & truck drivers don’t think it’s a good thing. Businesses don’t think it’s a good thing.”

U.S. Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA) wrote, “I represent the second largest container port in the U.S, I promise you it’s a bad thing.”

CNN’s Kaitlan Collins quoted Trump’s “good thing, not a bad thing” comment, then posted video of Seattle’s port commissioner saying, “We currently do not have any container ships at port right now.”

MSNBC anchor Stephanie Ruhle, who spent 14 years at top financial services companies before starting her journalism career, wrote that she had just sent the President’s comments “to every wall st source I have that supported our President.”

“Every response I got was some sort of embarrassed emoji.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Economic Fallacies: A Case Study in Misunderstanding

In a recent statement that defies basic economic principles, President Donald Trump has astonishingly claimed that a sharp decline in port traffic and the consequential drop in goods entering the U.S. is a positive development. Trump's rationale—that this slowdown means the country is "losing less money"—is not only a gross simplification of international trade dynamics but also a dangerous misunderstanding of economic health indicators.

The Misguided Celebration of a Slowdown

Trump's comments came amidst warnings that the slowdown could result in significant financial hardship for truckers and dock workers, potentially costing them their paychecks or even their jobs. Yet, the President praised the downturn, suggesting that reduced trade activity with countries like China, which he inaccurately claims was making over $1.1 trillion at the expense of the U.S., is somehow beneficial. This assertion is not only factually dubious—given the range of estimates he throws around from $500 billion to $1.1 trillion—but also reveals a fundamental misapprehension of how trade deficits work.

The Real Cost of Trade Curtailment

The backlash was swift and came from multiple quarters. Figures like U.S. Senator Mark Warner (D-VA) and U.S. Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), the latter representing the second-largest container port in the U.S., were quick to point out the real-world implications of such a slowdown. CNN’s Kaitlan Collins and MSNBC anchor Stephanie Ruhle highlighted the dissonance between Trump's comments and the views of experts and the business community, with Ruhle noting the embarrassed reaction from Wall Street sources.

A Dangerous Economic Philosophy

Trump's remarks underscore a dangerous economic philosophy that prioritizes misguided perceptions of winning and losing over the complexities of global trade and the well-being of American workers. The idea that the U.S. benefits from less trade is a simplistic and harmful notion that ignores the benefits of a global economic system that has lifted millions out of poverty, fostered innovation, and created vast markets for American goods and services.

Furthermore, Trump's celebration of the trade downturn as a cost-saving measure is a false economy. The real cost of diminished port traffic extends beyond the immediate job losses for dock workers and truckers. It signals reduced economic activity, diminished exports, and a potential spiral of retaliatory measures from trading partners. In the long term, these factors can contribute to a weaker economy, less innovation, and fewer opportunities for American businesses and workers.

Conclusion

President Trump's comments on the port traffic slowdown reveal a concerning disregard for the complexities of international trade and the economic realities faced by American workers. His celebration of what is essentially an indicator of economic contraction as a victory in a misguided economic war with China is not only misleading but potentially damaging to the very fabric of the American economy. It's crucial that economic policies and pronouncements be grounded in reality and aimed at fostering growth, rather than scoring rhetorical points based on flawed understandings of trade dynamics.
