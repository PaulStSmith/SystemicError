The Bootlicker's Lament — When Anti-Union Pride Becomes Economic Suicide

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Myth of the Exceptional WorkerThe Right-to-Work LieThe Comp Time ConThe Union AdvantageThe Corporate PlaybookThe FutureFinal Thought

This piece was sparked by a Quora post asking why Democrats supposedly voted to "raise taxes on the working class" and "reject tax breaks on Social Security, overtime, and tips." The post quickly devolved into a political food fight, but one particular participant, a user named Ryan Shankey, served as the perfect embodiment of the problem: workers who mistake corporate exploitation for personal empowerment.

There is a special tragedy to watching a man cheer for his own degradation. It’s the labor equivalent of Stockholm Syndrome—workers who’ve suffered under exploitative conditions for so long, they’ve convinced themselves that fighting back is weakness. Ryan proudly declared his disdain for unions, his contempt for collective bargaining, and his belief that he alone could outcompete the protections a union provides. It’s the kind of arrogant delusion that would be comical if it weren’t so depressingly widespread.

Ryan isn’t the problem. He’s the symptom.

Let’s be clear: the real villain here is a decades-long campaign by corporate interests, right-wing think tanks, and bought-off politicians to convince workers that unity is a threat and loyalty to your boss is a virtue. The result? A workforce trained to see their own exploitation as a merit badge.

Ryan sneers that unions “hold companies hostage.” That they “donate to Democrats.” That they “don’t improve quality.” All while boasting that he, as a solo operator, will somehow outmaneuver the collective bargaining power of hundreds or thousands of organized workers.

What he doesn’t seem to realize is that the reason he has a weekend, an eight-hour day, or any shred of workplace safety is because of the very unions he dismisses. The reason his employer even pretends to offer benefits is to preempt unionization. His freedom is a facade; his power is a joke. And the saddest part? He’s proud of it.

The Myth of the Exceptional Worker

At the core of Ryan’s mindset is a fantasy: that he is different. He is smarter. He works harder. That somehow, he will rise above the system grinding his peers into paste. It’s the same myth that fuels lottery tickets and multilevel marketing: the delusion that exceptionalism will save you from structural rot.

But the real world doesn’t work like that. When the boss decides to slash wages by 10%, as one commenter noted in the thread, everyone suffers—except the union workers. When layoffs come, individual performance won’t save you. And when your employer moves operations overseas to save a few cents per unit, your "go-getter attitude" won’t stop the bulldozer.

The Right-to-Work Lie

Another recurring theme in the thread was the sanctity of so-called "Right-to-Work" states. These laws are sold as pro-freedom, but in practice, they mean you have the right to be fired for any reason—or none at all—and no right to organize without retaliation. In Ryan’s fantasy world, that’s freedom. In reality, it’s economic feudalism.

Right-to-work doesn’t empower workers. It empowers bosses. It’s a Trojan horse built by the Chamber of Commerce and rolled into state legislatures with the full backing of the same billionaires who fund anti-union campaigns.

The Comp Time Con

Much of the original Quora thread centered around the bait-and-switch known as comp time. Workers described being forced to accept time off instead of overtime pay—only to discover they were never allowed to use that time. It was a paper promise. A scam. One man shared how the stress of overwork led to a stroke. Others described giving up family time, personal health, and even their retirement savings just to stay afloat.

Ryan had nothing to say about that. Because in his world, if you're suffering, it's your fault. Not the system's. Not the company's. Yours.

This is what anti-union propaganda has wrought: a generation of workers trained to eat shit and call it steak.

The Union Advantage

Contrast that with the stories of those who were unionized. One worker retired with a full pension and healthcare. Another collected over a year’s worth of back pay for unused comp time thanks to a union contract. Another credited their union with preventing layoffs, protecting benefits, and giving them real leverage in the workplace.

These aren’t anecdotes—they’re the outcomes of decades of organized struggle. And it’s not just about money. It’s about dignity. About not begging for crumbs. About knowing your worth and having the collective power to demand it.

The Corporate Playbook

The corporate strategy is simple:

  1. Divide the workforce.

  2. Convince half that they’re better off alone.

  3. Undermine union efforts with fear, propaganda, and legal hurdles.

  4. Pocket the difference.

Ryan is exactly the kind of worker this system counts on. Loud. Proud. And completely blind to how thoroughly he’s been played.

The Future

As AI and automation creep into more industries, the need for solidarity will only grow. Individual bargaining will become even more laughable in the face of algorithmic management and faceless megacorps. Ryan’s "I’m doing just fine" attitude might last another five years—ten if he’s lucky. But without collective power, he’ll end up right where every exploited worker does: discarded, unprotected, and bitter.

Final Thought

If you’re reading this and you see yourself in Ryan—stop. Look around. Ask yourself: who benefits from my hatred of unions? Who gains when I’m isolated and powerless?

Because it sure as hell isn’t you.

Workers of the world aren’t asking for pity.
They’re demanding power.
And that starts by rejecting the lie that you’re better off alone.
