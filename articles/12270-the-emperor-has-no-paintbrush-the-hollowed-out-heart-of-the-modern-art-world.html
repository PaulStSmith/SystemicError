The Emperor Has No Paintbrush: The Hollowed-Out Heart of the Modern Art World

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There was a time when art held a mirror to the world—not just its form, but its soul. The brush captured the depth of a gaze, the glint of light on silver, the slaughter of war, and the curve of divine figures bathed in symbolism and story. Art was a skill, a calling, and often, a defiance. It chronicled power, myth, revolution, and beauty.

And now? A blue rectangle stacked atop a yellow rectangle sold for $46.5 million.

Let’s dispense with the polite veneer: the modern art world has become a farce. A self-reinforcing echo chamber where talent is optional, meaning is malleable, and price has replaced purpose.

From Purpose to Pretension

Before the camera, art had to be representational. It was the only way to immortalize kings, queens, saints, and sinners. From Rembrandt to Vermeer, from Da Vinci to Caravaggio, technique mattered because it served the weight of what was being portrayed. Even a humble still life of pears could radiate meticulous devotion, discipline, and quiet reverence for reality.

Then came photography. Suddenly, realism was mechanical. So artists turned inward, abstracted, deconstructed. At first, this was vital. Necessary. Modernism gave us movements that shattered boundaries: Cubism, Dadaism, Expressionism. But somewhere between Rothko and Koons, the gears sheared off the axle.

The Cult of Concept

Today, the most elite galleries peddle works that look indistinguishable from the contents of a municipal recycling bin. Or a toddler’s tantrum with finger paint. But don’t you dare say that aloud. The moment you question it, you’re branded a philistine. The failure isn’t with the art—it’s with you.

This is the core doctrine of the conceptual cult: if you don’t understand why a blank canvas titled "Silence" is genius, it’s because your mind is unrefined.

This allows the art world to operate like a high-society Ponzi scheme. Inflated by critics, validated by academia, legitimized by galleries, and exploited by billionaires who don’t give a damn what it looks like—so long as the price tag appreciates.

The Buyer Isn’t Stupid. Just Soulless.

Let’s be clear: the person who paid $46.5 million for two rectangles of paint isn’t an idiot. They're worse. They're part of the system that knows the piece is meaningless, but still treats it like a relic of divine insight because it's a way to shuttle money from one offshore account to another.

This isn't patronage. It's money laundering with better lighting.

The Critics Are Accomplices

Art critics today don’t analyze. They canonize. They don't say, "This is good because..." They say, "This is good because I say so."

They treat alienation as a feature, not a flaw. They praise confusion as if it's depth, and elevate incoherence to sacred mystery. If you can sell a banana taped to a wall for six figures, it’s not brilliance—it’s indictment.

They are the priesthood of a faith that forgot the divine.

The Death of Standards

What happens when art is “whatever you say it is”? Everything becomes art. And when everything is art, nothing is.

The term collapses under its own weight. Like a word repeated until it dissolves into gibberish, "art" has become so broad, so subjective, so boundless that it lost its gravitational pull.

Worse, the elevation of the banal devalues the truly extraordinary.

Meanwhile, Real Artists Starve

There is more talent in a comic book convention artist alley than in an entire wing of MoMA. Digital painters, character designers, street artists, tattooists, animators—they pour hundreds of hours into work that demands anatomy, color theory, composition, and imagination. Yet they’re excluded from the elite circle for being “commercial” or “lowbrow.”

In other words: they have fans. They make things people actually like. And in the poisoned logic of the high art world, that makes them unworthy.

Because the goal isn't to connect. The goal is to exclude.

The Rebellion Has Begun

But all is not lost.

The real art world—the one that speaks to people, that stirs something primal, that inspires and challenges and consoles—is thriving. It’s online. It’s underground. It’s on Instagram and ArtStation and DeviantArt. It’s in murals and sketchpads and indie video games. It's in the hands of people who didn’t ask for permission.

It’s made by those who don’t care if Sotheby’s ever returns their calls.

Conclusion: Burn the Gallery, Build a Studio

The modern art market is not a cathedral. It’s a casino for the bored and bloated. It thrives on mystification, exclusion, and the deliberate rejection of skill.

But art itself—real art—survives because it doesn’t need permission to exist. It doesn’t need a critic to explain it. It just is. And you know it when you see it, because it moves you without a price tag or a pretentious blurb.

The emperor has no paintbrush. But the rest of us do.
