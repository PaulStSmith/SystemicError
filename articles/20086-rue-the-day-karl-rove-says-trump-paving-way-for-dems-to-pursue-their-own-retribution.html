Legendary Republican strategist Karl Rove says America is “already exhausted” of President Donald Trump only 100 days into his second term.

Rove, one of the architects of the $3 trillion Iraq War, observed in a recent Wall Street Journal essay that Trump won last year on a stated goal of lowering prices and improving the economy, but what he’s delivered is trade war, international and domestic market instability, along with a litany of unrequested objectives. Some of these unwanted ideas “concocted on the fly” include the “Department of Government Efficiency and removing fluoride from drinking water.”

Rove criticized the effectiveness of Trump’s over-the-top use of executive orders over traditional legislation because the next president can dismantle each order with an announcement.

“And there’s something shocking about this White House to an old-school politico like me: It doesn’t spend much time drawing attention to the president’s successes. Rather than patiently explaining his actions and why they’re good for Americans, the president and his advisers move from one thing to another, seemingly at random,” said Rove, the former deputy chief of staff for George W. Bush and a protégé of Lee Atwater.

He also noted Trump’s vengeful nature: “There’s way too much retribution. Most of the president’s revenge attempts will end badly for him. … Republicans could rue the day they set a new justification for retaliation from Democrats.”

The proof is in the polling, said Rove. Trump began his term with 50.5 percent approving, 44.3 percent disapproving in the RealClearPolitics polling average a week after he was sworn in, he says, but those same poll numbers went upside down by mid-March and two days ago represented a nearly 10-point margin shift in the wrong direction.

“My hunch is things will get worse before they get better,” Rove warned.

Our Analysis:

The Unvarnished Truth About Trump's Second Term: A Dissection

Karl Rove, a name synonymous with Republican strategic planning and the controversial orchestrator of the $3 trillion Iraq War, has thrown a grenade into the Trumpian echo chamber with his recent op-ed in The Wall Street Journal. Rove's critique, coming from a figure deeply entrenched in Republican politics and strategy, is not just another voice in the political wilderness. It's a piercing alarm bell from within the fortress.

The Promises vs. The Reality

Trump's re-election was seemingly predicated on a platform of economic revitalization: lowering prices and improving the economy. Yet, what the Trump administration has delivered thus far is a far cry from these promises. Instead of focusing on economic growth and stability, Trump's tenure has been characterized by trade wars, market instability, and a series of whimsical and unrequested objectives. Rove highlights the absurdity of these initiatives, such as the creation of a "Department of Government Efficiency" and the removal of fluoride from drinking water, as examples of policy-making "concocted on the fly."

Executive Orders: A Double-Edged Sword

Rove's critique of Trump's over-reliance on executive orders is particularly scathing. This approach to governance, while offering the allure of quick action, is inherently fragile. Each executive order stands as a temporary edifice, easily dismantled by the next administration with little more than a stroke of a pen. This method, according to Rove, underlines a fundamental misunderstanding of the American legislative process and the importance of building lasting policy through Congress.

A Presidency Without Celebration

It's not just the policies themselves that draw Rove's ire, but the manner in which they are presented and promoted to the American public. Trump's administration, as Rove notes, has an unusual penchant for avoiding the spotlight on its successes, instead opting for a chaotic and seemingly random progression from one initiative to another. This lack of focus and clarity, coupled with a failure to effectively communicate the intended benefits of the president's actions, serves only to alienate and confuse the electorate.

The Price of Vengeance

Rove also touches on a more sinister aspect of Trump's presidency: his vengeful nature. The political landscape, always a battleground, has been further toxified by Trump's penchant for retribution. This approach not only undermines the dignity of the office but also sets a dangerous precedent for political discourse and action in the country. The tale of revenge, as Rove ominously predicts, is likely to end badly for Trump, with the Republican Party left to reckon with the fallout.

The Polling Precipice

The proof, as they say, is in the pudding—or in this case, the polling. Rove points to a significant decline in Trump's approval ratings, a nearly 10-point margin shift in the wrong direction. This stark reversal of fortune, from a majority approval at the onset of his term to a prevailing disapproval, underscores the growing dissatisfaction among the American populace.

The Prognosis

Rove's conclusion is bleak: "My hunch is things will get worse before they get better." Coming from a figure of Rove's caliber and background, such a statement should not be taken lightly. It's a dire warning that the current trajectory of Trump's presidency is unsustainable and potentially catastrophic for the Republican Party and the country as a whole.

In essence, Rove's essay is not just a critique but a clarion call for a return to principled, effective governance. The question now is, will this call be heeded, or will it be lost in the cacophony of the current political moment? Only time will tell, but if history is any guide, ignoring such warnings comes at a perilous cost.
