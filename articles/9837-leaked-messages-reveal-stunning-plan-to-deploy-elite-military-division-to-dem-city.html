Leaked messages reveal stunning plan to deploy elite military division to Dem city

Top Trump administration officials were discussing plans to deploy an elite military division to Portland, Oregon, according to leaked text messages reviewed and reported on by the Minnesota Star Tribune late Friday.

“Between you and I, I think [Defense Secretary] Pete [Hegseth] just wants the top cover from the boss if anything goes sideways with the troops there,” wrote Patrick Weaver, Hegseth’s advisor, in a text message on the encrypted messaging app Signal, according to the Minnesota Star Tribune.

“82nd is like our top tier [quick reaction force] for abroad. So it will cause a lot of headlines. Probably why he wants [Trump] to tell him to do it.”

Hegseth has already deployed 200 National Guard troops into Portland at the direction of President Donald Trump. But, according to Weaver’s messages, plans were actively being discussed to deploy the Army’s 82nd Airborne infantry division, an elite military unit that specializes in rapid deployment and parachute assaults.

Weaver’s messages were sent to Deputy Homeland Security Advisor Anthony Salisbury and other high-level officials, with Salisbury receiving said messages and responding – sometimes “profanely” – while in “clear view of others” during an event in Minnesota, leading one individual who was “troubled by seeing sensitive military planning discussed so openly” to contact the Minnesota Star Tribune with images of the text messages, and under the condition of anonymity.

White House spokesperson Abigail Jackson declined to comment on the subject matter of the test messages to the Minnesota Star Tribune, but did chastise the outlet for “shamefully” reporting on the information, and called its reporters “morally bankrupt.”

The 82nd Airborne division has been deployed in both world wars, and most subsequent wars the United States has been involved in. While its deployment in Portland would be unprecedented, Hegseth – according to the Minnesota Star Tribune’s reporting – was at least actively considering it, and awaiting explicit approval from Trump to give the order.

Trump has justified his order to deploy troops to Portland by calling the city “war-ravaged,” with the deployment coming after deployments inChicago, Illinois, Washington, D.C. and Los Angeles,California.

Our Analysis:

The Unholy Marriage of Military Might and Political Theater

In a revelation that should chill the spine of any American wary of the creeping militarization of civil society, the Minnesota Star Tribune has uncovered discussions at the highest levels of the Trump administration about deploying the elite 82nd Airborne Division to Portland, Oregon. This information, sourced from leaked text messages, peels back the curtain on a disturbingly cavalier attitude towards the use of America’s military power within its own borders.

The Plot Thickens with Secrecy and Signals

The characters in this political drama communicate through encrypted messages, a method that screams secrecy and a desire to keep the public in the dark. Patrick Weaver, an advisor to then-Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, is cast in the role of the messenger, while the Deputy Homeland Security Advisor Anthony Salisbury plays the recipient of these clandestine communiques. Their casual banter about deploying a unit renowned for its rapid deployment and parachute assaults into an American city is nothing short of alarming.

The Irony of the “Top Cover”

Weaver’s message that Hegseth seeks “top cover from the boss” if “anything goes sideways with the troops” is particularly revealing. It underscores a fundamental truth: those who were considering this action knew it was fraught with risk and controversy. The term “top cover” itself is a damning indictment, suggesting that political protection was a prerequisite for a decision with profound implications for civil liberties and public safety.

A Profane Disregard for Transparency

The fact that these discussions were happening in the open, with Salisbury responding “profanely” in view of others, underscores a brazen disregard for the sensitivity of military planning. It took an individual, presumably appalled by the recklessness on display, to bring this to light. This whistleblower, acting under the condition of anonymity, becomes the unsung hero of this narrative, exposing a cavalier attitude toward military deployment on U.S. soil.

The White House’s Moral Bankruptcy

In a response that could be ripped from the pages of an Orwellian playbook, White House spokesperson Abigail Jackson not only declined to address the substance of the leaked messages but also attacked the Minnesota Star Tribune for its audacity to report on them. Jackson’s labeling of the reporters as “morally bankrupt” is a classic case of projection, a diversionary tactic to vilify those shining a light on potentially unconstitutional actions.

Unprecedented and Unsettling

The article notes that deploying the 82nd Airborne on American streets would be unprecedented. This is not a word used lightly. The 82nd Airborne Division’s storied history in combat across the globe starkly contrasts with the idea of its soldiers landing in Portland. The mere consideration of such a deployment for crowd control or to quell civil unrest is a testament to a dangerous overreach, blurring the lines between the battlefield and the home front.

Justifying the Unjustifiable

President Trump’s justification, framing Portland as “war-ravaged,” is a gross mischaracterization that serves to demonize protest and dissent. This narrative, used to justify the deployment of National Guard troops and potentially the 82nd Airborne, is a stark reminder of how language can be weaponized to frame civic unrest as a battlefield, thus normalizing the idea of a militarized response to domestic issues.

Conclusion

This episode, as reported by the Minnesota Star Tribune, should serve as a wake-up call. The discussions amongst top Trump administration officials about deploying the 82nd Airborne Division against American citizens represent a dangerous flirtation with totalitarian tactics. It’s a stark reminder of the need for vigilance and accountability at all levels of government, especially when the lines between military action and civil governance begin to blur.
