“Good Moral Character” Expansion: A Nazi Loyalty Test in Disguise

The Trump Administration has rolled out yet another grotesque addition to its long record of authoritarian cruelty: a “new” test for citizenship applicants, expanding the evaluation of “good moral character.” The language is couched in bureaucratic vagueness and PR spin, but strip away the layers and you see it for what it is: the codification of ideological vetting, the imposition of a political loyalty test, and the construction of a two-tier system of belonging. It is, in essence, a Nazi policy—designed to divide the “good” citizens from the “bad,” reward conformity, and criminalize dissent.

Let’s not mince words: this is not about morality, character, or protecting the republic. It’s about power, control, and the weaponization of citizenship itself.

The Long History of “Good Moral Character”

Historically, U.S. immigration law required naturalization applicants to show “good moral character” (GMC) for a set period, usually 3 to 5 years, depending on their residency path. For decades, the definition was narrow and relatively stable: avoid serious crimes (murder, rape, aggravated felonies, genocide), pay your taxes, don’t engage in fraud, and you were fine. Even the so-called “conditional bars”—DUIs, domestic violence, gambling offenses—were tightly defined and allowed for rehabilitation.

The system wasn’t perfect, but it was predictable. It created a legal framework that gave immigrants a clear sense of the expectations. Good moral character was about absence of criminality, not about ideological conformity.

Enter Trump.

The Trumpian Mutation

The USCIS memo, released with the kind of smug sanctimony characteristic of Trump’s bureaucrats, reframes GMC into a sprawling, subjective checklist. Officers are instructed to take a “holistic approach,” weighing not just whether an applicant avoided wrongdoing but whether they actively embody “the standards of average citizens of the community in which the alien resides.”

Read that again. Citizenship is no longer a matter of meeting legal requirements; it’s about matching the vibe of “average” Americans. And who defines “average”? A USCIS officer, guided by political directives from the most authoritarian administration in modern U.S. history.

The policy also adds “positive attributes” to the ledger—sustained community involvement, family caregiving, educational attainment, legal employment history, tax compliance. On paper, this looks benign. In practice, it’s a trap. These “positive” factors aren’t neutral measures; they’re opportunities for political gatekeeping.

Imagine: volunteering at a church is “good moral character.” Volunteering at a mosque could be framed as suspicious. Working in a corporate job is stability. Working as a labor organizer? Subversion. Paying taxes on time is compliance. Criticizing U.S. tax policy at a protest is “anti-American.”

And worst of all, the memo explicitly allows officers to deny applicants for acts that are “technically lawful” but deemed “contrary to the average behavior of citizens.” Translation: protest, speech, or association—even when lawful—can now be a mark of “bad moral character.”

This is ideological policing dressed in the garb of moral assessment.

Citizenship as a Prize for the Loyal

The spokesperson’s framing makes the authoritarian intent explicit: “U.S. citizenship is the gold standard of citizenship—it should only be offered to the world’s best of the best.”

This rhetoric is poison. Citizenship is not a prize handed out to the “best of the best.” Citizenship in a democracy is a legal right and a contract between person and state. It is grounded in law, not vibes. It is not a privilege to be dangled in front of immigrants as a reward for groveling, nor is it a gold medal for patriotic performance.

The language of exclusivity—“best of the best”—is lifted directly from fascist traditions, where belonging to the nation was cast as an honor bestowed only on the ideologically pure. It echoes the Nazi obsession with Volksgemeinschaft—the “people’s community”—in which only those deemed racially and politically fit could belong. Everyone else was excluded, marginalized, or eliminated.

The Slippery Slope to Denaturalization

The memo doesn’t just reshape the pathway to citizenship; it opens the door to stripping citizenship already granted. Trump has already floated denaturalizing Elon Musk and Zohran Mamdani, signaling that this weapon is not limited to hypothetical immigrants but can be wielded against political enemies.

The Justice Department memo urging civil attorneys to “prioritize and maximally pursue denaturalization proceedings” makes it even clearer. This is not a policy about ensuring fairness; it’s a weapon of repression. Naturalized citizens—25 million strong, more than half of America’s foreign-born population—now live under the shadow that their citizenship is conditional, revocable, and subject to ideological scrutiny.

Denaturalization is supposed to be rare, reserved for fraud. Under Trump, it becomes a political bludgeon. Criticize the administration? You might find yourself accused of “bad moral character.” Associate with the wrong cause? Say goodbye to your passport.

This is the very definition of authoritarianism: the power to exile.

Nazi Echoes: Volksgemeinschaft and Loyalty Tests

The parallels to Nazi Germany aren’t rhetorical flourish; they’re direct and chilling. The Nazis also defined belonging not only by law but by “moral” and “cultural” fit, enforced through vague, subjective criteria. Jews, Roma, communists, socialists, dissidents—anyone outside the prescribed “norm”—were deemed “degenerate,” stripped of citizenship, and ultimately persecuted.

Trump’s policy doesn’t need gas chambers to be authoritarian. It follows the same blueprint: define “average” or “good” citizenship narrowly, weaponize state agencies to police conformity, and use bureaucratic discretion to enforce ideological loyalty. The difference is only in degree, not in kind.

The Smokescreen of “Holistic Review”

Supporters may claim this policy is “fairer” because it considers positive attributes, not just negatives. This is a lie. The introduction of vague positives simply broadens the scope of discretion. In immigration, discretion equals power. And under Trump, power equals repression.

A “holistic review” sounds fair—until you realize it allows officers to deny applications for entirely subjective reasons. Someone with no criminal record, stable employment, tax compliance, and long-term residence could still be denied because their activism is deemed “anti-American” or their community involvement “not commensurate” with the supposed average.

In other words, the policy isn’t holistic—it’s elastic. Elasticity means officers can stretch the rules to fit any outcome, particularly the outcomes that align with Trump’s political project: fewer immigrants of color, fewer dissenters, fewer Muslims, fewer critics.

Free Speech Under Siege

The lawsuits already emerging underline the danger. Stanford’s student newspaper is suing over targeted deportation of pro-Palestinian non-citizens. The American Association of University Professors is suing Marco Rubio for the administration’s “ideological deportation policy.” These cases aren’t outliers; they’re the canaries in the coal mine.

By expanding the grounds of “bad moral character” to include lawful acts, Trump has effectively criminalized dissent for immigrants. If you’re not a citizen yet, your First Amendment rights now come with an asterisk. And once naturalized, the looming threat of denaturalization ensures those same rights remain conditional.

This is chilling effect by design.

The Broader Trump Immigration Project

This policy doesn’t exist in isolation. It is part of a larger tapestry:

  • Ending birthright citizenship – rewriting the Constitution to exclude children born on U.S. soil.

  • The Muslim ban 2.0 – targeting dozens of nations for blanket exclusion.

  • Troops at the border – militarizing immigration enforcement.

  • Social media scrutiny – surveilling applicants’ private lives.

  • “Trump Card” citizenship sales – turning belonging into a commodity.

  • Selective refugee policies – welcoming white South Africans while slamming the door on others.

Each thread tightens the net, pulling the U.S. closer to an ethnostate vision in which whiteness and loyalty to Trump define who belongs.

Abolish ICE, Tear Down the Machine

This moment also proves what abolitionists have argued for years: ICE and USCIS are not neutral bureaucracies. They are political machines that can and will be weaponized by authoritarians. You cannot “reform” agencies whose very existence rests on criminalizing migration and policing belonging. You dismantle them.

The expansion of “good moral character” shows exactly why abolition is necessary. Left unchecked, these agencies metastasize, finding new ways to strip people of rights, dignity, and even citizenship itself.

Conclusion: Citizenship as a Battlefield

Trump’s redefinition of “good moral character” is not about character. It’s about control. It transforms citizenship from a legal process into a battlefield for ideological conformity. It punishes dissent, rewards compliance, and builds the infrastructure for authoritarian exclusion.

Call it what it is: a Nazi policy. A policy that weaponizes vagueness, injects politics into law, and treats belonging as a prize to be rationed out to the loyal.

Citizenship is not a medal for obedience. It is not a “gold standard” to be awarded to the “best of the best.” It is a legal and democratic right. And any administration that tries to redefine it into a tool of exclusion is not merely tinkering with policy—it is laying the foundation for fascism.

The Trump Administration’s expansion of “good moral character” must be resisted at every level: in the courts, in the streets, and in the realm of public opinion. Because once belonging is conditional, once citizenship becomes revocable, once dissent becomes disqualifying, democracy itself ceases to exist.

And make no mistake: that is exactly the point.
