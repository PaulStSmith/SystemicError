'Strung along again': Trump hit by scathing editorial after glaring hoodwink

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks with Russian President Vladimir Putin, as they meet to negotiate for an end to the war in Ukraine, at Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson in Anchorage, Alaska, U.S., August 15, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump is being “taken for a ride” by Russian President Vladimir Putin, The Guardian editorial board wrote in a scathing analysis published on Friday.

This comes as U.S. diplomats move toward pushing for a peace plan that pressures Ukraine to give Russia much of what it wants — and amid reporting that White House adviser Steve Witkoff went behind Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s back to coach Russian counterparts on how to persuade Trump of their view.

“Mr Putin has no interest in a ceasefire followed by talks where Ukraine’s rights as a sovereign nation would be defended and reasserted. He seeks the capitulation and reabsorption of Russia’s neighbour into Moscow’s orbit,” wrote the board. “Whether that is achieved through battlefield attrition, or through a Trump-backed deal imposed on Ukraine, is a matter of relative indifference. On Thursday, the Russian president reiterated his demand that Ukraine surrender further territory in its east, adding that the alternative would be to lose it through ‘force of arms’. Once again, he described Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s government as ‘illegitimate’, and questioned the legally binding nature of any future agreement.”

This is nothing new; however, the board noted, there is a unique danger at this moment of Trump trying to help Putin accomplish this objective.

“Mr Trump is being strung along again,” wrote the board. “But the clear and present danger is that a combination of ‘peacemaker-in-chief‘ presidential vanity, a desire to do business with Russia and Mr Zelenskyy’s sudden political vulnerability will tempt him to do Mr Putin’s dirty work for him. After four years of resistance, sacrifice and suffering, Ukraine must not be bullied into a cynical carve-up, which would leave it permanently vulnerable to Russia’s aggression, jeopardise Europe’s future security and inspire authoritarian regimes worldwide.”

Trump has always expressed sympathies toward Putin, who was found by an extensive federal investigation to have interfered on Trump’s behalf in the 2016 election. But in recent months, Trump has grown more conflicted, expressing frustration that Putin is uninterested in peace.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Perilous Dance with Putin: A Closer Look

The Guardian’s editorial board recently laid bare a troubling dynamic at play between former President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, painting a picture of geopolitical naiveté and manipulation that threatens not just Ukraine’s sovereignty, but the broader security architecture of Europe and the principles of democratic self-determination worldwide. This analysis digs deeper into the implications of Trump’s actions and the dangerous precedent they set.

Trump’s Vulnerability to Putin’s Machinations

The assertion that Trump is being “taken for a ride” by Putin is not just a casual observation; it’s a profound indictment of Trump’s foreign policy acumen—or lack thereof. Putin, a seasoned manipulator on the world stage, appears to be playing 4D chess, while Trump is stuck playing checkers. The editorial suggests that Trump’s ego and his desire to be seen as a peacemaker are being exploited, pushing him toward decisions that would ultimately serve Russia’s interests over those of Ukraine, the United States, and global democracy.

The Alarming Scenario of a Trump-backed Deal

The notion of U.S. diplomats pushing for a peace plan that essentially hands Russia “much of what it wants” is not just alarming; it’s a strategic blunder of historic proportions. It signals a willingness to capitulate to an authoritarian regime’s demands, undermining the very essence of sovereign integrity and encouraging aggressive territorial ambitions. The involvement of White House adviser Steve Witkoff, allegedly going behind Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s back, adds a layer of intrigue and subterfuge that reads like a plot from a Cold War thriller—only the stakes are all too real.

The Illusion of Peace and the Reality of Capitulation

Putin’s clear disinterest in a ceasefire that does not result in Ukraine’s capitulation is a stark reminder of his long-term objectives. Describing the Ukrainian government as “illegitimate” and demanding further territorial concessions under the threat of force underscores the Kremlin’s disdain for international law and the principles of national sovereignty. Trump’s potential role in facilitating this outcome would not only be a betrayal of Ukraine but a blow to global stability.

The Wider Implications of Trump’s Actions

The editorial rightly points out the danger of Trump attempting to assist Putin in achieving his objectives. Beyond the immediate threat to Ukraine, such actions would jeopardize Europe’s future security, embolden authoritarian regimes worldwide, and signal a retreat from the principles of democratic governance and international solidarity. After years of resistance and sacrifice, Ukraine’s fight against Russian aggression is emblematic of the broader struggle between authoritarianism and democracy.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

Trump’s expressions of sympathy towards Putin, coupled with his apparent willingness to entertain Russian demands, should alarm anyone concerned with the principles of democracy and international order. The Guardian’s editorial is a clarion call for vigilance and resistance against efforts to undermine Ukraine’s sovereignty and, by extension, the security and democratic values of the broader international community.

In conclusion, the dynamics between Trump and Putin, as outlined by The Guardian, are more than just a troubling subplot in international relations; they are a reminder of the constant vigilance required to protect democratic values and sovereign nations from the machinations of authoritarian leaders.
