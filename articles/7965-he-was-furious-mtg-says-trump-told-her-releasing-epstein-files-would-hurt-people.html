'He was furious': MTG says Trump told her releasing Epstein files would 'hurt people'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 7, 20254 min read

Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) is now offering new details of conversations she had with President Donald Trump pertaining to deceased child predator Jeffrey Epstein.

CBS’ 60 Minutes teased its interview of Greene in a post to X on Friday. In the clip, reporter Lesley Stahl is seen asking the Georgia Republican about behind-the-scenes conversations she had with the president about her push to compel the Department of Justice (DOJ) to release all remaining evidence on Epstein from his two federal criminal investigations.

“We did talk about the Epstein files, and he was extremely angry at me that I had signed the discharge petition to release the files,” Greene said. “I fully believe that those women deserve everything they’re asking, they’re asking for all of it to come out. And they deserve it. And he was furious with me.”

“What did he say?’” Stahl asked.

“He said that it was going to hurt people,” Greene replied.

Greene was a persistent thorn in Trump’s side, constantly needling him about the Epstein files during his unsuccessful attempt to defeat the bipartisan discharge petition to release the documents. Trump ultimately relented and indicated he would sign the legislation if it reached his desk. Both chambers of Congress passed it by near-unanimous margins.

The Georgia lawmaker announced last month she would not seek another term in Congress, after Trump posted a tirade against her on his Truth Social platform calling her a “ranting lunatic.” According to Fox News, Greene may be nursing a grudge against Trump over his refusal to back her hypothetical camaign for next year’s U.S. Senate race in Georgia.

Greene’s full interview with 60 Minutes will air Sunday on CBS. It will be her first sit-down interview since she announced her retirement from the House of Representatives.

Our Analysis:

The Tangled Webs of Politics, Power, and the Epstein Files: A Closer Look

In a recent revelation that reads like a script from a political thriller, Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.) has brought to light her conversations with former President Donald Trump concerning the infamous Jeffrey Epstein. The details, teased in an upcoming “60 Minutes” interview, paint a picture of internal conflict, power struggles, and a quest for truth that could easily be dismissed as fiction—if it weren’t so deeply rooted in reality.

The Unlikely Crusader

Greene, often a polarizing figure in American politics, emerges in this narrative as an unexpected champion for transparency and justice. Her push to compel the Department of Justice (DOJ) to release all remaining evidence from Epstein’s federal criminal investigations is nothing short of bold. It’s a stark reminder that political figures cannot be neatly categorized into heroes and villains, as Greene defies the expectations many might have of her.

Trump’s Fury Unleashed

The revelation that Trump was “extremely angry” and “furious” with Greene’s actions adds a layer of complexity to the former president’s persona. His alleged reasons, that the release of the Epstein files “was going to hurt people,” invite speculation. Who exactly was he worried about? Friends? Allies? Himself? Trump’s opposition to transparency, as described by Greene, casts a long shadow over his administration’s commitment to justice and accountability.

The Political Fallout

Greene’s announcement that she will not seek another term in Congress, following a tirade from Trump on his Truth Social platform, signals a dramatic conclusion to her controversial career. Yet, this decision also raises questions about the cost of political dissent within one’s party, especially when it challenges powerful figures. Trump’s refusal to back Greene’s hypothetical campaign for the U.S. Senate race in Georgia may have been the final straw, but it also underscores the fragility of political alliances.

The Bigger Picture

This episode is more than just a feud between two high-profile Republicans. It’s a window into the murky depths of political power, where the quest for truth often collides with personal ambitions and loyalties. The Epstein files, emblematic of systemic corruption and the exploitation of power, remain a litmus test for politicians’ commitment to justice.

As Greene’s “60 Minutes” interview approaches, one can’t help but wonder what further revelations await. Will this lead to a renewed push for transparency, or will it simply fade into the backdrop of the 24-hour news cycle? The answers to these questions will not only shape the legacies of those involved but also signal the direction in which our political system is headed.

In the end, the saga of Greene, Trump, and the Epstein files is a reminder that in politics, as in life, the truth is often stranger—and more complicated—than fiction. It challenges us to look beyond the surface, question our assumptions, and demand accountability, no matter how uncomfortable it may be.
