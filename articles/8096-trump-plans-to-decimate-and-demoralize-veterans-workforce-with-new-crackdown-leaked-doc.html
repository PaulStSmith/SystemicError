Trump plans to 'decimate and demoralize' veterans workforce with new crackdown: leaked doc

An internal memo delivered to the Veterans Affairs team has ordered them to make an internal database documenting non-US citizens.

The leaked document, seen by The Guardian, has prompted outrage from government representatives who believe the order was made to “decimate and demoralize” VA workers. Democratic congresswoman Delia Ramirez, who is part of the oversight and investigations subcommittee on the House veterans affairs committee, said there could be “far-reaching” implications for the Trump administration’s VA changes.

The Department of Veterans Affairs is in the process of creating a “new internal database” to log and monitor non-US citizens “employed or affiliated” with the government department. A VA spokesperson did not rule out the possibility of this internal information being fed to other government departments, including Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE).

A spokesperson said, “VA will share any adverse findings with the appropriate agencies to ensure anyone who is not authorized to be in the US is dealt with accordingly.”

The memo seen by The Guardian suggests the system will be in place as early as the end of this year. It reads, “By December 30, 2025, the office of operations, security, and preparedness must provide the secretary of veterans affairs a report of all non-United States citizens who are employed by or affiliated with VA.”

Pete Kasperowicz, a VA spokesperson said in a statement: “VA is required by federal law to continuously vet all employees and affiliates, such as unpaid researchers and others who may have access to VA data or systems, to ensure they meet the federal government’s trusted workforce standards.”

Democratic congresswoman Delia Ramirez said, “List-making by the state is an authoritarian tactic meant to stoke fear. At the direction of Secretary Collins, the VA is persecuting noncitizen employees who provide essential services and benefits to our veterans.”

“The reported memo could have far-reaching implications. Attacking immigrants authorized to work is just another way [US president] Trump and the [VA] Secretary seek to deconstruct, decimate, and demoralize the VA workforce.”

Nayna Gupta, policy director at the American Immigration Council went on to say the internal data collection is an “intimidation” tactic used by the government.

She claimed, “Once information is collected on who is a non-citizen, and the exact status and posture of their protections and rights to be in the United States, it becomes incredibly easy for the federal government to make an effort to get that information.”

“This data collection and reporting is a form of intimidation, in a context where a list of names of non-citizens can so obviously get into the hands of an agency pursuing this agenda.”

Our Analysis:

The VA’s Controversial New Database: A Deep Dive

In an age where data is power, the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) seems to be stepping into murky waters with its latest maneuver. A leaked internal memo, as reported by The Guardian, reveals plans to create a database of non-US citizens employed or affiliated with the VA. This move has sparked a storm of criticism and concern, with accusations of intimidation and authoritarian tactics being hurled at the Trump administration. Let’s dissect this contentious development with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism it deserves.

The Database: An Overview

The document outlines an order to compile a report by December 30, 2025, listing all non-United States citizens working with the VA. At first glance, this might seem like a mundane bureaucratic exercise, but the implications are far from benign. The VA spokesperson’s admission that this information could be shared with agencies like Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) sends a chilling message. It’s not just a list; it’s a potential tool for targeted actions against noncitizen employees.

The Official Justification

Pete Kasperowicz, a VA spokesperson, wraps this directive in the cloak of legal compliance, stating, “VA is required by federal law to continuously vet all employees and affiliates,” to meet the government’s trusted workforce standards. Yet, this explanation raises more questions than it answers. Is this an existing requirement now being enforced with renewed vigor, or is it a pretext for a more insidious agenda?

The Critics’ Perspective

Democratic congresswoman Delia Ramirez and Nayna Gupta from the American Immigration Council have lambasted the initiative. Ramirez sees it as a “list-making by the state,” an authoritarian tactic designed to instill fear among noncitizen employees. Gupta goes further, labeling it a form of intimidation. Their criticisms underscore the potential for this database to be a weapon, rather than a mere administrative tool.

Between the Lines

The timing and context of this memo are critical. In the shadow of an administration known for its hardline stance on immigration, the creation of such a database cannot be viewed in isolation. It’s a piece in a larger puzzle of policies aimed at reshaping the landscape of immigration and employment of noncitizens in the United States.

The notion that this database is merely about ensuring compliance with federal law is, at best, a half-truth. At worst, it’s a smokescreen for a campaign to “decimate and demoralize” a section of the VA’s workforce, as Ramirez puts it. The potential sharing of information with ICE hints at a future where employment at the VA comes with the risk of deportation for noncitizens.

The Broader Implications

This move by the VA is not just about the potential persecution of noncitizen employees. It’s a reflection of a broader agenda that seeks to redefine who gets to participate in the American workforce and under what conditions. It sends a message that noncitizen employees, regardless of their contributions, are under scrutiny and possibly at risk.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

The VA’s plans for a database of non-US citizens is more than a policy shift; it’s a bellwether for the direction in which certain elements within the government wish to steer the country. This initiative should not be seen in isolation but as part of a broader narrative concerning immigration and employment in the United States.

As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, it’s our responsibility to question, critique, and demand transparency and fairness in how policies are crafted and implemented. The VA’s database, as it stands, is a step backwards, not forwards, in achieving a society that values all of its members, regardless of their nationality.
