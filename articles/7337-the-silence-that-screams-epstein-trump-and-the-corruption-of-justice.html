The Silence That Screams: Epstein, Trump, and the Corruption of Justice

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 24, 20254 min read

What does innocence look like when the lights are finally turned on?

That is the question Lawrence O’Donnell posed on The Last Word, and it is the right place to begin. The partial release of the Epstein files has not merely reopened an old scandal. It has exposed a pattern of behavior so consistent, so revealing, that pretending not to see it now requires willful blindness.

This is not about rumor. It is not about guilt by association. It is about reactions — and, more tellingly, the absence of them.

The Innocent Person Standard

O’Donnell framed the issue with prosecutorial clarity. If your name appears in the Epstein files and you are innocent — genuinely innocent — there is exactly one rational response: demand transparency. Demand that everything be released. Leave no ambiguity, no shadows, no room for speculation.

Bill Clinton’s office did exactly that. It issued a clear statement calling for the immediate release of any materials that refer to, mention, or contain photographs of him. No delay. No conditions. No evasive language.

That response established a baseline. A control case.

Once that baseline exists, every deviation from it becomes meaningful.

Silence Is Not Neutral

Now contrast that response with Donald Trump’s.

Trump has never demanded the full release of materials referring to him. Not once. Instead, he has worked to prevent disclosure, minimized his relationship with Jeffrey Epstein, and repeatedly misrepresented the extent of his contact.

The newly released files show that Trump flew on Epstein’s plane in the 1990s more often than he ever admitted. At least four of those flights included Ghislaine Maxwell — later convicted of sex trafficking minors — among the passengers.

After Maxwell’s conviction, Trump did not express outrage or sympathy for the victims. He offered a remark that was as revealing as it was grotesque: “I wish her well.”

Silence here is not passive. It is strategic.

The Ohio Billionaire Problem

Then there is Les Wexner, Epstein’s most important business partner and financial patron. A man vastly wealthier than Epstein, whose relationship with him has never been convincingly explained.

Wexner’s name appears in a 2019 Justice Department email titled “Co-Conspirators.” His lawyer rushed to assure the press that Wexner was not a target, not a co‑conspirator, and had cooperated fully with investigators.

If that is true, the obvious question follows: why not demand full disclosure?

Why not release a statement, as Clinton did, calling for the immediate publication of all materials referring to or depicting Les Wexner?

Silence, once again, does the talking.

Subpoenas, Victims, and an Unfinished Map

The released documents describe ten co‑conspirators. Some were located and served with grand jury subpoenas in Florida, Boston, New York City, and Connecticut. Others were listed as “out of pocket.” Investigators were preparing to interview approximately twenty‑five victims.

This was never a closed case. It was a stalled one.

For years, the public was told there were no co‑conspirators, no further investigations to pursue, nothing left to uncover. That claim did not originate with the victims. It came from people with a vested interest in ending scrutiny.

A Justice Department in Capture

The most damning argument O’Donnell makes is not about Trump’s proximity to Epstein. It is about Trump’s control over the institutions meant to investigate crimes like Epstein’s.

Under Trump, the Department of Justice behaved less like an independent enforcer of federal law and more like a personal defense firm. The attorney general, the deputy attorney general — who also served as Trump’s personal criminal defense lawyer — and the FBI director all opposed releasing the Epstein files.

They insisted the case was closed. They claimed there were no co‑conspirators. They argued that disclosure served no purpose.

That position collapsed the moment Congress, pushed by Epstein’s survivors, forced the release of the files over their objections.

A Justice Department that fights transparency in a sex‑trafficking case forfeits its credibility entirely.

Why “It Would’ve Been Weaponized” Is a Lie

Trump’s legal defenders have argued that any damaging information about him would have been “weaponized” long ago if it were real.

That claim is demonstrably false.

The Epstein files include an unsubstantiated allegation by Epstein’s brother suggesting Trump was responsible for Epstein’s death in prison. There is no evidence for that claim — and crucially, it existed during the Biden administration.

No one released it. No one weaponized it. Because restraint still existed.

The argument only works if one assumes every administration is as corrupt as Trump’s. That assumption is projection, not analysis.

Survivors Forced the Truth

The Epstein files were not released because powerful men wanted them released. They were released because survivors refused to be silent.

Congress did not act out of courage. It acted because moral clarity was imposed on it by people who endured abuse and lived to demand accountability.

When Trump attacked lawmakers who supported the release, calling them traitors, one former ally responded with brutal honesty: only evil people hide this and protect those who participated.

She was right.

The Clock Is Ticking

This is not the end of the Epstein story. It is the beginning of long‑delayed accountability.

If you are innocent, transparency is your ally.

If you obstruct, delay, or remain silent, you are telling the public exactly what you fear.

The truth is no longer buried. It is scheduled.

And the rendezvous with it is inevitable.
