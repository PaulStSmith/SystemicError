Revealed: Prominent left-wing gadfly Noam Chomsky 'in regular contact' with Epstein

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Noam Chomsky in November 2020, Wikimedia Commons

The Guardian reports that prominent linguist and philosopher Noam Chomsky had deeper ties to convicted sex-trafficker Jeffrey Epstein than the occasional political and academic discussion.

Emails disclosed in November by Republican members of the U.S. House Oversight Committee detail the correspondence Epstein had with “political, academic and business luminaries, including the Bill Clinton White House’s treasury secretary Larry Summers and Steve Bannon, the longtime ally of Donald Trump,” the Guardian reports. They also reveal Epstein and Chomsky were close enough to discuss musical interests and even potential vacations.

“Perhaps the most telling of the Chomsky-related document in question was a letter of support for Epstein attributed to Chomsky with the salutation ‘to whom it may concern’. It is not dated, but it contains a typed signature with Chomsky’s name and citing his position as a University of Arizona laureate professor,” the Guardian said. This was a role Chomsky began in 2017, nearly ten years after Epstein pleaded guilty in 2008 to state charges of solicitation of prostitution with a minor.

“I met Jeffrey Epstein half a dozen years ago. We have been in regular contact since, with many long and often in-depth discussions about a very wide range of topics, including our own specialties and professional work, but a host of others where we have shared interests. It has been a most valuable experience for me,” the letter states.

In the letter, Chomsky marvels at Epstein’s exhaustive list of personal connections with powerful national and international men.

“Once, when we were discussing the Oslo agreements, Jeffrey picked up the phone and called the Norwegian diplomat who supervised them, leading to a lively interchange,” wrote Chomsky, recounting how Epstein had arranged for him to meet with former Israeli prime minister Ehud Barak.

Epstein had also introduce Chomsky “to the world of jazz and its wonders,” the letter continued.

It concluded, “The impact of Jeffrey’s limitless curiosity, extensive knowledge, penetrating insights and thoughtful appraisals is only heightened by his easy informality, without a trace of pretentiousness. He quickly became a highly valued friend and regular source of intellectual exchange and stimulation.”

Another notable communication in the stack of House Oversight releases involves a 2015 email wherein Epstein offered Chomsky use of his residences in New York and New Mexico.

The Guardian reports Chomsky is not the only renowned Massachusetts academic to be ensnared in the Epstein scandal, with Larry Summers surrendering a teaching role at Harvard University – where he was once president – after his email correspondence with Epstein revived questions about their relationship.

Our Analysis:

The Chomsky-Epstein Connection: A Deeper Dive Into Disturbing Disclosures

The recent revelations unearthed by The Guardian about Noam Chomsky’s interactions with convicted sex-trafficker Jeffrey Epstein have stirred more than just the academic pot; they’ve spilled scalding truths onto the public’s lap. This isn’t merely about casual exchanges over coffee; this is a demonstration of how the intellectual elite can sometimes find themselves entangled in the web of the morally bankrupt, under the guise of intellectual and cultural exchange.

Chomsky, a figure long revered for his critiques of power structures and his anarchist leanings, seems to have found himself in a rather uncomfortable spotlight. The disclosed emails, courtesy of Republican members of the U.S. House Oversight Committee, paint a picture of a relationship that went beyond the occasional academic tête-à-tête. Chomsky’s correspondence with Epstein included discussions on musical interests and even potential vacations, a level of familiarity that doesn’t square easily with the image of the aloof intellectual.

The most damning piece of evidence is a letter of support for Epstein, allegedly from Chomsky, which sings praises of Epstein’s “limitless curiosity, extensive knowledge, penetrating insights and thoughtful appraisals.” This letter, lacking a date but bearing a significant weight of implications, was drafted years after Epstein’s 2008 guilty plea to charges of solicitation of prostitution with a minor. One must ask: how does an intellectual giant reconcile Epstein’s criminal past with the effusive commendations in this letter?

Chomsky’s marvel at Epstein’s “exhaustive list of personal connections” and the recounting of Epstein’s ability to directly engage with international figures like Ehud Barak and a Norwegian diplomat overseeing the Oslo agreements, further complicates the narrative. It suggests a level of admiration for Epstein’s connections that is deeply unsettling, considering the context of Epstein’s later crimes.

Furthermore, Epstein’s offer of his residences to Chomsky in 2015 for his use adds another layer of complexity to their relationship. It’s a gesture that goes beyond mere acquaintance, hinting at a deeper bond or, at the very least, a significant level of trust or indebtedness.

The article also notes that Chomsky is not the only academic caught in the Epstein scandal’s wake. Larry Summers, former president of Harvard University, found himself relinquishing a teaching role after his own communications with Epstein came to light. This pattern of entanglement among the academic elite with Epstein is disturbing, raising questions about the judgment and moral compass of these individuals.

In light of these revelations, one must ponder the ethical obligations of public intellectuals. The disclosure of Chomsky’s relationship with Epstein not only tarnishes his reputation but also serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of intellectual elitism becoming bedfellows with moral ambiguity. The intellectual community must reflect on the implications of their associations, recognizing that their public and private engagements have the power to either reinforce or undermine their credibility and the values they purport to uphold.

In conclusion, the Chomsky-Epstein connection is a stark reminder of the complex interplay between power, intellect, and morality. As the public digests these revelations, the discourse around intellectual responsibility, accountability, and the ethics of association is sure to intensify. The intellectual elite must navigate these waters with care, lest they find themselves drowning in the wake of their questionable choices.
