'Promises not kept': Wall Street insider tears apart Trump's grim economy

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Economic Damage Is Not a Mystery. It Is the Point.

The Power Was Never Missing

Donald Trump holds the office, the megaphone, and the policy machinery. That is the only fact that matters when prices rise, debt climbs, and households get squeezed. The source article leans on a Wall Street insider, Steve Rattner, to catalog the damage: slowing growth, a swelling deficit, stalled manufacturing employment, tariffs generating refunds, inflation outpacing paychecks, gas above $4, mortgage rates stuck near 7 percent, Medicaid cuts, and health insurance up 26 percent.

That is not bad luck. That is governance.

Blame Is the Oldest Scam

Trump says he inherited a broken economy from Joe Biden. That line is doing exactly what it is designed to do: move responsibility away from the person currently steering the state and onto the last administration. The source text itself undercuts the excuse. It notes that Biden’s presidency included record-low unemployment, even if inflation crushed his approval ratings.

Trump did not win by offering a serious economic program. He won by weaponizing frustration over prices and then promising relief “on Day 1.” The result, according to the source, is the opposite.

Promises as Cover

“Promises made, promises kept” is a branding exercise, not an economic record. Rattner’s point is blunt: many of Trump’s promises are causing the harm critics predicted, and the promises he has failed to keep are the ones that would have mattered to ordinary people.

The pattern is familiar. Trump talks like a man cleaning up a mess, while his administration produces the mess. Tariffs are not abstract symbolism when they trigger refunds. Medicaid cuts are not line items when they grow every year. Health insurance spikes are not statistical noise when they land on family budgets. This is what economic propaganda looks like when it meets the balance sheet.

The Costs Fall Downward

Trump’s defenders like to talk about “the economy” as if it were a shared national mood. It is not. It is a distribution of pain. When inflation rises above 3 percent and the Federal Reserve may respond by raising rates, the people who pay are the ones carrying credit card debt, trying to buy a home, or already trapped by expensive borrowing.

That is the political function of Trump’s economics: shift costs downward, then speak as if he is restoring strength. A president can brag about “the greatest economy we’ve ever had, by far” while forcing households to absorb higher prices, higher borrowing costs, and weaker public support. The boast is not evidence. It is camouflage.

The Democratic Failure Inside the Story

There is also a harder lesson here for Democrats. The source notes that frustration over inflation helped sink Biden, and that many voters blamed Kamala Harris for it. That is not proof that the electorate was rational. It is proof that Democrats failed to translate real gains, like low unemployment, into durable political trust while inflation did its damage.

That does not make Trump’s case legitimate. It makes Democratic strategy incomplete. If a party cannot defend material gains against price panic, then it leaves the door open for demagogues who promise relief and deliver a more expensive version of the same cruelty.

The Pattern Beneath the Numbers

This is bigger than one bad presidency. It is the standard authoritarian economic move: claim inherited disaster, promise easy fixes, govern for spectacle, and dump the bill on everyone else. Trump’s second term, as described here, is not just underperforming. It is exposing how political lies survive when institutional power is concentrated in the hands of a man who treats public trust as a disposable prop.

The lesson is not that the economy is too complicated for accountability. The lesson is that accountability is exactly what Trump’s politics is built to evade.
