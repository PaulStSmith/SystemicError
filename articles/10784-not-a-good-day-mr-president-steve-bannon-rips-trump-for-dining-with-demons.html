'Not a good day Mr. President': Steve Bannon rips Trump for dining with 'demons'

Steve Bannon ripped into his former boss, President Donald Trump, on Saturday in an episode of his far-right “War Room” podcast, attacking the president – or at least “the people around” him – over Thursday’s White House dinner with tech CEOs, whom Bannon derided as “demons.”

Trump hosted at the White House a number of high-profile business leaders in the technology sector, among them Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, Apple CEO Tim Cook, and Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates.

Bannon, who served as Trump’s first White House chief of staff, has frequently criticized Washington’s friendliness with corporations, going so far as to call for increased taxes on wealthier Americans and corporations in what could be considered a rhetorical break from most conservative lawmakers and influencers alike.

And, regarding Trump’s recent dinner with tech CEOs, Bannon said it was a major mistake.

“That was not a good day, Mr. President,” Bannon said. “We're trying to break up Google. We're trying to break up Facebook. We're trying to break up Microsoft, and hell, throw Walmart and all of them in there. The concentration of corporate power with government power is what is destroying this country.”

Bannon also went on to juxtapose the optics of Trump dining with wealthy tech CEOs with the testimony this week of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy before the Senate Finance Committee. Despite facing scrutiny during the hearing from both Democratic and Republican lawmakers alike, Bannon characterized RFK Jr.’s performance as “magnificent,” and compared it unfavorably to Trump’s cozying up to tech CEOs.

“Look at Bobby Kennedy Thursday; a magnificent performance as he stood in the breach and took incoming for three hours and called these folks out,” Bannon said. “And what happened afterwards? The people in the White House around President Trump thought it was a good thing to have 50 demons around the table around President Trump, including Zuckerberg, Bill Gates and Google.”

Our Analysis:

Bannon’s Blistering Critique: Trump’s Dinner with Tech Titans

In what could only be described as a fiery denouncement, Steve Bannon has unleashed a scathing critique of his one-time boss, President Donald Trump, over a recent high-profile engagement with the moguls of Silicon Valley. Bannon's platform of choice? His far-right “War Room” podcast, where he spared no vitriol in addressing what he perceives as a betrayal of the very principles that propelled Trump to the presidency.

The Gathering of “Demons”

Bannon's language, ever so colorful and loaded, referred to the tech CEOs—among them, Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, Apple CEO Tim Cook, and Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates—as “demons.” This is not merely a casual choice of words but a calculated effort to demonize the very essence of corporate America, which he argues, in cahoots with government power, is leading to the country's downfall.

The event that sparked Bannon's ire was a dinner hosted by Trump at the White House, a seemingly innocuous gathering designed to foster dialogue between the government and the technology sector. However, Bannon sees it as nothing short of sacrilege. His argument? That by aligning himself with these corporate titans, Trump is essentially fraternizing with the enemy.

A Rhetorical Break from Conservative Orthodoxy

What makes Bannon’s critique particularly noteworthy is its deviation from traditional conservative rhetoric. Calling for increased taxes on wealthier Americans and corporations places Bannon in an unusual position, one that aligns more closely with progressive economic policies than with the tax-cut fervor that typically characterizes conservative policy advocacy.

The Kennedy Contrast

Bannon juxtaposes Trump’s dinner with the recent testimony of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy before the Senate Finance Committee. Kennedy’s performance, which Bannon lauds as “magnificent,” stands in stark contrast to Trump’s cozying up to the tech elite. This comparison is designed to highlight what Bannon perceives as a divergence from the populist ethos that ostensibly defines Trump’s appeal.

The Crux of Bannon’s Argument

At the heart of Bannon's discontent is the notion that the amalgamation of corporate and government power is the root of America’s decline. He is not merely critiquing a dinner; he is underscoring a systemic issue that he believes is eroding the fabric of American democracy.

“The concentration of corporate power with government power is what is destroying this country.”

This statement encapsulates Bannon’s thesis and serves as a rallying cry for those who share his disillusionment with the current state of affairs.

Conclusion

Bannon’s attack is more than just a former advisor lashing out at his erstwhile boss; it is a manifestation of a deep-seated belief in the dangers of corporate-government collusion. By framing his criticism around this specific event, Bannon is attempting to awaken a broader awareness of what he perceives as the existential threats facing the nation.

Whether one agrees with Bannon’s perspective or not, it’s clear that his commentary adds a layer of complexity to the ongoing discourse surrounding the intersection of politics, corporate power, and governance. It’s a stark reminder that the battles being fought are not just in the realm of policy and governance but in the very ideals that underpin American democracy.
