Trump gives his own White House ballroom architect the boot after clashes: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump’s Architectural Ambitions: A Deep Dive into Chaos and Caprice

The demolition of the East Wing of the White House during construction of U.S. President Donald Trump’s proposed ballroom is seen from the reopened Washington Monument, following the longest shutdown of the government in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 15, 2025. REUTERS/ Jessica Koscielniak

President Donald Trump fired his own ballroom architect after clashing with an architect over expanding the project.

The boutique architecture firm led by James McCrery II was reportedly replaced after three months when questions surfaced over whether the group could manage the large-scale plan, The Washington Post reported Thursday.

“It is unclear whether McCrery stepped back voluntarily, but the men parted on good terms and remain so, according to one of the people familiar with the project, all of whom spoke on the condition of anonymity to discuss internal conversations,” The Post reported.

Trump apparently wanted to increase the size and scope of the project, “but it was McCrery’s firm’s small workforce and inability to hit deadlines that became the decisive factor in him leaving,” a source told the outlet.

The president has decided that architect Shalom Baranes will take over the job. Baranes’ design firm has designed and renovated government buildings in Washington, D.C. for decades and has taken on projects that include the main Treasury building near the White House and the General Services Administration headquarters.

The $300 million ballroom is one of the largest renovations expected at the White House and follows the demolition of the East Wing, the historic corridor and location of the former first lady’s office. Trump has also signaled plans to renovate the National Mall.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Architectural Ambitions: A Deep Dive into Chaos and Caprice

In a move that encapsulates the tumultuous and often unpredictable nature of his administration, President Donald Trump has dismissed his ballroom architect, a decision that starkly illustrates the chaotic decision-making process that seems to permeate his tenure. The boutique architecture firm, led by James McCrery II, finds itself sidelined after a mere three months, ostensibly over concerns regarding the firm’s capacity to handle the project’s expanded scope. However, as reported by The Washington Post, the narrative is muddied by whispers of missed deadlines and a workforce too scant to meet the ambitious demands of a president known for his penchant for grandiosity.

The departure of McCrery’s firm from this $300 million endeavor is emblematic of the broader inefficiencies and ego-driven decisions that have characterized the Trump administration. The decision to replace McCrery with Shalom Baranes, a firm with a storied history of renovating significant government buildings in Washington, D.C., underscores a fundamental misunderstanding or perhaps disregard for the intricate balance between vision and feasibility.

Shifting Goals, Shifting Grounds

Trump’s desire to increase the “size and scope” of the ballroom project is indicative of his broader approach to governance: an insistence on upscaling without a clear appreciation for the practicalities involved. This penchant for the grandiose, while dismissing the meticulous planning and expertise required, is a microcosm of his administration’s approach to policy and governance. The report that McCrery’s “small workforce and inability to hit deadlines” was a decisive factor in his ousting speaks volumes about the expectations placed on professionals in Trump’s orbit: be limitless, be infallible, or be gone.

The irony here is palpable. Trump, whose career in real estate ostensibly prepares him for such undertakings, seems to falter when faced with the realities of managing large-scale projects within the public sector. The replacement of McCrery with Baranes is not just a change of hands; it’s a stark admission that even Trump’s ventures are subject to the same constraints and challenges that affect all such projects, regardless of the commander in chief’s desires.

The Cost of Ambition

The $300 million price tag attached to the ballroom renovation is staggering, not just in financial terms but also in what it represents about presidential priorities. At a time when the nation grapples with myriad crises—ranging from healthcare to economic instability—the focus on such an extravagant project may strike many as a misallocation of focus and resources. Moreover, the mention of plans to renovate the National Mall signals a broader agenda to reshape American landmarks in Trump’s image, a venture that, while potentially laudable for its vision, raises questions about legacy, intent, and the prioritization of national interests over personal legacy building.

Conclusion: A Legacy of Contradictions

In dismissing James McCrery II over what appears to be a mismatch between vision and capability, Trump has inadvertently shone a light on a recurring theme of his presidency: the clash between ambition and reality. The firing is more than a contractual disagreement; it’s a manifestation of the systemic errors that plague an administration marked by impulsivity and a relentless pursuit of grandeur, often at the expense of practicality and prudence.

As the project moves forward under the guidance of Shalom Baranes, one can only hope that the lessons of this episode are not lost. The saga of the ballroom renovation, with its twists and turns, serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked ambition and the importance of grounding vision in reality. In the grand architecture of American governance, the foundations matter as much as the facade.
