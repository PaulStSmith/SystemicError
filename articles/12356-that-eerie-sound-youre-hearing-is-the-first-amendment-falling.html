That eerie sound you’re hearing is the First Amendment falling

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Paramount-Skydance Merger: A Frontal Assault on Democracy

Paramount Global and Skydance logos are seen in an illustration. REUTERS/Dado Ruvic

One of the largest mergers in media history was approved Thursday by the FCC — an $8 billion marriage between Paramount and Skydance Media.

This was epic not as a business story but as a broadside against democracy. The agency established in 1934 as an independent honest broker was deployed as a weapon of domestic war by President Donald Trump.

Like any major merger, this one had twists and turns and complexities. This one had more than its share, as The New York Times reported:

“In recent weeks, Paramount has been engulfed in turmoil stemming from the company’s strained relationship with the Trump administration. The company paid $16 million this month to settle a lawsuit brought by President Trump. Critics — including CBS’s ‘Late Night’ host, Stephen Colbert — said the settlement was effectively a payoff to secure approval from the Trump administration, claims the company flatly rejected.”

In the end, however, the drama was dwarfed by an unprecedented, naked assault on a national media establishment that Trump has long slandered as “the enemy of the people.” And in true authoritarian form, the Leader’s will was executed by a shameless lackey — FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, who tweeted:

“President Trump took on the legacy national media. He smashed the facade that they — and their Hollywood and New York execs — get to control the narrative. President Trump is now stacking up the wins with more to come.”

Such servile sycophancy from an FCC chairman is certainly without precedent in the independent agency’s 91-year history. But so is having the chair’s role filled by a bootlicker who co-authored Project 2025’s section on the FCC.

In Trump II, it’s barely a speed bump on the road to dictatorship.

The FCC’s 2-1 decision was put in its place by the lone dissenter and Democrat on the panel, Commissioner Anna Gomez:

“After months of cowardly capitulation to this Administration, Paramount finally got what it wanted. Unfortunately, it is the American public who will ultimately pay the price for its actions.

In an unprecedented move, this once-independent FCC used its vast power to pressure Paramount to broker a private legal settlement and further erode press freedom. Once again, this agency is undermining legitimate efforts to combat discrimination and expand opportunity by overstepping its authority and intervening in employment matters reserved for other government entities with proper jurisdiction on these issues.

Even more alarming, it is now imposing never-before-seen controls over newsroom decisions and editorial judgment, in direct violation of the First Amendment and the law.”

That eerie sound you’re hearing is the crumbling of the First Amendment. As I wrote here, the CBS capitulation to autocracy will go down as one of the most cowardly and damaging surrenders in American media history.

Tempting as it might be to blame it all on Trump, the ultimate culprit in the story is a media giant willing to sell its soul to an extortionist.

And as Commandant Carr put it so bluntly, there’s more to come.

Our Analysis:

The Paramount-Skydance Merger: A Frontal Assault on Democracy

In what can only be described as a chilling testament to the erosion of democratic norms, the recent approval of the $8 billion merger between Paramount and Skydance Media by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) marks not just a monumental moment in media history, but a stark broadside against the very principles of democracy. This decision epitomizes the weaponization of regulatory bodies under the Trump administration, a trend that is as alarming as it is blatant.

The Unholy Alliance Between Paramount and the Trump Administration

At the heart of this saga lies a sordid tale of political machinations, where Paramount's tumultuous relationship with the Trump administration culminated in a $16 million settlement — a move critics have rightfully lambasted as a brazen payoff to curry favor and secure merger approval. Despite the company’s attempts to deny these allegations, the sequence of events reads like a playbook from an authoritarian regime, not a democratic republic.

Brendan Carr: A Case Study in Sycophancy

The role of Brendan Carr, the FCC Chairman, in this debacle cannot be overstated. Carr's unabashed sycophancy, evidenced by his public adulation of Trump's assault on the national media, is not just unbecoming of his position but signals a dangerous precedent. His declaration of Trump's victory over the "legacy national media" is not just a betrayal of the FCC's mandate to act as an independent regulator; it's a blatant celebration of the erosion of press freedom.

The Lone Voice of Reason: Commissioner Anna Gomez

Commissioner Anna Gomez emerges as the solitary figure of integrity in this narrative, her dissent highlighting the dire implications of the FCC's actions on the American public and the principles of press freedom. Gomez's condemnation of the FCC's overreach and its encroachment on First Amendment rights underscores the gravity of the situation. This isn't merely about regulatory malfeasance; it's about the deliberate undermining of democratic institutions and norms.

The Crumbling of the First Amendment

What we are witnessing is nothing short of the crumbling of the First Amendment, a cornerstone of American democracy. The capitulation of CBS to what can only be termed as autocratic demands is not just cowardly; it is a dangerous precedent that threatens the very fabric of press freedom in the United States. This episode is a stark reminder of the vulnerability of media giants in the face of political extortion and the lengths to which they will go to appease authoritarian impulses.

The Real Culprit: A Media Giant’s Faustian Bargain

While it might be tempting to lay the blame solely at the feet of the Trump administration, Paramount's willingness to sell its soul in this extortionate exchange is equally culpable. This merger is not just a business transaction; it is a testament to the insidious influence of political power over media independence. The chilling effect on editorial judgment and newsroom decisions cannot be overstated, setting a perilous precedent for the future of media freedom.

In Conclusion: A Dark Chapter in American Media

The approval of the Paramount-Skydance merger is a dark chapter in the annals of American media, a chapter that will be remembered for its stark illustration of the fragility of democratic institutions in the face of authoritarian impulses. As Commissioner Gomez rightly points out, it is the American public that will ultimately pay the price for Paramount’s actions — a price that extends far beyond the realms of media and into the very heart of democracy itself.

And as the ominous words of Commandant Carr remind us, "there’s more to come." This is not the end but a foreboding glimpse into a future where media independence is sacrificed at the altar of autocratic ambition. It is a future we must resist with every fiber of our democratic being.
