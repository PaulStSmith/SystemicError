'Shocking': Kristi Noem's DHS caught in 'brazen lie' as old photo used to promote strikes

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem speaks during a roundtable on antifa, an anti-fascist movement President Donald Trump designated a domestic “terrorist organization” via executive order on Sept. 22, at the White House in Washington, D.C. on Oct. 8, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and the Department of Homeland Security are accused of lying with an old boat photo that has “zero ties to the current situation” and using the image to promote attacks on alleged drug boats after Colombia’s president questioned the strikes.

In a post on the DHS News account posted Monday on X, the agency attempted “to discredit Colombian President Gustavo Petro, who had accused Trump of murdering an innocent fisherman in an attack on a boat the U.S. claimed was run by drug smugglers,” The Daily Beast reports Tuesday.

Petro is one of the first world leaders to challenge the Trump administration’s attacks and call for a criminal investigation.

The since-deleted post read:

“Colombian President claims one of the Narco boats destroyed by the US Naval Task Force was ‘just a poor Colombian fisherman’. Does this look like a fishing boat? It looks like he had tons of bait (cocaine, attracts lots of fish). Colombian president is a liar!!” the post said.

Social media users called out Noem’s DHS, saying the image of the boat with apparent bags of drugs came from a 2024 operation reported by the Spanish newspaper El País. That investigation happened about 1,000 kilometers south of the Canary Islands.

“This photo is from a drug bust that occurred in 2024, zero ties to the current situation,” according to a community note that was added as a fact-check for users, The Beast reports. “Photo is taken from the video attached in the article below.”

The administration has come under fire for sharing multiple images and videos that are misleading or false, as suspicions over the attacks and the legality grow. At least six known U.S.-led attacks have been launched in the Caribbean Sea, resulting in an estimated 29 people dead.

“They lie so brazenly that it is still shocking to me,” said Krystal Ball, political commentator and podcaster.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Misleading Maneuvers of Homeland Security and the Misuse of Media

In a recent revelation that reads like a page ripped straight from the playbook of political subterfuge, Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) have been caught in a web of deceit, using an outdated photograph to fan the flames of controversy surrounding alleged drug boats. This act of misinformation not only undermines the credibility of the DHS but also highlights the dangerous game of manipulating public perception for political ends.

The Misleading Post: A Deep Dive

The DHS, through its News account on X (formerly Twitter), attempted an audacious move to discredit Colombian President Gustavo Petro. President Petro, stepping out of the international sideline, dared to challenge the narrative spun by the Trump administration concerning an attack on a boat, purportedly operated by drug smugglers, which resulted in the tragic death of an innocent fisherman. The DHS’s response? A post that reeked of sarcasm and misinformation, suggesting that the boat, laden with “tons of bait (cocaine),” was anything but innocent.

However, the DHS’s narrative began to crumble when eagle-eyed social media users pointed out the glaring error: the photograph used to vilify the Colombian fisherman was from a 2024 operation reported by El País, located approximately 1,000 kilometers south of the Canary Islands—bear no relevance to the current controversy.

The Pattern of Deception

This incident is not an isolated one. The Trump administration and the DHS have come under scrutiny for sharing multiple images and videos that are either misleading or outright false. This pattern of behavior raises alarming questions about the extent to which government agencies are willing to manipulate facts and visuals to justify their actions, particularly those actions leading to fatal outcomes, as seen in the Caribbean Sea where at least 29 people have reportedly lost their lives in U.S.-led attacks.

A Dangerous Precedent

The use of disinformation by the DHS sets a perilous precedent. It erodes public trust in government institutions, a cornerstone of democratic governance. When those charged with the safety of the homeland resort to tactics reminiscent of authoritarian regimes, one must ask: who benefits from these narratives?

The answer, unfortunately, is not the public, nor the victims of these fatal encounters at sea, but rather the political actors who manipulate these narratives to serve their own agendas. The flippant nature of the DHS’s attempt to discredit President Petro—calling him a liar without substantiation and using irrelevant evidence—speaks volumes about the current state of political discourse propagated by those in power.

The Role of Fact-Checking and Accountability

The quick response by social media users and the subsequent fact-check highlight the critical role of vigilance in an era of misinformation. The community’s ability to fact-check and call out false narratives is a beacon of hope amidst the dark tactics of misinformation. However, this incident also underscores the need for greater accountability and transparency within government agencies. It is incumbent upon these institutions to uphold the highest standards of truth and integrity, ensuring that their actions and statements do not undermine the very fabric of democratic society.

Conclusion: A Call for Integrity

The story of the DHS’s misleading post is not just a tale of political intrigue; it’s a stark reminder of the ongoing battle for truth in the age of misinformation. As we navigate these troubled waters, let us demand more from our leaders and governmental institutions. Let us not be swayed by the currents of deceit but rather anchor ourselves in the pursuit of integrity and accountability. For in the end, it is these values that will guide us to safer shores.
