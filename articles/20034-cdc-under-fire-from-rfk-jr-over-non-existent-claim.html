CDC under fire from RFK Jr. over non-existent claim

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., already under scrutiny for attempting to fire the director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, is now attacking the CDC for information on its website that he wrongly claims characterizes “abortion” as one of the 10 greatest advances in medical science — while also appearing to suggest vaccines and fluoridation should not be on that list.

There are several versions of the list, one titled, “Ten Great Public Health Achievements — United States, 1900-1999,” and dated April 1999 — more than a quarter century ago.

It includes: “Vaccination, Motor-vehicle safety, Safer workplaces, Control of infectious diseases, Decline in deaths from coronary heart disease and stroke, Safer and healthier foods, Healthier mothers and babies, Family planning, Fluoridation of drinking water, and Recognition of tobacco use as a health hazard.”

Abortion does not appear on any of the lists NCRM was able to locate.

One portion mentions contraception, but not abortion.

Kennedy, a promoter of conspiracy theories — especially about vaccines — on Thursday refused to discuss his attempt to fire the CDC Director. He told Fox News, “It would be inappropriate for me to comment on a personnel issue.”

“What I will say is, you know, there is, President Trump has very, very ambitious hopes for CDC right now. CDC has problems. We saw the misinformation coming out of COVID.”

“They got the testing wrong, they got the social distancing, the masks, the school closures that did so much harm to the American people,” he charged.

“Today on CDC’s website right now, they list the 10 top advanced greatest advances in medical science, and one of them is abortion,” Kennedy inaccurately claimed. “The other is fluoridation, another is vaccine.”

READ MORE: ‘Frogs in a Boiling Pot’: Trump Blasted After Again Insisting ‘I’m Not a Dictator’

STAT News reported on Thursday that “Kennedy’s brief critique of the CDC ranged from incontrovertible missteps by the agency, such as its failure to handle the onslaught of Covid testing at the beginning of the pandemic, to issues such as masking, where there is still scientific debate, to issues such as fluoridation, which is broadly viewed by the medical community as beneficial. He also appeared to leave key context out of his description of the list of great medical achievements on the CDC’s website.”

Kennedy also told Fox News viewers that the CDC’s “priorities” need to be examined, and denounced what he alleged was a “malaise” at the nation’s top health agency.

“This agency, the gold standard science [sic] and do what it was when we were growing up, which was the most respected health agency in the world.”

In 2024, The New York Times characterized Kennedy as a “leading vaccine skeptic and purveyor of conspiracy theories,” and someone who has “promoted a conspiracy theory that coronavirus vaccines were developed to control people via microchips,” and “endorsed the false notion that antidepressants are linked to school shootings.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Misfire from HHS Secretary Kennedy

In a recent spectacle of misinformation, Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has made headlines once again, but for all the wrong reasons. Kennedy, a figure already shrouded in controversy due to his attempts to remove the director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), has now launched an erroneous attack on the CDC concerning its list of the 10 greatest advances in medical science—a list that apparently exists only in Kennedy's misinterpretation.

The Misrepresentation

Kennedy's claim, as reported, is that the CDC's website lists "abortion" as one of these top medical advances. However, this assertion doesn't just stretch the truth—it outright snaps it. The list in question, dating back to April 1999 and titled, “Ten Great Public Health Achievements — United States, 1900-1999,” makes no mention of abortion. The advancements celebrated include vaccination, motor-vehicle safety, and the control of infectious diseases, among others. It's noteworthy that Kennedy's critique also took aim at vaccines and fluoridation, two extensively studied and universally recognized public health triumphs.

Ignoring the Facts

Kennedy's refusal to engage in a meaningful discussion about his attempt to fire the CDC director, deflecting with a vague nod to "President Trump's very, very ambitious hopes for CDC," showcases a troubling evasion of accountability. His attack on the CDC, accusing it of disseminating misinformation during the COVID-19 pandemic, selectively highlights errors while ignoring the broader context of an unprecedented global health crisis. It's a classic move—acknowledge the missteps that fit your narrative, ignore the complexities of rapid-response science.

A Pattern of Conspiracy

This isn't the first time Kennedy has been embroiled in controversy over his views on public health. Described by The New York Times as a “leading vaccine skeptic and purveyor of conspiracy theories,” Kennedy has a history of endorsing fringe and debunked theories, including the thoroughly discredited idea that vaccines contain microchips for controlling the population. His stance on antidepressants and school shootings further cements his reputation as an advocate for baseless and harmful misinformation.

The Real Priority

Kennedy's latest tirade against the CDC, particularly his mischaracterization of its list of medical advancements, is more than just a factual misstep; it's a dangerous diversion from the genuine priorities needed to safeguard public health. His focus on non-issues and conspiracy theories detracts from critical discussions on how to enhance the CDC's operations and improve public health strategies.

Conclusion: Misguided Leadership at a Critical Time

At a time when public trust in health institutions is paramount, Kennedy's actions and statements represent a significant systemic failure. Misinformation, especially from high-ranking officials, undermines the efforts of health professionals and scientists working tirelessly to protect the public. Leadership in health and human services demands a commitment to evidence-based policy and an unwavering respect for scientific facts. Kennedy's current trajectory is a disservice to these principles, necessitating a sharp critique and call for accountability in the face of such systemic error.
