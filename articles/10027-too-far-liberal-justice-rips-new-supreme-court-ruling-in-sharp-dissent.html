'Too far': Liberal justice rips new Supreme Court ruling in sharp dissent

A liberal Supreme Court justice tore into her conservative colleagues’ latest decision on Friday afternoon after the high court delivered a significant win to President Donald Trump, allowing his administration to continue freezing nearly $4 billion in foreign aid funding that Congress had allocated.

Justice Elena Kagan issued a scathing dissent in the Supreme Court case Department of State v. Aids Vaccine Advocacy Coalition, noting the emergency application raised “novel issues fundamental to the relationship between the President and Congress.”

She said the high court was working in “uncharted territory” and that the “stakes are high: At issue is the allocation of power between the Executive and Congress over the expenditure of public monies.”

She lamented that they had less than three weeks to consider the application — “a short fuse” — and that they did so “with scant briefing, no oral argument, and no opportunity to deliberate in conference.”

“Because of how this case came to us, we likewise do not have the benefit of a pertinent court of appeals decision, much less a set of decisions expressing different views. In a few weeks’ time—when we turn to our regular docket—we will decide cases of far less import with far more process and reflection,” she wrote.

Kagan credited her colleagues for emphasizing it was merely a “preliminary view” on the issues rather than a “final determination on the merits.”

“But even at that, the majority goes too far,” she said. “The Executive has not met our standard for emergency relief—the appropriately high bar we have erected because a stay like this one disrupts ‘the ordinary processes of administration and judicial review.’”

She added the Executive “has not ‘made a strong showing that [it] is likely to succeed on the merits’... Nor has it shown that, in the absence of relief, it will suffer irreparable harm. We therefore should have denied this application, allowed the lower courts to go forward, and ensured that the weighty question presented here receives the consideration it deserves.”

She warned that the conservative majority’s ruling will have a lasting impact.

“The effect is to prevent the funds from reaching their intended recipients—not just now but (because of their impending expiration) for all time,” she wrote.

Kagan also took a shot at Trump’s argument about irreparable harm. The administration said that to comply with a lower court’s order to spend the $4 billion, it would have to negotiate with other countries or international groups. The administration complained it would have to “advocate against its own objectives,” “undermin[ing]” its view that the expenditures are “contrary to U. S. foreign policy.”

Kagan was unsympathetic to the administration’s plight, calling it “just the price of living under a Constitution that gives Congress the power to make spending decisions through the enactment of appropriations laws.”

“If those laws require obligation of the money, and if Congress has not by rescission or other action relieved the Executive of that duty, then the Executive must comply. It cannot be heard to complain, as it does here, that the laws clash with the President’s differing view of ‘American values’ and ‘American interests.’ ... That inconsistency, in other words, is not a cognizable harm, to be weighed in the equitable balance. It is merely a frustration any President must bear.”

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court’s Controversial Decision: A Critical Analysis

In a striking display of judicial overreach, the Supreme Court has handed President Donald Trump a contentious victory, allowing his administration to keep nearly $4 billion in foreign aid funding—explicitly allocated by Congress—frozen. This decision not only underscores the deep ideological divide within the court but also raises alarming questions about the balance of power between the executive and legislative branches of the United States government.

Justice Kagan’s Scathing Dissent

Justice Elena Kagan, in her formidable dissent, lays bare the troubling implications of the court’s decision in the case of Department of State v. Aids Vaccine Advocacy Coalition. Kagan’s critique is not just a disagreement on legal grounds; it’s a dire warning about the erosion of democratic principles and the usurpation of Congressional authority by the executive branch, facilitated by the Supreme Court itself.

The Rushed Process

Kagan points out the hastiness with which the court approached a matter of such gravity—deciding on an issue with far-reaching consequences in less than three weeks, with minimal briefing and no oral arguments. This “short fuse” approach, as she aptly describes it, is not only unprecedented but also inexcusable, particularly when the stakes involve the very allocation of power between the President and Congress over the expenditure of public money.

The Majority’s Preliminary View: A Dangerous Precedent

While the majority attempted to downplay their decision as merely a “preliminary view,” Kagan astutely argues that even this stance goes too far. The executive branch, she notes, failed to meet the high bar for emergency relief, a standard set precisely to prevent the disruption of ordinary administrative and judicial processes. The executive’s inability to demonstrate likely success on the merits or to prove irreparable harm in the absence of relief should have been sufficient grounds to deny the application. Yet, the conservative majority chose to overlook these critical deficiencies, setting a dangerous precedent for future cases.

The Lasting Impact

Kagan’s warning about the lasting impact of the majority’s ruling cannot be overstated. By siding with the executive, the Supreme Court has effectively prevented the allocated funds from reaching their intended recipients, not just temporarily but potentially forever, due to their impending expiration. This decision, in essence, nullifies Congress’s power of the purse—a cornerstone of its legislative authority.

The Executive’s “Irreparable Harm”

Perhaps the most striking aspect of Kagan’s dissent is her rebuttal to the administration’s claim of “irreparable harm.” The notion that complying with a lower court’s order would force the administration to negotiate against its own objectives is, in Kagan’s view, no harm at all. It’s simply the cost of adhering to the Constitution, which clearly grants Congress the authority to make spending decisions. The administration’s frustration with laws that clash with the President’s view of “American values” and “American interests” is, as Kagan eloquently puts it, “merely a frustration any President must bear.”

Conclusion: A Call for Judicial Restraint

Justice Kagan’s dissent is not just a critique of a flawed decision; it’s a clarion call for judicial restraint and respect for the separation of powers enshrined in the Constitution. The Supreme Court’s ruling in favor of the Trump administration is a stark reminder of the court’s shift under its conservative majority—a shift that threatens to undermine the foundational principles of American democracy. As Kagan implies, the court’s role is not to rubber-stamp executive overreach but to safeguard the delicate balance of power that defines our republic. The decision in Department of State v. Aids Vaccine Advocacy Coalition is a misstep that we, as a nation, cannot afford to repeat.
