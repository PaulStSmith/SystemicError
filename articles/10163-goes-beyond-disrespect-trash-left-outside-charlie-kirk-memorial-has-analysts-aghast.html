'Goes beyond disrespect': Trash left outside Charlie Kirk memorial has analysts aghast

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 24, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A pair of left-wing podcasters were aghast on Monday at videos depicting the amount of trash left outside of slain conservative activist Charlie Kirk’s memorial.

Kirk’s memorial was held in Glendale, Arizona on September 21. The event reportedly drew about 90,000 attendees and included speeches from President Donald Trump, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, Kirk’s widow Erika, and several MAGA personalities.

After the event, Arizona news outlet CBS 5 reported on the amount of trash left near the stadium. Videos also appeared on social media showing trash littering multiple areas around the stadium.

Podcast hosts Sam Seder and Emma Vageland of “The Majority Report” responded to the videos in a new episode.

“I would have expected better,” Seder said. “I mean, you’re going to a memorial for gosh sakes. I think Charlie Kirk was from Arizona. It goes beyond disrespect. It’s like disdain.”

“Steven Miller says, ‘We’re the ones who build,’” Vageland said, referring to Miller’s speech during Kirk’s memorial. “Looks like it’s mountains of trash.”

Our Analysis:

The Disrespectful Aftermath of Charlie Kirk’s Memorial: A Reflection of Deeper Issues

In an event that was supposed to honor the memory of conservative activist Charlie Kirk, the aftermath painted a starkly different picture—one of disregard and neglect. The memorial, held in Glendale, Arizona, attracted a massive crowd of around 90,000 attendees, including high-profile figures such as President Donald Trump, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard. Yet, the respect and solemnity one would expect at a memorial were starkly contradicted by the scene left outside the stadium: mountains of trash littering the area, a physical manifestation of disrespect that goes beyond mere oversight.

A Missed Opportunity for Respect and Reflection

The presence of such notable figures, including the widow of Charlie Kirk, Erika, alongside several MAGA personalities, positions this event within the heart of conservative America. It was not just a memorial but a significant gathering for the conservative community to reflect, mourn, and honor one of their own. However, the aftermath, as reported by Arizona news outlet CBS 5 and highlighted in social media videos, reveals a jarring disconnect between the values purportedly celebrated inside the stadium and the reality outside.

Left-Wing Podcasters Shed Light on the Irony

The response from left-wing podcasters Sam Seder and Emma Vageland of “The Majority Report” cuts through the facade with incisive clarity. Their astonishment and critique underscore a fundamental expectation of decency and respect at memorials, which seems to have been grossly overlooked by attendees. Seder’s disappointment, terming the act as “beyond disrespect” and akin to “disdain,” particularly when considering Kirk’s Arizona roots, points to a deeper problem of communal responsibility and respect for public spaces.

Vageland’s reference to Steven Miller’s speech during the memorial, where he states, “We’re the ones who build,” juxtaposed with the reality of “mountains of trash,” serves as a potent metaphor for the hypocrisy often observed in political rhetoric versus action. This visual of neglect starkly contrasts with the conservative ethos of personal responsibility and respect for community values.

The Bigger Picture: A Reflection of Societal Values

This incident transcends mere environmental negligence; it is emblematic of a broader issue of societal and political dissonance. When the attendees of a memorial, presumably supporters and admirers of the deceased, leave behind a trail of trash, it prompts a critical examination of the values being espoused versus those being practiced. It’s a stark reminder that actions speak louder than words, and in this case, the actions observed speak to a disturbing lack of communal and environmental respect.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Responsibility

The disrespect shown at Charlie Kirk’s memorial through the sheer amount of trash left behind should serve as a wakeup call. It’s a moment for self-reflection not just for those who attended but for the wider community to consider the implications of their actions on the environment and on the values they purport to hold dear. This incident should not just be seen as an isolated act of negligence but as a reflection of deeper societal issues that need addressing. In the end, the legacy of a memorial should be one of respect and honor, not disdain and neglect.
