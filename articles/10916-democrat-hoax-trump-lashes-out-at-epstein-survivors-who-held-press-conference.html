'Democrat hoax!' Trump lashes out at Epstein survivors who held press conference

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts as he and the President of Poland Karol Nawrocki (not pictured) meet in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., September 3, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump suggested that a press conference being held by survivors of sex offender Jeffrey Epstein was a "Democrat hoax."

As the survivors were speaking outside the Capitol on Wednesday, a reporter asked Trump if his Justice Department was "protecting any friends or donors."

"So this is a Democrat hoax that never ends," the president complained. "You know, it reminds me a little of the Kennedy situation. We gave him everything over and over again, more and more and more, and nobody's ever satisfied."

"But it's really a Democrat hoax because they're trying to get people to talk about something that's totally irrelevant to the success that we've had as a nation since I've been president," he continued. "So what they're trying to do with the Epstein hoax is, get people to talk about that instead of speaking about the tremendous success, like ending seven wars."

"I ended seven wars. Nobody's going to talk about because they're going to talk about the Epstein, whatever."

Trump argued that his government had "given thousands of pages of files" about Epstein to Congress.

"And I think it's, I think, really, I think it's enough because I think we should talk about the greatness of our country and the success that we're having," he insisted. "And that's what I want to talk about. That's what we should be talking about. Not the Epstein hoax."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump's "Democrat Hoax" Strategy

In a recent spectacle that can only be likened to a circus act, President Donald Trump has once again waved his magic wand of deflection, this time pointing it at the survivors of sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, labeling their press conference a "Democrat hoax." As these survivors stood outside the Capitol, daring to voice the injustices they endured, Trump dismissed their pain and the gravity of their allegations with a flick of his rhetorical wrist, instead attempting to redirect the narrative toward what he perceives as the "tremendous success" of his presidency.

The Dismissal as a Hoax

Trump's assertion that the press conference was a "Democrat hoax" is a brazen attempt to undermine the credibility of the survivors and minimize the severity of Epstein's crimes. It's important to note the pattern here: whenever Trump is faced with criticism or allegations that paint his administration in a negative light, he often cries "hoax." This tactic is not just a deflection but a deliberate effort to muddle the truth and sow doubt among his base.

The Kennedy Comparison

Trump's comparison of the Epstein survivors' plight to the "Kennedy situation" is both perplexing and misplaced. The vague reference seems to be an attempt to draw parallels between his perceived victimization by endless investigations and scrutiny, and... what, exactly? The ambiguity of his statement only serves to distract from the issue at hand: the survivors' quest for justice and accountability.

"Irrelevant to Our Nation's Success"

Trump's claim that the Epstein case is "totally irrelevant to the success we've had as a nation" under his presidency is not just callous—it's a gross misrepresentation of the presidency's responsibilities. The implication that the pursuit of justice for sex crime survivors could ever be considered a distraction from national success is appalling. It suggests a profound misunderstanding (or perhaps, a willful ignorance) of the role of the president in ensuring justice and safeguarding the rights and well-being of all citizens.

The War on Wars

"I ended seven wars," Trump proclaims, without specifying which wars he's referring to. This statement, much like his administration, lacks the clarity and evidence to be deemed credible. It's a bold claim, one that seemingly aims to overshadow the Epstein "hoax" with a grandiose declaration of peace-making. Yet, without context or proof, it remains another empty assertion meant to divert attention from the pressing issue of sexual abuse and the powerful individuals who perpetuate it.

The Greatness of Our Country

The president insists that the focus should be on the "greatness of our country" and its successes, rather than on what he dismisses as the Epstein hoax. This is perhaps the most telling part of his strategy: the implication that American greatness cannot coexist with the acknowledgment of its failures and the pursuit of justice for its victims. Trump's attempt to shift the conversation away from Epstein and toward his own perceived achievements is a clear demonstration of his priorities and his penchant for elevating his own narrative above all else.

Conclusion

In Trump's world, the seriousness of sex trafficking and the plight of its survivors can be brushed aside as mere distractions from his narrative of success. His remarks are not just an affront to the survivors of Epstein's crimes but to all those who seek justice in the face of power and privilege. By labeling the survivors' press conference a "Democrat hoax," Trump not only dismisses their pain but also further entrenches the dangerous precedent that truth and accountability are malleable concepts, subject to the whims of those in power. Let us be clear: the only "hoax" here is the illusion of leadership that Trump attempts to project.
