Key Trump policy becomes 'political kryptonite' for South Texas Republicans

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 17, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Texas Republicans and the Immigration Quagmire: A Tale of Strategy Gone Awry

Image via Public Domain / Picryl.

Texas Republicans spent years building relationships within the Latino community, in hopes they could pull some of the conservatives over to their side. They were successful, too, until President Donald Trump stepped in.

The aggressive immigration crackdown has now become “political kryptonite” for South Texas Republicans, who touted the idea of tough border security before it turned into mass deportations, violence and shootings, Politico reported Sunday.

Rep. Monica De La Cruz (R‑Texas), who flipped a 120‑year Democratic stronghold in 2022, watched as her district Trump won by 18 points in 2024, has shifted. No one is calling for mass deportations anymore. While Trump pledged to go after the “worst of the worst,” his Department of Homeland Security is nabbing children, mothers, American citizens, people with valid visas and those with work permits.

Rep. Tony Gonzales (R-Texas), whose district runs along the Mexico border, wants the GOP to focus on what he calls “convicted criminal illegal aliens.”

But there simply aren’t enough immigrant murderers, rapists and other criminals to meet the 3,000-person daily mandate set by Trump’s White House deputy chief of staff, Stephen Miller.

Many Republican lawmakers are trying to distance themselves from the Trump administration after two shooting deaths of American protesters sent approval polls into a deeper slump.

“President Trump made a promise, and he’s kept that promise by securing the border. That was stage one,” De La Cruz told Politico. “Now we’re at stage two, which is having a conversation of true immigration reform.”

Mike Madrid, a Republican strategist, told Politico that the GOP had a brilliant way to address the situation, then threw it all away.

“With the border secure and Latinos responding to ICE raids and government overreach, the districts that Republicans thought were their future a year ago are likely to be their undoing. Hard to find another situation in the past 50 years where a political party has squandered a generational opportunity like this,” he said.

Daniel Guerrero, CEO of the South Texas Builders Association, told Politico that the federal raids and attacks on random construction sites is causing huge problems for the industry.

“The sentiment is pretty clear across the table, that nobody really expected this magnitude of enforcement,” said Guerrero. He confessed that it is what he voted for in 2024 when he cast a ballot for Trump.

“There’s limited resources, period. And we want those limited resources to be focused on the worst of the worst, the criminal immigrants that have come in,” De La Cruz said. “We have legal immigrants in our district who have work visas that they don’t want to go out to work because some may have fear about the process that is currently being administered.”

But Guerrero isn’t giving De La Cruz any props.

“People feel abandoned because you never showed face, and now that there’s an actual crisis, you want to show face?” he said. “It’s like, dude, it’s a little too late, man.”

Our Analysis:

Texas Republicans and the Immigration Quagmire: A Tale of Strategy Gone Awry

The Republican Party’s foray into the Latino community of Texas is a vivid portrayal of political ambition that has hit an iceberg, with the Titanic in this metaphor being none other than President Donald Trump’s aggressive immigration policies. Here’s a breakdown of how this strategy has unfolded, or rather, unraveled, under the glaring spotlight of reality.

The Initial Strategy: A Sweet Dance with the Latino Community

For years, Texas Republicans had been wooing the Latino community, a demographic rich with conservative values that resonate well with the GOP’s traditional stances on family and economy. The charm offensive was paying dividends, with significant inroads being made. This wasn’t just a flirtation; it was a full-blown courtship.

Enter Trump: The Bull in the China Shop

However, the romance was short-lived. President Trump, with his hallmark bravado, transformed the GOP’s nuanced approach to border security into an unbridled crusade against immigration. This wasn’t just tough talk on security; it morphed into a draconian policy of mass deportations, indiscriminate sweeps catching not just the “worst of the worst,” but children, mothers, and legal residents in its net. The political cost of this shift was astronomical, turning what was once a Republican asset into “political kryptonite.”

The Fallout: A 180-Degree Shift

The article highlights the dramatic shift in political fortunes for Republicans in South Texas, a region where the hardline immigration stance has backfired spectacularly. Rep. Monica De La Cruz, who managed to flip a Democratic stronghold after 120 years, has seen the tide turn against her. The aggressive immigration crackdown has alienated the very constituency the GOP had spent years cultivating.

The Consequences: From Bad to Worse

The backlash has been swift and severe. The GOP’s dream of cementing its hold on South Texas is crumbling, with Latino voters recoiling from the party’s embrace of Trump’s harsh immigration policies. Republican lawmakers, caught between the rock of Trump’s base and the hard place of electoral reality, are scrambling to recalibrate their stance, but for many, it’s too little, too late.

The Verdict: A Missed Opportunity

Republican strategist Mike Madrid’s lament that the party “squandered a generational opportunity” couldn’t be more apt. The GOP had a golden chance to build a lasting coalition that could have solidified its dominance in Texas and beyond. Instead, it chose to double down on a strategy of division and exclusion, alienating a key demographic group in the process.

The Industry’s Plea: Focus on the “Worst of the Worst”

The sentiment from the business community, particularly from figures like Daniel Guerrero, CEO of the South Texas Builders Association, underscores the real-world impact of these policies. The indiscriminate crackdown has sown fear and confusion, disrupting industries that rely on immigrant labor. The call for a more nuanced approach, targeting genuine criminal elements rather than casting a wide net, is a plea for sanity in an increasingly insane policy landscape.

Conclusion: A Political Maelstrom

The article paints a bleak picture of a party at odds with its own future. The GOP’s embrace of Trump’s immigration policies has not only alienated the Latino community in Texas but has also exposed deep rifts within the party itself. As the 2024 elections loom, the Republicans’ handling of this issue will be a litmus test for their political viability in a state that is increasingly in play. The question remains: Can the GOP mend the bridge it has so recklessly burned, or will it continue to be engulfed by the flames of its own making?
