Furious Trump continues rage about polls as he vows: 'We will be making a fortune'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing Trump's Latest Truth Social Tirade: Fact vs. Fiction

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump looks on at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, U.S., February 18, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

President Donald Trump took to Truth Social late Monday night to continue his furious reaction to polls claiming he was losing support — and promising the U.S. would soon be rich.

In the posting tirade, he first alleged, “The USA lost Billions of Dollars A DAY in International Trade under Sleepy Joe Biden. I have now stemmed that tide, and will be making a fortune, very soon.”

Just two minutes later, he posted, “The Polls from the Fake News are, like the News itself, FAKE! We are doing GREAT, better than ever before.” The post follows several outlets, including those that are typically pro-Trump, which showed him down in the polls. Fox News polls show Trump’s approval rating dropped to 44%. Similarly, Rasmussen showed a 5-point drop for Trump’s approval rating.

The posts were made just before midnight and were followed by two links. The first was to a pollster saying the first 100-day polls were skewed. The second is a Fox Business article saying foreign and domestic investments have totaled over $5T under Trump, and the economy has added 451k jobs for Americans.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Latest Truth Social Tirade: Fact vs. Fiction

In his latest nocturnal outburst on Truth Social, former President Donald Trump unleashed a series of claims that, upon even the most cursory examination, oscillate between misleading bravado and outright falsehoods. Let's dissect the assertions and juxtapose them with the cold, hard facts.

Claim 1: "The USA lost Billions of Dollars A DAY in International Trade under Sleepy Joe Biden"

Trump's rhetoric here is a classic example of his penchant for hyperbolic statements that lack grounding in quantifiable reality. International trade balances are complex and cannot be distilled into such simplistic, alarmist terms without ignoring the multifaceted nature of global economies. Moreover, attributing day-to-day fluctuations in trade balances to the actions of a single administration demonstrates a misunderstanding (or a deliberate misrepresentation) of how international trade works.

Claim 2: "I have now stemmed that tide, and will be making a fortune, very soon."

This statement is bewildering for a multitude of reasons, not least of which is Trump's use of the present tense ("I have now stemmed") to describe actions taken during a presidency that ended in January 2021. Unless Trump has resumed a role in government that the public is unaware of, this claim is at best delusional. Furthermore, the notion of a country 'making a fortune' simplifies the economy to the level of a business venture, ignoring the complexities of national and global economic health.

Claim 3: "The Polls from the Fake News are, like the News itself, FAKE!"

Trump's dismissal of unfavorable polls as "FAKE" is a tired tactic, aimed at discrediting any source of critique or challenge. It's crucial to note that the polls he disparages include those from Fox News and Rasmussen, outlets that have previously been cited by Trump and his supporters as credible when their outputs were favorable. This selective skepticism towards polls reveals a strategy not of reasoned critique but of outright denialism.

The Reality Check

  • Fox News and Rasmussen Reports, hardly bastions of anti-Trump sentiment, have documented a decline in Trump's approval ratings. This inconvenient fact for the Trump narrative indicates a broader trend that cannot be simply waved away as 'fake news'.

  • Economic claims about making the U.S. "rich" again are nebulous and unsubstantiated. The reference to foreign and domestic investments totaling over $5 trillion and the addition of 451,000 jobs need context; such figures, while impressive at face value, do not exist in a vacuum. Job growth and investment are influenced by a myriad of factors, including global economic conditions and policies set long before Trump's administration.

In summary, Trump's late-night missives on Truth Social serve as a microcosm of his broader approach to public discourse: bold assertions with little to no grounding in reality, a strategic discrediting of any critical sources, and a narrative of success detached from the complexities of governance and economic management. As always, it's essential that these claims are scrutinized and not taken at face value. In the world of Trump's social media, the line between fact and fiction is not just blurred—it's disregarded entirely.
