A historically Jewish newspaper compared President Donald Trump's tactics to a scheme used by Germany's Adolf Hitler before World War II.

In an op-ed for Forward this week, author Terrence Petty likened Trump's demonization of his self-declared enemies to Hitler's attempt to blame Jewish people for Germany's loss in World War I. The tactic was known as the Dolchstosslegende, or stabbed-in-the-back legend.

" Trump has created an American version of Adolf Hitler's 'Dolchstosslegende,' also known as the 'stabbed-in-the-back' lie," the article in Forward said.

Dolchstosslegende alleged that Germany did not lose the First World War, but instead was betrayed by Jews.

"Donald Trump has created an American version of the Dolchstosslegende, propagating a myth that the nation is being led to ruination by Joe Biden and the Democrats, prosecutors who go after Trump, judges who rule against him, non-MAGA news media, practitioners of 'wokeism,' and elite universities, among others," Petty wrote.

"All of this is utter nonsense, of course, but this American stabbed-in-the-back lie is at the core of Trump's assaults on democracy."

The author compared Hitler's first speech as chancellor on Feb. 10, 1933, to Trump's second inaugural address, which he insisted "reads like a stabbed-in-the-back manifesto."

"For many years, a radical and corrupt establishment has extracted power and wealth from our citizens, while the pillars of our society lay broken and seemingly in complete disrepair," Trump said during the speech in January.

Looking forward, Petty wondered if the U.S. was going through the early stages of authoritarianism.

"There are some signs of hope," he noted. "Some law firms and universities are fighting back against Trump's efforts to coerce them into bending to his will. Citizens are mobilizing, legions of them taking to the streets in protest of Trump's power grabs. Courts have been ruling against his attempts to subvert the law. But none of this seems to faze him. And as appeals play out in rulings against him, he keeps using his authoritarian jackhammer against the foundations of American democracy."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Echoes of History in Trump's Rhetoric

The recent op-ed in Forward by Terrence Petty draws a harrowing parallel between President Donald Trump's rhetoric and one of history's darkest figures, Adolf Hitler, specifically tying Trump's narrative tactics to the infamous Dolchstosslegende. This comparison isn't thrown around lightly, given the weight of history and the atrocities tied to Hitler’s regime. Petty's argument, grounded in Trump's demonization of perceived enemies and the propagation of the "stabbed-in-the-back" lie, merits a critical examination, especially as it relates to the broader implications for democracy in the United States.

Trump's Manufactured Mythos vs. Hitler's Dolchstosslegende

At the heart of Petty's critique is the assertion that Donald Trump has crafted an American variant of the Dolchstosslegende, a narrative once leveraged by Hitler to falsely claim Germany was betrayed from within during World War I, rather than defeated. Trump, according to Petty, has spun a similar tale of betrayal, casting Joe Biden, Democrats, the judiciary, the media, and other entities as architects of America's supposed downfall. This, Petty argues, is not just misleading but fundamentally undermines the democratic framework by sowing division and mistrust.

Critical Analysis: It's essential to dissect the analogy with care. While the comparison is evocative, equating Trump's political rhetoric with Hitler's pre-World War II maneuvers is a potent claim. The Dolchstosslegende was a foundational myth that contributed to the rise of Nazi Germany, a regime responsible for genocide and global conflict. Trump's narrative, while undeniably divisive and corrosive to public discourse, operates within a markedly different historical and political context. The risk, however, as Petty underscores, is in underestimating the impact of such narratives on the democratic fabric of society.

The Assault on Democracy

Petty's commentary extends beyond historical parallels, critiquing Trump's ongoing assault against the pillars of American democracy. From attempts to coerce universities and law firms to the demonization of the media and judiciary, Trump's actions, as described by Petty, represent a concerted effort to undermine institutional integrity and erode public trust.

Critical Analysis: Here, Petty's argument finds firmer ground. Regardless of one's stance on the Trump-Hitler comparison, the pattern of behavior that seeks to delegitimize critical democratic institutions is unmistakable and troubling. This behavior does echo authoritarian tactics, where power is centralized, and dissent is stifled.

Signs of Resistance and Hope

Despite the grim picture painted, Petty identifies a silver lining. The resistance from law firms, universities, citizens, and courts against Trump's maneuvers offers hope. This pushback is crucial, reflecting the resilience of democratic institutions and the public's willingness to defend democratic norms.

Critical Analysis: This observation is vital, emphasizing not just the dangers posed by authoritarian tendencies but also the capacity for resistance and renewal within democratic societies. It underscores the importance of vigilance and active participation in the democratic process to counter threats, whether they be from within or without.

Conclusion

Terrence Petty's op-ed in Forward serves as a stark reminder of the dangers posed by divisive and authoritarian rhetoric. While the comparison between Trump and Hitler should be navigated with caution, the underlying concern about the erosion of democratic norms and institutions is both valid and urgent. The ultimate takeaway is the necessity for constant vigilance and active resistance to protect the democratic ideals that underpin American society.
