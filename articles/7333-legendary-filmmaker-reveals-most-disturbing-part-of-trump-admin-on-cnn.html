A legendary filmmaker revealed what America’s Founding Fathers would likely consider the “most disturbing” part of President Donald Trump’s second administration in an interview with CNN.

Trump’s second administration has been marked by his swift efforts to amass executive power. Trump has attacked independent agencies, fired multiple independent watchdogs, and sought to take control of the federal purse.

Filmmaker Ken Burns, who has chronicled American history across several films, said during an interview on CNN’s “Inside Politics with Dana Bash” on Wednesday that the Founding Fathers anticipated someone like Trump would eventually come along to challenge American democracy. However, they would not have expected Congress to cede as much power to the executive as it has.

“What they did for us is extraordinary,” Burns said of the Founding Fathers.

“I think they’d come back, and they would not be upset that somebody was trying to take more executive power,” he continued. “They’d be upset that Article One, about the legislative branch, had ceded so much power. I think that would be the most disturbing thing about what’s going on right now.”

Our Analysis:

The Filmmaker’s Insight: A Critical Examination

In a recent CNN interview, Ken Burns, a filmmaker renowned for his deep dives into American history, offered a poignant critique of President Donald Trump’s second administration, specifically highlighting the expansion of executive power and the erosion of independent checks within the government. Burns, leveraging his extensive knowledge, speculated on the Founding Fathers’ potential dismay at current political dynamics, drawing a direct line between historical foresight and present-day political failings. This analysis unpacks Burns’ observations, emphasizing the critical implications for American democracy.

Trump’s Quest for Power: A Disturbing Trend

Burns’ commentary zeroes in on a disturbing trend within the Trump administration: an unabashed consolidation of executive power. Trump’s tactics include assaulting independent agencies, dismissing multiple independent watchdogs, and attempting to dominate federal financial decisions. These actions not only underscore a blatant disregard for the democratic safeguards embedded within the U.S. Constitution but also reflect a broader strategy to undermine the very fabric of American governance.

Founding Fathers: Prophetic Concerns

The insight that Burns offers hinges on a profound understanding of the Founding Fathers’ intentions. They meticulously crafted a system designed to prevent the concentration of power, foreseeing the potential for individuals like Trump to emerge. However, Burns poignantly notes that while the Founding Fathers anticipated challenges to democracy, they likely “would not have expected Congress to cede as much power to the executive as it has.”

This observation is critical: it highlights a fundamental breakdown in the system of checks and balances that is supposed to define American government. The legislative branch, once envisioned as a robust counterbalance to executive ambition, appears to have abdicated its responsibilities, allowing for an unprecedented accrual of power by the presidency.

The Legislative Lapse: A Cause for Alarm

Burns pinpoints the crux of the issue: the legislative branch’s failure to uphold its duty. This is not merely a matter of partisan politics; it is a systemic failure that threatens the core principles of American democracy. The willingness of Congress to yield ground to the executive branch, whether through inaction or complicity, has facilitated a dangerous shift in the balance of power.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

Ken Burns’ analysis serves as a stark wake-up call. The issues at hand transcend the Trump administration, touching on the very essence of American governance. Burns challenges us to reflect on the responsibilities of Congress and the importance of safeguarding the system of checks and balances that defines the United States. It is a reminder that the strength of a democracy is only as robust as its commitment to upholding the principles upon which it was founded.

In essence, Burns’ remarks are a clarion call to action: a reminder that vigilance, engagement, and adherence to foundational democratic principles are paramount, especially in times when they are most under threat. The legacy of the Founding Fathers, according to Burns, is not merely historical; it is a living, breathing guide that demands respect, understanding, and, above all, protection.
