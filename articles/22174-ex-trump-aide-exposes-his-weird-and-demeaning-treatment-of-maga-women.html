Ex-Trump aide exposes his 'weird' and demeaning treatment of MAGA women

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Trump’s Court of Disposable Women

The Story, Briefly

Anthony Scaramucci says Donald Trump treats women in his orbit as transactions, not people, and anonymous MAGA insiders told the Daily Mail that the expectations placed on women around Trump are punishing: constant availability, careful appearance, and obedience that spills into private life, including during maternity leave. That reporting is the setup. The real story is who controls the system and who pays for it.

Power Has a Name

This is not a mystery of personality. It is a hierarchy. Donald Trump holds the power, sets the terms, and creates the atmosphere in which women are measured by compliance, presentation, and usefulness. The women around him are not co-equal participants in a political project. They are assets to be managed, displayed, and discarded.

Scaramucci’s language is blunt because the structure is blunt. Trump’s orbit is built around one person’s appetite and everyone else’s proximity to it. That is not “chemistry” or “team dynamics.” It is domination with branding.

The Cost Is Gendered

The source makes clear that women in Trump’s circle are expected to be exceptional at their jobs and also to perform a second, unpaid assignment: look right, dress right, stay available, and absorb the demands of a man who “barely sleeps.” That is not a professional standard. It is an instrument of control.

The reported pressure on Karoline Leavitt, including calls during maternity leave, is the most revealing detail in the piece. It shows the real function of the White House under Trump: the state bends around the convenience of the leader, and women are expected to prove loyalty by making their bodies and family lives available to the machine.

Misdirection Is the Point

The soft lie in stories like this is that the problem is Trump’s “weird” behavior, as if the issue were social awkwardness or sexual eccentricity. That framing flatters the institution by shrinking the harm. It makes structural subordination sound like gossip.

Call it what it is: misogyny as operating procedure. Trump does not merely offend women. He uses them. He rewards flattery, demands submission, and punishes dissent. The women around him are expected to absorb the degradation while also laundering his image through proximity. That is not confusion. It is deliberate.

The Enablers

Scaramucci is speaking now as a critic, but he is also part of the record. He was inside the first-term operation. The anonymous MAGA women are inside the current one. Their willingness to describe exhaustion does not absolve the system; it confirms it. People close to Trump know exactly what the arrangement is. They stay until the arrangement becomes unbearable or politically impossible.

That is the broader institutional failure here. The problem is not that Trump’s staff did not understand him. The problem is that too many people understood him and worked for him anyway, then acted surprised when a man who treats people as transactions built a workplace that runs on transaction and fear.

The Pattern

This story is about gender, but it is also about authoritarian power in miniature. Trump’s model is simple: loyalty must be visible, dependence must be total, and humanity is optional. Women in his orbit are not just asked to serve; they are asked to disappear into service while still looking camera-ready.

That is the political meaning of the piece. Trump does not merely govern through ideology. He governs through personal control, humiliation, and selective reward. The misogyny is not incidental to that model. It is one of its clearest expressions.
