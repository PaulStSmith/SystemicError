'Not nodding in agreement': CNN guest shuts down Heritage Foundation podcaster over Kimmel

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Virginia Allen and Lulu Garcia-Navarro/CNN

A pair of podcasters clashed Friday morning on CNN over President Donald Trump's administration pressuring ABC to drop late night talk show host Jimmy Kimmel from the airwaves.

The network's parent company Disney indefinitely suspended Kimmel after Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair, the two biggest local TV operators that each have mergers up for Federal Communications Commission approval, pressured ABC to end "Jimmy Kimmel Live!" over his comments about slain right-wing activist Charlie Kirk.

"Ultimately, ABC had the call to make," said Virginia Allen, who hosts two podcasts for the Heritage Foundation's "Daily Signal" website. "We're going to back him no matter what, and they chose not to because they saw it as a losing issue because they saw Kimmel as a liability than an asset."

"CNN This Morning" host Audie Cornish cut in to ask whether the pressure came from the Trump administration or the public, and Allen quickly blamed public pressure for the move.

"I do think, regardless of political viewpoint, I think the public recognizes that it is inappropriate to go on television less than a week after someone was killed and to make comments that are not only really derogatory in some ways, but, quite frankly, also very misleading, as to the facts of the case," Allen said, "and I think that's what so many Americans took issue with, and just thought was very insensitive at a time when many Americans are truly grieving, and not only grieving the loss of Charlie Kirk, but grieving the fact that someone who championed free speech, wanted to have dialog and conversations ultimately was killed because of that."

Cornish noted that Kirk's memorial and funeral were schedule for this weekend, and she turned to New York Times podcaster Lulu Garcia-Navarro and inaccurately noted that she had been nodding.

"No, not nodding in agreement," Garcia-Navarro said. "I think, listen, I'm going to give a little bit of a civics lesson, which is this: In a democracy, you need to have free speech... It's not about the free market, it's about explicit threats from the FCC chairman that were acted on because they have business in front of the FCC."

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr – who wrote a chapter on reshaping the agency he now leads for the Heritage Foundation's Project 2025 blueprint – threatened to revoke the broadcasting licenses for stations that aired Kimmel's show hours before Disney pulled the plug.

"The FCC is incredibly powerful," Garcia-Navarro added. "It grants licenses, it actually says what deals can go together. We've already seen a version of this with the Paramount deal, where basically ... the parent company of CBS look at the president and say we're going to pay you over a suit that you have, and lo and behold, right after –"

Cornish cut in at that point, before Garcia-Navarro was presumably going to point out that CBS canceled "The Stephen Colbert" show shortly after settling Trump's defamation suit.

Our Analysis:

A Scathing Review: The Kimmel Controversy and the Power Play Behind It

In a recent clash on CNN, the curtain was pulled back to reveal a disturbing tableau of power dynamics, censorship, and the thin veneer of democracy that seems to be peeling away under the pressure of political agendas. The incident involving the suspension of Jimmy Kimmel by ABC, following pressure from the Trump administration, is not just a story about a late-night host being taken off the air. It's a chilling narrative about the erosion of free speech, the wielding of governmental power to silence opposition, and the complicity of corporate media in these endeavors.

The Facts Unraveled

Let's dissect the key points. ABC decided to indefinitely suspend Jimmy Kimmel after receiving pressure from Nexstar Media Group and Sinclair, two colossal local TV operators with pending mergers needing the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) green light. This move came after Kimmel's comments about the slain right-wing activist Charlie Kirk, which were deemed offensive by some. Virginia Allen, representing the Heritage Foundation, quickly pointed the finger at public pressure, rather than acknowledging the elephant in the room - the Trump administration's involvement.

However, Lulu Garcia-Navarro of The New York Times cut through the facade with a reminder that in a democracy, free speech is paramount. She highlighted that the real issue at hand was not market forces or public opinion, but rather "explicit threats from the FCC chairman," Brendan Carr, who, interestingly, has ties to the Heritage Foundation. Carr's threat to revoke broadcasting licenses mere hours before Disney's decision to pull the plug on Kimmel is a stark reminder of the power wielded by those in charge of regulatory bodies.

A Dangerous Precedent

This incident sets a dangerous precedent. When political figures can influence media corporations to silence voices that critique or oppose them, we are treading on thin ice. The fact that this power play was masked under the guise of responding to public pressure is even more alarming. It reflects a calculated move to manipulate public opinion, suggesting that the outcry was significant enough to warrant such an extreme reaction.

The Heritage Foundation's Virginia Allen's attempt to frame the decision as a response to public sensitivity and grief is a thinly veiled attempt to divert attention from the underlying issue of censorship and political pressure. The argument that Kimmel's comments were "insensitive" during a period of mourning is a red herring, distracting from the critical discourse on free speech and the right to critique and question.

The Role of the FCC and Corporate Media

The involvement of the FCC, under the leadership of a chairman with clear political affiliations and agendas, is a glaring example of how governmental agencies can be weaponized against dissenting voices. The FCC's role in this saga is not just about regulating the airwaves; it's about controlling the narrative and suppressing opposition.

Furthermore, the complicity of corporate media in these power dynamics cannot be overlooked. ABC's decision to suspend Kimmel, influenced by threats to their business interests, highlights the precarious balance between corporate media's profit motives and their responsibility to uphold democratic values like free speech.

In Conclusion

The Kimmel controversy is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger, more disturbing trend towards authoritarianism and censorship. It serves as a wake-up call to the public, media, and policymakers to guard against the encroachment on fundamental democratic principles. The role of the media as the Fourth Estate is to hold power to account, not bow to it due to threats or financial incentives. In the battle for the soul of democracy, silence and complicity are the true enemies.
