'We just got duped': Fox News attacks Trump's 'rigged' FIFA event

Fox News hosts expressed disappointment after claiming that FIFA had “rigged” an event with President Donald Trump and other world leaders.

During a Friday ceremony at the Kennedy Center, Trump received FIFA’s first annual peace prize before participating in a draw with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum.

“So you have to basically mingle the balls a little bit,” FIFA President Gianni Infantino explained to the leaders. “You pick one. You open it. You see, there is a little paper inside... And then you can show it to the public.”

One by one, the leaders picked a ball representing the team that would play the first match in their home country. Coincidentally, Carney picked Canada, and Sheinbaum picked Mexico.

“I think I know what this is going to be now,” Trump said before his turn. “This is shocking.”

Trump was unsurprised to see that he had picked the United States.

Following the ceremony, the Fox News hosts said they were not impressed.

“I told you there’s some issues with FIFA, didn’t I?” guest host Kevin O’Leary noted. “I mean, that was so rigged. It’s ridiculous.”

“I said, okay, here we go. Every ball. This is an old trick from the circus. Every ball has the country in it that the leaders in front of. There’s no chance this wasn’t going to happen,” he continued. “The probability, if this was actually mixed with all the countries involved, that they would all get their own country was practically zero. If you think about it, that it’s impossible.”

“We just got duped,” co-host Emily Compagno agreed.

“I think what’s happening backstage right now is whoever marketing idea that was, whatever executive it was, they’re getting whacked right now,” O’Leary speculated.

Our Analysis:

The Grand Illusion: FIFA’s “Rigged” Peace Prize Ceremony

In a spectacle that could only be concocted in the fever dreams of those with a penchant for theatrics over transparency, the FIFA-hosted ceremony at the Kennedy Center unfolded more like a magic show than an event befitting international dignitaries. At the heart of this charade, President Donald Trump, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum were participants in a draw that seemed less about chance and more about a predictable, prearranged outcome.

The Setup

The ceremony’s premise was simple: each leader would draw a ball, revealing a paper inside that would determine the country playing the first match. Predictably, when each leader drew a ball, they miraculously selected their own country. This “coincidence” was so blatant that even Trump prefaced his selection with, “I think I know what this is going to be now. This is shocking.” The only shock here is that anyone would feign surprise at the outcome.

Fox News Weighs In

Enter the chorus of disapproval from Fox News, where guest host Kevin O’Leary decried the event as “rigged” and “ridiculous.” O’Leary’s comparison of the draw to “an old trick from the circus” aptly summarizes the farcical nature of the proceedings. Co-host Emily Compagno’s lamentation that “we just got duped” would be touching if it weren’t so painfully obvious from the outset. The suggestion that the marketing minds behind this stunt are “getting whacked right now” by O’Leary is a whimsical take on corporate accountability, albeit one that misses the broader issue.

The Real Trick

The true spectacle here isn’t the apparent rigging of the draw — which, let’s face it, defies the very essence of probability and chance. No, the real trick is how FIFA, an organization already marred by controversies and allegations of corruption, continues to stage such displays of orchestrated camaraderie and peace. Awarding Trump its first annual peace prize is another layer of irony, considering his contentious and divisive tenure as president.

Conclusion: The Illusion of Integrity

This event, and the reactions it elicited, spotlight a profound problem that extends well beyond the realm of sports and into the very fabric of modern political and media landscapes. The illusion of integrity and fairness is frequently shattered by the reality of manipulation and orchestration. That Fox News hosts would express disappointment and surprise at this “revelation” is either a testament to their naivety or a cynical play at outrage for ratings.

In a world where genuine peace and solidarity are desperately needed, events like these serve as stark reminders of the systemic error in our global leadership and the entities that support them. The FIFA ceremony wasn’t just a poorly executed magic trick; it was a microcosm of the systemic deceit and theatricality plaguing our international stage.
