Flagged by CBS News reporter Scott MacFarlane, the filing involved New York Police Department officer Thomas Webster, who was sentenced to 10 years in prison for his role in the Jan. 6, 2021, attacks on the U.S. Capitol. The Justice Department wrote in a 2022 statement that Webster was accused of “assaulting a law enforcement officer with a deadly or dangerous weapon and related charges for his actions during the breach of the U.S. Capitol...”

When President Donald Trump entered office on Jan. 20, 2025, his first act was to issue a blanket pardon for all of those who were involved in the attacks, whether convicted, sentenced, or not. It meant that Webster was allowed to go free.

Since then, Webster submitted a filing requesting a “writ of coram nobis,” which is a petition for the court to admit an error and correct the “errors of law in its own judgment,” according to Cornell Law School.

“It is a common law tradition that allows the court to correct its judgment and administer justice should facts that would have changed the judgment but were outside the record and unknown to the court at the time of judgment surface,” the explainer continues.

The filing from Pirro’s team opposes Webster’s demand, despite the administration’s position that Webster and other defendants did nothing wrong on Jan. 6.

“First, Webster failed to plead any adverse consequence stemming from his convictions and likely could not satisfy this burden based upon his specific claims. He therefore lacks standing to sue. Second, Webster failed to provide any valid reason for not raising his claims sooner—despite having the opportunity and ability to so— than in the present petition. And third, Webster’s constitutional claims establish no fundamental error warranting extraordinary coram nobis relief. The Court should deny the petition,” the filing begins.

But what MacFarlane pointed out was page four of the 38-page filing.

“Webster ‘recognized that the bicycle racks were meant to keep people back. But he tried to get past them nonetheless,’” the filing continues. “Webster confronted the ‘overwhelmed’ [Metropolitan Police Officer Noah] Rathbun, hurling insults and telling the officer to ‘take [his] s--t off,’ which both Webster and Officer Rathbun acknowledged were fighting words. ... Officer Rathbun then pushed Webster away from the bike rack ‘to create some distance between he and the perimeter.’”

“Webster did not stop though,” the filing notes. “He snatched at and pushed the bike rack towards Officer Rathbun. Webster pushed the rack into Officer Rathbun a second time, which concerned Officer Rathbun ‘that that portion of the perimeter would have been compromised[.]’ Webster’s actions prompted Officer Rathbun to ‘incidentally’ contact Webster’s face with an open hand that Officer Rathbun was ‘taught’ to use in crowd control situations.”

When Pirro was nominated and sworn into the office, The Bulwark posted a commentary from Thomas Joscelyn and Norm Eisen that recalled, “Pirro has defended Trump’s sweeping pardon of the January 6 defendants and convicts, claiming they were ‘hostages. She has asserted that they were prosecuted by the Department of Justice ‘based upon their political beliefs’ and dismissed the violence at the U.S. Capitol as a Biden administration ‘narrative.’”

Our Analysis:

The Perplexing Paradox of Jeanine Pirro’s Legal Maneuvering

In a twist that could only emerge from the depths of a political landscape as tumultuously surreal as a Dali painting, U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro—a figure synonymous with staunch conservatism and unwavering loyalty to former President Donald Trump—finds herself embroiled in a legal paradox that reads more like a plot from Kafka than a chapter in American jurisprudence.

The Case of Thomas Webster

At the heart of this saga is Thomas Webster, a former NYPD officer sentenced to a decade behind bars for his involvement in the January 6, 2021, Capitol insurrection. Webster’s actions, as described in court documents, were not those of a peaceful protester caught in the wrong place at the wrong time but of someone who actively sought to breach the very fabric of American democracy.

Webster, wielding a deadly weapon against a law enforcement officer, became emblematic of the violent undercurrents that propelled the Capitol attack. Yet, in an act that stunned observers and legal scholars alike, President Trump, upon reclaiming the Oval Office in 2025, issued a blanket pardon for all January 6 participants. This move, ostensibly a broad stroke of clemency, did little to quell the tumult it aimed to address; instead, it added fuel to the ever-burning fire of division.

The Irony of Opposition

Enter Pirro, whose legal team’s opposition to Webster’s request for a writ of coram nobis—a plea to correct a court’s judgment based on new evidence—drips with irony so rich it would make Alanis Morissette blush. Despite her public defense of January 6 defendants as mere political prisoners of a vindictive Biden administration, Pirro’s filing vehemently argues against Webster’s petition.

The document points out, with a level of detail that borders on the meticulous, Webster’s aggressive actions against a Metropolitan Police officer, painting a picture of a man far from the innocent patriot some narratives have depicted. It’s a stark contrast to Pirro’s televised rhetoric, highlighting a chasm between public posturing and legal reality that is as wide as it is deep.

Political Theater at Its Finest

This situation encapsulates the essence of political theater, where characters play roles that shift with the wind of public opinion and legal necessity. Pirro, once a vocal critic of what she perceived as the persecution of January 6 defendants, now finds herself in a position where she must publicly acknowledge the indefensible actions of those same individuals.

The opposition to Webster’s filing is not just a legal document; it is a testament to the cognitive dissonance that plagues our political discourse. It reveals a landscape where convictions are malleable, and allegiances are as stable as a house of cards in a hurricane.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

This episode, emblematic of the broader dysfunctions within our political system, underscores the need for a return to principle over partisanship. The case of Jeanine Pirro and Thomas Webster isn’t just a legal oddity; it’s a stark illustration of the systemic error that occurs when political loyalty trumps objective reality.

As observers of this unfolding drama, we must ask ourselves: When will the curtain fall on this theater of the absurd? The answer, unfortunately, remains as elusive as the concept of justice in this convoluted narrative.
