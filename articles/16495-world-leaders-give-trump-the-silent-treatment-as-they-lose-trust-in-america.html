World leaders give Trump the silent treatment as they lose trust in America

Trump’s Accords Gambit Is Not Peace. It Is Damage Control.

A Failed War Needs a Trophy

The reporting is straightforward about the immediate trigger: Trump is trying to bolt new Arab signatories onto the Abraham Accords while the US and Iran negotiate an end to their months-long war. The demand is not happening because the region suddenly discovered a new appetite for normalization. It is happening because Washington and Tel Aviv need a winner they can market after “Operation Epic Fury” left both weaker, more exposed, and more dependent on a diplomatic costume change.

That is the real context. This is not peacemaking from a position of strength. It is image management after strategic overreach.

Who Holds Power

The actors with actual institutional power are obvious. Trump controls US pressure, weapons, and the diplomatic leverage of the American state. Netanyahu controls Israel’s military campaign and the occupation apparatus that keeps grinding on in southern Lebanon and Gaza. Those two decide what gets called peace, what gets called security, and what gets presented as regional order.

The countries being courted are not equal partners in this setup. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Jordan, the UAE, and Bahrain are being asked to validate a framework built by Washington and Jerusalem. Their role is to absorb the political cost of blessing an arrangement that primarily serves US and Israeli needs.

Palestine Is the Obstacle They Want to Pretend Is Optional

The article is clear that the Abraham Accords were never a neutral peace project. They were built after the US moved its embassy to Jerusalem, closed the PLO office in Washington, and announced that West Bank settlements were no longer treated as illegal. Then came the “Peace to Prosperity” plan, which promised economic development while treating Palestinian statehood as expendable.

That is the central fraud. The accords are presented as regional normalization, but they are really a mechanism for laundering Palestinian dispossession through Arab recognition of Israel. Saudi Arabia’s refusal to sign without guarantees of Palestinian self-determination makes the hypocrisy hard to hide. So does the fact that public opinion in Pakistan, Qatar, Turkey, and elsewhere remains overwhelmingly pro-Palestinian.

The Misdirection Is Deliberate

Trump’s latest demand tries to turn a failed war into a diplomatic checksum. Netanyahu gets to pretend that deeper ties with Arab governments offset the reality of continued occupation and regional backlash. Trump gets to resurrect a signature foreign policy brand while the US faces depleted weapons stocks, a global energy shock, unsettled Gulf allies, and Israeli reluctance to accept any Iran peace deal.

This is how power misdirects blame: it presents a coalition of coercion as a peace framework and then frames refusal as obstruction. But the refusal here is not confusion or irrationality. Pakistan has already rejected the demand, and Saudi Arabia is unlikely to comply. The problem is not that the region fails to understand the offer. The problem is that the offer is politically toxic because it asks Arab states to endorse Israeli power while Palestine remains unresolved.

The Region Is Not Standing Still

The more important fact is that the region is developing alternatives. The article notes that Saudi Arabia has reportedly floated a regional non-aggression pact including Iran, modeled loosely on Helsinki-style détente. That is not a cosmetic adjustment. It is a rejection of the US habit of imposing a security architecture that serves American and Israeli priorities first and everyone else’s stability later, if at all.

That shift matters because it shows where the initiative is moving. The Abraham Accords were sold as inevitable. They are now looking like a dead-end framework whose usefulness is shrinking as regional actors try to build something less humiliating and less extractive.

What This Story Actually Shows

The pattern is familiar: military failure gets translated into diplomatic theater, Palestinian rights get treated as negotiable residue, and weaker regional governments are pressured to ratify the narrative of success. That is not a peace process. It is an attempt to convert coercion into legitimacy.

The silence Trump’s demand received may be the most revealing detail in the piece. It suggests the old US script still exists, but its audience is thinning. Washington and Tel Aviv can still issue demands. They can still threaten, bargain, and stage-manage. What they can no longer do as easily is force the region to confuse submission with stability.
