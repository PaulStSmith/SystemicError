'Not sure she'll sell': 'Far-right' Stefanik creating shock waves in NY gubernatorial race

Until recently, Rep. Mike Lawler (R-New York) was viewed as the most likely person to win New York State's 2026 GOP gubernatorial primary — and the most electable if he runs against incumbent Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul in the general election should he become the nominee.

Lawler is conservative, but he isn't far-right. And his frequent appearances on liberal-leaning MSNBC demonstrate that he is quite capable of having a polite conversation with Democrats.

Lawler's supporters view him as a Republican in the George Pataki vein. New York is a blue state, but Pataki served as governor from 1995-2006 and fared well among swing voters and centrist Blue Dog Democrats.

But New York State's 2026 gubernatorial race is becoming more complicated because of another possible candidate: Rep. Elise Stefanik (R-New York).

Stefanik was President Donald Trump's nominee for U.S. ambassador to the United Nations, but Trump withdrew the nomination — not because he is upset with her (Stefanik is a strident Trump loyalist), but because he reportedly wanted her to stay in Congress. Republicans have only a small majority in the U.S. House of Representatives, and Trump reportedly wanted to avoid the uncertainty of a special election in Stefanik's district.

GOP strategist Jay Townsend is concerned about how well Stefanik would fare against Hochul in the general election.

When Stefanik first entered Congress during the Barack Obama era, she was considered a traditional pro-business Republican along the lines of 2012 GOP presidential nominee and ex-Massachusetts Gov. Mitt Romney. And she was an outspoken Trump critic in 2016. But Stefanik later flip-flopped, taking a far-right MAGA turn and becoming a combative, in-your-face, highly performative Trump supporter.

Townsend told The Hill, "Elise's trouble will be after she wins the party nod, which I think she can if she wants to. But she's so far right that I'm not sure she'll sell in New York."

The Hill's reporting on Stefanik's possible gubernatorial run is drawing a lot of discussion on X, formerly Twitter.

Conservative strategist Rina Shah tweeted, "Elise Stefanik's potential run for NY Governor signals a GOP shift — trading suburban appeal for populist fire. Challenging Hochul in '26 could energize the base, but risks alienating moderates in a blue state. The Empire State's future hinges on this gamble."

Former CIA agent Matt Castelli posted, "Let's be clear: Stefanik hates this job. She's not from NY-21. She loathes the people. She invested a decade of her life with one aim: to get ahead. House leadership, VP stakes, UN Amb nominee-all dashed to end up back in the job she hates. She will be miserable and unbearable."

Our Analysis:

The GOP's New York Conundrum: Lawler vs. Stefanik

In the unfolding drama of New York State's 2026 GOP gubernatorial primary, we find ourselves observing a classic political tableau: the moderate versus the zealot. Rep. Mike Lawler, the erstwhile front-runner, represents a seemingly bygone era of Republican politics—a time when being conservative didn't necessarily mean adhering to the far-right extremes. Contrastingly, Rep. Elise Stefanik embodies the Trump era's transformation of the party, where allegiance to the MAGA movement and a penchant for combative politics reign supreme.

Lawler's Moderate Appeal: A Pataki Redux?

Lawler's positioning as the moderate candidate, reminiscent of former Governor George Pataki's tenure, is an interesting strategy in a state that has veered decidedly blue. His willingness to engage with liberal media on platforms like MSNBC demonstrates an attempt to bridge the ever-widening partisan divide. This approach has its supporters casting him in the mold of a Republican capable of winning over swing voters and centrist Democrats—a necessity in a state like New York.

However, one must question the viability of this strategy in today's polarized political climate. The era of Pataki was a different time, and the GOP of yesteryears is not the GOP of today. Can a moderate Republican still find traction in a party that has been reshaped by the likes of Trump and his acolytes?

Stefanik: The MAGA Transformation

Enter Elise Stefanik, whose political metamorphosis from a pro-business Republican critical of Trump in 2016 to a "far-right MAGA turn" figure underscores the party's shift over the past decade. Stefanik's alignment with Trump, despite its performative and combative nature, signals a departure from the party's traditional moorings towards a more populist, nationalist approach.

GOP strategist Jay Townsend's concerns about Stefanik's electability post-primary nod are warranted. New York, for all its pockets of conservatism, remains a largely blue state. Stefanik's far-right stance, while potentially energizing the base, risks alienating the moderate and independent voters essential for a statewide victory.

The Empire State's Political Gamble

The discussion surrounding Stefanik's potential gubernatorial run, as highlighted by Rina Shah and Matt Castelli, encapsulates the broader GOP dilemma. Shah's observation that Stefanik's run signifies a "GOP shift—trading suburban appeal for populist fire" is particularly poignant. It's a risky strategy, banking on the base's energy at the potential cost of broader appeal.

Castelli's harsh critique of Stefanik's ambitions and dissatisfaction with her current role further complicates her narrative. It paints a picture of a politician more concerned with personal advancement than genuine service—a narrative that, if it gains traction, could severely undermine her gubernatorial bid.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The potential showdown between Lawler and Stefanik for the New York GOP gubernatorial nomination is emblematic of the broader struggle within the Republican Party. On one side stands the moderate, attempting to navigate a path back to bipartisanship and broader appeal. On the other, the far-right loyalist, banking on populist fervor to carry the day.

The outcome of this internal contest will not just determine the GOP's gubernatorial nominee but will also signal the direction the party is heading. Will it be a return to a more centrist approach, or will the populist fire continue to burn brightly? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the Empire State's future indeed "hinges on this gamble."
