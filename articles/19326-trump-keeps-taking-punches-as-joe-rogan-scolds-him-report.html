Trump keeps taking punches as Joe Rogan scolds him: report

CNN reporter Aaron Blake says President Donald Trump is typically quick to strike back at the slightest offense or criticism, often resorting to name-calling in his online clapbacks.

But the president is meekly taking every blow that bro-MAGA influencer Joe Rogan is hurling at him with no response.

“Rogan has broken with Trump on several major issues since mid-2025. And polling shows the issues he’s picked happen to be some of Trump’s biggest political liabilities – including the war with Iran, the Jeffrey Epstein files and immigration enforcement,” said Blake.

On Tuesday, the popular podcaster lit into Trump’s ongoing assault on Iran, accusing Trump of breaking his promises to America First conservative voters who are sick of sinking U.S. cash into endless, overpriced wars.

“But it just seems so insane based on what he ran on,” Black quotes Rogan saying on his show. “I mean, this is why a lot of people feel betrayed, right? He ran on no more wars and these stupid senseless wars, and then we have one that we can’t even really clearly define why we did it.”

“It just doesn’t make any sense to me – unless we’re acting on someone else’s interests, like particularly Israel’s interests,” Rogan added. “It just didn’t make any sense to me.”

Similarly, Rogan has blasted Trump’s secrecy and handling of the Epstein files, painting it as yet another betrayal of his young voters, particularly the young first-time Trump voters who took a chance on Trump in the 2024 elections.

Last month, Blake said Rogan called the FBI’s claim of no evidence Epstein had clients “the gaslightiest gaslighting s—— I’ve ever heard in my life.”

Days later, Rogan blasted Trump’s Justice Department over it’s suspicious redactions to the Epstein files, particularly the ones incriminating Trump.

“Like, what is this? This is not good. None of this is good for this administration,” Rogan said. “It looks f—— terrible. It looks terrible.”

Uncharacteristically, the combative president has yet to slam Rogan as he burns every other critic who’s invoked his name. Asked about Rogan’s criticisms last month by NBC News, Blake said Trump shows nothing but esteem for Rogan.

“I think he’s a great guy, and I think he likes me, too,” Trump said.

The explanation for Trump’s newfound meekness may be Rogan’s substantial audience, which Trump is leery of alienating, said Blake.

“If there’s one figure who epitomized President Donald Trump’s ability to cobble together a winning coalition in 2024, it might have been Joe Rogan — the influential podcaster who made big news by endorsing Trump on the eve of the election after interviewing him,” said Blake. On the flipside, much ink has been spilled about the Kamala Harris campaign not booking a date with Rogan’s podcast and the detrimental effect that might have had on her bid to become president.”

As long as Rogan’s audience remains large (and significantly male) Trump’s docility will likely continue, regardless of how hard Rogan hounds him.

“And, you know, liking me isn’t important,” Trump added to his response to NBC News. “What happens is that — I think we do a phenomenal job, but I don’t think we’re good at public relations.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of CNN Article on Trump’s Response to Joe Rogan’s Criticism

Institutional Power and Decision-Makers

First, it’s vital to understand who holds institutional power in the contexts discussed: the war with Iran, the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files, and immigration enforcement. The President of the United States, currently Donald Trump, wields substantial power over foreign policy, including military decisions, and has significant influence over the Department of Justice and immigration policies. Thus, Trump and his administration are the primary decision-makers in these areas.

Joe Rogan, a popular podcaster and influencer, has a different kind of power: influence over public opinion. While Rogan cannot make policy decisions, his platform allows him to critique those in power, potentially swaying public sentiment.

Framing and Responsibility

The article suggests that Trump’s lack of retaliation against Rogan’s criticism is notable, given his history of responding harshly to detractors. This framing implies a deviation from Trump’s usual behavior due to Rogan’s influence and audience size. It’s important to recognize that the article does not misdirect responsibility; it clearly identifies Trump’s actions and decisions as the subject of Rogan’s criticism. However, it suggests Trump’s silence as strategic, avoiding alienation of Rogan’s large and predominantly male audience, which may have contributed to his electoral success.

Analysis of Criticisms

War with Iran

Rogan’s criticism of Trump’s actions regarding Iran directly challenges the President’s fulfillment of campaign promises. Given Trump’s institutional power and his administration’s responsibility for military engagement, this critique is well-placed. The decision to engage militarily with Iran is squarely within the purview of Trump’s presidential powers.

Jeffrey Epstein Files

The handling of the Epstein files, particularly the accusations of “gaslighting” and suspicious redactions incriminating Trump, falls under the Department of Justice, over which Trump, as president, has influence. Rogan’s criticisms point to a lack of transparency and possible misuse of power for personal protection. These allegations, if true, reflect a serious abuse of institutional power.

Immigration Enforcement

Similar to the war with Iran, immigration policy and its enforcement are under the Trump administration’s direct control. Rogan’s critique, while not detailed in the article, likely centers on the administration’s actions not aligning with campaign promises or human rights standards.

Conclusion: Misplaced Criticism or Valid Concerns?

The article’s framing accurately places responsibility for the criticized actions on Trump and his administration, who hold the institutional power to make and implement decisions in these areas. Joe Rogan’s criticisms are directed towards those in power, challenging the administration’s decisions and policies. The article suggests Trump’s respect for Rogan and his audience as reasons for his uncharacteristic silence, highlighting a strategic choice rather than a shift in behavior driven by accountability.

The article does not misdirect responsibility; rather, it underscores the potential influence of public figures like Rogan on political discourse and the strategic considerations of political leaders in responding to criticism. The critique by Rogan and the reporting by CNN both align with holding the powerful accountable, a cornerstone of democratic society. This analysis finds the article’s narrative to be grounded in an accurate understanding of institutional power and responsibility, providing a clear view of the dynamics at play without resorting to false equivalence or lazy narratives.
