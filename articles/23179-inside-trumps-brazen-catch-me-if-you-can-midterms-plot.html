Inside Trump’s brazen ‘catch-me-if-you-can’ midterms plot

Based on an article by Terry H. Schwadron

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real Decision-MakersMail Ballots as a TargetThe Emergency Is ManufacturedThe Court Is Not Neutral HereThe Pattern Is Sabotage by Procedure

Power First, Law Later

A week after the Supreme Court let “likely illegal” work move forward anyway, the pattern was already clear: the governing method is to break things first and force everyone else into after-the-fact litigation. The reporting here connects that method to Trump’s push to use the Postal Service to choke off mail ballot delivery unless states submit to an untested federal citizenship-check scheme.

The Real Decision-Makers

The institutional power is not diffuse. Trump is setting the objective. The Justice Department is packaging the legal argument. The Postal Service is being used as the enforcement arm. And the Supreme Court, by treating obvious legal defects as procedural nuisances, is helping normalize the tactic.

That is the actual structure of the story. Not confusion. Not bureaucratic misunderstanding. A deliberate effort to make the executive branch behave as if the Constitution is optional until a court can physically stop it.

Mail Ballots as a Target

The ballot fight is not about administrative tidiness. It is about controlling who can vote and how much friction can be inserted before a ballot ever reaches a voter. The article says Trump wants mail ballots “erased,” and the mechanism matters: delay delivery, impose an untested vetting system, and create enough uncertainty that states scramble while deadlines pass.

North Carolina’s deadline to mail ballot applications came and went while the legal fight was still unresolved. That is the point of the operation. If the government can make election administration unstable in the middle of an election cycle, it does not need to win the underlying policy argument cleanly. It only needs to manufacture enough delay to blunt the process.

The Emergency Is Manufactured

The article correctly identifies the central inversion: the supposed emergency is the one Trump is creating. The legal system is being pushed into an endless cycle of injunctions, emergency appeals, and last-minute rulings while the administration acts as if it has already won.

Meanwhile, the truly reckless acts are treated with less urgency because they do not fit the partisan panic script. Unauthorized construction, attacks on cultural institutions, and spending public money without congressional approval are not framed as emergencies by the same people who suddenly discover procedural scruples when voting rights are involved. That tells you what the power center is actually afraid of: not lawlessness itself, but lawlessness when it threatens their grip on elections.

The Court Is Not Neutral Here

The Supreme Court’s posture matters because it has consequences. When the Court lifts injunctions first and asks questions later, it teaches the executive branch that illegality is a workable strategy so long as it is rapid enough. Jackson’s “catch-me-if-you-can” description is more than rhetoric; it captures a governing model in which the administration runs ahead of the law and dares opponents to catch up.

Roberts’s acknowledgment that the underlying conduct may be “likely illegal” should have been the end of the matter. Instead, it became a permission structure. That is not neutrality. It is institutional tolerance for executive sabotage.

The Pattern Is Sabotage by Procedure

This story is not really about ballots alone. It is about how authoritarian politics hides inside administrative form. You do not always get a dramatic seizure of power. More often, you get a Postal Service rule, a filing workaround, a rushed deadline, and a court system that mistakes procedural delay for restraint.

That is the larger lesson. Trump’s politics depend on converting every public system into a tool for coercion while pretending the fight is merely technical. The democratic answer is not to admire the cleverness of the scheme or to flatten it into another abstract “election issue.” It is to name the machinery clearly: executive overreach, court-enabled impunity, and a sustained effort to make voting harder for the people most likely to oppose him.
