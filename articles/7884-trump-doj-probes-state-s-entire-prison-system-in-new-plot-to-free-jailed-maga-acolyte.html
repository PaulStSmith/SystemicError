Trump DOJ probes state's entire prison system in new plot to free jailed MAGA acolyte

President Donald Trump’s Justice Department has come up with a new way to try to pressure the state of Colorado to release Tina Peters, a MAGA-favoring former elections clerk who went to prison for tampering with election equipment to try to prove Trump’s 2020 election conspiracy theories.

According to The New Republic, “Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon and the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division are pretending to care about prisoners’ human rights in an apparent effort to get conspiracy theorist, former state official, and convicted 2020 election fraudster Tina Peters out of a Colorado jail.”

Dhillon announced on X, “Under my direction, @CivilRights has opened an investigation into the entire Colorado prison system following multiple reports of unconstitutional and legally insufficient carceral conditions. Prisoners have civil rights.”

The letter doesn’t specifically mention Peters, simply attacking the Colorado prison system for “failing to provide adequate medical care and safe sanitary physical conditions of confinement,” and potentially “housing biological males in units designated for females.”

But both supporters and detractors of the administration immediately recognized this as a ploy to create a pretext for Peters’ release. The Bulwark’s Will Sommer wrote, “New Trump pressure on Colorado to release election conspiracy theorist Tina Peters—who Trump can’t pardon because she’s imprisoned on state charges.”

This comes after a federal judge rejected Peters’ bid for habeas corpus on Monday.

It also comes after the Justice Department reportedly considered another far-fetched scheme to force Peters’ release on the pretense that she is a witness in a federal investigation, then transferring her to a minimum security “Club Fed” facility, similar to the deal made with former Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche opposed this idea, leading to fury against him from some Trump supporters.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Maneuvering: The Tina Peters Saga

In the latest chapter of the Trump administration’s theater of the absurd, the Justice Department has concocted a novel strategy that reads like a playbook straight out of a political satire. Under the guise of championing prisoners’ human rights—a noble cause when genuine—the Department, spearheaded by Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon, has launched an investigation into the Colorado prison system. The official reason? Concerns over “unconstitutional and legally insufficient carceral conditions.” However, the subtext is impossible to ignore: this move is a thinly veiled attempt to pressure Colorado into releasing Tina Peters, a MAGA-aligned former elections clerk imprisoned for tampering with election equipment in a misguided quest to validate Trump’s 2020 election conspiracy theories.

The Pretense of Concern

The audacity of Dhillon’s statement on X, where she announced the investigation under her direction, is staggering. The letter from the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division doesn’t mention Peters by name, yet it doesn’t need to. The implications are as subtle as a sledgehammer. Claiming to target the entire Colorado prison system for failing “to provide adequate medical care and safe sanitary physical conditions of confinement,” and for potentially “housing biological males in units designated for females,” the Department’s sudden concern for the welfare of Colorado’s prisoners is both commendable and highly suspect.

Recognizing the Ploy

The reaction was swift and telling. Supporters and detractors of the administration alike saw through the smokescreen. As Will Sommer of The Bulwark aptly noted, this is a direct attempt by Trump to exert pressure on Colorado for Peters’ release—a maneuver made all the more desperate by the fact that Trump can’t pardon Peters due to her being imprisoned on state charges.

This episode arrives hot on the heels of a federal judge’s rejection of Peters’ bid for habeas corpus. Furthermore, it’s revealed that the Justice Department had previously toyed with another harebrained scheme to secure Peters’ release, suggesting she could be a witness in a federal investigation, thereby transferring her to a minimum security facility—a “Club Fed,” akin to the arrangement for Ghislaine Maxwell, Jeffrey Epstein’s sex trafficking accomplice. This plan, opposed by Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, sparked outrage among some Trump supporters, underscoring the internal divisions and the lengths to which Peters’ allies are willing to go.

Conclusion: The Intersection of Politics and Justice

The Tina Peters saga is a stark illustration of how political agendas can distort the pursuit of justice. Dhillon’s move, under the pretense of addressing civil rights violations, raises serious questions about the integrity of the Justice Department’s motivations. It’s a reminder that in the theater of political maneuvering, the line between genuine concern for prisoners’ rights and strategic exploitation of those rights can become dangerously blurred.

This incident should serve as a wake-up call for those who value the principles of justice and democracy. When political agendas take precedence over factual evidence and the rule of law, the very foundations of our democratic institutions are at risk. The actions of the Trump Justice Department in the case of Tina Peters are not just an affront to the state of Colorado; they are an affront to the American people and to the principles upon which this country was built.
