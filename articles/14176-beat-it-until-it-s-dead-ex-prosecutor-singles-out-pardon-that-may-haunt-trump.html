'Beat it until it's dead': Ex-prosecutor singles out pardon that may haunt Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20255 min read

Donald Trump (Photo by Kevin Lamarque for Reuters)

During a recent interview with journalist Jim Acosta (formerly of CNN), former assistant U.S. Attorney Glenn Kirschner — who prosecuted cases in the prestigious District of Columbia office during both the George W. Bush and Barack Obama administrations — argued that the near-constant drumbeat of news from Trump's second term has had a discouraging effect on Americans. However, he said that telling Americans one of Trump's recent pardons has the power to severely damage Trump's credibility with mainstream Americans.

In the segment, Kirschner made the case that the pardon of former nursing home executive Paul Walczak stands out among Trump's other pardons as perhaps the most egregious. And he said Democrats could stand to reap significant political gains in upcoming elections by repeatedly harping on the Walczak case, in which Trump issued a pardon after Walczak's mother attended a $1 million per-plate fundraiser for a Trump-affiliated group.

READ MORE: 'Disturbing': Expert breaks down 'sheer garishness' of Trump's Oval Office makeover

"We have to focus in one thing, and we have to beat it until it's dead, and get the people to understand what it really means," Kirschner said.

Kirschner reminded viewers that Trump repeatedly promised to "protect Social Security" and to "back the blue [law enforcement]" while running for the presidency, before walking through the Walczak case. As the head of a Florida-based healthcare company that ran long-term care facilities, Walczak was convicted of failing to pay employment taxes and failing to file several years of individual tax returns. He was also ordered to pay more than $4 million in restitution. However, Kirschner said that Trump's pardon of Walczak betrayed both of those key campaign promises.

"What he was doing was taking Social Security, taking Medicare, taking Medicaid out of his salary, out of the payments he was making to nurses and healthcare employees, and what was he doing with it? Not delivering it to the United States government as he was required by law to do," Kirschner said. "He is lavishing himself with private jets, he's buying himself a $2 million yacht."

"All of federal and local law enforcement agencies ... 'the blue' investigated these crimes for years," he continued. "Donald Trump pardons him. He doesn't only pardon him from his jail term, he says 'I am killing this court order that requires you to pay back the $4.4 million that you owe the federal government in Social Security.' So let's put it to the test. Protecting Social Security? My a--! Backing the blue? My a--. The blue is the one that investigated and helped bring charges in this case!"

Our Analysis:

The Walczak Pardon: A Case Study in Trump's Betrayal

In a political landscape that often feels like it's spinning out of control, the pardon of former nursing home executive Paul Walczak by Donald Trump has emerged as a particularly galling example of corruption and betrayal. According to former assistant U.S. Attorney Glenn Kirschner, this pardon not only encapsulates Trump's disregard for his own campaign promises but also highlights a wider issue of moral decay within his administration.

Trump's Promises vs. Reality

Trump's campaign was built on a plethora of promises, with "protecting Social Security" and "backing the blue" (law enforcement) being among the key pillars. Yet, the pardon of Walczak, a convicted criminal who embezzled funds meant for Social Security and Medicare, stands as a stark contradiction to these promises. Kirschner's analysis brings to light the audacity of Trump's actions. Walczak didn't just fail to pay taxes; he diverted funds meant for the most vulnerable, using them to fund an opulent lifestyle that included private jets and a $2 million yacht. This act alone should have made him anathema to any administration claiming to protect Social Security and support law enforcement. Instead, Trump's pardon effectively erased Walczak's crimes, including a court order to repay $4.4 million in stolen funds.

The Egregious Nature of the Walczak Pardon

What sets the Walczak pardon apart from Trump's litany of questionable pardons is not just the severity of Walczak's crimes, but the blatant pay-for-play implication. The pardon came after Walczak's mother attended a $1 million per-plate fundraiser for a Trump-affiliated group. This reeks of corruption and is a slap in the face to the rule of law and the principle of justice for all. It's a clear message that, under Trump's administration, justice could be bought and sold.

Democrats' Opportunity

Kirschner suggests that Democrats could seize upon the Walczak case as a means to galvanize political support, and he's not wrong. At its core, this pardon is a litmus test for Trump's credibility and integrity. By focusing on this case, Democrats have the opportunity to highlight not just a singular act of corruption but to frame it within the larger context of Trump's failure to live up to his own promises. It's a narrative that has the potential to resonate with mainstream Americans, especially those who took Trump at his word.

Conclusion: A Betrayal of American Values

The Walczak pardon is emblematic of the systemic issues that plagued Trump's presidency. It wasn't just a betrayal of campaign promises; it was a betrayal of American values. The pardon demonstrates a flagrant disregard for the rule of law, a disdain for the principles of justice and fairness, and a callous indifference to the needs of the most vulnerable in society. Kirschner's call to action is not just about political strategy; it's about holding leaders accountable for their actions. In the case of Trump and the Walczak pardon, it's clear that the former president failed this test spectacularly.
