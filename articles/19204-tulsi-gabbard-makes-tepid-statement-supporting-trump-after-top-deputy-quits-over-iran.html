Tulsi Gabbard makes tepid statement supporting Trump after top deputy quits over Iran

Former Representative Tulsi Gabbard, U.S. President Donald Trump’s nominee to be director of national intelligence, testifies before a Senate Intelligence Committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., January 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard indicated tepid support for President Donald Trump. Still, she did not endorse the war in Iran following the resignation of Joe Kent, one of her top deputies.

“After much reflection, I have decided to resign from my position as Director of the National Counterterrorism Center, effective today,” Kent said in an X post on Tuesday. “I cannot in good conscience support the ongoing war in Iran. Iran posed no imminent threat to our nation, and it is clear that we started this war due to pressure from Israel and its powerful American lobby.”

In a statement hours later, Gabbard spelled out her role in the war.

“Donald Trump was overwhelmingly elected by the American people to be our President and Commander in Chief,” she said. “As our Commander in Chief, he is responsible for determining what is and is not an imminent threat, and whether or not to take action he deems necessary to protect the safety and security of our troops, the American people and our country.”

“The Office of the Director of National Intelligence is responsible for helping coordinate and integrate all intelligence to provide the President and Commander in Chief with the best information available to inform his decisions,” she added. “After carefully reviewing all the information before him, President Trump concluded that the terrorist Islamist regime in Iran posed an imminent threat and he took action based on that conclusion.”

Gabbard, however, was careful not to endorse the war.

Our Analysis:

The Real Story Behind the Tepid Support

In a recent development that has stirred the political cauldron, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard’s lukewarm support for President Donald Trump’s actions regarding Iran, juxtaposed with Joe Kent’s resignation from the National Counterterrorism Center, paints a complex picture of internal discord and external pressures. The narrative requires a careful dissection to identify the locus of power, decision-making, and potential misdirection in framing the responsibilities.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

At the heart of this controversy is the institutional power wielded by President Trump, as the Commander in Chief, and the advisory role played by Tulsi Gabbard as the Director of National Intelligence. The decision to engage in what Kent describes as an “ongoing war in Iran” falls squarely on President Trump. He, as noted by Gabbard, is “responsible for determining what is and is not an imminent threat, and whether or not to take action he deems necessary to protect the safety and security of our troops, the American people and our country.”

This clear delineation of responsibility underscores that the ultimate decision-making power rested with President Trump. Gabbard’s role, as she herself outlined, was to coordinate and integrate intelligence to inform the President’s decisions, not to make those decisions herself.

Misdirection of Responsibility

The article’s framing potentially misdirects responsibility toward Gabbard by spotlighting her tepid support for Trump and her careful avoidance of endorsing the war. While her stance is indeed newsworthy, it inadvertently shifts focus away from the central figure who made the consequential decision—the President. Gabbard’s reticence to endorse the war, juxtaposed with Kent’s resignation and condemnation, might suggest a divergence of opinion within the intelligence community. However, it is critical to remember that Gabbard’s role is advisory, not decisional.

Kent’s resignation and subsequent statement offer a poignant critique of the rationale behind the war, highlighting a perceived lack of imminent threat from Iran and suggesting external pressures influenced the decision to start the conflict. This perspective adds an essential layer to understanding the dynamics at play, suggesting that the decision to engage militarily was not only the prerogative of the President but also potentially subject to external influences.

Conclusion: The Essence of Accountability

In unraveling the narrative, it becomes evident that President Trump, by virtue of his institutional role and decision-making capacity, holds the primary responsibility for the actions taken against Iran. While Gabbard’s and Kent’s positions provide valuable insights into the administration’s internal dynamics and the intelligence community’s perspectives, they do not alter the locus of accountability.

The framing of the article, while capturing the complexity of the situation, should not detract from the fact that in the American political system, the President, as Commander in Chief, bears the ultimate responsibility for military engagements. Gabbard’s non-endorsement and Kent’s resignation underscore moral and ethical considerations within the administration but do not equate to decision-making authority over military actions.

In this light, criticism or support for the war in Iran must be directed appropriately, ensuring that accountability is not misplaced and that the narrative accurately reflects the distribution of power and responsibility within the government.
