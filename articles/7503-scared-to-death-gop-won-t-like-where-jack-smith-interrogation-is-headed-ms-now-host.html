'Scared to death' GOP won't like where Jack Smith interrogation is headed: MS NOW host

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 19, 20254 min read

Jack Smith (MS NOW screenshot)

The decision by House Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan (R-OH) to allow special counsel Jack Smith to testify only in private will come back to haunt Republicans, and things could get worse.

That is the opinion of “Morning Joe” co-host Joe Scarborough as he went on an extensive rant on Thursday morning, where he accused GOP lawmakers of being “scared to death” of what Smith would reveal to the public.

On Wednesday, Smith spoke behind closed doors, where he told lawmakers he had evidence of the president’s criminality.

That led to the MS NOW host’s tirade.

“It seems to me Republicans in the House should be smart enough to understand that they don’t want to catch this runaway truck, because if the dog catches the truck, that is bad news for the Republicans,” Scarborough exclaimed.

“It’s bad news for Donald Trump, and it blows to bits, blows to bits. Their lie about this investigation — they want no part of this.”

“They will not let Jack Smith testify publicly,” he shouted. “Let me say that again: they’re scared to death. They’re scared to death of Jack Smith being in front of a microphone, testifying. Testifying publicly about what he has. And so they need to just shut up and go away on this issue because they’re not going to like where it ends up for the Republican Party. They don’t want the truth out there, because if they did, they would have let Jack Smith testify publicly.”

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Silence: GOP’s Fearful Strategy

In a move that reeks of political cowardice and strategic evasion, House Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan (R-OH) has decided to let special counsel Jack Smith testify only in private, a decision that can only be seen as an attempt to shield the public from potentially damning evidence. This decision has sparked a rightful outrage from “Morning Joe” co-host Joe Scarborough, who didn’t mince words when he accused GOP lawmakers of being “scared to death” of what Smith might reveal. His fiery critique lays bare the underlying fear gnawing at the Republicans: the truth.

Scarborough’s Rant: A Mirror to Republican Anxiety

Scarborough’s extensive tirade on Thursday morning wasn’t just a rant; it was a scalpel slicing through the facade the GOP hoped to maintain. His words, “they’re scared to death of Jack Smith being in front of a microphone,” highlights not just a momentary lapse in judgment but a systemic pattern of avoidance and suppression. By choosing to keep Smith’s testimony private, the GOP has inadvertently admitted to the weight of his evidence, evidence that purportedly points directly at the president’s criminality.

The Consequences of Silence

The decision to muzzle the truth by refusing to let Smith testify publicly is not just an act of political self-preservation; it’s an act of self-sabotage. Scarborough rightly points out the inevitable outcome of this strategy: “It’s bad news for Donald Trump, and it blows to bits, blows to bits. Their lie about this investigation — they want no part of this.” In their desperate attempt to control the narrative, the GOP has merely spotlighted their own panic and desperation. This isn’t just bad optics; it’s political hara-kiri.

“They want no part of this”

Scarborough’s assertion that Republicans “want no part of this” is a damning indictment of a party so entangled in its web of deceit that it cannot face the potential consequences of transparency. The implication here is clear: the GOP is fully aware of the incriminatory nature of the evidence against them, so much so that they would rather stifle its public dissemination than confront it head-on. This is not the behavior of a party confident in its innocence but that of a group terrified of its culpability being laid bare.

The GOP’s Strategic Miscalculation

The GOP’s decision to keep Jack Smith’s testimony under wraps is a strategic miscalculation of epic proportions. In their bid to protect their own, they have instead broadcasted their fears and insecurities for the world to see. This action—or rather, inaction—will not safeguard them from the repercussions of the truth. Instead, it will only amplify the eventual fallout, turning what could have been a controlled burn into a wildfire that threatens to consume them.

In conclusion, the GOP’s attempt to silence Jack Smith is a glaring testament to their dread of transparency and accountability. This decision does not merely reflect a momentary lapse in judgment but underscores a pervasive culture of avoidance within the party. As Scarborough so aptly put it, they are “scared to death,” and rightly so, for the truth, once unleashed, will not discriminate in its devastation. The Republicans would do well to remember: the truth, no matter how deeply buried, will eventually find its way to the surface, and when it does, no amount of political maneuvering will shield them from its consequences.
