The Associated Press called the race for incumbent Mayor Aftab Pureval just under 45 minutes after polls closed Tuesday night with 78 percent of the unofficial vote, compared to just 22 percent for Bowman, with all 190 of the city’s precincts reporting.

That was actually an improvement on Bowman’s performance in the May primary election, when the Republican won the right to appear on the November ballot by placing second with just 13 percent of the vote in the heavily Democratic southwest Ohio city.

The vice president, who has a home in the city’s Walnut Hills neighborhood and grew up in nearby Middletown, tossed a last-minute endorsement toward his half-brother in that election, but spent virtually no political capital on him since then.

“Hey Cincinnati!” Vance posted on X back in May. “My brother Cory Bowman is running for mayor and is on the ballot today for the primary. He’s a good guy with a heart for serving his community. Get out there and vote for him!”

Vance did weigh in on an August brawl in downtown Cincinnati, saying the city’s law enforcement had “allowed lawlessness to run wild,” but did not specifically tie the issue to his half-brother’s long-shot campaign.

“Bowman was the longest of long-shot candidates for mayor since direct election of Cincinnati’s mayor began in 2001,” said WVXU’s Howard Wilkinson, a longtime political columnist. “Despite the drubbing, there are those who believe Bowman may not be done with politics.”

Republicans are potentially looking at Bowman as a candidate for Ohio’s 1st congressional district after a GOP redistricting measure last week flipped Democratic Rep. Greg Landsman’s seat from “leans Democrat” to “leans Republican,” Wilkinson said.

The 36-year-old Bowman, who shares a biological father with the vice president but grew up estranged from the 41-year-old Vance, appears to have used different addresses for his voter registration and his taxes.

He’s a Butler County, Ohio, native who opened a church in Cincinnati’s West End neighborhood affiliated with an evangelical church in Tampa, where he spent most of his adult life.

Our Analysis:

The Vance Family Political Saga: A Closer Look

In the latest episode of the Vance family’s foray into politics, we witness Cory Bowman, half-brother to Vice President JD Vance, face a crushing defeat in Cincinnati’s mayoral race. The results were not just a defeat; they were an outright humiliation, with Bowman securing a mere 22 percent of the vote against incumbent Mayor Aftab Pureval’s overwhelming 78 percent. This debacle unfolded in a city that has shown a strong Democratic leaning, making Bowman’s attempt seem almost quixotic from the start.

A Tale of Two Brothers

It’s interesting to note the dynamic between JD Vance and Cory Bowman. Despite sharing a biological father, the two grew up estranged, marking a complex family backdrop against which this political drama unfolds. Vance, riding high in his role as Vice President, offered a tepid endorsement of Bowman’s mayoral bid—a move that seems more an obligatory familial nod than a serious political endorsement. His involvement, or the conspicuous lack thereof, speaks volumes. Why the reticence? Was it a calculated political move to distance himself from a campaign doomed to fail, or simply a reflection of their personal estrangement? Vance’s half-hearted shoutout on X (formerly Twitter), and his subsequent silence, could be construed as a strategic decision to preserve his own political capital rather than squander it on a lost cause.

The Political Machine Grinds On

Despite the electoral drubbing, political commentators suggest that Bowman’s career might not be over. The Republican party seems to be eyeing him for Ohio’s 1st congressional district, a move that reeks of opportunism. Following a GOP redistricting measure that flipped the seat from “leans Democrat” to “leans Republican,” Bowman is suddenly back in the game. This maneuver is a stark reminder of how redistricting, often criticized as gerrymandering, can manipulate the electoral landscape to favor one party over another.

A Question of Integrity

Bowman’s dual addresses for voter registration and taxes raise eyebrows. This discrepancy, though seemingly minor, points to a larger issue of transparency and integrity in public officials. How can voters trust a candidate who plays fast and loose with the basics of electoral eligibility?

Furthermore, Bowman’s establishment of a church in Cincinnati, affiliated with an evangelical church in Tampa, adds another layer to his political persona. It suggests a blending of religious conviction with political ambition, a combination that has historically been both potent and controversial in American politics.

Conclusion: The Vance-Bowman Political Brand

The Vance-Bowman saga is a microcosm of broader political dynamics: familial connections, opportunistic endorsements, and the shadowy mechanics of redistricting, all against a backdrop of personal estrangement and political ambition. Cory Bowman’s mayoral bid may have ended in a landslide defeat, but it seems the political machinery is already gearing up for his next act.

The question remains: Will Bowman carve out his own political identity, or will he remain in the shadow of his more famous sibling? And more importantly, how will voters respond to a candidate who has already faced significant scrutiny in his first high-profile race? As this saga unfolds, it will be interesting to see whether Bowman can redefine himself or whether he will be perpetually cast as a footnote in his brother’s political narrative.
