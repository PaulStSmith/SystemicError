'Gaslighting the public': MAGA election head in swing state erupts at GOP

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Supporters wearing ‘MAGA’ caps gather ahead of a campaign rally featuring Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump in Grand Rapids, Michigan, U.S., November 4, 2024. REUTERS/Emily Elconin

Tensions within the Republican leadership of Maricopa County, Arizona, have erupted into open conflict, with key figures now battling each other in court over control of local election administration, Phoenix New Times reported Friday.

In a lawsuit filed Thursday in Maricopa County Superior Court, County Recorder Justin Heap — an outspoken election skeptic aligned with the pro-Trump Make America Great Again (MAGA) wing of the GOP— accused the Republican-led Board of Supervisors of undermining his office and overreaching their authority.

Heap alleges the board is consolidating power by withholding funding from his office and absorbing its information technology operations into the county’s broader system, effectively stripping him of his independence.

He claims the board’s moves amount to “gaslighting the public” and obstructing his legally mandated responsibilities.

He is asking the court to order the restoration of his office’s budget and IT staff, per the report.

"From day one, I promised the voters of Maricopa County that I would deliver more secure, more honest, and more transparent elections for all voters, regardless of political party," Heap said in a media release.

"Similarly, I have promised to utilize every tool under state and federal law, including Arizona’s judicial system, to accomplish these very popular objectives.”

But Board chairman Thomas Galvin and vice chairwoman Kate Brophy McGee fired back, calling the suit “absurd” and “frivolous.”

They argue Heap misunderstands the legal boundaries of his role and is pushing a partisan agenda outside his authority.

The legal clash highlights growing fractures within the Republican Party as internal power struggles increasingly spill into public view — especially over the contentious issue of election integrity.

Our Analysis:

Maricopa County's GOP Meltdown: A Scathing Analysis

In the latest chapter of the Republican soap opera unfolding in Maricopa County, Arizona, the spectacle of intra-party warfare has taken a dramatic leap from the backrooms of political maneuvering straight into the courthouse. This isn't just a local skirmish over administrative duties; it's a microcosm of the broader existential crisis gnawing at the foundations of the GOP, particularly around the incendiary issue of election integrity.

At the heart of this debacle is County Recorder Justin Heap, a figure who has not only drunk the MAGA Kool-Aid but seems to be attempting to brew his own batch. Heap's lawsuit against the Republican-led Board of Supervisors is not just a legal battle; it's a bold declaration of the ideological chasm slicing through the party. By accusing the Board of "gaslighting the public" and obstructing his "legally mandated responsibilities," Heap isn't merely lamenting operational grievances; he's fashioning himself as a martyr in the MAGA crusade for "more secure, more honest, and more transparent elections."

Heap's rhetoric, steeped in the language of election skepticism, mirrors the broader MAGA movement's strategy of sowing doubt about electoral processes and outcomes. It's a dangerous game that undermines public trust in democracy, yet it's become a staple among certain GOP factions. The irony here is palpable: Heap accuses the Board of Supervisors of "consolidating power" and stripping him of independence, yet his actions and affiliations suggest a willingness to erode the very foundation of democratic integrity in service of a partisan agenda.

The response from Board chairman Thomas Galvin and vice chairwoman Kate Brophy McGee was swift and scathing, labeling the suit "absurd" and "frivolous." This isn't just a retort; it's a clear indication that not all Republicans are willing to ride the MAGA wave to the shores of conspiracy theories and election denialism. Galvin and McGee's pushback is a reminder that there are still voices within the party who recognize the perilous path Heap and his ilk are treading.

What we're witnessing in Maricopa County is a microcosm of the Republican Party's broader identity crisis. The legal clash between Heap and the Board of Supervisors underscores the tension between those who seek to perpetuate the myth of a stolen election and those who are desperately trying to steer the party back to a semblance of sanity. This isn't just about control over local election administration; it's about the soul of the GOP and, by extension, the health of American democracy.

As this drama unfolds, one thing is clear: the Republican Party is at a crossroads, with Maricopa County serving as both battleground and bellwether. The outcome of this legal spat will likely resonate far beyond the confines of county government, signaling either a step back from the brink or a further descent into the quagmire of conspiracy and conflict. The stakes couldn't be higher, and the eyes of the nation are watching.
