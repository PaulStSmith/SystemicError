'How you lose a race': GOP airs 'serious concern' as Democrats make waves in red states

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The GOP’s Worrying Pre-Election TremorsThe GOP’s Strategic MisstepsA Wake-Up Call

Aftyn Behn, Democratic nominee and Tennessee state representative, is hoping to flip a Republican House seat to represent Tennessee’s 7th District in a special election against GOP candidate Matt Van Epps. (Courtesy of Aftyn Behn)

Republican Party officials have a “serious concern” that Democrats could make huge gains in typically red states.

Lower polling numbers than expected for the GOP and candidates who are, according to insiders, not taking their races seriously, are worrying Republicans running election campaigns this year. A special election Tuesday in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District could be an indicator of how the 2026 midterms will turn out — and GOP members worry it’s leaning towards a Democrat sweep.

Democrat House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries suggested the party had been “over-performing” in parts of the country that Trump’s administration had flipped in the 2024 election.

He told NOTUS, “Democrats have been over-performing the 2024 Trump numbers since the very beginning of his presidency, starting in late January in Iowa, where we flipped a district that Donald Trump had just won.”

While Republican Matt Van Epps is projected to win the race for the Tennessee state representative seat, Democratic candidate Aftyn Behn is reportedly closer than the GOP had hoped. In some races, however, the Republican Party has slipped well behind.

A strategist speaking on Republican Andy Ogles’ campaign suggested that the financial gap between how much cash the former Maury County mayor had raised and how much the Democrat candidate had raised is now a bridge too far.

An unnamed strategist said, “I mean, he’s raising like no money. You can’t just lie around and do nothing. That’s how you lose any race — primary or general.” Other GOP strategists have a “very serious concern” over how Ogles is running his campaign for reelection.

Rep. Richard Hudson, chair of the National Republican Congressional Committee, backed the anonymous strategist and said the strength of a campaign comes from its financial backing.

He said, “But obviously, the more money you have in your campaign account, the stronger you are, the more opportunity you have to communicate with the voters. You might have raised a red flag for me.”

Gov. Andy Beshear (D) would suggest the flip of Trump voters from red to blue will appear in the midterms thanks to the president’s “betrayal” of his voters.

Beshear said, “So when you look at that flip of Trump voters, it’s both that our candidates were more focused on where people are right now — I’m trying to make their lives a little bit easier and a little bit better — and Donald Trump has betrayed them with the way he’s governed this last year.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP’s Worrying Pre-Election Tremors

In a rather telling revelation, Republican Party officials have sounded the alarm over what appears to be a looming catastrophe for the GOP in traditionally red states. This concern, ostensibly rooted in lower-than-expected polling numbers and a lackluster approach to campaigning by GOP candidates, underscores a deeper crisis within the party.

The upcoming special election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District is being viewed as a canary in the coal mine for the 2026 midterms. The palpable anxiety among GOP members that this could foretell a Democrat sweep is telling. Democrat House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries’ remarks that Democrats have been “over-performing” in areas previously won by Trump in the 2024 election only adds salt to the wound. The narrative that the GOP’s stronghold on certain districts is slipping is not just embarrassing; it’s a stark indicator of a party in turmoil.

Matt Van Epps is projected to win the race for the Tennessee state representative seat, yet the margin by which he’s leading over Democratic candidate Aftyn Behn is reportedly narrower than the GOP had hoped. This scenario isn’t isolated. The GOP’s financial woes, as highlighted by the campaign struggles of Republican Andy Ogles, only serve to paint a picture of a party at odds with not just its base, but also with the reality of modern campaigning. The admission by an unnamed strategist and Rep. Richard Hudson, chair of the National Republican Congressional Committee, that financial backing plays a crucial role in campaign strength is a blatant acknowledgment of their precarious position.

Governor Andy Beshear’s comments shed light on a profound shift in voter sentiment, suggesting that the flip of Trump voters from red to blue in the midterms could be attributed to the president’s “betrayal” of his voters. Beshear’s analysis points to a Democratic strategy that’s more in tune with the immediate needs and concerns of voters, as opposed to the perceived failures and betrayals of the Trump administration.

The GOP’s Strategic Missteps

The GOP’s predicament is not just a cautionary tale of poor polling numbers or inadequate campaign financing; it’s a reflection of a party struggling to adapt to the evolving political landscape. The reliance on past victories and the apparent underestimation of the Democratic strategy have placed the GOP in a defensive position, scrambling to retain control in traditionally red states.

The financial disparities between GOP candidates and their Democratic counterparts underscore a broader issue of engagement and appeal. The Republican Party’s inability to galvanize its base and secure substantial campaign funding hints at a deeper disconnect with voters. This is not merely a case of complacency, but a systemic failure to recognize and adapt to the shifting priorities of the electorate.

Moreover, the Republican Party’s apparent shock at the Democrats’ over-performance in areas previously won by Trump reveals a significant miscalculation of the political climate. The assumption that previous victories would translate into continued success has led to a complacency that’s now haunting the GOP.

A Wake-Up Call

The GOP’s current state of alarm should serve as a wake-up call. It’s a stark reminder that political landscapes are perpetually in flux, and past victories are no guarantee of future success. The party’s inability to effectively campaign, coupled with a failure to resonate with the evolving concerns of voters, has set the stage for what could be a significant shift in the 2026 midterms.

In light of these developments, the Republican Party must reassess its strategies, priorities, and engagement with voters. The upcoming special election in Tennessee’s 7th Congressional District will not only be a test of the GOP’s current standing but also an indicator of the party’s ability to adapt and respond to the challenges ahead. Without significant introspection and adjustment, the GOP risks not just losing traditionally red states, but also its relevance in an ever-changing political arena.
