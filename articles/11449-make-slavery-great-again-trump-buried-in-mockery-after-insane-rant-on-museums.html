'Make slavery great again!' Trump buried in mockery after 'insane' rant on museums

President Donald Trump was roasted online Tuesday after he bemoaned that museums only show the negatives of slavery.

Trump attacked "woke" museums for addressing the troubling history of the United States, particularly the kidnapping of people of color from Africa, their forced labor, and the torture and rape they endured.

"Trump thinks we've done enough remembering about how bad slavery was. If you've doubted his racism and inhumanity - you can stop doubting now," wrote former host Keith Olbermann on X.

"Slavery was very, very, very bad, and I hope that view continues to be reflected in our national museums," said deputy opinion editor James Hohmann at the Washington Post.

"Why doesn't the Smithsonian talk more about how (sic) the upsides of slavery?" mimicked journalist Aaron Rupar.

"Trump wants to cover up slavery like it’s in the Epstein files," former campaign reporter and White House correspondent Sam Youngman.

Legal analyst and Substacker Aaron Parnas questioned Trump's demand that museums talk about "the Future."

"Also, why would museums talk about the future?" Parnas questioned.

The group Republicans Against Trump called the claim "Insane."

"So now he wants to force them to discuss how 'good' slavery was?" questioned ex-Republican Rep. Joe Walsh (IL).

Professor of human rights law Steve Peers mocked, "MAKE SLAVERY GREAT AGAIN!"

"Dear Democrats, the president of the United States being pro slavery is a 'kitchen table issue' to this Black man," said reporter Oliver Willis.

Others linked Trump's comments to Jeffrey Epstein's "sex slavery," alleging that Trump was a supporter of it while he was palling around with the man.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Distorted Vision of History: A Scathing Review

In a bewildering display of historical ignorance and insensitivity, President Donald Trump has once again ignited the flames of controversy. This time, his remarks lamenting the focus of museums on the grim realities of slavery have not only showcased a glaring disregard for the suffering endured by millions but also a dangerous inclination towards whitewashing the darkest chapters of American history.

Trump's lamentation about museums showcasing the "negatives" of slavery is not just a casual remark; it is a deeply troubling manifestation of a mindset that seeks to minimize, if not outright deny, the atrocities of the past. The backlash from various quarters, including journalists, legal analysts, and politicians, underscores the collective outrage and disbelief at such a revisionist perspective.

The Outcry: A Unified Front Against Revisionism

Keith Olbermann's assertion that Trump's comments should erase any doubts about his "racism and inhumanity" is not an overreaction but a stark realization. When a leader of the free world exhibits such a blatant disregard for historical truth, it calls into question the moral compass guiding their leadership.

James Hohmann's hope that the heinousness of slavery continues to be reflected in our national museums is a reminder of the role these institutions play in educating future generations. They are not merely repositories of art and artifacts but are guardians of historical truth, ensuring that the horrors of the past are not sanitized but presented in their full, unvarnished reality.

Aaron Rupar and Sam Youngman's scathing sarcasm, likening Trump's demands to discussions of the "upsides" of slavery or comparing them to the secrecy of the Epstein files, highlights the absurdity and danger of attempting to rewrite history to suit a more palatable narrative.

Aaron Parnas and Joe Walsh's incredulity at Trump's suggestion for museums to focus on the "Future" while simultaneously glossing over the brutal realities of slavery, encapsulates the broader concern of allowing such narratives to gain traction.

Steve Peers' mocking of Trump's stance as an attempt to "MAKE SLAVERY GREAT AGAIN!" and Oliver Willis's poignant reminder that the president's stance on slavery is indeed a "kitchen table issue" for African Americans, are powerful rebukes of Trump's revisionism.

Concluding Thoughts: The Danger Ahead

The reactions to Trump's comments reflect a society deeply divided but also a segment that remains fiercely committed to truth and historical integrity. The attempt to sanitize the horrors of slavery, to seek nonexistent "upsides," is not only intellectually dishonest but morally reprehensible.

As we navigate these tumultuous times, it is imperative that we remain vigilant against attempts to rewrite history, to diminish the suffering of those who came before us. The path to a more just and equitable society lies not in the erasure of uncomfortable truths but in confronting them, learning from them, and ensuring they are never repeated.

In the end, Trump's remarks and the ensuing outcry should serve as a clarion call to all who value historical accuracy and moral clarity. The fight for the soul of our nation is not just about policy or politics; it's about preserving the truth of our collective past, no matter how painful.
