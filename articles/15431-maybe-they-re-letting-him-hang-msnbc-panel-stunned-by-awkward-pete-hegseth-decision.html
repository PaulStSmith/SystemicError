'Maybe they're letting him hang': MSNBC panel stunned by 'awkward' Pete Hegseth decision

A major announcement by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth that he is canceling the Women Peace and Security program at the Defense Department that was instituted during Donald Trump's first term left an MSNBC panel scratching their heads Wednesday morning.

According to a report from Politico on Tuesday, Hegseth described the program in a post on X as: "yet another woke divisive/social justice/Biden initiative that overburdens our commanders and troops — distracting from our core task: WAR-FIGHTING."

However, as the report noted, "Trump signed the program into law and multiple members of Trump’s current cabinet backed the effort as members of Congress."

As the "Morning Joe" panel noted, this is yet another misstep by the embattled Hegseth who was given a lukewarm endorsement by Trump in a recent interview with The Atlantic this week with the president admitting, "I think he's gonna get it together."

Noting his latest cancellation of another program, co-host Mika Brzezinski, pointed out, "It seems awkward, but you know, Pete, he doesn't believe women should serve in the military or was, you know, has said that at one point and –– doesn't want that program and that is just awkward."

"Did he not have some process or staff member who said, 'Hey boss, you should just know this was the origin of this?'" MSNBC contributor Richard Haas added with a laugh. "I mean, there's a pattern here you announce policies and afterwards you do the homework as opposed to the other way around."

"You know, maybe they're letting him hang, maybe they knew exactly what he was doing," Brzezinski suggested.

"Look, I mean, I think we're all waiting on Pete Hegseth to show some evidence of competence," conservative columnist David French later added, "Just some, I mean, we have this pattern that we have seen from the beginning, which is he face plants publicly in some dramatic way, and then he runs out and does push-ups with the troops to sort of show that he's a man of the people. Then he comes back out and face plants again and got to run and go do burpees with the troops, this time to sort of show he's a man of the people and just this, this cycle. I think it is growing exhausting for some people within the Trump inner circle."

Our Analysis:

Hegseth's Folly: A Masterclass in Incompetence and Irony

In a move that can only be described as a tragicomic blend of irony and ineptitude, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the cancellation of the Women Peace and Security program at the Defense Department, a program initiated during the reign of none other than Donald Trump. This decision has left many, including a bewildered MSNBC panel, grappling with the sheer absurdity of the situation.

Hegseth's Justification: A Paradox Wrapped in an Oxymoron
Hegseth took to X to lambast the program as a "woke divisive/social justice/Biden initiative," seemingly oblivious to the fact that it was Trump who signed the program into law, with support from members of his cabinet. The irony is so thick here you could cut it with a knife. Hegseth's attempt to paint the program as a product of Biden's administration is not just misleading; it's an outright fabrication, highlighting a stunning lack of research and understanding.

The MSNBC Panel's Bewilderment: A Sign of the Times
The reaction from the MSNBC panel, ranging from confusion to outright laughter, underscores the absurdity of Hegseth's actions. Mika Brzezinski's commentary on Hegseth's known stance against women serving in the military further illuminates the convoluted logic behind canceling a program aimed at empowering women in peace and security roles. It's as if Hegseth is trying to erase his own administration's legacy in a bid to combat so-called "wokeness."

A Pattern of Incompetence: Hegseth's Modus Operandi
What's perhaps most alarming is the evident pattern of haphazard decision-making without due diligence, as noted by MSNBC contributor Richard Haas. This shoot-first-ask-questions-later approach to governance is not just irresponsible; it's dangerous, particularly when it comes to matters of defense.

David French's characterization of Hegseth's cyclical pattern of public faceplants followed by attempts to appear relatable by doing push-ups with the troops is both hilarious and sad. It's a stark reminder of the performative aspect of Hegseth's public persona, where physical displays of masculinity are somehow expected to compensate for glaring deficiencies in leadership and critical thinking.

Conclusion: Beyond the Faceplants

This episode is more than just another gaffe by an embattled Trump appointee; it's a microcosm of the larger systemic issues plaguing the current administration. The cancellation of the Women Peace and Security program under false pretenses is not just an isolated incident of incompetence; it's indicative of a broader disdain for facts, expertise, and the legacy of one's own party.

As Hegseth presumably prepares for his next set of push-ups, one can't help but wonder: when will the performative politics end? When will substance take precedence over spectacle? If Hegseth's latest blunder is any indication, we might be waiting a while.
