'So cringe': Trump dragged over 'creepy' Christmas card

Public reaction to a recent Christmas card greeting posted on the White House’s official X account probably isn’t what President Donald Trump was expecting.

A graphic of Trump giving his signature thumbs up, along with the words “Daddy’s Home,” accompanies the White House’s exuberant greeting of “HOME FOR THE HOLIDAYS!” posted late Wednesday night.

The phrase “daddy’s home“ has been adopted by Trump’s supporters and used in official White House communications as a catchphrase and meme, particularly during the holiday season and following a NATO summit event in 2025.

But not everyone is embracing it, with many posting tweaked versions of the original showing, among other things, Trump sleeping and Vice President JD Vance on his lap dressed as an elf.

Daily Kos staff reporter Emily C. Singer shared it on X, saying, “This is so f—— weird.”

Geopolitical commentator Marcus d’Osint posted, “I don’t think this appropriate considering a bunch of evidence out of the Epstein files just dropped.”

A retired pastor who goes by Dr. Mike on X used Elon Musk’s AI tool Grok to construct his reply, saying, “GROK’s ADVICE: ‘IT Would NOT be ADVISABLE to ENTRUST YOUR DAUGHTER to TRUMP!’ ‘His comments about women, including inappropriate remarks about Ivanka, such as calling her a ‘great piece of ass’ & saying he ‘might date her if she weren’t his daughter’, raise major concerns.”

OTC trading platform Unich.com wondered, “Did the White House just drop a Christmas album cover?”

Over on Instagram, comments range from “soooo cringe” and “creepy” to “creep,” and “gag,” while others wondered if that home meant “the nursing home?”

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Presidential Decorum: A Holiday Spectacle

The latest holiday greeting from the White House, featuring President Donald Trump with his thumb up and the phrase “Daddy’s Home,” is a vivid illustration of the ongoing erosion of presidential decorum. This greeting, splashed across the White House’s official X account, encapsulates the bizarre fusion of pop culture memes with official state communication. It’s not just weird; it’s a stark departure from any semblance of traditional presidential behavior.

The Cult of Personality: “Daddy’s Home”

The phrase “Daddy’s Home” has morphed from a supporter’s chant into an official White House catchphrase, a transition that is as alarming as it is absurd. This catchphrase, particularly its use in conjunction with a Christmas greeting, is emblematic of the cult of personality that has enveloped Trump’s presidency. It’s a troubling sign when the leader of the free world is reduced to a meme, celebrated not for policy achievements but for his ability to dominate the news cycle with the bizarre and the controversial.

Public Backlash: From Satire to Concern

The public’s reaction to this Christmas card ranges from satire to outright concern, illustrating the profound divisiveness of Trump’s approach to presidency. Emily C. Singer’s reaction, “This is so f—— weird,” is a succinct summary of the collective bewilderment. Meanwhile, geopolitical commentator Marcus d’Osint raises a valid point about the inappropriateness of such frivolity amid serious allegations linked to Trump, highlighted by recent revelations from the Epstein files. The use of humor to deflect or diminish the gravity of such concerns is a dangerous precedent.

The response from a retired pastor, utilizing Elon Musk’s AI tool Grok, underscores the deep discomfort many feel about Trump’s past comments on women, including his own daughter. This discomfort is not just a matter of political disagreement but speaks to fundamental issues of character and morality.

The Misfired Metaphor

The attempt to liken Trump’s presidency or his return to the White House to a father returning home for the holidays is a misfired metaphor that fails to resonate with a significant portion of the American public. Instead of instilling a sense of comfort or leadership, it conjures images of divisiveness and controversy. The varied responses on social media, likening the greeting to everything from a cringe-worthy attempt at humor to suggestions of senility (”the nursing home?”), reflect a broader unease with the current state of presidential communication.

Conclusion: The Dangers of Demeaning the Office

This holiday greeting is not just a one-off oddity; it’s a symptom of a larger problem. When the office of the president is reduced to memes and catchphrases, it undermines the gravity and dignity of the position. It’s a dangerous game, turning serious political discourse into fodder for internet jokes and polarizing commentary. The role of the president, especially in their communication, should be to unite, to inspire, and to lead, not to descend into the arena of pop culture memes and divisive rhetoric.

The White House’s decision to embrace “Daddy’s Home” as a holiday greeting is a misstep that highlights a broader issue: the need for a return to a more respectful, dignified, and unifying form of presidential communication. As we move forward, one can only hope for a shift away from this divisive triviality towards a more serious and respectful discourse befitting the office.
