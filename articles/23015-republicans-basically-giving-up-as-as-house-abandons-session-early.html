Republicans 'basically giving up' as as House abandons session early

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 4, 20262 min read

A Majority That Governs by Avoidance

The Choice Was the Story

The House is not drifting into paralysis. The House GOP is steering it there. Republicans control the House, the Senate, and the White House, and they still chose to cut off the last two weeks of September session, stretching the pre-election break into seven weeks. That is not incapacity. It is a governing decision.

Power Held, Power Withheld

When one party holds the institutional levers, “do nothing” stops being a description and becomes a tactic. The majority decides what gets a vote, what gets delayed, and what gets buried until everyone has moved on. In this case, House Republicans made the schedule change, then used the calendar to manufacture inactivity and call it normal business.

The result is measurable. The 119th Congress has landed among the lowest-output sessions on record, with only 30 public laws passed in 2026 and 64 laws enacted as of September 3. That is the footprint of a majority that has the power to legislate and is choosing not to.

Misdirection by Calendar

The usual trick here is to hide political cowardice inside procedural language. A schedule change sounds administrative. A vote pause sounds routine. A seven-week campaign break sounds like governance has merely slowed down. It has not. It has been deliberately throttled.

The source makes clear that the House GOP is not responding to some external blockage. It is creating the blockage itself. That matters because it exposes the central lie of this Congress: the idea that Republican leaders are overwhelmed by the demands of governing rather than actively avoiding them.

The Record Is Dysfunction, Not Discipline

The people defending this kind of collapse like to dress it up as fiscal restraint, hardball, or strategic patience. But a legislature that cannot or will not meet is not disciplined. It is unserious. When floor time is lost at historic levels, the loss is not abstract. Bills die. Oversight dies. Public obligations get shoved aside so members can spend more time insulating themselves from accountability.

That is why the Protect College Sports Act is stuck in the House until at least mid-November, if the Senate ever moves it. The point is not that one bill is delayed. The point is that a majority with complete control is treating lawmaking as optional.

The Real Pattern

This is what modern Republican governance looks like when it is not wrapped in culture-war theater: hoarding power, refusing responsibility, and stretching the calendar until the work disappears. They campaign against “not working,” oppose paid leave and social protections, then lean on taxpayer-funded salaries and healthcare while producing as little as possible.

The larger political pattern is simple. Authoritarian-leaning politics does not always announce itself with dramatic crackdowns. Sometimes it shows up as institutional sabotage, organized laziness, and a ruling party that would rather abandon the job than use power in a way that might require public accountability.
