Trump setting up GOP for 'landslide' loss with eye-popping plan: conservative scholar

President Donald Trump’s latest threat to extend mass deportations to naturalized U.S. citizens would lead Republicans into disaster in the midterms, a conservative legal scholar warned on Friday.

Trump’s proposal came in a rant on Thanksgiving, in response to the shooting of National Guard troops in Washington, D.C., allegedly carried out by an Afghan national who was granted a special immigrant visa for helping the U.S. military and CIA.

“I will permanently pause migration from all Third World Countries to allow the U.S. system to fully recover, terminate all of the millions of Biden illegal admissions ... end all Federal benefits and subsidies to noncitizens of our Country, denaturalize migrants who undermine domestic tranquility, and deport any Foreign National who is a public charge, security risk, or non-compatible with Western Civilization,” said Trump.

“These goals will be pursued with the aim of achieving a major reduction in illegal and disruptive populations, including those admitted through an unauthorized and illegal Autopen approval process,” Trump continued. “Only REVERSE MIGRATION can fully cure this situation.”

“Denaturalize” means stripping citizenship from immigrants who have already been granted it, potentially years or decades ago — and that will lead to utter chaos that will get the GOP wiped out in elections, wrote Daniel Di Martino, a conservative Manhattan Institute scholar who fled tyranny in his homeland.

“If legal immigrants begin to be deported without having committed any crime, get ready for Republicans to lose the midterms by a landslide,” Martino wrote on X. “In addition to the havoc and economic destruction a large travel ban would cause ahead of the World Soccer Cup next year.”

“There are ways to do this, and ways not to,” he continued. Rather than “retroactively harming people,” he argued, it would be better to reform regulations that determine whether an immigrant is a “public charge,” or net recipient of federal assistance, during the naturalization process.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of Trump’s Latest Immigration Rant

In what reads like a dystopian policy proposal, President Donald Trump has once again ignited a firestorm of controversy with his latest tirade against immigrants. This time, his crosshairs are trained not just on illegal immigration but also on the very foundation of American citizenship: naturalized U.S. citizens. Trump’s Thanksgiving rant, ostensibly sparked by the tragic shooting of National Guard troops, spiraled into a diatribe that threatened to reshape the landscape of American society and politics.

The Proposal: A Recipe for Disaster

Trump’s proposal is as sweeping as it is draconian. He vows to “permanently pause migration from all Third World Countries,” “terminate all of the millions of Biden illegal admissions,” and shockingly, “denaturalize migrants who undermine domestic tranquility.” The implications of such policies are not just troubling; they are profoundly undemocratic.

The notion of denaturalizing citizens, in particular, is a stark departure from fundamental American values. Citizenship, once granted, is a sacrosanct bond with the nation, a testament to its welcoming promise. To retroactively strip individuals of their citizenship because they are deemed to “undermine domestic tranquility” is a concept so vague and susceptible to abuse that it’s bewildering it’s being considered by a former president.

The Fallout: Economic and Political

Daniel Di Martino, a conservative legal scholar, issues a stern warning: pursue this path and prepare for a GOP catastrophe at the midterms. The economic havoc a large travel ban could wreak, especially ahead of an international event like the World Soccer Cup, is substantial. But beyond the economic ramifications lies a deeper, more insidious threat: the erosion of the rule of law and the principles of justice.

Martino’s critique underscores a critical flaw in Trump’s proposal: the retroactive punishment of individuals who have lived, contributed to, and integrated into American society. It’s a policy that reeks of authoritarianism, not democracy.

A Path Forward?

Martino suggests a more measured approach would involve reforming the regulations surrounding the “public charge” determination in the naturalization process. It’s a rational stance, emphasizing reform over retribution. Yet, one can’t help but ponder whether such suggestions will fall on deaf ears, given the extremity of Trump’s rhetoric.

The Bigger Picture

Trump’s rant is not just an isolated outburst; it’s symptomatic of a broader, more distressing trend towards nativism and xenophobia within certain segments of American politics. The targeting of naturalized citizens, the backbone of the nation’s immigrant narrative, is a troubling escalation.

In Conclusion:

Trump’s proposal, as outlined, is not just a political misfire that could doom the GOP in the midterms; it is a fundamental assault on the principles of fairness, justice, and democracy. The very idea of denaturalizing citizens, of eroding the sanctity of the naturalization process, should be anathema to anyone who values the ideals upon which the United States was founded.

As this discussion unfolds, one must remember that the strength of a nation lies not in its ability to exclude and expel, but in its capacity to embrace and integrate. Let’s hope wisdom prevails over rhetoric before the fabric of the nation is irreparably torn.
