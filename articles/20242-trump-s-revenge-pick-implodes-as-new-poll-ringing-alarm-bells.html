Trump's revenge pick implodes as new poll 'ringing alarm bells'

Trump Picked a Symbol of Rot, Not a Candidate

The Poll Is the Symptom

A New York Times/Siena poll has Paxton tied with Democrat James Talarico in Texas, with only 38 percent of likely voters saying Paxton has “good character.” That is the surface story. The real story is that a major party, and Donald Trump in particular, elevated a man whose political identity is scandal, grievance, and self-protection, then acted surprised when the public noticed.

Power Chose This

The institutional power here belongs to Trump and the Republican machine that followed him. Trump backed Paxton over longtime Sen. John Cornyn in the primary, which means this was not a random candidacy drifting through the system. It was a deliberate choice to reward a loyalist with a documented trail of corruption allegations, impeachment, and public embarrassment.

That is how power works in this era: it does not merely tolerate misconduct, it promotes it when the misconduct is politically useful.

Misdirection as a Governing Style

The coverage can easily drift into horse-race language, as if the problem is simply whether Paxton is “in trouble” or whether Democrats might flip a seat. That framing softens what is actually happening. The issue is not volatility in the poll. It is the decision to treat scandal as a manageable branding problem instead of a disqualifying record.

Paxton was impeached by Texas Republicans over fraud and obstruction of justice allegations. He has been criticized for a “constant stream of scandals.” He has also been publicly associated with lavish travel and a personal life that reinforces the sense of elite impunity. None of that is confusion. It is consequence.

Who Enabled the Outcome

Paxton did not build this alone. Trump amplified him. Texas Republicans advanced him far enough to make him a serious Senate contender. The state’s political class has already shown that it will entertain serious misconduct if the right faction benefits.

That is the enabling structure: a party that claims discipline, but actually enforces loyalty; a donor-and-faction system that rewards hardline alignment; and a media environment that translates corruption into campaign chatter instead of institutional failure.

Talarico’s Attack Lands Because the Record Is Ugly

Talarico’s message on X works because it points directly at the contrast the electorate can see: Paxton as a man accused of serving himself, not the state. Whether every flourish of the attack is election rhetoric or hard fact, the underlying political point is straightforward. Paxton’s career has been built around protecting Paxton.

That is the kind of candidate parties nominate when they decide that identity and fealty matter more than credibility.

Corruption Is the Point

This race is not an accident of bad polling. It is a demonstration of how the modern Republican coalition normalizes personal corruption when it is attached to movement loyalty. The scandal is not a distraction from the politics. It is the politics.

When a party keeps promoting damaged, self-dealing figures and then asks voters to pretend the damage is incidental, it is not suffering from bad judgment. It is operating a system that converts institutional power into protection for the worst people available.
