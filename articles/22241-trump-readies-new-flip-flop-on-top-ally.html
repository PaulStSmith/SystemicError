Trump readies new flip-flop on top ally

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Tariffs as Extortion, Diplomacy as Cover

One Man Holds the Lever

The reporting says a U.S.-Canada tariff deal is sitting on Donald Trump’s desk, with a 50 percent duty set to hit unless he signs off on relief. That is the whole power structure in one sentence: one president, one arbitrary deadline, one threatened blow to a neighboring economy.

The article presents this as a negotiation. More honestly, it is coercion with paperwork.

Canada Is Not the Aggressor Here

Trump has already spent months alienating Canada, from the “51st state” taunt to meddling around an Alberta secession fantasy. Now the same White House is pressing Ottawa to roll back retaliatory measures while asking for concessions on autos and dairy. That is not diplomacy between equals. It is a stronger government demanding tribute after starting the fight.

The framing matters because it can blur who created the crisis. Canada did not invent this tariff war. Trump did. Ottawa is responding to damage already done.

The Article Soft-Pedals the Damage

The piece treats the possible deal as a sign of “conciliatory” movement. That is too polite for what is happening. Trump threatens a 50 percent duty, then asks the other side to bargain down the consequences. Calling that conciliation sanitizes a method that is closer to hostage-taking than statecraft.

The real story is not whether Trump can be persuaded to stop. It is that the U.S. president has turned tariff policy into a private pressure campaign, with allies, industries, and consumers forced to absorb the instability.

Uncertainty Is the Weapon

The economist quoted in the source gets to the heart of it: investment depends on predictable costs and demand, and Trump’s tariff regime destroys that predictability. That is not collateral damage. It is the mechanism. Uncertainty is how this kind of power works. It makes businesses hesitate, disrupts planning, and forces everyone else to price in the possibility of presidential whim.

The damage is bilateral, but the accountability is not. A trade system built around sudden punishment serves one political ego and punishes two economies.

The Pattern Behind the Noise

This is the familiar authoritarian trick in market clothing: manufacture chaos, then present yourself as the only person who can relieve it. The press is often drawn to the drama of “talks” and “deals,” but the governing reality is simpler. Trump escalates. Others scramble. He decides whether relief exists.

That is the political meaning of this story. Not trade policy. Not brinkmanship. A presidency using economic leverage to normalize obedience, while the costs are spread across workers, consumers, and allied countries that had no role in creating the crisis.
