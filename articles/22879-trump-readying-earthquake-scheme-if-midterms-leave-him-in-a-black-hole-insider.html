Trump readying earthquake scheme if midterms leave him in a 'black hole': insider

Trump Is Preparing to Make Oversight Useless

Power Sits With the Executive

The reporting here is not about a vague constitutional “crisis.” It is about a president who still commands the machinery of the executive branch and may use that machinery to nullify oversight if Democrats retake Congress. The key fact is simple: subpoenas, inquiries, and contempt referrals mean very little if the administration refuses to cooperate.

That is the center of gravity in this story. Trump is not reacting passively to accountability. He is reportedly preparing to preempt it.

The Plan Is Obstruction, Then Pardons

According to the source, the scheme has two parts: pardon people who might face congressional scrutiny, and instruct administration officials to stonewall investigations. That is not confusion, and it is not chaos in the ordinary sense. It is a deliberate effort to turn executive power into a shield against legislative review.

The reporting makes clear who enables the outcome: Trump, his officials, and an administration already described as having a propensity to disobey court orders. If those officials refuse to comply, then the investigative process collapses into paper threats and referrals that go nowhere.

Democrats Are Not the Story Here

The article’s framing risks sliding toward procedural theater, as if the main drama were what Democrats will subpoena or how forcefully they will message impeachment. That is not the decisive question. The decisive question is whether the executive branch obeys the law when it becomes inconvenient.

Democrats may initiate inquiries, but they do not control the agencies that would be required to respond. The power imbalance is the story. Treating this as a contest between equally matched players obscures the basic fact that one side controls the machinery being investigated.

The Real Target Is Accountability Itself

The reported list of likely subpoenas is revealing: financial records, inauguration attendees, CEOs, family networks, and people “within a certain radius of the Oval Office.” That is the outline of a political economy, not a handful of isolated misdeeds. It points to the connective tissue between political power, wealth, and immunity.

Trump’s reported move to pardon ahead of inquiry is the clearest possible admission of what this system protects. The point is not to answer allegations. The point is to make consequences vanish before they can attach.

Institutional Cowardice Is Built In

The mention of Attorney General Todd Blanche is doing a lot of work here. If contempt referrals land with an attorney general unlikely to move aggressively, then the enforcement layer is already compromised. That is not a bug in the system Trump is exploiting. It is the system as designed under capture: accountability routed through loyalists, then quietly buried.

This is how authoritarian behavior advances inside formally democratic institutions. It does not always arrive with a dramatic break. Sometimes it arrives as noncompliance, delay, legal nullification, and the steady conversion of law into a suggestion.

The Pattern Is Clear

The larger pattern is executive impunity backed by institutional weakness. Trump sees Congress as something to outlast, not answer to. He sees pardons as a firewall, subpoenas as theater, and loyal officials as the real enforcement mechanism. That is why the reporting matters: it shows a presidency preparing not merely to govern, but to immunize itself from democratic scrutiny.

The lesson is not that oversight is impossible in theory. It is that oversight without willingness to enforce consequences is ornamental. And when executive power is used to strip oversight of teeth, democracy is not being defended by process. It is being drained by it.
