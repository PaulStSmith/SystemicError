Trump's new comments sending GOP into 'really dangerous phase': Republican consultant

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump’s insistence that America is enjoying the “greatest economy ever in history” may end up backfiring on Republicans later this year, warned one GOP consultant on Sunday, as most Americans remain unhappy with the direction of the economy, with an overwhelming majority still living paycheck to paycheck.

“I’m popular and I’ve done well,” Trump told Fox Business’ Larry Kudlow last week, speaking on the performance of the economy. “I mean, I think we have the greatest economy actually ever in history.”

While several stock market indexes have shown positive growth in recent weeks, for millions of Americans, such growth is “feckless.” Around 40% of Americans don’t have a 401(k) to reap the benefits of a well-performing stock market, and more than half of Americans are unable to afford a single $500 emergency expense.

And yet, Trump has struggled to heed the warnings of some of those around him to acknowledge Americans’ economic hurdles, something that Whit Ayres, a GOP consultant with more than 40 years of experience, warned could end up backfiring in the upcoming midterms spectacularly.

“House Republicans are entering a really dangerous phase,” Ayres said, speaking with The Washington Post in its report Sunday. “They have to defy history. They need everything, they need a president who has the loudest megaphone in the country’s history.”

Instead of acknowledging the financial struggles millions of Americans continue to endure, Trump has declared the affordability crisis to be a “hoax” manufactured by Democrats. It’s this rhetoric, Ayres argued, that stood in stark contrast to past presidents that had successfully navigated moments of economic hardship.

“Presidents who’ve done a good job capturing the sentiments of the American people are those who articulate a message that is consistent with what most people feel,” Ayres said. “Bill Clinton was probably the best of anyone at that, but it’s very difficult to persuade Americans to believe something they’re not feeling in their daily lives.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s “Greatest Economy Ever” Claim

Donald Trump’s assertion that America is currently experiencing the “greatest economy ever in history” isn’t just a monumental overstatement; it’s a glaring example of disconnection from the economic realities of millions of Americans. In a recent conversation with Fox Business’ Larry Kudlow, Trump boasted about the economy’s performance under his watch, a perspective that, while perhaps comforting to him, is starkly removed from the lived experiences of a significant portion of the American populace.

The Reality for Most Americans

While Trump revels in stock market gains, he conveniently ignores the fact that around 40% of Americans don’t have a 401(k) to benefit from such growth. This statistic isn’t just a number; it represents a vast swath of the population for whom stock market highs are as distant to their daily lives as the moon. Moreover, the harrowing fact that more than half of Americans can’t afford a $500 emergency expense underscores a pervasive economic vulnerability that no amount of presidential boasting can mask.

The GOP’s Dangerous Disconnect

Whit Ayres, a GOP consultant with over four decades of experience, underscores a critical concern: Trump’s unwillingness to acknowledge the economic struggles of millions could spectacularly backfire in the upcoming midterms. Ayres’ warning is not just about the potential political fallout for the GOP; it’s a cautionary tale about the perils of disconnected leadership. Instead of addressing the affordability crisis head-on, Trump dismisses it as a “hoax” manufactured by Democrats. This strategy, if it can be called that, not only alienates those suffering under economic strain but also undermines the very essence of empathetic and effective leadership.

The Art of Presidential Persuasion

Past presidents have understood that in times of economic hardship, the American people don’t just want policies; they want empathy. They want to know their leader sees them, hears them, and is actively working to alleviate their burdens. Bill Clinton is highlighted as a president who excelled at capturing the sentiments of the American populace. Trump’s failure to do so not only demonstrates a lack of empathy but also a fundamental misunderstanding of presidential leadership. Persuading Americans to believe in an economy that doesn’t reflect their daily realities is not just difficult; it’s politically tone-deaf.

Conclusion

Trump’s claim of presiding over the “greatest economy ever” is not just factually dubious; it’s a stark manifestation of his disconnect from the economic struggles of everyday Americans. His rhetoric, steeped in denial and deflection, threatens not only his party’s prospects in the midterms but also the very fabric of American unity and trust in leadership. As the GOP consultant Whit Ayres rightly points out, the upcoming elections present a dangerous phase for House Republicans, one that requires more than just the loudest megaphone—it demands a leader who understands and addresses the economic realities of all Americans, not just those reflected in stock market gains.
