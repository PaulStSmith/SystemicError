‘Made some lame excuse’: Trump officials are openly dodging Congress’ lawful demands

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Dodging Accountability: The Trump Administration’s Defiance of Congressional Oversight

U.S. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem attends a House Homeland Security hearing entitled “Worldwide Threats to the Homeland,” on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S. December 11, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Top Trump administration officials are dodging lawmakers’ requests to testify before congressional committees, and in some cases, “not even” adhering to “legally required” requests according to one Democratic senator, The Washington Post reported Saturday.

“Many of these folks are required to come before Congress on a regular basis as a matter of law – and they’re not even doing the legally required parts, much less the additional oversight that’s sometimes needed when there are big issues pending,” said Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), speaking with the Post.

For instance, the Senate Foreign Relations Committee had only “two full committee hearings with administration witnesses this year,” the Post reported, a significant downgrade from the 24 hearings and briefings held in 2021 during the Biden administration. Similarly, the House Foreign Affairs Committee saw just 12 appearances from administration officials this year, down from the 47 in 2021, the first year of the Biden administration.

Examples include Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, who’s been asked to testify before the Senate Judiciary Committee about her agency’s aggressive immigration raids across the nation, but has refused to appear, and despite receiving two invitations.

“During her confirmation process, Secretary Noem committed to appearing before this committee when invited,” said Sen. Chuck Grassley (D-Iowa) during a committee hearing, the Post reported. “I expect better cooperation from the secretary early next year.”

As to why Noem was dodging the Senate Judiciary Committee, Sen. Dick Durbin (D-IL) told the Post that she had “made some lame excuse,” but that he would happily “co-sign a bipartisan subpoena if that’s what it takes” to get her to appear.

Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has also defied requests to appear before a Senate committee – the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee – and despite a previous pledge to Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA) that he would testify before the committee once a quarter if asked.

“Failure to conduct an oversight hearing on Secretary Kennedy’s actions would be an abdication of our responsibility – both from a moral perspective and as a matter of sound public health policy,” wrote Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) and 10 other Democrats in a letter to Cassidy.

Our Analysis:

Dodging Accountability: The Trump Administration’s Defiance of Congressional Oversight

In a revelation that should surprise no one but disappoint everyone, top officials from the Trump administration are engaging in what can only be described as a shameless evasion of congressional oversight. According to a report from The Washington Post, these officials are not just skirting around the edges of their responsibilities—they’re blatantly ignoring them, even when legally mandated to comply. This isn’t just a failure of protocol; it’s a brazen display of contempt for the democratic processes that underpin the United States government.

Senator Elizabeth Warren’s comments to the Post underscore a grim reality: officials required by law to report to Congress are treating these obligations as mere suggestions rather than mandates. The numbers speak volumes. The Senate Foreign Relations Committee witnessing a drastic drop from 24 hearings and briefings in 2021 to a paltry two this year is not just a statistic; it’s a symptom of a deeper malaise affecting the body politic.

The case of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem refusing to testify about aggressive immigration raids is particularly galling. Noem’s actions—or rather, her lack of action—flies in the face of commitments made during her confirmation process. Senator Chuck Grassley’s expectation of “better cooperation” sounds almost quaint in the face of such flagrant disregard for accountability.

Then there’s the curious case of Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., whose defiance of requests to appear before the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee not only breaks a promise but also, as Senator Bernie Sanders and colleagues pointed out, represents a failure of moral responsibility and sound public health policy.

What we’re witnessing isn’t just an aversion to oversight; it’s an assault on the very principles of transparency and accountability that should guide public service. When officials treat legal requirements as optional, they erode trust in the institutions they’re supposed to serve. This isn’t just about dodging uncomfortable questions or evading scrutiny—it’s about undermining the democratic oversight mechanisms that keep government actions in check.

Why does this matter? Because without accountability, there’s no trust. Without trust, the fabric of our democracy unravels. The actions of these Trump administration officials are not just administrative oversights; they’re deliberate choices that weaken the very foundations of our system of governance.

In a functioning democracy, no one is above the law—not even those appointed by a president known for his disdain for democratic norms. The refusal of Trump administration officials to testify before congressional committees is not just disappointing; it’s a dangerous precedent that threatens the integrity of our democratic institutions.

As this saga unfolds, one can only hope that the mechanisms of accountability can be restored and strengthened. Otherwise, we risk descending further into a system where power, unchecked by oversight, becomes its own law—a scenario that should concern anyone who values the principles upon which the United States was founded.
