'Knew it was coming': Wall Street Journal bashes Trump's 'self-destructive tariff folly'

President Donald Trump’s attempts to renegotiate America’s trade deals have become a “self-destructive tariff folly,” according to the Wall Street Journal’s conservative editorial board.

In a new op-ed published on Sunday, the editorial board argued that reports of a $10 billion bailout for America’s soybean farmers reveal that Trump’s tariff policy is not working as the president says it is. Trump’s tariff policy towards China has caused the country to stop buying American soybeans. The move significantly disrupted America’s soybean industry because China was one of America’s largest soybean customers.

“You knew it was coming,” the editors wrote. “As President Trump’s tariffs damage farmers and businesses across the U.S., the victims are besieging the Administration for relief. The long lines at the Commerce and Agriculture departments are the latest proof of self-destructive tariff folly.”

Trump has repeatedly said his tariff policy would generate a windfall of revenue for the federal government. The editors pushed back on that claim.

“The looming bailout is a refutation of the claim that tariffs are cost-free,” the editorial continued. “They aren’t if, like soybean growers, you are the target of retaliation. Mr. Trump likes to say that tariffs are a windfall for the Treasury, but not if much of that revenue is going back out the door in subsidies to offset the tariff harm.”

“The farm fiasco underscores another truth about tariffs, which is that they expand what Mr. Trump used to call ‘the swamp,’” it added. “Industries and individual companies hit by tariffs are flocking to Washington to lobby for relief. The Beltway bandits on K Street have never had it so good.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tariff Turmoil: A Critical Analysis

In a striking critique from the Wall Street Journal’s conservative editorial board, President Donald Trump’s trade policy, particularly his penchant for tariffs, has been labeled as nothing short of a “self-destructive tariff folly.” This assessment comes in light of a proposed $10 billion bailout for America’s soybean farmers, a direct consequence of Trump’s tariff tango with China. Let’s dissect this debacle with the precision of a surgeon and the bite of a critic unafraid to call out the emperor’s new clothes.

The Soybean Saga

At the heart of this fiasco is Trump’s tariff policy towards China, a move that has backfired spectacularly. China, once a voracious buyer of American soybeans and a key player in the U.S. agricultural economy, has ceased its purchases, leaving the soybean industry in disarray. This isn’t just a minor hiccup; it’s a full-blown economic convulsion for the American heartland. The Wall Street Journal aptly notes, “You knew it was coming,” highlighting the predictability of the adverse effects of such tariffs on American farmers and businesses.

The Bailout Band-Aid

Trump’s administration, now scrambling to mitigate the fallout, proposes a $10 billion bailout for soybean farmers. This maneuver is a glaring admission of failure, contradicting Trump’s earlier claims that tariffs would be a financial boon for the federal government. Instead, we’re witnessing a grotesque spectacle where the supposed revenue from tariffs is funneled back into subsidies to soothe the self-inflicted wounds of the tariff war. The irony is as rich as it is tragic.

Tariffs and the Swamp

One of the most damning indictments of Trump’s tariff policy is its unforeseen consequence of expanding “the swamp” Trump so vocally vowed to drain. The Wall Street Journal points out that industries and companies hit by tariffs are now besieging Washington, lobbying for relief. This has turned K Street, home to many lobbying firms, into a frenzied hive of activity. The tariffs have not only failed to protect American industries but have also enriched the Beltway bandits, making a mockery of Trump’s anti-establishment posturing.

The Bottom Line

The debacle of Trump’s tariff policy serves as a stark reminder of the dangers of populist economic policies that fail to grasp the complexities of global trade. The soybean saga is not just a tale of failed policy; it’s a cautionary tale of economic nationalism gone awry. The $10 billion bailout may provide temporary relief, but it’s a Band-Aid on a self-inflicted gunshot wound.

In their critique, the Wall Street Journal editorial board has not just offered an analysis but a rebuke of a policy that has proven to be economically myopic and politically self-serving. As observers and critics, we must demand more from our leaders than policies that lead to such “self-destructive tariff folly.” The true cost of these tariffs isn’t just measured in dollars and cents but in the erosion of trust in economic governance and the unnecessary hardship imposed on the American people.
