Many Republican Party members believe the GOP has “declared war on the president” with its vote on the Jeffrey Epstein bill, according to a report published Monday.

A 427-1 vote to release Epstein’s files passed earlier this month, with Donald Trump confirming he would sign a bill to release the files should it reach his desk. The Senate voted unanimously to pass the bill, which has since been signed by the president.

But a Republican representative believes the vote was a loyalty test within the Republican Party masterminded by Thomas Massie. Kentucky congressman Massie had pushed for a vote on the Epstein files, heading up a bipartisan group, according to Politico.

Representative Don Bacon sympathized with House Speaker Mike Johnson, saying he “tries his best” to keep the party together. But the Epstein vote has tested the GOP, and Bacon said the discharge petition for the files was an act that essentially “declared war on the president.”

He said, “They made it pretty clear that if you get on the discharge petition, you’ve declared war on the president, in so many words.” Despite being supportive of Johnson, Bacon has signed several discharge petitions of his own, including one to sanction Russia and aid Ukraine.

Former Trump allies like Marjorie Taylor Greene have distanced themselves from the president in recent weeks, with the prominent MAGA figure defecting from Trump’s good books — and ultimately leaving Congress — after backing the release of the Epstein files so publicly.

Further resignations could be on the cards too as an insider shares that some representatives are sick of being treated “like garbage.“ Raw Story previously reported some representatives in the House were fed up of Johnson’s attitude.

“This entire White House team has treated ALL members like garbage. ALL,” said one senior House Republican. “And Mike Johnson has let it happen because he wanted it to happen. That is the sentiment of nearly all — appropriators, authorizers, hawks, doves, rank and file.

“The arrogance of this White House team is off putting to members who are run roughshod and threatened. They don’t even allow little wins like announcing small grants or even responding from agencies. Not even the high profile, the regular rank and file random members are more upset than ever. Members know they are going into the minority after the midterms.”

Our Analysis:

GOP Infighting and the Epstein Files: A Loyalty Test or a Declaration of War?

In a recent development that has sent shockwaves through the corridors of power, the Republican Party appears to be at war with itself. A staggering 427-1 vote to release Jeffrey Epstein’s files has been interpreted by many within the GOP as an outright “declaration of war on the president.” This move, spearheaded by Kentucky Congressman Thomas Massie, has exposed deep fissures within the party, revealing a tumultuous blend of loyalty, betrayal, and political maneuvering.

The Loyalty Litmus Test

At the heart of this controversy is a loyalty test masquerading as a legislative process. The unanimous Senate vote and near-unanimous House vote, followed by the president’s signature, suggest an overwhelming bipartisan consensus on the surface. However, the undercurrents tell a different story. Representative Don Bacon’s comments shed light on the internal strife, casting the vote as an act of rebellion against the president. This is not just about Epstein’s files; it’s about power, control, and allegiance within the GOP.

Thomas Massie: The Mastermind or the Maverick?

Thomas Massie’s role in pushing for the release of the Epstein files positions him as either a mastermind of political strategy or a maverick challenging the party’s status quo. By framing the vote as a loyalty test, Massie has cleverly navigated the political landscape, forcing members to publicly declare their stance. Yet, this maneuver has broader implications, suggesting a rift between those who prioritize transparency and accountability and those who value party unity above all else.

The Fallout: Resignations and Defections

The repercussions of this vote are far-reaching, with high-profile defections and potential resignations looming. Figures like Marjorie Taylor Greene, once staunch allies of the president, have found themselves ostracized for supporting the release of the files. This public rupture within the party indicates a deeper ideological divide, with the battle lines drawn not just over policy, but over loyalty to individual leaders.

The “Garbage” Treatment and White House Arrogance

The sentiment expressed by a senior House Republican that the White House team has treated members “like garbage” is a damning indictment of the current administration’s approach to governance. This perceived arrogance and dismissiveness towards the legislative branch underscore a toxic relationship that could have lasting implications on the party’s cohesion and effectiveness. The frustration over being sidelined and disrespected is palpable, hinting at a larger issue of power dynamics and respect within the GOP.

Conclusion: A Party at a Crossroads

The controversy surrounding the Epstein files vote is emblematic of a party at a crossroads. As the GOP grapples with its identity and future direction, questions of loyalty, transparency, and leadership dominate the discourse. The unfolding drama suggests a party struggling to balance its principles with the pragmatic realities of political survival. As more details emerge and the fallout continues, one thing is clear: the Republican Party is facing a crisis of its own making, with the potential to redefine its course for years to come.

In the end, the saga of the Epstein files vote is not just a footnote in political history; it is a mirror reflecting the deep divisions, challenges, and choices facing the GOP. Whether this moment leads to introspection and reform or further discord and division remains to be seen. But one thing is certain: the battle lines are drawn, and the war within the Republican Party is far from over.
