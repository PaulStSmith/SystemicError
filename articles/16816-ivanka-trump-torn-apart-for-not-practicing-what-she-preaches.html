Stoic Misappropriation: Ivanka Trump’s Philosophical Posturing

Misusing Marcus Aurelius

Ivanka Trump’s attempt to align herself with Stoic philosophy, specifically through her admiration for Marcus Aurelius’ “Meditations,” is a clear case of intellectual posturing gone awry. In her statements, she claims profound influence from Aurelius, suggesting that his writings guide her thinking. Yet, according to critiques by Stoicism expert Ryan Holiday, her actions starkly contradict the principles she espouses. This discrepancy between proclaimed values and actual behavior is not just a personal failing but a strategic misdirection employed frequently by political figures looking to sanitize a tarnished brand.

The Real Stoic Message

Contrary to the passive, apolitical interpretation that some of Ivanka’s supporters might prefer, Stoicism, as discussed by Matt Johnson in The Bulwark, involves rigorous self-scrutiny and an active commitment to civic duty. Marcus Aurelius himself, a Roman emperor, wrote extensively about the responsibilities of leadership and the importance of moral integrity in governance. This aspect of Stoicism underscores a significant distortion in Ivanka’s application of the philosophy. Stoicism is not merely about personal peace but about engaging responsibly and ethically with the world.

The Commodus Comparison

Ryan Holiday’s scathing response to Ivanka’s Stoic posturing, where he compares her and her siblings to Commodus, the notoriously self-serving Roman emperor and son of Marcus Aurelius, encapsulates the deeper issue. This analogy is not just a clever jab but a pointed critique of how Ivanka, much like Commodus, appears to leverage philosophical gravitas for personal gain while neglecting the profound civic and ethical duties that true adherence to Stoicism would entail. Her selective quoting of Aurelius serves more to obscure than to illuminate her political persona.

Political Quietism vs. Active Responsibility

The article touches on an essential misunderstanding propagated by some MAGA proponents: that Stoicism advocates for political quietism. This interpretation is convenient for those who wish to cloak inaction or self-serving politics in a veneer of philosophical calm. However, as Johnson rightly points out, Stoicism’s true call is toward active civic engagement and responsibility, particularly for those in power. Ivanka’s public philosophical declarations are thus not just a personal misreading but a public, political act of misdirection.

Broadening the Narrative

Ivanka Trump’s use of Stoicism is indicative of a broader pattern within certain political circles: the tactical employment of intellectual and historical references to construct an unearned ethical or philosophical credibility. This strategy is particularly insidious because it exploits the public’s respect for historical wisdom while distracting from the unethical behaviors and policies actually being endorsed or enacted. By wrapping harmful agendas in the cloak of philosophical respectability, figures like Ivanka Trump attempt to elevate their public standing while eschewing the substantive ethical work that true philosophical commitment would demand.

Conclusion: The Stain of Misused Philosophy

Using Stoicism as a shield or a marketing tool rather than a guide to ethical living is a form of intellectual dishonesty that has tangible political consequences. It allows individuals and movements to mask ambition, cruelty, and opportunism behind a facade of depth and thoughtfulness. For the public, the task then is to scrutinize not just the philosophical quotes that politicians deploy, but the larger actions and policies they support. True philosophy demands consistency between thought and action, a standard that must be upheld if we are to hold our leaders accountable to the values they claim to cherish.
