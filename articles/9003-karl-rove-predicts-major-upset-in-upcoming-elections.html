Karl Rove predicts major 'upset' in upcoming elections

Longtime Republican strategist Karl Rove is cautioning Democrats from being overconfident heading into Tuesday’s high-profile elections taking place across several states.

Mediaite reported Monday that Rove told Fox News that even though polls show Democrats leading in the top three marquee contests of the evening — New Jersey and Virginia’s gubernatorial elections and the New York City mayoral race — Republicans have reason to be hopeful in two of those three elections. He also argued that while Republicans will likely lose the gubernatorial election in reliably blue Virginia, they may capture a separate statewide seat.

“Trump lost [New Jersey] in 2020 by 16 points. He lost it by six last time around. So the number one thing that the Republican candidate needs to do is he needs to build on the Trump bump,” Rove said of New Jersey Republican gubernatorial candidate Jack Ciattarelli. “He needs to get those people who moved into the Trump column between 2020 and 2024, he needs to them out in an off-year election. To do that, he needs to hold on to the Trump Hispanic and non-White gains.”

“If I were a betting man, I’d say New Jersey could be an upset, the Republicans are gonna gain in the legislature,” he continued. “In Virginia, I think we’re going to have a surprise because the Republicans are gonna hold on to the AG slot. And I think they’re gonna keep their losses in the House of Delegates to less than what the Democrats hoped they would get.”

According to polls from late October, Ciattarelli trails Democrat Mikie Sherrill by six to eight points, while Democrat Abigail Spanberger has a solid double-digit lead over Republican Winsome Earle-Sears in Virginia. And even though Democrat Zohran Mamdani has a significant lead over both former Gov. Andrew Cuomo (who is running as an independent after losing to Mamdani in the Democratic primary) and Republican Curtis Sliwa in New York City’s mayoral race, Rove said a Mamdani win would provide plenty of political ammunition for Republicans in the future.

“In New York, we’re gonna get the gift that keeps on giving,” he said. “We’re gonna have a Democratic socialist mayor who wins in the low 50s or mid 50s, in a city where 62 percent of the eligible voters are registered Democrats, 22 percent are registered independents, and only 16 percent are Republicans, conservatives, or members of little fringe left-wing parties.”

CNN data analyst Harry Enten said last week that If Democrats sweep all three of the major races on Tuesday, it would only be the sixth time in 90 years they’ve accomplished the feat. Each of the past five times Democrats won all three elections, they went on to win a majority in the U.S. House of Representatives in the following year’s midterm elections.

Our Analysis:

The Rove Gambit: A Masterclass in Political Spin

In a recent maneuver that can only be described as classic Karl Rove, the longtime Republican strategist has laid out a curious, if not bewildering, playbook for the upcoming high-profile elections in New Jersey, Virginia, and New York City. Speaking to Fox News, and reported by Mediaite, Rove attempts to conjure an aura of hope for the Republican party, despite the grim forecasts from recent polls. Let’s dissect this with the precision of a political autopsy.

The Unfounded Optimism

Rove’s optimism, particularly towards the New Jersey gubernatorial race and a separate statewide seat in Virginia, seems to be based more on wishful thinking than on solid ground. He places his bets on the ‘Trump bump’, urging New Jersey Republican candidate Jack Ciattarelli to mobilize the demographic that shifted towards Trump between the 2020 and 2024 elections. However, the reliance on a demographic shift that favored Trump seems like a shaky foundation, at best, for a campaign strategy, especially considering the polarizing figure Trump continues to be in American politics.

The Misplaced Bet

Rove’s advice for Ciattarelli to focus on Trump’s gains among Hispanic and non-White voters is perplexing, given the complex and varied political leanings within these groups. To assume that a ‘Trump bump’ among these demographics will automatically translate into support for Ciattarelli is not just optimistic; it’s strategically naive. Furthermore, polls from late October show Ciattarelli trailing Democrat Mikie Sherrill by six to eight points, a gap that seems too wide for the so-called ‘Trump bump’ to bridge effectively.

The “Gift” of New York City

Rove’s comments on the New York City mayoral race are equally baffling. He frames a potential win by Democrat Zohran Mamdani, a Democratic socialist, as a “gift that keeps on giving” for Republicans, suggesting it would provide ample political ammunition in the future. This assertion overlooks the fact that New York City’s unique political landscape hardly serves as a microcosm for the nation at large. To gleefully anticipate a Mamdani victory as a boon for Republican narratives is to misunderstand the complexities of local versus national politics.

The Historical Context

The cherry on top of Rove’s analysis is the historical note provided by CNN data analyst Harry Enten, stating that a sweep by Democrats in these races could herald a majority in the U.S. House of Representatives in the following year’s midterm elections. This historical pattern underscores the precarious nature of Rove’s optimism, suggesting that his projections may be more rooted in hope than in reality.

Conclusion

Karl Rove’s cautioning against Democratic overconfidence might have been intended as a rallying cry for Republicans, but it reads more like a political Hail Mary. The reliance on a dubious ‘Trump bump’, the speculative gains among Hispanic and non-White voters, and the misinterpretation of a potential Democratic socialist victory in New York City, all point to a strategy grasping at straws.

In the grand chessboard of American politics, Rove’s moves are not the calculated strategies of a grandmaster but the flailing of a player in check, desperately knocking pieces over in the hopes of stalling an inevitable checkmate. The truth remains that wishful thinking and strategic miscalculations are no substitutes for a compelling message and genuine voter engagement.
