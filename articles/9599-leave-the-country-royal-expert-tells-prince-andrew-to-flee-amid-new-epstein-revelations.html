'Leave the country': Royal expert tells Prince Andrew to flee amid new Epstein revelations

Prince Andrew has been advised to “leave the country” after the latest revelation about his links to the late sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

Newly revealed emails, apparently between King Charles’ younger brother and the disgraced financier, show Andrew saying “we are in this together” after an infamous photo of him with teenage abuse victim Virginia Giuffre was first published in 2001. The Sun‘s royal editor Matt Wilkinson urged him to leave Britain.

“If he has an honorable bone in his own body, he should leave the country,” Wilkinson wrote.

The email, released by The Sun on Sunday, conflicts with an interview the Duke of York gave to the BBC stating that he had ceased contact with the convicted child sex offender by Feb. 28, 2011, when the photo of him, Giuffre and Ghislaine Maxwell was first made public.

The duke also urged Epstein in that email to “keep in close touch” and expressed a wish to “play some more soon.”

“Andrew’s Jeffrey Epstein scandal will not go away so maybe it’s finally time the arrogant Duke did,” Wilkinson wrote. “The Palace has let it be known that Andy should at the very least ‘uninvite himself’ for Christmas with the family. But the notion that he is capable of using his best judgment for the wider benefit of himself and his family is naive and laughable.”

The duke gave up his official duties in 2019 due to the Epstein scandal and is no longer a working royal, but he continues living at Royal Lodge, and the government — not the king — has the power to strip his title.

“The late Queen and now the King have tried everything to punish the Duke but are now left with few levers to pull,” Wilkinson wrote. “It is now clear Andrew’s Newsnight interview was not only a car crash but not completely candid either.”

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Prince Andrew: Between Scandal and Royal Disgrace

In a saga that seems to reach new depths with each passing revelation, Prince Andrew’s entanglement with the late sex offender Jeffrey Epstein has once again seized headlines, this time with a damning twist. Newly unearthed emails, showcasing exchanges between him and Epstein, have laid bare the prince’s unsettling assurances of solidarity: “we are in this together.” This phrase alone, set against the backdrop of his association with a convicted child sex offender and the infamous photo with Virginia Giuffre, a teenage abuse victim, paints a portrait of royal disgrace that’s hard to dismiss or mitigate.

The Sun’s Scorching Advice: Leave the Country

The advice from The Sun’s royal editor, Matt Wilkinson, is as blunt as it is significant: Prince Andrew should “leave the country.” Such a drastic call underscores not just the severity of the prince’s alleged missteps but also the palpable frustration and disdain that have brewed within the public and media alike. Wilkinson’s assertion that Andrew should flee if he possesses “an honorable bone in his own body” speaks volumes about the perceived lack of accountability and honor in his actions to date.

A Web of Lies?

The email exchange between Andrew and Epstein, as revealed by The Sun, starkly contradicts the prince’s previous attempts to distance himself from the financier, notably in his BBC interview where he claimed to have ceased contact with Epstein by February 28, 2011. Yet, the casual tone of their communication, with Andrew expressing a desire to “play some more soon,” belies a deeper connection and familiarity, throwing his credibility and the veracity of his public statements into serious question.

The Royal Response: A Laughing Stock?

Wilkinson’s critique of the royal family’s handling of the scandal, describing the notion that Andrew is capable of using his best judgment for the benefit of himself and his family as “naive and laughable,” is a damning indictment of not only Andrew but the institution that shields him. Despite relinquishing his official duties in 2019, Andrew’s continued residence at Royal Lodge and the apparent impotence of the monarchy and government to strip him of his title or privileges highlight a profound systemic failure.

Conclusion: The Tarnished Duke

Prince Andrew’s scandal has evolved from a personal disgrace to a litmus test for the monarchy’s integrity, transparency, and accountability. The late Queen and now King Charles’ alleged efforts to “punish” the Duke fall short if the core issue remains unaddressed: the monarchy’s inability to extricate itself from the shadows of scandal through meaningful action. As Wilkinson aptly puts it, the Newsnight interview was not just a “car crash” but a glaring beacon of Andrew’s lack of candor.

In the end, the Prince Andrew debacle is not just about the fall from grace of a royal figure but a reflection on the monarchy itself. It begs the question: How can an institution, so steeped in tradition and public scrutiny, navigate the troubled waters of modern scandals? And more importantly, can it ever truly cleanse itself of the stain left by those who betray its values? Only time will tell, but for now, Prince Andrew remains a symbol of royal disgrace and systemic inertia.
