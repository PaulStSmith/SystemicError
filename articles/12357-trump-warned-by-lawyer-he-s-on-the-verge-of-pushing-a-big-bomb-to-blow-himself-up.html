Any thought that Donald Trump might have about issuing a pardon to convicted human sex trafficker Ghislaine Maxwell should be squashed immediately if he wants to have a chance of surviving the Jeffrey Epstein files firestorm, according to one lawyer.

During an appearance on MNBC's "The Weekend," conservative attorney George Conway, who is no fan of the president, had some advice for Trump as his DOJ works with Maxwell for possible testimony.

When it comes to offering a pardon to Maxwell after her conviction on child sex trafficking and felony conspiracy among other charges, Conway suggested Trump would be toying with fate.

Speaking with the host, he quizzed them about previous Trump pardons by asking, "What did Roger Stone and [Paul] Manafort get charged with and what were they found guilty of?"

"Oh gosh, I mean, that's like a decade ago. I can't even remember," Jonathan Capehart admitted.

"You will always remember what Ghislaine Maxwell was convicted of –– that's the point," Conway exclaimed. "It's very simple. It's like it's worse than just this abstract. You know, this went on for years. This is what, a thousand victims? Two of the women who talked, they were girls when they were molested. Two of the victims who testified, or two of the four who testified at trial, were 14. 14 -year-old girls when Epstein first had sex with them."

"Ghislaine Maxwell was right there telling them what to do, how to do it, taking their passports away so they couldn't get off Epstein Island, okay?" he elaborated. "This is not going to work for Donald Trump, okay? He does this, he's pushing a big bomb to blow himself up."

Our Analysis:

The Ghislaine Maxwell Pardon Proposition: A Ticking Time Bomb for Trump

The very idea that Donald Trump might entertain issuing a pardon for Ghislaine Maxwell, a convicted human sex trafficker, is as ludicrous as it is dangerous. George Conway, a conservative attorney with no love lost for the former president, laid out a stark warning on MNBC's "The Weekend." His advice? Squash any pardon thoughts immediately if Trump wishes to navigate the perilous waters of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal without capsizing his own ship.

The Unpardonable Nature of Maxwell's Crimes

Ghislaine Maxwell's crimes are not just egregious; they are abhorrent to the very fabric of societal norms and legal ethics. Convicted on charges of child sex trafficking and felony conspiracy, among others, Maxwell's actions were not merely criminal but morally repugnant. Conway's point is razor-sharp: unlike the financial and political machinations that landed figures like Roger Stone and Paul Manafort in hot water, Maxwell's crimes involve the systemic abuse and exploitation of minors. The victims, some as young as 14, were not abstract figures but real girls whose lives were irrevocably altered by Maxwell's actions and the depraved world of Jeffrey Epstein.

Trump's Pardon Record: A Precedent of Controversy

Donald Trump's presidency was marked by a series of controversial pardons that often seemed more rooted in personal loyalty and political expediency than in any concept of justice or rehabilitation. Conway's invocation of Stone and Manafort serves as a reminder of this pattern. However, as Conway astutely points out, a pardon for Maxwell would not just be another entry in a list of questionable decisions; it would be an act of political and moral self-immolation.

The Political Fallout: A Bomb Ready to Detonate

Toying with the idea of pardoning Maxwell is akin to Trump pushing the detonator on his own political future. The Epstein scandal, with its dark web of abuse, exploitation, and high-profile connections, remains a potent source of outrage and disgust. Aligning himself with Maxwell, even indirectly through the suggestion of a pardon, would not merely be a bad look for Trump—it would be an unforgivable sin in the eyes of the public and possibly even his base.

Conclusion: A Line that Must Not Be Crossed

In the grand chessboard of political maneuvering, there are moves that are risky but calculated, and then there are moves that are outright suicidal. A pardon for Ghislaine Maxwell falls squarely into the latter category. George Conway's warning is not just sound legal advice; it's a clarion call for common sense in a political landscape that too often seems devoid of it. For Trump, any thoughts of pardoning Maxwell should not just be squashed; they should be obliterated from consideration. Anything less would not just be a tactical error but a moral failure of the highest order.
