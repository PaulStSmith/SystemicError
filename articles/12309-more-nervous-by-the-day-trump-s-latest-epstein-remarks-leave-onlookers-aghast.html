'More nervous by the day': Trump's latest Epstein remarks leave onlookers aghast

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures as he meets with British Prime Minister Keir Starmer (not pictured) at Trump Turnberry golf club on July 28, 2025 in Turnberry, Scotland, Britain. Christopher Furlong/Pool via REUTERS

Among the comments President Donald Trump made when speaking from the U.K. on Monday was that he had turned down the "privilege" of going to sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein's private island — a location believed to be a center for his abuse of underage girls.

The phrase left onlookers aghast.

"And by the way, I never went to the island, and Bill Clinton went there, supposedly 28 times," Trump claimed during the press conference. "I never went to the island, but Larry Summers, I hear, went there. He was the head of Harvard, and many other people who are very big people. Nobody ever talks about them.

"I never had the privilege of going to his island, and I did turn it down, but a lot of people in Palm Beach were invited to his island. In one of my very good moments, I turned it down. I didn't want to go to his island."

It prompted those online to highlight the idea that Trump would call the trip to the island a "privilege."

"He never had the “privilege” of going to Epstein’s island?!?!" exclaimed podcaster and political commentator Jo Carducci.

Podcaster Brian Allen posted on X, “'Privilege' is doing a lot of work here."

Filmmaker Jeremy Newberger questioned, "The privilege of going to a rape island?"

"'The privilege' is crazy," commented the Never Trump organization, The Lincoln Project.

Former MSNBC newsman Keith Olbermann posted on X, "Trump describes going to Epstein's Island as a 'privilege.' Last Friday he said of the names he spouts here 'I'll give you a list.' Trump is burying himself."

The Tennessee Holler media outlet said on X, "Trump claims nobody has asked him about the pardon, says the Epstein files are a 'hoax' Garland/Comey/Biden tampered with (and forgot to release?), says he never had the 'privilege' of going to Epstein’s Island."

Meanwhile, AltNationalParks, a "resistance" account created in 2017 in response to Trump's first term, observed, "Trump is working really hard to convince people the Epstein files are just a 'hoax' now. During his meeting with Starmer on today in Scotland, he brushed off questions, claiming, 'They can put things in the files that are fake.'"

"He said he hadn’t been paying much attention to his own MAGA base’s demands to release them because the files were 'run by my enemy.' And his final defense? 'If there was anything in there, they would have used it in the last election.' He’s sounding more nervous by the day," the account also said.

That last point prompted a response from former metro editor at Chicago Tribune, Mark Jacob, who questioned Trump's logic on Bluesky, "Trump says 'bad, sick' Democrats could have doctored the Epstein files to make him look bad. Great logic by Cheetolini. He's suggesting the Dems framed him, but forgot to release the dirt, let him win the election, and then quietly turned over the Epstein files to him."

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Deflection: Trump's "Privilege" and the Epstein Island Controversy

In a recent verbal escapade that can only be described as both bewildering and disturbing, former President Donald Trump managed to stir the pot once again. Speaking from the U.K., Trump asserted that he had turned down the "privilege" of visiting Jeffrey Epstein's notorious private island, a statement that has left many either incredulous or outright appalled.

The "Privilege" of Association

Trump's choice of words — calling a potential visit to Epstein's island a "privilege" — is not only grotesquely tone-deaf but also indicative of a deeper, more troubling mindset. Epstein's island, for those who have been living under a rock or in denial, has been widely recognized as a hub for the sexual abuse of underage girls. To refer to an invitation to such a place as a "privilege" is to dance on the graves of decency and morality.

The Tangled Web of Names and Accusations

Trump doesn't stop at this baffling assertion. He proceeds to throw names around, including Bill Clinton and Larry Summers, insinuating their association with Epstein's sordid affairs while emphasizing his own supposed refusal to partake. This tactic is classic Trump: deflecting criticism by pointing fingers elsewhere. However, the gravity of the situation and the horrific crimes associated with Epstein's name make this deflection particularly egregious. It's not a political squabble; it's a matter of human rights and criminal activity.

The Online Outrage

The reactions online were swift and scathing. Figures like Jo Carducci and organizations like The Lincoln Project expressed their disbelief and horror at Trump's remarks. Yet, in the grand scheme of Trump's rhetoric, this "outrage cycle" has become all too familiar. It raises the question: are we becoming desensitized to the absurdity?

Trump's Attempt to Rewrite the Narrative

Further into his diatribe, Trump tries to convince his audience that the Epstein files are a "hoax" and dismisses any incriminating evidence as potentially fabricated by his adversaries. This narrative — painting himself as the perennial victim of a vast conspiracy — is as predictable as it is dangerous. It undermines the very real suffering of Epstein's victims and mocks the legal process.

The Logic (or Lack Thereof)

Mark Jacob's commentary on Trump's insinuation that Democrats could have used the Epstein files against him but chose not to, exposes the flaw in Trump's logic. It's a convoluted conspiracy theory that doesn't hold up under the slightest scrutiny. Trump's suggestion that his enemies would go to the lengths of framing him but then fail to capitalize on it is absurd, to say the least. It's a testament to his ability to construct a reality that suits him, facts be damned.

Conclusion: The Moral Quagmire

Trump's remarks on Epstein's island and the subsequent fallout are more than just another entry in the long list of his controversial statements. They reflect a moral quagmire, a willingness to trivialize sexual abuse for the sake of political one-upmanship. Trump's deflections, his victim narrative, and his conspiracy theories do not just undermine the rule of law; they insult the intelligence of the public and the dignity of Epstein's victims.

In this twisted narrative where visits to sites of alleged horrific crimes are considered "privileges," and where serious accusations are dismissed as hoaxes, one has to wonder: Where does the line of decency lie in American politics, and has Trump finally crossed it? Or more pertinently, how many more lines will we allow to be crossed before holding such rhetoric accountable?
