Trump openly cheers starvation as deal-making tactic: ​'There’s no anything!'

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts as he speaks to reporters onboard Air Force One, on travel from West Palm Beach, Florida, to Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., February 16, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

As Cuba nears its fourth week under crippling Trump administration sanctions that have shuttered hospitals and starved people of food, President Donald Trump boasted Monday at how the brutal campaign was an effective negotiation tactic.

“Cuba right now is a failed nation, they don’t even have jet fuel for airplanes to take off, they’re clogging up their runway,” Trump proudly boasted to reporters aboard Air Force One late Monday night. “I have Marco Rubio talking to Cuba right now, and they should absolutely make a deal. It’s really a humanitarian threat.”

The United States has sought to topple the Cuban government since the late 1950s after revolutionaries ousted the U.S.-backed dictator Fulgencio Batista, who historians say maintained Cuba as a “virtual slave state” to the benefit of American companies. The United States has imposed a crippling embargo on the Caribbean nation since 1958.

On Jan. 29, however, Trump signed an executive order that imposed new tariffs and penalties on any nation supplying Cuba with oil, setting the nation on the path to an outright humanitarian collapse, the United Nations warned earlier this month.

“So we’ll see how it all turns out, but Cuba and us, we are talking,” Trump continued. “In the meantime, there’s an embargo – there’s no oil, there’s no money, there’s no anything!”

Trump was also asked whether he would consider invading Cuba in a similar manner to the Trump administration’s attack on Venezuela and capture of its president, Nicolás Maduro. Trump refused to rule out such an attack.

“I don’t want to answer that, why would I answer that?” Trump scoffed. “If I was, it wouldn’t be a very tough operation, as you can... figure. But I don’t think that’ll be necessary.”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Trump’s Cuban Sanctions and Their Humanitarian Impact

The recent remarks by President Donald Trump regarding the crippling sanctions imposed on Cuba not only highlight a brash foreign policy approach but also underscore a troubling disregard for human suffering. As Cuba enters its fourth week under these sanctions, the toll on its citizens and infrastructure, particularly hospitals, is alarming. Trump’s boasting about the effectiveness of these sanctions as a negotiation tactic is a stark reminder of how geopolitical strategies can often overshadow basic human rights.

The Devastating Impact of Sanctions

It’s crucial to underline the severity of the situation in Cuba. Hospitals shuttered, people starved of food, and a nation deprived of essential oil supplies paint a grim picture of a country pushed to the brink of humanitarian collapse. The United Nations’ warning of an outright humanitarian collapse should be a wake-up call, yet it seems to be met with a cavalier attitude from the Trump administration.

Historical Context and Ongoing Embargo

The United States has had a contentious relationship with Cuba since the late 1950s, following the Cuban Revolution. The imposition of a crippling embargo since 1958 has been a cornerstone of U.S. policy towards Cuba, aimed ostensibly at pressuring the Cuban government but often resulting in hardship for the Cuban people. Trump’s recent executive order, which further tightens the noose by imposing new tariffs and penalties on nations supplying Cuba with oil, is a continuation of this punitive approach.

Trump’s Rhetoric vs. Reality

Trump’s remarks aboard Air Force One reveal a troubling disconnect between his perception of successful negotiation tactics and the reality of their humanitarian implications. His boastful tone, when discussing the dire situation in Cuba, is indicative of a broader trend in his foreign policy strategy: a willingness to leverage human suffering for political gains. Moreover, the mention of Senator Marco Rubio’s involvement in talks with Cuba does little to reassure those concerned about the administration’s commitment to finding a humanitarian solution.

The Specter of Military Intervention

Perhaps most alarming is Trump’s refusal to rule out military intervention in Cuba, drawing a parallel with the administration’s actions in Venezuela. This flippant approach to the prospect of military action in a sovereign nation further underscores the administration’s cavalier attitude towards international norms and the sovereignty of nations. The suggestion that an invasion of Cuba “wouldn’t be a very tough operation” is not only a gross oversimplification of military intervention but also a dangerous underestimation of its potential consequences.

Conclusion

The current U.S. policy towards Cuba, as articulated by President Trump, is a troubling mix of economic strangulation and military brinkmanship. The humanitarian crisis unfolding in Cuba deserves more than to be used as a bargaining chip in geopolitical negotiations. The international community, and particularly the United States, must prioritize the welfare of the Cuban people over the cold calculus of power politics. The path forward should be one of dialogue and diplomacy, not further deprivation and threats of military force.
