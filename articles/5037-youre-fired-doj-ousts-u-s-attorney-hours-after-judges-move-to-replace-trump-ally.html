‘You’re fired!’: DOJ ousts U.S. Attorney hours after judges move to replace Trump ally

By Paulo Santos

The Justice Department said Friday that a newly appointed interim U.S. attorney in Virginia was fired just hours after federal judges selected him to lead the influential office, triggering a fresh clash between the judiciary and the Trump administration.

According to CNN, judges in the Eastern District of Virginia unanimously appointed veteran defense attorney James Hundley to serve as interim U.S. attorney after Trump loyalist Lindsey Halligan was forced out last month.

Shortly after the appointment, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche unceremoniously announced in a social media post that the new nominee was out.

“Here we go again,” Blanche wrote Friday. “EDVA judges do not pick our US Attorney. POTUS does. James Hundley, you’re fired!”

Not long after, White House deputy chief of staff Dan Scavin, an assistant to the president, added, “Check your email, James.”

Hundley did not immediately respond to requests for comment. CNN reported it has reached out to the Justice Department.

Halligan’s brief tenure at the Trump Justice Department ended after failed attempts to prosecute the MAGA leader’s political opponents, including former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James. A federal judge later ruled Halligan’s appointment invalid, writing that the power to appoint an interim U.S. Attorney rests with the court until a Senate-confirmed nominee is approved, CNN reported

Our Analysis:

The Judiciary vs. The Trump Administration: A Power Struggle Unveiled

In a move that encapsulates the ongoing battle for control between the judiciary and the Trump administration, the Justice Department’s recent dismissal of a newly appointed interim U.S. attorney in Virginia, James Hundley, mere hours after his selection, marks a critical point of contention. This isn’t just a clash of titans; it’s a vivid illustration of the administration’s blatant disregard for judicial authority and established norms.

The Unprecedented Dismissal

James Hundley, a veteran defense attorney with an extensive background, was unanimously chosen by judges in the Eastern District of Virginia to serve as interim U.S. attorney, stepping in after the departure of Lindsey Halligan, a known Trump loyalist. Halligan’s forced exit—stemming from unsuccessful attempts to prosecute political opponents of MAGA, including figures like James Comey and Letitia James—should have been a wake-up call. Instead, what followed was a display of petulance and power play by the Trump administration.

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s social media announcement of Hundley’s firing is not just unceremonious; it’s an affront to the process and the people involved. “Here we go again,” Blanche’s post reads, a cavalier dismissal of judicial authority that undermines the very foundation of the U.S. legal system. The addition of White House deputy chief of staff Dan Scavin’s mocking comment, “Check your email, James,” adds insult to injury, showcasing a troubling level of disrespect for judicial decisions and the individuals they affect.

The Core of the Conflict

At the heart of this debacle is a fundamental misunderstanding or, more accurately, a willful ignorance of the balance of power. The federal judges’ unanimous decision to appoint Hundley was not a whimsical choice but a reflection of the legal framework that governs interim appointments. The subsequent ruling that invalidated Halligan’s appointment further clarifies this point: the court holds the power to appoint an interim U.S. Attorney until a Senate-confirmed nominee is in place.

This incident is a stark reminder of the Trump administration’s recurrent attempts to override judicial authority and concentrate power within its ranks. The dismissal of James Hundley is not merely a procedural quibble; it’s a symptom of a much larger issue—a systemic error, if you will—where the executive branch seeks to undermine the judiciary’s independence and authority.

Looking Forward

The repercussions of this power struggle extend far beyond the individuals directly involved. It signals to the judiciary, the legal community, and the public that the Trump administration is willing to flout legal norms and precedents to maintain control and push its agenda. This isn’t just about who gets to appoint a U.S. attorney; it’s about the integrity of the U.S. legal system and the balance of power that keeps our democracy in check.

As this saga unfolds, it’s imperative that we, as a society, remain vigilant. We must demand accountability, uphold the independence of our judiciary, and resist any attempts to erode the foundational principles that underpin our democracy. The dismissal of James Hundley should not be viewed in isolation but as a warning sign of the systemic errors that threaten the very core of our democratic institutions.
