Kid Rock is two decades past his peak — and his fame pales compared to Bad Bunny

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 8, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Singer Kid Rock testifies on the cost of concert tickets before a Senate Commerce, Science, and Transportation Committee Consumer Protection, Technology, and Data Privacy Subcommittee hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., January 28, 2026. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Forbes Magazine reports Turning Point USA’s choice of country-rapper Kid Rock to perform at an alternative halftime show during the Super Bowl was two decades too late.

Conservative organization Turning Point is looking to build a halftime home for frustrated Trump voters riled at the idea of Spanish-speaking U.S. citizen Bad Bunny headlining the NFL show. But Kid Rock, whose real name is Robert James Ritchie, isn’t likely to hit with many people under the age of 40.

“Bad Bunny has far more career streams in the United States — 26.8 billion, compared to 4.9 billion by Kid Rock — and many more globally,” reports Forbes. “He was Spotify’s top artist globally of 2025, ranking No. 5 in the United States, while Billboard named his Grammy-winning album “Debí Tirar Más Fotos” the eighth-biggest album of 2025. Bad Bunny has charted 113 songs on the Hot 100, including three top 10 hits last year.”

The MAGA-branded Kid Rock, meanwhile, hasn’t had a song on the Hot 100 chart in more than a decade, when “First Kiss” hit No. 66 in 2015. And he hasn’t had a top 10 single since 2002. His last album, “Bad Reputation,” peaked at No. 124 at release in 2022 — and it toppled from the chart one week later, marking the worst-performing studio album of his career, reports Forbes.

Turning Point USA, led by Erika Kirk — the widow of late cofounder Charlie Kirk — announced Kid Rock in its “All-American Halftime Show,” lineup somewhat late in the process, suggesting that building the lineup was no easy feat. Other performers include country singer Brantley Gilbert, Grammy nominated singer Lee Brice and country singer Gabby Barrett, which critics have described as a troupe with minimal recognition.

But the NFL is likely to stick to its guns on its decision enflaming the MAGA world.

In 2024, NFL games accounted for 70 percent of the most-watched U.S. telecasts, according to MS NOW. That means there may be no more room for the league to expand domestically. With that in mind, NFL leaders are likely looking to expand viewership beyond the America-only MAGA crowd.

“The league needs new audiences,” reports MS NOW, “and nobody’s better positioned to attract a global audience than Bad Bunny.”

“NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell defended booking Bad Bunny both after his Grammys speech and after the choice had sparked backlash,” reports Forbes. “Goodell called Bad Bunny ‘one of the great artists in the world’ earlier this week, saying he understands his ‘platform is used to unite people and to be able to bring people together.’”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Halftime Show Hullabaloo: Kid Rock vs. Bad Bunny

In an era where cultural wars seem to be the main course, Turning Point USA’s decision to feature Kid Rock in an alternative Super Bowl halftime show is like serving leftovers from two decades ago to a party expecting a gourmet meal. Forbes Magazine’s report on this peculiar choice is a glaring spotlight on the disconnect between conservative entertainment choices and the pulse of the broader, more diverse American populace.

The Misstep of Nostalgia Over Relevance

Kid Rock, a.k.a. Robert James Ritchie, epitomizes the MAGA movement’s nostalgic grip on a bygone era of American pop culture. With no Hot 100 hits in over a decade and a last album that practically belly-flopped on arrival, choosing Kid Rock as a headliner screams of a desperate clutch at straws that have long since drifted out of reach. In contrast, Bad Bunny’s staggering 26.8 billion career streams in the United States and his position as Spotify’s top global artist of 2025 highlight a glaring oversight by Turning Point USA: the world, and indeed the U.S., has moved on.

A Misguided Attempt at Counterprogramming

Turning Point’s “All-American Halftime Show” appears to be a reactionary move, aimed at creating a safe space for Trump voters irked by the NFL’s embrace of global superstar Bad Bunny. This decision, however, reeks of a fundamental misunderstanding of the current cultural landscape. While the organization may believe it’s tapping into a vein of widespread discontent, what they’re really doing is isolating themselves further from the mainstream, and more critically, from younger generations who are more diverse, inclusive, and globally minded than any before them.

The NFL’s Smart Play

The NFL’s decision to stick with Bad Bunny, despite the backlash from certain quarters, is a testament to the league’s understanding of its need for expansion and inclusivity. With NFL games accounting for a whopping 70 percent of the most-watched U.S. telecasts in 2024, the choice to feature a global phenomenon like Bad Bunny is not just smart; it’s essential for the league’s growth. NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell’s defense of Bad Bunny underscores a commitment to unity and inclusivity, values that are increasingly important in today’s fractured society.

Conclusion: Out of Touch and Out of Time

Turning Point USA’s choice of Kid Rock for an alternative halftime show is not just two decades too late; it’s a stark illustration of how out of touch certain factions are with the evolving American demographic and cultural landscape. While they aim to offer a sanctuary for those disillusioned by the NFL’s direction, what they’re really doing is highlighting their own irrelevance. The stark contrast in the artists’ current relevance and appeal couldn’t be clearer, and it sends a strong message about the direction in which America’s heart beats: forward, not backward.

In attempting to create a cultural counterpoint, Turning Point and Kid Rock find themselves not as bold dissenters, but as relics of a past era, shouting into a void while the rest of the country dances to a different tune. The NFL, in embracing artists like Bad Bunny, is not just playing a different game; they’re playing the long game, looking towards a future that is inclusive, diverse, and unapologetically global.
