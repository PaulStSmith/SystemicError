Chaos as Trump crackdown triggers massive nursing home crisis: 'I'm expecting the worst'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 27, 20254 min read

A staffing crisis looms for nursing homes as the Trump administration’s crackdown on immigration eliminates work authorization for immigrant employees who care for vulnerable seniors.

At a Miami facility, more than a third of one facility’s workforce — mostly immigrants from Haiti — have already lost their jobs, Notus reported Friday.

“Sincerely, I’m expecting the worst,” Anne-Mercie Blot, a naturalized U.S. citizen from Haiti and certified nursing assistant, told the outlet.

Beginning Feb. 3, more than 330,000 Haitians stand to lose their work permits, triggering a mass exodus across the elder-care sector.

“We don’t know after that how many patients they’re gonna add to the load that we have to take care of,” Blot said.

The Trump administration has systematically dismantled immigrant work programs in addition to escalating deportations.

Rob Liebreich, CEO of Goodwin Living in northern Virginia and Washington, D.C., let go of 14 migrants who had their work permits stripped. Sixty more employees face an uncertain future over their permanent legal status.

“It’s been very challenging to stay current with all the changes that our team members are having to face, and obviously they’re wanting to focus on their work and service to older adults,” he said. “And at the same time, there’s a lot of confusion and a lot of shifts and changes and, unfortunately, ultimately a lot of fear.”

The timing couldn’t be worse. America’s 65-and-older population has exploded, and immigrants comprise about a fifth of nursing assistants and over 40 percent of home-health aides.

“It’s a demographic ticking time bomb,” Madeline Zavodny, an economics professor at the University of North Florida, told the outlet. “Unless policies change, we’re not going to have as big of a labor supply of this group of immigrants who are providing really needed work.”

Texas care operator Adam Lampert warned that losing 20 percent of his workforce would necessitate price hikes and worse service.

“These are people that are handling your grandmother, your grandfather, your aunt, your uncle; these people have to go through background checks,” he said. “We know where they come from. They have to have their papers.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fallout of Immigration Crackdowns on Nursing Homes

The latest maneuver by the Trump administration to tighten immigration controls has flung open the doors to a crisis that’s been simmering under the surface, one that threatens to unravel the fragile fabric of care provided to our nation’s seniors. The decision to strip work authorization from immigrant employees in nursing homes is not just a policy adjustment; it’s a blatant disregard for the welfare of the most vulnerable among us and a slap in the face to those who tend to their needs with dedication and compassion.

The Human Cost of Policy

At a Miami facility, the real-world impact of this policy is stark, with more than a third of its workforce—predominantly immigrants from Haiti—already ousted from their positions. Anne-Mercie Blot’s apprehension, a naturalized U.S. citizen from Haiti, encapsulates the looming disaster as she braces for the added strain on an already overstretched workforce. The bleak reality that over 330,000 Haitians are on the verge of losing their work permits spells a mass exodus from the elder-care sector, a sector already teetering on the brink of collapse under the weight of an aging population.

The Administration’s Myopic Vision

The Trump administration’s systematic dismantling of immigrant work programs, coupled with an uptick in deportations, reveals a myopic vision that fails to recognize the indispensable role immigrants play in the elder-care industry. Rob Liebreich’s experience of having to let go of 14 migrant workers is a testament to the turmoil and uncertainty these policy changes wreak on nursing homes and their staff.

A Demographic Ticking Time Bomb

As the 65-and-older demographic burgeons, the reliance on immigrant labor in nursing homes has never been more critical. Immigrants constitute a significant portion of nursing assistants and home-health aides, with figures hovering around a fifth and over 40 percent, respectively. Madeline Zavodny’s characterization of the situation as a “demographic ticking time bomb” is apt, underscoring the urgent need for policy shifts to sustain a labor supply crucial for providing care to the elderly.

The Economic Ripple Effect

Adam Lampert’s warning encapsulates the economic ripple effect of these policies. Losing 20 percent of his workforce would not only necessitate price hikes but also degrade the quality of service provided to seniors—our grandparents, aunts, and uncles—who deserve nothing but the best care. This scenario highlights the broader implications of the administration’s actions, affecting not just the immigrants and their immediate employers but also the families of those who rely on these essential services.

Conclusion

The Trump administration’s crackdown on immigration, particularly its impact on nursing homes, is a glaring example of policy-making divorced from the realities on the ground. It’s a policy borne out of a fundamental misunderstanding or, worse, a callous indifference to the needs of the elderly and the invaluable contributions of immigrant workers. This approach not only jeopardizes the well-being of vulnerable seniors but also undermines the very fabric of compassion and care that should define our society. It’s high time for a policy reevaluation, one that places humanity and common sense at its core.
