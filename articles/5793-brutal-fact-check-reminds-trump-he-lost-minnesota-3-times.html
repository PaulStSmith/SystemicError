Brutal fact-check reminds Trump he lost Minnesota — 3 times

President Donald Trump on Monday was once again reminded that he lost Minnesota — three times.

Trump spoke to his former FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino Monday morning on the latter’s online streaming show, which Bongino launched after hastily leaving the bureau in January. Trump was Bongino’s first guest.

Speaking with Bongino, Trump mused about his desire to federalize elections in blue states.

“These people were brought to our country to vote, and they vote illegally,” Trump said to Bongino. “And it’s amazing the Republicans aren’t tougher on it. The Republicans should say, ‘We want to take over, we should take over the voting in at least 15 places.’”

“The Republicans ought to nationalize the voting,” Trump added.

The comment came after the president and Bongino discussed Minnesota, where Trump has targeted immigrants for the past month and killing two protesters.

“There’s something in the water up there,” Trump told Bongino. “I love the state. I won the state three times, but got no credit for it.”

CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale explained on X that the reason “he doesn’t get credit for winning three times because he lost it three times.”

Trump lost Minnesota in 2016, 2020 and again in 2024. The state hasn’t voted for a Republican for president since Richard Nixon in 1972.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Unfounded Claims and the Push to Federalize Elections

In a recent appearance on Dan Bongino’s online streaming show, President Donald Trump reiterated his unfounded claims about election fraud, specifically targeting immigrants and proposing the federalization of elections in “blue states.” This commentary not only underscores Trump’s continued dissemination of misinformation but also highlights a dangerous proposition to undermine state autonomy over elections—a cornerstone of American democracy.

Misinformation and Reality

Trump’s assertions that immigrants are “brought to our country to vote, and they vote illegally” is a baseless claim that has been debunked numerous times by various fact-checkers and studies. This narrative, steeped in xenophobia and misinformation, serves only to further polarize the public and erode trust in the electoral process.

Furthermore, Trump’s claim of winning Minnesota “three times” is blatantly false. As CNN fact-checker Daniel Dale pointed out, Trump has indeed lost Minnesota in all three presidential elections in which he was a candidate: 2016, 2020, and 2024. Minnesota has not voted for a Republican presidential candidate since Richard Nixon in 1972, making Trump’s assertion not just incorrect, but absurdly so.

The Danger of Federalizing Elections

Trump’s suggestion to “nationalize the voting” in at least 15 places is a stark departure from the United States’ long-standing tradition of allowing states to oversee their own elections. This proposal is not just about oversight; it’s a direct attack on the decentralized nature of U.S. elections, which serves as a protective measure against nationwide election fraud.

The implication that Republicans should “take over” the voting process in certain areas is particularly alarming. It reveals a desire not just for oversight, but for control—a move that would undoubtedly benefit Trump and his allies while threatening the integrity and fairness of the electoral process. It’s a power grab, thinly veiled as a concern for election integrity.

Conclusion

Donald Trump’s conversation with Dan Bongino serves as a reminder of the former president’s willingness to spread misinformation, attack immigrants, and propose drastic measures to manipulate electoral outcomes in his favor. His baseless claims about election fraud, combined with the dangerous proposition to federalize elections, should not be taken lightly. These statements are not just rhetorical flourishes; they’re indicative of a broader assault on the principles of democracy and fair play.

As we dissect Trump’s comments, it’s crucial to remain vigilant against such attempts to undermine public trust in the electoral process and to protect the autonomy that ensures the integrity of our elections. The spread of misinformation and the proposal to centralize control over elections pose significant threats to our democracy. It’s imperative that these propositions be met with the scrutiny and opposition they deserve.
