Trump so 'detached from reality' he may not even know how wrecked he is: report

Trump’s War of Ignorance Is Still a War of Power

Power, Not Confusion

The reporting centers a simple fact: Donald Trump is the one with the authority, and the Iran disaster is his own making. The source describes a president who rushed into confrontation expecting an easy win, then drifted into stalemate while pretending the situation might still resolve itself. That is not mere confusion. It is what happens when one man’s impulse gets fused to the machinery of state.

The Decision Node Is the White House

If Trump is slow to grasp the damage, that does not reduce his responsibility. It sharpens it. He chose the escalation, he owns the delay in reversing course, and he is the only actor with the power to end it. The article’s most revealing detail is not that he is surrounded by yes-men, but that his circle seems to function as an insulation layer, filtering out reality so the president can keep inhabiting his preferred fantasy.

The Chorus of Enablers

The article rightly points to the surrounding apparatus: advisers in the Situation Room, propagandists on Fox, and other loyalists who either want him to “finish the job” or are too afraid to tell him the truth. That is not a communication problem. It is a permission structure. Bad news is treated as disloyalty, while fantasy becomes policy. When intelligence briefings are reduced to material that flatters the president or simply shows destruction, the state is not misinformed by accident. It is being curated.

Misdirection as Strategy

The piece also exposes how easily blame gets pushed downward onto people who do not control the outcome. Voters are left with higher prices. Markets are left pretending the war will end on schedule. The public is left to absorb the costs while the decision-makers hide inside their own projections. Even the article’s point about “grumbling voters” can mislead if taken too far: the problem is not voter confusion. It is a governing class that treats consequences as someone else’s problem until they become politically inconvenient.

The Market Fantasy

There is something almost comic in the description of traders behaving as if every Trump hint will produce a clean exit. That is not sophistication. It is denial dressed up as pricing logic. Oil markets responding to every social post like a weather vane do not reflect clarity about policy. They reflect the same deeper sickness: powerful actors betting that chaos will somehow resolve itself because acknowledging the real trajectory would force someone to act.

Crisis as a Political Method

This story is not just about Iran. It is about how authoritarian-minded politics works when fused with incompetence: create the emergency, deny its scale, reward the loyalists who confirm the script, and let everyone else pay the bill. The article’s strongest insight is that Trump’s problem is not simply ignorance. It is a system built to preserve his ignorance long enough for the damage to become everyone else’s problem.
