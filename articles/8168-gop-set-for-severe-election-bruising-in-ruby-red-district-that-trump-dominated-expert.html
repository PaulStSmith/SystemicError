GOP set for severe election bruising in ruby red district that 'Trump dominated': Expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 2, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A special election in a deep-red Tennessee congressional district could serve as a bellwether for next year’s midterm elections, and CNN’s data expert Harry Enten said the results will likely give Democrats a good feeling about their chances for retaking congressional majorities.

Democratic state Rep. Aftyn Behn and Republican Matt Van Epps will face off Tuesday for a chance to replace Republican Mark Green, who left Congress in July for a private sector job. Polling ahead of the election shows a much closer race than many expected.

“We can’t make it to the end of the year without first holding a special election in Tennessee’s seventh congressional district this Tuesday, and it is a ruby-red district in which the Democrats should not even come close to winning,” Enten said. “It had a GOP rep — of course, that rep decided to step aside — and it’s also a district in which Donald Trump has absolutely dominated. I mean, take a look at his margins in the past couple of elections: In 2016, he won it by 17 points. In 2020, Donald Trump won it by 15. In 2024, he won it by 22 points, a close GOP win on Tuesday. While it would still be a win, it would be a sign of weakness for the Republican Party.”

“So what do we actually expect on Tuesday in Tennessee’s seventh congressional district?” Enten continued. “We’ll take a look here. ... The most likely outcome is a GOP win but by under 10 points, a 71 percent chance. Compare that to this, look at this: There’s actually a chance of a Democrat win, a 15 percent chance of a Democratic win in a district that Donald Trump carried by 22 points, 22 points back in 2024.”

“So even here, if the GOP wins by under 10 points, that most likely outcome is actually a very bad outcome for Republicans compared to the presidential baseline,” he added. “We’re talking about Democrats outperforming the 2024 presidential baseline by double digits.”

Democrats have outperformed their 2024 results by an average of 15 points in special elections in Arizona, Florida, Texas and Virginia, and since 2005, parties that outperformed in special elections went on to win the U.S. House majority in the following election five out of five times, Enten said.

“It looks like the most likely outcome is Democrats outperforming the 2024 baseline by at least 10 percentage points, and that could be very, very good news for them come 2026,” Enten said.

“Even if Democrats are not able to capture the seventh district, it is a very good sign for them going forward,” he added. “Now, of course, we haven’t held a special election yet. We’ll have to wait and see what happens, and the midterm election is still, what, 11 months away? But at this point, all signs point to Democrats doing significantly better on Tuesday than they did back in 2024 in Tennessee’s seventh congressional district, and when you put that together with the prior special House elections this year, it paints a very good picture for Democrats heading in to 2026.”

Analyzing the Political Winds: A Look at Tennessee’s Special Election

The upcoming special election in Tennessee’s seventh congressional district is being touted as more than just a fight for a seat; it’s being framed as a potential harbinger for the 2026 midterm elections. According to CNN’s Harry Enten, the dynamics of this race could leave Democrats with a spring in their step, but let’s dissect this with the critical eye it deserves, shall we?

First off, the characterization of the district as “deep-red” sets the stage. This isn’t just any Republican stronghold; it’s a district where Donald Trump boasted significant margins of victory across three elections, with a whopping 22-point lead in 2024. Yet, here we are, staring down the barrel of a race that’s alarmingly close for the GOP. Enten’s analysis points to a GOP win by under 10 points as the most likely outcome, a scenario that carries a 71 percent probability. But the fact that there’s even a 15 percent chance of a Democratic win in such a district is nothing short of astonishing.

It’s crucial to underscore the magnitude of this shift. A Republican victory by less than a double-digit margin in a district that Trump carried by 22 points is not just a victory; it’s a wake-up call. It symbolizes a potential crack in the GOP’s armor, a vulnerability that was previously unthinkable in such a loyalist territory.

Enten further bolsters his argument by pointing out that Democrats have consistently outperformed their 2024 results in special elections across Arizona, Florida, Texas, and Virginia. The trend is undeniable and the precedent clear: outperforming in special elections has historically been a precursor to winning the U.S. House majority. If history repeats itself, the GOP might be in for a rude awakening come 2026.

What’s particularly telling about this analysis is the broader implication for the Republican Party. If a district that was comfortably in their pocket is suddenly competitive, what does that say about their grip on power? It suggests a shift in the political landscape, one that could see previously safe districts becoming battlegrounds.

However, in the grand scheme of things, it’s crucial to remember that one election does not a trend make. While the special election in Tennessee’s seventh district is indeed significant, it’s but a single piece of the puzzle. The GOP will undoubtedly scrutinize the results and strategize accordingly. Likewise, Democrats should avoid overconfidence; a good showing in a special election, while promising, guarantees nothing for the midterms.

In conclusion, while this special election is being billed as a potential indicator for the midterms, both parties would do well to remember that politics is an ever-evolving game. Trends are important, but they’re not destiny. The only certainty in politics is change, and the road to 2026 is still long and unpredictable. What remains clear, however, is that the results of this special election will be dissected and debated as both parties look to gauge the mood of the electorate and adjust their strategies for the battles that lie ahead.
