GOP's Kasich called out by MSNBC analyst after frantic rant about crime-ridden cities

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

John Kasich, Basil Smilke (MSNBC screenshot)

Former Ohio governor and current MSNBC contributor John Kasich got some surprising pushback on the network on Monday afternoon after he backed Donald Trump’s claim that major urban centers in the U.S. are wracked with uncontrollable crime.

Following Donald Trump’s rambling press conference where he suggested a dictator might be required to get the cities under control, Kasich spoke with host Chris Jansing to give his take, but did not find agreement with MSNBC’s Basil Smilke.

According to Kasich, known for analyzing topics from both sides of the aisle, he believes that the cities Trump has been focused on, all of which are governed by Democratic Black mayors, need to work more closely with the Republican president.

Balking at the accusations that Trump and his inner circle are fomenting a “manufactured crisis” at a time when his administration is under the dark cloud due to his relationship with convicted pedophile Jeffrey Epstein, Kasich stated, “I mean, I have to tell you, I have such a different take than what we've just heard that talk about a man manufactured crisis.”

“Have you been in Chicago? Have you been in Baltimore?” he excitedly exclaimed. “My daughter went to school in Chicago. I remember the day she graduated there were shootings and killings right outside of her building. And in Baltimore, I have friends that go down to Johns Hopkins, get treated for medical conditions. I mean, they're – they are nervous about ever going there. There are parts of Baltimore that he tells me he wouldn't go into. And this is not some right-wing person; this is somebody who wants to be able to go into some of our great cities and be able to be safe.”

After making the case for co-operation with the Trump administration, MSNBC’s Smilke offered, “I absolutely disagree because if this were a crisis, rents wouldn't be $4,000 a month for a studio, right? We have an affordability crisis in some of these cities because people want to move there, people want to live there.”

“So you can't have this manufactured crisis and think that it's a real crisis if you have, you know, if there is a demand for $4,000-a-month studio apartments,” he added. “So having said that, the one point, and I agree with everything you said, and one of the things that you said at the end is where I want to pick up on because, Chris, I’m sorry, you said that, you said at the end that iIwant to pick up on, because what does get left out in this conversation is that the people that live in those communities, these communities where the all this alleged crime is taking place, those residents are not passive and wanting to try to make their communities better.”

“So what they do is they do partner with law enforcement,” Smilke elaborated. “They go to the the local police precincts, they are in those community organization meetings with federal and local law enforcement to try to find solutions to these problems. If you want the federal government to be involved, there are tons of nonprofits that get support to be violence interrupters to go into violent situations, to try to bring some peace to their neighborhoods.”

“None of that is really getting discussed here,” Smilke complained. “What the president is saying is he wants to have law enforcement with long guns in these neighborhoods, to put fear in the minds of those residents in communities that are largely led by Black mayors. Where is the partnership that's not being discussed?”

Our Analysis:

The Misdirection Play: Kasich's Crime Narrative Under the Microscope

Former Ohio Governor John Kasich's appearance on MSNBC, where he echoed Donald Trump’s narrative of rampant crime in major urban centers, provides a glaring example of the dangerous misdirection that often characterizes political discourse in the United States. Kasich, attempting to adopt a stance of bipartisan concern, inadvertently (or perhaps deliberately) steps into a minefield of political rhetoric and misinformation.

Kasich’s Crime Crusade: A Closer Look

Kasich's claims hinge on the assertion that cities, specifically those under Democratic Black mayors, are besieged by uncontrollable crime. This assertion not only simplifies complex socio-economic issues into a single, digestible narrative but also dangerously skirts the line of racialized political rhetoric. The former governor’s anecdotal evidence—citing personal experiences in Chicago and Baltimore—does little to provide a comprehensive understanding of urban crime or its root causes. Instead, it plays into a fear-mongering narrative that has been historically used to justify over-policing and underinvestment in Black and brown communities.

The Manufactured Crisis Argument

Kasich balks at the idea of a "manufactured crisis" while simultaneously bolstering a narrative that seems to do just that. The invocation of Trump’s suggestion that a dictator might be needed to quell urban unrest is not only alarmingly authoritarian but also deflects from the systemic issues at hand. To suggest cooperation with an administration that has shown little interest in addressing the root causes of urban poverty and crime is to miss the forest for the trees.

Smilke’s Reality Check

MSNBC’s Basil Smilke provides a necessary counterpoint to Kasich’s narrative, highlighting the multifaceted nature of urban living and the dangers of over-simplification. Smilke’s argument that high rent prices in these supposedly crime-ridden cities indicate a demand for urban living—and by extension, a complexity to the urban experience that Kasich’s narrative overlooks—is particularly poignant. Additionally, Smilke’s emphasis on community efforts to improve safety and quality of life presents a stark contrast to the doom-and-gloom picture painted by Kasich. It underscores the agency and resilience of urban communities that are often left out of the conversation.

The Unspoken Partnership

Smilke’s critique of the administration's approach to urban crime—favoring militarized policing over community partnership—hits at the core of the issue. Kasich’s failure to acknowledge the role of community organizations and violence interrupters in addressing urban violence reveals a significant blind spot in his analysis. The focus on law enforcement as the primary solution to crime, especially in communities of color, overlooks the systemic issues of inequality, lack of access to quality education, jobs, and healthcare, which are the real drivers of crime.

Conclusion: Beyond the Crime Narrative

Kasich’s comments on MSNBC are a stark reminder of the need for a more nuanced and fact-based discussion around crime in America’s cities. While the former governor may believe he is advocating for bipartisan solutions, his rhetoric inadvertently reinforces harmful stereotypes and overlooks the systemic reforms needed to address urban crime effectively. In contrast, voices like Smilke’s, which advocate for community partnership and a deeper understanding of urban dynamics, offer a more constructive path forward. It's high time the national conversation moved beyond sensationalized narratives and towards genuine, systemic solutions.
