'Become relevant again': Newsom trolls Bed, Bath & Beyond with brutal post after snub

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20254 min read

Gov. Gavin Newsom (D-CA). (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

A business executive trying to resurrect a classic retail brand proclaimed Wednesday that they would not open any brick-and-mortar retail stores in California — and Gov. Gavin Newsom hit back with a snappy retort.

Marcus Lemonis, a businessman and philanthropist who has hosted the CNBC show "The Profit," is in the process of creating a physical retail presence for Bed, Bath & Beyond. The original home goods retail chain bearing that name, after struggling for years to compete with Amazon, filed for bankruptcy and liquidated in 2023. Overstock.com proceeded to buy the intellectual property, create a web-only version of the store, then take an ownership stake in another chain of home goods retailers, which has opened a physical Bed, Bath & Beyond location in Nashville with plans to expand or convert more locations.

Lemonis, in an announcement this week, went out of his way to say this new company wouldn't operate in the Golden State, proclaiming on X, "This isn’t about politics — it’s about reality. California’s system makes it nearly impossible for businesses to succeed, and I won’t put our company, our employees, or our customers in that position."

Newsom's official press account, however, took this as a political jab — and responded in kind.

"Like most Americans, we thought Bed, Bath & Beyond no longer existed — after their bankruptcy & closure of every store," wrote the account, replying to the far-right influencer account "LibsOfTikTok" promoting the announcement. "We wish them well as they seek to become relevant again as they try to open a 2nd store."

Newsom's account has been on a relentless trolling spree against President Donald Trump, mockingly copying his syntax and mannerisms. The governor, who has sparked rumors of presidential ambitions for 2028, is also in the middle of unveiling a redistricting proposal to draw out Republicans in retaliation for the similar plan from the Texas GOP to rig lines against Democrats.

Our Analysis:

The Retail Resurrection Ruckus: A Closer Look

In a story that reads like a classic clash of the titans, Marcus Lemonis, the business magnate known for his role on CNBC's "The Profit," has thrown down the gauntlet in a state vs. entrepreneur standoff that could only happen in the ever-contentious political and economic arena of California. At the heart of this saga is the attempt to breathe new life into Bed, Bath & Beyond, a retail brand that, like a phoenix, is trying to rise from the ashes of bankruptcy and liquidation.

The California Conundrum

Lemonis's declaration to exclude California from his retail revival plan is a headline-grabbing move, but is it just bravado, or is there substance to his claims? The executive cites the "nearly impossible" business environment in California as the deal-breaker. This is a bold statement, yet it's one that resonates with a familiar narrative: the critique of California's regulatory and tax landscape as hostile to business.

Is Lemonis's decision pragmatic caution or a calculated publicity stunt? It's tempting to latch onto the dramatic flair of his announcement, but it's essential to dissect the underlying issues. California, with its stringent regulations and high costs, presents undeniable challenges to businesses. However, it's also a state with a massive consumer base and significant economic power. Lemonis's blanket rejection may speak more to the symbolic gesture of taking a stand against perceived over-regulation than to a nuanced business strategy.

Newsom's Retort: Political Theater or Genuine Indignation?

Governor Gavin Newsom's snarky comeback, engaging with the "LibsOfTikTok" account no less, adds another layer of spectacle to the story. Newsom, ever the political animal, seems to relish these public spats, leveraging them to cultivate a certain image both of himself and California. His dig at Bed, Bath & Beyond's relevance might hit below the belt, but it's strategic, aiming to underscore the state's economic resilience and attractiveness to businesses despite the challenges.

Newsom's response, while entertaining, also skirts around addressing the legitimate concerns that Lemonis raises. By focusing on the quip rather than the critique, Newsom misses an opportunity to engage in a meaningful conversation about California's business environment.

The Bigger Picture

Both Lemonis and Newsom are playing their roles in a larger drama that encompasses California's identity as a business hub and its political positioning on the national stage. This incident is emblematic of the broader debates surrounding state governance, economic policy, and the balance between regulation and business freedom.

However, what's often lost in these high-profile exchanges are the nuances and complexities of the issues at hand. California's business environment is not a monolith; it varies greatly by industry, geography, and other factors. Similarly, the challenges of resurrecting a retail brand in the current economic climate are multifaceted and cannot be fully captured in snappy social media exchanges.

Conclusion: Beyond the Banter

As entertaining as the Lemonis-Newsom spat might be, it's crucial to look beyond the banter to understand the real stakes. California's business climate, while challenging, is not insurmountable, and the success of a revived Bed, Bath & Beyond will depend on more than just the choice of retail locations.

This saga serves as a reminder of the importance of engaging with the substance over the spectacle. As observers, it's our responsibility to sift through the snark to find the signal in the noise—a task that is becoming increasingly difficult in our hyper-politicized reality.
