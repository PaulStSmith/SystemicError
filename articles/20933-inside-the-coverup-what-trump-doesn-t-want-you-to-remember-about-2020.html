Inside the coverup: What Trump doesn't want you to remember about 2020

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The People With PowerThe Reopening Was a Political BargainRacial Disparity Became the JustificationFauci as Human ShieldThe Fraud of Blame-ShiftingThe Pattern

Fauci Was the Decoy. Trump Made the Choice.

The week’s circus around Dr. Anthony Fauci was not about truth-seeking. According to the source reporting, Republicans used the Senate stage to turn a public-health official into a scapegoat while the real record remained their preferred hiding place: Trump and Jared Kushner’s political decision to shift from prevention to reopening, even as Covid deaths concentrated among Black and Hispanic workers.

The People With Power

The relevant power was never with Fauci. It sat in the White House, in the GOP machinery around Trump, and in the media ecosystem laundering their choices. Trump had the authority to declare emergencies, direct federal action, and set the tone for reopening. Kushner assembled the response around loyalists and political convenience. Rand Paul, Josh Hawley, Fox, the Chamber of Commerce, and Freedomworks supplied the downstream noise and ideological cover.

That is the basic structure the article tries to bury under the theater of Fauci cross-examination. Fauci can be attacked; he cannot be blamed for the fact that Trump and his allies controlled the levers and used them badly on purpose.

The Reopening Was a Political Bargain

The source lays out a timeline that matters. In March 2020, the country shut down because the virus was moving, uncertainty was high, and death was rising fast. Then came April 7, when major reporting showed Black Americans were dying at disproportionately high rates. After that, the tone changed. The article says the Trump operation recognized a political opportunity: force “essential” workers back, reopen quickly, and absorb the human cost where it would fall hardest.

That is not confusion. That is selection. The policy logic described here was simple and ugly: protect the white-collar electorate where possible, expose low-wage workers where necessary, and call it recovery.

Racial Disparity Became the Justification

The most revealing part of the story is how openly the right adjusted once the racial distribution of death became clearer. The article describes Fox hosts and Republican voices pivoting from alarm to indifference, suddenly treating mass death as acceptable once Black and Hispanic workers were the ones most exposed. That is the political meaning of “essential” in this account: essential to supply chains, not to the people issuing the slogans.

This is where the article is strongest. It shows how race was not incidental to the pandemic response; it was operational. The economy was reopened over the bodies of workers who could not stay home, while salaried white workers telecommuted and were told the danger had been exaggerated.

Fauci as Human Shield

The assault on Fauci serves a clear function in the source text: it redirects anger away from decision-makers and toward the nearest expert with a recognizable face. Republicans could posture as defenders of liberty and anti-bureaucratic truth-tellers while dodging the one question that matters: who chose a strategy that accepted mass death as a political cost?

That is why the perjury-trap theatrics matter less than the cover-up function. Fauci is useful to them because he is visible and not politically armored. Trump, Kushner, and the media apparatus around them needed a subordinate villain to keep the public from asking why reopening became a bargaining chip once the deaths landed on Black and Hispanic communities.

The Fraud of Blame-Shifting

The source is explicit that Trump then blamed Blue-state governors for the consequences of a federal strategy his own team helped drive. That inversion is the whole game. Centralized power creates the conditions, then decentralization becomes the excuse. Federal choices produce predictable outcomes, then local Democratic officials are made to absorb the blame.

This is not just mendacity. It is method. The administration allegedly used federal authority to shape the death curve, then used partisan media to recast the results as Democratic failure. That is how political cruelty survives in plain sight: by laundering intent through procedural noise.

The Pattern

The larger pattern is authoritarian governance with a racial target and a propaganda wrapper. First comes the decision to treat some lives as disposable. Then comes the narrative machine to obscure who decided, who benefited, and who died. Then comes the ritualized attack on experts, because expertise is one of the few things that can still contradict power in public.

That is what this story reveals. Not a messy pandemic response. Not a tragic misunderstanding. A ruling coalition chose economic optics and electoral advantage over mass prevention, then used racial hierarchy to make the toll politically survivable. The Fauci spectacle is just the garnish on a much older American habit: make the vulnerable carry the cost, then blame someone safer.
