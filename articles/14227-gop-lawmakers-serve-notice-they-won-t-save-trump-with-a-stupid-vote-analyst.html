GOP lawmakers serve notice they won't save Trump with a 'stupid vote': analyst

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 31, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Tariff Turmoil: Trump's Solo Battle Against Economic Sense

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Despite a court stepping in late Thursday and reviving Donald Trump's campaign of tariffs against U.S. allies, one political strategist predicted it will all fall apart in the long run and Republican lawmakers won't be there to bail the president out.

During an appearance on MSNBC's "Way Too Early," former Sen. Joe Manchin (I-WV) adviser Jonathan Kott told host Ali Vitali that GOP members of Congress won't want to admit to voters they made a "stupid vote" and will instead leave the president to fend for himself and take the blame for the economic chaos.

"I think there was a Democratic theory of well, at some point, the market turmoil will just be so bad that either Trump will have to make a u-turn on this –– TACO –– or someone will have to step in, whether it's the Senate or advisers saying, 'Alright, we can't keep doing this,'" Vitali prompted her guest. "But it does feel like the markets are starting to factor in the unpredictability. So is that the end of the Democratic plan there? Like, have they presupposed the wrong notion here?"

"No," Kott replied. "Look, Treasury Secretary Bessent stepped in, he helped. Congress is not going to step in. Republicans have already said we don't want any part of this. They're not going to step in on tariffs. They have said, we don't want to take the votes on this because they know at some point Donald Trump's not going to be on the ballot, and those senators are going to have to face voters and say, 'Hey, I took this stupid vote and raised prices on you, and Donald Trump's not going to be there to defend me.'"

"Sen. Mike Crapo (R-ID) has already said we're not adding this to reconciliation, even though Trump has said this will be billions and billions of dollars. Nope. We don't want any part of this," he added. "So, I think all of Congress has said 'President, this is on you,' and Democrats are happy to let him make this fight –– watch the markets go up and down. The uncertainty is what's killing him."

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Turmoil: Trump's Solo Battle Against Economic Sense

In what can only be described as a political circus of economic policies, Donald Trump's campaign of tariffs against U.S. allies has been given a temporary lifeline by a court ruling. However, this revival is likely nothing more than a flicker before the inevitable extinguish, as predicted by political strategist and former adviser to Sen. Joe Manchin, Jonathan Kott. In an appearance on MSNBC's "Way Too Early," Kott provided a candid forecast of the political fallout that is expected to ensue.

GOP's Cold Feet: A Tale of Political Survival

The Republican lawmakers, often seen as Trump's unwavering battalion, appear to have finally found the hill they're unwilling to die on: tariffs. Kott's insights reveal a GOP in a state of self-preservation, acutely aware of the ticking political time bomb that siding with such economically detrimental policies could be. The fear of having to face voters with the confession of a "stupid vote" is palpable. This anticipated abandonment by the Republican lawmakers is not just a crack but a chasm forming in the party's unity, which Trump once seemed to command with an iron grip.

The Economic Chaos: A Predictable Disaster

Kott's analysis brings to the forefront the undeniable reality of the economic chaos these tariffs could unleash. The argument is not merely speculative; it's grounded in the understanding of basic economic principles that tariffs, more often than not, hurt the imposing country's economy. The cost is invariably passed down to consumers in the form of higher prices, a reality that no political spin can mask.

The Democratic Stance: Watching from the Sidelines

Interestingly, Democrats seem to be taking a backseat approach, content in watching the GOP and Trump navigate the self-inflicted economic turmoil. This strategic distance could be perceived as a calculated move, allowing the Republican party to showcase its internal conflict and policy failures in real-time. The mention of Treasury Secretary Bessent stepping in as a lone voice of reason underscores the isolation Trump faces, even within his administration.

The Market's Verdict: Uncertainty is the Only Certainty

The reaction of the markets to the unpredictability of Trump's economic policies speaks volumes. Kott points out the palpable uncertainty that is "killing" Trump's standing. In economics, as in politics, uncertainty is a harbinger of instability, something markets react to negatively. This sentiment has been echoed across various platforms, with financial analysts and investors voicing concerns over the long-term implications of such erratic policy-making.

Conclusion: A Self-Inflicted Wound with Long-Term Consequences

In sum, the analysis provided by Jonathan Kott on MSNBC's "Way Too Early" paints a grim picture of the future of Trump's tariff campaign. The expected fallout is not just limited to economic repercussions but extends to the political arena, where Trump is likely to find himself increasingly isolated. The Republican lawmakers' reluctance to support the tariffs is a telling sign of the shifting political winds, possibly indicative of a broader reevaluation of Trump's policy directions. As the saga unfolds, one thing is clear: the tariff campaign, revived by a court ruling, is on borrowed time, with its architect facing a lonely battle ahead.
