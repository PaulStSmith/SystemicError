The Billion-Dollar Lie: How Undocumented Immigrants Quietly Prop Up America's Safety Net

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 21, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Let’s start with the punchline: in 2022, undocumented immigrants paid $96 billion into the U.S. Social Security and Medicare systems.

Read that again.

Now sit with the staggering hypocrisy of it all.

For years, the American right has peddled a fantasy that undocumented immigrants are a parasitic drain on the nation’s resources. “They don’t pay taxes!” is the rallying cry shouted from Fox News to family barbecues. The phrase has been weaponized to dehumanize and justify cruel immigration policies, ICE raids, family separations, and calls for a wall that wouldn’t stop a single wire transfer.

But here’s the inconvenient truth: millions of undocumented immigrants do pay taxes. And not just sales tax or property tax through rent—we’re talking payroll taxes. Real contributions. Billions of dollars. Paid through ITINs (Individual Taxpayer Identification Numbers), which the IRS issues precisely because it knows there are undocumented workers who want to comply with tax laws. These workers have taxes automatically withheld from their paychecks, often using false or borrowed Social Security numbers they will never benefit from.

And benefit they won’t.

Because despite paying into the system, they are ineligible to receive Social Security or Medicare. Their money supports the retirements and medical care of citizens who might scream for their deportation while cashing checks fattened by immigrant labor.

Imagine that: being forced to contribute to a system designed to exclude you.

When confronted with this fact, most people react with disbelief. The viral video this article draws from features everyday Americans stunned by the truth. “I wasn’t aware of that,” they repeat, eyes wide. Of course they weren’t. Because the truth has been buried beneath layers of xenophobic propaganda.

This isn’t a bug. It’s the system working as intended: exploit undocumented labor, extract wealth from it, then vilify and discard the human beings behind it. The U.S. economy benefits massively from this two-faced arrangement. Employers get cheap, compliant labor. The government gets tax revenue without long-term obligations. And conservative politicians get a scapegoat they can demonize to win elections.

Everyone wins. Except the immigrants.

This isn’t just unjust. It’s theft on a national scale, committed with bureaucratic precision and sanctioned by silence.

And then came Trump.

No administration in modern history wielded cruelty as policy more gleefully than Donald Trump’s. Family separations weren’t a side effect—they were the goal. The point was pain. Fear. A loud, televised message to undocumented communities: you are not safe here.

Under Trump, ICE operated like a paramilitary force. People were yanked from jobs, hospitals, schools. Deportation was no longer a reluctant process—it was a campaign slogan. And in that rampage, no thought was given to the economic shockwave. No recognition that undocumented immigrants weren’t just “illegals,” but workers. Taxpayers. A financial backbone.

Here’s what most don’t realize: undocumented immigrants are one of the main reasons Social Security and Medicare haven’t already collapsed. They pay in without ever taking out. Their money props up a system that’s otherwise teetering on insolvency.

So let’s do the math.

If Trump or any future administration succeeds in mass deportation—not only is it a human rights catastrophe, it’s an economic suicide pact. $96 billion won’t magically be replaced. That revenue vanishes. The trust funds that support Medicare and Social Security will begin bleeding out even faster. Your grandma’s check? Your dad’s hospital bill? Good luck.

The system only works because of the very people America is hunting down.

The next time someone huffs that undocumented immigrants are a burden, ask them how much they paid into Social Security last year. Ask them how much they know about ITINs. Ask them if they knew their retired uncle’s Medicare is partially funded by a guy named José or Jorge who works 12-hour shifts washing dishes and hasn’t seen his family in a decade.

Then drop the number: $96 billion.

Now, who’s the real freeloader?
