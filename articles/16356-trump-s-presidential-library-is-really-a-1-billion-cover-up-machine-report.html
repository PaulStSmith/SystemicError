Manipulating Memory: Trump’s Presidential Library and the War Over Public Records

A Monument to Opacity

Donald Trump’s plan for his presidential library starkly deviates from the tradition of presidential libraries serving as public resources. Instead, Trump envisions a complex that includes a hotel and retail outlets, while simultaneously ensuring that every document it houses remains under his control. This is not a mere deviation from tradition; it’s a deliberate obstruction of public access to historical records.

Institutional Power and Personal Control

Trump, wielding power reinforced by his position and political influence, aims to redefine the ownership of presidential records. His approach challenges the Presidential Records Act of 1978, a law ensuring that presidential documents are public property, managed by the National Archives and Records Administration. Trump’s legal maneuvers, supported by a Justice Department shaped by his appointees, represent an unprecedented assertion of personal over public interest.

Legal and Political Manipulations

The legal framework Trump is constructing around his records is not just about controlling history; it’s about shielding himself from accountability. By asserting that these documents are his personal property, Trump seeks to block any potential legal challenges or investigations that might use these records as evidence. His administration’s legal strategies, particularly the sweeping claims made by a loyalist in the Office of Legal Counsel, are designed to fortify his control over these documents indefinitely.

The Role of Money in Memory

The funding strategy for Trump’s library also raises significant ethical questions. By soliciting donations from companies with whom he has settled lawsuits, and beginning his fundraising immediately upon his second inauguration, Trump blurs the lines between personal business dealings and presidential duties. The exemption of the library from state and local property taxes, under the guise of it being an educational facility, while it simultaneously operates potentially profitable ventures, underscores a misuse of public trust and tax laws.

The Bigger Picture: A Shift in Presidential Accountability

Trump’s actions reflect a broader pattern of behavior among powerful political figures who seek to manipulate public institutions for personal gain. This maneuver to control presidential records is part of a larger assault on transparency and public accountability. By privatizing presidential history, Trump not only shields himself from scrutiny but also sets a dangerous precedent for future administrations.

Conclusion: Guarding Against the Privatization of Power

Trump’s presidential library plan reveals a fundamental threat to the democratic oversight of executive power. It is a stark example of how personal interests are increasingly infringing upon public rights. The fight over these records isn’t just about historical documentation; it’s about ensuring that power remains visible and accountable to the public. As this situation develops, it will be crucial for watchdogs, historians, and the public to remain vigilant and assert the collective ownership of our nation’s history.
