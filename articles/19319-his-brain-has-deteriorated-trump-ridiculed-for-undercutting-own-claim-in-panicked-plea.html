‘His brain has deteriorated’: Trump ridiculed for undercutting own claim in panicked plea

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks to members of the Republican Party, at Trump National Doral Miami in Miami, Florida, U.S., March 9, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

President Donald Trump drew widespread mockery Saturday after seemingly contradicting his own claim that Iran’s military had been “destroyed 100%,” yet warning in the same breath that it could still threaten shipping in the region.

“His postmodernism has completely spiraled out of control as his brain has deteriorated,” wrote progressive political commentator and podcast host Kyle Kulinski Saturday in a social media post on X.

Trump took to Truth Social Saturday to insist that his administration had completely “destroyed” Iran’s military capabilities. In the same sentence, however, he also noted that Iran’s military was still capable of sending “a drone or two,” deploying mines and launching “close range missiles” to keep closed the Strait of Hormuz, a major shipping route through which a fifth of the world’s oil trade travels.

“In a single unhinged post Trump is saying that the Iranian military is COMPLETELY DESTROYED BUT they are STILL CAPABLE OF ATTACKING ships in the Strait of Hormuz?” wrote X user “Robin,” a frequent political commentator and Trump critic, to their nearly 20,000 followers. “Even the stupid, ignorant MAGA have to see how insane that is? Why are they so willing to be LIED to?”

Iran has vowed to keep the Strait of Hormuz closed in response to the U.S.-Israeli attacks on the Middle East nation, reportedly sparking panic within the Trump administration as oil prices skyrocket by levels not seen since the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

Trump has attempted to dismiss panic around surging gas prices, insisting last week that higher prices at the pump would be temporary, and were a “very small price to pay” to topple Iran’s government.

Trump also expressed hope that several other countries would join in the U.S. effort to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, a plea that one critic ridiculed as the president “begging for help.”

“Hopefully China, France, Japan, South Korea, the UK, and others, that are affected by this artificial constraint, will send Ships to the area so that the Hormuz Strait will no longer be a threat by a Nation that has been totally decapitated,” Trump wrote.

“In the meantime, the United States will be bombing the hell out of the shoreline, and continually shooting Iranian Boats and Ships out of the water. One way or the other, we will soon get the Hormuz Strait OPEN, SAFE, and FREE!

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump’s Contradictory Statements on Iran’s Military Capabilities

The Context

The article in question reports on a series of statements made by President Donald Trump, which on the surface appear contradictory. Trump claims that Iran’s military has been “destroyed 100%,” yet also asserts that Iran still poses a threat to shipping in the Strait of Hormuz through the potential use of drones, mines, and missiles. This analysis aims to unpack the claims, the power dynamics at play, and the accuracy of the criticisms levied towards Trump.

1. Institutional Power

In this situation, the institutional power lies squarely with the President of the United States, who has extensive control over foreign policy and military action. As commander-in-chief, Trump has the authority to direct military operations and shape the U.S. response to international threats. His administration’s actions and strategies regarding Iran significantly impact international relations, global oil markets, and military tensions in the region.

2. Decision-Making

The decisions to engage militarily with Iran and to attempt to manage the fallout from such actions, including the strategic navigation of the Strait of Hormuz, originate from the Trump administration. Trump’s own statements, as reported, reflect strategic decisions made by his administration to neutralize what they perceive as a threat from Iran while also attempting to mitigate the economic repercussions of these military actions.

3. Framing and Responsibility

Critics, as mentioned in the article, accuse Trump of contradictory messaging and question the logic of his statements. The criticism focuses on the apparent inconsistency in claiming total destruction of Iran’s military capabilities while simultaneously acknowledging their capacity to disrupt major shipping routes. This critique does not misdirect responsibility; it directly addresses the statements made by the individual who holds the institutional power to make and act on such assessments—President Trump.

Analysis of Criticism

The criticism of Trump’s statements, especially from figures like Kyle Kulinski and X user “Robin,” hinges on highlighting what they perceive as a cognitive dissonance in Trump’s assertions. The critique is not unfounded given the apparent contradiction in the claims of total military destruction and the ongoing threat posed by Iran. However, it’s essential to consider that such statements could be strategic in nature, aiming to project strength while acknowledging ongoing security challenges. The framing of the critique, while sharp, does not misplace responsibility but rather questions the coherence and implications of Trump’s messaging strategy.

Conclusion

The criticism leveled at Trump for his contradictory statements about Iran’s military capabilities and threats does not misdirect responsibility. It squarely addresses the comments made by the individual with the ultimate institutional power over U.S. military and foreign policy decisions. While the nature of political rhetoric can sometimes involve hyperbole or simplification, the pointed critiques reflect legitimate concerns over the clarity and consistency of messaging on critical national security issues. The focus should perhaps be less on the mocking of perceived inconsistencies and more on seeking clarity about the actual state of Iran’s military capabilities and the strategic objectives of U.S. policy in the region. Rejecting lazy narratives requires acknowledging the complexity of military and diplomatic strategy, even as we hold leaders accountable for their public statements.
