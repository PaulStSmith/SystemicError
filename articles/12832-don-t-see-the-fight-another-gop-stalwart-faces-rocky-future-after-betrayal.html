'Don't see the fight': Another GOP stalwart faces rocky future after 'betrayal'

U.S. Senator Joni Ernst (R-IA) and John Thune (R-SD) face reporters after the weekly Senate Republican caucus policy luncheon on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., June 8, 2021. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

There are growing concerns among Republican party leaders that Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) may be the next lawmaker to decide to walk away from Congress after having what Politico's Rachel Bade called a "rough" year.

Appearing on MSNBC's "Morning Joe" to expand upon her report for Politico that Ernst is showing a general lack of enthusiasm for another campaign, Bade told the hosts that several battles Ernst has endured in the past year have soured her on staying in office after 2026.

"She has sort of gone through some of the motions suggesting that she could run again, she just hired a campaign manager, she's raised about $1 million for her reelection effort, which is not super impressive, but it's also not nothing," Bade began. "But behind the scenes, I'm hearing from Senate Republicans that they just don't see the fight in her anymore."

Pointing out that the Iowa Republican "has told people recently that she hasn't even decided whether she wants to run again," she added, "Number one is that she previously was in leadership. [Sen.] Tom Cotton (R-AR), who is actually a close friend of hers, ran against her and totally ousted her from that position. I'm told that she actually felt rather betrayed, and that was a huge sore point for her for a while."

"Then remember back in January, she totally got dragged by MAGA influencers online when she was sort of holding out her support for Pete Hegseth ––obviously had a lot of concerns about him," she continued. "Ultimately, she voted for him, but that was after this really ugly campaign where she was basically made an example of by the White House and White House allies."

"They even suggested that she wanted the job herself, which, I was told at the time was totally false and a smear campaign against her," she elaborated. "And that has sort of stuck with her. I mean, even her Senate Republican colleagues saw that and were like, wow, okay, we don't want that to happen to us, we're going to get in line. And so between both of those things, you know. It hasn't been exactly a great year for her."

Our Analysis:

The Waning Warrior: Joni Ernst's Congressional Conundrum

In a narrative that's as predictable as it is disheartening, Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) finds herself at a crossroads, teetering on the brink of political oblivion. The whispers of her dwindling enthusiasm for another term in Congress are growing louder, a testament to the brutal battles and personal betrayals that have marked her recent tenure. Rachel Bade's report for Politico, further dissected on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," paints a picture of a senator who has been through the wringer, emerging less inclined to endure another round.

The Signs of a Senator in Distress

Ernst's actions suggest a politician going through the motions rather than one gearing up for a vigorous campaign. The hiring of a campaign manager and the raising of $1 million for her reelection effort might seem commendable on the surface, but in the high-stakes arena of political fundraising, this is hardly a figure that screams confidence. It's more akin to dipping a toe in the water rather than diving in for the political swim of her life.

Betrayal Among Allies

The tale of betrayal by Sen. Tom Cotton (R-AR), a supposed ally, is particularly telling. Leadership battles are nothing new in the political sphere, but when they come at the cost of personal relationships and perceived loyalty, they leave a lasting sting. Ernst's ousting from her leadership position by Cotton is not just a professional setback; it's a personal affront that has evidently shaken her resolve.

MAGA Influencers and Public Humiliation

The saga with Pete Hegseth and the subsequent dragging through the mud by MAGA influencers adds another layer to Ernst's disillusionment. The very public smear campaign that ensued, suggesting Ernst desired Hegseth's position for herself, was not just a political maneuver; it was a character assassination. It's a stark reminder of the cutthroat nature of modern American politics, where allies can become adversaries overnight, and loyalty is often a one-way street.

The Impact of a Rough Year

The cumulative effect of these events on Ernst cannot be understated. To be publicly humiliated, to have one's loyalty questioned, and to endure betrayal by close friends would leave anyone questioning their future in such a toxic environment. The Iowa senator's hesitancy to commit to another run is not just a personal predicament; it's a symptom of a broader issue within the Republican Party and American politics as a whole.

Ernst's potential departure from Congress would not just signify the loss of a seat for the Republicans; it would underscore the increasingly unbearable toxicity of political life in Washington, D.C. When seasoned politicians like Ernst begin to question their place in the system, it's a clear sign that the system itself is broken.

In the end, Ernst's struggle is emblematic of the broader challenges facing those who wish to serve their country but find themselves caught in a relentless cycle of political warfare. Whether she decides to fight another day or hang up her gloves, her ordeal should serve as a wake-up call to the corrosive nature of current political discourse and the urgent need for reform.
