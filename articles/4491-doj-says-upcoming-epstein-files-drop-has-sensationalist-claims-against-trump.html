DOJ says upcoming Epstein files drop has 'sensationalist claims against Trump'

President Donald Trump’s Justice Department wants the American people to know that the still-unreleased files of the late Jeffrey Epstein contain “sensationalist claims against Trump.”

The Justice Department officials previously said some files included fake or false materials that were sent to the FBI by the public and may “contain untrue and sensationalist claims against President Trump.”

“The US justice department [sic] is to release nearly 50,000 ‘missing’ Jeffrey Epstein files this week,” reported The Times on Wednesday. “The documents, which are referred to in the tranche released in late January but appear to have been withheld by the department, potentially contain unverified claims about President Trump, including FBI reports detailing a woman’s unsubstantiated allegations of sexual misconduct against him.”

Specifically, the files are supposed to contain FBI memos summarizing an interview with a woman who came forward after Epstein was arrested in 2019. During that conversation, the woman said she had been sexually assaulted by both Epstein and Trump in the mid-1980s when she was a minor.

Despite these allegations, a White House spokesperson told The Times that “just as President Trump has said, he’s been totally exonerated on anything relating to Epstein.”

Even some of Trump’s fellow Republicans have turned on him on the issue of the Epstein files, including Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky and former Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene of Georgia.

“Release the documents,” Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.), a member of the Judiciary Committee, said in a statement. “Redact the names of the victims. Don’t release photographs, naked or otherwise, of minors. Release the documents. This is not going to go away until there is full disclosure.”

Kennedy continued, “This is not going away until there’s full disclosure and the American people want to know, and they’re entitled to know, who if anyone, did Epstein traffic these women to … and why they weren’t prosecuted.”

Trump’s relationship with Epstein goes back to 1985, when the former purchased his Mar-a-Lago estate near Epstein in Florida. By 1989, Trump was attending a party on a yacht called Lady Ghislaine after Epstein’s associate and billionaire Robert Maxwell’s daughter, Ghislaine Maxwell. In 1992 Trump and Epstein were recorded by NBC News partying with Buffalo Bills cheerleaders and other guests, including many young girls. At one point Trump and Epstein stare at girls on the dance floor and Trump appears to say “Look at her, back there.... she’s hot.” They continued to regularly socialize through the 1990s and 2000s.

According to CNN’s Aaron Blake, Trump’s attempts to bury the scandal have only elevated its profile.

“In a statement, the White House called the allegations against Trump ‘false and sensationalist’ and pointed to a previous DOJ statement that ‘some of the documents contain untrue and sensationalist claims against President Trump,’” Blake wrote. “But the plausibility of the woman’s claims isn’t the main point; the point is that an increasingly politicized Justice Department — one where a massive banner of Trump was hung last week — did not release documents containing allegations about the president.”

He added, “In a vacuum, that would be problematic. But next to everything else, it’s really bad.”

Our Analysis:

The Epstein Files: A Tangled Web of Sensationalism and Silence

The Justice Department’s latest maneuver in the ongoing saga surrounding the late Jeffrey Epstein’s unreleased files is nothing short of a theatrical performance, designed to preemptively discredit any allegations against former President Donald Trump. This strategy, however, raises more questions than it answers, casting a long shadow over the department’s impartiality and Trump’s vehement denials of wrongdoing.

Sensational Claims or Convenient Scapegoats?

Officials from Trump’s Justice Department have been quick to label the unreleased Epstein files as containing “sensationalist claims.” This preemptive strike aims to undermine the credibility of the files before they even see the light of day. It’s a classic move from the political playbook: discredit, deny, and deflect. Yet, the very insistence on highlighting the potential for “untrue and sensationalist claims” only serves to amplify public curiosity and skepticism.

The Allegations: At the heart of the controversy are allegations of sexual misconduct against Trump, detailed in FBI memos that summarize interviews with a woman who claims she was sexually assaulted by both Epstein and Trump in the mid-1980s. These are serious accusations that demand thorough investigation, not preemptive dismissal as mere sensationalism.

The Call for Transparency

Amidst the chaos, voices from both sides of the political aisle are calling for full disclosure. Figures such as Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.), former Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-Ga.), and Sen. John Kennedy (R-La.) have advocated for the release of the documents, with appropriate redactions to protect victims’ identities. This bipartisan push underscores a fundamental truth: the American public deserves transparency, especially when allegations of this magnitude are at stake.

Trump’s Tenuous Relationship with Epstein

Trump’s associations with Epstein only add layers of complexity to the narrative. Their social interactions, documented through photographs and video footage, span decades. From parties at Mar-a-Lago to gatherings with young cheerleaders, these documented moments paint a picture of familiarity and camaraderie between Trump and Epstein. These historical ties do not, in themselves, prove any wrongdoing on Trump’s part. However, they do warrant a closer examination of Trump’s insistence on his total exoneration.

The Politicization of Justice

CNN’s Aaron Blake hits the nail on the head when he points out the problematic nature of the Justice Department’s handling of these documents. The department’s decision not to release documents containing allegations against Trump, especially under an administration that has not shied away from displaying its political allegiances, is deeply troubling. This action—or lack thereof—contributes to a growing perception of a Justice Department that is increasingly politicized and less focused on impartial justice.

A Path Forward

The saga of the Epstein files is far from over. As calls for transparency grow louder, the Justice Department finds itself at a crossroads. Will it continue to shield Trump from public scrutiny, or will it prioritize the pursuit of truth and justice? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the American people deserve full disclosure and accountability, no matter where the chips may fall.

In conclusion, the Epstein files controversy is a stark reminder of the intricate web of power, privilege, and secrecy that often surrounds those at the highest echelons of society. As we navigate this complex narrative, we must demand transparency, hold the powerful to account, and ensure that justice is served without fear or favor. The integrity of our institutions and the principles of our democracy depend on it.
