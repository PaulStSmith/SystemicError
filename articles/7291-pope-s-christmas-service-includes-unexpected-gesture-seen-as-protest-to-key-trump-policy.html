Pope's Christmas service includes 'unexpected gesture' seen as protest to key Trump policy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 26, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Cloaked Rebuke: Pope Leo XIV’s “Unexpected Gesture”

Pope Leo XIV conducts Mass in the Sistine Chapel at the Vatican, May 9, 2025. Vatican Media/Simone Risoluti ­Handout via REUTERS

Pope Leo XIV made an “unexpected gesture” at his Christmas service that is being taken as a dig at Trump‘s key policy.

The pope, who has been accused of making “pointed remarks” aimed at Trump in the past, gave a Christmas speech largely devoid of political references. But the issue of immigration crept in, as Reuters reports:

“Leo, celebrating his first Christmas after being elected in May by the world’s cardinals to succeed the late Pope Francis, has a quieter, more diplomatic style than his predecessor and usually refrains from making political references in his sermons. In a later Christmas blessing, the pope, who has made care for immigrants a key theme of his early papacy, also lamented the situation for migrants and refugees who ‘traverse the American continent’,” the report states. “Leo, who has in the past criticized U.S. President Donald Trump’s immigration crackdown, did not mention Trump. In a Christmas Eve sermon on Wednesday, the pope said refusing to help the poor and strangers was tantamount to rejecting God himself.”

He also raised eyebrows when he reportedly stepped out of the church into bad weather to great those in attendance.

“Pope Leo’s Christmas celebrations began with an unexpected gesture of hospitality. Before the liturgy even started, the newly elected pontiff — the first American to lead the Church — walked out into St. Peter’s Square to welcome about 5,000 people standing under a cold downpour,” according to another report. “The basilica was filled to its 6,000-person capacity, yet Leo made sure those stuck outside knew they weren’t forgotten. ‘I admire and respect and thank you for your courage and your wanting to be here this evening,’ he told them, praising their perseverance ‘even in this weather’.”

It continued:

“It was a small moment, but a telling one: the pope literally stepped beyond the comfortable confines of the church to be with people who had no room inside. That image set the stage for the message he would soon deliver in his homily.”

Christopher Hale, a ’20 Democratic nominee for Congress, connected it to immigration.

“Midnight Mass at St. Peter’s Basilica was standing-room-only — and thousands stood outside in the cold rain. Before the liturgy began, Pope Leo stepped away from the altar and walked into the square to greet them. It wasn’t just an act of kindness. It was a visible sign of the homily to come: a society that shuts out migrants and the poor shuts out Christ,” according to Hale.

That message was also shared by ex-GOP lawmaker and prominent Trump critic Barbara Comstock.

Our Analysis:

A Cloaked Rebuke: Pope Leo XIV’s “Unexpected Gesture”

In the grand theater of global politics, subtle gestures often speak louder than bombastic declarations. Pope Leo XIV’s actions during his Christmas service are a masterclass in this nuanced form of communication. The pontiff, who ascended to the helm of the Catholic Church earlier this year, has already distinguished himself by choosing a path markedly different from his predecessor, Pope Francis. However, beneath the veneer of a “quieter, more diplomatic style,” Leo’s recent gesture—a seemingly benign act of hospitality—unveils a potent critique of former U.S. President Donald Trump’s immigration policies.

The Gesture: A Symbol of Inclusivity

Pope Leo’s decision to step out into the cold downpour to greet about 5,000 people who couldn’t find room inside St. Peter’s Basilica is emblematic of a leader who not only preaches about inclusivity and care for the marginalized but also walks the walk—literally. This act of welcoming those left in the cold is a vivid metaphor for the Pope’s stance on immigration and care for the “strangers” among us, echoing the biblical call to welcome the alien and the sojourner.

Subtle Dig or Sermon in Action?

While the article carefully notes that Pope Leo did not explicitly mention Trump, the context is hard to ignore. Trump’s tenure was marked by a hardline approach to immigration, characterized by policies that were widely criticized by human rights advocates for their exclusionary and often inhumane treatment of migrants and refugees. Pope Leo, in his sermon, equated the refusal to help the poor and strangers with rejecting God—a statement that, while not naming names, clearly rebukes the ethos of the Trump administration and its supporters.

Christopher Hale and Barbara Comstock, figures from opposite ends of the political spectrum, both see Pope Leo’s actions as indicative of a broader message: a society that excludes migrants and the poor is, fundamentally, a society that excludes Christ. This message, rooted in Christian doctrine, transcends political boundaries, offering a moral critique of policies that dehumanize and marginalize.

The Real Question: Will It Matter?

While Pope Leo’s Christmas gesture and the accompanying sermon send a clear message of solidarity with migrants and a call for more humane policies, one must ponder the real-world impact of such actions. The Catholic Church, with its billion-strong following, certainly wields significant moral influence. Yet, in the face of deeply entrenched political divisions and the rise of nationalist sentiments in various parts of the world, the efficacy of moral suasion is an open question.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection

Pope Leo XIV’s “unexpected gesture” serves as a poignant reminder of the power of leadership that embodies the principles it preaches. In a world rife with division, his actions offer a beacon of hope for a more inclusive and compassionate society. However, the ultimate impact of his message depends on its reception by those in power and the broader public. Will it be dismissed as mere pontifical platitudes, or will it inspire a genuine reevaluation of how we treat the most vulnerable among us? Only time will tell, but for now, Pope Leo’s Christmas service stands as a subtle yet profound critique of exclusionary politics.
