GOP pollster 'seeing sirens' as new poll finds women abandoning Trump in droves

Based on an article by David Edwards

Paulo SantosAugust 28, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Women Are Leaving, and Republicans Own the Bill

The Numbers

A Republican pollster just described a problem that cannot be hand-waved away: women are moving sharply away from the GOP, with Democrats ahead by 18 points among women in her survey. Trump’s approval with women sits at 32 percent, his own voters are splitting off, and the issues driving the break are brutally ordinary: the cost of living, the economy, health care, Medicare, and Social Security.

That is the context. The political meaning is larger.

Power Has a Name

The people in power are not anonymous “vibes” or “market forces.” Republicans control the presidency, and Trump is the face of that control. When women say the economy is failing them, they are not describing a mysterious weather system. They are reacting to the party holding the levers of government and claiming credit or blame as convenient.

That matters because the GOP has spent years treating women’s concerns as a polling problem instead of a governing mandate. The party is not losing women because women have become more emotional or less informed. It is losing them because it is offering too little beyond grievance, denial, and escalation.

The Excuse Machine

Christine Matthews’s line about Trump spinning everything is more revealing than any campaign memo. That is the Republican operating system: deflect, relabel, blame someone else, then act surprised when reality does not cooperate.

This is not innocent confusion. It is a political technique. When prices stay high and the White House says “not us,” voters are being asked to absorb the consequences while the people with power escape responsibility. That works until it doesn’t. Women, in this survey, are no longer buying the story.

What Women Are Pricing In

The numbers on married women and white women matter because Republicans have long treated those blocs as dependable insurance. That insurance is expiring. The party’s sales pitch used to rest on culture war loyalty layered over economic anxiety. But affordability is not a side issue. It is the issue.

Kat Cammack’s statement is the only part of the GOP response that sounds remotely grounded in reality: nobody owes them a vote. She is right, though perhaps not in the way she intends. Tax breaks on tips and overtime do not erase a broader record of governing by symbolism while leaving women to manage the actual cost of daily life. A doubled child tax credit is a talking point, not a substitute for a durable economic settlement.

The Real Pattern

The deeper pattern is simple: authoritarian-leaning politics always wants obedience without accountability. It floods the zone with blame, insists that every failure belongs to someone else, and then frames electoral backlash as an unfair misunderstanding.

Women are not misunderstanding. They are responding to a party that wants their votes but not their judgment, their labor but not their leverage, their trust but not their power. That is why these numbers matter. They are not just a warning for Republicans; they are evidence that political contempt has a cost, and women are making Republicans pay it.
