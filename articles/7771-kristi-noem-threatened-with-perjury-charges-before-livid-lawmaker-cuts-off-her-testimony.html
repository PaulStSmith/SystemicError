Kristi Noem threatened with perjury charges before livid lawmaker cuts off her testimony

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by DHS Secretary Kristi Noem

The former chair of the House Homeland Security Committee lost his patience with DHS Secretary Kristi Noem during a heated confrontation that was televised nationally on Thursday morning.

Toward the end of his allotted time, Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS) repeatedly asked the embattled the Donald Trump appointee over which administration vetted the refugee from Afghanistan who has been accused of shooting two National Guardsmen, one critically, in Washington, D.C., weeks ago.

With Thompson pointing out that Noem has been blaming President Joe Biden for the shooting, he asked her repeatedly to admit that Trump had a hand in Rahmanullah Lakanwal’s asylum approval earlier this year.

Pressed on the case, Noem repeatedly stated, “The application on the asylum was thoroughly filled out by information that was gathered by the Biden administration.”

“So the Biden administration approved the asylum?” the Democratic lawmaker asked.

“The asylum application was put into place under the rules established by the Biden administration,” Noem parried again as Thompson asked the chair to reclaim his time.

“I don’t want to charge, file, perjury charges against you, but I’m of the opinion that the Trump administration, DHS, your DHS, approved the asylum application,” Thompson warned her.

“The asylum application moved forward under all of the information and vetting processes that were put in place under the Biden administration, which is when vetting happened,” Noem repeated. “And that’s what President Trump has changed under President Trump’s administration ---”

That led Thompson to cut her off with, “It’s obvious you don’t want to answer the question,” before asking Trump appointee, Joe Kent, of the National Counterterrorism Center who was seated to Noem’s right, to take “a shot at it,” where Kent repeated Noem’s talking point to Thompson’s frustration.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: A Masterclass by DHS Secretary Kristi Noem

In what can only be described as a political circus, former House Homeland Security Committee Chair, Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS), found himself entangled in a verbal joust with DHS Secretary Kristi Noem. The stage was a nationally televised confrontation that not only showcased the art of deflection but also underlined the deep partisan divides that plague our nation’s discourse on security and immigration.

At the heart of this heated exchange was the questioning of who bore the responsibility for vetting Rahmanullah Lakanwal, the Afghan refugee accused of a grave crime. Thompson, with a persistence that could only be fueled by genuine frustration, sought to unravel the narrative that the Biden administration was solely to blame for the oversight. Noem, a Donald Trump appointee, stuck to her guns, or rather, her talking points, with a tenacity that was both admirable and maddening.

The Blame Game

Noem’s relentless assertion that “The application on the asylum was thoroughly filled out by information that was gathered by the Biden administration” was a masterclass in deflection. Her ability to navigate the question without directly answering it was, in a twisted sense, impressive. But let’s not mince words here: this was a blatant attempt to sidestep accountability.

The Democratic lawmaker’s pointed questioning sought to underscore a critical fact: the Trump administration, under which Noem served, had a hand in the asylum approval process. Yet, Noem’s responses were nothing short of a verbal mirage, designed to shift the blame entirely onto the Biden administration.

A Circular Dance of Evasion

As the exchange progressed, or more accurately, regressed, it became painfully clear that Noem had no intention of conceding any ground. Her repeated insistence that the vetting processes in question were put in place under the Biden administration, despite the application moving forward during Trump’s tenure, was a testament to the lengths some officials will go to avoid admitting any semblance of responsibility.

Thompson’s frustration was palpable and justifiably so. The refusal to acknowledge the shared responsibility in the vetting process not only undermines the integrity of the discussion but also insults the intelligence of the public. It’s a dangerous game, playing fast and loose with facts, especially on matters as serious as national security and the vetting of refugees.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure to Communicate

This televised spectacle was more than just a heated debate over policy and administration responsibility; it was a glaring example of the systemic failures in our political discourse. When officials, tasked with the safety and security of our nation, choose deflection over transparency, it erodes public trust and hinders meaningful progress.

In a world where accountability seems increasingly like a relic of the past, Noem’s performance was a stark reminder of the challenges that lie ahead. It’s high time for a return to a discourse grounded in fact, responsibility, and, above all else, honesty. But, as this exchange has shown, we have a long road ahead.
