Corn was referring to Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-MO), a junior senator elected in 2022 who has recently shown he is much more capable of translating Trumpism into a “coherent ideology that melds nativism and oligarchism.” Corn wrote in a new column for The Daily Beast about one of Schmitt’s speeches from November, where Schmitt laid out his vision for America.

Corn described Schmitt’s speech as an “articulate and sophisticated embrace of blood and soil.”

“His message was that America is a land not for those who have come here drawn by its ideals and promises but for those whose ancestors were on the wagon trains and who conquered the frontier,” Corn wrote.

“All of this is present in the Trumpism espoused by Dear Leader,” Corn continued. “But Schmitt more aptly combines explicit nativism, implied white supremacy, purported economic populism, and anti-elitism into a neat package, and he’s a damn good salesman for this noxious brew.”

“Schmitt’s fact-twisting—the claim that Soros’ organization supplies bricks to violent protesters has been debunked repeatedly but remains a right-wing article of faith—and his amnesia about January 6 and other horrific violence from the right show that he is yet just another MAGA fabulist willing to lie and misrepresent to exploit hate, paranoia, and distrust to score political points,” Corn wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Rise of a MAGA Fabulist: A Closer Look at Eric Schmitt’s Dangerous Ideological Cocktail

Columnist David Corn’s recent warning about Senator Eric Schmitt (R-MO) as a potentially dangerous heir to the MAGA movement should set off alarm bells for anyone concerned about the trajectory of American politics. In his column for The Daily Beast, Corn paints a vivid picture of Schmitt as not just another run-of-the-mill MAGA devotee, but as a sophisticated manipulator of its most toxic elements. Let’s dissect the claims and why they signal a worrying evolution of Trumpism into something even more insidious.

Schmitt’s Vision: A Toxic Brew of Nativism and Oligarchism

Corn’s analysis of Schmitt’s speech from November is particularly damning. Schmitt, according to Corn, has managed to articulate a version of Trumpism that is both more coherent and more dangerous. This vision, described as an “articulate and sophisticated embrace of blood and soil,” is nothing short of alarming. Schmitt’s America is not a land of opportunity for all, but rather a playground for those whose ancestors “were on the wagon trains and conquered the frontier.” This rhetoric is not just exclusionary; it’s fundamentally un-American, betraying the nation’s ideals of diversity and inclusion.

The Dangerous Alchemy of MAGA Ideology

Corn’s assertion that Schmitt combines “explicit nativism, implied white supremacy, purported economic populism, and anti-elitism” into a “neat package” deserves closer scrutiny. Each of these elements on their own is deeply problematic, but together, they form a volatile mix that can easily exploit societal divisions. Schmitt, as Corn points out, is a “damn good salesman” for this ideology, making him particularly dangerous. It’s the evolution of MAGA from a chaotic set of grievances under Trump to a more coherent, yet equally toxic ideology under Schmitt that should worry us.

The Lies and Amnesia of a MAGA Fabulist

Corn’s critique of Schmitt’s willingness to embrace debunked conspiracy theories and his selective amnesia about events like January 6th is a stark reminder of the MAGA movement’s detachment from reality. Schmitt’s “fact-twisting” and exploitation of “hate, paranoia, and distrust” are indicative of a broader trend within the MAGA sphere, where facts are malleable and history is rewritten to suit the narrative. This is not just about political opportunism; it’s a calculated assault on truth and democracy.

A Call to Vigilance

David Corn’s column is more than a critique of Eric Schmitt; it’s a clarion call to those who care about the future of American democracy. The rise of figures like Schmitt, who can package the MAGA movement’s most dangerous elements into a coherent and appealing ideology, represents a significant threat. It’s not just the spread of nativism, oligarchism, or white supremacy that we should be concerned about; it’s the emergence of leaders capable of selling this ideology to the public in sophisticated and dangerous new ways.

In conclusion, while Schmitt may be a new face in the MAGA movement, the ideas he peddles are as old and as dangerous as ever. What makes him particularly worrying is his ability to present these ideas in a way that may resonate with a broader audience. The fight against this kind of ideology requires vigilance, a commitment to truth, and a determination to uphold the values that truly make America great.
