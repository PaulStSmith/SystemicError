DOJ reamed as judge finds it likely retaliated against group that criticized Trump

A federal judge in Maryland has issued a temporary restraining order against the Justice Department for terminating grants from the Office on Violence Against Women to the American Bar Association, finding that the decision was likely retaliation for the group's criticism of President Donald Trump and therefore a violation of the First Amendment.

The grants provided $3.2 million to the bar association to train lawyers to represent survivors of domestic and sexual violence. After Attorney General Pam Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche canceled the payments, they said it was because the association was engaged in "activist causes" that ran against the Justice Department's mission, but did not elaborate further. However, per The Guardian, this decision came after the bar association publicly said Trump's mass firings at federal agencies were unlawful, and Trump hit back, calling the legal trade group a "snooty" bunch of "leftist lawyers."

The ABA sued over the cancellations, arguing it amounted to retaliation for political speech. The DOJ disputed this and further argued the court lacked jurisdiction to even hear the case.

In a ruling issued on Wednesday, U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper, an appointee of former President Barack Obama, sided with the ABA, finding that the Trump administration's arguments made no sense.

"The government claims that it had a nonretaliatory motive for terminating the grants: They no longer aligned with DOJ’s priorities," wrote Cooper. "But the government has not identified any nonretaliatory DOJ priorities, much less explained why they were suddenly deemed inconsistent with the goals of the affected grants."

Moreover, the judge continued, "the government’s different treatment of other grantees suggests this justification is pretextual. DOJ did not terminate any other OVW grants, and, at oral argument, the government conceded that other grant recipients continue to conduct similar training functions with OVW money ... The government has offered no nonretaliatory explanation for why it continues to fund these other OVW grantees after terminating the ABA’s grants, or why these other grantees’ projects still effectuate DOJ’s priorities while the ABA’s do not. Finally, DOJ also purported to terminate two grants that, by their terms, had already ended, making it even less plausible that DOJ conducted an individualized analysis of whether each grant aligned with DOJ policy."

"Based on all this, the Court cannot but conclude that the ABA is likely to succeed on its claim that Defendants terminated the agreements because of its protected activity in violation of the First Amendment," concluded Cooper.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Shadows of Retaliation: A Federal Judge's Stand Against Trump-Era Vendettas

In a recent ruling that reads like a script from a legal thriller, a federal judge in Maryland has delivered a stinging rebuke to the Justice Department's decision to terminate grants from the Office on Violence Against Women to the American Bar Association (ABA). This decision, cloaked in the veneer of bureaucratic prudence, was unmasked as a likely act of retaliation by the Trump administration against the ABA for its criticism of former President Donald Trump. Let's dissect this judicial drama, where the principles of free speech and the stark realities of political retribution collide.

The Heart of the Matter

At its core, this dispute revolves around a $3.2 million grant intended to train lawyers to represent survivors of domestic and sexual violence—a noble and necessary cause if there ever was one. Yet, in what appears to be a petulant response to criticism, Attorney General Pam Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche axed this funding, citing the ABA's engagement in "activist causes" as incongruent with the Justice Department's mission. This reasoning, as flimsy as it is, fails to mask the retaliatory stench wafting from the decision, especially in light of Trump's derogatory remarks branding the ABA as a "snooty" assembly of "leftist lawyers."

The Legal Battlefield

The ABA, standing its ground, sued over the cancellations, framing it as a clear-cut case of retaliation for political speech—a fundamental breach of the First Amendment. The Justice Department's counter, claiming the court lacked jurisdiction, was as weak as it was transparent. U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper, an Obama appointee, saw right through it. Cooper's ruling is a masterpiece of judicial clarity, systematically dismantling the Trump administration's arguments and exposing them for the farce they are.

Judge Cooper's Verdict

Cooper's findings are damning. The government's attempt to rationalize its actions falls apart under scrutiny. The claim that the ABA's grants were terminated because they no longer aligned with the DOJ's priorities is eviscerated by the lack of any "nonretaliatory DOJ priorities" that could justify such a decision. The disparity in treatment between the ABA and other grantees, who continue to receive funding for similar training functions, further erodes the government's position, highlighting the pretextual nature of their justification.

Moreover, the DOJ's baffling decision to terminate two grants that had already ended smacks of a farcical attempt to wield power without purpose, further undermining any pretense of a principled stand against the ABA.

Conclusion: The First Amendment Triumphs

Cooper's ruling reaffirms the sanctity of the First Amendment, serving as a bulwark against the capricious use of power to silence critics. The Trump administration's efforts to penalize the ABA for its protected speech not only undermines the fundamental principles of free speech but also jeopardizes critical services for survivors of domestic and sexual violence.

In the end, this ruling is a clear message to those in power: the First Amendment cannot be trampled upon, and attempts to retaliate against political speech will not be tolerated. It's a victory not just for the ABA, but for all who believe in the principles of free expression and the rule of law. Let this serve as a reminder that in the United States, no one—not even the President—is above the Constitution.
