'Are you for real?' GOP senator under fire for response to Trump taking down racist post

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 8, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Masterclass in Political Cowardice: Cassidy’s Feeble Response to Trump’s Racism

Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA) speaks on Capitol Hill. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA) found himself on the receiving end of even more derision on Friday night over his comments on a racist video shared by President Donald Trump.

The 79-year-old president shared a video created by the conservative Patriot News Outlet that had an image of Barack and Michelle Obama’s smiling faces superimposed on the bodies of apes in a jungle, with a clip of the 1961 song “The Lion Sleeps Tonight.” The post shocked the consciences of even some Republican allies.

The post was eventually taken down.

But Cassidy — already the subject of widespread condemnation over his decision to cast a pivotal vote to confirm Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as Secretary of Health and Human Services — found himself under fire again when he thanked Trump for taking the post down.

“Thank you to President Trump for taking down the post about the Obamas,” wrote Cassidy on X. “He made significant inroads with his outreach in the African American community which we need to continue. His post sent the wrong message despite how it may have been originally intended.”

Cassidy’s tepid criticism led to immediate ridicule on social media.

Fred Guttenberg, whose daughter Jaime was slain in the Parkland school shooting, hit back on X.

“Are you for real? He knowingly posted racist trash. Racism is in his DNA. And you thank him? You pathetic enablers are an embarrassment to America. Your tweet reinforces for me that the enablers are the problem. In 9 months, America will vote you back to the minority.”

X commentator Lynn G. Henning added, “Is this a remotely serious response from an allegedly substantive Senator?”

Janice Hough, a supporter of Minnesota Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar, wrote on X, “How it was ‘originally intended?’ It was intended to be racist and hateful. Just like most of what he does. For shame Dr. Cassidy, for shame.”

Ron Filipkowski, editor-in-chief of the progressive outlet Meidas Touch, chided on X, “I found the dumbest most pathetic post on the internet today.”

TV Anchor Pedro Andrade simply wrote, “Pathetic.”

Dr. Drew Timmons wrote, “One of the worst Tweets of all time. Incredible how bad this is.”

California congressional candidate Eric Garcia wrote, “How do republicans stand up without a spine?”

Comedian Tom Ryan wrote on X, “Quick question Senator Cassidy. Did removing the post undo the impact of the original post? It didn’t and you know it didn‘t.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Political Cowardice: Cassidy’s Feeble Response to Trump’s Racism

In what reads like a parody of political spinelessness, Senator Bill Cassidy (R-LA) has once again demonstrated the shocking depths to which some elected officials will sink to avoid confronting the vile behavior of their party leaders. This time, Cassidy found himself embroiled in controversy over his response to a blatantly racist video shared by President Donald Trump—a move that should have been an easy slam dunk for any politician with a shred of integrity. Instead, Cassidy chose to thank Trump for removing the offensive post, a gesture so pitiful it would be laughable if it weren’t so deeply troubling.

Let’s dissect Cassidy’s response, or rather, his non-response, to this incident. Trump, in a move that surprises no one familiar with his modus operandi, shared a video created by the conservative Patriot News Outlet featuring Barack and Michelle Obama’s faces superimposed on the bodies of apes. This is not just racist; it’s the kind of bottom-feeder racism that one might expect to find in the darkest corners of the internet, not on the Twitter feed of a former President of the United States.

In the wake of this abhorrent action, Cassidy took to social media not to condemn Trump, but to thank him for taking down the post. His statement, dripping with an almost surreal level of detachment, thanked Trump for his “outreach in the African American community,” as if a single act of removing a post could erase the impact of sharing such vile content in the first place.

“Thank you to President Trump for taking down the post about the Obamas,” Cassidy wrote, in what can only be described as a masterclass in missing the point. “He made significant inroads with his outreach in the African American community which we need to continue. His post sent the wrong message despite how it may have been originally intended.”

The backlash was swift and merciless, with voices ranging from Fred Guttenberg, a father forever scarred by the Parkland school shooting, to TV anchor Pedro Andrade condemning Cassidy’s response as, at best, woefully inadequate, and at worst, complicit in the very racism Trump’s post embodied. Critics pointed out the obvious: Trump’s actions were not a misguided attempt at humor but a deliberate propagation of racism—a fact Cassidy’s statement glaringly ignores.

It’s important to note Cassidy’s historical context here. This isn’t his first rodeo in the arena of controversial decisions. His vote to confirm Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as Secretary of Health and Human Services already had him in hot water. This latest incident only adds to the narrative of a politician more concerned with party lines than moral clarity.

Cassidy’s tepid criticism and subsequent ridicule on social media highlight a broader issue within the Republican Party—a seeming inability or unwillingness to unequivocally condemn racism within its ranks. Cassidy had an opportunity to take a stand, to show that there are lines that should not be crossed, regardless of political allegiance. Instead, he chose to thank the man who crossed that line, thereby normalizing a level of discourse that should have no place in our political landscape.

In a world where actions speak louder than words, Cassidy’s action—or lack thereof—speaks volumes. It tells us that the bar for what constitutes acceptable behavior in politics has not just been lowered; it has been buried. And for those looking for courageous leadership in these troubled times, Cassidy’s response serves as a stark reminder of just how elusive that quality has become in certain quarters of our political system.

In nine months, America will have the opportunity to respond at the ballot box. One can only hope that the voters remember moments like this—a moment when a senator could have stood up for decency and chose instead to bow to the basest elements of his party. Cassidy’s failure to condemn Trump’s racism is not just a personal failing; it’s a systemic error that speaks to the broader moral bankruptcy at play in today’s political arena.
