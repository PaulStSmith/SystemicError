White House 'in a bind' as it seeks GOP help with mess Elon Musk's DOGE created: report

Elon Musk leaves following a luncheon with members of the Senate Republican Conference on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., March 5, 2025. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

A decision by Donald Trump to let the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) dictate pulling funding from government agencies has put the White House in a corner that will require a bail-out from the Republican-controlled Congress.

However, as an MSNBC report notes, GOP lawmakers don't appear to want to put themselves on the line by actually approving the yanking of funds.

As Devin O’Connor, of the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities wrote on Saturday, the Trump White House is reluctant to ask GOP lawmakers to step in to take responsibility for the cuts proposed by Elon Musk's DOGE -- and for good reason.

As he wrote, the White House is in "a bind of its own creation" because it can't "legally" cancel funding, it can only delay it.

That's where Congress comes in.

According to O'Connor, "Under that law, the Senate can approve a rescission package with only a majority vote — no filibuster allowed. But even with that lowered hurdle, the Trump administration is struggling to find much among its unlawful freezing of funds that at least 50 Republican senators are willing to publicly support," adding, "Part of the White House’s problem is that any rescission proposal transmitted to Congress highlights that the administration has been illegally impounding funds — both those included in that package and, perhaps more crucially, those not included."

As the report notes, Republicans are leery of having unpopular cuts linked to their vote, and that a proposed rescission package has already "run into a buzzsaw of opposition from House and Senate Republicans.”

Asking why the GOP-majority Congress isn't leaping to take credit for the gutting of the government that they have publicly praised, O'Connor offered, "The answer is clear: More than 100 days since the White House began illegally impounding funds, it seems that no one wants to publicly own DOGE’s actions that continue to hurt people across the country."

Our Analysis:

The White House's Corner: Trump, DOGE, and GOP's Reluctance

In a move that could only be described as both audacious and reckless, Donald Trump has once again demonstrated his penchant for political maneuvers that leave both logic and legality in the dust. By allowing the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), spearheaded by none other than Elon Musk, to dictate the withdrawal of funding from government agencies, Trump has effectively painted the White House into a corner. A corner that, according to an MSNBC report, requires an unlikely bailout from a Republican-controlled Congress that appears to have more sense, or perhaps more accurately, more concern for their political futures, than to back such a contentious move.

GOP's Cold Feet

Devin O’Connor of the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities hits the nail on the head, describing the situation as a "bind of its own creation" for the Trump White House. The administration finds itself in a legal quagmire, being unable to "legally" cancel funding outright but instead only able to delay it. This is where the GOP Congress comes in, or rather, should come in, if it weren't for their glaring reluctance to support the rescission of funds proposed by DOGE. It’s not just a matter of legal technicalities; it’s a matter of political survival.

The report highlights a critical aspect of this debacle - the administration's actions are not just unpopular; they're illegal. The attempt to impound funds unlawly has not only put the government's functioning at risk but has also exposed GOP lawmakers to potential political fallout. The fact that any rescission proposal would shine a glaring light on the administration's illegal activities is a bitter pill that at least 50 Republican senators are understandably hesitant to swallow.

The Buzzsaw of Opposition

The resistance from House and Senate Republicans is not just a minor hiccup; it's a "buzzsaw of opposition". This phrase alone should signal to the Trump administration the sheer folly of their actions. The GOP-majority Congress, which one might expect to be in lockstep with Trump's agenda, has instead shown a remarkable (and perhaps unexpected) level of prudence in not wanting to be associated with these unpopular and illegal cuts.

The Unanswered Question

O’Connor poses a question that gets to the heart of the matter: why isn’t the GOP-majority Congress jumping at the chance to take credit for the dismantling of government they have so publicly championed? The answer, while clear, is as damning as it is depressing. More than 100 days into this farce, it seems that no one, not even those who have historically advocated for slashing government spending, wants to publicly own DOGE’s actions. Actions, it must be emphasized, that continue to inflict harm across the country.

Conclusion

The situation encapsulates a broader issue within the current political landscape - a stark willingness to endorse radical measures behind closed doors but a cowardly reluctance to support them publicly when faced with the potential for public backlash. This saga is not just a reflection of Trump's governance by chaos but also a testament to the GOP's ongoing struggle between ideological purity and political pragmatism. In the end, it appears that self-preservation might just be the only bipartisan belief in Washington.
