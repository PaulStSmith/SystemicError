The Smithsonian's Cowardice: When History Is Rewritten by the State

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 5, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In a move that would make Orwell blush, the Smithsonian Institution has reportedly caved to political pressure from the Trump-aligned White House and sanitized its exhibit on presidential impeachments. According to journalist Marc Fisher, the American History Museum has removed Donald Trump from its list of presidents who “seriously faced removal,” now claiming that only Andrew Johnson, Richard Nixon, and Bill Clinton qualify for that dubious honor.

Let’s be clear: this isn’t a matter of nuance or interpretation. This is an outright fabrication—a revision of recent, well-documented, and nationally traumatic events. Trump was impeached twice by the House of Representatives. Twice. That alone makes him historically unique. The argument that he didn’t “seriously face removal” is an absurd deflection rooted in the Senate’s cowardice, not the legitimacy or seriousness of the charges brought against him.

Erasing Reality: A Political Act Disguised as Curatorship

Museums are not supposed to bend to the political winds of the moment. They are meant to preserve facts, culture, and truth for future generations. The Smithsonian is not some fringe blog or cable news outlet—it's the national museum system. Its duty is to the American people, not to whichever megalomaniac occupies the Oval Office.

The moment a museum changes the historical record to avoid offending political power, it has ceased to be a museum. It becomes propaganda. This shift is not subtle; it’s a red siren for anyone paying attention. Sarah Matthews, a former Trump White House official herself, rightly called it out: "When a history museum rewrites history to appease the president, it stops being a museum and starts being state propaganda. Straight-up Orwellian."

Orwellian indeed. In 1984, Orwell wrote of a dystopian regime that rewrote the past to control the present. The Party’s slogan was, “Who controls the past controls the future; who controls the present controls the past.” It appears Trumpists have taken that lesson to heart.

The Hypocrisy of the "Law and Order" President

Trump’s defenders will spin this as a harmless clarification. They’ll claim that the Senate never came close to convicting him, so he never “seriously” faced removal. But this is gaslighting at its most corrosive. The gravity of an impeachment doesn’t rest solely on conviction—it lies in the process, the investigation, the evidence, and the public reckoning that follows.

In Trump's case, both impeachments were based on serious charges: the first for abuse of power and obstruction of Congress over his Ukraine extortion scheme; the second for inciting a violent insurrection against the U.S. government on January 6, 2021. That is not something you quietly erase from a museum placard without becoming complicit in authoritarian erasure.

The Smithsonian’s Shame

If the Smithsonian bowed to political pressure, it has lost its moral authority. By choosing to exclude Trump from a list of presidents who faced removal, the museum isn’t just rewriting history—it’s invalidating the trauma and chaos of those years. It’s gaslighting the American public and future generations into believing those impeachments didn’t matter.

Let’s be frank: This is a cowardly betrayal of the Smithsonian’s own mission to “increase and diffuse knowledge.” It sets a dangerous precedent where every new administration can scrub the exhibits clean of their embarrassments, like tyrants airbrushing out the disgraced from state photos.

Public Institutions Must Withstand Political Pressure

This is bigger than Trump. This is about what kind of country the United States wants to be. If public institutions like the Smithsonian can’t withstand political pressure to distort historical fact, then democracy is already on its knees. A nation that cannot tell the truth about its own leaders is not a republic—it is a regime.

Academic historians, museum curators, archivists, and educators must speak up. There must be accountability. The American public deserves to know why this change was made, who ordered it, and whether it was driven by internal cowardice or external bullying. Silence only enables further whitewashing.

The Stakes of Memory

Historical memory isn’t just about preserving facts. It’s about shaping how a society understands itself. If the American History Museum can’t find the backbone to say, “Yes, Trump was impeached—twice—and that matters,” then it has no business pretending to be the custodian of national memory.

What’s next? Will they remove Watergate from the Nixon exhibit because he resigned before conviction? Will they claim the Civil Rights Movement was bipartisan harmony? Or perhaps that Japanese internment camps were patriotic housing?

We’re standing at a precipice where truth is under siege from within the very institutions meant to guard it. It’s not just Trumpism; it’s the normalization of lying to the public through the placid tones of officialdom.

This is how authoritarianism solidifies: not through a dramatic coup, but by death by a thousand quiet revisions.

Conclusion: Call It What It Is

Let’s stop mincing words. What the Smithsonian has done is historical malpractice. It’s a chilling sign that truth can be negotiated away if the political cost is too high. The American people must demand the full and unvarnished truth from their public institutions—especially about presidents who tried to burn the country down on their way out the door.

Trump’s name doesn’t belong hidden. It belongs exactly where it should be: in the museum, under the glass, tagged with the word “twice impeached.”
