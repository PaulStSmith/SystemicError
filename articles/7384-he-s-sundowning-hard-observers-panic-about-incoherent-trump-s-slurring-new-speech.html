'He's sundowning hard': Observers panic about 'incoherent' Trump's 'slurring' new speech

Donald Trump’s latest speech set off a firestorm of people online saying the address was “slurring” and “incoherent.”

Trump’s remarks in North Carolina on Friday raised red flags for many. One of the most-cited examples was him saying, “I took cognitive tests. By the way, not easy. The first question is like what is this and they show a lion, giraffe, fish and a hippopotamus. And they say which is the giraffe.”

Garry Kasparov responded to that, asking, “Who is running the country?”

National security analyst Barry R McCaffrey also pounced on that, saying, “This is our President. The most powerful nation on earth. The poor man is incoherent. Three more years. Dangerous future ahead.”

That led Dem lawmaker Ted Lieu to ask, “Why do doctors keep giving Trump cognitive tests?”

Popular liberal influencer Spiro’s Ghost also seized on that same testing line, simply writing, “oh my god.”

That same account also flagged another quote from Trump’s remarks:

“I renamed the Gulf ofMexicoto the Gulf of America, right? That was a good one. I checked... for my whole life, I didn’t do it in my first term because I was the hunted. I had bigger problems. I had these animals trying to attack me at Mar-a-Lago,” he said. “They went into my wife’s closet... it sounds strange, but they looked at her drawers.”

The disjointed nature of the story Trump told led Spiro’s Ghost to wrote, “Wait…is he so dementia laden that he is saying that he couldn’t change the name in his first term (2017-2021) because of Mar-A-Lago raid in 2022?!? WUT???”

In another awkward moment, Trump was talking about insurance companies when he said, “They are totally beholden” and then said the letters “i-n.”

Governor Newsom Press Office pounced on that, writing, “He’s sundowning hard.”

Trump also caught attention with his rant against Marjorie Taylor Greene, which led ex-prosecutor Ron Filipkowski to say, “If you listen to this rant and think this demented, malevolent, addled, rambling old fool is fit to be president, you might be a MAGA cultist.”

Commenting on another Trump quote, “Her undergarments, sometimes to referred to as panties…. I think she steams them,” activist Amy Siskind said, “Listen to him slurring and sounding nuts!”

“Why isn’t his mental health a top story every single day?!?” she asked.

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump’s Latest Rhetorical Spiral: A Dive into Incoherence and Alarm

In a spectacle that has become all too familiar yet remains shockingly jarring, Donald Trump’s recent speech in North Carolina has set the internet ablaze with concern, confusion, and a hefty dose of incredulity. The former president, known for his unorthodox speaking style, seems to have outdone himself, leaving many to question not just the content of his speech but his cognitive capabilities.

The Cognitive Test Conundrum: A Giraffe in the Room

Trump’s anecdote about undergoing cognitive tests inadvertently became a focal point of concern. His detailed recounting of identifying animals such as a lion, giraffe, fish, and a hippopotamus as part of a cognitive assessment prompted a reaction that ranged from bewildered amusement to outright alarm. Chess grandmaster Garry Kasparov’s response, “Who is running the country?” underlines a chilling question that transcends political affiliations: What does it say about the state of leadership when such narratives become the norm rather than the exception?

The Gulf of Incoherence

Trump’s claim of renaming the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America is, on its face, a ludicrous assertion that seems to serve no purpose other than to stoke his base with nationalist sentiment. However, it’s his justification for not doing so in his first term—blaming the raid on Mar-a-Lago, an event that occurred after his presidency—that raises significant red flags about his grasp on reality. The confusion is so palpable that even Spiro’s Ghost, a popular liberal influencer, couldn’t help but express disbelief at Trump’s temporal disarray.

Sundowning or Showmanship?

The term “sundowning” thrown around by the Governor Newsom Press Office is a loaded one, typically associated with the late-day confusion experienced by those with dementia. While it’s a serious accusation to level, Trump’s disjointed speech patterns, including a bizarre foray into the topic of insurance companies followed by a cryptic “i-n,” provide ammunition to those concerned about his mental fitness.

A Divisive Figure’s Unraveling Rhetoric

Trump’s rant against Marjorie Taylor Greene, described by ex-prosecutor Ron Filipkowski as the ramblings of a “demented, malevolent, addled, rambling old fool,” underscores the divisive figure’s penchant for alienating even his staunchest allies. His fixation on Greene’s “undergarments” and the subsequent slurring noted by activist Amy Siskind only add to a portrait of a man who may be losing his grip not just on his audience, but on coherence itself.

The Deafening Silence

Amy Siskind’s exasperated question, “Why isn’t his mental health a top story every single day?!?” echoes a sentiment that many share. In any other era, the continuous, public display of cognitive decline—or at the very least, severe incoherence—by a former president and potential future candidate would be cause for national concern. Yet, it seems to have been relegated to the sidelines, drowned out by the cacophony of daily outrages.

In dissecting Trump’s speech, one cannot help but wonder about the broader implications. Is this the new normal? Have we become so desensitized to the absurd that we can no longer recognize it for what it is—a glaring systemic error in our political discourse and leadership? As observers, critics, and citizens, the onus is on us to demand better, to not let such episodes slide into the abyss of forgotten news cycles. For if we do, we risk normalizing a level of discourse that is not just incoherent, but fundamentally dangerous.
