Trump’s “Weaponization” Fund Is a Power Laundering Scheme

The Setup

Donald Trump, his sons, and the Trump Organization sued the IRS in January, claiming massive damages over leaked tax filings. The reported settlement now under challenge would create a $1.8 billion fund, block IRS audits of Trump’s prior returns, and spare him from nearly $100 million in potential liability. Thirty-five retired federal judges say the court was kept in the dark about the deal and should reopen the case.

Where the Power Actually Sat

This was never a story about a private citizen fighting a faceless bureaucracy. The real power sat with a sitting president and the federal government he controls, using the Justice Department as a tool in a case touching his own finances and political interests. That is the central fact the arrangement tries to blur: Trump was not standing outside the state. He was inside it, steering the machinery while claiming to be its victim.

The Settlement’s Real Function

The advertised purpose is “weaponization” relief. The structure points somewhere else. A commission controlled by the president would distribute $1.776 billion in taxpayer dollars, while Trump gains permanent protection from audits. That is not a neutral repair of public harm. It is a political payoff system wrapped in the language of grievance.

The Judges Named the Trick

The retired judges did what institutions often refuse to do until the damage is already done: they named the deception. Their filing says Trump and federal prosecutors failed to disclose the settlement when they moved to dismiss the underlying case. They also describe an “extraordinary” and “historically unprecedented” collusive settlement. That language matters because it strips away the ceremonial respectability that usually surrounds elite misconduct. If the court was misled, then this was not just a hard bargain. It was institutional manipulation.

What the Framing Hides

The phrase “weaponization fund” is doing propaganda work. It converts personal legal exposure into a public-relations campaign, and it recasts patronage as restitution. The source notes that critics believe the fund would primarily serve Trump allies and January 6 insurrectionists. Even without overclaiming beyond the record, the political logic is obvious: first invent a category of injured loyalists, then build a cash pipeline for them out of public authority.

The Pattern

This is the modern authoritarian trick in miniature: claim persecution, capture the institution, and use state power to erase your own accountability while rewarding your base. The cowardice here is not abstract. It belongs to prosecutors willing to enable the arrangement, to officials willing to dress it up as procedure, and to a political class that treats open self-dealing as just another transactional bargain. The fraud is not only in the money. It is in the effort to make domination look like legal process.
