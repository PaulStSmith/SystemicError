'Never seen this': Records buried on why Elon Musk sicced cops on his neighbors

Texas officials are suppressing a journalist's public records request into why tech billionaire Elon Musk called the police on his neighbors in the upscale Austin-area community of West Lake Hills.

According to Lauren McGaughy of The Texas Newsroom at KUT, "Elon Musk has called the cops on some of his neighbors in West Lake Hills, TX. So I asked for all police calls made from that address from the last few years. The sheriff wouldn't give me the records." Then, she said, two days ago, Attorney General Ken Paxton's office stated in a filing that those records could not be released because there would be a "substantial risk of physical harm."

This kind of stonewalling is unprecedented, McGaughy wrote.

"I have seen this exception [from the Texas Public Information Act] for elected officials like judges, who have come under attack. I have not seen it for a private businessman."

Musk, who has been moving more of his operations from California to Texas in recent years, has taken an aggressive hard line against anyone who reveals information about his personal activity and movements, even when that information is legally supposed to be public.

In one of the most high-profile examples, after Musk took over the Twitter platform and renamed it to X, he banned an account that was tracking the movements of his private jet based on publicly logged flight plan information with the Federal Aviation Administration, and then proceeded to ban a cohort of journalists who had done nothing more than report on the controversy about it. Trump's FAA this year changed the rules to prevent this information from being trackable altogether.

More broadly, any attempt by reporters to detail the identities of people working under Musk's Department of Government Effiency (DOGE) task force have been met with fury from Musk's supporters, with a recent deep dive into how DOGE employee Luke Farritor dismantled the U.S. foreign aid system sparking fierce debate online about whether he has a right to operate outside of the public eye.

Our Analysis:

The Iron Curtain of Information in Texas: Musk, Paxton, and Public Records

In an era where transparency is preached but seldom practiced, the unfolding drama in Texas surrounding Elon Musk and his battles against public scrutiny stands as a stark emblem of the power dynamics skewing in favor of the wealthy and influential. Lauren McGaughy's experience with Texas officials, as detailed in her report, is not just a story of denied access; it's a narrative that exposes the underbelly of how power operates in the shadows, shielded by the very laws meant to ensure transparency.

Suppressing Scrutiny: Musk's Legal Fortress

Elon Musk's move from California to Texas was not just a change of address but a strategic relocation into a more favorable legal and political climate. His aggressive stance against any form of scrutiny, as evidenced by his reaction to the tracking of his private jet movements and the suppression of journalists, is not just about privacy. It's a demonstration of how wealth and influence can be wielded to carve out a sphere of untouchability. The incident where Musk called the police on his neighbors only to have the related public records cloaked under the guise of "substantial risk of physical harm" is a testament to this power play.

Ken Paxton's office, in siding with Musk, has set a dangerous precedent. By invoking an exception meant to protect elected officials under threat, for a billionaire businessman, they've not just stretched the interpretation of the Texas Public Information Act; they've contorted it to serve the interests of the elite. This maneuver is not merely about protecting Musk but about reinforcing a legal and bureaucratic bulwark against public accountability.

The Chilling Effect on Journalism and Public Discourse

The broader implications of this saga extend far beyond Musk or the specifics of the denied public records request. It's about the chilling effect such actions have on journalism and the public's right to know. When journalists are hindered from reporting on matters of public interest, democracy suffers. The banishment of journalists from the Twitter platform, now X, for merely reporting on a controversy, underscores a troubling trend towards silencing watchdogs through intimidation or legal loopholes.

Moreover, the transformation of the FAA's rules to obscure the tracking of private jet movements is emblematic of how regulatory bodies can be influenced to serve the interests of the powerful, further eroding public access to information.

The DOGE Task Force: A Case Study in Opacity

The Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) under Musk's purview and the subsequent backlash against journalists attempting to scrutinize its operations, particularly the dismantling of the U.S. foreign aid system by Luke Farritor, is a case study in how opacity is being institutionalized. The fierce debate around the right of individuals like Farritor to operate outside of public scrutiny, abetted by Musk's supporters, raises critical questions about the balance between privacy and accountability, especially when public policy and resources are at stake.

Conclusion: A Call for Reckoning

The situation unfolding in Texas around Elon Musk and the suppression of public records is more than a local issue; it's a national conversation about power, transparency, and the role of the media in holding the powerful accountable. The actions of Musk and the complicity of figures like Ken Paxton in erecting barriers to information are symptomatic of a broader systemic ailment that threatens the foundational principles of democracy.

As we navigate these murky waters, the need for vigilance, rigorous journalism, and public engagement has never been more critical. The battle for transparency and accountability is ongoing, and it is one that demands our collective attention and action.
