Trump attacks Harvard students: 'Can't add 2 and 2'

As part of his ongoing attack on Harvard University, President Donald Trump attacked students admitted to the Ivy League school, saying that students there "can't add."

While signing an executive order on an unrelated matter, Trump criticized "billions of dollars" given to Harvard by the government. The U.S. funds medical research and more. Last week, the U.S. National Science Foundation listed 193 grants worth nearly $150 million that were being terminated. There are also 56 grants from the U.S. Department of Defense worth $105 million that were canceled.

A reporter asked Trump whether he intends to target international students at any other universities.

Trump replied, "We're looking at a lot of things. Billions of dollars have been given to Harvard. How ridiculous is that? Billions."

He also cited Harvard's $52 billion endowment fund while charging "student loans."

The Associated Press reported in March that Harvard announced that any student whose family makes less than $200,000 will receive free tuition.

When asked why Trump wouldn't want some of the best and brightest to come to the United States, Trump promised he did, but that "a lot of them need remedial math."

"Did you see that? Where these students can't add two and two and they go to Harvard," Trump claimed.

All students who attend Harvard must take the SAT or ACT tests, their website says. Both tests ask significantly more complicated questions than simple addition, practice tests show.

"They're going to teach remedial math at Harvard," Trump said. "Now, wait a minute. So, why would they get in? How can somebody that can't add or has very basic skills, how do they get into Harvard? Why are they there? Then you see those same people picketing and screaming at the United States or screaming at, you know, they're antisemitic or they're something. We don't want troublemakers here. But how do people that can't — when Harvard comes out with a statement that they're going to teach some of their students remedial math — that's basic math, uh, that's not the deal."

Remedial math is not "basic math," which is simple addition, a remedial contemporary math final exam test at Study.com showed.

Trump was not admitted to Harvard, rather, he went to the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School. The Washington Post’s Michael Kranish reported in 2019 that Trump was able to get an interview after his older brother made a call to the admissions office in 1966.

James Nolan, who recalled the interview with Trump to the Post, described Trump's dad, Fred Trump Sr., as seeking to "ingratiate" himself. He also said that Trump had a higher "acceptance score" based on his college experience. The acceptance rate in 1966 wasn't available on the school's website, but it showed that in 1980, the acceptance rate was "slightly greater than 40%." Trump bragged that getting into Penn was "super genius stuff." Nolan recounted, "it was not very difficult."

Trump was rejected from the University of Southern California and began his college career at Fordham's Bronx campus in 1964 until 1966, reported The Chronicle of Higher Education in 2016.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Misguided Assault on Harvard: A Closer Look

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of misplaced priorities, President Donald Trump has launched an unfounded attack on Harvard University and its students. His remarks, which range from questioning the mathematical abilities of Harvard students to criticizing the institution's financial support from the government, betray a profound misunderstanding of not only higher education but also the very principles of meritocracy and academic excellence.

The Billion Dollar Distraction

Trump's fixation on the "billions of dollars" given to Harvard by the government is a classic case of misdirection. While he lambasts these figures, he conveniently omits the purpose behind them: funding crucial medical research among other vital academic pursuits. The termination of grants worth hundreds of millions of dollars from the U.S. National Science Foundation and the Department of Defense might sound like financial prudence to Trump, but to those in the know, it's nothing short of a disaster for American leadership in science and technology.

The Endowment Enigma

Trump's harping on Harvard's $52 billion endowment fund while simultaneously attacking the institution for charging student loans is a bewildering contradiction. Harvard's financial aid policy, which offers free tuition for families making less than $200,000, is a testament to the university's commitment to accessibility and diversity. Yet, Trump's comments paint a picture of an elitist institution hoarding wealth at the expense of the American taxpayer. This narrative is not just flawed; it's fundamentally dishonest.

The Math Myth

Trump's claim that Harvard students "can't add two and two" is as laughable as it is ludicrous. His suggestion that the university teaches remedial math is a gross misrepresentation of the facts. Anyone with a basic understanding of the SAT or ACT tests knows that these assessments go far beyond simple arithmetic. The notion that Harvard, a global beacon of academic excellence, would admit students incapable of basic math is not only insulting but also patently false.

The Real Issue: Meritocracy or Nepotism?

Perhaps the most ironic twist in Trump's tirade against Harvard is his own academic history. Having attended the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School — not through his academic credentials alone but with a helpful push from familial connections — Trump's criticism reeks of hypocrisy. His claims of "super genius stuff" fall flat when juxtaposed with his own path to Penn, facilitated not by meritocratic achievement but by the influence and wealth of his family.

Conclusion: A Misguided Assault

Trump's attack on Harvard and its students is more than just a series of misguided statements; it's an assault on the values of higher education and intellectual rigor. By spreading misinformation and leveraging his platform to undermine one of America's most prestigious institutions, Trump not only showcases his ignorance but also threatens the very foundations of academic freedom and excellence.

In the end, this episode should be seen for what it truly is: a baseless and counterproductive vendetta against an institution that represents the best of what America has to offer in terms of education, research, and global leadership. It’s high time we focus on the real issues at hand, rather than entertain the baseless criticisms of an administration that seems perpetually at war with the pillars of knowledge and truth.
