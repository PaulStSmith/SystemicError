'It's a mistake': Trump under fire in Congress after nuclear 'gift' to adversaries

WASHINGTON — Democrats on the Hill bashed President Donald Trump’s idea to perform underground tests of American nuclear weapons on Thursday, arguing that it would be a “gift” to foreign adversaries.

On Wednesday, Trump posted on Truth Social that he wanted America’s military to begin testing nuclear weapons “immediately” because other countries appear to be testing the weapons as well. Critics have said Trump’s claim is false, and that the other countries he referred to, Russia and China, have not tested nuclear weapons in decades.

“Because of other countries’ testing programs, I have instructed the Department of War to start testing our Nuclear Weapons on an equal basis,” Trump posted. “That process will begin immediately.”

Democrats on the Hill bashed the idea on Thursday.

“This is a gift to the People’s Republic of China,” said Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ). “Maybe the president thinks that they test their nuclear weapons. They do not. Nobody has tested nuclear weapons in decades except for the North Koreans. It would be a mistake for us to do this because we have very little to gain.”

“Neither China, nor Russia, nor the United States has done any nuclear testing since 1996,” said Sen. Ed Markey (D-MA). “We are far more capable at that technology than China. It would be a gift to China militarily if the United States began nuclear testing because the Chinese could start immediately and become evermore sophisticated.”

“The whole framework of nuclear nonproliferation is to promote the security of the world,” Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-OR) said. “The agreement is that the nuclear powers of the world would work toward a nuclear-free world. And so, resuming tests essentially encourages other nations that they might as well pursue a nuclear program.”

Our Analysis:

Underground Rumbles: Trump’s Nuclear Test Proposal Sparks Outrage

In a move that can only be described as both bewildering and dangerously shortsighted, President Donald Trump has floated the idea of the United States resuming underground nuclear tests. This suggestion, seemingly plucked from the ether of ill-conceived military bravado, has rightfully ignited a firestorm of criticism from Democrats on Capitol Hill. Let’s dissect the layers of folly and potential global peril embedded in Trump’s proposal, which appears to be more of a geopolitical blunder than a sound strategic maneuver.

Trump’s Misguided Missile of a Proposal

Trump’s call to arms—or rather, to bombs—was broadcasted via Truth Social, where he declared an immediate commencement of nuclear weapons testing by the U.S. military. His justification? A baseless claim that other countries, specifically Russia and China, are conducting their own tests, thus necessitating an American response. This assertion doesn’t just flirt with inaccuracy; it marries it. Critics, including those with a semblance of geopolitical awareness, have been quick to point out that Trump’s allegations are not rooted in reality. Both Russia and China, alongside the United States, have not conducted nuclear tests since 1996. This isn’t a mere oversight; it’s a glaring misrepresentation of current global nuclear policies.

Democrats Decry the Dangerous Diversion

Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) didn’t mince words, labeling Trump’s idea a “gift” to the People’s Republic of China—a nation that, contrary to Trump’s claims, has abstained from nuclear testing for decades. Kelly’s assessment cuts to the core of the issue: not only is Trump’s proposal based on a false premise, but it also risks undoing the fragile stability achieved through years of nuclear nonproliferation efforts.

Sen. Ed Markey (D-MA) took the critique a step further, emphasizing the technological edge the United States currently holds over China in nuclear capabilities. To resume testing would be to squander this advantage, inviting China to accelerate its own nuclear program and potentially surpass U.S. sophistication in this domain.

Echoing this sentiment, Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-OR) highlighted the broader implications for global security. The resumption of nuclear tests by the U.S. would not only break a decades-long international consensus but could also embolden other nations to pursue their own nuclear ambitions, thereby undermining the collective goal of a nuclear-free world.

A Foolhardy Gambit with Global Stakes

What Trump proposes is not a show of strength but a profound miscalculation of the current geopolitical landscape. The assertions used to justify this course of action are not only unfounded but dangerously misleading. The potential ramifications extend far beyond domestic political theater, threatening to destabilize an already precarious global order.

The consensus among Democrats is not just a partisan rebuttal but a clarion call to reason. To embark on a path of nuclear testing in today’s world is to play with fire—fire that could engulf the planet in a conflagration of conflict and retaliation.

In sum, Trump’s proposal is a stark reminder of the perils of leadership untethered from the realities of international diplomacy and nuclear responsibility. It’s a proposal that should not only be questioned but vehemently opposed by all who value global security and the pursuit of a world free from the specter of nuclear devastation.
