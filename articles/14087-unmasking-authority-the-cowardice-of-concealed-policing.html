Unmasking Authority: The Cowardice of Concealed Policing

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

From Protection to IntimidationThe Legal and Ethical AbyssPolitical WeaponizationThe Public’s Right to KnowFinal Thoughts: Tear Off the Masks

When uniformed law enforcement officers hide their faces while executing arrests or dispersing protests, they aren’t protecting their identities from criminals — they’re protecting themselves from accountability. This practice, increasingly normalized in authoritarian circles, is a flashing red warning sign in any democracy worth the name. It is a betrayal of the social contract, a deliberate obfuscation of power, and a symptom of an institution that fears being seen.

Let’s be absolutely clear: police officers are public servants. They are entrusted by the people to enforce the law, not to act above it. In every functional democracy, that trust is maintained through transparency, oversight, and a chain of accountability that includes names, badge numbers, and body cameras. Masking one’s face during public enforcement actions undermines all of that. It turns law enforcement into a faceless militia.

Joyce White Vance, former U.S. Attorney and law professor, captured the sentiment with blunt clarity: “Law enforcement officers should NEVER cover their faces when making arrests or conceal their identities (unless they are working undercover). They work for us. We have a right to know who they are. These face coverings are frightening and un-American, as though they’re ashamed of what they’re doing.”

And she’s right. But shame isn’t even the half of it.

These officers aren’t covering their faces because they feel guilty about overreach or violence. They do it because they know — consciously or not — that what they are doing would not withstand scrutiny. They fear the day the political winds shift and their actions are no longer protected by the party in power. The masks aren’t for COVID. They’re not for safety. They are a hedge against future prosecution.

From Protection to Intimidation

It’s important to separate context here. Medical masks during a pandemic? Fine. But full tactical gear, including balaclavas or black masks, while raiding homes, grabbing protesters off the street, or enforcing politically loaded laws? That’s not health protocol. That’s theater. Intimidation theater.

The masked officer becomes a symbol, not of the law, but of unchallengeable force. They are made deliberately inhuman. You cannot appeal to their conscience if you can’t see their eyes. You cannot report them if you don’t know their name. They become an enforcer of policy, not a guardian of rights.

This is how authoritarian regimes operate. Look at Belarus. Look at Russia. Look at Hong Kong. Anonymous security forces drag dissidents off the streets, and no one can say who did it. That is the road we are paving when we allow American officers to shield their identities in the course of their duties.

The Legal and Ethical Abyss

Legally, this practice should be dead on arrival in any system that purports to value civil rights. The Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments guarantee due process, equal protection, and protection against arbitrary state action. But what due process can exist when the agent of the state is a ghost in a uniform?

Even body cameras become irrelevant when the officer’s face is hidden. How do you testify against someone you can’t identify? How do you file a complaint? What judge or jury will convict when the defense can argue that it might have been someone else in the same uniform?

And let’s not pretend this is accidental. ICE officers, federal riot squads, and certain militarized police units have taken to removing name tags, covering badges, and blacking out unit insignias. These aren’t bugs. They’re features. They signal to the public that the rule of law is conditional, and that these agents are above it.

Political Weaponization

The timing of this trend isn’t a coincidence. We see it ramp up during political unrest, racial justice protests, or during sweeps against immigrants. These operations are often partisan in nature, driven by political agendas, not public safety. When the tools of law enforcement are weaponized by the ruling party, anonymity becomes armor against the fallout.

Which brings us to the chilling comment that accompanied the viral image: “They are not ashamed of what they are doing. They cover their face because they know what they are doing is illegal and don’t want to be recognised and prosecuted when their party loses the next election.”

That’s not hyperbole. That’s a historical pattern. Nuremberg was filled with men who claimed they were “just following orders” — orders they carried out with masks on, behind closed doors. History judges harshly those who hide while enforcing unjust regimes.

The Public’s Right to Know

Democracy dies in darkness, and these masks are its blackout curtains. Public servants must be identifiable, especially when they wield the authority to detain, restrain, and potentially kill. If their actions can’t survive the light of public scrutiny, then those actions are not legitimate.

You cannot claim to serve the people while hiding your face from them. You cannot call yourself an enforcer of law while relying on anonymity to dodge its consequences.

Final Thoughts: Tear Off the Masks

It’s time to rip away the normalization of masked policing. Officers not working undercover must display their names, faces, and affiliations. Anything less is an affront to the Constitution, a threat to civic order, and a betrayal of every principle the badge is supposed to represent.

If your actions are just, you won’t need a mask.

If your actions are unjust, you don’t deserve the uniform.

And if you need to hide because you fear the consequences of a future in which justice might return—then you’re not a peace officer.

You’re just a coward with a badge.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
