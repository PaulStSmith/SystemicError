As the U.S. completed its fourth day of the 2025 federal shutdown, the president was golfing in Virginia. The news spread like wildfire on social media, with critics from all over weighing in.

HuffPost reporter S.V. Dáte said, “It is the fourth day of the government shutdown but DO NOT WORRY! It is not adversely affecting the president.”

“He is on the golf course, the 73rd time in this second term,” he added.

MAGA satire account Cuckturd chimed in, “Trump closed down all National Parks, but approved continued funding for his golf expenses. Recreation for me, but not for the peasants that pay the bills.”

Former MSNBC personality Keith Olbermann also added, “Again: Trump hasn’t been SEEN or HEARD live, in real time, since Tuesday. Supposedly golfing today. Press pool literally prevented from seeing him.”

Ex-prosecutor Ron Filipkowski said, “Our dementia-addled President In Name Only hacks it up on the golf course again this weekend while rage-boy Gestapo Miller seethes and plots the 4th Reich.”

PatriotTakes, which says it’s “dedicated research monitoring and exposing right-wing extremism and other threats to democracy,” even posted a video of the golf excursion.

“Video of Trump golfing at his Virginia golf club today,” according to the group. “This is what he was doing instead of working to keep the government open.”

Our Analysis:

The Luxury of Apathy: Trump’s Golfing Escapade During the Government Shutdown

In what can only be described as a glaring exhibit of flagrant disregard for the plight of millions of Americans, former President Donald Trump decided that the middle of a federal government shutdown was the perfect time to hit the links. As the U.S. trudged through its fourth day of governmental paralysis in 2025, Trump was spotted indulging in his favorite pastime: golfing in Virginia. This decision has, quite predictably, ignited a firestorm of criticism from various corners of the social and political spectrum.

The Critics Speak

S.V. Dáte of HuffPost encapsulated the irony of the situation with biting precision, pointing out the stark contrast between the president’s leisurely activities and the ongoing crisis affecting the nation. The sentiment of Dáte’s observation is clear: while the country grapples with the consequences of a shutdown, Trump’s priorities lie elsewhere, specifically on the golf course for the 73rd time in his second term. This is not just an indictment of Trump’s choice of leisure but a damning commentary on his leadership—or the lack thereof.

The MAGA satire account Cuckturd took the criticism further, highlighting a cruel irony in Trump’s governance: the closure of National Parks juxtaposed with the ensured continuation of his golfing escapades. This quip about “Recreation for me, but not for the peasants that pay the bills” cuts to the heart of the matter, underscoring a perceived elitism and disconnect from the average American’s reality.

Keith Olbermann and Ron Filipkowski add layers to this critique, focusing on Trump’s visibility—or the troubling lack thereof. Olbermann’s point about Trump not being “SEEN or HEARD live, in real time” since the shutdown began paints a picture of a leader who is not only physically but also figuratively absent in a time of crisis. Filipkowski’s harsh jab at Trump’s mental acuity and the sinister mention of “rage-boy Gestapo Miller” plotting the “4th Reich” might be hyperbolic, yet it emphasizes the gravity with which some critics view Trump’s leadership and the company he keeps.

The Symbolism of the Golf Course

The visual of Trump golfing, as shared by PatriotTakes, becomes a powerful symbol of his presidency’s perceived failings. It’s not just about a game of golf; it’s about what choosing to play golf during a governmental shutdown signifies—a profound detachment from the responsibilities of leadership and an insensitivity to the struggles of the governed.

Conclusion: A Scathing Indictment

The situation brings to mind Marie Antoinette’s apocryphal “Let them eat cake” — a leader so removed from the suffering of their people that their actions become a caricature of indifference. Trump’s decision to golf amidst a federal shutdown is not merely a personal choice but a political statement, one that resonates with a disturbing lack of empathy and a failure to grasp the gravity of the moment.

Critics, as showcased in the article, have not held back in their condemnations, and rightly so. The juxtaposition of leisure and crisis, of the golf course and the shutdown, serves as a stark reminder of the systemic errors in leadership that prioritize personal pleasure over public service. In Trump’s golfing during the shutdown, we find not just a moment of political misjudgment but a broader, more scathing indictment of his tenure as a leader who was, all too often, absent from the tee when it was his turn to drive.
