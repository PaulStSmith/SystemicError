White House in chaos as they reverse Trump plan to 'eliminate' FEMA before storms hit

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20263 min read

When the State Sabotages Its Own Rescue Crew

The Power That Matters

The central fact here is not FEMA’s “inability to get money out the door.” It is that Donald Trump’s administration controlled the money, the appointments, and the chain of command, and used that power to choke the agency it was supposed to run. Kristi Noem, with Corey Lewandowski operating as her de facto chief of staff, did not stumble into dysfunction. According to the reporting, they throttled payments, drove out senior leadership, and helped push out roughly 20% of FEMA’s workforce.

That is not bureaucratic drift. That is institutional sabotage by the people with the authority to issue the damage.

The Source Summary

CNN’s Gabe Cohen reports that FEMA was left with more than $15 billion in unspent funds while lawmakers waited months for disaster money already awarded. The agency was stripped of staff, stalled on basic obligations, and even failed to make critical payments for utilities and security operations tied to dangerous materials. Now, with hurricane season and other disasters approaching, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin is trying to reverse some of the cuts and red tape. Trump has also brought Cameron Hamilton back to lead FEMA after previously firing him for refusing to support plans to abolish the agency.

Blame Assigned Sideways

The administration’s preferred excuse is managerial incompetence: FEMA supposedly could not move money fast enough, so the people in charge had to intervene. That framing is convenient because it turns a deliberate political project into a housekeeping complaint. But the sequence in the reporting points the other way. Noem withheld money. The White House backed the effort to gut the agency. Hamilton was removed precisely because he objected to the plan.

This is the standard misdirection of power: break the machine, then accuse the machine of failing.

The Damage Was the Point

The workforce cuts, stalled payments, and leadership purge were not side effects. They were the method. The administration reportedly wanted to diminish FEMA in part because it was unwilling to center the reality of man-made climate change. So instead of confronting the disasters that climate change intensifies, it tried to downsize the agency tasked with responding to them.

That is a familiar authoritarian reflex: deny the problem, weaken the institution, then pretend the resulting chaos proves the institution was never necessary. The victims are not the architects. They are the people waiting for aid when the infrastructure has already been hollowed out.

Damage Control After the Fact

Now the White House is scrambling to undo what it did. Mullin is said to be racing to unwind the very restraints Noem imposed. Hamilton is back, which tells you the administration has moved from demolition to improvisation. It is trying to restore capacity only after showing how much capacity it was willing to destroy.

The Signal chats, the wiped messages, and the concern from lawyers that evidence was destroyed add another layer: this was not just reckless governance, it was managed recklessness. Decisions were being coordinated through channels that left the public with less visibility into how the dismantling was carried out.

The Larger Pattern

This is what anti-government politics looks like when it wins office: not shrinking the state in some abstract libertarian sense, but crippling the parts of government that ordinary people need most and then shifting blame onto those same institutions when the consequences arrive. FEMA was not failing in isolation. It was being broken by the people claiming to supervise it.

The pattern is clear. Power centralizes, responsibility gets dispersed, harm gets renamed as inefficiency, and the public is left to absorb the bill for ideological vandalism.
