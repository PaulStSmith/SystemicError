Trump golf course set to become 'permanent presidential sanctuary' under new plan: report

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosOctober 8, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

What happened

RawStory, citing The Washington Post, reports that lawyers for Donald Trump’s West Palm Beach golf club are seeking to convert part of the county-owned course into a permanent presidential retreat modeled on Camp David. The proposal would transfer a parcel to the federal government; Trump would fund presidential facilities, while his family company would continue operating the surrounding private club.

Attorney Harvey Oyer pitched the site to Palm Beach County commissioners as a destination for presidents, foreign leaders, and international summits, with complimentary club memberships offered to future presidents. County Administrator Joe Abruzzo said the county was communicating with the General Services Administration and would select a parcel. The reporting does not establish that a transfer has been approved or that the retreat will be built.

Public power, private property

The institutional authority belongs to Palm Beach County and the federal government. County officials control the land, while federal participation would be necessary to give the proposed retreat official status. Trump’s lawyers can request the arrangement; public officials would have to enable it.

That distinction matters because this is not simply a private developer proposing a resort. It is a sitting president’s business proposing that government land and presidential functions be integrated with a club still operated by his family company. The club reportedly produced $36.9 million for Trump during his first year back in office. Official importance could predictably make the surrounding private property more commercially valuable, whether or not financial gain is the plan’s stated purpose.

The presidency as an amenity

Camp David is a government facility serving the presidency. This proposal would place a presidential facility inside a commercial environment bearing Trump’s identity and controlled by his company. The public component would supply prestige, security significance, and a steady procession of powerful visitors; the private operator would retain the business surrounding it.

Offering future presidents complimentary memberships does not resolve that entanglement. It institutionalizes it. Presidents would be invited to participate in a private club whose value had been elevated by an official federal presence. The arrangement would blur public office and private hospitality long after Trump left office.

Branding disguises the transaction

Oyer emphasized global recognition and the benefits to Palm Beach County’s “brand.” The anonymous White House official emphasized security, geography, and proximity to an airport named for Trump. Those may be practical arguments, but they divert attention from the central political question: why should public authorities create a lasting presidential institution within an incumbent president’s privately operated commercial property?

The framing converts a conflict-of-interest problem into tourism promotion. County prestige becomes the sales pitch; presidential infrastructure becomes the product enhancement. RawStory’s account provides no evidence that officials conducted an open competition, compared publicly owned alternatives, or established safeguards against private benefit.

The larger pattern

The proposal reflects a broader form of personalized government: public institutions are made to orbit the officeholder’s properties, name, and business network. The danger is not hidden in procedural complexity. It is visible in the structure of the deal.

If approved as described, government would confer enduring official significance on a site whose surrounding commerce remains under the president’s family control. That is how self-dealing becomes normalized without ever being called self-dealing: officials describe it as legacy, lawyers market it as civic promotion, and the public is asked to treat the enrichment of private property as a gift to the presidency.
