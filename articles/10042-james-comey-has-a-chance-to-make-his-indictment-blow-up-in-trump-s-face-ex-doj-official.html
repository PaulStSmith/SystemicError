That is the opinion of Andrew Weissmann, the former chief of the Fraud Section at the DOJ, who appeared on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” on Friday morning.

Speaking with the hosts, Weissmann admitted he was stunned that Halligan had to present her case to a grand jury herself, a task normally assigned to a prosecutor in her office, and suggested Comey should attack the charges head-on and as swiftly as possible.

Pointing out that the Trump-appointed U.S. attorney prior to Halligan chose “to resign rather than bring this case,” Weissmann added, “And so that should give everyone pause. It is so unusual for anyone to resign over something like this.”

“And we have seen it over and over in Trump 2.0, where career people and now even a political appointee of Donald Trump himself saying, ‘I can’t stomach this,’” he continued. “And so for both of those reasons, this seems extraordinarily thin.”

“What I am waiting for is whether James Comey’s defense team asks for an immediate trial,” he proposed. “Because, if this really is as thin as we suspect, that’s something they could do. And that, I think, could sort of blow up in Donald Trump’s face having gone this far.“

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Political Theatre: The Halligan-Comey Saga

In what reads like a script torn from the pages of a political drama too convoluted for daytime television, the recent indictment of James Comey by newly-appointed U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan has thrown the proverbial cat among the pigeons. But not just any pigeons—these are the battle-hardened, seen-it-all-before types who’ve survived the relentless storm of U.S. politics. Andrew Weissmann, a veteran of the Department of Justice, appeared on MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” with a critique so sharp, it could slice through the thickest layer of political obfuscation.

The Flawed Indictment
Let’s cut to the chase: Weissmann’s commentary lays bare the skeletal framework of what he views as a fundamentally flawed indictment. The notion that this legal maneuver could be “tossed immediately” is not just a reflection on the indictment’s fragility but an indictment in itself of the motivations behind it. It’s as if Halligan, in her zeal (or perhaps under pressure), skipped the rulebook on prosecutorial discretion and opted for a legal Hail Mary. The fact that the previous Trump-appointed U.S. attorney chose resignation over prosecution speaks volumes, transforming what could have been a footnote in legal textbooks into a screaming headline.

Prosecutorial Peculiarities
In a move that deviates from standard operating procedure, Halligan took it upon herself to present the case to a grand jury. This is no small detail. In the chess game of legal strategy, who presents the case can be as crucial as the evidence itself. This departure from norm raises red flags, suggesting either a lack of confidence in her team or an overabundance in her own legal acumen. Weissmann’s astonishment at this choice is telling; it’s not every day you see a high-ranking official take on such tasks personally, unless, of course, there’s more at play than meets the eye.

The Bigger Picture
What’s particularly striking about Weissmann’s critique is the broader political context it sketches. The resignation of a Trump appointee over the prospect of bringing this case, the direct involvement of the U.S. Attorney in presenting to the grand jury—these elements paint a picture of a justice system under strain. It’s not just about the legal standing of the indictment but the political machinations that led us here. The narrative of Trump 2.0, as Weissmann puts it, is one of career professionals and political appointees alike reaching their breaking point.

The Anticipated Outcome
Weissmann’s anticipation of Comey’s defense strategy is the cherry on top of this legal and political sundae. By suggesting an immediate trial, he’s essentially daring the prosecution to bring their best game forward—well knowing that if the case is as “extraordinarily thin” as he suspects, it could spectacularly backfire on the architects of this indictment, with Donald Trump caught in the blast radius.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Legal and Political Hubris
This saga, as dissected by Weissmann and broadcasted for the world to see, serves as a stark reminder of the intertwining of law and politics in the U.S. justice system. The indictment of James Comey, as presented by Lindsey Halligan, might very well end up being a masterclass in what not to do. Whether it crumbles under legal scrutiny or not, the spectacle has already underscored the volatility and vulnerability of justice when subjected to the whims of political power plays. In the end, this may very well turn into an embarrassing chapter for those who orchestrated it, with repercussions that ripple far beyond the courtroom.
