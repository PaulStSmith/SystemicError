'Cook this liar': Ex-GOP lawmaker slams Mike Johnson's claim Trump was Epstein 'informant'

By Paulo Santos

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA). (Photo credit: Gage Skidmore)

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) defended President Donald Trump's conduct throughout the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking case files controversy, going so far as to claim with no evidence that Trump was an "FBI informant" against Epstein, and saying that he banished Epstein from Mar-a-Lago upon realizing his abuse of young girls — which is contradicted by the public timeline.

"He's not saying that what Epstein did was a hoax, it's a terrible, unspeakable evil. He believes that himself. When he first heard the rumor, he kicked him out of Mar-a-Lago. He was an FBI informant to try to take this stuff down. The president knows, and has great sympathy for the women who suffered these unspeakable harms. It's detestable to him. He and I have spoken about this as recently as 24 hours ago."

But former Rep. Denver Riggleman (R-VA), who previously assisted the House January 6 Committee investigation, cut through Johnson's obfuscations in a lengthy post to X.

"Ok. I’ve had enough," wrote Riggleman. "RELEASE THE SIGNED EPSTEIN FBI INFORMANT AND WITNESS DOCUMENTS. If our POTUS was an FBI informant or witness, then why did he call for the release of the Epstein list and run his campaign on it… and then block it?"

"Wouldn’t he know the contents and those that were 'innocent' or guilty?" Riggleman continued. "Why did he say democrats were in it and not have names he can prove? Did he know the 'globalists and deep state' participants? Was he a primary witness? Did he watch or participate in rape and then get immunity? Why didn’t he say that he was an FBI informant about Epstein in his first term or the first 8 months of this term? Why would he threaten GOP House members saying it was 'hostile' act if they voted for the Massie bill? Has he always been an FBI informant? Did he inform on the mob in NY? Were his casinos FBI fronts? When did he become an FBI informant? Are there documents available to release? Did he conspire with Ghislaine? Was he always aware of Epstein?"

"Facts and data," he added. "All of us need to be a million pound s---hammer of facts and data. I am. The media should be able to destroy this narrative and cook this liar."

Johnson bragged earlier this week that he had the votes to stop a bipartisan discharge petition compelling Congress to seek the release of all outstanding files on the Epstein case — even as it became clear he didn't.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Unraveling the Epstein Narrative

In the latest act of political theater, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has ascended the stage with a script that reads more like fiction than the stark reality we face. Defending President Donald Trump's entanglement with the Jeffrey Epstein sex trafficking scandal, Johnson's remarks are a masterclass in obfuscation and unfounded claims. Let's dissect the narrative piece by piece, shall we?

The Claim of the Informant

Johnson posits, with the confidence of a man walking a tightrope without a net, that Trump was an "FBI informant" in the case against Epstein, a claim as bold as it is evidence-free. This assertion flies in the face of the public timeline, a pesky little thing called facts, which Johnson seems eager to ignore. The claim that Trump banished Epstein from Mar-a-Lago "upon realizing his abuse of young girls" contradicts known timelines and raises more questions than it answers.

Riggleman's Reality Check

Enter former Rep. Denver Riggleman (R-VA), a voice of reason amidst the cacophony of Johnson's claims. Riggleman, with the precision of a surgeon, cuts through the obfuscation. His demands are simple: RELEASE THE SIGNED EPSTEIN FBI INFORMANT AND WITNESS DOCUMENTS. If Trump indeed played the role of an informant, why the secrecy? Why the contradictions? Riggleman's inquiry exposes the gaping holes in Johnson's narrative, laying bare the absurdity of the claim.

A Litany of Unanswered Questions

Riggleman's retort is a veritable barrage of questions that Johnson and, by extension, Trump seem unwilling or unable to answer: - Why did Trump, if aware of the contents of the Epstein files, not act on this knowledge sooner? - If Trump was indeed an informant, why was this not disclosed earlier, potentially leveraging it in his political campaigns? - The insinuation that Trump might have been involved in Epstein's activities and then granted immunity is a serious allegation that demands scrutiny.

The Art of Deflection

Johnson's narrative is a classic case of political deflection, a smoke and mirrors act designed to confuse and mislead. By boasting about nonexistent support to stop a bipartisan effort for transparency, Johnson inadvertently highlights the desperation of their position. The truth, it seems, is not something they wish to uncover.

Conclusion: The Million Pound S---hammer

Riggleman's call for a "million pound s---hammer of facts and data" is a rallying cry for those weary of manipulation and misinformation. In a world where political narratives often diverge from the truth, it is incumbent upon us, the media, and the public, to demand accountability and veracity.

The Epstein saga, with its deep and dark implications, deserves more than the theatrics of Johnson and the oblique defenses of Trump. It demands a thorough investigation, unimpeded by political allegiances or fear of repercussions. The victims of Epstein's heinous crimes deserve no less than the full illumination of truth, not the shadowy insinuations of those who would use this tragedy for political gain.

The narrative spun by Johnson is not just implausible; it's irresponsible. It's time to discard the script of deflection and demand the unvarnished truth. Only then can we hope to untangle the web of deceit and bring those responsible to justice.
