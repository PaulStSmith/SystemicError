'It ain't working': MSNBC hosts laugh at Trump's inability to escape Epstein scandal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 21, 20254 min read

Eugene Daniels (MSNBC screenshot)

Donald Trump's scrambling to put down a revolt by his MAGA followers who are furious about the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files earned him laughter from the two co-hosts of MSNBC's "The Weekend."

As host Eugene Daniels noted, the president is struggling to deal with the fall-out over his Department of Justice sitting on evidence against the accused child sex predator who died while in custody under mysterious circumstances.

As he explained, "He admitted, quote, 'nothing will be good enough for the troublemakers and radical left lunatics making the request. It will always be more, more, more.' And the troublemakers he's referring to could be among his own MAGA faithful."

"As Ashley Parker writes in The Atlantic, quote: 'In particular, Trump has raged against MAGA influencers who, in his estimation, have profited and grown famous off their association with him and his political movement.' That's according to a White House official and a close outside adviser."

"That adviser added that: quote, 'The president has bigger fish to fry, and he said what he wants to move on',"' he continued before joking, "But it ain't working," which led Daniels and co-host Jonathan Capehart to start laughing.

Our Analysis:

Trump's MAGA Revolt: A Comedy of Errors

In a twist that seems straight out of a political satire, Donald Trump finds himself in the midst of a rebellion. This isn't a coup led by seasoned politicians or a well-orchestrated plot by his adversaries. No, this revolt comes from within the very beast he nurtured — his MAGA followers. The cause of their fury? The handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files. The response from the public and media, as captured by the hosts of MSNBC's "The Weekend"? Laughter.

The Irony of MAGA's Discontent

The former president, who has made a career out of courting controversy and playing to the basest instincts of his base, is now at the receiving end of their discontent. The issue at hand — the Department of Justice's apparent lethargy in dealing with evidence against Jeffrey Epstein, a man whose demise in custody reads like a page from a conspiracy theorist’s playbook, is no minor scandal. It's the kind of story that Trump's base, fueled by conspiracy theories and a deep distrust of the 'deep state', would typically devour. Yet, here, the anger is directed at Trump himself.

Eugene Daniels's commentary hits the nail on the head. Trump's attempt to dismiss the concerns as the rumblings of "troublemakers and radical left lunatics" is not only a gross misjudgment of the situation but a hilarious mislabeling. These aren't the mythical 'leftist mobs' Trump has long warned about; these are his own followers, biting the hand that once fed them conspiracy theories as truths.

Trump's Self-Made Predicament

As Ashley Parker notes in The Atlantic, Trump is particularly incensed at MAGA influencers — those who have ridden the Trump wave to fame and fortune. His frustration is palpable, yet it reeks of hypocrisy. After all, these influencers are merely following the playbook Trump himself wrote: leverage his political movement for personal gain. That a White House official and an outside adviser have to point this out is both comical and indicative of the chaos that perpetually surrounds Trump.

The adviser's quote, that "the president has bigger fish to fry," would be laughable if it weren't so tragic. Trump, a man who has always craved the spotlight, now finds himself unable to control the narrative or the actors he once directed with ease. His insistence on moving on, as if he can simply turn the page on this episode, is a testament to his perennial inability to grasp the consequences of his actions.

Conclusion: A Laughing Matter?

As Daniels and Jonathan Capehart's laughter indicates, there's a certain schadenfreude in watching Trump squirm under the pressure of his own making. Yet, amidst the laughter, there's a darker undercurrent. The handling of the Epstein files is no laughing matter. It's a grave issue that speaks to the heart of justice and accountability. That it has been reduced to a punchline in the ongoing circus of Trump's post-presidency life is a sobering reminder of the systemic errors at play.

Trump's current predicament is a self-inflicted wound, a direct result of his nurturing of a base that thrives on conspiracy and grievance. As he scrambles to put down this revolt, one can't help but wonder if this is the moment the pied piper of MAGA begins to lose his tune. But then again, in the world of Trump and his followers, the next twist is just around the corner. The laughter, however, might be harder to come by.
