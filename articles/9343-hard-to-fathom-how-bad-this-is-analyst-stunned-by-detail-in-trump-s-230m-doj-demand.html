Greg Sargent, staff writer and podcast host at The New Republic, argues that Trump is turning the presidency into “a massive Bribe Delivery System” and that he’s likely to get the payout from the DOJ.

“It’s hard to fathom how bad this is. Start with the claims themselves: In 2023, Trump sought damages from DOJ from the Russia investigation, which he’s called a ‘hoax’ for years. But while that probe had some serious problems, DOJ’s inspector general concluded it was legitimately predicated, and a GOP-led Senate committee, chaired by the fellow who’s now Trump’s secretary of state, confirmed in August 2020 that Russia did attempt to swing the election to Trump. Of course his campaign’s potential role in this had to be investigated,” Sargent writes.

One of the people who will decide on Trump’s payment demands is Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, a former personal attorney for Trump. Blanche, who is now second in command at the DOJ, also met with convicted sex trafficker and Jeffrey Epstein’s former partner Ghislaine Maxwell in July.

But that’s not even the worst part — the payment can probably be completed without even being revealed publicly, Sargent explains.

“Regardless, for Trump to continue seeking these payments as president is even more wildly corrupt. The conflict-of-interest issues involved in Blanche making this decision are obvious. This is probably unconstitutional too,” he writes.

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), ranking Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee, is investigating the demand and said that the payments can be made without immediate public disclosure.

“Our reading is that, even though this is a private settlement, it doesn’t have to be disclosed anywhere until there is an accounting of where all the money has gone at the end of the year,” Raskin told The New Republic.

“The domestic emoluments clause says the president may not receive any compensation at all from the U.S. government or the states beyond his official salary,” Raskin said. “This means he cannot be ordering government officials to write checks to the president.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s DOJ Demand: A New Low in Presidential Corruption

In a move that can only be described as audaciously corrupt, President Donald Trump has reportedly demanded a staggering $230 million from the Department of Justice (DOJ) as damages stemming from the Russia investigation, which he has long decried as a “hoax.” This demand, as analyzed by Greg Sargent of The New Republic, not only underscores Trump’s relentless effort to undermine the legitimacy of the Russia probe but also highlights a disturbing transformation of the presidency into what can best be termed as a “massive Bribe Delivery System.”

The Unfathomable Depths of Corruption

It’s hard to overstate the audacity of Trump’s demands. The Russia investigation, despite its controversies, was deemed legitimately predicated by the DOJ’s inspector general. Furthermore, a GOP-led Senate committee, chaired by none other than Trump’s own pick for secretary of state, confirmed in August 2020 that Russia indeed attempted to influence the election in Trump’s favor. Given these findings, the investigation into Trump’s campaign was not only warranted but necessary.

Yet, Trump’s insistence on seeking damages for the investigation crosses a new line in presidential corruption. This is not merely about Trump challenging the legitimacy of the probe; this is about the former president leveraging his position to exact financial revenge from the very government he once led.

Conflict of Interest and Constitutional Violations

Adding to the scandal is the involvement of Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, a former personal attorney for Trump, who is now in a position to influence the decision on Trump’s payout. Blanche’s past connections to Trump and his recent meeting with convicted sex trafficker Ghislaine Maxwell only serve to deepen the ethical quagmire surrounding this case.

The potential for these payments to be made without public disclosure, as Sargent notes, is not just alarming but likely unconstitutional. The domestic emoluments clause explicitly prohibits the president from receiving any form of compensation from the U.S. government beyond their official salary. Trump’s actions, therefore, not only raise serious ethical concerns but also potentially violate the very Constitution he swore to uphold.

A Brazen Attack on Democratic Norms

What this scenario paints is a grim picture of a presidency transformed into a personal enrichment scheme, where the powers of the highest office in the land are abused for personal gain. The implications for the rule of law and democratic governance are profound. When a president can demand and potentially receive vast sums of money from the government with little to no public oversight, it sets a dangerous precedent for future administrations and erodes the foundational principles of accountability and transparency.

The Urgent Need for Investigation and Accountability

The investigation by Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD) into Trump’s demands is a crucial step toward uncovering the extent of this corruption. It is imperative that these findings are made public and that those involved are held accountable. If the allegations prove true, this would not only represent a blatant abuse of power but also a stark violation of the Constitution.

In conclusion, Trump’s $230 million DOJ demand is not just “hard to fathom” in its audacity; it is a stark reminder of the lengths to which he will go to subvert democratic norms and institutions for personal benefit. The implications of this case are far-reaching, underscoring the urgent need for transparency, accountability, and a reaffirmation of the rule of law.
