Revealed: New report exposes how much MAGA has co-opted 'patriotism'

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosJuly 7, 20263 min read

Patriotism Was Not Stolen. It Was Rebranded.

The Source of the Shift

Politico’s poll finds that Americans still feel pride in the country, but they increasingly assume that “patriotism” belongs to Republicans. That matters because it shows a branding success, not a civic truth. Donald Trump and the MAGA movement did not discover patriotism; they monopolized its symbols, then trained the political press and the public to treat that monopoly as natural.

The report gets part of the story right: the right has spent years making nationalism central to its identity, and it has paid off. But the deeper issue is power. Trump’s movement controls the loudest institutional megaphone in the Republican Party, and the GOP has allowed itself to become the delivery system for his version of national belonging.

Who Gets To Be “The Nation”

The most revealing detail in the poll is not that people across the spectrum say they are patriotic. It is that respondents are more likely to imagine a proud American as Republican than Democratic. That is ideological capture in plain sight. The right has worked to equate the flag, the border, and the nation with itself, while Democrats are treated as suspicious by default, as if democratic politics and national loyalty cannot coexist.

That framing is politically useful for the right because it turns opposition into disloyalty. Once a movement can define itself as the country, every critic becomes an outsider. That is not an accident of rhetoric. It is an authoritarian habit of mind.

The Real Machinery

The article notes that right-wing parties have surged by exploiting border security fears and economic anxiety. That is the mechanism: grievance is converted into identity, and identity is converted into permission. Trump’s promised “widespread deportations” and aggressive trade politics are not incidental policy positions. They are performances of domination staged as patriotism.

This is where the power sits. Not in abstract “national pride,” but in who gets to define the nation’s enemies, whose pain gets instrumentalized, and which institutions are expected to enforce the story. Republican messaging under Trump has made national identity central because it is easier to rule through suspicion than through competence.

Politico’s Mildness Problem

The reporting is useful, but its language softens the violence of the transformation. “Partisan” is too polite for what is happening. This is not simply that one side likes flags more. It is that the far right has turned patriotic language into a sorting mechanism for obedience. The press often describes that process as a cultural trend, which conveniently hides the strategists and officeholders who built it.

That is the misdirection. When authoritarian politics succeeds, coverage often describes the resulting confusion instead of naming the people who engineered it. Trump and the GOP did not merely benefit from a shift in public perception; they cultivated it, weaponized it, and made it politically costly for anyone else to speak in national terms without sounding defensive.

Democrats Should Not Copy the Lie

The Democratic response should not be to imitate the right’s nationalism and pretend that the flag itself is the issue. The problem is not that patriotism is right-coded in some poll. The problem is that the right has been allowed to define patriotism as exclusion, punishment, and ethnic hierarchy, while too much of elite political discourse treats that capture as a branding problem instead of a democratic emergency.

Democrats do not need to apologize for loving the country. They need to expose the fraud: that Trump’s version of patriotism is really submission to strongman politics wrapped in ceremony. Democratic politics should insist that the nation is not owned by one party, one race, or one movement built around grievance and power.

The Larger Pattern

This story is about a familiar democratic failure. The right seizes a universal civic value, stains it with hierarchy, and then forces everyone else to argue on its terrain. The result is not just partisan advantage. It is civic corrosion. When authoritarian movements successfully colonize words like patriotism, they are not merely winning a messaging war. They are narrowing the range of legitimate political identity.

That is the pattern here: capture the symbol, control the meaning, and use the meaning to discipline the public. The antidote is not neutral cheerleading for the nation. It is refusing to let the nation be defined by those who treat democracy as a costume and domination as love of country.
