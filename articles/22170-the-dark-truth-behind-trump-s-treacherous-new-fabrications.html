The dark truth behind Trump's treacherous new fabrications

Based on an article by Robert Reich

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Delusion Is Not the Problem. Power Is.

Trump’s latest boasts are the familiar kind of garbage: sky-high approval, miracle economics, “total control” over a strategic waterway, as if reality were just another prop on Truth Social. The source article treats this as evidence that he has lost his grip on reality and should be removed under the 25th Amendment. That is too neat. It turns a political crisis into a psychiatric one and hands the rest of the system an excuse to keep doing what it has been doing all along.

Who Actually Holds Power

The central fact is not Trump’s mouth. It is the office he still occupies and the apparatus that keeps responding to him. A president can lie this openly only when institutions around him continue to absorb, repeat, and operationalize the lie. The source nods at the “sycophants surrounding him” and the networks airing his failures, which is closer to the truth than the diagnosis. His power is not solitary. It is distributed through loyalists, media enablers, and elected officials who prefer paralysis to confrontation.

The Lie Is the Method

Trump’s claims are not random outbursts. They are governance by fabrication. The point is not that he believes everything he says. The point is that he treats public language as a weapon against public reality. That is a political technique, not just a personality defect. When he claims victory where there is defeat, he is not confused in a harmless way; he is trying to force everyone else to negotiate with his version of events.

Why the Framing Is Too Small

The source article wants the reader to focus on whether Trump is “dangerously delusional.” Maybe he is. But that framing is politically limited. It invites the audience to stare at the man and ignore the machinery around him. The harder question is why so many powerful actors keep behaving as if managing his mood is more urgent than stopping the damage. Once the debate becomes medicalized, responsibility gets blurred. The problem stops looking like enablement and starts looking like tragedy.

The 25th Amendment Is Not a Strategy

Calling for removal under the 25th Amendment may sound decisive, but it is also a confession that the political system has run out of courage. If the only response to a destructive presidency is to hope other officials suddenly discover backbone, then the real failure happened long before any constitutional procedure was invoked. The source article points toward removal, but it does not explain who will do it, who will resist it, and why the same political class that normalized this presidency would suddenly become a guardian of democracy.

The Pattern Beneath the Spectacle

This story is about more than Trump’s latest fabrication. It is about a political order that tolerates domination as long as it is packaged as noise. It is about institutions that wait for total collapse before admitting the obvious. And it is about a media ecosystem that keeps translating authoritarian behavior into personality analysis, as if the problem were a broken mind rather than a system that keeps granting power to wreckers and then asking everyone to be patient.

What This Reveals

The deeper lesson is brutally simple: authoritarian politics do not require competence. They require repeated accommodation. Trump can brag, distort, and sever himself from reality because the surrounding system still treats his conduct as something to interpret instead of something to defeat. The crisis is not that one man might be delusional. The crisis is that American institutions are still built to absorb delusion, delay accountability, and call that stability.
