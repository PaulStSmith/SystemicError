Pete Hegseth just committed the 'worst-case scenario' offense: military justice group

By Paulo Santos

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth attends a meeting at the Pentagon. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth was condemned on Friday by a non-governmental group of military justice experts who said he had committed the “worst-case scenario.”

NIMJ, which bills itself as “the only non-governmental organization in the United States to educate about and encourage a fair court-martial and military justice system,” issued a press release ahead of the weekend addressing a recent scandal from Hegseth. Specifically, the group said it “condemns any attempt by military leaders to punish Senator Mark Kelly, a military retiree, for his speech” in connection with a video he and other veterans made for members of the military.

“Any adverse action against Senator Kelly would threaten core constitutional principles. It blurs the separation of powers by allowing the Executive Branch to punish a member of the Legislative Branch for speech made in the performance of his duties as a Senator,” according to the group. “Any adverse action chills First Amendment expression. When military leaders punish speech they do not like, they send a message that honest debate invites retaliation. Service members quickly learn that loyalty to the Constitution may carry risk if their words displease those in power. If the military can punish a Senator for speech that a senior military official does not like, no viewpoint of any military member or retiree is safe.”

The group further added, “Senator Kelly’s speech is not punishable under the UCMJ. Although the military code criminalizes certain types of speech that may affect the military mission, the Senator’s remarks are far from criminal. He simply restated a fundamental principle of military law: service members must obey lawful orders and disobey unlawful orders,” the group wrote before concluding, “Finally, the process here was flawed from the start. Secretary Hegseth first publicly announced that he wanted to punish Senator Kelly for his speech, then sent the case to a subordinate leader for his independent decision. That puts the subordinate in an impossible position: either deliver some form of punishment that Secretary Hegseth demands or potentially be fired. That is not how justice in the military works or should work.”

Legal expert Ryan Goodman quoted the release, “Secretary Hegseth’s zeal to have the military punish Senator Kelly is a worst-case scenario of this problem,” and then added, “National Institute of Military Justice Statement Against Secretary Hegseth’s Threats to Punish a Senator (disclaimer: I am a NIMJ Distinguished Fellow).”

Our Analysis:

The Disturbing Assault on Constitutional Principles by Secretary Hegseth

In a move that can only be described as a brazen affront to constitutional safeguards, U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has found himself at the center of a controversy that reeks of authoritarian overreach. The National Institute of Military Justice (NIMJ), a beacon for military justice education and fairness, has issued a damning condemnation of Hegseth’s recent scandal. This is not just about politics; it’s about the very foundation of American democracy and the principles it stands for.

A Clear and Present Danger to Free Speech

The crux of this debacle lies in Hegseth’s apparent attempt to punish Senator Mark Kelly, a military retiree, for engaging in protected speech. Senator Kelly, leveraging his experience and platform, participated in a video aimed at members of the military, a move that according to NIMJ, Hegseth has sought to penalize. The implications of such actions are chilling, to say the least. It represents a direct assault on the First Amendment rights that are supposed to be sacrosanct in American society.

“Any adverse action chills First Amendment expression,” the NIMJ rightly points out. When military leaders, bolstered by the executive branch, punish speech they find disagreeable, it sends a harrowing message: conformity is demanded, dissent is punished. This not only stifles honest debate but undermines the very loyalty to the Constitution that service members vow to uphold. If a Senator, protected by the constitutionally enshrined separation of powers, can be targeted for his speech, what hope do ordinary citizens or service members have against such tyranny?

The Misuse of the UCMJ

The Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) is invoked by Hegseth in this quixotic quest against Senator Kelly. Yet, as NIMJ astutely points out, Senator Kelly’s speech is not punishable under the UCMJ. His remarks, grounded in the fundamental principle that service members must obey lawful orders and resist unlawful ones, are far from criminal. They are, in fact, essential reminders of the military’s duty to the law and morality over blind obedience.

A Flawed and Prejudicial Process

The process by which Hegseth has sought to exact this punishment is equally troubling. By announcing his intent to punish Senator Kelly before even sending the case for review, Hegseth not only prejudged the outcome but placed his subordinate in an untenable position: either bow to the whims of power or face potential repercussions. This is not justice; it is coercion. It makes a mockery of the military’s judicial system and erodes trust in its fairness and impartiality.

The Verdict

Ryan Goodman’s characterization of Hegseth’s actions as a “worst-case scenario” is not an overstatement. It is a dire warning. Hegseth’s zeal to punish a Senator for engaging in protected speech underlines a disturbing trend of ignoring constitutional limits and principles for political or personal vendettas.

The actions of Secretary Hegseth, as outlined by the NIMJ, are not just an attack on Senator Kelly but an attack on the very principles that underpin American democracy. It’s a stark reminder that the battle for free speech and constitutional rights is ongoing, and vigilance is the price of liberty.

In this instance, the NIMJ has not only highlighted a systemic problem but has stood up for the values that define justice in the military and beyond. It’s a call to action for all who value democracy and the rule of law to stand against such overreaches of power. Let this be a clarion call: The principles of justice and constitutional rights must be defended, now more than ever.
