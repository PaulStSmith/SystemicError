'Disgusting': Trump sparks outrage over 'tone deaf' marble bathroom boast

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump arrive to host a Halloween event at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 30, 2025. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

Brutal reactions rolled in Friday as people slammed President Donald Trump‘s social post bragging about the White House bathroom redesign, calling his post “tone deaf” and “disgusting.”

Nearly 42 million Americans were at risk of going hungry as the Trump administration tried to decline using emergency funds for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, or SNAP, food benefits. As this was going on, Trump shared photos of the new renovations, including a marble floor and walls, and golden bathroom fixtures and trash can. The bathroom is part of the Lincoln Bedroom, the guest suite at the White House once used by former President Abraham Lincoln as an office.

“The Refurbished Lincoln Bathroom in the White House – Highly polished, Statuary marble!” Trump proudly boasted on his social media platform, Truth Social.

Social media users had quite a few things to say after seeing the president’s post — noting the timing of it all:

“Millions of people are being kicked off of food assistance and millions can’t afford health care anymore. But don’t worry everyone! Trump got a new bathroom. So tone deaf, out of touch, and disgusting,” Democratic influencer Harry Sisson writes on X.

“We are in a serious govt shutdown, but instead of negotiating, Trump posted several pics of his WH bathroom remodel today and now this. He is currently headed to Palm Beach for another golf weekend,” MediasTouch Editor-in-Chief Ron Filipkowski wrote on X.

“People can’t afford food and their healthcare costs are tripling, and Trump is bragging that he remodeled his bathroom,” Melanie D’Arrigo, president of the Campaign for New York Health, wrote on X.

“Gotta love the idea of Trump as working class hero as he smashes the East Wing for a gilded Ball Room, hocks a new marble bathroom and spends the weekend at Mar a Lago as families worry about affording food because he’s choosing to stop SNAP benefits,” Neera Tanden, former advisor to the president, wrote on X.

“Disgraceful and incredibly tone deaf. Millions of Americans will lose food aid tomorrow but Trump says, look at my cool new marble bathroom! So glad it’s ‘highly polished.’ I hate that dull marble look,” Pam Fessler, former NPR News correspondent, wrote on X.

Our Analysis:

The Gilded Disconnect: Trump’s Marble Musings Amidst America’s Hunger Crisis

In the latest display of flagrant insensitivity, President Donald Trump’s recent social media boast about the White House bathroom renovations has sparked a maelstrom of criticism, illustrating a chasm of disconnect between his administration’s priorities and the dire realities facing millions of Americans. With nearly 42 million citizens on the brink of hunger, Trump’s “tone deaf” and “disgusting” fixation on marble floors and golden fixtures is not just a faux pas; it’s a glaring emblem of his administration’s systemic failings.

A Lavish Lavatory Over Lives

Trump’s social media platform, Truth Social, became the stage for this latest act of presidential vanity, as he shared photos of the revamped Lincoln Bathroom, complete with “Statuary marble” and a golden trash can. This opulent revelation came at a moment when his administration was actively trying to withhold emergency funds for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), a lifeline for those 42 million Americans teetering on the edge of food insecurity.

Public Outcry: Voices of Reason in a Sea of Insensitivity

The backlash was swift and sharp. Democratic influencer Harry Sisson hit the nail on the head, labeling the post “tone deaf, out of touch, and disgusting.” MediasTouch Editor-in-Chief Ron Filipkowski and Melanie D’Arrigo, president of the Campaign for New York Health, echoed this sentiment, highlighting the juxtaposition between Trump’s luxury and the plight of Americans struggling to afford food and healthcare.

Neera Tanden’s commentary perhaps cuts the deepest, portraying Trump as a faux “working-class hero” juxtaposed against his marble-clad reality, all while “families worry about affording food.” Pam Fessler’s sarcasm about Trump’s preference for “highly polished” marble over the “dull” variety only underscores the absurdity and insensitivity of the President’s priorities.

The Stark Reality

The core issue here is not just about a bathroom renovation. It’s about what this renovation represents: a leadership so engulfed in its vanity and luxury that it has become blind to the suffering of its people. The Trump administration’s decision to prioritize aesthetics over assistance, to focus on the superficial rather than the substantive, speaks volumes about its disregard for the very Americans it claims to serve.

This episode is a poignant reminder of the systemic error at the heart of Trump’s presidency: a consistent failure to address, or even acknowledge, the real and pressing needs of the American people. As millions face the grim reality of hunger and healthcare woes, the President’s marble and gold distractions are not just “tone deaf”; they are a resounding gong signaling an administration profoundly out of touch with the nation’s pulse.

In the end, this is not just about a bathroom. It’s about the stark contrast between those who dine in marble halls and those who go hungry. It’s a narrative of neglect, of priorities so misaligned that they threaten the very fabric of American society. And as the criticism rolls in, one can only hope it serves as a wake-up call to an administration seemingly lost in its own reflection.
