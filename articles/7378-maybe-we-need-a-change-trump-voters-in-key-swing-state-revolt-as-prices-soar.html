'Maybe we need a change': Trump voters in key swing state revolt as prices soar

By Paulo Santos

President Donald Trump carried Clare County in Michigan by significant margins in the 2024 presidential election, but his supporters in the rural community are readying to ditch the president as prices continue to soar and food pantries become overwhelmed, Reuters reported Sunday.

“I would probably vote the way the conditions are going,” said Bob Benjamin, a retired auto worker and Trump supporter in Clare County who’s been forced to rely on a local food pantry amid soaring costs, speaking with Reuters on how he sees himself voting in 2028.

“If [Trump is] doing good, if you can see it coming out of a hole, then I give it two more years. But if it’s starting to go back down again, well maybe we need a little change.”

Trump campaigned heavily on lowering prices for Americans, pledging on the campaign trail to “bring down the prices of all goods,” and “starting day one.” Prices, however, have only increased since Trump took office in January, with the costs of all food rising by 2.1 percentage points between January and November.

Energy costs skyrocketed by nearly 10% during the first five months of 2025, an increase “substantially higher than in previous years,” due in no small part to the rapid development of Artificial Intelligence data centers, a key priority of the Trump administration that carry astronomical energy demands and are often subsidized by neighboring communities.

Taylor Ludwig, a Trump supporter and 35-year-old mother of three, has also resorted to relying on food pantries to help make ends meet. When asked whether she intended on voting for the Republican candidate come next presidential election cycle, she said she wasn’t so sure.

“I’m not just gonna follow along somebody like a sheep,” Ludwig said on her support for Trump, speaking with Reuters. “I will follow you until I know it’s not OK to.”

Similarly, inflation has also continued to climb since Trump took office in January. As of November, inflation wasabout 2.7%higher than it was a year ago, slowing modestly in late 2025, which, while not as dramatic as the hyper inflation seen during the COVID-19 pandemic, still inched upward.

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Trump’s Economic Promises: A Critical Analysis

In the heart of Clare County, Michigan, a narrative unfolds that starkly contrasts the roaring campaign promises of President Donald Trump. Despite securing a significant victory in the 2024 presidential election within these rural confines, Trump’s stronghold appears to be waning as the harsh reality of economic distress sets in for his once staunch supporters.

The Core Issue: Rising Prices and Overwhelmed Food Pantries

The article paints a vivid picture of disillusionment among Trump’s base, as soaring costs and the increasing reliance on food pantries become a grim new normal for many. Bob Benjamin, a retired auto worker and previously a Trump supporter, voices a sentiment that is likely echoed across the county: conditional loyalty. Benjamin’s pragmatic approach to his 2028 vote is a testament to the shifting sands of political allegiance when faced with economic hardship. His words, “If [Trump is] doing good...But if it’s starting to go back down again, well maybe we need a little change,” underscore a broader disenchantment rooted in unmet economic promises.

Trump’s Pledge Versus Reality

Trump’s campaign trail was littered with grandiose pledges to “bring down the prices of all goods,” a promise that has evidently fallen flat. The statistics are telling — with a 2.1 percentage point increase in the costs of all food between January and November, and a nearly 10% hike in energy costs during the first five months of 2025. These figures not only highlight the failure to curb price rises but also expose the detrimental impact of Trump administration’s priorities, such as the rapid development of Artificial Intelligence data centers. These centers not only carry “astronomical energy demands” but also burden neighboring communities, presumably through subsidies, exacerbating the economic strain on already struggling households.

A Shift in Supporter Sentiment

Taylor Ludwig, another Trump supporter and a mother of three, encapsulates the growing discontent among the electorate. Ludwig’s determination to not “follow along somebody like a sheep” signifies a critical awakening among voters who feel betrayed by unfulfilled economic promises. This sentiment is further amplified by the continuous climb of inflation since Trump took office, with a 2.7% increase as of November, reflective of a modest slowdown yet a persistent uptick in the cost of living.

The Verdict

The Reuters report from Clare County serves as a microcosm of the broader disillusionment facing the Trump administration. The narrative of economic distress and unmet promises is a potent reminder that political loyalty is not immutable, especially when foundational issues such as the cost of living and economic stability are at stake. The voices of Benjamin and Ludwig are emblematic of a constituency ready to reconsider their allegiance if the economic trajectory under Trump does not improve.

In essence, the unfolding economic reality in Clare County is a stark testament to the chasm between campaign rhetoric and governance. As the 2028 presidential election looms, the Trump administration would do well to heed the growing discontent rooted in economic hardship. Failure to address these concerns may not only result in a significant political shift in rural strongholds like Clare County but also underscore a systemic failing to uphold the very promises that once secured Trump the presidency.
