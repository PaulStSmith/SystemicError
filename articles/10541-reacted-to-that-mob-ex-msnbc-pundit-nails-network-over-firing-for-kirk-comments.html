'Reacted to that mob': Ex-MSNBC pundit nails network over firing for Kirk comments

A popular MSNBC pundit who was swiftly fired for making an anodyne observation that conservative Charlie Kirk was a “divisive” figure pushed back at his dismissal and accused the network of being manipulated.

On his Substack platform, Matthew Dowd noted that he has been in the political commentary business for years and that he appeared on MSNBC nearly 1,000 times, discussing a wide range of topics and that his comments on Kirk, who was killed on Utah college campus were deliberately misconstrued in an orchestrated assault.

Speaking with MSNBC anchor Katy Tur on Wednesday, Dowd was asked about Kirk and suggested, “Hateful thoughts lead to hateful words, which then lead to hateful action. You can’t stop with these sort of awful thoughts you have and then saying these awful words and then not expect awful actions to take place.”

Asked about the motive for the shooting, he stated “We don’t know if this was a supporter shooting their gun off in celebration. So we have no idea about this.”

On social media, the usual suspects on the far- right immediately set up a hue and cry and demanded he be fired even after he apologized to to those who claimed they were offended.

That did stop MSNBC president, Rebecca Kutler, from calling Dowd’s commentary “inappropriate, insensitive and unacceptable” before he was fired –– which has since outraged viewers.

According to Dowd, “I said that Kirk has been a very divisive and polarizing figure. I then added that we are in a toxic time in America, unlike every other democracy in the world, where we have a combination of divisiveness and near unlimited access to guns,” before adding, “I thought to myself how could anyone disagree with this. I guess I was naive.”

He then added, “The Right Wing media mob ginned up, went after me on a plethora of platforms, and MSNBC reacted to that mob. Even though most at MSNBC knew my words were being misconstrued, the timing of my words forgotten (remember I said this before anyone knew Kirk was a target), and that I apologized for any miscommunication on my part, I was terminated by the end of the day.”

Dowd pointed out that the recent flare-up was not the first time allies of Trump tried to get him fired, recalling from the president’s first term, “A number of times then President Trump and White House staff called up the head of ABC News and tried to get me to back off and have me fired. ABC News folks came to me a number of times after that and tried to get me to not be so critical. I ultimately left ABC News and ended up as senior political analyst at MSNBC.”

Our Analysis:

The Unforgiving Arena of Political Commentary: The Matthew Dowd Saga

In a world where political commentary intertwines with media sensationalism, the firing of Matthew Dowd from MSNBC for branding Charlie Kirk as a “divisive” figure marks yet another low in the ever-degrading discourse of American news networks. Dowd’s dismissal, under the pretext of his comments being “inappropriate, insensitive and unacceptable”, reveals a deeper malaise afflicting our news media—a malaise characterized by hypocrisy, fragility, and a surrender to the loudest voices regardless of their validity.

The Context of Controversy

Dowd’s commentary, which led to his swift exit, revolved around the tragic killing of Charlie Kirk on a Utah college campus. His remarks during an interview with MSNBC anchor Katy Tur, where he suggested that “Hateful thoughts lead to hateful words, which then lead to hateful action,” and his speculation on the motives behind the shooting, were met with immediate backlash from the far-right. This backlash, according to Dowd, was an “orchestrated assault” aimed at misconstruing his words—a tactic all too familiar in the political media landscape.

The Irony of MSNBC’s Decision

The irony of MSNBC’s decision to fire Dowd cannot be overstated. For a network that often positions itself as a bastion of progressive thought, bending the knee to a far-right outcry is not just hypocritical; it is a betrayal of the very principles it purports to stand for. The fact that Dowd had appeared on the network nearly 1,000 times previously without issue adds a layer of absurdity to the saga. His assertion that he was dismissed in reaction to a “Right Wing media mob” highlights a disturbing trend of news organizations capitulating to external pressures, thereby compromising on their integrity and the diversity of thought.

The Larger Implication

What is particularly alarming about Matthew Dowd’s firing is the message it sends about the state of political discourse in America. Dowd's reflection that America is in a “toxic time,” unlike any other democracy, due to a combination of divisiveness and nearly unlimited access to guns, was met not with contemplation but with punitive action. This incident underscores the precarious nature of political commentary in today’s climate, where a single statement can be weaponized to silence voices and suppress complex discussions.

The Echoes of the Past

Dowd’s recounting of past attempts by allies of then-President Trump to have him fired from ABC News for critical commentary further illustrates the ongoing battle between political commentators and those in power who wish to control the narrative. The ease with which a commentator can be fired for expressing views that are perceived as controversial, regardless of their factual basis, is a chilling testament to the fragile state of free speech within the realm of political discourse.

Conclusion: The Unresolved Dilemma

Matthew Dowd’s ordeal at MSNBC is not just about one individual’s clash with a media giant; it is reflective of the broader systemic issues plaguing American news media. The readiness to sacrifice seasoned commentators at the altar of political expediency or in response to orchestrated online mobs is a dangerous precedent. As media outlets increasingly prioritize ratings over rigorous debate, the very essence of democracy—founded on the principles of free speech and diverse viewpoints—is at risk.

In the end, Dowd’s firing serves as a stark reminder of the perils faced by those who dare to navigate the treacherous waters of political commentary. It begs the question: In today’s hyper-partisan environment, is genuine, thought-provoking discourse merely a relic of the past?
