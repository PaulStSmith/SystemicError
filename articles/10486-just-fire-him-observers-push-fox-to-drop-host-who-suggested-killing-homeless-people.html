'Just fire him!' Observers push Fox to drop host who suggested 'killing' homeless people

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 15, 20254 min read

Shutterstock

An offhand comment made by Fox News personality Brian Kilmeade about using “involuntary lethal injection” on mentally ill homeless people set off a wave of outrage on Saturday morning, and led to calls for him to be fired later that same day.

Conservative attorney and anti-Trump activist George Conway responded to Kilmeade's Fox segment, during which Kilmeade suggested that we "just kill them," saying, "I have no words for this."

But former prosecutor Elizabeth de la Vega wasn't at a loss for words. Responding to Conway, she said, "I have a few. Just fire him, @FoxNews. Now. Today."

In a separate post, the analyst wrote, "Let's not even hold back here. Brian Kilmeade should be fired. NOW."

Former presidential speechwriter Jon Lovett said, "I know we’re not easily shocked but I am surprised to see Kilmeade spitball about euthanizing homeless people."

"Other two on the couch don’t even seem fazed," he added. "Any other undesirables you’d add to the list, as long as we’re brainstorming?"

"This is a new low" for Fox News, declared ex-GOP lawmaker Adam Kinzinger in a Saturday video. "What has happened in this country?"

Kinzinger says if Fox News doesn't fire Kilmeade, the news outlet has no right to complain about anything anybody says.

Lauren Windsor wrote, "Fire Brian Kilmeade, @FoxNews!"

On Sunday, Kilmeade apologized for his remarks.

"In the morning, we were discussing the murder of Iryna Zarutska in Charlotte, North Carolina and how to stop these kinds of attacks by homeless, mentally ill assailants, including institutionalizing or jailing such people so they cannot attack again. Now during that discussion, I wrongly said they should get lethal injections. I apologize for that extremely callous remark," he said. "I am obviously aware that not all mentally ill, homeless people act as the perpetrator did in North Carolina and that so many homeless people deserve our empathy and compassion.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into Brian Kilmeade's Controversial Commentary

In a recent eruption of controversy, Brian Kilmeade, a Fox News personality, has sparked outrage with his offhand comment advocating for the use of “involuntary lethal injection” on mentally ill homeless individuals. This comment, made in a light seemingly too casual for the gravity of the suggestion, has ignited a firestorm of condemnation from various corners of the political and public sphere.

The Outrage Machine in Full Swing

The backlash was swift and unforgiving. George Conway, a conservative attorney known for his anti-Trump stance, expressed his disbelief, stating, "I have no words for this." However, Elizabeth de la Vega, a former prosecutor, found the words that Conway could not, calling directly for Kilmeade's firing in a tone that matched the gravity of Kilmeade's original transgression. She wasn’t alone; from Jon Lovett’s incredulity at Kilmeade’s "spitball about euthanizing homeless people" to Adam Kinzinger’s declaration of this event as a "new low" for Fox News, the consensus was clear: Kilmeade's comments were beyond the pale.

The Apology: Too Little, Too Late?

Following the outcry, Kilmeade issued an apology, attempting to contextualize his comments within a discussion on how to prevent attacks by "homeless, mentally ill assailants." He admitted his remarks about lethal injections were "wrong" and "extremely callous," acknowledging the need for empathy and compassion towards the homeless population.

This apology, however, reads as a classic case of too little, too late. Kilmeade's initial suggestion of lethal injection as a solution to homelessness and mental illness represents a shocking devaluation of human life, cloaked in the guise of public safety concern. It's a sentiment that, once aired, cannot be neatly packed away with a simple apology. The damage – to public discourse, to the perception of Fox News, to Kilmeade's own reputation – has been done.

The Bigger Picture

Kilmeade's comments and the subsequent fallout highlight a disturbing trend in our public discourse: the casual dehumanization of vulnerable populations for shock value or political point-scoring. It's a tactic that's not only morally reprehensible but dangerously short-sighted, contributing to a climate where empathy and understanding are in short supply.

Moreover, the reaction from both the public and figures like Conway, de la Vega, and Kinzinger underscores a growing intolerance for such rhetoric. It suggests that, despite the polarized state of our current political landscape, there are still lines that many agree should not be crossed.

Conclusion

Brian Kilmeade's "apology" does little to undo the harm of his initial statement. It's a stark reminder of the power of words, especially when spoken by individuals with significant media influence. Fox News, and Kilmeade himself, must reckon with the reality that the outrage sparked by his comments is not just about political correctness. It's about basic human decency and the recognition that some suggestions are so beyond the pale that they cannot — and should not — be excused as mere slip-ups in judgment.

In this instance, the systemic error within our media and public discourse becomes glaringly apparent. It's not just about one individual's mistake; it's about a culture that allows, and sometimes even encourages, such reckless dehumanization. As we navigate these treacherous waters, it's incumbent upon us all to demand better, not only from our media personalities but from ourselves.
