'Arming the problem': Ex-Trump operative slams president for making school shootings worse

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20254 min read

Donald Trump speaks to the media on board Air Force One . REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

A former Donald Trump insider who has himself experienced a child hiding from an active shooter skewered the president for making the school shooting problem even worse.

Former Trump associate Lev Parnas, who says he used to be "deep inside the Trump machine" and now reports from the outside, published an article called "The Dreaded Phone Call No Parent Should Ever Get." In the piece, Parnas talks about his own past with a daughter texting him while hiding from a potential shooter.

"I remember everything about that day. I remember the panic in my chest as I jumped in my car and sped toward my daughter’s high school. As I drove, my mind was racing with every dark thought imaginable — was she safe, where was she hiding, would I ever see her again?" Parnas recalled, before taking it to Trump.

"And here’s what makes me sick: instead of tackling this head-on, Donald Trump wastes taxpayer money on vanity projects and theatrics. Hundreds of millions on ballrooms, parades, and photo-ops with the National Guard," Parnas wrote. "He parades himself as 'tough on crime' and 'tough on immigration,' but when it comes to the real crisis — kids being gunned down in classrooms — he is silent."

The ex-Trump insider went on to ask, "What about our children? Where is his toughness then? Instead of protecting schools, he pushes to make it easier to get assault rifles. Just think about that."

Parnas then quoted Trump as saying, “I will protect the right of self-defense everywhere it is under siege, and I will sign concealed-carry reciprocity. Your Second Amendment does not end at the state line.”

Parnas added:

"And this isn’t just talk. In 2023, he reaffirmed that pledge. And now, in 2025, House Republicans have introduced the so-called Constitutional Concealed Carry Reciprocity Act, which would force every state to recognize concealed carry permits issued anywhere in the country. Trump has already vowed to sign it into law. That’s not protecting kids — that’s arming the problem."

Parnas further wrote, "This must stop. Enough is enough."

"For our children, for our future — it’s time to act," he concluded.

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Safety: Trump's Misguided Priorities

In a scathing critique, Lev Parnas, a former insider of the Donald Trump administration, pulls back the curtain on what he describes as a catastrophic failure to address the epidemic of school shootings in the United States. Parnas, once entrenched in the so-called "Trump machine," has emerged as an unlikely yet compelling critic, particularly given his harrowing personal encounter with the nightmare of a potential school shooting involving his own daughter.

The Reality of Fear vs. The Spectacle of Power

Parnas's recounting of the terror he felt upon receiving a text from his daughter, who was hiding from a potential shooter, cuts through the political rhetoric to remind us of the visceral fear that parents across the nation face. This fear, he argues, is only exacerbated by Trump's focus on "vanity projects and theatrics" over substantive policy measures that could protect children in their classrooms. The hundreds of millions spent on "ballrooms, parades, and photo-ops with the National Guard" stand in stark contrast to the lack of action toward safeguarding schools from gun violence.

The Paradox of 'Tough on Crime'

Trump's self-promotion as being "tough on crime" and "tough on immigration" is lambasted by Parnas as nothing short of hypocritical. When the crisis involves "kids being gunned down in classrooms," Trump's voice, according to Parnas, is conspicuously absent. This critique highlights a disturbing disconnect between Trump's tough-on-crime persona and his actual policy priorities, especially his advocacy for making it easier to obtain assault rifles.

Legislative Insanity: The Constitutional Concealed Carry Reciprocity Act

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of Parnas's critique is his focus on the so-called Constitutional Concealed Carry Reciprocity Act, which Trump has vowed to sign into law. This legislation, which would force every state to recognize concealed carry permits issued anywhere in the country, is emblematic of a broader trend toward loosening gun control measures at a time when stricter regulations are desperately needed. Parnas rightly identifies this as not a solution to the crisis of school shootings but an exacerbation of the problem. By prioritizing the rights of gun owners over the safety of children, Trump and his allies in Congress are, in Parnas's view, "arming the problem."

Conclusion: A Call to Action

Lev Parnas concludes his critique with a simple yet powerful declaration: "Enough is enough." This sentiment, shared by countless Americans, serves as a rallying cry for those who believe that the time for action is long overdue. Parnas's transformation from a Trump insider to a critic provides a unique perspective on the failures of an administration that, in his view, has prioritized political spectacle over the safety of the nation's children.

In the end, Parnas's article is not just an indictment of Trump but a call to recognize and address the real and present danger of school shootings. It is a reminder that policy and legislation should be guided by the imperative to protect the most vulnerable among us, rather than by political expediency or the interests of powerful lobbies. The true measure of a society's values lies in how it protects its children, and by that metric, Parnas argues, we are failing.
