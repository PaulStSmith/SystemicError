Trump's go-to economic boast vanishes in new data: report

Based on an article by Matthew Chapman

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

Trump Lit the Fuse, Then Tried to Sell the Smoke

The Numbers Finally Caught Up

The source story is simple enough: inflation-adjusted wage gains that once gave Trump a talking point have been erased, with compensation now only 0.1 percent higher since January 2025. That is not a minor wobble. It is the bill arriving after an energy shock, and households are paying it in reduced purchasing power.

Power Sat With the President

The person with actual institutional power here is Donald Trump. He is the president. He chose the war in Iran, and the source says that war has helped push the Strait of Hormuz into practical shutdown, disrupting global energy markets and driving prices up everywhere. This is not an external force “happening to” the administration. The administration helped create the conditions.

The Real Decision Was the One That Made the Crisis

The decisive action was not a speech or a slogan. It was the war itself, and apparently the possibility that Trump at one point considered ending it without resolving the Strait of Hormuz crisis. That detail matters because it shows the administration treating a strategically self-inflicted energy crisis as something to narrate around, not solve. The damage is political and economic, but the responsibility is operational.

The Cover Story Is Absurd

Trump’s response follows the usual pattern of authoritarian-grade economic denial: deny the problem, then improvise a miracle story. He says there is nothing wrong with the economy. He says, “I love the inflation.” He also claims he is secretly transporting 100 million barrels of oil to solve the Strait of Hormuz crisis, a claim the source says could not be verified. That is not governance. It is misdirection dressed up as confidence.

The Article’s Framing Is Too Polite

The weakest part of the source framing is the suggestion that this is merely a new inflation development or an unfortunate market squeeze. It is more direct than that. Trump’s war has produced a predictable energy shock, and his public posture is to deny consequence while inventing private fixes no one can verify. That is not confusion. It is blame management.

The Pattern Is Familiar

This is the recurring structure of power under Trump: create a crisis, deny the cause, claim mastery, and let the public absorb the costs. Workers lose wage gains. Energy prices rise. The president keeps the microphone. The lesson is not that institutions failed to explain the problem. The lesson is that institutional power can manufacture pain, then try to control the story of that pain before the numbers become undeniable.
