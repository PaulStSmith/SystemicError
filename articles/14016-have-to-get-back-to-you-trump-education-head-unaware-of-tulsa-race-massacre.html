'Have to get back to you': Trump education head unaware of Tulsa Race Massacre

Education Secretary Linda McMahon indicated that she was unaware of the Tulsa Race Massacre under questioning about President Donald Trump's "illegal" restrictions on diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) policies in U.S. schools.

During a Wednesday hearing before the House Committee on Education & Workforce, Rep. Summer Lee (D-PA) said that the Trump administration had "undoubtedly revived the culture of racism."

"They've made it clear that open attacks on Black and brown and other marginalized communities is not just tolerated, but it's encouraged," Lee told McMahon. "So let's talk about so-called illegal DEI, as you all have called it, a phrase you continually bring up that I'm still unclear on, especially after three federal judges have preliminarily ruled that your illegal DEI guidance is likely unconstitutional and unenforceable. Illegal, as it were."

"Would you say that it would be an illegal DEI for a lesson plan on the Tulsa Race Massacre?"

"I'd have to get back to you on that," McMahon replied.

"Do you know what the Tulsa Race Massacre is?" Lee wondered.

"I'd like to look into it more and get back to you on it," McMahon stated.

"How about social studies standards that teach President Biden won the 2020 election?" Lee pressed.

"I think our study should all be taught accurately," McMahon insisted.

"Yes or no?" the Democratic lawmaker demanded. "No, no, no. The question was, do you believe that social study standards that teach that President Biden won the 2020 election is an illegal DEI? Yes or no?"

"I think I have said we should teach accurately," McMahon repeated.

"No, you have not answered the question," Lee noted. "I don't understand why you're incapable of answering you."

McMahon, however, insisted she had answered the question.

"No, the answer is yes or no," Lee argued.

"The answer is I believe history has to be taught accurately," McMahon said again.

Our Analysis:

McMahon's Evasive Dance on DEI and Historical Awareness: A Masterclass in Political Obfuscation

In a recent hearing that could only be described as a theatrical display of ignorance and evasion, Education Secretary Linda McMahon's responses—or lack thereof—underscore a troubling trend in the Trump administration's approach to diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) policies in U.S. schools. Representative Summer Lee's (D-PA) interrogation of McMahon not only highlighted the administration's alleged revival of a culture of racism but also showcased McMahon's apparent lack of basic historical knowledge and her reluctance to take a clear stance on DEI initiatives.

Unpacking the Evasion

The Ignorance on Tulsa Race Massacre: McMahon's admission of needing to "look into" the Tulsa Race Massacre is either a shocking revelation of ignorance or a calculated attempt to sidestep acknowledging one of the most horrific instances of racial violence in American history. The massacre, which occurred in 1921, saw the destruction of the Greenwood District in Tulsa, Oklahoma, also known as "Black Wall Street," by white supremacist mobs. That an Education Secretary might be unaware of such a pivotal event—or feign ignorance—is both astounding and deeply concerning.

DEI Policies Under Fire: McMahon's vague responses to questions regarding the Trump administration's stance on DEI policies further illuminate a disturbing trend of obfuscation. The administration's labeling of these policies as "illegal" despite preliminary rulings from three federal judges suggesting otherwise speaks volumes about its commitment to undermining efforts aimed at promoting diversity and inclusion within educational settings.

Election Denial?: When pressed on whether teaching that President Biden won the 2020 election would be considered an "illegal DEI," McMahon's refusal to provide a straightforward answer is telling. Her insistence on teaching history "accurately" without acknowledging the legitimacy of the 2020 election results is a veiled nod to the dangerous and unfounded election fraud narratives that continue to polarize the nation.

The Implications

McMahon's responses—or the lack thereof—during the hearing are emblematic of a larger, more systemic issue within the Trump administration's approach to education and historical truth. This evasion tactic not only undermines the importance of DEI initiatives but also signals a willingness to distort or ignore historical facts and democratic processes for political gain.

The refusal to engage honestly with questions about DEI and the Tulsa Race Massacre suggests an administration more concerned with preserving a particular narrative than promoting an inclusive and accurate educational curriculum. Such positions, if left unchecked, risk further entrenching racial and cultural divides within American society.

Conclusion

The hearing with Education Secretary Linda McMahon was more than just a missed opportunity for clarity on the Trump administration's education policies. It was a stark reminder of the dangers posed by political leaders who choose evasion over honesty and partisanship over historical accuracy. In a time when the U.S. is grappling with its history of racial injustice and seeking ways to foster a more inclusive society, the need for clear, informed, and committed leadership in the realm of education has never been more acute. McMahon's performance was a disservice not just to the position she holds but to the very principles of education and democracy.
