'Surprising no one!' Trump official blasted for hijacking credit for Biden’s upgrades

Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy is under fire for claiming credit for new Amtrak trains, as well as funding and improvements, all of which came from President Joe Biden’s landmark bipartisan infrastructure law — the nation’s largest infrastructure investment in more than half a century.

“Announcing the NextGen Acela!” Secretary Duffy declared on Wednesday. He touted the “brand new beautiful trains” delivered by U.S. DOT and Amtrak that will, he wrote, “increase reliability,” “lower ticket costs,” and “improve Amtrak profitability.”

“This is all part of @POTUS’ vision to Make Travel Great again,” Duffy claimed.

READ MORE: Trump Orders Death Penalty for All D.C. Homicides, Defying Long Ban

But several critics were quick to point to President Joe Biden’s Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) that was signed into law in November 2021.

CNN senior reporter Edward-Isaac Dovere wrote: “as detailed on the Amtrak website, the improvements that Secretary Duffy says are all part of Trump’s ‘vision to Make Travel Great again’ are in fact from the Biden-signed bipartisan infrastructure act, and which were underway long before Trump took office again in January.”

The “information about the source of the funding for the ‘new era of rail’ — a priority for famously Amtrak-loving Biden — is up on the Amtrak site here,” he noted, pointing to this Amtrak website, which reads:

“This is a once-in-a-generation opportunity for Amtrak and passenger rail to play a central role in improving how people move and securing our economic future. This historic legislation provides $66 billion for rail, including: $22 billion directly to Amtrak over five years to repair or replace aging assets, modernize our fleet, improve station accessibility, and other capital projects and purposes defined under the law.”

Also, Amtrak noted, “$44 billion for Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) discretionary grants to support various improvements to passenger and freight railroads.”

Others also blasted the DOT chief.

“Sean Duffy, surprising absolutely no one, taking credit for something brought to you in large part by the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, signed by President Biden,” charged former chief Pentagon spokesperson Chris Meagher.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Credit Appropriation: Unpacking Secretary Duffy's Claims

In a display of political grandstanding that would make even the most seasoned spin doctors blush, Secretary of Transportation Sean Duffy has managed to embroil himself in a controversy that cuts to the very core of the current administration's understanding of the word "credit." At the heart of the matter is Duffy's audacious attempt to claim ownership over the new Amtrak trains, along with related funding and improvements—a feat he attributes to what he calls President Trump's vision to "Make Travel Great Again."

The Biden Infrastructure Law: A Forgotten Genesis?

Let's dissect this with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned cynic. The crux of Duffy's claim lies in his enthusiastic announcement of the "NextGen Acela" trains, described in almost poetic terms as "brand new beautiful trains" heralding an era of increased reliability, lowered ticket costs, and improved Amtrak profitability. A visionary narrative, if one conveniently forgets the actual visionary behind this: President Joe Biden and his Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA).

Signed into law in November 2021, the IIJA represents the most significant investment in American infrastructure in over half a century. With a whopping $66 billion earmarked for rail, including $22 billion directly to Amtrak, the act is nothing short of a lifeline for the future of American rail travel. Edward-Isaac Dovere of CNN points out a glaring oversight (or calculated omission) in Duffy's narrative: these improvements and investments stem directly from Biden's IIJA, not from the Trump administration's policies or visions.

Reality Check: The Biden-Amtrak Nexus

The narrative pushed by Duffy crumbles under the slightest scrutiny, much like a cookie in the hands of an eager child. The Amtrak website itself serves as the tombstone for Duffy's claims, outlining in no uncertain terms how the IIJA is poised to revolutionize rail travel in America. This is not just about repairing aging assets or modernizing fleets; it's a comprehensive blueprint for a more accessible, efficient, and economically viable rail system, courtesy of the Biden administration.

The Fallout: Critics Weigh In

Critics, both from within and outside the political sphere, have been quick to lambast Duffy's attempt at rewriting history. Chris Meagher, a former chief Pentagon spokesperson, minces no words in highlighting the absurdity of Duffy's credit grab, emphasizing the role of the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law—a product of Biden's presidency—in bringing these rail improvements to fruition.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Humility and Honesty

In a world increasingly bereft of humility and honesty, Duffy's misadventure serves as a cautionary tale. It underscores the importance of giving credit where it's due, rather than attempting to hijack accomplishments for political brownie points. As we navigate the murky waters of political discourse, let's not lose sight of the facts. The new era of rail in America is, unequivocally, a testament to President Biden's infrastructure vision—a vision that seeks to make travel not just great again, but better than it ever was.

In the end, Duffy's claims are not just a systemic error; they're a stark reminder of the ongoing battle between truth and fabrication in the political arena. As co-authors of reality, it's our duty to ensure the former prevails.
