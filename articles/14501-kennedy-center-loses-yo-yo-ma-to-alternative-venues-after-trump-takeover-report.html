The hits keep coming for the John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts, more commonly known as the Kennedy Center, as Donald Trump's stewardship has driven away more acts.

Since the president supplanted former Board Chair David M. Rubenstein with himself, and tapped MAGA ally Richard Grenell to oversee operations, the world famous venue has seen multiple acts cancel and ticket sales plummet after Trump made his case to rid the center of "woke" acts.

Adding to the center losing a production of the highly-popular musical "Hamilton," notable classical musician and 2011 Kennedy Center Honoree Yo-Yo Ma will be taking his cello to a different venue this year.

EXCLUSIVE: Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

According to a report from the Washingtonian, booking powerhouse Washington Performing Arts has parted ways with the Kennedy Center this season.

After stating the organization "will not utilize The Kennedy Center,” a spokesperson explained their intention is to "thoughtfully match artists and their work with venues that best serve the art, the audience, and the moment“ and then added, "we assessed the variety of artists and performances we have in this upcoming season and decided it was best to explore new spaces that offer fresh possibilities.”

The organization announced new venues, stating, "Sigourney Weaver and the Pacifica Quartet at Sixth and I for WPA’s annual Ruth Bader Ginsburg Memorial Concert, the Chicago Symphony Orchestra at Strathmore, Yo-Yo Ma at Strathmore, and Midori at Sixth and I."

In May, an analyst said the exodus of donors, subscribers and artists has made "everything worse" at the Kennedy Center.

According to the New Yorker's Katy Waldman, "... no matter how well or badly the center was doing before Trump, the Trump anti-Midas touch is in operation here," before pointing out, "Not only are ticket sales down, but donations have been paused, artists are pulling out. All of the spigots for money have slowed or have been turned off."

Our Analysis:

The Kennedy Center's Descent: A Trumpian Tale of Exclusion and Decline

The John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts, an emblem of cultural sophistication and artistic freedom, finds itself in the throes of a distressing transformation. With Donald Trump at the helm, replacing former Board Chair David M. Rubenstein and appointing MAGA stalwart Richard Grenell to oversee operations, the Kennedy Center has embarked on a precipitous decline. The raison d'être of this cultural institution appears to have been fundamentally altered, as evidenced by the exodus of performances and the plummeting ticket sales following Trump's crusade against "woke" acts. The departure of the highly acclaimed musical "Hamilton" and the 2011 Kennedy Center Honoree, the classical musician Yo-Yo Ma, marks a significant blow to the center's prestige and cultural standing.

A Strategic Exodus

The Washingtonian report sheds light on the strategic withdrawal of the Washington Performing Arts from the Kennedy Center. This decision, ostensibly to "thoughtfully match artists and their work with venues that best serve the art, the audience, and the moment," speaks volumes. It's not merely a logistical realignment but a pointed critique of the Kennedy Center's current stewardship. The move to explore "new spaces that offer fresh possibilities" is a diplomatic euphemism for avoiding a venue that has become politically compromised and artistically stifled under Trump's governance.

The Trump Anti-Midas Touch

Katy Waldman of the New Yorker eloquently captures the essence of the Kennedy Center's plight with the notion of the "Trump anti-Midas touch." Everything Trump touches seems to turn not to gold, but to lead. The exodus of donors, subscribers, and artists has exacerbated the center's woes. Ticket sales have dwindled, donations have stalled, and the center's lifeblood—its artists—are fleeing. This isn't merely a shift in patronage or a change in artistic direction; it's a comprehensive dismantling of the center's foundational principles.

Implications and the Road Ahead

The Kennedy Center, under Trump's stewardship, provides a stark example of how political ideologies and personal vendettas can corrode the essence of cultural institutions. The center's decline is not just a loss for the patrons and artists directly involved but signifies a broader cultural setback. The departure of esteemed acts and the severance of ties with booking powerhouses like Washington Performing Arts underscore a troubling trend towards cultural homogenization and political polarization within spaces that were once considered sacrosanct and above the fray.

In the end, the Kennedy Center's struggle is symptomatic of a larger cultural malaise. The erosion of artistic freedom and the politicization of cultural spaces pose significant threats to the vibrancy and diversity of the arts. The center's future, much like the broader cultural landscape, hangs in the balance, contingent upon the willingness of those in power to prioritize artistry over ideology and inclusion over exclusion. But based on the current trajectory, the prognosis is grim. The Kennedy Center, once a beacon of cultural excellence and artistic liberty, now stands as a cautionary tale of how quickly and thoroughly a cherished institution can be compromised.
