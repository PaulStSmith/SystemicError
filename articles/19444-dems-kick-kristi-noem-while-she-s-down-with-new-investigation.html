Dems kick Kristi Noem while she's down with new investigation

The New York Times reports the hits on former Homeland Security head Kristi Noem just keep coming.

“Her firing doesn’t absolve her or relieve her of potential liability for perjury, and we are going to pursue an investigation of the evidence that she lied, because it relates to corruption in the administration,” said Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.).

Blumenthal told the Times that Democrats have evidence to suggest that Noem’s advisor Corey Lewandowski had approved contracts for the Department of Homeland Security, despite having no authority to do so. Worse, Blumental said Noem had lied under oath during a Tuesday Senate hearing when she claimed that Lewandowski had not approved contracts.

During the Tuesday Senate hearing, Blumenthal asked Noem directly: “Does Corey Lewandowski have a role in approving contracts, and if so, what is that role?”

“His role is as a special government employee, and special government employees work for the White House and the administration,” Noem said without answer.

“So, he does have a role,” Blumenthal pressed.

“No,” Noem eventually said.

The next day, the Times reports Blumenthal sent a letter to Noem suggesting she had misrepresented Lewandowski’s role.

“Evidence suggests that your testimony was false,” Blumenthal wrote, adding that department records showed that Mr. Lewandowski had “personally approved contracts” and that employees believed his signature was a “green light” for spending.

The Times reports Democrats cannot currently launch a full investigation without the support of Republicans, who control of the Senate. But Blumenthal can still hold public forums on the topic, send letters requesting information and solicit whistle-blowers at a time when the Trump administration and its lieutenants are sensitive to reports of corruption and ineptitude.

Noem was fired from her post as Homeland Security secretary after two days of ruthless grilling from both Democrats and Republicans — who are wary of administrative incompetence as Congress moves into what will likely be a brutal midterm election for the GOP.

Our Analysis:

The Unraveling of Kristi Noem: A Study in Alleged Corruption and Incompetence

In a political drama that has all the makings of a Shakespearean tragedy, former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem finds herself embroiled in a scandal that could redefine the word ‘accountability’ in American politics. The New York Times report sheds light on a series of allegations that, if proven true, could not only tarnish Noem’s career but also expose a deeper level of corruption within the administration she once served.

The Allegations: A Web of Lies and Unauthorized Power

At the heart of this controversy is Corey Lewandowski, a name that seems to emerge whenever there’s a political firestorm. According to Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.), evidence suggests that Lewandowski, serving as a special government employee, overstepped his bounds by approving contracts for the Department of Homeland Security without any legal authority. The implications of such actions are staggering, hinting at a blatant disregard for the legal frameworks that govern contract approvals within federal agencies.

But the plot thickens. Blumenthal’s accusations against Noem don’t merely stop at unauthorized contract approvals. The more damning allegation is that Noem perjured herself under oath during a Senate hearing. When pressed by Blumenthal about Lewandowski’s role in approving contracts, Noem’s response was, at best, a masterclass in evasion, eventually culminating in a direct denial. However, Blumenthal’s subsequent letter to Noem, armed with evidence to the contrary, paints a picture of deliberate deception.

The Political Fallout: A Looming Shadow Over the GOP

Noem’s firing from her post as Homeland Security Secretary after just two days of intense scrutiny from both sides of the aisle signifies a broader concern within the GOP. With the midterm elections on the horizon, the last thing the party needs is a scandal that highlights administrative incompetence and potential corruption. The bipartisan unease surrounding Noem’s actions and the subsequent investigation suggests a rare moment of agreement in an otherwise polarized political landscape.

The Road Ahead: A Test of Political Will and Integrity

As the saga unfolds, the limitations faced by Democrats in launching a full investigation, due to the Senate’s Republican majority, underscore a significant challenge in holding public officials accountable. Blumenthal’s pivot to public forums, solicitation of whistle-blowers, and requests for information represents a strategic, albeit limited, approach to shedding light on the allegations against Noem and, by extension, the administration.

The narrative that emerges from the New York Times report is one of power, deception, and a quest for accountability. It serves as a reminder of the importance of transparency and integrity in public service. As this story develops, it will undoubtedly test the resolve of political leaders and institutions to uphold these values in the face of mounting evidence of wrongdoing.

In closing, the controversy surrounding Kristi Noem and Corey Lewandowski offers a compelling case study in the mechanisms of power and the ease with which it can be abused. It also highlights the critical role of vigilant oversight and the press in uncovering truths that those in power would prefer to keep hidden. As we watch this drama unfold, one thing is clear: the political ramifications of these allegations will reverberate far beyond the individuals involved, potentially shaping the landscape of American politics for years to come.
