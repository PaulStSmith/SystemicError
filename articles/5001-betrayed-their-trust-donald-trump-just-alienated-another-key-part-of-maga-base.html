'Betrayed their trust': Donald Trump just alienated another key part of MAGA base

By Paulo Santos

During his 2024 campaign, Donald Trump broadened his appeal by ramping up his outreach to Latinos, independents, swing voters, tech bros, Generation Z and the Manosphere. Trump’s 2024 coalition also included the MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) movement, which started with conspiracy theorist Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s presidential campaign. But now, according to the conservative website The Bulwark, he is alienating MAHA activists.

RFK Jr., son of the late Sen. Robert F. Kennedy Sr. (D-Massachusetts) and nephew of President John F. Kennedy, initially challenged then-President Joe Biden in the 2024 Democratic presidential primary. But after exiting that primary, he continued his presidential campaign as an independent before ending his campaign altogether, joining the Republican Party, and endorsing Trump. Now, RFK Jr. serves as secretary of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) under the second Trump Administration.

Trump recently issued an executive order demanding more production of the pesticide glyphosate, which many environmentalists consider dangerous.

In an article published by The Bulwark on February 22, reporter Jonathan Cohn explains, “The reaction on social media was exactly what you’d expect to hear from environmentalists on the left: ‘absolutely disgusting and shameful,’ ‘literally no justification for the way this was done,’ ‘a middle finger to public health.” But these posts didn’t come from Trump’s progressive critics. They came from some of his most enthusiastic supporters: influencers who identify as part of the Make America Healthy Again movement.”

Cohn adds, “MAHA is the loose coalition of activists, social media figures, and like-minded voters who believe America is being slowly poisoned by the food and pharmaceutical industries. Their preoccupations include fighting environmental toxins and hawking wellness products, promoting natural diets, and challenging mainstream science on vaccines.”

RFK Jr. draws strong criticism from Democrats for anti-vaxxer views they consider dangerous, although they agree with some of his ideas: more exercise, eating fewer processed foods, eating more fruits and vegetables.

Trump, meanwhile, is known for his unhealthy diet, which includes consuming huge quantities of McDonald’s food and Diet Coke. Yet his eating habits have managed to coexist with MAGA within the MAGA movement.

“Early on, (MAHA) felt their faith was being rewarded,” Cohn explains. “Trump tapped Kennedy to lead the Department of Health and Human Services, with a mandate to ‘ensure that everybody will be protected from harmful chemicals, pollutants, pesticides, pharmaceutical products, and food additives.’ But that was then. In the wake of Wednesday’s executive order and a series of episodes leading up to it, MAHA’s leaders say they feel like Trump has betrayed their trust and, in the process, alienated their followers.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Coalition: A Convergence of Contradictions

In a political landscape that continually defies expectation, Donald Trump’s latest foray into coalition-building reads like a political strategist’s fever dream. By attempting to court everyone from Latinos and independents to tech bros, Generation Z, and the Manosphere, Trump’s 2024 campaign strategy appears to throw the conventional political playbook out the window. Yet, the inclusion of the MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) movement into this eclectic mix might just be the most bewildering chapter in this saga of strange bedfellows.

The Unlikely Alliance with RFK Jr.

Robert F. Kennedy Jr., a figure synonymous with controversial anti-vaccine rhetoric, transitioning from a Democratic primary challenger to a Trump-endorsing Republican, is a plot twist worthy of a political thriller. His appointment as secretary of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) under Trump’s administration represents an alliance that, at its core, seems fundamentally flawed. This union between Trump, with his notorious fast-food diet, and the MAHA movement, with its focus on combating “the food and pharmaceutical industries,” is as unstable as it is unfathomable.

The Glyphosate Gambit

The issuance of an executive order to increase the production of glyphosate, a decision that flies in the face of environmental and health concerns, is where the cracks in this fragile coalition begin to show. The backlash from MAHA supporters, as reported by The Bulwark, underscores a glaring oversight in Trump’s coalition-building strategy: the presumption that ideological loyalty would trump (pun intended) deeply held beliefs about health and environmental preservation.

The scathing reactions from Trump’s own supporters reveal a betrayal that could have been anticipated by anyone who understood the core values of the MAHA movement. Yet, in his characteristic overreach, Trump seems to have miscalculated the extent to which his diverse support base would follow him, particularly when his decisions directly contradict the principles of one of his key constituencies.

The MAGA and MAHA Dichotomy

The ironic coexistence of Trump’s unhealthy dietary habits within the MAGA movement, juxtaposed with the health-centric MAHA philosophy, highlights a broader theme of contradiction within Trump’s political strategy. It’s a vivid illustration of a campaign trying to be all things to all people, ultimately satisfying none. The MAGA movement, with its disregard for political correctness and establishment norms, clashes fundamentally with the MAHA movement’s focus on health and wellness.

The Fallout

As Trump alienates the MAHA activists, one must wonder about the sustainability of a coalition built on such shaky ground. The fallout from the glyphosate executive order might just be the beginning of a larger unraveling, as more supporters begin to question the alignment of Trump’s actions with their own values.

In conclusion, the Trump 2024 campaign’s attempt to broaden its appeal through a diverse coalition might be its undoing. By trying to court groups with fundamentally opposing views, Trump risks not only alienating key supporters but also rendering his political brand incoherent. The glyphosate debacle is a potent reminder that in politics, as in life, you cannot please all the people all the time. And for Trump, the cost of trying to do so might be the loyalty of his most ardent followers.
