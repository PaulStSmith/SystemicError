A former Department of Justice official took a shot at newly-appointed U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan, who is spearheading the prosecution of former FBI Director James Comey, noting that she is needing extra help due to her lack of experience.

During an appearance on MSNBC’s “The Weekend,” attorney Liz Oyer, who reviewed pardons at the Department of Justice, was asked what she made of the appointment of Halligan since she has no experience in criminal matters.

“Yeah, Lindsey Halligan is a sham prosecutor,” she told co-host Eugene Daniels. “She has no relevant experience. She has no knowledge of what she’s doing. She apparently had to be tutored in how to present a case to the grand jury, so that she could do so to get this indictment against James Comey.”

“She had to do that because nobody else in the entire Eastern District of Virginia, U.S. attorney’s office, which has hundreds of employees, was willing to go into that grand jury and present that evidence,” she added.“Donald Trump looks for one thing in people who he puts in high level positions, and that is loyalty to Donald Trump,” she observed.

“And that is really not the quality that U.S. attorneys are supposed to have. They take an oath to uphold the Constitution. They are there to serve the people of the United States.”

On Friday, MSNBC reported that Halligan was accused of “awkwardness” when dealing with the Comey paperwork with the judge.

Our Analysis:

A Critique of Inexperienced Prosecutorial Appointment and the Shadows of Political Allegiance

In an era where the lines between political loyalty and professional integrity seem increasingly blurred, the recent developments surrounding the appointment of Lindsey Halligan to lead the prosecution against former FBI Director James Comey have ignited a firestorm of criticism and concern. The crux of this controversy? Halligan’s glaring lack of experience in handling criminal matters, an issue so pronounced that it has drawn scathing remarks from former Department of Justice officials and raised eyebrows across the legal community.

Liz Oyer’s Bold Assertion: “A Sham Prosecutor”

Liz Oyer, a former attorney within the Department of Justice, did not mince words during her appearance on MSNBC’s “The Weekend,” where she lambasted Halligan as a “sham prosecutor.” Oyer’s critique hinges on a critical point: Halligan’s apparent inexperience in the realm of criminal law. This isn’t just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring gap in Halligan’s qualifications for a role as consequential as leading the prosecution against a high-profile figure like James Comey.

The Tutoring Episode: A Symptom of a Larger Problem

The revelation that Halligan required tutoring to present a case to the grand jury is not just an embarrassing tidbit; it’s a damning indictment of her unpreparedness for this role. This episode serves as a stark manifestation of the pitfalls of appointing officials based on criteria other than merit and relevant experience. The fact that no other attorney in the Eastern District of Virginia’s U.S. attorney’s office was willing to take on this case speaks volumes about the controversial nature of the prosecution and the potential motivations behind Halligan’s appointment.

Donald Trump’s Alleged Criterion: Loyalty Above All

Liz Oyer’s observations on Donald Trump’s alleged preference for loyalty in his appointments strike at the heart of a troubling trend in political appointments. The suggestion that Halligan was selected for her loyalty to Trump rather than her legal acumen is deeply concerning. U.S. attorneys are sworn to uphold the Constitution and serve the public interest, not the whims of any individual, be they a former president or otherwise. This departure from the principles of impartiality and professionalism undermines the integrity of the legal system and erodes public trust in its institutions.

The “Awkwardness” Incident: A Red Flag

The reported awkwardness Halligan displayed in handling the Comey paperwork with the judge is more than just an uncomfortable moment; it’s indicative of her unsuitability for this high-stakes role. Such incidents raise serious questions about her capability to manage complex and sensitive legal proceedings, further underscoring the imprudence of her appointment.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale of Political Patronage

The controversy surrounding Lindsey Halligan’s appointment and subsequent actions is a glaring example of the dangers inherent in allowing political considerations to override meritocracy and expertise in the legal profession. It serves as a cautionary tale about the erosion of legal norms and the potential consequences of prioritizing loyalty over competence. As this saga unfolds, it will undoubtedly continue to spark debate and introspection about the values and principles that underpin the American legal system.
