Todd Blanche made a fatal error — and it will punish him

Based on an article by Ray Richmond

Paulo SantosAugust 15, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Todd Blanche Is Not a Lawyer for the Nation. He Is the Invoice

Todd Blanche is on track to become attorney general after a confirmation process that functioned less like scrutiny than like a controlled demonstration of Republican surrender. He met with Epstein survivors just long enough to satisfy two senators. He signed a paper declaring Trump’s “Anti-Weaponization Fund” dead. He offered just enough stagecraft to keep the machinery moving. That is the whole story the Senate wants to pretend is complicated.

Power Is Not in the Hearing Room

The real decision-maker here is Donald Trump. Blanche is not being elevated because of independence, expertise, or even credibility. He is being installed because he is useful. The source makes that plain: his value is obedience, not judgment.

That is why the confirmation farce matters. Cornyn and Tillis did not exercise leverage. They performed it. They demanded rituals, received documents, and then blessed the outcome they had already decided to accept. This is not constitutional oversight. It is the laundering of a corrupt appointment through procedural theater.

DOJ as Trump’s Private Firm

The article is right to describe the Department of Justice as Trump’s personal law office. That is not hyperbole; it is a description of institutional capture. Blanche’s reported role is to protect Trump, not the public. The “retroactive” IRS immunity for Trump and his family is a giveaway dressed up as paperwork, and the supposedly dead slush fund is presented in a way that leaves the door open for revival whenever Trump wants it.

This is how authoritarian patronage works in plain sight. The office is occupied, the title is formal, and the substance is private benefit. The law becomes a shell game for one man’s liabilities and one faction’s reward structure.

Misdirection as Method

The article also shows how Republican power operates by making deliberate cruelty look like administrative ambiguity. Blanche’s treatment of Epstein survivors is not a misunderstanding. It is contempt with a brief meeting attached. The transfer of Ghislaine Maxwell to a country club lockup is not an accident of bureaucracy. It is a choice that tells victims exactly how little their harm matters once it collides with power.

The same pattern runs through the effort to keep the January 6 payoff apparatus alive. These are not “concerns” about justice. They are plans to compensate men who attacked the Capitol, then use them as intimidation muscle. The language of bureaucracy is being used to conceal a political protection racket.

The Violence Stack

What the article describes is not a single scheme but a stack of contingencies: courts as fallback, Blanche as enabler, pardoned rioters as enforcement, ICE as intimidation, and even the possibility of electronic manipulation through Musk’s satellites. Whether every one of those fears materializes is a separate question. The structure matters either way.

Trump appears less interested in winning cleanly than in retaining enough coercive capacity to avoid losing meaningfully. That is why the article’s line about ICE matters. ICE should be abolished, not “reformed” into gentleness. Any outfit that can be folded into a strategy of domestic intimidation is not a neutral enforcement agency. It is an authoritarian instrument waiting for orders.

The Democratic Problem Is Not Complacency

The source is strongest when it sees that Trump is not behaving like a candidate desperate to persuade. He is behaving like a ruler who expects one of several force-backed exits to work. But the article also reveals a Democratic obligation that cannot be outsourced to mood or messaging: the only thing standing between this kind of governance and actual capture is institutional resistance backed by electoral force.

That means the answer is not a faith-based appeal to norms. It is opposition organized around consequences: blocking the grift, protecting voting power, and refusing to let Democrats drift into managerial passivity while Republicans build a coercive state around a single family’s impunity.

The Pattern Beneath the Person

Todd Blanche is not the anomaly. He is the model. The point of this appointment is to prove that loyalty can be purchased, law can be bent, victims can be dismissed, and the machinery of state can be repurposed into an instrument of personal protection. That is the governing pattern: corruption made procedural, cruelty made administrative, and authoritarianism made to look like a paperwork problem.
