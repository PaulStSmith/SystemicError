The Supreme Court's increasing use of the so-called "shadow docket" to issue quickie rulings mostly in Donald Trump's favor was on the receiving end of a smack-down on MSNBC on Sunday morning.

With legal expert Melissa Murray explaining the maneuver is normally something that is used for case management, Slate legal analyst Dahlia Lithwick noted that the spare rulings, shorn of detailed legal explanations and citations, is currently giving Trump free rein to do as he pleases -- without justification or reasoning..

"Maybe a good way to think about it is through the lens of this legitimacy question, this court's legitimacy question that we've been talking about for years now, and the sort of plummeting public trust in the court," she began. "Well, what is the single most essential thing that a judicial body can do to instill in the public the sense that this is a lawyerly, sober fact-backed business."

Our Analysis:

The Shadow Docket: A Cloak of Secrecy Around the Supreme Court

In what can only be described as a judicial travesty, the Supreme Court's increasing reliance on the so-called "shadow docket" has become a tool that, quite frankly, seems tailor-made for the whims of Donald Trump. This mechanism, as highlighted in a recent MSNBC segment with legal experts Melissa Murray and Dahlia Lithick, reveals a disturbing trend: the erosion of judicial transparency and accountability.

The shadow docket, for those uninitiated in the arcane workings of the Supreme Court, is supposed to be about case management. Yet, as Lithwick points out, it has morphed into a secretive fast lane that allows for decisions shorn of any detailed legal explanations or citations. In essence, it's a judicial hit-and-run, leaving the public and the lower courts scrambling in the aftermath.

Legitimacy at Stake

The crux of the issue, as Lithwick aptly notes, is the legitimacy of the Court itself. With public trust in this institution plummeting, one would think the Court would be bending over backwards to demonstrate its sober, reasoned approach to justice. Instead, what we have is a Court that appears to be playing fast and loose with the very principles it's supposed to uphold.

The shadow docket's lack of transparency is antithetical to the purpose of a supreme judicial body. Opinions, the bread and butter of legal precedent, are reduced to mere afterthoughts. This not only undermines the Court's credibility but also leaves lower courts without a compass, guessing how to interpret these top-level decisions.

Slot Machine Justice

Lithwick's characterization of the Court's actions as "slot machine justice" is both scathing and accurate. There's a sense of randomness, a lack of deliberation that feels wholly unbecoming of the highest court in the land. The fact that some decisions come with little to no explanation, and at times, without even disclosing how each justice voted, is a mockery of the judicial process. It's as if the Court has adopted a "don't ask, don't tell" policy that serves no one, least of all the public it's supposed to serve.

The Trump Factor

The shadow docket isn't just a procedural quirk; it's a political weapon. The article makes it clear that Donald Trump has been a primary beneficiary of this opaque process. This raises alarming questions about the impartiality of the Court and the extent to which it's willing to be perceived as an enabler of Trump's agenda. The Supreme Court, through its actions, seems to be granting Trump "free rein" to operate without the usual checks and balances that detailed legal scrutiny provides.

Conclusion

The use of the shadow docket by the Supreme Court is a systemic failure that undermines the very foundation of judicial accountability and transparency. It transforms the Court into an entity that appears more interested in expedience than in justice. This trend is dangerous, not just for the immediate legal outcomes it produces, but for the long-term impact on the Court's legitimacy and the public's trust in this vital institution.

In the end, the Supreme Court must remember that it's not just the decisions it makes that matter, but how those decisions are made. Transparency, detailed reasoning, and accountability are not optional accessories; they are the bedrock of a credible judiciary. Anything less is a disservice to the American public and a blow to the rule of law.
