According to former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade, the courts are beginning to lose patience with Donald Trump and members of his administration and a reckoning is coming.

During an appearance on MSNBC with host Ali Velshi, McQuade was asked about the legal setbacks Trump's DOJ has suffered this past week which included a late-night ruling from the Supreme Court halting shipping more immigrants out of the U.S.

As she quipped to the host, "If they're dancing, Ali, they're dancing with two left feet."

Our Analysis:

The Courts' Waning Patience with Trump: A Legal Reckoning Afoot?

In a recent appearance on MSNBC, former U.S. Attorney Barbara McQuade delivered a biting critique of Donald Trump's Department of Justice (DOJ) and its apparent legal floundering, suggesting a growing impatience within the judiciary towards the former administration. McQuade's observations, particularly regarding a Supreme Court ruling that put a halt to the expedited deportation of immigrants, underscore a broader legal struggle faced by Trump and his cohorts. The legal landscape appears increasingly hostile to the former president's legal maneuverings, signaling a potential reckoning on the horizon.

The Dance of Incompetence

McQuade's metaphor of the DOJ "dancing with two left feet" aptly characterizes the clumsy and often bewildering legal strategies employed by the Trump administration. It's a vivid illustration of a team out of sync with the basic principles of law and governance, stumbling over its own arguments and facing setback after setback in the courts. This imagery of ineptitude and disarray is not just a casual observation but a scathing indictment of a Justice Department that seems to have lost its way.

Legal Absurdities and Constitutional Contortions

One of the most "outrageous" arguments, as McQuade points out, involved the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia. The Trump administration's interpretation of a court order—to "facilitate" Garcia's release from an El Salvador prison as merely allowing him entry should he somehow reach the U.S. border—borders on the absurd. This argument not only misreads the court's directive but also showcases a flagrant disregard for constitutional rights, particularly the right to due process. McQuade's critique here highlights a troubling trend within the Trump DOJ: a willingness to bend and twist legal interpretations to the point of breaking, all in service of a broader agenda that often runs counter to the very fabric of constitutional governance.

A Predicted Reckoning

McQuade's analysis suggests that the judiciary's patience is not infinite. There is a sense that accountability is on the horizon—that the Trump administration's legal contortions and constitutional dismissals will eventually meet a reckoning. The critical question, as McQuade poses, is not if but when this reckoning will come, and what form it will take. Will the courts merely scold and reprimand, or will there be tangible consequences for these legal transgressions?

Conclusion: A Dance of Desperation

The portrait McQuade paints is one of desperation—a former administration and its legal teams grasping at straws, employing every conceivable tactic to delay and deflect, even as the walls of legal accountability close in. This dance of desperation, characterized by both incompetence and audacity, may well be reaching its final act. As the judiciary grows increasingly impatient with Trump's legal maneuvers, the looming question is not just about the immediate legal battles at hand, but about the broader implications for justice and accountability in the wake of an administration that often seemed to place itself above the law.
