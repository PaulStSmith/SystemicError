MAGA outcast is tearing apart Trump's base — and she shows no signs of stopping: analysis

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 5, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unstoppable Candace Owens: A Frankenstein’s Monster of Right-Wing Media

Candace Owens speaking at the 2018 Young Women’s Leadership Summit, hosted by Turning Point USA at the Hyatt Regency DFW Hotel in Dallas, Texas, Gage Skidmore

Former MAGA figure Candace Owens has come under fire for conspiracy theories, yet carries on relentlessly even as President Donald Trump‘s base shows signs of fracturing, an analyst said Wednesday.

Salon‘s Sophia Tesfaye described how right-wing media can’t stop Owens, who has continued to tout claims about Charlie Kirk‘s death and his widow Erika Kirk, most recently in “Bride of Charlie.” Owens’ YouTube series focuses on her thoughts about Erika Kirk’s “lies” and has suggested that she was involved in her husband’s murder or “ulterior motives” to take over Turning Point USA, the organization her husband led.

“The conservative media machine is currently discovering, to its evident horror, that it has no idea how to shut one of its biggest creations,” Tesfaye wrote. “Candace Owens was handed a megaphone by the late Charlie Kirk, feted by Donald Trump and praised by Focus on the Family as one of ‘the many Black giants of the conservative movement.’ Now she is being called a vampire and a schizophrenic by the very people who built her platform. And she keeps growing.”

“But the real reason right-wing media cannot stop Candace Owens is that they built her,” Tesfaye wrote. “And, more importantly, they built the engine that fuels her: the machinery of conspiratorial media, which is immune to the tools that might once have contained it.”

Conservative media has a history of inciting outrage and distrust to capture its audience, something Owens has also employed in her own messaging.

“The more outrageous the claim, the greater the engagement,” Tesfaye wrote. “The more distrust sowed toward institutions — universities, media, elections, public health, the FBI — the more loyal the audience becomes.”

In her personal attacks on Erika Kirk, Owens has shifted the focus not just to Charlie Kirk and his assassination, but also to the current problems facing the MAGA coalition.

“For all its focus on Erika Kirk, ‘Bride of Charlie’ is not really about her. Nor is it even really about Charlie Kirk,” Tesfaye explained. “It is about an identity crisis on the American right — what happens when a media movement decides that the thrill of the conspiracy, the pleasure of the accusation, the dopamine of the ‘truth bomb’ matters more than the actual truth. Right-wing media cannot stop Candace Owens because they cannot renounce the incentives that made her powerful.”

This has given Owens momentum — with no signs of slowing down.

“This tension is not going away,” Tesfaye added. “It mirrors broader fractures within MAGA-world over foreign policy, particularly U.S. support for Israel. It reflects a generational shift in how political influence is wielded. Perhaps most ominously, it exposes how readily antisemitic narratives can find purchase in spaces primed to distrust ‘globalists’ and ‘elites.’”

Our Analysis:

The Unstoppable Candace Owens: A Frankenstein’s Monster of Right-Wing Media

In a recent article from Salon, Sophia Tesfaye offers a compelling examination of the paradox that is Candace Owens—a figure who continues to thrive on controversy and conspiracy theories, much to the chagrin of her conservative creators. Owens, once a darling of the MAGA movement and the conservative media, has transformed into something of a rogue agent, uncontainable by the very forces that propelled her to prominence.

The Inescapable Truth: You Reap What You Sow

The conservative media landscape has long been a fertile ground for the growth of figures like Owens. By championing conspiratorial media as a tool to engage and enrage their audience, these platforms have inadvertently created a monster they can no longer control. Owens, with her relentless pursuit of controversy—most recently epitomized by her series “Bride of Charlie,” which insinuates dark theories surrounding Charlie Kirk’s death and attacks his widow, Erika Kirk—embodies the apex of this strategy gone awry.

The Mechanisms of Madness: How Right-Wing Media Cultivates Chaos

Tesfaye astutely points out that the conservative media’s strategy has always been to incite outrage and distrust. The more outrageous Owens’ claims, the more engagement she receives. This cycle of distrust towards institutions (be it media, academia, or public health) only serves to solidify her base’s loyalty, creating a self-sustaining loop of conspiracy and paranoia.

A Reflection of a Larger Crisis

However, as Tesfaye suggests, the issue at hand transcends Owens herself. The real story here is the identity crisis gripping the American right—a crisis that Owens both symbolizes and exacerbates. In her unyielding quest for attention and influence, Owens has laid bare the underlying tensions within MAGA-world, including disputes over foreign policy and a generational shift in political strategy. Perhaps most concerning is the ease with which antisemitic narratives gain traction in these circles, a testament to the dangerous power of a movement built on distrust and conspiracy.

The Unsolvable Dilemma

The conservative media’s inability to rein in Owens is emblematic of a broader problem: a movement that has prioritized sensationalism over truth, and division over unity. As Tesfaye concludes, the tensions and fractures within the MAGA coalition are not likely to dissipate anytime soon. This is not merely a reflection of Owens’ personal trajectory but a damning indictment of the entire conservative media ecosystem.

In summary, Candace Owens is not an anomaly but a natural consequence of the right-wing media’s strategy. She is the personification of a movement that has long valued the “thrill of the conspiracy” over the pursuit of truth. The fact that this movement now finds itself at odds with its own creation is a testament to the inherent dangers of building a platform on a foundation of misinformation and paranoia. The conservative media’s Frankenstein monster walks among us, and it shows no signs of slowing down.
