'Loyalty to God': Former MAGA 'tradwife' unravels her 'religious psychosis'

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosSeptember 9, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Who Held PowerThe Machinery of SubmissionWhat Broke the SpellThe Article’s Blind SpotThe Bigger Pattern

Theocracy, Domesticity, and the Lie of “Provision”

A Michigan evangelical MAGA mother tells a familiar story in a way that strips it bare: she was taught to surrender to her husband, avoid politics, distrust independent media, and trust that God would handle the bills. Then reality arrived. She had worked as a respiratory therapist, lived through the pandemic, lost her spouse in a motorcycle accident, and watched the worldview she had been sold collapse under material pressure. The source story is about one woman’s deconstruction. Politically, it is about an ecosystem that trained her to abandon agency and call that virtue.

Who Held Power

The power in this story was never with her.

It sat with the husband whose “leadership” she was told to submit to. It sat with the pastor and the church culture that taught political ignorance as obedience. It sat with the spiritual authorities who told her that media outside their bubble was “demonic.” And it sat with a broader evangelical-MAGA world that turned dependency into doctrine and called it faith.

That is the structure: men, pastors, and political operators setting the terms while women are instructed to confuse passivity with holiness.

The Machinery of Submission

The tradwife fantasy is not a lifestyle trend. It is political conditioning with a softer interface.

The source shows the sequence clearly: church upbringing, religious intensification during postpartum depression, ideological withdrawal from politics, and totalized submission to husband and church. That is not accidental drift. It is a pipeline. First, remove independent judgment. Then, recast financial insecurity as a test of obedience. Then, make external information suspect. Once a person is isolated from politics and material reality, control becomes easy to rationalize.

This is how authoritarian culture works in domestic form. It does not need jackboots when it can use theology.

What Broke the Spell

The break came from material reality, not rhetorical persuasion.

She had training and experience as a respiratory therapist. The pandemic made the gap impossible to ignore: her professional knowledge was in direct conflict with what the president and church leaders were saying. Then her spouse died, and the church response reportedly included a grotesque “blessing in disguise” line from the pastor’s wife. That is the moral bankruptcy at the center of this world: when real suffering appears, the response is not compassion but ideological bookkeeping.

This is not merely hypocrisy. It is cruelty disguised as spiritual interpretation.

The Article’s Blind Spot

The source story wants to present a personal awakening, and that part matters. But it still risks softening the larger indictment.

The problem was never that she was “deep in religious psychosis” in some abstract vacuum. The problem was the system that cultivated dependence, rewarded submission, and treated political ignorance as a feminine virtue. The article correctly notes her former beliefs, but the larger story is not about one woman’s confusion. It is about a movement that profits from turning women into captive audiences for male authority and right-wing propaganda.

The blame belongs where power actually sits: in institutions that exploit fear, grief, and economic stress to keep people docile.

The Bigger Pattern

This is what authoritarian politics looks like when it enters the home, the church, and the body.

It does not always arrive as open state violence. Sometimes it arrives as “traditional” femininity, anti-politics piety, and promises that God will sort out the rent. Sometimes it arrives through men who want obedience, pastors who want deference, and political movements that want voters who do not ask questions. The result is the same: diminished agency, distorted reality, and a population easier to command.

The lesson here is not that one woman got lost. It is that a whole political culture was built to make losing yourself feel like righteousness.
