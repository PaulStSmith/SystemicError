Trump and Starmer responded to British press questions about the relationship between the two countries, including the AI and technology partnership, Russia's attacks and the ongoing war in Ukraine, war and genocide in Gaza, tariffs and oil prices, Bagram Air Base in Afghanistan and the ousted former Ambassador Peter Mandelson — who Trump claims he doesn't know despite a photo showing them chatting in the Oval Office several months ago. He also said Jimmy Kimmel was fired over ratings.

Users on X and Bluesky responded to the Trump's remarks and Starmer's responses.

"Starmer has pulled out all the stops to flatter and impress Trump, but still hasn’t managed to get him to drop his damaging tariffs, take on Putin or tell Netanyahu to stop waging genocide. It’s time for the UK to stand tough with allies who share our values," Leader of the Liberal Democrats, Ed Davey, wrote on X.

"I'm not sure that reporting on Trump in the old-fashioned way makes any sense. You need to really prefix everything with, 'In a rambling, off-the-cuff speech that he may well contradict tomorrow...'" writer Rick Burin wrote on Bluesky.

"I’d love for the UK to disassociate from Trump & the US. They’re unreliable partners; Trump, utterly morally bankrupt, always will be. For now, sadly, we need them onside: Ukraine has, since Feb 2022, been WWIII in everything but name. We can’t – yet – fight Russia (& China? India?) without the US," writer John Manning wrote via Bluesky.

"After encouragement from Starmer, the British Govt fell for the huge expense of entertaining Trump. Maybe they get a stay on tariffs but rolling out the red carpet won’t make either one of them popular in the UK. This should be Starmer’s last hurrah," a user named Carrie wrote on Bluesky.

"I am noting on President Trump’s UK visit, the most the significant conversations, beyond significant pledged investment bringing in business to engage in diplomacy and dealmaking, is the tech partnership and AI discussions between the US and UK. I am guessing this is being talked about today between President Trump and Prime Minister Keir Starmer. This was a topic of the original visit by Starmer to the US months ago and missed by press-it was my question at the time, what will this AI/tech race partnership look like? Who will engage in joint innovation and how? And how will the US and UK align on AI development freedom, ethics, free speech and privacy laws to satisfy the goal of joint innovation?" journalist Kate Jones asked via X.

"BBG: Trump says Jimmy Kimmel was fired for 'lack of talent' and bad ratings - 'not free speech' at the UK presser that just wrapped. This is bulls---- on so many levels I don't know where to begin..." another user wrote on Bluesky.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Theater of the Absurd: Trump and Starmer’s Joint Press Conference

The joint press conference between President Donald Trump and British Prime Minister Keir Starmer has unleashed a cacophony of reactions that range from bewildered to downright incredulous. The event, which ostensibly aimed to address key issues between the two nations, instead veered into the realm of the surreal, touching on everything from AI partnerships to alleged war and genocide, with a sprinkling of celebrity gossip for good measure.

The Charade of Diplomacy

At first glance, one might mistake this for a robust dialogue between allies. However, a closer inspection reveals an exercise in futility and flattery, where substantial policy discussions were overshadowed by Trump's characteristic bravado and evasion. The mention of topics such as Russia's aggression, the war in Ukraine, and the genocide in Gaza should prompt serious discourse. Instead, they were reduced to bullet points in Trump’s global sideshow.

Tariffs, Tech, and Trump’s Amnesia

The discussions around tariffs and the AI and technology partnership could have been the silver lining. Yet, they were clouded by Trump's claim of not knowing Peter Mandelson, a statement as believable as his assertion that Jimmy Kimmel was fired over ratings – a distraction so blatantly false it borders on comedy.

The Liberal Democrats’ leader, Ed Davey, encapsulated the frustration many feel, criticizing Starmer for failing to sway Trump on any significant issue. It’s a poignant reminder of the ineffectiveness of appeasement and the need for a firmer stance on the global stage.

Public Perception and the Press

The public's reaction, as seen through comments on X and Bluesky, ranges from disenchantment to outright condemnation. Rick Burin’s suggestion to preface reports on Trump with a disclaimer about his inconsistency is a sad commentary on the state of political discourse. Meanwhile, John Manning’s lamentation on the necessity of the U.S. as an ally, despite its unreliable leadership, speaks volumes about the desperation felt by those watching global conflicts unfold.

Carrie’s comment on the “huge expense of entertaining Trump” raises an important point about the cost of these diplomatic endeavors, both financially and in terms of political capital. The question remains: what tangible benefits, if any, will come from this visit?

The Real Issues at Hand

Journalist Kate Jones touches on perhaps the most critical aspect of this entire spectacle – the AI/tech race partnership. Her inquiry into the nature of this partnership, and how it aligns with broader issues of freedom, ethics, free speech, and privacy laws, is the kind of substantive discussion that should be at the forefront of these diplomatic exchanges. Yet, it seems almost an afterthought in the circus that unfolded.

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity

This press conference could have been a moment for substantive dialogue on pressing global issues. Instead, what we witnessed was a parade of missed opportunities, overshadowed by Trump’s theatrics and Starmer’s inability to pivot the conversation to more meaningful ground. The spectacle served as a stark reminder of the challenges facing global diplomacy today, where style too often trumps substance, leaving the audience wondering if the joke is on us.
