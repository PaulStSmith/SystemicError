Trump just changed how federal money is handled — and it’s not normal

Based on an article by Thom Hartmann

Trump’s Loyalty Budget

Federal money as a partisan weapon

Trump is not “mismanaging” federal spending; he is using it as a loyalty test. The source story centers on a basic fact the political press keeps trying to blur: the administration cancelled billions in green energy grants, and the Justice Department filing says that, with one exception, the terminated grants were in states that voted for Kamala Harris. That is not policy drift. That is state power being sorted by political obedience.

Who actually decided

The decision sits where power always sits: in the White House, in the Department of Energy, and in a Republican governing coalition that treats public money as a partisan prize. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Congress there was no political bias. The department then admitted in court that the grant cancellations were based solely on whether the recipient’s state was politically blue. That is the part that matters. The lie came before the confession, which is how modern authoritarianism often works: deny first, document later.

The frame is the corruption

The source is right to call this corruption, but the deeper point is that the corruption is the governing logic. Trump is also reportedly steering FEMA disaster funds by vote choice and blocking Medicaid payments to blue states while pardoning convicted healthcare fraudsters. That is not contradiction. It is the same method in different uniforms: punish disfavored constituencies, reward the loyal, and call it administration. The message is blunt enough that even the administration’s own legal filings cannot hide it.

Democrats are not the problem here

The article correctly notes the asymmetry, even if it could say it more cleanly. Democrats are not doing the equivalent. The Democratic Party is described as broke, while Republicans are flush with oligarch money and Trump’s SuperPAC is stuffed with more. That imbalance is not a moral accident. It reflects a party system in which one side still expects to win by governing and the other expects to win by coercion, cash, and procedural sabotage. If Democrats are weak, that is a strategic failure inside a democracy coalition. It is not the same thing as turning the federal government into a partisan extortion racket.

State-level sabotage

The same logic is spreading through Republican-run states. Oklahoma, South Dakota, Florida, and Missouri are all using supposedly technical election rules, petition barriers, and initiative sabotage to make popular power harder to exercise. The pattern is obvious: when voters choose the wrong thing, the ruling side changes the rules. When voters organize anyway, the ruling side raises the cost, narrows the gate, or rewrites the district map. This is not incidental governance. It is anti-democratic hardening.

The real warning

The larger pattern is regional punishment dressed up as administration. That is how governments teach people that citizenship is conditional. History does not need to be repeated here to recognize the outline: when one faction uses state resources to privilege its own territory and punish its opponents, resentment becomes structural, not rhetorical. The United States is not at risk because people disagree. It is at risk because one party is turning disagreement into material deprivation and calling that normal government.
