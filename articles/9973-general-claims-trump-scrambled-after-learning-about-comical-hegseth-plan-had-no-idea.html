General claims Trump scrambled after learning about 'comical' Hegseth plan: 'Had no idea'

A retired U.S. Army general questioned the motivation for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth ordering military commanders from their posts around the globe to a lecture in Virginia.

The Pentagon chief is calling hundreds of senior military leaders to a Marine Corps base in Quantico for a pep talk about the warrior ethos, along with President Donald Trump. And retired general Barry McCaffrey told MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” the plan was ill-conceived at best.

“Look, you know, this will be a very respectful audience tomorrow,” McCaffrey said. “Our culture, our political culture in the country says there’s two civilians, only the president and the secretary of defense, who give orders to the armed forces.

“I think Secretary Hegseth had no idea what he was doing calling a no-notice meeting without specifying what the topic would be. I think he did want to harangue the senior enlisted and military leaders of the country about warrior culture.”

“It’s a hard one to pull off,” he added. “These people, as you say, grew up in 20 years of combat, from lieutenant or E-1 to the end of their career they’re now serving, and then I think President Trump had no idea what was going on, learned of it, horns in. This ought to be a very unusual, almost comical session. Why did he draw attention to this, as if we were going to go to war over what, Greenland or something? So it’s a real mess.”

Hegseth should not expect a rousing reception from the military brass, McCaffrey said.

“A lot of civilians don’t understand a military audience doesn’t applaud or take enthusiastic recognition of a speech by a senior officer,” McCaffrey said. “I have a feeling they’re going to pass a word it had better look like that on TV on this one. Look, this is all such an unusual situation. The day the American people make a final determination on an elected president, the military completely, 100 percent switches over to being respectful and supportive under the law with this new administration.”

“By the way, they all come in nervous about these generals with all their ribbons, and are they going to pay any attention to us?” McCaffrey added. “Normally, in about three to six months, they realize, no, these people actually know what they’re doing and they are going to be compliant with our policy directives. This administration has been chaotic. Now, Secretary Hegseth actually has a combat infantry badge, served in Iraq and Afghanistan, and Princeton grad, there’s every indication that he could have been very successful, but so far it looks shallow, sort of chaotic. It’s a drama going on constantly.”

Our Analysis:

The Hegseth Pep Talk: A Misguided Attempt at Warrior Ethos or Political Theater?

In an extraordinary and bewildering move, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has summoned military commanders from around the globe for what can only be described as a pep talk at a Marine Corps base in Quantico, Virginia. The event, which also features the presence of President Donald Trump, ostensibly aims to bolster the warrior ethos among hundreds of senior military leaders. However, the motivations and implications of this gathering raise more questions than they answer, painting a picture of confusion and potential misdirection at the highest levels of military leadership.

A Questionable Call to Arms

Retired General Barry McCaffrey’s commentary to MSNBC’s “Morning Joe” cuts to the heart of the issue. McCaffrey questions the utility and wisdom of such a sudden and vaguely defined assembly, particularly under the guise of reinforcing a warrior ethos. This characterization is peculiar, if not outright absurd, considering the decades of combat experience embodied by the attendees. These are individuals who have not only lived the warrior ethos but have also led others through the harrowing realities of modern warfare. To suggest they require a refresher on the concept is not only patronizing but also displays a profound misunderstanding of military culture and professionalism.

The Spectacle of Chaos

McCaffrey’s insights further reveal the chaotic underpinnings of the current administration’s approach to military leadership. The spectacle of summoning seasoned commanders for what appears to be a hastily arranged photo-op does little to instill confidence in the strategic direction of the Pentagon. Moreover, the involvement of President Trump, seemingly as an afterthought, adds an element of political theater to an event that, under any other administration, might have been considered a farcical anomaly.

The Warrior Ethos Redefined?

The concept of the warrior ethos is steeped in values such as courage, integrity, and loyalty. It is a set of principles that guide soldiers in battle and in peace, shaping their actions and decisions. The decision to use this ethos as the centerpiece for a gathering of this nature not only trivializes these values but also risks politicizing them. The implication that this ethos needs reinforcement from the top down suggests a disconnection from the realities of military service and leadership.

A Misguided Attempt at Leadership

It is clear from McCaffrey’s observations that the military brass is unlikely to be swayed by grandstanding. The military’s culture of respect and discipline is often misunderstood by civilians, and the expectation of enthusiastic applause for political speeches is misplaced at best. This misunderstanding underscores a broader issue within the administration: a lack of appreciation for the professionalism and expertise of the military leadership. These are not individuals who respond to whimsical displays of authority but rather to thoughtful, informed strategy and leadership.

Conclusion: Drama Over Direction

In his commentary, McCaffrey points out that despite Hegseth’s commendable military service and academic credentials, his leadership appears “shallow” and “chaotic.” This situation exemplifies the ongoing drama that seems to engulf this administration, overshadowing substantive policy and strategic direction. The decision to convene this meeting, under the circumstances described, does not bode well for the administration’s understanding or respect for military leadership and ethos. It seems, then, that this event is less about enhancing the warrior ethos and more about the spectacle of power—a spectacle that is unlikely to fool or impress the seasoned military leaders it seeks to inspire.
