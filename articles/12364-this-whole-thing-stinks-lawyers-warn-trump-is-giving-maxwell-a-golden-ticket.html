Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche has met with Maxwell and her lawyer over the last couple of days. The meetings have led some to speculate that Trump may be dangling a pardon for Maxwell in exchange for favorable testimony from her. The president seemingly confirmed those rumors on Friday when he said he is allowed to pardon her.

This setup has set off alarm bells for lawyers like Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA), who worked as an assistant U.S. attorney for six years, and Ben Meiselas, who practices criminal law at the California-based law firm Geragos and Geragos.

"Clearly, this errand that Todd Blanche, the number-two at the Justice Department, is going on now is in his capacity as Trump's defense attorney," Schiff said in a recent interview. "He wants to know what this convicted child sex trafficker has to say about Donald Trump. And, certainly, she wants a pardon."

"This whole thing stinks to high Heaven," Schiff continued.

Meiselas added that the Trump administration's explanation for reaching out to Maxwell also lacks credibility. Trump has claimed that his administration is simply reaching out to Maxwell to make contact. However, the Trump administration successfully prosecuted Maxwell in 2019, a prosecution that the since-fired prosecutor Maurene Comey led.

Comey is the daughter of former F.B.I. Director James Comey, whom Trump has sought to portray as someone who created the "hoax" surrounding Jeffrey Epstein and Maxwell, Meiselas added.

"[Trump] is kind of giving Ghislaine a golden ticket for a retrial," Meiselas said.

Our Analysis:

The Murky Waters of Justice and Political Power

The recent revelations about President Donald Trump's Justice Department orchestrating multiple meetings with convicted sex trafficker Ghislaine Maxwell have cast a long, dark shadow over the integrity of the American justice system. The involvement of Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, who reportedly met with Maxwell and her lawyer, raises serious questions about the motives behind these meetings. But when we dig into the details and the implications, the stench of corruption becomes unmistakable.

The Pardon Carrot

At the heart of this matter is the insinuation that Trump might be leveraging the power of presidential pardon as a bargaining chip for favorable testimony from Maxwell. This is not just speculation; Trump's own comments have fanned the flames of these rumors. The idea that a president could use his pardoning power in such a transactional manner is not only morally repugnant but a grotesque abuse of power. It's a manipulation of the justice system for personal gain, a scenario that would make even the most cynical political observers blanch.

The Voices of Alarm

The alarm bells are not being rung without cause. Figures like Sen. Adam Schiff, with his background as an assistant U.S. attorney, and Ben Meiselas, a practicing criminal lawyer, have voiced their concerns loudly and clearly. Schiff's assertion that Todd Blanche is acting more as Trump's defense attorney than as the Deputy Attorney General is a damning indictment of how justice is being administered. The phrase "this whole thing stinks to high Heaven" might as well be the understatement of the century.

A Credibility Chasm

The Trump administration's explanation for these meetings—that they were merely reaching out to Maxwell—does not pass the smell test. Given that Maxwell was successfully prosecuted under this administration, the notion that they're just now looking to "make contact" is laughably thin. The undercurrents of this narrative, with the dismissal of prosecutor Maurene Comey and Trump's vendetta against her father, James Comey, add layers of intrigue and potential obstruction.

A Golden Ticket for a Retrial?

What Meiselas suggests, that Trump is offering Maxwell a "golden ticket for a retrial," is a chilling prospect. It implies a willingness to undermine the judicial process and potentially allow a convicted sex trafficker to escape justice, all for political protection. This is not just an affront to the victims of Maxwell and her associate Jeffrey Epstein; it's a slap in the face to the very concept of justice.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error

This episode is yet another example of the systemic error at the heart of the current administration's approach to justice and governance. The manipulation of the justice system, the abuse of presidential power, and the complete disregard for moral and ethical standards are hallmarks of a regime that views power as an end in itself. It's a stark reminder that the battle for the soul of American democracy is ongoing and that the forces of corruption and cronyism are always lurking, ready to undermine the principles upon which the country was founded. The question remains: will the American public and its institutions stand firm against such blatant abuses, or will they be eroded by the corrosive influence of power unchecked?
