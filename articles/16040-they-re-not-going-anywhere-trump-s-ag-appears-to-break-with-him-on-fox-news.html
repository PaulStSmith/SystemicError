'They're not going anywhere': Trump's AG appears to break with him on Fox News

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 17, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Sparky's Analysis:Trump's Bizarre Prison Proposal: A Deep Dive

FILE PHOTO: U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi speaks as she announces an immigration enforcement action during her first press conference at the Justice Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 12, 2025. REUTERS/Craig Hudson/File Photo

President Donald Trump's attorney general appeared to rebuff an opportunity to endorse her boss's recent idea to send Americans convicted of violent crimes to prisons in El Salvador.

Trump reiterated the idea on Monday during an Oval Office meeting with Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele, in which Trump told reporters, "homegrowns are next," referring to U.S. citizens.

"Homegrown criminals are next," Trump told Bukele. "I said homegrowns are next, homegrowns are next. You've got to build about five more places. It's not big enough."

Trump clarified he was focusing on "violent offenders" and signaled that his administration is looking into the legality to determine if such deportations are possible.

The eye-popping suggestion became the topic of discussion on Fox News' "Jesse Watters Primetime." Watters asked Bondi about Trump's idea to incarcerate U.S. citizens overseas. But she didn't seem to endorse the plan.

"The president was musing about sending some of the most horrible people in this country down to that mega prison," said Watters, later asking, "Is that legal to do? Is that something you're allowed to do?"

"Well Jesse, these are Americans that he's saying committed the most heinous crimes in our country. Crime is going to decrease dramatically because he has given us a directive to make America safe again. These people need to be locked up as long as they can, as long as the law allows. We're not going to let them go anywhere. If we have to build more prisons in our country, we will do it."

"Right," Watters replied with a chuckle. "That's what I thought."

Sparky's Analysis:

Trump's Bizarre Prison Proposal: A Deep Dive

In a recent spectacle of policy improvisation, President Donald Trump floated an idea that veers into the territory of the absurd, suggesting the deportation of American citizens convicted of violent crimes to prisons in El Salvador. This proposal, aired during an Oval Office meeting with Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele, is not just bizarre; it is a glaring testament to the administration's floundering grasp on legal and ethical standards.

Trump's Dangerous Musings

Trump's comments, "homegrowns are next," not only exemplify a disturbing disregard for constitutional rights but also hint at a deeper, more insidious attempt to outsource the American penal system. The notion of sending "homegrown criminals" to El Salvador to expand incarceration facilities abroad is so outlandish that it borders on dystopian fiction.

Legal and Ethical Quagmire

The legal implications of such a proposal are monumental. The suggestion to deport U.S. citizens for imprisonment overseas blatantly disregards fundamental rights guaranteed by the Constitution. It's a slippery slope when a leader proposes exiling its citizens as a solution to domestic crime, evoking historical precedents that are, without exception, universally condemned.

Attorney General's Lukewarm Response

The response from Trump's attorney general, rather than offering a firm rebuke, meanders into tacit acceptance, framing this draconian idea as a crime reduction strategy. This response is not only legally dubious but morally bankrupt. It showcases a startling willingness to entertain authoritarian impulses under the guise of making "America safe again."

Fox News: A Platform for Autocratic Fantasies

The discussion on Fox News' "Jesse Watters Primetime" further exemplifies the media's role in normalizing radical and legally questionable proposals. The chuckles shared between Watters and the attorney general as they contemplate the logistics of such a policy highlight a jarring disconnect from the gravity of the proposition.

Conclusion: A Defiant Stand Against Reality

Trump's proposal to deport American convicts to El Salvador is more than just a policy suggestion; it is a litmus test for the nation's commitment to its foundational principles. The lack of immediate and widespread condemnation from within his administration and among his supporters is alarming.

This episode should serve as a wake-up call. It's a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance in preserving the rule of law and human rights against the whims of an administration seemingly untethered from reality and legal constraints. The idea of banishing citizens is not only impractical but profoundly un-American, highlighting a systemic error in the current political discourse that must be rectified.
