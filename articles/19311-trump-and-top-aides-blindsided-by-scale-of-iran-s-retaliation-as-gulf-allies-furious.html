Trump and top aides blindsided by scale of Iran's retaliation as Gulf allies 'furious'

President Donald Trump and some of his closest advisers were caught off guard by the scope of Iran’s military response to U.S. strikes, while key Gulf allies have privately expressed anger at the White House’s decision to escalate the conflict, according to a Wall Street Journal report.

The publication reported Friday that “the president and some aides were surprised at the breadth and scope of Iran’s retaliation,” which included missile and drone launches targeting regional countries from Azerbaijan to Oman, according to people familiar with the matter.”

The response has raised fears of a wider regional conflict and complicated the administration’s efforts to frame the operation as a success, the Journal said. Trump has publicly suggested the mission was largely accomplished, telling supporters this week: “We’ve won.”

Gulf allies have reacted sharply behind closed doors.

“Allies in the Gulf are privately furious with the U.S., according to diplomats and others familiar with the matter,” the Journal reported Friday. “They blame the Trump administration for triggering a war that put them in the crosshairs and pierced their image of a luxurious, business-friendly locale free of the region’s chaos.”

Trump’s war in Iran has also left Americans in the region vulnerable. The State Department urged citizens to depart or shelter in place only after fighting had intensified, with commercial airspace already shutting down in parts of the Middle East.

Analysts say Trump now faces difficult choices between seeking an exit or risking deeper instability, according to the Journal.

“U.S. officials, however, say privately there are no plans to withdraw forces - more Marines and warships are headed to the region - and some expected the fighting to last weeks or longer,” the report said.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Wall Street Journal Report on Trump’s Response to Iran’s Military Actions

The Wall Street Journal’s report sheds light on a critical escalation in the Iran-U.S. conflict under President Donald Trump’s administration. This analysis aims to dissect the narrative, identify the decision-makers, evaluate the distribution of blame, and assess the framing of the actors involved.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

  1. President Donald Trump and His Administration: As the Commander-in-Chief and head of the executive branch, President Trump wields significant institutional power in matters of foreign policy and military engagement. The decision to escalate the conflict with Iran, therefore, falls squarely within the purview of the presidency and his closest advisers.

  2. U.S. Military and State Department: These entities carry out the decisions made by the President and his administration. While they hold institutional power in planning and execution, their actions are contingent upon the directives from the White House.

  3. Gulf Allies: Although mentioned as being privately furious, Gulf allies do not have direct institutional power over U.S. decisions. Their influence is more diplomatic and economic rather than decisory in the context of U.S. military actions.

Decision Outcomes and Responsibilities

The decision to escalate the conflict, as reported, surprised President Trump and some of his aides in terms of Iran’s military response’s breadth and scope. This indicates a potential underestimation of Iran’s capabilities or a misjudgment in strategic planning. The decision-making here can be attributed to:

  • President Trump and Close Advisers: Given their surprise at Iran’s retaliation, it reflects a gap in strategic foresight and understanding, laying the responsibility for the escalation and its consequences firmly at their feet.

  • Intelligence and Military Strategy Teams: If these teams provided assessments that failed to accurately predict Iran’s response, there is shared responsibility. However, the ultimate decision rested with the President and his advisers, emphasizing their primary accountability.

Framing and Misdirection

  • The article suggests a dichotomy between the administration’s public portrayal of the operation as a success and the private realities and concerns—both within the U.S. and among Gulf allies. This discrepancy highlights an attempt to frame the narrative around victory, potentially misdirecting the public from the operation’s ramifications and the risks of further escalation.

  • The reaction of Gulf allies, while significant, is framed in a way that might shift some perceived responsibility onto them for the consequences of the U.S.’s decisions. However, it’s clear they had limited control over the U.S.’s actions.

  • The timing of the State Department’s advisory for Americans in the region is presented as a reactive measure, further complicating the administration’s management and framing of the crisis.

Conclusion

The Wall Street Journal report, when dissected, points toward a significant underestimation by President Trump and his advisers of Iran’s military capabilities and the broader consequences of their decision to escalate. The responsibility for the decision, its execution, and the aftermath rests primarily with the U.S. administration, despite attempts to frame the situation as a success or shift partial blame to external factors such as the reactions of Gulf allies.

The narrative around “winning” and success masks a complex reality of miscalculation, strategic misjudgment, and the potential for deepened regional instability. This analysis rejects any scapegoating or misdirected blame and emphasizes the crucial role of accurate intelligence, strategic foresight, and careful consideration of geopolitical dynamics in decision-making at the highest levels of government.
