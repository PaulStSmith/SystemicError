Top Trump adviser breaks with boss over 'rigged' job numbers accusation

U.S. President Donald Trump gestures before boarding Air Force One as he returns to Washington, D.C., in Lossiemouth, Scotland, Britain, July 29, 2025. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein

As controversy mounts over President Donald Trump’s indiscriminate, unilateral firing of the Commissioner of Labor Statistics on Friday, his top economic adviser, National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett—one of the administration’s most visible defenders—appeared to break with the President’s justification for the firing of Dr. Erika McEntarfer.

After being reminded that the markets believed the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ substantial downward revisions of numbers for April and May, wiping out a huge portion of any jobs gains, Hassett was asked: “Do you think we are starting to see a real slowdown in the jobs market?”

Rather than support Trump’s claim that “the numbers were phony,” “wrong,” and “rigged,” Hassett told CNBC, “Yeah, I think that the jobs numbers were slower than we expected.”

And then he explained the process, and why BLS does revisions.

“I think that, like, one of the explanations for revisions is they have more complete data, and so I think it is likely that the revisions are a better read of the data if they’re not being manipulated, and so, yeah, I would say that it’s a little bit weaker,” he acknowledged before promoting President Trump’s “Big Beautiful Bill” that guts Medicaid, SNAP, and cuts Medicare.

But he doubled down, declaring that while he believes “there are a lot of really good reasons to be super optimistic about the second half of the year,” he said, “absolutely that jobs number, if the revision turns out to be true, does suggest that there’s less momentum than we thought.”

Our Analysis:

Trump Administration's Economic Narrative: A Tangled Web of Contradiction

In an era where political theater often overshadows the sobering realities of governance, the recent controversy surrounding President Donald Trump's abrupt dismissal of the Commissioner of Labor Statistics takes the stage as the latest act in a drama rife with contradiction and political maneuvering. At the heart of this latest episode is a stark divergence between Trump's brazen claims and the more measured, albeit convoluted, acknowledgment of economic realities by his top economic adviser, Kevin Hassett.

The Unilateral Firing: A Blatant Act of Political Will

Trump's indiscriminate, unilateral firing of Dr. Erika McEntarfer, the Commissioner of Labor Statistics, on a seemingly whimsical pretext, is emblematic of a troubling disregard for the integrity and independence of statistical agencies. The President's justification for this abrupt dismissal, grounded in assertions that the jobs numbers for April and May were "phony," "wrong," and "rigged," not only undermines the credibility of the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) but also serves as a stark reminder of Trump's propensity to reshape reality to suit his narrative.

Hassett's Break with Trump: A Veil of Reason in a Sea of Chaos

In an unexpected twist, Kevin Hassett, the National Economic Council Director and one of the administration's most visible defenders, appeared to diverge from Trump's unfounded claims. When pressed on the issue, Hassett's response to CNBC was telling. His acknowledgment that the jobs numbers were "slower than we expected" and his subsequent explanation that revisions by the BLS often result from the acquisition of more complete data, stand in sharp contrast to Trump's baseless allegations of manipulation. Hassett's concession that "it is likely that the revisions are a better read of the data if they’re not being manipulated" not only undercuts the President's narrative but also lends a semblance of rationality to an otherwise bewildering situation.

The Juxtaposition of Economic Policy and Reality

Hassett's brief moment of clarity, however, was quickly overshadowed by his promotion of President Trump's "Big Beautiful Bill" - a legislative proposal that starkly contrasts with the economic realities facing many Americans. The bill, which proposes gutting Medicaid, SNAP, and cutting Medicare, seems at odds with the economic challenges highlighted by the revised jobs numbers. Hassett's subsequent doubling down on optimism for the second half of the year, despite acknowledging the potential validity of the revised jobs numbers, encapsulates the administration's attempt to straddle the line between acknowledging economic challenges and pushing forward with policies that could exacerbate those very challenges.

Conclusion: An Administration at Odds with Itself

This latest controversy underscores a broader theme of contradiction and confusion within the Trump administration. The President's willingness to dismiss key officials based on dubious claims, coupled with his advisers' attempts to navigate these tumultuous waters, paints a picture of an administration at odds with itself. As Hassett attempts to thread the needle between supporting the President and acknowledging economic realities, the American public is left to decipher a tangled web of political rhetoric and policy proposals that may have profound implications for the nation's economic future.

In the final analysis, the Trump administration's handling of this controversy not only raises questions about its commitment to factual accuracy and transparency but also highlights the inherent tensions in its approach to economic policy. As the drama unfolds, one can only hope for a semblance of coherence and integrity in the governance of the nation's economy.
