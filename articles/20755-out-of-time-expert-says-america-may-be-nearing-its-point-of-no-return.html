Out of time: Expert says America may be nearing its point of no return

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20263 min read

Fascism Does Not Arrive Empty-Handed

Omer Aziz’s point, as reported here, is not that Trump invented authoritarian politics. It is that Trump is a vehicle for a project already comfortable using constitutional machinery against dissidents, journalists, and political enemies. That matters because the story is not about one man’s pathology. It is about which institutions are willing to be turned.

The State Is the Point

The decisive power in this story is not the street performer waving Nazi symbols. It is the executive apparatus, prosecutors, police, and state governments that can be pointed at opponents and made to look legal while doing political work. Aziz’s warning about the “weaponization” of government and the prosecution of political enemies is the core of the matter: once the state starts treating dissent as a target, the paper rules of democracy become camouflage.

That is the actual danger. Not spectacle. Not shock rhetoric. The danger is bureaucratic violence dressed up as procedure.

Trump Is the Face, Not the Whole Machine

Aziz describes Trump as an opportunist, someone who “cosplay[s] as a fascist” while borrowing the language, aesthetics, and impulses of the far right. That distinction matters. It keeps the analysis from becoming a cult of personality in reverse, where every institutional abuse is reduced to Trump’s ego alone.

Trump is not the entire explanation. He is the loudest symptom of a broader political ecosystem that rewards cruelty, normalizes retaliation, and treats state power as a partisan weapon. Republicans dismiss this as derangement because their strategy depends on making intentional abuse sound like emotional excess. That is misdirection, not rebuttal.

The Crackdown Is the Reveal

The reported declaration of “antifa” as a terrorist group while conservative states “throw the book at peaceful protesters” tells you what the project actually is. It is not order. It is selective punishment. It is an attempt to define resistance as criminal while shielding official violence from the same scrutiny.

Aziz is right to emphasize that arrests of nonviolent protesters will continue as Trump and his allies lose political battles. That is what declining movements do when they cannot win consent: they reach for the state. They do not persuade; they criminalize.

The Useful Idiots Are Not the Main Threat

The article is strongest when it distinguishes between the obvious extremists and the “fascists in suits.” The former are the mascots. The latter are the operators. The men waving flags in the street are useful as propaganda and distraction, but the more consequential force is the one embedded in offices, agencies, and legal systems.

That is why the story should not be read as a culture-war anecdote about noisy radicals. It is about institutional cowardice: officials and parties that tolerate escalation until the machinery is already moving against the public.

Youth Drift Is a Political Failure

Aziz links the attraction of fascism among some young voters, including young men of color, to neoliberal failure: financial collapse, outsourcing, greed, and the wreckage of the Iraq and Afghanistan wars. That diagnosis is blunt for a reason. People do not drift right because cruelty is aesthetically appealing. They drift when democratic institutions stop offering material dignity, belonging, or a credible future.

This is not an excuse for white supremacy. It is an indictment of the political economy that leaves alienation available for reactionaries to harvest. When mainstream politics delivers insecurity and humiliation, fascism arrives packaged as strength.

The Systemic Lesson

The article’s real warning is that authoritarian politics does not need to announce itself with camps and uniforms before it becomes lethal. It advances through familiar institutions, partisan discipline, legal pretexts, and public exhaustion. The United States is not being “tested” by fascism from outside. Its own machinery has long been prepared for exactly this abuse.

The democratic response cannot be sentimental. It has to be institutional, material, and ruthless about naming who is doing the harming. The problem is not confusion. The problem is power learning how to wear the law as a mask.
