'Not April Fools': MAGA lawmaker said to reach new low with 'depressingly funny' bill

The TRANS MICE Act Is Not a Joke. It Is a Loyalty Ritual.

The Source, Briefly

Rep. Nancy Mace, after losing her bid for governor and planning to leave Congress early next year, announced a bill she calls the “TRANS MICE Act.” It would bar federally funded research “aimed at altering an animal’s biological sex,” echoing Donald Trump’s false claim that taxpayers were funding experiments to make “mice transgender.” The claim collapses under basic biology: the research in question involves transgenic mice, not transgender mice.

Who Has Power Here

Mace is not exercising serious legislative power so much as using the machinery of Congress as a stage prop. She is a departing member trying to convert one more media cycle into political usefulness. The actual institutional power in this story sits higher up: Trump set the frame by lying, and Mace is now recycling that lie into bill form.

That matters. When a lawmaker turns a presidential fabrication into a proposal, she is not correcting misinformation. She is certifying it.

What The Bill Really Does

The bill does not solve a problem. It manufactures one. It takes ordinary biomedical research involving altered DNA and recasts it as “ideological cruelty,” a phrase built to smear scientists while pretending the premise is self-evident.

This is not confusion. It is deliberate category abuse. “Transgenic” becomes “transgender” because the point is not accuracy; the point is to weaponize disgust against research institutions and the people who work in them. The target is science literacy, and the weapon is stupid on purpose.

The Real Misdirection

The framing invites readers to laugh at the misunderstanding, but the political mechanism is uglier than a simple mistake. The joke is the cover. The bill lets Mace perform anti-science grievance while pretending she is protecting animals from some invented moral contagion.

It also shifts attention away from the source of the lie. Trump made the false claim first. Mace amplified it. The article’s comic tone risks making this look like an isolated embarrassment instead of what it is: an elected official laundering presidential misinformation into mock legislation for partisan gain.

Scapegoating As Governance

This is a familiar right-wing pattern. Find a technical term, strip it of meaning, attach sexual panic, and then present the resulting fantasy as public policy. The point is not to govern well. The point is to give a base audience a target and a villain.

Scientists become elitists. Research becomes ideology. Biological terminology becomes a punchline. And a lawmaker with no real future in office gets to exit the stage pretending she fought some grotesque cultural emergency instead of peddling nonsense.

The Pattern

The deeper story is not about mice. It is about how political decay turns institutions into carriers for falsehood. A false presidential claim becomes a congressional bill. A congressional bill becomes a social-media stunt. A stunt becomes evidence of “concern” for people who have already decided facts are optional.

That is the system at work: power invents a lie, lesser officials normalize it, and public institutions absorb the damage. The cruelty is not accidental. It is the product.
