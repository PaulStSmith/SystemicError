This week, President Donald Trump announced that he ordered a military attack on what he claimed were drug traffickers in a small boat in the Caribbean. However, two high-ranking Pentagon officials told The Intercept that the strike was a criminal attack on civilians — and the reasoning for it was created only after the attack.

“The U.S. is now directly targeting civilians. Drug traffickers may be criminals but they aren’t combatants,” one official said. “When Trump fired the military’s top lawyers the rest saw the writing on the wall, and instead of being a critical firebreak they are now a rubber stamp complicit in this crime.”

On Truth Social, Trump claimed the strike was “against positively identified Tren de Aragua Narcoterrorists."

The declassified video shows a small boat with four engines flying through the water when it explodes. It took days for the White House to give the public a story.

Experts called Hegseth’s logic "flimsy, if not farcical," The Intercept reported.

“Tren de Aragua being designated as a foreign terrorist organization is a purely domestic law enforcement designation. It offers no authority for the military to use deadly force,” Todd Huntley told the site. He's spent more than 23 years as an active-duty judge advocate and legal advisor for Special Operations forces. “Under international law, there’s no way this even gets close to being a legitimate use of force."

The report cited everyone from legal experts to members of Congress who have made similar comments.

Hegseth has indicated there will be more strikes to come.

“It won’t stop with just this strike,” he told Fox News on Wednesday. "Anyone else trafficking in those waters who we know is a designated narco-terrorist will face the same fate."

Our Analysis:

Unmasking a Dubious Strike: Trump's Caribbean Adventure

This week's revelation by President Donald Trump about ordering a military attack against alleged drug traffickers in the Caribbean reads like a script torn from the darkest corners of political fiction. Yet, the stark reality, as reported by The Intercept, lays bare a troubling narrative of misuse of military power and a brazen disregard for the rule of law—both domestically and internationally.

Trump's Claim vs. The Pentagon's Admission

Trump's announcement, laden with the bravado typical of his administration, was quickly undercut by the disturbing confessions of two high-ranking Pentagon officials. They categorically described the strike as a criminal attack on civilians, a revelation that should send shivers down the spine of any observer who values human life and the principles of justice. The officials further disclosed that the justification for this heinous act was concocted post-factum, painting a picture of an administration scrambling to cover its tracks.

The Alarming Silence of the Military's Legal Minds

The narrative takes an even darker turn with the mention of Trump's dismissal of the military's top lawyers. This act, according to one Pentagon official, transformed what should have been an independent and critical check on military operations into a "rubber stamp complicit in this crime." This chilling account underscores the dangerous erosion of institutional integrity under Trump's command.

The Dubious Designation and International Law

Trump's attempt to justify the strike on the basis of combating "Tren de Aragua Narcoterrorists" is met with skepticism and outright rejection by experts. The declassified video, rather than providing clarity, only added to the controversy, with the delayed and flimsy rationale offered by the White House being criticized as "flimsy, if not farcical."

Legal experts, including Todd Huntley with over 23 years of experience as a legal advisor for Special Operations forces, have vehemently argued that the designation of Tren de Aragua as a foreign terrorist organization does not grant the military any authority to use deadly force in this manner. Huntley's assertion that "Under international law, there’s no way this even gets close to being a legitimate use of force," serves as a damning indictment of the strike's legality.

A Dangerous Precedent

The implications of Hegseth's ominous warning of more strikes to come cannot be overstated. Such a stance not only threatens to further destabilize an already volatile region but also sets a dangerous precedent for the use of military force against perceived threats without clear legal justification or oversight.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The article shines a light on a deeply troubling episode that raises serious questions about the conduct of the Trump administration and its cavalier attitude towards human life, legal norms, and international standards. It is a stark reminder of the importance of accountability and the need for a vigorous and independent oversight mechanism to prevent such abuses of power.

In a world fraught with real threats and complex challenges, resorting to illegal and indiscriminate force is not only morally indefensible but strategically shortsighted. It is incumbent upon us, as informed citizens, to demand better from our leaders and to hold them accountable for their actions. Only through a relentless pursuit of truth and justice can we hope to prevent such egregious acts from occurring in the future.
