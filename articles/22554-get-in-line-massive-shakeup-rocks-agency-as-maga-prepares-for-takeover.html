'Get in line': Massive shakeup rocks agency as MAGA prepares for takeover

Based on an article by Alex Henderson

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Power, Not PersonnelThe Goal Is ObedienceExpertise Is the TargetThe 2017 Playbook Never LeftThe Real Pattern

Burgum’s Interior Purge Is Not Management. It Is Subordination.

Power, Not Personnel

The Interior Department is not undergoing some neutral “shakeup.” Secretary Doug Burgum is moving roughly 30 senior staffers out of their areas of expertise and into roles where they can be sidelined, broken down, or replaced. That is the power move here: not to improve the agency, but to make sure the agency belongs to the political class now running it.

The reporting makes the stakes plain. Career scientists and land managers are being displaced. The people who know how the department actually functions are being pushed aside. That is not administration. It is a purge by paperwork.

The Goal Is Obedience

The source gives away the logic in the staffers’ own words. One described the moves as punitive. Another said the point is to send a message: get in line, or this happens to you. That is the real function of the reassignments. They are not about matching skills to jobs. They are about converting the civil service into a compliance machine.

This is how authoritarian politics works inside the state. First you make expertise feel precarious. Then you teach everyone that competence offers no protection. After that, loyalty becomes the only safe career strategy.

Expertise Is the Target

Sen. Martin Heinrich is right to say Americans lose when seasoned scientists and land management professionals are shoved out of their lanes. Permits slow down. Wildfire protection weakens. Science-based land and water management gets degraded. Those are not accidental side effects. They are the price of treating public administration as patronage.

The administration is not merely ignoring institutional knowledge. It is eroding it on purpose. A department that cannot rely on its own long-serving experts becomes easier to control and harder to challenge. That is useful if your priority is power, not governance.

The 2017 Playbook Never Left

The article notes that this already happened in 2017 under Trump and Ryan Zinke, when Interior was thrown into chaos by mass reassignments. That history matters because it shows this is not improvisation. It is a repeatable method: disrupt the agency, scatter its leadership, and create a vacuum that political loyalists can fill.

The old excuse was confusion. That excuse is thin now. When the same pattern returns under the same political project, it is not confusion. It is institutional sabotage with a personnel memo attached.

The Real Pattern

Interior is being stripped down into something more governable from the top and less accountable from within. Career staff are being told, in effect, that their knowledge is disposable and their loyalty is under review. Two weeks to accept or resign is not a normal transition. It is a pressure tactic.

The larger pattern is simple. Authoritarian-minded administrations do not just capture agencies at the top. They hollow them out below, where expertise lives and where resistance can still slow damage. Democrats like Heinrich are right to call out the consequences, but the language should stay sharp: this is a purge of public capacity in service of political control. The point is not a better Interior Department. The point is an obedient one.
