Kavanaugh opinion lays out 'road map' for Trump to go around Supreme Court: legal expert

President Donald Trump is furious after losing his tariff case before the U.S. Supreme Court. The high court ruled that Trump overstepped his authority when he tried to make “emergency” tariff laws in retaliation against countries that weren’t nice to him.

Speaking about the matter on Friday, legal analyst and CNN reporter Paula Reid noted that even Trump’s own lawyers have been shocked by the Supreme Court granting him so much leeway on the “unitary executive theory.“

“So, there’s been a question about whether there still is a respect for separation of powers, and I think that’s what really matters,” Reid said. “That matters so much in the Trump administration right now because I think we’re seeing a lot of questions about how he might use his office to take over — he has taken over the Justice Department. Used that to pursue his enemies, use that to try and put his thumb on the scale of the election. Will there be a check on President Trump?”

CNN legal analyst Elliot Williams cited the separation between Justice Brett Kavanaugh and Chief Justice John Roberts, wondering if there’s an “intellectual fracas” brewing between the two conservatives on the law.

He predicted that over the next few years, there will likely be more conversation about the “Major Questions Doctrine.” It is an idea that in order for the president to act, Congress must give him the power to do so under the law.

Kavanaugh and Roberts clashed in the ruling today, where Kavanaugh said that an issue like this is “squarely in the authority of the president,” Williams characterized.

“So, I think the next several years of debates between the conservatives over what the reach of conservative jurisprudence ought to be,” he added. “We saw the road map for it here.”

New York Times White House correspondent Zolan Kanno-Youngs recalled the conversation he had with Trump in the Oval Office, where he was “candid” about “just how disappointed he would be in the justices he helped put on the Supreme Court. It almost seemed like he would personally be insulted by this.”

Kanno-Youngs noted that it will cause problems for Trump in other foreign policy because he uses tariffs as a “diplomatic sledgehammer, right? This is what he hangs over nations when they don’t do what he wants. It was his threat before Greenland.”

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court’s Rebuff to Trump’s Tariff Tantrum: A Closer Look

In an episode that could only unfold in the tumultuous saga of Donald Trump’s presidency, the U.S. Supreme Court has served a stinging rebuke to what can only be described as a flagrant overreach of executive power. The high court’s decision against Trump’s attempt to wield “emergency” tariff laws as a personal cudgel against countries he perceives as unfriendly is not just a legal defeat for the former president; it’s a resounding affirmation of the principle of separation of powers—a cornerstone of American democracy that Trump has repeatedly treated with the same respect one might give to an inconvenient speed bump.

The Shock of Accountability

The reaction from Trump and his camp to the Supreme Court’s ruling is akin to a child’s dismay upon learning that actions have consequences. Trump is “furious,” a sentiment that, while understandable from the perspective of a man used to bending institutions to his will, is ludicrous in the face of the constitutional overstep he attempted. The audacity of Trump’s legal maneuvers, to the extent that even his own lawyers were reportedly taken aback by how much leeway the Supreme Court had previously granted him, speaks volumes about the brazenness with which the Trump administration sought to reshape the balance of power in its favor.

The Mirage of the Unitary Executive Theory

CNN reporter Paula Reid’s observations underscore a chilling reality: the Trump administration’s flirtation with the “unitary executive theory” has been a thinly veiled attempt to concentrate power in the hands of the presidency at the expense of the legislative and judicial branches. This theory, taken to its extreme, could have laid the groundwork for a de facto autocracy, with Trump at the helm, unfettered by checks and balances. The Supreme Court’s decision is a bulwark against this drift toward executive overreach, yet the fact that such a defense was necessary is a sobering reminder of how close the American political system has come to the brink.

The Kavanaugh-Roberts Divide

The reported intellectual skirmish between Justices Brett Kavanaugh and John Roberts is more than just a juicy tidbit of Supreme Court gossip; it’s a window into the ongoing struggle within conservative jurisprudence over the limits of executive power. Kavanaugh’s stance, as characterized by CNN legal analyst Elliot Williams, appears to lean towards a more expansive view of presidential authority, whereas Roberts’ position suggests a more cautious approach, wary of the dangers of overconcentration of power. This debate among conservatives is crucial, as it will shape the contours of American law and governance for years to come, especially as the “Major Questions Doctrine” gains prominence.

Trump’s Sledgehammer Diplomacy

The implications of the Supreme Court’s decision extend far beyond the legal sphere; they strike at the heart of Trump’s foreign policy strategy, which, as New York Times White House correspondent Zolan Kanno-Youngs aptly puts it, has employed tariffs as a “diplomatic sledgehammer.” Trump’s blunt-force approach to international relations, characterized by a penchant for using economic threats to coerce other nations into compliance, has been curtailed. This is a setback not just for Trump’s ego, but for his entire modus operandi on the global stage.

Conclusion: A Reprieve, Not a Resolution

The Supreme Court’s ruling is a critical reprieve in the ongoing battle to preserve the checks and balances that underpin the American political system. Yet, it is far from a resolution. The very fact that such an overreach was attempted, and almost succeeded, is a stark reminder of the fragility of democratic institutions. It’s a call to vigilance, a warning that the price of liberty is eternal vigilance. Trump’s reaction, and the broader context of his administration’s endeavors to expand executive power, should be seen for what it is: a cautionary tale about the dangers of complacency in the face of authoritarian impulses.
