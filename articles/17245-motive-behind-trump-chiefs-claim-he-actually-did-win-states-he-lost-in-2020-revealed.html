Motive behind Trump chief’s claim 'he actually did win' states he lost in 2020 revealed

Manufactured Reality: Susie Wiles and the Ongoing Assault on Democratic Norms

Manufactured Reality: Susie Wiles and the Ongoing Assault on Democratic Norms

The Power at Play

In the ongoing saga of the 2020 election, Susie Wiles, President Donald Trump’s chief of staff, has made headlines again by suggesting that investigations could reveal Trump actually won the election. This assertion comes despite the overwhelming legal and electoral evidence to the contrary. Yet, the influence wielded by Wiles and her office is significant, not just in promoting baseless claims but in shaping a narrative that has tangible consequences for the fabric of American democracy.

Directing the Blame

Wiles, a high-ranking official within the Trump administration, is using her platform to echo and amplify disputed claims about the election. This is not a case of misdirected blame or confusion; this is a deliberate strategy to maintain political momentum among Trump’s base. The decision to seize all ballots from Fulton County by Trump’s FBI, leaving no copies, raises serious concerns about the integrity and transparency of their supposed investigation.

The Pattern of Deception

This narrative is a continuation of a well-documented pattern where Trump and his surrogates have consistently pushed the idea of a stolen election despite a lack of evidence. The legal battles that followed the 2020 election—62 lawsuits, the majority of which were dismissed or ruled against—illustrate a systematic attempt to subvert the electoral process through legal means. These efforts were supplemented by outlandish theories and accusations, ranging from blaming foreign conspiracies to outright fabrications about voting irregularities.

Consequences of the Charade

The ramifications of these actions are profound. By persistently promoting election fraud conspiracies, Wiles and her associates are not just undermining public trust in electoral integrity; they are actively shaping a political environment where facts are malleable and truth is politicized. This isn’t just about one election; it’s about setting a precedent where elections can be contested without evidence, where institutional trust is eroded for political gain.

Broader Implications

The broader political insight here is alarming yet clear: there is a concerted effort by certain factions within American politics to delegitimize democratic processes to maintain power. This strategy involves manipulating public perception and exploiting institutional mechanisms to challenge unfavorable outcomes. The endgame is not just about vindicating Trump but about altering the landscape of American political norms and expectations.

In conclusion, while Susie Wiles might present her claims under the guise of seeking truth, the reality is a calculated attack on the democratic principles that govern electoral integrity. This isn’t just about partisan politics; it’s about how democracy itself is upheld or dismantled in the sight of unfounded claims and power-driven narratives.
