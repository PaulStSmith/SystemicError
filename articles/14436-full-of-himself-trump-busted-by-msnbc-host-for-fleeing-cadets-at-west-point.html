'Full of himself' Trump busted by MSNBC host for fleeing cadets at West Point

U.S. President Donald Trump wears a ‘Make America Great Again’ (MAGA) hat as he attends the commencement ceremony at West Point Military Academy in West Point, New York, U.S., May 24, 2025. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

During a panel discussion on MSNBC on Donald Trump's commencement speech to cadets at West Point on Saturday, MSNBC's Alex Witt pointed out that the president broke with tradition by leaving immediately after.

Following the speech, which has become notable for the president discussing the usefulness of "trophy wives," Trump was escorted off-stage and left without taking part in either saluting or shaking hands with graduating cadets as previous presidents have done –– including President Joe Biden last year.

"Here's something else that some are going to call inappropriate," the MSNBC host addressed to former Barrack Obama HUD Secretary Julián Castro. "Because Trump skipped the tradition of past presidents who shake the hand of each graduate as they receive their diploma. In fact, last year, President Biden stood for more than an hour and returned the salute of over 1000 West Point graduates."

She added, "In contrast, Trump left yesterday before diplomas were even handed out," before pointing out the Vice President J.D. Vance spoke at the U.S Naval Academy and did stick around to greet each graduate.

"What are the optics here?" the MSNBC host pressed her guest.

"Well, I mean, I think what you can say about Trump is that he's full of himself," Castro replied. "He's self-absorbed, doesn't really care about the graduates that he's addressing, speaks in platitudes and, you know, says all of the right words in a speech to congratulate them but doesn't really care.

"That's why he's disrespecting them by wearing a MAGA hat, not shaking their hands, not taking the time," he continued. "I mean, you know, if you're a parent, they're in the audience and you're watching and this is the day that you've been looking forward to. And your son or daughter, look what they're accomplishing –– you have to find this so disrespectful."

Our Analysis:

The Art of Disrespect: Trump's West Point Departure

In the latest episode of political theater, Donald Trump managed to underscore his presidency's legacy with a gesture—or rather, the lack thereof—that speaks volumes more than his actual commencement speech at West Point. While MSNBC's Alex Witt and former HUD Secretary Julián Castro dissected the event, let's cut through the fluff and focus on the stark contrasts and underlying implications of Trump's actions.

Breaking Tradition: The Trump Way

Tradition in the political sphere often acts as a bridge between generations, a nonverbal communication of respect and continuity. Yet, Trump, in his infinite wisdom, chose to sever this connection in the most Trumpian way possible—by making a hasty exit post-speech, foregoing the customary handshakes and salutes afforded to the graduates by their commander-in-chief. This move is not merely a break from tradition; it is a glaring exhibition of self-absorption and a stark disregard for the cadets' achievements and the ceremonial significance of the event.

Biden vs. Trump: A Study in Contrast

To put things into perspective, President Joe Biden, in the previous year, stood for over an hour, returning the salute of more than 1,000 West Point graduates. This act, though physically demanding, symbolizes a gesture of respect and acknowledgment from the nation's leader to its future military leaders. It's a physical manifestation of the phrase, "I see you, I appreciate you, and I honor your commitment." Trump's decision to skip this tradition not only highlights his narcissistic tendencies but also places him in direct contrast with his successor, underscoring a lack of basic decency and respect for the military institution he once led.

The Optics of Self-Absorption

When Castro comments on Trump being "full of himself" and "self-absorbed," it's not just a throwaway critique. It's a pointed observation on how Trump's personal ego often eclipsed his official duties. Optics matter, especially in politics. By wearing a MAGA hat and prioritizing his departure over a time-honored tradition, Trump sent a clear message: His personal brand is more important than the graduates' accomplishments or the institution's traditions.

The Disrespect Felt 'Round the World

Imagine being a parent in the audience, bursting with pride over your child's significant achievement, only to watch the commander-in-chief treat the ceremony with such indifference. This act of disrespect doesn't just affect the graduates; it ripples through their families, the military community, and the country's perception of leadership. Trump's behavior is emblematic of his presidency's broader issues—a consistent failure to grasp the gravity of his role and the decorum it demands.

In Conclusion

Trump's departure from West Point is not just about a missed handshake or an unreturned salute. It's a microcosm of his tenure in office—marked by self-interest, disregard for tradition, and a profound lack of empathy. As we analyze these actions, let's remember that the symbols and traditions of our institutions hold weight. They are the threads that connect generations, embody values, and foster respect. When leaders choose to ignore these traditions, they're not just breaking protocol; they're unraveling the fabric of our collective identity and respect for those who serve.
