'Mark my words': Ex-GOP lawmaker makes huge prediction about Trump's 'game plan'

Donald Trump sent the military into Los Angeles not to prevent a crisis, but for his own chilling agenda, according to former Tea Party Republican lawmaker Joe Walsh.

Walsh, who recently announced his decision to formally become a Democrat after being politically independent for years, called Trump's behavior "performative hate."

"Let’s get something straight from the top: there is no national emergency in Los Angeles. The crisis is invented. Manufactured. Just like the 'trade crisis.' And it’s being used—once again—by Donald Trump to divide, to incite, and to expand his power," according to Walsh. "What is happening in Los Angeles is not a spontaneous breakdown of law and order. It’s not a war zone. It’s not a 'city of criminals,' as Trump’s Homeland Security head Kristi Noem outrageously claimed on Fox News. It’s a show. A performance. A dangerous, dishonest farce designed to justify authoritarianism."

For Walsh, this is about something huge: nixing the midterms.

Walsh continued, saying, "What is happening in Los Angeles right now, and what’s been happening for the past few days, has made it crystal clear again that Trump is itching to sic the U.S. military on the American people. That’s his game plan."

"And that will be his game plan when he tries to cancel the 2026 midterms. Mark my words," he added.

Walsh goes on to lash out at MAGA.

"But Trump, ever the showman, saw it as his big opportunity. He takes isolated protests—peaceful ones, for the most part—and broadcasts them through a megaphone of fear. A block and a half of demonstrators in a metro area of over 22 million becomes 'Los Angeles is burning down.' For God’s sake, MAGA! The man lies to your face and expects you to eat it up," the ex-lawmaker wrote. "Trump wants you to believe we are under siege. That’s Step One. Then comes Step Two: send in the military. Invoke the Insurrection Act. Declare martial law. And ultimately? Cancel elections."

He then added, "Sound extreme? It’s not hypothetical. In his first term, Trump ordered his Defense Secretary to authorize soldiers to open fire on American civilians protesting in Washington, D.C. Mark Esper refused. That’s the only reason it didn’t happen."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Dangerous Theater: A Closer Look

In an era where political theater often masks chilling agendas, the recent comments by former Tea Party Republican and newly declared Democrat Joe Walsh offer a stark warning. Labeling Donald Trump's military deployment to Los Angeles as "performative hate," Walsh paints a picture of a crisis not just exaggerated, but entirely fabricated for nefarious purposes.

The Manufactured Crisis

Walsh is unequivocal in his assessment: "there is no national emergency in Los Angeles." This statement cuts to the heart of the matter, challenging the narrative pushed by Trump and his administration. The invocation of a national crisis, Walsh argues, is a tactic straight out of Trump's playbook, likening it to the so-called 'trade crisis.' It's a strategy designed to divide, incite, and, most disturbingly, expand Trump's power.

Los Angeles: Not a War Zone

The depiction of Los Angeles as a war zone or a "city of criminals" by Trump's Homeland Security head, Kristi Noem, is called out by Walsh as nothing short of outrageous. This narrative, amplified by platforms like Fox News, is identified as a dangerous farce. It's a performance, aimed at justifying authoritarian measures under the guise of maintaining law and order.

The Ultimate Goal: Cancelling the Midterms

Walsh doesn't mince words when he suggests that Trump's actions are a prelude to a more sinister plan: "nixing the midterms." The deployment of the military is seen as a rehearsal, a testing of waters for a larger scheme to disrupt the democratic process itself. The warning is clear: what's unfolding could be a blueprint for cancelling the 2026 midterm elections.

MAGA's Willful Blindness

The former lawmaker's criticism extends to the MAGA movement, accusing it of complicity in Trump's deception. By exaggerating isolated protests and spreading fear, Trump manipulates his base into accepting increasingly authoritarian measures. Walsh's frustration is palpable as he condemns the willingness of MAGA supporters to buy into these blatant lies.

A History of Overreach

Walsh reminds us of a disturbing precedent: Trump's previous attempt to authorize military action against American civilians during protests in Washington, D.C. This incident, where Defense Secretary Mark Esper refused Trump's order, serves as a dire warning of what could have happened—and what might still occur if such powers are left unchecked.

Final Thoughts

Joe Walsh's transition from a Tea Party Republican to a Democrat is not just a personal political shift; it's emblematic of a larger concern about the direction of American democracy under Trump's influence. His warnings about the manufactured crisis in Los Angeles and the potential cancellation of future elections are a clarion call to remain vigilant. The events described are not merely political maneuvers; they are threats to the very fabric of democratic governance.

In analyzing Walsh's comments, it's crucial to recognize the broader implications of Trump's tactics. This is not just about Los Angeles or the midterm elections; it's about the integrity of democratic institutions and the resilience of the American political system. The performative hate that Walsh describes is a symptom of a deeper malaise, one that requires not just scrutiny but active resistance.
