'We should be clear who Charlie Kirk was!' AOC slams right-wing influencer on House floor

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Politicization of a Resolution: A Deep Dive

Screengrab / United States House Clerk

Just moments after a divided House voted Friday to adopt a resolution “honoring the life and legacy” of Charlie Kirk in the wake of his killing last week, Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) slammed the slain right-wing influencer as “ignorant” and "uneducated" for his numerous past controversial remarks, and accused House Republicans of “whitewashing American history.”

“We should be clear about who Charlie Kirk was; a man who believed that the Civil Rights Act that granted Black Americans the right to vote was a mistake,” AOC said, speaking on the House floor.

“Who, after the violent attack on Paul Pelosi, claimed that 'some amazing patriot' should bail out his brutal assailant, and accused Jews of controlling 'not just the colleges, it's the nonprofits, the movies, it's Hollywood, it's all of it.'"

AOC condemned political violence in all forms, but compared Republicans’ resolution honoring Kirk unfavorably with past resolutions condemning political violence, such as those adopted after the targeted killing of Minnesota State House minority leader Melissa Hortman, who was killed in June in an act of political violence.

Specifically, AOC took issue with portions of language included in the resolution, particularly its mention of Kirk’s “legacy,” or that Kirk had worked “tirelessly to promote unity,” a claim, AOC charged, was partisan in nature, divisive, and blatantly untrue.

“Today's resolution only underscores the majority's recklessness by choosing to author this condemnation and honoring on a purely partisan basis, instead of uniting Congress in this tragedy,” AOC said.

“...His rhetoric and beliefs were ignorant, uneducated, and sought to disenfranchise millions of Americans, far from the 'working tirelessly to promote unity' asserted by the majority in this resolution.”

AOC’s comments came after the passage of the resolution, which ultimately passed with a vote of 310-58, with 215 Republicans and 94 Democrats for the measure, and 58 Democrats voting against it.

Our Analysis:

The Politicization of a Resolution: A Deep Dive

In a move that typifies the current polarized state of American politics, the House recently voted to adopt a resolution "honoring the life and legacy" of the controversial right-wing influencer, Charlie Kirk. This decision, supported by a divisive vote of 310-58, has once again illuminated the stark divisions between the two major political parties in the United States. However, it's not the vote itself but the scathing critique by Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) that brings this issue into sharp relief.

The Heart of the Matter: Whitewashing or Honoring?

Rep. Ocasio-Cortez's condemnation of the resolution—and, by extension, Charlie Kirk—raises profound questions about the nature of political discourse and memory in America. She describes Kirk as "ignorant" and "uneducated" for his controversial remarks, including his problematic views on the Civil Rights Act and his inflammatory comments following the violent attack on Paul Pelosi. These are not minor quibbles or political nitpicking; they strike at the very core of what it means to honor a public figure's legacy.

The Allegation of Whitewashing

The accusation of "whitewashing American history" is particularly potent. By choosing to honor Kirk with a resolution, the House Republicans are accused of selectively ignoring his divisive and inflammatory rhetoric. This isn't just about differing opinions; it's about how the actions of political figures are remembered and enshrined in history. It's a bold claim by AOC, suggesting that the resolution is an attempt to sanitize Kirk's controversial legacy, presenting a version of history that omits significant and troubling aspects of his public life.

Comparative Condemnation: A Lack of Consistency

AOC's comparison of the resolution honoring Kirk with past resolutions condemning political violence, such as the one adopted after the killing of Melissa Hortman, is particularly telling. It highlights a perceived inconsistency in how the House responds to political violence and commemorates its victims. This comparison underscores a fundamental question: What values are being honored or condemned through such resolutions? The disparity in the House's response, as noted by AOC, suggests a partisan bias that privileges certain narratives over others.

"Promoting Unity"? A Partisan Legacy

The resolution's claim that Kirk worked "tirelessly to promote unity" is challenged head-on by AOC. She argues that his rhetoric and beliefs were, in fact, divisive and aimed at disenfranchising millions of Americans. This assertion calls into question not only the factual accuracy of the resolution's claims but also the criteria by which someone's legacy is deemed worthy of honor by the Congress. It's a stark reminder that the narratives crafted in such resolutions can either bridge divides or deepen them, depending on whose version of history is being told.

Conclusion: A Reflection of Our Times

The adoption of the resolution honoring Charlie Kirk, and the subsequent critique by AOC, is a microcosm of the broader political and cultural divides in America today. It's a reminder that the act of remembering is inherently political, shaping not only how we view the past but also how we navigate the present and future. In a world where truth and fact are increasingly contested battlegrounds, the debate over this resolution illustrates the critical need for a more nuanced and equitable approach to commemorating political figures. The resolution's passage, despite the objections, reflects a choice about which legacies are worth honoring—a choice that will undoubtedly continue to provoke fierce debate and reflection.
