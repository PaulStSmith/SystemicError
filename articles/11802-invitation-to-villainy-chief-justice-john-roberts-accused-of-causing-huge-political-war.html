The current war over gerrymandering in Texas, California, and other states is the result of Chief Justice John Roberts' Supreme Court, according to a legal expert.

James Sample, a professor of constitutional law at Hofstra University, appeared on MSNBC on Saturday to discuss Texas GOP "re-redistricting," and other key topics.

Sample described what he called an "underemphasized" point regarding the current "chaos": the Supreme Court caused it.

"The current chaos is a product of the U.S. Supreme Court," he elaborated. "In 2019, in a case called Rucho v. Common Cause, the Supreme Court essentially said, 'You know what? Partisan gerrymandering is anti-democratic. It is bad for democracy, but there's nothing we can do.' And saying that there was nothing that the court could do was a departure from prior practice," Sample said. "If you think about it this way, shoplifting is illegal, right? Very few shoplifters get caught, but you don't see shop owners putting up a sign in the window that says the police will never arrest you if you shoplift from our store."

He continued:

"In 2019, the Supreme Court of the United States put up that sign. The Texas GOP here is they're acting like villains. But the invitation to that villainy came from One First Street, NE, in Washington, DC, at the hands of the Roberts court."

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court's Invitation to Gerrymander: A Legal Expert's Take

In a recent appearance on MSNBC, James Sample, a professor of constitutional law at Hofstra University, laid bare the chaotic battleground of gerrymandering that's tearing through states like Texas and California. But let's not mince words here; according to Sample, the root of this democratic disarray can be traced back to none other than the Supreme Court under Chief Justice John Roberts. The bold assertion here is that the Court, through its 2019 ruling in Rucho v. Common Cause, essentially flung open the doors to the gerrymandering frenzy we're witnessing today. Let's dissect this claim and the profound implications it carries for the fabric of American democracy.

The Supreme Court's Green Light to Gerrymandering

In a move that reeks of judicial abdication, the Supreme Court, in Rucho v. Common Cause, declared partisan gerrymandering a democracy-damaging evil but then threw up its hands, claiming impotence in addressing it. This is the judiciary equivalent of acknowledging a house is on fire but deciding against calling the fire department. Sample draws a compelling parallel here: Imagine a world where shoplifters are given carte blanche because the authorities can't be bothered to pursue them. This isn't just negligence; it's an open invitation to anarchy.

The Texas GOP: Villains or Opportunists?

With the Supreme Court's stance, the Texas GOP's aggressive "re-redistricting" efforts can be seen in a new light. Are they villains for exploiting the system, or simply opportunists seizing the opportunity the Supreme Court laid at their feet? Sample suggests it's the latter, but let's not be too quick to absolve them of their villainy. Exploiting a loophole for undemocratic advantage still reeks of moral bankruptcy.

A Departure from Prior Practice

What makes the Supreme Court's decision particularly galling is its departure from prior judicial practice. Historically, the Court has not shied away from intervening in matters of grave constitutional importance. Their decision to step back from the issue of gerrymandering signals a troubling retreat from their duty to protect democratic principles. It's as if the guardians of democracy have decided to take an extended leave of absence at the very moment their vigilance is most needed.

The Cost of Inaction

The consequences of this judicial inaction are far-reaching. Beyond the immediate impact on electoral fairness and representation, this green-lighting of partisan gerrymandering undermines public confidence in the electoral process and, by extension, the very legitimacy of our democratic institutions. In the long term, this erodes the foundational principles upon which the United States was built.

Conclusion

James Sample's analysis is a clarion call to recognize the Supreme Court's complicity in the current state of democratic disarray. The Court's decision in Rucho v. Common Cause may well be remembered as a pivotal moment when the judiciary chose a path of passivity over protection, inviting chaos over safeguarding the constitutional order. As we grapple with the repercussions of this choice, one thing becomes glaringly apparent: the Supreme Court, under the stewardship of Chief Justice John Roberts, has not just failed to extinguish the flames of partisan gerrymandering but has actively fueled them.
