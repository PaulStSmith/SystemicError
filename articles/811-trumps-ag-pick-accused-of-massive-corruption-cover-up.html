Trump’s AG pick accused of massive corruption cover-up

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20262 min read

Blanche Is Not a Referee. He Is the Instrument.

The Setup

The source material is about Todd Blanche, Trump’s acting attorney general and nominee for the permanent job, and the case against him is plain: his record suggests not independence, but loyalty. The reporting ties him to Trump’s legal and political protection system, from the ballroom filing that read like a Truth Social post in legal drag to the immunity deal meant to shield the president and his family from future scrutiny.

Power Sits With Trump

The key fact here is not Blanche’s résumé. It is where authority actually flows. Trump chose him. Trump’s Senate allies are signaling compliance. John Thune’s comment that most Republicans are “pretty deferential to who the President wants” is the real constitutional confession in the story. The Senate is not being asked to evaluate a nominee on merit. It is being asked whether it will ratify the president’s choice to place a loyal operator atop the Justice Department.

The Audience Is Not the Court

Blanche’s White House ballroom filing is the clearest example of how this machine works. According to the source, it was soaked in Trump’s style, calling the National Trust “FAKE,” invoking “Trump Derangement Syndrome,” attacking a lawyer tied to Obama, and lavishing praise on Trump as a “highly successful real estate developer.” That language was not accidental sloppiness. It was political signaling. As Marcus notes, Blanche was not really writing for Judge Richard Leon. He was writing for an audience of one.

The Cover-Up Is the Job

Reich’s more serious claim is that Blanche helped devise the immunity settlement that could block future inquiry into Trump and his family’s finances. The source lists the alleged stakes: crypto ventures, critical minerals investments, drone manufacturing interests, foreign meetings tied to real estate deals, stock trading while in office, and pardons for financial criminals. The pattern is not subtle. The purpose is not legal resolution. It is insulation. If the settlement stands, the point is not to settle a dispute. It is to make future investigation harder or impossible.

The Senate Is the Gatekeeper It Refuses to Be

A Republican senator could block Blanche in committee. Four could stop him on the floor. That means this is not a technical inevitability. It is a test of whether senators will behave like an independent branch of government or like an appointment desk for Trump’s inner circle. The source says the answer is probably the latter. That is not a procedural detail. It is the mechanism by which executive corruption becomes normalized: the people with the power to slow it down decide that obedience is easier.

The Pattern Is Loyalist Statecraft

This story is bigger than one nomination. It shows a governing style built on personal immunity, institutional capture, and performative legalism. Trump benefits from officials who translate political loyalty into formal authority, then use that authority to sanitize his conduct, reward allies, and bury the trail. The language is legal. The function is protection. That is how authoritarian corruption often presents itself in a democracy that still has the paperwork of oversight but not always the will to use it.
