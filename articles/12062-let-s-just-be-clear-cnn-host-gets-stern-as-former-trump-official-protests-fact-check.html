Trump fired BLS head Erika McEntarfer over the lackluster jobs report last week, and posted to Truth Social Monday claiming, "Last weeks [sic] Job’s [sic] Report was RIGGED, just like the numbers prior to the Presidential Election were Rigged. That’s why, in both cases, there was massive, record setting revisions, in favor of the Radical Left Democrats."

Moore agreed that the numbers coming out are "just not accurate, and there are a lot of reasons for that, but they're getting worse and worse in each passing month and each passing year."

Moore added that he would have fired McEntarfer "not for political reasons, but just because I don't think they're doing a very good job."

"These are probably the most closely watched numbers that come out each month as a barometer for how the economy is doing," Moore continued. "We want accuracy, and right now, we're not getting accuracy. These revisions are larger than they've ever been before, and that means we're just not doing it very well."

But host Breanna Keilar jumped in, saying, "So, we should note that actually downward revisions have been, on average, smaller since 2003. You've noted before that survey responses have decreased, but the initial jobs reports have actually been more accurate than 2003. And I just want to note that what the president is saying —"

Moore started to protest, but Keilar persisted.

"Stephen, let's just be clear what the president is saying here. It's something different. You know revisions — Stephen, can we just get to the heart of this matter? Because revisions are not unusual."

Our Analysis:

Rigged Reports or Rigged Narratives? The Saga Continues

In a recent spectacle that can only be described as a circus of the absurd, former President Trump and his economic sidekick, Stephen Moore, took to the airwaves to cry foul over the latest jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS). Trump, in his usual style, hastily dispatched the head of BLS, Erika McEntarfer, over what he deemed a "lackluster" jobs report, and took to Truth Social with claims that could make even the most gullible skeptic raise an eyebrow. His allegations? That the jobs report was "RIGGED" against him, echoing a rhetoric that has become all too familiar to anyone who has followed his career.

The Allegations

Trump's assertion, supported by Moore, that the jobs report was manipulated to favor "Radical Left Democrats" is a bold one, especially considering the lack of concrete evidence presented. Moore, who served as a senior economic advisor during Trump's term, claimed the numbers are "just not accurate" and that inaccuracies are worsening over time. He justified McEntarfer's firing not on political grounds, but on a purported decline in the quality of the BLS's work. Yet, these claims hold little water when scrutinized.

The Reality Check

CNN host Breanna Keilar provided the much-needed reality check, pointing out that downward revisions have actually been smaller on average since 2003 than claimed by Moore. She also noted that initial jobs reports have been more accurate than in the past, undermining Moore's argument about declining accuracy. This clash of narratives brings us to an essential realization: revisions in jobs reports are a standard practice, not a political conspiracy. It's a process of refinement and accuracy, a concept seemingly alien to Trump and Moore.

The Political Theater

What we're witnessing is not a genuine concern for economic accuracy but a theatrical display of political grievance. Trump's quick move to fire McEntarfer and his blast on social media smack of a vendetta against perceived enemies rather than a substantive critique of economic methodologies. Moore's backing of these claims, despite evidence to the contrary, suggests a loyalty to Trump's narrative over economic truths.

Conclusion: A Distorted Reality

The rhetoric espoused by Trump and Moore is a dangerous game. It seeks to undermine public trust in crucial institutions like the BLS, painting a picture of a deep-seated conspiracy where there is none. This tactic not only distracts from real issues but also erodes the foundation of factual discourse essential for democracy.

In the end, the jobs report saga is a stark reminder of the lengths to which some will go to distort reality for political gain. It's a narrative as rigged as the accusations thrown at the BLS, a testament to the systemic error in our political dialogue where facts are sacrificed at the altar of convenience and vendetta. As observers and participants in this democratic experiment, it's our duty to cut through the noise, demand evidence, and uphold the integrity of our institutions against such baseless assaults.
