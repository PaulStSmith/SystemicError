'Absolute cult': Mockery ensues as GOP rebrands 'MAGA accounts' for newborns after Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 23, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

House Republicans released amendments Wednesday night about their massive megabill, and a slew of name changes caught the eyes of critics, who likened the move to cult-like behavior.

The first page of the list of amendments included the following language: "Page 10, in the item relating to section 110115, strike 'MAGA' and insert 'Trump'."

The swap of "MAGA" for "Trump" appears to refer to House Republicans' proposal to include seed money to be posted to "MAGA accounts" for children. The idea came from Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX), and includes a pilot program to start the accounts with $1,000.

The newly unveiled change appears to edit "MAGA accounts" to "Trump accounts" for children.

A Rules Committee vote was expected Wednesday night, followed by overnight floor action, according to Politico.

The rule change led to swift mockery on social media.

"An absolute cult," chided Democratic strategist Chris D. Jackson on X.Jake Sherman

"They are renaming the 'MAGA Accounts' ... 'Trump Accounts,'" remarked Jake Sherman of Punchbowl News, sharing a screenshot of the bill and about 10 of the proposed edits.

"For f---s sake," reacted Fred Wellman, host of the On Democracy Podcast.

Congressional reporter Jamie Dupree appeared equally stunned. "Almost 5 full pages of the GOP changes are nothing but striking the word 'MAGA' and inserting the word 'Trump.' (I'm not kidding)" wrote Dupree. Post

The anti-Trump group The Lincoln Project surmised on X, "Are House Republicans so embarrassed by this bill that they are finding anyway to place the blame on @realDonaldTrump?"

Adam Michel, director of tax policy studies at the Cato Institute, wrote, "Just when you thought it could get stupider than 'MAGA Account,' they've decided to rename it to... 'Trump Account,'" adding a face-palm emoji.

Scott Lincicome, also of the Cato Institute, echoed the sentiment. "Ugh. They could've at least made it one of those tortured acronyms, like 'Tax-free Reserve for Unborn Mighty Patriots' or something."

"The 'MAGA Account' proposal meant to create savings accounts for newborns is now called 'Trump Trusts,'" clarified Kadia Goba of Semafor.

Our Analysis:

House Republicans: A Study in Cult-Like Branding

In a move that blurs the line between political allegiance and personality cult, House Republicans have sparked a firestorm with their latest legislative antics. The renaming of "MAGA Accounts" to "Trump Accounts" in their sprawling megabill is not just a superficial change of terms; it represents a deeper, more disconcerting trend in the party's dynamics.

The Cult of Personality

The switch from "MAGA" to "Trump" in the context of seed money accounts for children—initially proposed as a pilot program by Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) with a starting amount of $1,000—encapsulates the GOP's continued descent into what can only be described as cult-like behavior. The term "MAGA," itself a hallmark of Donald Trump's presidency, already carried connotations of a movement deeply intertwined with one man's ideology and persona. The decision to then replace "MAGA" with "Trump" cements the notion that the party's allegiance is not to a set of principles or policies but to the individual himself.

Critics, understandably, have not held back. The transition has been ridiculed across social media, with Democratic strategist Chris D. Jackson condemning it as an "absolute cult" and others, like Fred Wellman and Jamie Dupree, expressing disbelief and mockery. The sentiment is clear: This is not normal political behavior; it's the deification of a political figure to an extent that should concern any observer of democratic institutions.

A Reflection of Embarrassment or Strategy?

One might ponder whether this renaming exercise reflects a sense of embarrassment among House Republicans regarding the bill's provisions or a calculated attempt to shift any potential blame onto Trump. The Lincoln Project's suggestion that this is an effort to place blame on Trump could hold water, but it seems more plausible that this is yet another demonstration of the party's unwavering commitment to Trump's brand. This allegiance to the Trump name, rather than the broader conservative movement or set of policy ideals, speaks volumes about the current state of the Republican Party.

The Satirical Response

The reaction from figures like Adam Michel and Scott Lincicome of the Cato Institute underscores the absurdity of the situation. Michel's face-palm emoji is a succinct summary of the collective disbelief, while Lincicome's humorous suggestion for a more tortured acronym—like "Tax-free Reserve for Unborn Mighty Patriots"—highlights the missed opportunity for at least a semblance of cleverness in branding.

Kadia Goba of Semafor provides a necessary clarification amidst the chaos: these "MAGA Accounts," now "Trump Trusts," are intended as savings accounts for newborns. Yet, this detail almost gets lost in the absurdity of the branding exercise, underscoring how the spectacle of allegiance overshadows substantive policy discussion.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

The renaming of "MAGA Accounts" to "Trump Accounts" is emblematic of a larger, more dangerous trend within the GOP—a trend where personality cults overshadow policy, where allegiance to an individual trumps (pun intended) allegiance to democratic principles. This episode, while seemingly trivial in the grand scheme, is a vivid illustration of a party that has veered off the path of healthy political discourse into the realm of idolatry. It's not just a matter of semantics; it's a reflection of a systemic error within the party's ranks, one that poses a genuine threat to the principles of democracy and governance.
