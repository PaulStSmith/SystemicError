"Six months into his second term, President Donald Trump’s job approval rating has dipped to 37%, the lowest of this term and just slightly higher than his all-time worst rating of 34% at the end of his first term," the report Thursday said.

Trump is down 10% since January and 17% down with independent voters to just 29%. The latter number is the lowest it has ever been in his political career.

New Republic staff writer Greg Sargent pointed to key numbers: "Overall approval: 37% On immigration: 38% On economy: 37% On trade: 36%, Federal budget: 29%."

"Yet corporate media will still ignore this and still cover Trump's every move like his popular as they amplify his lies over and over," commented SiriusXMProgress host Dean Obeidallah on Bluesky.

"This is bad (not Michael Jackson bad)," explained longtime Democratic pollster Cornell Belcher. "And with just a 29% approval among Ind voters… Republicans are ... going to need to re-gerrymander a lot more than just Texas to stay in power."

"Very rare to see Trump's polling drop below 40. He’s at 37%," remarked Republican political consultant Mike Madrid on X.

Deputy editor of Inside Elections, Jacob Rubashkin, pointed out, "This is the rare survey where Trump's overall approval largely matches his approval on specific issues."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tumultuous Approval Ratings: A Deep Dive into the Decline

In the latest Gallup poll, the numbers don't lie, but they sure do scream a warning. President Donald Trump, in a startling downturn, has hit his lowest approval rating in his second term, with a mere 37% of Americans approving of his job performance. This isn't just a dip; it's a nosedive, especially considering the group leading this charge away from Trump is none other than independents—the very demographic that played a pivotal role in his 2024 victory.

The Independent Exodus

The figure that stands out, stark and perhaps foreboding for the Trump administration, is the 29% approval rating among independent voters. This isn't just a low point; it's an unprecedented plummet to the depths of unpopularity unseen in his entire political career. To frame this within the narrative of political loyalty and shifting sands, one must recognize the significance of independents turning their backs on a president they once supported. It's not just a shift; it's a seismic quake.

Breaking Down the Numbers

Greg Sargen of New Republic shines a light on a breakdown that should have Trump's team on high alert:

  • Overall approval at 37%

  • On immigration, a paltry 38%

  • Economy and trade fare no better at 37% and 36%, respectively

  • And the federal budget? A dismal 29%

What these numbers tell us is not just a story of declining approval but a narrative of widespread disapproval across several key issues.

The Media's Role: Amplification vs. Accountability

Dean Obeidallah's commentary hits a nerve that's been raw for quite some time: the corporate media's relentless coverage of Trump, irrespective of the substance (or lack thereof) of his actions and statements. This isn't just about giving airtime; it's about the media's role in amplifying a narrative, sometimes at the cost of drowning out critical voices and perspectives. The question that arises: Is the media serving the public interest, or is it merely the loudspeaker for the sensational?

Gerrymandering: The Desperate Grip on Power

Cornell Belcher's observation is both humorous and harrowing. The idea that Republicans might need to "re-gerrymander a lot more than just Texas to stay in power" is a stark commentary on the lengths to which political entities will go to cling to power, even when the tide of public opinion is against them. Gerrymandering, a dark art of political manipulation, underscores the systemic issues that plague American democracy.

A Rare Alignment: Overall vs. Specific Approval

Jacob Rubashkin's point that Trump's overall approval "largely matches his approval on specific issues" is a rarity and signals a comprehensive dissatisfaction with his governance. This alignment suggests that Trump's policies and his persona are uniformly viewed through a critical lens, a scenario that bodes ill for any incumbent seeking re-election.

In Conclusion: The Warning Bells Toll

The Gallup poll serves as a clarion call to the Trump administration and the Republican Party. With independent voters, a critical segment of the electorate, turning their backs, and approval ratings hitting historic lows across the board, the message is clear: Change course or face potential electoral oblivion.

In the face of these damning numbers, one wonders how the narrative will shift as we inch closer to the next electoral showdown. Will the GOP heed these warning signs, or will they double down on strategies and rhetoric that have alienated a significant portion of the American electorate? Only time will tell, but as we dissect these numbers, the path forward seems fraught with challenges for Trump and his team.
