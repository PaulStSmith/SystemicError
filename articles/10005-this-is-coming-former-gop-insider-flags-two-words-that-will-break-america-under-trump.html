'This is coming': Former GOP insider flags 'two words that will break America' under Trump

Donald Trump is about to bring down the U.S. with two simple words, according to an independent political strategist who worked on former President George W. Bush’s campaign.

Political strategist Steve Schmidt, who Saturday published an article in which he argued that Pete Hegseth is setting the stage for a potential “coup,” followed that up with a piece called, The domestic enemies of the US Constitution.

In that essay from Sunday, he argues that “Donald Trump seems to be agitating for an American Tiananmen Square in Portland, Oregon, where American troops open fire on American civilians with the weapons purchased by American taxpayers to defend the nation from threats to our liberty.”

Calling attention to Trump’s threats against Portland, Schmidt wrote, “Heil Trump!”

“Trump has menaced Portland by ‘authorizing Full Force’ with the ‘if necessary’ caveat venomously spit as an afterthought,” he added. “Illegal orders are coming to America’s soldiers, sailors, airmen and marines from their debauched, debased, corrupt, un-American and psychologically-addled civilian chain of command. The orders will be passed down, and someone, somewhere will be expected to pull the trigger — under false pretenses — against make-believe enemies who, like the soldiers who will kill them, are America’s sons and daughters.”

After noting, “There is no doubt about the extent of the Trump contamination in our society, and the ravaged American vulture that the MAGA cancer has eaten through,” Schmidt flags the two words he says will bring everything down.

“The jackboot is coming down. America is filled with fools and cowards who cheer their own demise, and call it greatness. All the while, the Democratic leaders in Washington, DC, continue to appease Trump, lose to Trump, and fear Trump. There are two words that will break America: open fire,” the analyst wrote. “This is coming.”

He then added, “Those words will be studied for all the rest of American history. Every detail of every person’s involvement who passed them forward and down the chain of command will be known — from Trump to Hegseth and on down, all the way to the men who light up an American city on orders from their superiors to kill their fellow citizens.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The Prophetic Warning of Steve Schmidt

Steve Schmidt, a political strategist with credentials stretching back to the George W. Bush era, has issued a stark, chilling warning in his latest essay titled, “The domestic enemies of the US Constitution.” Schmidt’s cautionary tale pivots around the ominous potential of former President Donald Trump to incite violence akin to an “American Tiananmen Square” in Portland, Oregon. This analysis aims to dissect Schmidt’s arguments, separating hyperbole from grounded concerns.

Trump’s Threats: A Prelude to Chaos

Schmidt’s portrayal of Donald Trump as a figure “agitating for an American Tiananmen Square” is not only alarming but also strategically evocative. By drawing parallels between Trump’s rhetoric and the infamous 1989 massacre in China, Schmidt effectively amplifies the gravity of Trump’s threats against Portland. The use of “Heil Trump!” further paints a dystopian image, likening Trump’s authoritarian tendencies to those of historical tyrants.

The claim that Trump has “menaced Portland by ‘authorizing Full Force’” underscores a disturbing narrative where the former president’s words and actions potentially lay the groundwork for violent confrontations between military personnel and civilians. Schmidt’s assertion that these orders would be “illegal” and stem from a “debauched, debased, corrupt, un-American and psychologically-addled civilian chain of command” is a bold indictment of Trump’s leadership and its impact on American governance and society.

The MAGA Cancer and Democratic Inaction

Schmidt does not limit his critique to Trump and his direct supporters. He extends his scathing analysis to the broader societal and political landscape, referring to the “MAGA cancer” that has purportedly eroded American values and unity. This metaphor underscores the perceived depth of Trump’s influence, suggesting that it has metastasized beyond mere political discourse into the very fabric of American life.

Equally significant is Schmidt’s criticism of Democratic leaders, whom he accuses of appeasing and fearing Trump. By asserting that Democrats “continue to appease Trump, lose to Trump, and fear Trump,” Schmidt highlights a perceived failure of opposition forces to effectively counter Trump’s agenda. This criticism points to a broader issue of political paralysis and the inability of Democratic leaders to mobilize a robust response to Trump’s provocations.

“Open Fire”: The Two Words That Could Break America

The climax of Schmidt’s argument centers on the two words he believes could precipitate a national catastrophe: “open fire.” This phrase, according to Schmidt, symbolizes the potential for a direct order to military personnel to engage civilians in violent conflict. The assertion that these words “will be studied for all the rest of American history” amplifies the perceived magnitude of such an event, framing it as a pivotal moment that could redefine the American experience.

Schmidt’s warning is clear: the authorization of violence against American citizens by American soldiers, on orders from higher-ups loyal to Trump’s directives, would represent an unprecedented breach of the social contract and a direct assault on the principles of liberty and democracy.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

Steve Schmidt’s essay serves as a grim prognostication of what might be if certain forces within American politics are left unchecked. While some may argue that Schmidt’s portrayal verges on the hyperbolic, it is undeniably grounded in a profound concern for the nation’s democratic institutions and principles.

The invocation of “open fire” as the two words that could unravel the fabric of American society is a powerful, if unsettling, reminder of the fragility of democracy. It underscores the imperative for vigilance, accountability, and a recommitment to the values that underpin the American republic.

In dissecting Schmidt’s argument, it becomes evident that the greatest danger lies not in the capacity for violence per se, but in the erosion of democratic norms and the potential for authoritarianism to take root in American soil. The real systemic error, then, may not be in the actions of a single man or administration but in the collective failure to defend the principles that define America.
