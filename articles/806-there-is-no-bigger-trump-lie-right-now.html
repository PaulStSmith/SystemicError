There is no bigger Trump lie right now

Based on an article by Phyllis Bennis

Paulo SantosJune 15, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Real Ceasefire Is the One Washington Refuses to Enforce

Power, Not Confusion

The source story offers a narrow event: Trump calls Netanyahu, Netanyahu escalates in Lebanon, and commentators obsess over whether Israel will obey. That framing is already a dodge. The actual power relation is obvious. The United States funds, arms, and shields Israel; Israel uses that patronage to expand its regional violence; both leaders use “ceasefire” language as cover while keeping the machinery of war running.

The question is not whether Netanyahu is hard to manage. The question is why Trump keeps pretending he is powerless to stop the escalation when the American state is the one paying for it.

The Missing Threat

The article’s sharpest detail is also its most revealing omission from the public script: Trump did not threaten to cut off weapons and money. He called Netanyahu, reportedly cursed at him, and stopped there. That is not restraint. It is complicity dressed up as frustration.

This is how imperial power launders itself. The president performs anger at an ally while preserving the material conditions that make the ally dangerous. If Washington wanted Israel to stand down, it has a pressure point. The refusal to use it is the policy.

War as a Political Asset

Netanyahu needs the war because ceasefire exposes weakness. The source makes that plain. His career has been built on selling Iran as an existential menace and on presenting himself as the only man willing to keep the region under Israeli control. Endless war keeps that persona intact.

Trump has his own incentives. He wants the spectacle of domination, the fantasy of a president who can bend the Middle East to his will, and the ego boost of appearing tougher than Obama on Iran. In other words, the war is not a strategic accident. It is a political instrument for men who confuse destruction with authority.

The Language of Misdirection

Coverage that treats Israeli escalation as a mystery, or Trump’s phone call as a breakthrough, obscures the real arrangement. The source notes that commentators focused on whether Netanyahu would “abide” by Trump’s demand, while the actual enforcement mechanism never entered the frame. That is not just incomplete reporting. It is blame misdirection.

The same trick appears in the broader war narrative. The stated reasons keep shifting: nuclear threat, missiles, allies, shipping lanes, regime change, civilian protection. That instability is not evidence of uncertainty. It is evidence of a war searching for a justification after the decision to wage it was already made.

The Pattern Beneath the Bombs

What the article calls “mowing the grass” is the governing logic, not a metaphor. Repeated punishment of Gaza, Lebanon, and now Iran is how Israel signals hierarchy: this is who can strike, who can be struck, and who is expected to absorb it. The United States supplies the cost structure and the diplomatic insulation. Together they turn brute force into routine administration.

That same impunity is visible in Gaza, where the source cites tens of thousands of named dead and continuing starvation, and in Lebanon, where “ceasefire” coexists with bombing. The pattern is not an overreaction to security threats. It is the use of overwhelming violence to preserve regional and imperial rank.

The System Speaks Plainly

The story is not about a rogue ally testing a president’s patience. It is about two powers using war to preserve hegemony while pretending their hands are tied. Trump can end Israel’s escalation with one real threat. Netanyahu can keep escalating because Washington would rather stage disagreement than enforce discipline.

That is the system in plain sight: occupation financed as partnership, massacre narrated as complexity, and restraint performed only when power is meant to look innocent.
