Trump woos an ally that gave his monstrous role model an exile of obscene luxury

The Coup Is Administrative

One Judge, One Breach

A federal judge in Massachusetts temporarily blocked Trump’s people from trying to seize control of mail-in ballots through the Postal Board of Governors. That is the immediate story. The real story is uglier: a sitting president and his political machine are testing how much of the election infrastructure they can bend before anyone stops them.

This is not about paperwork or process. It is about a power grab aimed at the mechanics of voting itself, with Republicans in the executive branch trying to manufacture authority that neither the Constitution nor Congress gave them.

Personalism With American Paperwork

The source gets the larger frame right: this is personalist rule. Power is no longer organized around institutions with independent force. It is organized around one man’s grudges, appetites, and self-protection, with agencies turned into instruments of personal retaliation.

That is why the examples matter. The Justice Department becomes a revenge shop. The IRS is blunted. The Pentagon, FBI, and ICE are folded into the same political logic. The point is not policy drift. The point is conversion: public institutions are being repurposed to protect the ruler and punish enemies.

The Lie Is “Confusion”

There is nothing confused about indicting Letitia James with a prosecutor who had no real prosecutorial experience. There is nothing accidental about reopening attacks on E. Jean Carroll after juries found Trump liable. There is nothing innocent about federal investigations into nonprofits and journalists after they are labeled threats.

This is deliberate harm dressed up as law enforcement. The misdirection is the standard authoritarian trick: make coercion look like procedure, make revenge look like governance, and make institutional abuse sound like a technical dispute. The source is right to call this out. The harder truth is that the system is already doing too much of the regime’s work for it.

The Enablers Are Not Secondary

Republicans in Congress are not bystanders. They are the enabling infrastructure. They have the tools to check this and are refusing to use them. Their silence is not neutrality; it is collaboration with conditions.

The same goes for the billionaire class now lining up to subsidize the racket. When the source notes crypto scams enriching Trump, tax enforcement being gutted, and donor privilege being rewarded with state favor, it is describing a familiar arrangement: public power extracts from ordinary people while insulating the rich. This is not corruption at the margins. It is the operating model.

The Taxpayer Is Paying for the Harassment

Trump’s administration is not merely looking the other way at abuse; it is underwriting it. That includes the harassment of E. Jean Carroll with public resources, the shielding of tax cheats, and the hollowing out of the IRS so low-income people get audited while the wealthy get protected.

That is the real class logic here. The state is being stripped of its ability to collect from the powerful and redirected toward punishing the weak, the critical, and the inconvenient. ICE belongs in that same category of coercive machinery. It does not need a better trainer or cleaner branding. It needs to be abolished.

The Only Remaining Check

The source is right that elections remain the last nonviolent check when prosecutors are captured, tax collectors neutered, reporters cowed, and dissenters branded as threats. But that is not a comforting statement. It is a sign of how far the damage has already gone.

What this episode reveals is not just Trump’s ambition. It is the fragility of democratic institutions when one party decides it would rather share in the loot than defend the system. The lesson is systemic: authoritarianism advances when institutional power is treated as personal property, and when the people with the authority to stop it decide fear is safer than responsibility.
