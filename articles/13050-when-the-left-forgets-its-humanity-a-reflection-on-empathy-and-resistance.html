When the Left Forgets Its Humanity: A Reflection on Empathy and Resistance

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 6, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

In a recent video titled "Dear People On The Left...", a content creator issued a sobering plea to his fellow progressives. His message? Stop celebrating the deaths of children and innocent people just because they lived in a red state or had MAGA parents. It was not a call to abandon our values or mute our anger—it was a reminder to remain human.

The video responds to some truly vile online comments reacting to a deadly flood in Texas. In the aftermath, certain self-identified leftists took to social media with gleeful cruelty, writing things like, "Serves them right," or "These kids would have grown up to be MAGA anyway." Others mocked grieving parents, saying things like, "This is what your god gave you for voting for Trump."

Let’s be absolutely clear: This is not resistance. This is rot. This is the left becoming the very thing it despises.

Godless Gardener, who identifies as someone who grew up in a conservative household but found his way out, offered the most important insight of the video: "That’s how I grew up... and I didn’t stay that way." He reminds us that kids aren’t born MAGA. They are shaped, misinformed, indoctrinated. And they can change. They do change. But not if they’re dead. Not if we treat them as collateral damage in a war of ideology.

There’s a seductive righteousness that can come with punching down at the cruel, especially after years of GOP gaslighting, violence, and vitriol. When COVID-deniers finally succumbed to the virus they said was a hoax, many of us felt a twinge of satisfaction. But even the speaker, who admits to sharing that sentiment at times, called it what it was: dirty. Uncomfortable. And ultimately corrosive.

He draws a crucial distinction: If a person invokes divine intervention to brag about their survival, then yes, challenge them. Ask why their god spared them while their neighbors died. Point out the hypocrisy. But when someone is freshly grieving the loss of a child? That is not the time for a theological gotcha.

This isn’t about coddling fascists. This is about not becoming them.

We love to say, "If fascism comes to America, it will be wrapped in the flag and carrying a cross." But what if it also comes cloaked in moral smugness and meme-worthy cruelty from our side? What if the poison isn't just in Trumpism, but in the satisfaction some of us feel when MAGA families suffer?

The speaker reminds us of how MAGA came to be: alienation. People who felt ignored, disrespected, looked down on. Yes, they were lied to. Yes, they were manipulated by billionaires and propaganda networks. But that doesn’t erase the fact that they turned to the right because they didn’t feel welcome anywhere else. If the left now greets tragedy with mockery and celebration, we're helping build the next generation of MAGA—brick by brick, post by post.

It is hard to maintain empathy in the face of relentless cruelty. It is tempting to believe that the only language some people understand is pain. But as the speaker says, if we become just like them, we’re not the resistance. We’re the sequel.

Empathy is not weakness. It is not surrender. It is our firewall against the very barbarism we claim to oppose. Without it, our politics become performance. Our rage becomes cruelty. And our vision of a better world collapses under the weight of its own hypocrisy.

So no, we should not rejoice when children die—even if they were born into families who voted for monsters. We fight the monsters. Not their kids. We argue, we organize, we vote, we march. But we do not cheer graves.

Because if we do, we lose something far more important than an argument.

We lose ourselves.
