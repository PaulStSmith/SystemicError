'Hoo boy!' Supreme Court observers pounce as Kagan 'rips' Trump lawyer 'to shreds'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 16, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Supreme Court's Clash Over Birthright Citizenship

U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer and Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan (CNN screenshot)

Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan was relentless in her questioning of U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer Thursday as he presented the Trump administration argument to end birthright citizenship in the United States.

As part of his extreme immigration policy, President Donald Trump issued an executive order at the start of his second term proclaiming that children born in the U.S. to undocumented immigrants are not citizens.

Three federal judges ruled the order unconstitutional, which lead to the Supreme Court case.

Before the oral arguments, Trump claimed on Truth Social, "Birthright Citizenship is about the babies of slaves" and not about "people pouring into our Country from all over South America, and the rest of the World."

Kagan asked Sauer if "every single person" affected by Trump's executive order will have to "bring their own suit" before the court. After he attempted an answer, Kagan told Sauer to put his main argument aside, "because, I got to tell you, that does not fill me with great confidence. How else are we going to get to the right result here, which is on my assumption that the [executive order] is illegal?"

"That would be a profoundly wrong result," Sauer answered, before diving into the "1789 Judiciary Act."

"I mean, that's a lot of words," Kagan said, "and I don't have an answer for if one thinks — and, you know, look. There are all kinds of abuses of nationwide injunctions. But I think that the question that this case presents is that if one thinks that, it's quite clear that the [executive order] is illegal, how does one get to that result? In what time frame on your set of rules without the possibility of a nationwide injunction?"

Sauer was mid-sentence talking about nationwide injunctions, when Justice Amy Coney Barrett interrupted.

"Are you really going to answer Justice Kagan by saying, there's no way to do this expeditiously?"

Later, Kagan declared, "I'm suggesting that in a case in which the government is losing constantly… It's up to you to decide whether to take this case to us. If I were in your shoes, there is no way I'd approach the Supreme Court with this case."

Court observers on social media were stunned by the way the administration's argument was tanking.

Journalist Ed Krassenstein posted, "In the Trump administration's SCOTUS hearing where they are trying to overturn Birthright Citizenship, Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan calls out and criticizes the Trump administration for not respecting court decisions. We are a nation of laws! Don’t forget that!"

Meanwhile, @mmpadellan , with 1.3 million followers wrote, "Justice Kagan is ripping this dude Sauer to shreds in this Birthright Citizenship case, hoo boy!"

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Supreme Court's Clash Over Birthright Citizenship

In an era where the political landscape often seems more surreal than substantive, the Supreme Court's examination of birthright citizenship under the Trump administration offers a sharp reminder of the stakes involved. The article at hand delves into Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan's rigorous interrogation of U.S. Solicitor General D. John Sauer regarding the Trump administration's push to end birthright citizenship—a move that not only challenges established norms but also the constitution itself.

Trump's Executive Order: A Blatant Disregard for Constitutional Rights

At the core of this legal battle is President Donald Trump's executive order aiming to strip away citizenship from children born in the U.S. to undocumented immigrants. Labeling this policy as an "extreme immigration policy" is to understate its potential impact on the very fabric of American values and its disregard for the Fourteenth Amendment. Trump's justification, bizarrely tying the issue to “the babies of slaves,” not only misrepresents the historical context of birthright citizenship but also attempts to muddy the waters of a clear-cut constitutional guarantee.

Kagan's Interrogation: A Beacon of Judicial Scrutiny

Justice Elena Kagan's relentless questioning shines as a beacon of judicial scrutiny and responsibility. Her direct challenge to the administration's legal rationale—or the apparent lack thereof—exemplifies the Court's role in checking executive overreach. By pressing Sauer on the practical implications of the executive order and the enormity of its potential illegality, Kagan not only underscores the shaky ground on which the Trump administration stands but also highlights the broader implications of such a policy on individuals and the legal system.

Sauer's Response: A Symphony of Evasion

Solicitor General D. John Sauer's responses, as depicted in the article, come across as evasive and insufficiently grounded in a robust legal argument. His resort to the "1789 Judiciary Act" and the discussion of nationwide injunctions, while perhaps relevant, sidestep the core issue: the executive order's direct conflict with the Constitution. Justice Amy Coney Barrett's interjection further emphasizes the administration's struggle to defend the indefensible in a coherent, expedited manner.

Public Reaction: A Mirror to the Nation's Conscience

The reaction from court observers and social media commentators underscores the wider public's engagement and concern with this case. The portrayal of Justice Kagan's interrogation as a dismantling of the administration's argument captures the essence of what many see as a battle for the soul of American constitutional values.

Conclusion: A Test of Constitutional Allegiance

This Supreme Court case transcends the immediate legal arguments at hand, touching upon the foundational principles of the United States. The Trump administration's attempt to redefine birthright citizenship is not only a legal aberration but a direct challenge to the country's commitment to its Constitution. Justice Kagan's probing, as reported, does not merely aim to dissect a legal argument but to safeguard the integrity of American law and values. The outcome of this case could very well resonate through the annals of U.S. legal history, marking a moment when the judiciary was called upon to reaffirm the nation's allegiance to its founding principles.
