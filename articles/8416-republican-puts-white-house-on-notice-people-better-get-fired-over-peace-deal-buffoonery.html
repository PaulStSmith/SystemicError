Republican puts White House on notice: 'People better get fired' over peace deal 'buffoonery'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Fallout from a Controversial Peace Plan: A Closer Look

Rep. Don Bacon of Nebraska on May 16, 2024 (Philip Yabut/Shutterstock.com)

Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE), who sits on the House Armed Services Committee, on Saturday demanded “some people ... get fired” over the fallout from a proposed peace plan to end the war in Ukraine.

Bacon was specifically referring to a statement from State Department Principal Deputy Spokesperson Tommy Pigott, who on Saturday refuted claims made by U.S. senators at a press conference in Halifax, Nova Scotia.

According to Sens. Angus King (I-ME) and Mike Rounds (R-SD), Secretary of State Marco Rubio distanced the United States from the proposed plan, telling senators the leaked 28-point plan “is not of the administration’s position.”

The senators cited a phone call from Rubio which “came at their request,” Politico reports.

“Rubio, they said, agreed to walk them through the situation and gave the lawmakers permission to describe what he told them,” according to Politico.

In a statement on X, King described the plan as “essentially the wish list of the Russians.”

“This is blatantly false,” Pigott wrote Saturday on X in response to King’s tweet. “As Secretary Rubio and the entire administration has consistently maintained, this plan was authored by the United States, with input from both the Russians and Ukrainians.”

Responding to Pigott’s statement, Bacon called the peace plan fallout “gross buffoonery“ and demanded accountability for the saga.

“Some people better get fired on Monday for the gross buffoonery we just witnessed over the last four days,” he wrote on X.

“This hurt our country and undermined our alliances, and encouraged our adversaries,” Bacon added.

Bacon had previously spoken out against the leaked plan, which Politico reports sparked alarm among lawmakers as “global leaders railed] against it.”

“President Trump’s plan to force Ukraine to give up more territory, to cut its Army by more than half, to never join NATO nor let foreign troops in its territory is an abomination,” Bacon wrote Friday on X. “Freedom loving Americans must tell the President that we reject the worst appeasement seen since 1938. ... What makes President [Donald] Trump think we can now trust [Russian President Vladimir] Putin? This agreement weakens Ukraine and leaves them vulnerable to new Russian invasions in the years to come.”

Our Analysis:

The Fallout from a Controversial Peace Plan: A Closer Look

In a drama that reads like a poorly scripted reality TV show, Rep. Don Bacon (R-NE) has taken to social media to call for heads to roll over the contentious and confusing saga of a proposed peace plan to end the war in Ukraine. This political theatre, complete with accusations, denials, and public outrage, underscores the chaotic nature of foreign policy under scrutiny. But let’s dissect the layers of this debacle and what it signifies about the current state of American diplomacy.

Firstly, we have State Department Principal Deputy Spokesperson Tommy Pigott attempting to quash claims made by U.S. senators that seemingly put the United States at odds with its own proposed peace plan. Senators Angus King (I-ME) and Mike Rounds (R-SD) relayed information from Secretary of State Marco Rubio, suggesting the U.S. distanced itself from the leaked 28-point plan, which they labeled a “Russian wish list.” Here, the narrative begins to twist, turning into a he-said, she-said spectacle that would be entertaining if it weren’t so gravely serious.

Pigott’s rebuttal, claiming the plan was actually “authored by the United States, with input from both the Russians and Ukrainians,” throws a wrench into the already muddled narrative. This statement, meant to clarify, only muddies the waters further, raising questions about the nature of the U.S.’s diplomatic efforts and the true beneficiaries of such a plan.

Enter Rep. Don Bacon, who labels the entire episode “gross buffoonery” and demands accountability. Bacon’s outrage is not without merit; the public spat and contradictory statements indeed undermine the credibility of U.S. diplomacy and could potentially weaken alliances and embolden adversaries. However, Bacon’s previous condemnation of the leaked plan as an “abomination” and a form of appeasement eerily reminiscent of 1938 suggests a deeper ideological rift within the American political landscape about how to handle aggression from autocratic regimes.

The crux of the issue lies in the alleged contents of the peace plan, as described by Bacon: a proposal that would force Ukraine to cede territory, drastically reduce its military capabilities, and abandon aspirations of NATO membership, effectively leaving the nation vulnerable to future aggression. If true, such terms would indeed represent a significant victory for Vladimir Putin and a stark betrayal of the principles of freedom and sovereignty that the United States purports to defend.

What This Saga Reveals

  1. Conflicting Narratives: The discrepancy between what was reportedly communicated to senators by Marco Rubio and the State Department’s subsequent denial highlights a concerning lack of coherence in presenting U.S. foreign policy positions. Such disarray serves only to confuse allies and embolden adversaries.

  2. Ideological Divisions: The sharp critique by Bacon, particularly his comparison of the plan to 1938 appeasement policies, underscores the deep ideological divides over how to address Russian aggression. This division complicates the formulation of a cohesive and principled foreign policy stance.

  3. The Need for Transparency and Accountability: Bacon’s demand for firings over the debacle points to a broader issue of accountability in foreign policy decisions and communications. When proposals of such magnitude are leaked and debated in public forums without clear official stances, it erodes trust in the institutions responsible for safeguarding national security and international relations.

In conclusion, this episode is emblematic of the challenges facing American foreign policy: the struggle to maintain a unified front, the battle over ideological approaches to international conflicts, and the imperative for transparency and accountability. As this drama unfolds, one can only hope that clarity, principle, and a steadfast commitment to democratic values will prevail over confusion, inconsistency, and appeasement.
