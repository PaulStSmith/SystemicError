A reporter on Saturday flagged what she said is “the biggest thing that went wrong” with the Trump administration’s release of new Jeffrey Epstein files.

Fallon Gallagher, an award-winning legal affairs reporter for MS NOW, appeared on the network over the weekend. The host asked, “The DOJ rollout of these documents, it did not go as the law has required it to go. What went wrong? And what do you think we can expect to see and when?”

According to the reporter, “The biggest thing that went wrong here is that the DOJ was supposed to release all of the Epstein files by last night at 11:59. That has not happened.”

“They signaled that that wasn’t going to happen on time. So inherently, they’re not following the law,” she added. “Now, what we’ve learned today is that one of those photos, at least one of those photos appears to be deleted. There is a photo that is no longer on DOJ’s website that included a picture of a drawer with a number of framed photos. One of the photos that’s in the drawer is that famous photo that we’ve seen a number of times of Donald Trump, Melania Trump, Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell. That photo has mysteriously disappeared. We do not know why that photo has disappeared. We have asked the DOJ why it’s disappeared. We do not know yet. So that has now gone away.”

She further added, “The big thing about this entire release, though, is that there really isn’t that much new that we’re learning. I would say that this is more of a compilation rather than a full release. Basically, they’ve taken public files that have been from court documents, FOIA requests, House oversight drops, and compiled it all in one place with a number of new files from this, this new law.”

Our Analysis:

Delving into the DOJ’s Dubious Handling of the Epstein Files

The drama surrounding the Trump administration’s release of new Jeffrey Epstein files has taken a turn for the worse, as highlighted in a recent segment featuring Fallon Gallagher, an award-winning legal affairs reporter for MS NOW. As Gallagher pointed out, the Department of Justice’s (DOJ) mishandling of these documents not only undermines legal requirements but also raises alarming questions about transparency and accountability within the government.

The Missed Deadline: A Blatant Disregard for Legal Obligations

First and foremost, the DOJ’s failure to release all of the Epstein files by the stipulated deadline of 11:59 on the previous night is a clear violation of the law. Gallagher rightly emphasizes this point, stating, “They signaled that that wasn’t going to happen on time. So inherently, they’re not following the law.” This disregard for legal deadlines is not just a minor oversight; it’s indicative of a systemic failure to adhere to the principles of transparency and accountability that are supposed to underpin government operations.

The Mysterious Disappearance of a Controversial Photo

Even more troubling is the disappearance of a specific photo from the DOJ’s website - a photo showing Donald Trump, Melania Trump, Jeffrey Epstein, and Ghislaine Maxwell. The mysterious vanishing of this photo raises significant concerns about the manipulation of public records. Gallagher’s inability to get a straightforward answer from the DOJ about this disappearance is a glaring red flag. It suggests a possible attempt to whitewash or sanitize the historical record, particularly given the high-profile nature of the individuals involved.

A Compilation, Not a Revelation

Gallagher’s observation that the release is more of a compilation of previously available documents rather than a revelatory disclosure of new information is another critical point. It begs the question: what was the purpose of this release if not to provide the public with new insights into the Epstein case? This approach diminishes the potential impact of the release, reducing it to a mere reorganization of existing information rather than a meaningful contribution to public understanding.

Conclusion: A Missed Opportunity for Transparency

The DOJ’s handling of the Epstein files release represents a missed opportunity for the Trump administration to demonstrate a commitment to transparency and accountability. Instead, the process has been marred by legal non-compliance, suspicious omissions, and a lack of new, substantive information. This debacle underscores the need for a more rigorous and transparent approach to the release of government documents, especially those pertaining to cases of significant public interest.

The public deserves better. The handling of the Epstein files should serve as a sobering reminder of the importance of holding our institutions accountable and demanding higher standards of transparency. Without it, we risk eroding the very foundations of trust and integrity upon which our system is built.
