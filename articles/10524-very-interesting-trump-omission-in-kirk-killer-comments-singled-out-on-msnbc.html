'Very interesting' Trump omission in Kirk killer comments singled out on MSNBC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unsettling Silence: Trump, Kirk's Tragedy, and the FBI's Shaky Leadership

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Reviewing what has transpired since far-right conservative Charlie Kirk was gunned down on a Utah college campus on Wednesday and his alleged killer was taken into custody on Friday, one MSNBC host pointed to comments made by Donald Trump in the aftermath and what was missing.

Discussing what followed after 22-year-old Tyler Robinson was taken into custody, “The Weekend “ co-host Jonathan Capehart engaged Politico’s Rachel Bade on what to make of criticism of FBI Director Kash Patel’s performance and if the controversial Trump appointee still has the confidence of the White House.

“He's [Patel] absolutely seen an erosion with support with the base,” Bade remarked and later added, “People are not happy and you can see it if you look on Twitter, a lot of conservative commentators on Twitter who are saying it's time for us, people who support the Trump administration to have a discussion about whether he's the right person to lead the FBI.”

“So what will it matter, I guess is the question,” she continued. “It didn't matter with Attorney General Pam Bondi. I mean, we saw even more heat from her on [Jeffrey] Epstein and the president stuck with her. So all that really matters is what Donald Trump thinks and it's not yet clear if Trump would be displeased with him at this point. They're still putting out statements for a lot of these stories saying that they're with him.“

Noting a New York Times story on Patel’s shaky status, Capehart pointed out, “In that story that it was noted that when the president was talking about, I think it was the capture of the suspect, he talked about, he mentioned everybody, particularly Pam Bondi didn't mention the FBI director. “

That's really interesting,” Bade conceded.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Silence: Trump, Kirk's Tragedy, and the FBI's Shaky Leadership

In the wake of the shocking assassination of far-right conservative Charlie Kirk on a Utah college campus, the political discourse has spiraled into a cacophony of blame, speculation, and unnerving silence from certain quarters. Particularly, former President Donald Trump's response—or lack thereof—regarding FBI Director Kash Patel's handling of the situation has become a focal point of scrutiny.

Charlie Kirk's death, a seismic event by any measure, has been overshadowed not by a collective reflection on the state of our political discourse but by the political machinations and the bizarre theatre of loyalty within the Trump circle. MSNBC host Jonathan Capehart's conversation with Politico’s Rachel Bade sheds light on a peculiar kind of chaos that has ensued, one where the performance of key figures like Kash Patel is questioned not on the basis of efficacy or ethics, but on their standing with Trump and his base.

Kash Patel's tenure as FBI Director was always going to be controversial, given his appointment by Trump, a figure synonymous with polarizing leadership. However, the real meat of this issue isn't just about Patel's performance, which Bade suggests has seen "an erosion with support with the base." It's about the absurdity of measuring the effectiveness of the head of the Federal Bureau of Investigation through the fickle lens of social media sentiment and political allegiance.

The conversation between Capehart and Bade, while insightful, skirts around a larger, more disturbing reality. The implication that Trump's opinion is the be-all and end-all for Patel's future, even in the aftermath of such a significant failure of public safety, is indicative of a broader systemic dysfunction. It's a stark reminder of the dangers of politicizing institutions that should operate independently of the political winds and whims.

Donald Trump's selective silence is particularly telling. In his failure to mention Patel while acknowledging others, like Pam Bondi, in the context of Kirk's assassination and its aftermath, Trump signals where his priorities lie. It's not about the integrity of the investigation, the tragedy of the loss, or the implications for public safety. No, it seems to be about loyalty, optics, and political calculus.

What's equally frustrating is the media's role in perpetuating this narrative. The focus on Trump's reactions—or lack thereof—underscores a fixation on the spectacle over the substance. The real story here should be about addressing the causes of such violence, the safety of public spaces, and the integrity of our national security apparatus. Instead, we're left dissecting Trump's tweets and the implications of his silence.

In a sane world, the assassination of a public figure like Charlie Kirk would engender a unified call to action, a collective introspection into how our political rhetoric might fuel such acts of violence. Yet, here we are, parsing through the implications of Trump's social media strategy and what it means for Kash Patel's career.

The entire situation is a damning indictment of where we are as a society and the systemic errors that continue to plague our political and institutional landscapes. It's time to move beyond the spectacle and address the underlying issues at hand. But, as long as figures like Trump continue to wield outsized influence over the narrative, it's hard to see how we can make meaningful progress.
