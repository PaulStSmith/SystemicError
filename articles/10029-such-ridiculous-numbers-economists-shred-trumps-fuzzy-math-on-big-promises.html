‘Such ridiculous numbers’: Economists shred Trump’s ‘fuzzy math’ on big promises

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 28, 20254 min read

FILE PHOTO: U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks on tariffs in the Rose Garden at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 2, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria/File Photo

Multiple economists laid into President Donald Trump Saturday for his “fuzzy math” cited alongside his many lofty promises or supposed achievements, including one who called Trump’s cited figures “meaningless.”

Speaking with Axios in a report published Saturday, Wayne Winegarden, a senior fellow in business and economics at Pacific Research Institute, took a dive into Trump’s staggering claim that his tariff policy was generating $17 trillion in revenue.

“We could just say it’ll raise a gazillion dollars,” Winegarden said, adding that Trump’s purported figures don’t account for cost-increases and revised revenue outlooks sparked by increased tariffs. “These are such ridiculous numbers that they’re just as meaningless as that.”

Trump has routinely made lofty promises, many including specific figures, with another example being his pledge last week that his administration would be “reducing drug costs over the next year… by not 50% or 60%, [but] by 1,000%.”

“The Administration is committed to ensuring that drug companies make wealthy countries pay fairer rates for drugs and stop relying on Americans to subsidize the vast majority of global pharma research and development,” said Kush Desai, White House spokesperson, in an email to Axios.

That same White House spokesperson added that Trump was correct in his assertion that his policies would, in fact, see drug costs reduced by 1,000% over the next year.

But for William Padula, a health economist at the University of Southern California, Trump’s claim carried no “mathematical explanation.”

“I don’t have any mathematical explanation for that, other than we’d have to reconfigure our entire health system and infrastructure in order to operate more like other countries to get there,” Padula said.

Trump’s so-called “reciprocal tariffs” were resumed Aug. 7 following a 90-day pause, and with the economic impactsalready being feltacross the American economy ahead of their implementation; prices have increased, job growth has slowed dramatically and inflation has ticked up.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Economic Promises: A Masterclass in Fuzzy Math

In a recent Axios report, it seems President Donald Trump has once again dazzled us with his unparalleled ability to conjure financial figures from the ether. This time, his claims have drawn not just skepticism but outright derision from a cadre of economists. Trump’s assertions, ranging from the fantastical revenue generation of his tariff policy to the mind-boggling promise of reducing drug costs by 1,000%, have been scrutinized and found wanting in both substance and sanity.

The $17 Trillion Tariff Fantasy

Wayne Winegarden, a senior fellow in business and economics, delivered a scathing critique of Trump’s claim that his tariff policy would generate $17 trillion in revenue. Winegarden’s response was both blunt and dismissive, likening Trump’s figures to saying it’ll raise “a gazillion dollars.” The core issue here is not just the absurdity of the number but the blatant disregard for the economic repercussions such as cost increases and revised revenue outlooks that tariffs inevitably bring. Trump’s numbers, devoid of any grounding in economic reality, are as meaningless as they are misleading.

The 1,000% Drug Cost Reduction Pipe Dream

Trump’s pledge to slash drug costs not by 50%, not by 60%, but by 1,000% over the next year, is another testament to his penchant for fuzzy math. The White House, through spokesperson Kush Desai, doubled down on this claim, asserting Trump’s policies would indeed reduce drug costs by this fantastical figure. Yet, William Padula, a health economist at the University of Southern California, highlighted the utter lack of a mathematical basis for such a claim. Padula’s insight sheds light on the sheer implausibility of achieving such a feat without a complete overhaul of the U.S. health system to mirror those of other countries—a monumental task that Trump’s administration has neither planned for nor is likely capable of executing.

The Economic Consequences of Reciprocal Tariffs

Trump’s resumption of “reciprocal tariffs” on August 7, following a 90-day pause, has already begun to send shockwaves through the American economy. The effects—ranging from price increases to slowed job growth and rising inflation—are tangible demonstrations of the negative impacts of Trump’s economic policies. These consequences starkly contrast the rosy picture painted by his lofty promises and unfounded claims.

Conclusion: The Reality Behind the Rhetoric

In dissecting Trump’s economic promises, a clear pattern emerges: a detachment from economic reality, underpinned by a strategy of using exaggerated figures and promises to overshadow the tangible negative outcomes of his policies. This approach not only misleads the public but also undermines the integrity of the office he holds. The real cost of Trump’s fuzzy math—economic instability, increased living costs, and a future saddled with the repercussions of these policies—is too high a price for Americans to pay.

In essence, the economic fantasies peddled by Trump serve as a profound disservice to the American people, who deserve policies grounded in reality, not rhetoric. As these economists have made clear, it’s time for a return to sanity and sound economic policymaking.
