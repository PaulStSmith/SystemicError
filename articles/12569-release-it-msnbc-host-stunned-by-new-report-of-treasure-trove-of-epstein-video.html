'Release it!' MSNBC host stunned by new report of treasure trove of Epstein video

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 20, 20254 min read

MSNBC host Jonathan Capehart (MSNBC screenshot)

Reacting to a report that Steve Bannon is reportedly sitting on hours of raw footage where he interviewed accused pedophile Jeffrey Epstein, a stunned and excited MSNBC host blurted "Release it!" on Saturday morning.

Appearing on MSNBC's "The Weekend," NBC reporter Vaughan Hillyard filled in co-hosts Jonathan Capehart and Eugene Daniels on his reporting that Bannon had planned a documentary on Epstein and has a reported 15 to 16 hours of video of interviews with him before he died while in custody.

As Hilyard wrote, "Mark Epstein said that Bannon, the MAGA media personality, worked with his brother, then a convicted sex offender, after the Miami Herald in 2018 chronicled victims’ details of abuse by Jeffrey Epstein. The two men filmed together before Epstein’s arrest by federal authorities in the summer of 2019."

On Saturday morning he told an open-mouthed Capehart, "Steve Bannon sat down and worked with Jeffrey Epstein and, according to Mark Epstein, the two men filmed about 15 to 16 hours worth of footage. Steve Bannon, after Jeffrey Epstein's death, actually had a meeting in New York with Mark Epstein and was talking to him about getting some funding to put together a documentary. Mark Epstein said, 'No, I'm not giving you any money for this documentary.'"

He then added, "The American public hasn't seen this 15 hours of footage. I reached out to Steve yesterday, haven't heard back from him. He has suggested last week on his own podcast that potentially he may release a documentary come next year. but that goes back to the whole thing of there's 15 hours of raw footage that Bannon has and while he's calling for full transparency and the unsealing of all the evidence of the files from the DOJ, he is yet to release his own."

"That's primary source reporting that Steve Bannon has right there!" Capehart exclaimed. "Then release it!"

Our Analysis:

The Bannon-Epstein Chronicles: A Saga of Unreleased Tapes and Unanswered Questions

In what seems to be an endless cycle of bombshell revelations and eyebrow-raising associations, the latest twist in the sordid saga of Jeffrey Epstein involves none other than Steve Bannon, a figure synonymous with the MAGA movement and controversy in equal measure. According to recent reports on MSNBC, Bannon is sitting on a gold mine of raw footage—approximately 15 to 16 hours worth—stemming from interviews he conducted with the accused pedophile Jeffrey Epstein before his untimely death in custody. This revelation has understandably caused a stir, prompting calls for the immediate release of the footage.

The Plot Thickens

The narrative, as outlined by NBC reporter Vaughan Hillyard on MSNBC's "The Weekend," paints a picture of Bannon's ambitious, albeit morbid, project to document Epstein's perspective. This endeavor came after the Miami Herald's explosive 2018 report that brought renewed scrutiny to Epstein's vile activities. The fact that Bannon, a figure deeply embedded in the political right, chose to engage with Epstein, a convicted sex offender, for a documentary is a detail ripe for criticism and speculation. The interaction between Bannon and Epstein raises a myriad of ethical questions, not least of which pertains to the motivations behind such a project.

The Elephant in the Room: Transparency

The irony of the situation cannot be overstated. Bannon, a vocal proponent of "full transparency" when it suits his political narrative, is now the gatekeeper of potentially explosive material that the public, and more importantly, the justice system, might find invaluable. This duality of advocating for the unsealing of evidence by the DOJ while simultaneously hoarding hours of raw interviews with Epstein is a glaring contradiction that undermines Bannon's credibility and purported commitment to transparency.

A Call to Action

Jonathan Capehart's reaction on MSNBC, a mix of astonishment and urgency, encapsulates the public's rightful demand for these tapes to be released. If Bannon is in possession of "primary source reporting" that could shed further light on Epstein's nefarious activities and connections, withholding it from the public domain is not just morally indefensible; it's a disservice to the victims of Epstein's crimes and the broader pursuit of justice.

The Bottom Line

The saga of the Bannon-Epstein tapes is more than just a sensational headline; it's a litmus test for the integrity and principles of those who wield significant influence in the media and political spheres. The refusal to release the footage, under the guise of potentially producing a documentary, is a flimsy excuse that does not hold water in the court of public opinion or ethical journalism.

In an era where transparency is often championed but seldom practiced, the onus is on Bannon to prove that his allegiance to uncovering the truth is not selectively applied. Releasing the Epstein footage would be a step in the right direction, but the cynic in me doubts the priority here is justice or transparency. Instead, it appears to be yet another chapter in the playbook of political opportunism, where sensationalism trumps substance, and the pursuit of truth is a conditional endeavor, contingent on the potential for political gain or personal enrichment.

In conclusion, the call to "Release it!" is not just a demand for evidence; it's a clarion call for integrity, accountability, and a commitment to the principles of justice—values that seem to be in short supply in certain corridors of power. The ball is in Bannon's court, and the world is watching.
