Expert warns Trump's health rapidly declining: 'He’s losing the brakes'

By Paulo Santos

An expert Thursday warned that President Donald Trump‘s health has started to rapidly deteriorate as questions increase over his mental capacity and ability to lead, according to reports.

Dr. John Gartner, psychologist, psychiatrist, and a former assistant professor at John Hopkins Medical School, told British publication The i Paper that there have been increased concerns over the president’s health and suggested signs of cognitive decline.

“The main way to diagnose dementia is that we see a deterioration from someone’s own baseline in these four areas: language, memory, behavior, and psychomotor performance,” Gartner said.

“He’s deteriorated since his last administration noticeably but now we’re seeing deterioration almost week over week. The rate of decline is accelerating,” Gartner said.

Trump has made several statements, including his mix-up over Iceland and Greenland, among other comments that have led to further unease around his health.

“The high-pressure job can also accelerate cognitive dysfunction,” Gartner added.

The White House has said that the 79-year-old president has been diagnosed with chronic venous insufficiency — this can cause swollen ankles — and also stated that the president’s frequent hand shaking has caused hand bruising.

And while medical professionals have cautioned against diagnosing Trump based on his family history or jumping to any conclusions around his health, the increased signs have drawn health experts to consider “the wider state of his health” as Trump pushes to threaten military takeovers and his “military adventurism” with Venezuela.

“While this form does not produce the same level of memory decay as Alzheimer’s, it does produce tremendous disinhibition of behavior because it’s the frontal lobes that are the brakes of the brain. So that’s what inhibits us from acting out,’” Gartner told The i Paper.

“Part of his brain [appears to be] deteriorating disproportionately so he’s losing the brakes and this is someone who was always impulsive and always acted out aggressively… Whilst he’s becoming confused about what’s happening, he’s also becoming aggressively disinhibited to act in impulsive and erratic ways,” Gartner said.

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Art of Armchair Diagnosis

In a recent piece that reads more like a speculative medical drama than a report grounded in rigorous journalism, an article quoting Dr. John Gartner, a psychologist and psychiatrist, paints a dire picture of President Donald Trump’s health, particularly focusing on signs of cognitive decline. The narrative is alarmist, leveraging Gartner’s credentials to lend weight to observations that, while potentially concerning, tread dangerously close to the line of ethical medical practice and journalistic integrity.

The Expert Opinion

Dr. John Gartner, a name that carries weight given his background as a former assistant professor at Johns Hopkins Medical School, is cited extensively throughout the article. Gartner’s analysis hinges on observed behaviors of President Trump that he claims signify a rapid cognitive decline. However, it’s paramount to note that diagnosing from afar—without a direct examination—is not just frowned upon in medical circles but considered profoundly unprofessional. The American Psychiatric Association’s Goldwater Rule explicitly cautions against it, making Gartner’s public diagnosis a controversial move.

The Evidence? Questionable at Best.

The article points to Trump’s “mix-up over Iceland and Greenland” among other unspecified comments as evidence of his deteriorating mental state. Yet, to draw a straight line from such gaffes to a diagnosis of dementia is not just a leap—it’s a pole vault over the chasm of medical ethics. Politicians, irrespective of party lines, are prone to verbal missteps, and these incidents are hardly conclusive proof of cognitive decline.

Moreover, the mention of chronic venous insufficiency and resultant hand bruising as somehow linked to cognitive issues is a bewildering juxtaposition of unrelated health concerns, muddying the waters further. Chronic venous insufficiency’s inclusion seems to serve more as a dramatic embellishment than a pertinent fact.

The Ethical Quagmire

The ethics—or lack thereof—of diagnosing a public figure in such a manner cannot be overstated. While Gartner voices concern over Trump’s capacity to lead, particularly citing “military adventurism” with Venezuela, the leap to a diagnosis of cognitive decline from observed behavior crosses a boundary that should concern both medical professionals and the consuming public. It raises the question: Where is the line between legitimate public concern and sensationalism?

The Conclusion? A Grain of Salt

While the concerns over any president’s mental fitness for office are valid, the method of airing such concerns via speculative diagnoses does little to contribute to a reasoned public discourse. Instead, it fans the flames of polarization, encouraging a culture of armchair diagnosis that undermines both medical integrity and public trust.

In essence, the article, while cloaked in the guise of concern and expert analysis, veers dangerously close to sensationalism, sacrificing scientific rigor and ethical journalism at the altar of clickbait. Readers would do well to take such diagnoses with a hefty grain of salt and seek a more balanced, less speculative approach to understanding the complex realities of political leadership and health.
