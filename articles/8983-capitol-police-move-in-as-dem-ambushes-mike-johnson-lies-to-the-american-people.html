Capitol Police move in as Dem ambushes Mike Johnson: 'Lies to the American people!'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Rep. Chrissy Houlahan (D-PA) on Wednesday confronted Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) to his face over his refusal to even speak with Democrats over the government shutdown — and Capitol Police moved in to usher her away.

Johnson claims that his House has passed a continuing resolution to fund the government that should now pass the Senate — and he has nothing to negotiate. His stance is that the Senate must pass his bill before any conversations take place.

That House-passed CR, however, expires on Nov. 21.

Democrats have been speaking with some GOP senators about a plan to fund the government while restoring healthcare subsidies. Open enrollment began Nov. 1, and Punchbowl News’ Jake Sherman noted that the theory is that pressure will increase on Republicans after Americans see healthcare premium increases.

Houlahan wants to see Johnson begin to engage with Democrats over the shutdown, but her confrontation didn’t appear to go well.

“I’m asking you a question: if you’re ready to have a conversation with the other side?” she asked. “You represent all of us. You are the speaker for all of us, sir. You have an obligation not just to speak lies to the American people. You have an obligation to call the leadership of both parties and bring us together and solve this problem together, Mr. Speaker.”

Johnson blasted Houlahan, telling her to “respect free speech,” which she turned around and used against him.

It was then that a Capitol Police officer grabbed Houlahan’s shoulder to usher her away from the speaker, only to learn that she was a member of Congress. Members wear a special pin to indicate they are in the office. Some of the women hook it onto a necklace to wear it around their necks.

The officer asked to see the pin, and Houlahan lifted the necklace up.

“Ok. Thank you,” the officer says.

Johnson continued speaking on the Capitol steps to a small audience of media.

Our Analysis:

The Political Theater of a Shutdown: A Critical Analysis

In the latest episode of what can only be described as the ongoing saga of American political dysfunction, Rep. Chrissy Houlahan (D-PA) took a bold step that most in Congress seem to shy away from: she confronted Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) directly over the current governmental deadlock. This confrontation, however, was not just about the shutdown but about the broader issue of partisanship and the refusal of political leaders to engage in meaningful dialogue. Let’s dissect the layers of this incident and what it signifies in the grander scheme of American politics.

The Standoff

At the heart of this drama is Speaker Mike Johnson’s staunch refusal to engage with Democrats over how to end the government shutdown. His position is a testament to a worrying trend in politics: the abandonment of negotiation and compromise in favor of a my-way-or-the-highway approach. Johnson’s insistence that the Senate must pass a House-passed continuing resolution (CR) that expires on November 21, without any form of negotiation, is not just stubborn; it’s a dereliction of duty. The fact that this CR’s deadline looms without a clear path forward highlights the precariousness of relying on temporary funding measures instead of sustainable solutions.

The Confrontation and Its Fallout

Rep. Houlahan’s confrontation with Johnson was not just a moment of political theater but a necessary act of accountability. Her words, “You represent all of us. You are the speaker for all of us, sir,” cut to the core of what many seem to forget: elected officials are, first and foremost, public servants. The Speaker’s role is not to serve the interests of a single party but the entire legislative body — and, by extension, the American people.

However, Johnson’s retort to Houlahan to “respect free speech” is both ironic and telling. It reveals a fundamental misunderstanding (or perhaps a deliberate misinterpretation) of what free speech entails. Free speech is not just the freedom to express one’s opinions but also the responsibility to engage with opposing viewpoints constructively. The subsequent intervention by Capitol Police, mistaking Houlahan for a mere protester rather than a fellow member of Congress, adds a layer of absurdity to the situation. It underscores the unusualness of direct confrontations in today’s political climate, where such encounters are so rare that they’re mistaken for security threats.

The Bigger Picture

Beyond the immediate issue of the government shutdown and the healthcare subsidies at stake, this incident reflects the broader problem of polarization and gridlock in American politics. The refusal to negotiate, the vilification of the opposition, and the prioritization of party loyalty over public service are symptoms of a systemic illness plaguing the political system.

Conclusion

Rep. Chrissy Houlahan’s attempt to break through the partisan barrier may not have yielded the immediate results she hoped for, but it serves as a crucial reminder of the importance of dialogue and negotiation in governance. Speaker Johnson’s response, while disappointing, is hardly surprising in the current political landscape. However, it’s essential to recognize that such stances only serve to deepen divisions and exacerbate the challenges facing the nation.

As the government shutdown continues with no resolution in sight, one can’t help but wonder: when will the leaders we elect to serve us start putting the people’s needs above political posturing? The clock is ticking, and with each passing day, the American people grow weary of the political games that jeopardize not just healthcare subsidies but the very fabric of democracy.
