Some far-right extremists are relishing the idea of violence at the "No Kings" protests against President Donald Trump set to happen in over 2,000 cities this weekend. But others have the exact opposite take on the matter.

On Friday, Oath Keepers founder Stewart Rhodes told conspiracy theorist webcaster Alex Jones that Trump supporters ought to stay far away from these protests, lest they add fuel to what he imagines will be a Democratic false flag event.

"They want their Kent State moment," said Rhodes, referencing an infamous massacre of anti-Vietnam War student protesters at Kent State by the Ohio National Guard. "They're defending the indefensible, they're protesting against the deportation of child sex traffickers, smugglers for fentanyl that are killing Americans as proxies for communist China. They're defending the indefensible. So what they hope to see happen is U.S. troops or Trump supporters opening fire on 'peaceful protesters.'"

"That's why it's important for us — my advice to all Trump supporters is stay out of these protests, let them show their a--," said Rhodes, who was instrumental in the planning of attacks during the Jan. 6 insurrection and was convicted of seditious conspiracy. Trump went on to commute his sentence.

Rhodes' warning comes amid similar advice by other Trump loyalists, with Rep. Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL) posting on X, "Do NOT attend the No Kings protests. I have screen shots circulating that leftists are going to wear MAGA gear in an attempt to cause chaos."

The No Kings protests come at the same time as protests against mass deportations erupt in cities around the country, with some of the most prominent being in Los Angeles. Trump deployed the National Guard and Marines to try to disperse them, although California officials have said his federalization of the Guard was unjustified and illegal, and moved for a federal court to stop him.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Far-Right Extremism and the "No Kings" Protests

In the latest episode of the American political circus, far-right extremists have taken center stage, yet again, to deliver performances that would make even the most seasoned playwrights blush. The "No Kings" protests, aimed against President Donald Trump's policies, have predictably become a battleground not just of ideologies, but of the very concept of truth itself.

Stewart Rhodes: A Convicted Extremist's Advice

Stewart Rhodes, founder of the Oath Keepers, a group with a history as checkered as a chessboard, and a man convicted of seditious conspiracy for his role in the January 6 insurrection, has emerged from the shadows. In a conversation with conspiracy theorist Alex Jones, Rhodes spins a tale that would be laughable if it weren't so dangerous. He suggests Trump supporters avoid the "No Kings" protests to prevent a "Democratic false flag event."

Let's unpack that, shall we?

Rhodes references the Kent State massacre, a national tragedy, to draw a parallel with the current protests, claiming, without a shred of evidence, that these demonstrations defend "the indefensible," including "child sex traffickers" and "smugglers for fentanyl." This narrative doesn't just stretch the truth; it obliterates it.

The False Flag Narrative: A Dangerous Diversion

The false flag narrative is a classic in the far-right playbook. It's a preemptive strike against criticism, a way to delegitimize genuine grievances, and, most importantly, a method to rally the base around a perceived external threat. When Rhodes advises Trump supporters to "stay out of these protests," what he's really doing is reinforcing the us-vs-them mentality that has fractured American society.

Anna Paulina Luna's Echo Chamber

Rep. Anna Paulina Luna's (R-FL) warning on X (formerly Twitter) about leftists wearing MAGA gear to cause chaos is another verse in this tired song. It's a claim made without evidence, designed to sow distrust and fear. It's a tactic as old as politics itself: if you can't win the argument, muddy the waters.

The Bigger Picture: Protests and Response

The "No Kings" protests aren't occurring in a vacuum. They coincide with demonstrations against mass deportations, a policy that has drawn widespread condemnation. Trump's response, deploying the National Guard and Marines, is a show of force that echoes authoritarian regimes, not a democratic republic. California's challenge to the federalization of the Guard as "unjustified and illegal" underscores the legal and ethical quagmire Trump's administration is wading into.

Conclusion: The Cost of Lies

What's most alarming in this entire spectacle is the cost of these lies and manipulations. They're not just political strategies; they're tearing at the fabric of American democracy. When elected officials and convicted extremists peddle falsehoods with impunity, the truth becomes a casualty. The "No Kings" protests, and the far-right's reaction to them, are a stark reminder of the challenges facing America. It's a moment that requires not just passive observation, but active engagement and a commitment to facts over fiction.

In the grand scheme, Rhodes, Luna, and their ilk are not just voices in the wilderness. They are symptoms of a systemic error in American politics — one that values loyalty over truth, and power over justice. The road to repairing this error is long and fraught with obstacles, but it's a journey that must be undertaken. For the sake of democracy, let's hope it's not too late.
