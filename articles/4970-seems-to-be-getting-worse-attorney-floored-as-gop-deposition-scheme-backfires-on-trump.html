'Seems to be getting worse': Attorney floored as GOP deposition scheme backfires on Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 24, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:GOP’s Epstein Deposition Fiasco: A Masterclass in Self-Sabotage

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks as he hosts a Governors Dinner at the White House in Washington D.C., U.S., February 21, 2026. REUTERS/Aaron Schwartz

A lawyer was floored on Sunday as he discussed how the GOP’s scheme to ignore the deposition of a potential co-conspirator of Jeffrey Epstein’s backfired on the president.

Last week, the House Oversight Committee held a nearly five-hour deposition of Leslie Wexner, the former CEO of Victoria’s Secret. Not a single Republican attended the deposition hearing, where Wexner denied any involvement in Epstein’s crimes and argued that he was “conned” out of the nearly $200 million that he paid Epstein for real estate and tax services throughout their relationship.

Wexner’s name also appears on a document released by the Department of Justice listing him as a potential co-conspirator of Epstein’s, according to reports. Wexner has denied any wrongdoing.

Former Florida state attorney Dave Aronberg discussed how the GOP’s attempt to downplay the deposition backfired on the party during an interview on the “All Rise News” podcast with Adam Klasfeld on Sunday.

“This is a story that just has legs because of Trump and their drip, drip, drip instead of just ripping the band-aid off,” Aronberg said. “ ... It’s a self-inflicted wound, and the more they try to conceal and redact, the more interest people have in this. So, I am not surprised that we are here talking about this because it only seems to be getting worse.”

Wexner’s deposition also appeared to undercut some key claims about Epstein that President Donald Trump has made, like that nobody knew who Epstein was until after he died.

“It’s hard to say that Jeffrey Epstein was just a normal finance guy and no one knew about his secret life,” Aronberg said. “There was no secret life. That’s who Jeffrey Epstein was. Donald Trump said so in New York magazine, where he said, ‘He likes to have fun as much as I do. He likes him on the young side.’ Well, that’s what everyone knew about Jeffrey Epstein at the time because he walked around openly and notoriously with a harem.”

Our Analysis:

GOP’s Epstein Deposition Fiasco: A Masterclass in Self-Sabotage

In a turn of events that could only leave one’s jaw squarely on the floor, the GOP’s latest escapade into the world of political blunders has unfolded with all the grace of a bull in a china shop. The spectacle surrounding the deposition of Leslie Wexner, former CEO of Victoria’s Secret and a figure embroiled in the sordid and scandalous web of Jeffrey Epstein, serves as a stark testament to the adage: “Ignorance is bliss, but only until it spectacularly backfires.”

The Context: Last week, the House Oversight Committee held what one might argue was a crucial deposition lasting nearly five hours with Leslie Wexner. The meat of the matter? Wexner’s denial of any involvement in Epstein’s heinous crimes, coupled with his claim of being “conned” out of a staggering $200 million by Epstein for real estate and tax services. Here’s where the plot thickens – not a single Republican chose to grace the deposition with their presence.

The GOP’s Calculated Absence: This choice, or rather, strategic evasion, speaks volumes of the GOP’s apparent strategy (or lack thereof) to deal with the Epstein connection. Their absence screams an attempt to downplay and perhaps wish away the damning implications of such a deposition. But as fate would have it, this move didn’t just fall flat; it imploded.

Wexner’s Deposition and the Unraveling of a Scheme: According to reports, Wexner’s name features on a Department of Justice document listing him as a potential co-conspirator of Epstein’s. Despite his denials, the absence of GOP members at the deposition only amplifies the echo of these allegations. Former Florida state attorney Dave Aronberg’s comments during the “All Rise News” podcast can be summed up as watching someone meticulously dissect the GOP’s self-inflicted debacle. Aronberg poignantly notes the “drip, drip, drip” approach of the GOP, which only serves to fuel public intrigue and skepticism.

The Undercutting of Trump’s Claims: Among the debris of this fallout, one particular revelation stands stark against the backdrop of denial and obfuscation – the deposition’s contradiction of President Donald Trump’s claims about Epstein. Trump’s past remarks, notably his infamous comment to New York magazine about Epstein liking his companions “on the young side,” starkly contrasts with his attempt to frame Epstein as an unknown figure pre-death. This contradiction isn’t just a gap; it’s a chasm that exposes a troubling normalization of Epstein’s behavior within certain elite circles.

Conclusion: The GOP’s attempt to sideline the Wexner deposition is emblematic of a broader issue within the party – a penchant for strategic miscalculations that seem to serve no one, least of all themselves. By choosing to ignore the deposition, the GOP inadvertently amplified its significance, turning what could have been a controlled explosion into an unmitigated disaster. As the story continues to unfold, the GOP’s decision to “ignore and downplay” will likely be remembered as a textbook example of how not to manage a crisis. The self-inflicted wound Aronberg speaks of is not just a nick; it’s a gash that reveals a deeper rot within the party’s approach to scandal management. In their attempt to navigate the murky waters of the Epstein saga, the GOP has managed to do the one thing worse than being implicated – they’ve shown a flagrant disregard for accountability, transparency, and, ultimately, the truth.
