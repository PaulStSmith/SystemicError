Holiday spending plans plunge to lowest level ever as Trump economy triggers panic: data

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 5, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A person carries a Christmas bag after buying some items in a holiday market featuring artisans from around the world at Bryant Park in New York City, U.S., November 16, 2025. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

A new batch of data is offering more evidence that the US economy is in rough shape heading into the holidays.

The latest Economic Confidence Index released by Gallup on Thursday has found that Americans’ confidence in the economy has fallen by seven points over the last month, and now stands at its lowest level in more than a year.

Overall, Gallup found that just 21% of Americans currently describe the economy as excellent or good, while 40% describe it as poor. The outlook for the near future also looks grim, as more than two-thirds of Americans surveyed said the economy is currently getting worse.

This deteriorating economic confidence is weighing on Americans’ holiday shopping plans, as Gallup found that planned holiday spending expenditures have “plummeted” from just over $1,000 in October to $778 in November. The decline in spending expectations also occurred across all income groups, although it was particularly steep among low-income households, which slashed their estimated holiday spending by an average of $267.

Gallup noted that while it’s common for shoppers to trim their spending plans the closer it gets to the holidays, the drop between October and November this year was the biggest it has ever recorded, even “surpassing the $185 drop seen during the 2008 global financial crisis.”

The Gallup survey was not the only troubling economic data to drop on Thursday, as outplacement firm Challenger, Gray, and Christmas released its latest report showing that hiring in the US has slowed to its lowest level in the last 15 years, while layoffs now total their highest level since 2020, when the country was at the peak of the Covid-19 global pandemic.

The data on layoffs came just one day after global payroll processing firm ADP estimated that the US economy lost 32,000 jobs in November, with small businesses shouldering by far the most job losses.

President Donald Trump, who earlier this week dismissed concerns Americans might have about affordability as a “Democrat scam,” has reportedly decided to hit the road in an effort to convince voters that they’ve never had it so good.

According to Axios, Trump next week will start touring the country to tout his administration’s economic policies, and he is expected to “aggressively push back against criticism over the cost of everyday essentials—an issue that helped propel him to victory over Kamala Harris last year.”

However, a new poll published by Politico on Thursday shows that Trump may have an uphill climb selling his economy even to his own voters.

Overall, the poll found that 37% of voters who backed Trump last year now say that the cost of living crisis is the worst they have experienced in their lifetimes, while only 24% of 2024 Trump voters say that the cost of living crisis at the moment is “not bad.”

The poll also found that Trump’s efforts to blame former PresidentJoe Bidenfor the current state of the economy aren’t flying, as 46% of voters say that Trump is most to blame for the current state of the economy, compared to 29% of voters who put the primary blame on Biden.

Our Analysis:

Economic Doldrums: A Tale of Tarnished Confidence and Political Blame Games

In what reads like a grim forecast for holiday cheer, the latest Gallup Economic Confidence Index has unveiled a stark picture: America’s economic confidence is spiraling downwards, hitting its nadir in over a year. With only 21% of Americans giving the economy a thumbs-up, and an overwhelming wave of pessimism about its future trajectory, the findings are more than just numbers—they are a distressing signal of widespread angst and a prelude to a restrained holiday season.

The plummet in consumer confidence is not a shallow dip but a profound dive, with holiday spending expectations undergoing a freefall from over $1,000 to $778. This isn’t just a number—it’s a narrative of tightened belts and scaled-back holiday dreams, affecting all income groups, but striking the low-income households with particular ferocity. Their holiday budget has been slashed by an average of $267—a figure that’s not just a statistic but a story of deferred dreams and difficult choices.

This tightening of purse strings mirrors the 2008 global financial crisis, yet the article indicates this year’s drop is even more precipitous. This comparison is not just a measure of economic sentiment but a testament to the severity of the current mood. The shadows of the past loom large, yet the present seems even more daunting.

The employment landscape offers no respite, with hiring at a 15-year nadir and layoffs soaring to heights unseen since the 2020 pandemic peak. This is not merely a fluctuation in the job market; it’s a red flag signaling distress and a potential harbinger of tougher times ahead.

Enter President Donald Trump, whose strategy to counter this economic gloom appears to be a mix of denial and diversion. His dismissal of affordability concerns as a “Democrat scam” and plans to embark on a countrywide tour to extol his administration’s economic policies smack of a desperate attempt to polish the unpolishable. The audacity of attempting to sell a thriving economic narrative in the face of such dire data is a political gamble, one that seems to be facing stiff resistance from the very electorate that propelled him to power.

Indeed, a Politico poll punctures any inflated optimism, revealing a stark disconnect between Trump’s economic victory lap and the grim reality perceived by his voters. With 37% of his last year’s voters decrying the current cost of living crisis as the worst in their lifetimes, and a significant portion pinning the blame squarely on Trump over Biden, the political winds appear to be shifting. Trump’s attempt to rewrite the economic narrative faces not just skepticism but outright rejection.

This scenario is not merely an economic downturn but a political maelstrom. Trump’s efforts to deflect blame and dress up the economic data in celebratory garb are not just tone-deaf; they are a glaring misread of the room. The electorate is anxious, struggling, and, most importantly, not buying the spin. The article paints a picture of a nation at an economic and political crossroads, with its leaders seemingly out of step with the lived realities of its citizens.

In sum, the data doesn’t lie: the US economy is wading through troubled waters, and the holiday season is set to be more about economic survival than festive splurge. As political leaders attempt to navigate this storm, the real test will be their ability to acknowledge the harsh truths and craft policies that address the root causes of this economic malaise, rather than embarking on quixotic tours of denial.
