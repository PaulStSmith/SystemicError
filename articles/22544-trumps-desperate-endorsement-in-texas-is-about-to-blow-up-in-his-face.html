Trump’s 'desperate' endorsement in Texas is about to blow up in his face

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Didn’t Blink. He Picked the Extremist.

Brandon Herrera is not being elevated despite his record; he is being elevated because the Republican Party has decided that his record is survivable. The source material is blunt about the facts: Herrera has a documented trail of Nazi imagery, Holocaust jokes, and grotesque gun fantasies, yet Donald Trump now fully endorses him in Texas’s 23rd Congressional District, while millions of dollars flow through pro-MAGA channels and the GOP line hardens around the only candidate left standing.

The Party Made the Choice

The useful fiction here is that this is all some regrettable byproduct of a messy primary season. It is not. Republicans previously treated Herrera as radioactive. Trump’s lawyers even sent a cease-and-desist when Herrera used Trump’s image on campaign material. That was not confusion; that was a boundary.

Then the boundary became inconvenient.

Once there was “no other Republican to support,” Trump reversed course and embraced Herrera anyway. That tells you where actual power sits. The party does not have an ideology it occasionally violates. It has a hierarchy, and once the leader moves, the rest are expected to rationalize the move as inevitability.

Extremism Is Not the Bug

Herrera’s record is not being hidden by accident. It is being metabolized by the coalition that benefits from it. The source cites his Nazi imagery, his copy of Mein Kampf, his Holocaust jokes, and his grim little performance around veteran suicide. None of that disqualifies him in a serious way inside today’s Republican ecosystem because the ecosystem has already decided that shock is a feature, not a liability.

This is the pattern: the party publicly performs revulsion when convenient, then folds when the only remaining options are loyalty or embarrassment. Extremism is condemned only when it threatens control. Once it can be harnessed, it becomes branding.

Money Knows What It Is Funding

The $2.7 million flowing to Herrera through Texas Strategy Group matters because money is the least hypocritical part of politics. It does not pretend to be offended. It follows the path of power and bets on the outcome.

That funding is not a sign of inadvertent drift. It is evidence that pro-Republican and pro-MAGA forces are comfortable underwriting a candidate with an extremist record if he can be folded into the caucus. The same movement that likes to posture as the guardian of “values” is bankrolling a man whose politics are built on provocation, dehumanization, and performance cruelty. The values were never the point. Control was.

The Opponent Is the Contrast

Katy Padilla Stout, the Democratic opponent, is described here as running a traditional campaign on child welfare and education. That distinction matters because it exposes the imbalance in what each side thinks politics is for. One side is laundering extremism through institutional machinery. The other is trying to talk about governing.

This is where lazy political coverage often fails: it treats the contest as a generic horse race and drains the asymmetry out of it. But the asymmetry is the story. A district can be “neck-and-neck” while one candidate is backed by a party apparatus normalizing a candidate with open Nazi associations. That is not just competitive politics. That is institutional degradation in plain view.

What This Actually Reveals

The Republican Party is not being captured by its fringe. It is revealing how thin the wall always was between its mainstream and its most poisonous figures. When the movement needs a warm body, the prior objections disappear. When a candidate becomes useful, old standards are retired. When a district is conservative enough, the party stops pretending it cares what kind of person carries the label.

The larger lesson is ugly but clear: modern authoritarian politics does not require perfect coherence. It requires disciplined reversal. Yesterday’s unacceptable candidate becomes today’s “America First Patriot” the moment leadership decides the base can be managed and the damage can be absorbed. The scandal is not that Trump endorses Herrera. The scandal is that the party structure is built to make that endorsement feel normal.
