The president marked the occasion Tuesday afternoon at Arlington National Cemetery, where he praised his Cabinet secretaries in attendance, including Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who prefers to use the historical honorific of the Department of War for the office, used before it was changed as part of a post-World War II reorganization of the military and intelligence services.

“We’re opening up our country, should have never been closed,” Trump said. “I want to thank [Veterans Administration] Secretary Doug Collins, who’s outstanding, just done a fantastic job for the veterans. We have a 92 percent approval rating. We took that up from 38 percent from a certain previous administration that we won’t mention. Also, Secretary of War, we like the sound of that better than the other way, Secretary Pete Hegseth. Pete, thank you very much. Thank you Pete.”

The president’s remark sparked outrage on social media.

“This is a day that literally celebrates the end of war,” said journalist Timothy Burke.

“I come from a long family history of veterans and while they were proud to serve their country, they felt no pride in what they saw or what they had to do,” posted freelance artist Luke Russell. “May this entire administration burn in the eternal flames of Hell.”

“I, for one, am shocked - <shocked> - that the guy who couldn’t be bothered to fully participate in the 2018 WWI Armistice centennial because of rain is desecrating Veterans Day,” added Mark Copelovitch, a political science professor the University of Wisconsin.

“After a term and a half, he still doesn’t know the difference between Veteran’s Day and Memorial Day,” noted Bluesky user Randy Cain. “Just a hint, Mr. President, it’s not Memorial Day.”

“Only Donald Trump could assert, in all seriousness, that the most important factor in a superpower’s military might is ... branding,” opined historian Kevin Kruse.

“Fun fact, Hegseth is still just officially titled the Defense Secretary,” added Bluesky user Jase. “The title Secretary of War is literally just a nickname they reverted to just to sound tough.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump’s Veterans’ Day Fiasco: A Study in Misplaced Priorities and Historical Ignorance

In an unsettling display that veered off the somber path traditionally associated with Veterans’ Day, President Donald Trump’s speech at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier was as shocking as it was inappropriate. The event, designed to honor those who have served, somehow mutated into a platform for Trump to glorify war and indulge in self-congratulation, revealing a profound misunderstanding of the day’s significance.

Glorifying War on a Day of Remembrance

Trump’s praise for his Cabinet, particularly Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, for preferring the archaic and ominously aggressive title of “Secretary of War,” is a startling choice of focus. This preference for a term that harks back to a more openly belligerent era in American history is more than just a semantic quibble; it’s a disturbing glimpse into the administration’s mindset. Hegseth’s title, as Trump gleefully noted, harks back to a time before the Department of Defense’s post-World War II reorganization aimed at reflecting a more peaceful, global stance. This reversion to old terminologies isn’t nostalgic; it’s regressive and dangerous.

The Misuse of a Solemn Occasion

Veterans’ Day is a time to reflect on the sacrifices made by military personnel, to honor their commitment and the horrors they often endured. It is not an occasion to “thank” administration officials or to boast about approval ratings, as Trump did by highlighting VA Secretary Doug Collins’ work. This diversion from the day’s purpose is not just a matter of poor taste; it’s a profound disrespect to the very people supposedly being honored.

Social Media Backlash: A Public Outraged

The reaction on social media was swift and scathing. Journalist Timothy Burke and freelance artist Luke Russell articulated the deep offense taken by those with personal and familial connections to military service. The sentiment that Veterans’ Day celebrates peace and the end of conflict seemed lost on Trump, whose comments were seen as desecrating the day’s true meaning.

Political science professor Mark Copelovitch and Bluesky user Randy Cain pointed out Trump’s apparent confusion between Veterans’ Day and Memorial Day, further underscoring a disturbing lack of understanding or respect for these distinct occasions. Historian Kevin Kruse’s critique of Trump’s obsession with “branding” over substance in military matters was particularly poignant, emphasizing the superficiality of Trump’s approach to governance.

Conclusion: A Leadership Out of Touch

The article paints a picture of a president and administration deeply out of touch with the nation’s history, its values, and the solemnity of occasions like Veterans’ Day. Trump’s speech was not just a missed opportunity to truly honor veterans but a disturbing exhibition of misplaced priorities and historical ignorance. It’s a stark reminder that leadership requires not just strength but sensitivity and an understanding of the nation’s core principles — principles that seem alarmingly absent in these remarks.

In the end, one is left to ponder the cost of such leadership — a cost paid in the currency of national unity, respect, and the solemn recognition of sacrifice. On a day meant for reflection and reverence, the administration chose instead to glorify war and tout personal achievements, a choice that speaks volumes about its priorities and its understanding of the nation it leads.
