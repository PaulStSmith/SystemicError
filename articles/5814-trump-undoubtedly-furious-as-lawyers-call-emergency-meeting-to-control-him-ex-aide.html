Trump 'undoubtedly furious' as lawyers call 'emergency meeting' to control him: Ex-aide

A coalition of prosecutors from across the nation are flying to Washington, D.C. this week to develop an action plan for holding the Trump administration accountable amid its ongoing immigration raids, Ex-White House official Miles Taylor exclusively reported Tuesday.

Those prosecutors are part of the new organization Fight Against Federal Overreach, first revealed last week and established to put together a strategy for holding federal immigration officials accountable for their sometimes violent and arguably unconstitutional actions.

“This week, something unusual is happening in the nation’s capital,” Taylor wrote Tuesday on his Substack “Defiance News.”

“Local prosecutors from across the United States are flying to Washington, D.C. for an emergency meeting about a simple idea that shouldn’t be controversial: No federal agent is above the law. Yet the Trump administration will undoubtedly be furious to know the gathering is taking place, as the organizers are readying efforts to hold its agents accountable for misconduct.”

Federal officials are notoriously “almost impossible to sue” for misconduct and enjoy far greater immunity than state and local officials. While the Justice Department would typically be the entity to hold federal officials accountable for misconduct, the Trump administration has declared such officials to have “absolute immunity,” a proclamation made by Vice President JD Vance last month in defense of the killing of Renee Good.

The emergency meeting held by the new organization was unusual in more ways than one, with one Homeland Security official telling Taylor that they couldn’t think of any historical precedent for such a gathering.

“When you see local prosecutors having to take on federal agents, you know we’re in uncharted territory,” the official told Taylor on the condition of anonymity. “This is a consequence of the administration’s abuses of power.”

It’s unclear what direction the prosecutors will steer toward in their efforts to hold the Trump administration accountable, though a number of potential pathways have already been proposed by legal scholars. One suchproposalis to push for state lawmakers to authorize citizens to sue federal officials for violations to the Constitution, an idea first advanced by constitutional scholar Akhil Reed Amar in 1987 and recently revived in discussions surrounding immigration enforcement.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Look: Prosecutors vs. Trump Administration’s Immigration Raids

In a move that can only be characterized as both bold and unprecedented, a coalition of prosecutors from across the nation is descending on Washington, D.C. this week, aiming to craft an action plan to hold the Trump administration accountable for its controversial immigration raids. This gathering, as reported by former White House official Miles Taylor, signals a critical moment of resistance against federal overreach—a concept that should alarm anyone with a vested interest in the integrity of the American legal system.

The formation of the Fight Against Federal Overreach organization is a clear response to the sometimes violent and arguably unconstitutional actions taken by federal immigration officials. The very existence of this coalition underscores a fundamental principle: No federal agent is above the law. Yet, the Trump administration, notorious for its declaration of “absolute immunity” for federal officials, as asserted by Vice President JD Vance, stands in stark opposition to this principle.

The Problem of “Absolute Immunity”

The concept of “absolute immunity” for federal officials is not only troubling but fundamentally flawed. It creates a dangerous precedent that federal agents can act without fear of repercussion, even in cases of misconduct or abuse of power. This stark imbalance of accountability, where federal officials enjoy far greater immunity than their state and local counterparts, is a glaring systemic error that demands rectification.

The Unusual Gathering

The emergency meeting of local prosecutors in D.C. is not just unusual; it’s historic. It represents a pivotal moment where local authorities are stepping up to challenge federal overreach, an endeavor that carries significant risks but is vital for the preservation of constitutional rights. The fact that this meeting is happening at all is a testament to the severity of the Trump administration’s actions and the desperate need for accountability mechanisms that can effectively counteract such abuses of power.

Potential Pathways for Accountability

Among the proposals discussed is an intriguing one: allowing citizens to sue federal officials for constitutional violations. This idea, first proposed by constitutional scholar Akhil Reed Amar in 1987, represents a powerful tool in the fight against federal overreach. By empowering citizens to hold federal agents directly accountable, it could serve as a significant deterrent against misconduct.

Conclusion: A Fight for Justice

The prosecutors’ gathering in Washington, D.C., represents a beacon of hope in a landscape often marred by political and legal cynicism. It’s a bold stance against the notion of “absolute immunity” and a fight for the fundamental principle that no one, not even federal agents, is above the law.

As this drama unfolds, one thing is clear: the battle for accountability and justice within the Trump administration’s immigration enforcement policies is far from over. It’s a pivotal moment that could define the contours of federal accountability for years to come. The prosecutors’ efforts to challenge the status quo are not just commendable; they are essential for the preservation of constitutional rights and the rule of law in the United States.
