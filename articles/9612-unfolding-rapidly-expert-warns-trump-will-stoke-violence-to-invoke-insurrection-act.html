‘Unfolding rapidly’: Expert warns Trump will 'stoke violence' to invoke Insurrection Act

Reich, the author of 20 books, said on Thursday: “Trump wants to invoke the Insurrection Act, to punish anyone who opposes him.”

In his video, he played a clip of the President saying recently, “If you take a look at what’s been going on in Portland, it’s been going on for a long time and that’s insurrection. I mean, that’s pure insurrection.”

Military, legal, and political experts this week warned after Trump vowed to invoke the Insurrection Act if he deems it necessary — a move some see as the culmination of an authoritarian trajectory he has been telegraphing since taking office. No president has used the law in more than three decades, and then only in limited, localized crises.

Reich went on to say, “I know all of this is frightening, and I don’t want to unduly alarm you, but you need to be aware of this imminent danger. It’s unfolding very, very rapidly.”

Explaining what he described as Trump’s four-point plan, Reich said step one “is to deploy ICE into so-called blue cities, run by Democrats. These masked and armed ICE agents are wreaking havoc on American cities and violating due process. They’re arresting people outside immigration courtrooms. They’re raiding homes in the middle of the night, and detaining children and adults, including American citizens. They admit to using racial profiling.”

Step two, he said, is “exaggerate the scale and severity of the protests.”

Step three: “Deploy National Guard troops.”

“Trump is deploying hundreds of National Guard troops from red states, like Texas, into blue states like Oregon, and Illinois, against the wishes of Democratic governors,” Reich said. “This is continuing to enrage an already outraged public — which is the whole point.”

“Trump wants to stoke actual violence, which would make it easier for him to unleash the final step in his plan, which is step four, invoke the Insurrection Act.”

“The Insurrection Act empowers a president to federalize the National Guard and use the U.S. military to suppress civil disorder, insurrection, or armed rebellion against the government,” Reich explained. “Everything done by Trump has been a preamble to invoking this act, and being able to unleash troops against his perceived political enemies who oppose his regime in advance of the 2026 midterms. It would be the ultimate step in Trump’s authoritarian power grab.”

He said that “Trump and his enablers want violent confrontations in order to justify their moves. So, please, remain peaceful if you protest, or if you encounter ICE agents, or National Guard troops where you live. Do not give Trump what he wants.”

On Wednesday on Substack, Professor Reich wrote: “The direction we’re going is either martial law or civil war.”

Also on Wednesday, U.S. Senator Chris Murphy, one of the most outspoken members of the Democratic Party, took to the Senate floor to explain what he said is President Trump “enacting a well thought out plan so he and his allies can rule forever.”

“We aren’t on the verge of an authoritarian takeover,” the Connecticut Democrat declared. “We are in the middle of it.”

Our Analysis:

Reich’s Warning: A Closer Look at Trump’s Alleged Authoritarian Blueprint

In a chilling exposition, Robert Reich, former Secretary of Labor and current professor of public policy, has thrown a spotlight on what he perceives to be a dark and deliberate path being carved by President Donald Trump towards authoritarian rule. Reich’s analysis, rooted in Trump’s own words and actions, deserves a rigorous examination, not just for its audacity but for the implications it carries for the American political landscape.

The Insurrection Act Boogeyman

At the heart of Reich’s argument is Trump’s purported intention to invoke the Insurrection Act. This act, a tool not wielded by a president in over three decades, is seen by Reich as the nuclear option in Trump’s arsenal, aimed squarely at those who oppose him. Given Trump’s own categorization of ongoing unrest in places like Portland as “pure insurrection,” the threat cannot be dismissed lightly. However, the leap from tough talk to the actual deployment of military forces against American citizens is monumental and fraught with legal, moral, and political peril.

The Four-Step Plan to Chaos

Reich outlines a so-called four-step plan by Trump, starting with deploying ICE agents in Democratic-run cities, escalating protests, deploying National Guard troops, and culminating in the invocation of the Insurrection Act. This narrative, while compelling in its coherence, requires a leap of faith. It assumes a level of Machiavellian foresight and control that one might argue exceeds Trump’s demonstrated capacity for strategic planning.

Step One: ICE as an Instrument of Fear — Reich’s depiction of ICE operations paints a grim picture of due process violations and racial profiling. While ICE’s aggressive tactics are well-documented, the leap to using these actions as a stepping stone to martial law is a quantum one. The lack of due process and the impact on communities are deeply concerning in their own right and merit stringent scrutiny and reform.

Step Two: Exaggerating Protests — The narrative of exaggerating the scale of protests serves a dual purpose for Trump, according to Reich: to justify further intervention and to fan the flames of public outrage. While there’s evidence of Trump’s penchant for hyperbole, the connection to a grand authoritarian scheme is more tenuous.

Step Three: Deploying the National Guard — The mobilization of the National Guard by Trump, particularly against the wishes of state governors, is a troubling sign of federal overreach. Yet, it’s a tactic that falls within the realm of Trump’s legal authority, even if morally and politically contentious.

Step Four: The Insurrection Act — This is the climax of Reich’s authoritarian narrative. The act’s invocation would indeed be a drastic measure, one that would likely face fierce legal challenges and public resistance. It’s a scenario that underscores the importance of vigilance but also requires a critical assessment of its likelihood.

The Reaction: Calls for Peace and Vigilance

Reich, alongside voices like Senator Chris Murphy, is unequivocal in their call for peaceful protest and awareness. The invocation to remain peaceful, to not “give Trump what he wants,” is both a plea for moral high ground and a tactical advice to avoid playing into potential authoritarian narratives.

Conclusion: Between Alarm and Awareness

While Robert Reich’s warnings are grounded in observable actions and rhetoric from Trump, the jump to a fully-fledged authoritarian takeover via the Insurrection Act is a leap across a chasm of legal, military, and public resistance hurdles. It’s a scenario that requires not panic but preparedness, not fear but factual rebuttal and resistance. The dangers of an authoritarian slide are real, particularly in a climate of heightened division and unrest. However, the strength of American institutions, the vigilance of its citizenry, and the resilience of its democratic norms are formidable bulwarks against such a slide. Reich’s analysis, though perhaps alarmist, serves as a crucial clarion call to all who value democracy to remain engaged, informed, and ready to act in defense of constitutional principles.
