Supreme Court has become 'willing accomplices' to Trump's 'lawlessness': expert

President Donald Trump appears to have found six accomplices in his quest to dismantle the federal government, according to one lawyer.

Michael Waldman, President and CEO of The Brennan Center, wrote in an analysis that the conservative supermajority of the Supreme Court "are proving willing accomplices to a constitutional coup, all without leaving a trace." He cites the court's use of the so-called "shadow docket" in major cases as a prime example of its complicity.

Shadow docket is a term that refers to an order or motion from the Supreme Court in which the justices say they have not reached a final decision.

The most recent case that follows this trend is McMahon v. New York, a case where the Supreme Court allowed Trump to dismantle the Department of Education by firing all of its workers.

Presidents are not allowed to shut down government agencies without a vote from Congress. The Supreme Court's ruling allowed Trump to side-step that requirement, and did so without any of the justices attaching their name to the opinion.

"This major ruling follows a disturbing recent pattern," Waldman argued. "In the past few months, the justices have used the shadow docket to let the president fire independent agency heads, in clear violation of 90 years of precedent. They’ve allowed the administration to deport people to countries where they never lived. And they’ve given effective approval to the Pentagon’s move to bar transgender people from serving in the military."

"All these measures involve a passive-aggressive jurisprudence: We aren’t making a big ruling, you see, just addressing something done by other judges," he continued. "Though the rulings are technically temporary, the damage is done."

The shadow docket rulings also seem to have emboldened Trump, who could continue to axe important government agencies in the future, Waldman argued.

"As checks and balances are under assault, the Court has chosen to be complicit," Waldman wrote. "It has found a new way to hand power to a lawless executive. At a moment of historic peril, history may record few rulings of consequence — just wreckage strewn across the constitutional landscape."

Our Analysis:

The Shadow Docket: An Instrument of Constitutional Sabotage

Michael Waldman's analysis unveils a stark reality where the U.S. Supreme Court's conservative supermajority acts not as impartial arbiters of justice but as enablers in President Donald Trump's apparent quest to dismantle the federal government. This isn't a mere allegation; it's a meticulously observed pattern of behavior that should alarm any advocate of a balanced and accountable government.

The Supreme Court's Complicity

The term "shadow docket" sounds like something out of a political thriller, yet it's distressingly real. It refers to decisions made without full briefing or oral argument, often without a signed opinion. These are not the transparent, well-reasoned decisions that one might expect from the highest court in the land. Instead, they are opaque and unaccountable, leaving significant impacts on policy and governance without clear justification or authorship.

The case of McMahon v. New York is emblematic of this troubling trend. Here, the Supreme Court allowed President Trump to essentially dismantle the Department of Education by firing all of its workers, sidestepping the clear requirement that such drastic actions need Congressional approval. This isn't just an administrative reshuffle; it's a frontal assault on the principles of separation of powers that underpin the U.S. Constitution.

A Disturbing Pattern of Rulings

Waldman's analysis points to a series of decisions that reveal a consistent and disturbing pattern. From allowing the firing of independent agency heads to enabling deportations to countries where individuals have never lived, to barring transgender people from military service, the Supreme Court has used the shadow docket to effectuate significant policy changes under the radar.

These decisions, Waldman argues, represent a "passive-aggressive jurisprudence" that claims to be temporary and minimal in scope but results in permanent and far-reaching damage. This is not the mark of a judiciary committed to justice or even to the stability and predictability that are the hallmarks of a conservative legal philosophy. It is, instead, the hallmark of a court that has abdicated its responsibility to act as a check on executive overreach.

The Consequences of Judicial Abdication

What Waldman underscores—and what should deeply concern us—is the emboldening effect these decisions have on a presidency marked by its disregard for legal norms and constitutional boundaries. The shadow docket has become a tool for circumventing legislative oversight and for consolidating power in the executive branch, setting a dangerous precedent for future administrations.

The Supreme Court's role in a constitutional democracy is to safeguard the rule of law and to ensure that no branch of government exceeds its bounds. When it fails in this duty, it doesn't just fail the parties before it in a specific case; it fails the nation. It contributes to the erosion of the very foundations upon which its authority and legitimacy rest.

Conclusion: A Call to Vigilance

Waldman's analysis is not just an indictment of the current Supreme Court; it's a clarion call for vigilance. The use of the shadow docket to enact significant policy changes without public scrutiny or accountability represents a fundamental threat to the constitutional order. It's a method of governance by fiat, not by law, and it's a development that every citizen, regardless of political affiliation, should view with concern.

As we navigate these troubled waters, it's essential to remember that the structures of governance and the rules of law are not just abstract concepts; they are the bedrock of democracy. When they are undermined, it's not just policies that are affected—it's the very idea of government of the people, by the people, for the people.
