Trump’s gold smartphone company just hit another roadblock

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 4, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Mirage of Trump’s “Made in USA” Smartphone

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a press conference in the Roosevelt Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Supporters of President Donald Trump eager to buy one of his branded smartphones will now have to wait a little longer.

The Daily Beast reported Monday that the official release of Trump’s gold smartphone — dubbed the T1 — has now been pushed back to March. This marks the third delay that customers have had to endure since the phones were first announced in June of 2025.

Initially, customers were told that the $499 smartphone (which required a $100 down payment to pre-order) would be ready by August of 2025. Then, Trump Mobile announced that the phones would be released before the end of 2025. But in December, the T1 phones had been delayed once again, with the company blaming a slowdown in shipping due to the government shutdown last fall.

The T1 phone has been accused of marketing to customers using “deceptive practices,” according to a complaint filed with the Federal Trade Commission by Rep. Robert Garcia (D-Calif.) and Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) last month. The Democratic lawmakers took issue with Trump’s company claiming that the phones would be made in the United States — a claim the company’s website deleted within days of the first T1 announcement.

“To claim that a product is ‘Made in USA,’ marketers must meet a very specific set of FTC standards, violations of which come with civil penalties,” read the letter from the two lawmakers.

Trump Mobile is renting its network coverage from T-Mobile, which requires specific certification. According to the Beast, the certification process “involves a consultation, a series of compatibility and functionality tests, and a final review.”

Todd Weaver, who is the founder and CEO of phone company Purism (which is the United States’ sole smartphone manufacturer), told NBC News last year that it is effectively impossible to not source at least some parts from China. Weaver said a certain crystal that powers a smartphone’s motherboard is only available in China.

“There just isn’t a company yet providing that single crystal,” Weaver said.

Our Analysis:

The Mirage of Trump’s “Made in USA” Smartphone

In the latest episode of What Will Trump Brand Next?, we’ve been treated to a saga that could only be termed as the Trump T1 Smartphone Debacle. The grand unveiling of this so-called gold smartphone, branded with the ex-president’s name, has hit its third delay. Trump enthusiasts, who have been on tenterhooks since June of 2025, now face the grim reality of waiting until March for their precious devices. Initially priced at a hefty $499—with a $100 down payment for those eager to secure their allegiance in mobile form—customers have been left in the lurch, victims of a promise as unstable as the man’s previous business ventures.

The narrative spun by Trump Mobile—a venture that promised a smartphone “Made in USA”—has quickly unraveled, exposing what appears to be a classic Trumpian move: bold claims with scant regard for reality or regulation. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC), under the watchful eyes of Rep. Robert Garcia (D-Calif.) and Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.), has been alerted to potentially deceptive practices. These claims of domestic manufacturing vanished from the company’s website faster than a tweet from Trump’s now-defunct Twitter account, following the first announcement of the T1.

Let’s be crystal clear: to adorn a product with the label of ‘Made in USA’, one must not merely wave a flag and hope for the best. The FTC has stringent standards that are clearly not just guidelines but requirements—requirements that seem to have been treated with the same respect Trump shows the truth. Swiftly, the claim disappeared into the ether, perhaps in the same place where all his other unfulfilled promises reside.

But the deeper issue at hand is not just about false advertising or another delayed product launch. It’s about the feasibility of such a claim in the current global tech landscape. Todd Weaver, CEO of Purism, sheds light on a stark reality: the impossibility of producing a smartphone today without sourcing at least some components from China. A certain crystal, essential for the smartphone’s motherboard, is only available in China—a fact that punctures the balloon of Trump Mobile’s ‘Made in USA’ fantasy.

Renting network coverage from T-Mobile, Trump Mobile not only faces challenges in delivering a phone but also in ensuring it meets the necessary certifications for functionality and compatibility. The process, as described, involves rigorous testing—a concept seemingly alien to a brand more accustomed to the art of the deal than the art of compliance.

As Trump Mobile’s T1 phone saga drags on, it becomes a metaphor for the broader Trump brand: much ado about nothing, wrapped in gold foil, with a hefty price tag attached. The delay and the drama surrounding the phone’s release, combined with the deceptive marketing tactics, serve as a reminder of the perils of believing in a brand that consistently overpromises and underdelivers.

In the grand scheme of things, this smartphone fiasco is a small blip in the Trump universe. Yet, it’s emblematic of a larger pattern of behavior—flouting rules, skirting regulations, and exploiting loyal supporters’ trust. As we await the next chapter in this saga, one can’t help but wonder: What will Trump brand next? And more importantly, will anyone ever learn?
