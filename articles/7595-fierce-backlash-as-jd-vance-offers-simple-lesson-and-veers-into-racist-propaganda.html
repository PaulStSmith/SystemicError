Fierce backlash as JD Vance offers 'simple lesson' — and veers into racist propaganda

Vice President JD Vance was blasted online for cranking up a racist message during a speech Tuesday in Pennsylvania on the economy.

The vice president addressed the affordability crisis in the crucial swing state a week after President Donald Trump attempted to do the same. While Trump claimed the issue was a Democratic “hoax,” Vance preached patience and blamed higher housing costs on illegal immigration.

“Why have rents gone down for four consecutive months?” Vance said. “Because we’re starting to get those illegal aliens out of the United States of America. Those criminals, those gang members, those people who are taking homes that ought by right to go to the people in this room, and your children and your grandchildren. It’s simple economics. If you have fewer people, fewer illegal aliens, trying to buy homes that means American citizens are finally going to be able to afford a home again. It’s very, very simple economics.”

Vance’s remarks, which came at a Uline shipping supplies distribution facility near Allentown, were met with fierce backlash.

“The administration is entirely post-truth,” posted attorney Ken White.

“Getting more and more clear that the [White House] has nothing left to offer on [economic] policy except zero sum lebensraum posting,” noted economist Joey Politano. “You can tell JD’s been stewing in Euro/UK/Canada immigration discourse too much because no American’s primary complaint about undocumented immigrants is that they contribute too little to housing supply. You hear it more in countries with more public housing & fewer immigrants in construction.”

“S--t is way too expensive still [and] that’s very clearly not why rent has gone down in certain places!!!!” replied Zeteo’s Asawin Suebsaeng.

“’Gang activity inflates rents’ is too racist and stupid for most people but JD Vance isn’t most people,” wrote popular Bluesky user Grudgie the Whale.

“I’m pretty pessimistic about Americans in general but not so sure ‘everything sucks because of illegal immigrants’ is really going to work at this point,” opined Bluesky user Brent is Saw Gerrera-pilled.

“JD Vance blaming immigrants for housing costs while standing in front of a company that literally lobbies against affordable housing is performance art,” stated progressive influencer Alex Cole.

“Vance lying about rents in order to demonize immigrants,” wrote Bluesky user Mark Moffett. “The racism is blatant.”

“Oh look JD Vance ascribing racism to something that happens every year,” added Bluesky user Desarrayed, linking to a Fox News report showing trended lower during winter months.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: JD Vance’s Economic Fallacies and Racist Rhetoric

In a recent speech in Pennsylvania, Vice President JD Vance decided to take a detour into the abyss of xenophobic economic analysis, blaming higher housing costs on illegal immigration. This approach not only showcases a disturbingly simplistic understanding of economics but also a willingness to fan the flames of racism for political gain.

Scapegoating Immigrants for Economic Woes

Vance’s argument rests on a grossly misleading premise: that the expulsion of undocumented immigrants directly correlates with a decrease in housing costs. He boldly claims, “Why have rents gone down for four consecutive months? Because we’re starting to get those illegal aliens out of the United States of America.” This assertion is a textbook example of scapegoating, a tactic as old as politics itself, where a complex issue is blamed on a vulnerable group to distract from more substantive causes and solutions.

The Misuse of Economic Principles

Calling Vance’s explanation “simple economics” is an insult to the field. The housing market is influenced by a myriad of factors, including interest rates, supply and demand, zoning laws, and indeed, immigration. However, to single out undocumented immigrants as the primary driver of housing costs is not just overly simplistic; it’s factually incorrect. Economists like Joey Politano have pointed out the absurdity of Vance’s stance, noting that the American discourse around undocumented immigrants rarely centers on their impact on the housing supply.

A Tone-Deaf Performance

Vance’s choice of venue—a Uline shipping supplies distribution facility—adds a layer of irony to his performance. As highlighted by progressive influencer Alex Cole, standing in front of a company that lobbies against affordable housing while blaming immigrants for housing costs is nothing short of hypocritical. It underscores a disconnect between the purported concerns for ordinary Americans and the political and economic interests Vance represents.

Racism Disguised as Economic Policy

The backlash Vance received underscores a broader societal exhaustion with racist dog whistles disguised as economic policy. Phrases like “those criminals, those gang members,” used to describe undocumented immigrants, serve no purpose other than to dehumanize and scapegoat an entire group of people. Bluesky users and commentators like Mark Moffett and Asawin Suebsaeng have rightly called out Vance’s rhetoric for what it is: a blatant display of racism.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Distraction

JD Vance’s speech is a dangerous distraction from the real issues plaguing the American economy. By focusing on undocumented immigrants as the source of America’s housing crisis, Vance not only spreads misinformation but also diverts attention from meaningful solutions that could address the root causes of high housing costs, such as reforming zoning laws, increasing the supply of affordable housing, and tackling income inequality.

In the end, Vance’s comments reflect a broader strategy within certain political factions to exploit economic anxieties for electoral gain. It’s a strategy that relies on division and discrimination, one that should be met with fierce resistance and a demand for policies grounded in reality, not racism.
