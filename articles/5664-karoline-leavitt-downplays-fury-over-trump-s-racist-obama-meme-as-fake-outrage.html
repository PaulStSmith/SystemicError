Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Deflection and Degradation

In a move that would make even the most seasoned cynics balk, the White House Press Secretary, Karoline Leavitt, decided that the best response to an indefensibly racist act was not an apology, remorse, or even silence, but rather a scathing attack on those who dared to call it out. After President Donald Trump posted a video depicting Barack and Michelle Obama with their heads superimposed on the bodies of apes, the backlash was swift and entirely justified. However, rather than addressing the blatantly racist nature of the video, Leavitt chose to blast the media and invoke the tired and wholly irrelevant defense of “fake outrage.”

“This is from an internet meme video depicting President Trump as the King of the Jungle and Democrats as characters from the Lion King,” Leavitt told multiple news outlets, as if this explanation somehow mitigates the racism on display. The comparison is not only ludicrous but deeply insulting, reducing the first Black president and first lady of the United States to cartoon caricatures in a manner steeped in centuries-old racist imagery.

What’s even more alarming is Leavitt’s dismissive directive to the media: “Please stop the fake outrage and report on something today that actually matters to the American public.” This statement is a masterclass in deflection, implying that the media’s focus on a clearly racist act by the President of the United States is somehow unwarranted and trivial. It is a blatant attempt to downplay the severity of the president’s actions and to redirect attention away from a deeply troubling display of bigotry.

In a stark contrast, California Governor Gavin Newsom’s response on X (formerly Twitter) cuts through the noise with a call to action that is both clear and necessary: “Disgusting behavior by the President. Every single Republican must denounce this. Now,” he wrote. Newsom understands the gravity of the situation and the importance of unequivocal condemnation from all corners of the political spectrum. His call for Republicans to denounce Trump’s behavior is not just a plea for political solidarity, but a test of moral fortitude in the face of racism.

The Takeaway

What we’re witnessing is not just a failure of the current administration to address its own perpetuation of racism, but an active effort to normalize and deflect criticism through whataboutism and attacks on the free press. The message from Leavitt and, by extension, the White House, is clear: blatant racism will not only be defended but those who dare to challenge it will be attacked and discredited.

It’s a dangerous and all too familiar pattern that undermines the very fabric of democracy and the principles of equality and justice. The media’s role in calling out such behavior is not just important but essential, and attempts to dismiss their reporting as “fake outrage” only serve to highlight the administration’s inability to engage in meaningful self-reflection or to take accountability for its actions.

In the end, Newsom’s response should be the standard, not the exception. The onus is on every elected official, regardless of party affiliation, to denounce racism unequivocally. Anything less is complicity in a system that continues to marginalize and dehumanize based on race. The time for fake outrage is over. The time for real action is now.
