Trump voters do the unthinkable — and prove they aren’t untouchable after all

By Paulo Santos

While the media has covered extensively Democratic successes in the 2025 off-year elections, there is one story that has been dramatically undercovered. This is the fact that the 2025 Virginia and New Jersey gubernatorial races show that Democrats can win over Trump voters.

Granted, these are not dramatic slices of the Trump coalition, but they are enough in these hyperpolarized times to win elections.

According to CNN polling, in New Jersey, Rep. Mikie Sherrill, in her race for governor, was able to win 7 percent of those who had voted for Donald Trump in 2024. Interestingly, the Virginia exit polling data shows that Rep. Abigail Spanberger won an identical 7 percent of Trump voters.

The New York Times’ Nate Cohn is one of the few journalists who has pointed to the New Jersey and Virginia Democrats’ ability to win over Trump voters. He concludes that:

Instead, the two Democrats won so decisively because they also flipped a crucial sliver of voters who said they supported Mr. Trump in 2024. Ms. Sherrill and Ms. Spanberger both won 7 percent of Mr. Trump’s supporters, according to the exit polls. It may not seem like much to flip 7 percent of Mr. Trump’s backers, but consider: When a voter flips, it adds one voter to one party and also deducts one from the other, making it twice as significant as turning out a new voter.

Looking at the exit polling data makes it clear that while the Democrats’ margins in New Jersey and Virginia were helped by increased Democratic turnout, winning over 2024 Trump voters was critically important.

One of the key parts of the Trump coalition has always been strong and even almost overwhelming support from rural voters. An analysis by Politico of the Virginia gubernatorial race shows that:

Spanberger’s victory was largely driven by massive turnout in northern and eastern Virginia’s urban areas. But she picked up support across the state’s deep-red central and western counties, where Trump’s tariffs have hit the manufacturing and agricultural industries especially hard. Even as her GOP opponent won most of those places, Spanberger posed the best performance by a statewide Democratic candidate in several cycles, according to a POLITICO analysis of voting data in the localities classified as “rural” by the federal government.

To her great credit, Spanberger targeted rural voters and consistently hammered away on how the Trump administration’s tariff policies were hurting them. In comparison with former Vice President Kamala Harris’s performance in 2024, Spanberger outperformed Harris’ margin in 48 of Virginia’s 52 rural localities. The exit polling shows that Spanberger won 46 percent of rural voters — an eight-point deficit to Republican candidate Lieutenant Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, and a 19-point swing from 2021 gubernatorial Democratic nominee Terry McAuliffe’s 27-point disadvantage.

There is also data in the exit polling data indicating that Democrats won back in 2025 Hispanic voters who backed Trump in 2024. The Washington Post reports:

This year, most Democratic statewide candidates won Latino voters by at least 30 points in exit polls, re-creating the margins their party held before 2024. In New Jersey, 18 percent of Latino voters who backed Trump last year cast their ballot for the Democratic gubernatorial candidate, exit poll data showed.

The fact that Democrats won over Trump voters in 2025 has profound implications for Democrats in both the 2026 midterms and the 2028. The message is clear: Some Trump voters will back Democrats if the candidates reach them where they are and talk to them about the issues that they care about most. To assume that all Trump voters are absolutely committed to Trump no matter what the circumstances is a mistaken assumption that only hurts Democrats. Successful politics is always about addition.

Hopefully, Democrats learn from their success in 2025 and realize that they can make some Trump voters part of their winning coalition.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Underreported Shift: Democrats Winning Over Trump Voters

In the aftermath of the 2025 off-year elections, a crucial narrative has been remarkably downplayed by the mainstream media: Democrats have successfully chipped away at the Trump voter base, a development with potentially seismic implications for upcoming electoral cycles. While the media circus has been preoccupied with painting a broad stroke of Democratic victories, the subtleties of these wins, particularly in Virginia and New Jersey’s gubernatorial races, offer a far more intriguing story.

Flipping the Trump Vote: A Small but Significant Victory

The fact that Representatives Mikie Sherrill (NJ) and Abigail Spanberger (VA) each won over 7 percent of Trump’s 2024 voters might seem trivial at first glance. However, considering the hyperpolarized state of American politics, this small percentage represents a remarkably significant shift. The narrative here isn’t merely about adding numbers to the Democratic tally; it’s the symbolic value of converting Trump loyalists. This dual effect—subtracting from one side while adding to the other—doubles the impact of flipping each voter.

Nate Cohn of The New York Times is one of the few to shine a spotlight on this phenomenon, emphasizing the decisive nature of these victories that extend beyond mere Democratic turnout. This is a critical piece of analysis that deserves more attention than it’s received, as it underscores a potential weakness in the seemingly impenetrable armor of the Trump coalition.

The Rural Vote and Hispanic Voters: Cracking the Code

The victories of Sherrill and Spanberger were not confined to urban strongholds; they made significant inroads into rural areas, traditionally seen as Republican bastions. Spanberger’s campaign, in particular, targeted rural voters by focusing on the adverse effects of Trump’s tariff policies on local industries. This strategic move not only improved her performance in rural localities but also hinted at a broader discontent that Democrats can tap into.

Moreover, the shift in Hispanic voters back towards Democrats in 2025, reversing the trend from 2024, highlights a critical miscalculation in Republican strategies. Assuming unwavering loyalty from demographic groups based on past support is a flawed approach. Democrats’ success in winning back a significant portion of Hispanic voters by at least 30 points in exit polls is a testament to the fluidity of voter allegiance.

Beyond 2025: Implications for Future Elections

The message from these results is loud and clear: Democrats have a viable path to winning over Trump voters. This isn’t about transforming the entire Trump base overnight but rather about recognizing the potential for strategic inroads into this demographic. The assumption that all Trump supporters are unswervingly loyal to the former president is not only condescending but strategically unsound.

Looking ahead to the 2026 midterms and the 2028 presidential election, Democrats would do well to internalize the lessons from 2025. Engaging with voters on issues they care deeply about, rather than writing off certain groups as unreachable, could be the key to expanding their coalition. Politics, after all, is about addition.

The underreporting of this shift not only does a disservice to the nuanced dynamics of voter behavior but also overlooks a critical strategy that could shape the future of American electoral politics. In a political landscape rife with division and polarization, understanding the motivations and concerns of those across the aisle isn’t just good politics—it’s essential for democracy.

Martin Burns, with his background in journalism and political campaigning, captures the essence of this pivotal moment. Yet, it’s the broader media landscape that now faces a challenge: to look beyond the surface of electoral victories and delve into the transformative shifts occurring beneath. The story of Democrats winning over Trump voters in 2025 deserves more than a footnote in political analysis; it demands a reevaluation of how we understand voter loyalty and political identity in contemporary America.
