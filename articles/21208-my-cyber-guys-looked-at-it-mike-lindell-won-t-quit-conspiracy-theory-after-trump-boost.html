'My cyber guys looked at it': Mike Lindell won't quit conspiracy theory after Trump boost

Based on an article by Michelle Griffith, Minnesota Reformer

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Endorsement Is Not a Boost. It Is a Racket.

The Setup

Donald Trump endorsed Mike Lindell for Minnesota governor, and Lindell instantly treated it as proof of legitimacy. The actual picture in the source is uglier: a cash-starved candidate with unpaid property taxes, a campaign with about $12,000 on hand, and a political operation built on grievance, conspiracy, and a presidential seal of approval.

Who Has Power Here

Trump has the power. Lindell has access. That is the relationship. Trump’s endorsement is not a democratic judgment or a serious governing assessment; it is a loyalty certificate handed to a man whose public identity is fused to election denial and victimhood politics. Lindell is not being elevated because he has demonstrated competence. He is being rewarded because he remains useful to Trump’s movement.

The Numbers Tell The Story

The source does something Lindell cannot do in real time: it checks the record. Lindell claims he raised “upwards of $3 million,” while the campaign finance record cited by the reporter shows just over $1 million raised since December and roughly $12,000 in cash on hand at the end of May. His Tonka Bay property taxes were still unpaid. Those are not trivial side notes. They are the material facts behind a candidate who wants voters to trust him with a state budget while he cannot keep his own obligations current.

Misdirection As Strategy

Lindell’s interview is a demolition of accountability by deflection. When pressed on money, taxes, or public records, he pivots to the press, “attacks,” “fake evidence,” and his supposed persecution by “our own government.” That is the familiar authoritarian trick: turn scrutiny into abuse, turn documentation into conspiracy, and turn personal failure into martyrdom. The article largely lets him perform that routine at length, which makes the reporter’s edits and record checks do the necessary corrective work.

What This Campaign Actually Represents

This is not a serious governing campaign interrupted by controversy. It is a political identity project built out of Trump loyalty, election falsehoods, and resentment toward institutions that still insist on facts. Lindell talks about affordability and tax cuts, but the central promise is not policy competence. It is permission: permission to ignore evidence, permission to dress up self-inflicted failure as persecution, and permission to treat public office as a stage for a movement that confuses fraud allegations with political virtue.

The Larger Pattern

The deeper story is how easily American conservatism now launders private instability through public power. A presidential endorsement can still substitute for qualifications. A candidate’s financial disorder can be recast as patriotic struggle. A record-backed correction can be dismissed as a hit job. This is what happens when a political coalition is organized less around governing than around protecting its own myths. It does not merely tolerate dishonesty. It promotes it, then asks the public to call that leadership.
