Stephen Miller gets fired up over CNN anchor's challenge: 'You walked right into that!'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 8, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller started yelling after CNN anchor Boris Sanchez challenged his claims that terrorist activity is happening in Oregon.

A CNN anchor and White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller got into a fiery exchange Monday afternoon after the anchor challenged him, with Miller saying, “You walked right into that one!”

CNN’s Boris Sanchez interviewed Miller and questioned what the administration planned to do in Oregon, pushing back on claims that the administration is going after terrorist organizations and questioning why the National Guard isn’t heading to Dallas, Texas, after a sniper shooting at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement facility.

That’s when Miller got visibly upset — raising his voice and yelling over Sanchez.

“Because the Dallas Police Department and the governor of Texas — wow, you walked right into that one — have responded to every call for assistance and help,” Miller claimed. “They gave a stand-down order in Chicago, and they gave a stand-down order in Portland. Do you realize that there is 11,000 federal law enforcement officers in Oregon? That’s larger than the size of the FBI; local and state police are resourced to deal with this kind of riotous assembly, but they have refused. They’ve been given a stand-down order. So they’ve arrested some 70 people since June.”

Miller also claimed an “orchestrated campaign of terrorism and violence against ICE officers,” named the Dallas attack, and also alleged that people are publishing photos of ICE agents’ families and “threatening them with murder or threatening with death.”

“And they’re physically attacking them in the street each and every day,” Miller said. “And yet, shamefully, the mayor and governor in Portland and Oregon have refused to render aid, leaving ice officers to street fight every single night against these terrorists.”

He said the administration has the right to federalize the National Guard. And then Sanchez pushed back again. He asked for evidence this time about an alleged “domestic terrorism” campaign.

“You are going to charge someone with terrorism?” Sanchez questioned.

“I’d like to,” Miller responded, and then clarified that no actual charges are underway.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of CNN’s Fiery Exchange with White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller

In a recent interview that could only be described as a political theater of the absurd, CNN’s Boris Sanchez found himself in a heated exchange with White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller. The confrontation, filled with accusations, denials, and the classic diversionary tactics we’ve come to expect from this administration, sheds light on a broader narrative of political manipulation and media engagement.

The Clash Over Oregon and Dallas

Sanchez’s questioning of the administration’s focus on Oregon, while seemingly ignoring the violence in Dallas, Texas, was a legitimate query, given the sniper shooting at an Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facility. Miller’s response, however, was not only evasive but also indicative of a deeply troubling mindset within the administration. Miller’s retort, “You walked right into that one!” reflects not just a gotcha moment but a broader strategy of combative engagement with the press. It’s an attempt to deflect and redirect, rather than address the substance of the query.

The Stand-Down Orders

Miller’s claims of stand-down orders in Chicago and Portland, and the assertion of an 11,000-strong federal law enforcement presence in Oregon, are both startling and revealing. The numbers he throws around—larger than the size of the FBI—are meant to shock and awe. But they beg the question: Why is such a massive federal presence necessary, and what does it say about the administration’s stance on local governance and civil unrest?

The Allegation of Terrorism

Miller’s narrative of an “orchestrated campaign of terrorism and violence against ICE officers” is a classic example of fear-mongering. By painting the protesters as terrorists, the administration seeks to justify its heavy-handed response. However, the lack of evidence provided by Miller, especially when challenged by Sanchez for proof of the alleged domestic terrorism campaign, reveals a disturbing tendency to use serious allegations as political tools without the requisite substantiation.

The Role of Local Authorities

Miller’s criticism of the mayor and governor in Portland and Oregon for their failure to “render aid” to ICE officers, accusing them of leaving officers to “street fight every single night against these terrorists,” is a blatant attempt to shift blame. It ignores the complexities of the situation, including the legitimate grievances of the protesters and the role that federal intervention has played in escalating tensions.

The Right to Federalize the National Guard

Miller’s assertion that the administration has the right to federalize the National Guard is technically accurate but contextually misleading. The implication that this right might be exercised in response to what the administration perceives as domestic terrorism is a slippery slope towards authoritarianism. It’s a stark reminder of the administration’s willingness to leverage federal power against its own citizens.

Conclusion

The exchange between Sanchez and Miller was more than just a fiery debate; it was a microcosm of the current administration’s approach to dissent and unrest. Miller’s responses, characterized by evasion, accusation, and the demonization of protesters, are indicative of a broader strategy of deflection and division. In this political climate, where facts are often disregarded in favor of narrative, it’s more important than ever to scrutinize the statements of those in power.

This incident underscores the need for vigilant, fact-based journalism and public discourse. The stakes are too high, and the consequences of accepting such narratives without scrutiny are too great. It’s a reminder that in the face of political manipulation, the truth is our most valuable, and often our only, weapon.
