Republicans quietly slip 'religious freedom' clause into funding bill

House Republicans have inserted anti-LGBTQ language into a $66 billion must-pass funding bill for the U.S. Department of Homeland Security, effectively granting civil immunity under federal law to individuals and organizations that discriminate against same-sex couples—by citing a religious or moral belief that marriage should be limited to one man and one woman. It also bans the federal government from taking a range of actions against those who hold and act on anti-same-sex marriage beliefs.

Section 544 bans the use of federal funds to take any "discriminatory action" against someone who cites their "sincerely held religious belief" or "moral conviction" that marriage is only "a union of one man and one woman."

A portion of the provision exactly matches language U.S. Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX) urged the House Appropriations Committee to include in 2023 legislation. Rep. Roy cited praise from anti-LGBTQ hate group leader Tony Perkins and other anti-LGBTQ activists in his press release urging inclusion of the amendment in a 2023 bill. It is not known who drafted or approved the current 2025 provision.

Journalist Jamie Dupree, who writes Regular Order at Substack, first reported on the provision in the DHS funding bill.

The language could prohibit the government from withholding federal funds from a federally-funded religious school that fired a teacher who supports same-sex marriage. It could block the IRS from revoking the tax-exempt status of organizations that promote the belief that marriage is only between one man and one woman. It could ban the federal government from taking action against a hospital that receives federal funds if it refused certain services in some cases.

While the language is not found in The Heritage Foundation's Project 2025, some of the core principles in Section 544 echo its recommendations.

Project 2025 calls on the federal government to "Protect faith-based grant recipients from religious liberty violations and maintain a biblically based, social science–reinforced definition of marriage and family," and "Provide robust protections for religious employers," while it denounces "the bullying LGBTQ+ agenda."

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the DHS Funding Bill's Anti-LGBTQ Provision

In an audacious move that reeks of discrimination cloaked in the guise of religious freedom, House Republicans have embedded deeply concerning anti-LGBTQ language into a crucial $66 billion funding bill for the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. This provision, not merely a footnote but a stark statement of intent, seeks to grant civil immunity under federal law to those who discriminate against same-sex couples under the banner of religious or moral convictions. Let’s dissect this poisonous clause and its implications with the precision it deserves.

The Provision of Prejudice

Section 544, the heart of this controversy, essentially acts as a shield for individuals and organizations that wish to deny services, employment, or recognition to same-sex couples by citing a "sincerely held religious belief" or "moral conviction" that marriage is exclusively a union between one man and one woman. This isn't just a step back; it's a leap into a past where discrimination found safe harbor under the law.

Who's Behind This?

The fingerprints of U.S. Rep. Chip Roy (R-TX) are all over this provision. Roy, in an astonishing display of defiance against progress and equality, has not only pushed for this language but also draped his efforts in the approval of anti-LGBTQ hate group leaders like Tony Perkins. The lack of transparency about who exactly drafted or approved the 2025 provision only adds a layer of sinister mystery to the whole endeavor.

The Implications Are Dire

The potential ramifications of this provision are as far-reaching as they are alarming. Consider the following scenarios: - A federally-funded religious school could fire a teacher for supporting same-sex marriage, with no fear of federal repercussions. - Organizations that openly promote discrimination against LGBTQ+ individuals could retain their tax-exempt status. - Hospitals receiving federal funds could deny certain services based on these so-called moral convictions.

Echoes of a Broader Agenda

While this language might not be directly lifted from The Heritage Foundation's Project 2025, the parallels are impossible to ignore. The project's call for the federal government to embrace a "biblically based" definition of marriage and family, and to protect religious employers, mirrors the objectives of Section 544. This is not about safeguarding religious freedom; it's about codifying discrimination.

The Bottom Line: A Dangerous Precedent

Let's be unequivocally clear: this provision is a dangerous precedent that threatens to institutionalize bigotry under the guise of protecting religious freedom. By intertwining discrimination with essential funding for the Department of Homeland Security, House Republicans have ensured that this debate is not just about ideological differences but about the very fabric of equality and justice in the United States.

In a society that values dignity and equality for all, there is no room for laws that discriminate under the cloak of morality. The inclusion of Section 544 in the DHS funding bill is a blatant attempt to erode the rights and protections of the LGBTQ+ community. It's a move that should not only be challenged but vehemently opposed by anyone who believes in the principles of freedom and equality upon which this country was founded.
