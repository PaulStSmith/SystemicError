Frothing MAGA will implode once it sees the true source of its hate

Based on an article by Tara Dublin

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

MAGA Was Not Confused. It Was Organized.

The Real Center of Power

The source is trying to talk to MAGA as if persuasion alone can unwind what is fundamentally a power structure. That is the first mistake and the first clue. The real institutional power has not been sitting in the hands of random followers in red hats. It has sat with Trump, the Republican machinery that normalized him, and the political operators who turned grievance into loyalty and loyalty into obedience.

The letter names the obvious truth more clearly than most political coverage does: Trump is not a passive beneficiary of MAGA. He is the organizer of its discipline.

The Base Is the Instrument

The story frames MAGA as a group trapped in groupthink, and that is not wrong. But groupthink is not an accident here. It is a political technology. Trump gave the movement a permission structure, then kept it moving by rewarding denial and punishing reality. The red hat was never just merch. It was a signal of submission, a public badge that said the tribe mattered more than evidence, civic duty, or even self-interest.

That matters because it shifts the question. The problem is not that millions of people were merely confused. The problem is that confusion was cultivated into political utility.

Scapegoats Are the Fuel

The letter is strongest when it identifies scapegoating as the engine. Trump’s method was simple: identify enemies, repeat the lie, and let followers dump their frustration onto targets that were safer than the man doing the exploiting. “Fake news” was not a slogan. It was a shield against accountability. It trained supporters to treat contradiction as persecution.

That is the larger pattern. Authoritarian politics does not need followers to understand every lie. It only needs them to accept a hierarchy of resentment in which the leader is never responsible and someone weaker is always available to blame.

January 6 Was Not a Breakdown

The source is right to insist that January 6 was not a misunderstanding. It was the logical endpoint of years of conditioning. Once a movement learns to treat a leader’s falsehoods as loyalty tests, violence becomes a possible extension of politics rather than a rupture from it. Trump’s pardons, as the letter notes, do not change that record. They just show how little shame is required to keep the machine running after the fact.

Coverage that treats that day as chaos or madness launders intent. The point was never confusion. The point was coercion.

The Cost Is Material

The letter briefly names tariffs, cuts to agencies, and the claim that red-state families are paying more. That is where the moral story becomes an institutional one. Trumpism does not just ask followers to abandon reality. It takes money from them, weakens the public systems they rely on, and then sells the damage back to them as strength.

This is why the usual language about “working-class conservatives” is so often a dodge. If a political project strips services, inflates costs, and still holds its base through identity discipline, the issue is not lack of information. It is the successful conversion of material harm into tribal loyalty.

What This Story Really Shows

The letter wants to speak to MAGA as if the movement can be reasoned out of itself. That is generous, and probably unrealistic. The deeper truth is harsher: Trump did not merely win followers. He trained them. Republican elites enabled him. Media ecosystems amplified him. Social platforms gave him a megaphone. And millions of people were taught to mistake degradation for belonging.

That is the systemic error. Politics fails when it treats deliberate authoritarian formation as emotional confusion. The danger is not just what Trump believes, or even what his followers believe. It is the alliance between a con artist at the top and an audience conditioned to protect him from reality.
