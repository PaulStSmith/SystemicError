The Free Market, GOP-Style

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 4, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Republicans love the phrase “free market” until consumers start using it.

In Florida, rooftop solar exposed the scam with unusual clarity. A homeowner spends thousands of dollars on panels, takes on the financing risk, the maintenance risk, the roof risk, and the long wait for payoff. Then, after making that private investment, the homeowner uses less electricity from the utility.

In any honest free-market story, that is consumer choice. That is competition. That is exactly what conservatives claim to worship.

But in Florida’s utility politics, it became a problem to be punished.

The 2022 net-metering bill, HB 741/SB 1024, was not merely about making solar customers pay a reasonable fee for grid access. A fixed grid-maintenance charge can be debated. The grid exists. Solar customers still use it for backup, distribution, and reliability. Fine.

The rot was in the phrase “lost revenues.”

The Florida Senate’s own summary of HB 741 said the bill would authorize utilities to petition for certain fixed charges and “recover specified lost revenues” from customer-owned renewable generation. That is the whole game, right there. Not “pay for the wires.” Not “share infrastructure costs.” But compensate the utility for electricity customers did not buy. The bill passed the Florida House and Senate before Gov. Ron DeSantis vetoed it in April 2022. The official bill page records the lost-revenue language and the veto. Florida Senate

That is not capitalism. That is monopoly entitlement with a flag pin.

And of course this happened in Republican-controlled Florida. SB 1024 was filed by Republican Sen. Jennifer Bradley, according to the Senate bill page. Florida Senate Reporting also tied the push to Florida Power & Light, the state’s dominant utility. Axios summarized Miami Herald reporting that FPL wrote legislation aimed at slowing rooftop solar before making political donations to the bill’s eventual sponsors. Axios

Energy and Policy Institute reported that Bradley and Rep. Lawrence McClure had both received tens of thousands of dollars from investor-owned utilities, including NextEra, Duke, and TECO-linked money. Energy and Policy Institute

So the public gets the sermon. The utility gets the statute. The politician gets the check. And the homeowner gets told that saving money is unfair.

This is the real GOP theory of the “free market”: markets are free when corporations are free to exploit consumers. Markets become “distorted” the moment consumers find a way around the tollbooth.

Generate your own power? Lost revenue.

Repair your own tractor? Lost revenue.

Block ads? Lost revenue.

Use open-source software? Lost revenue.

Buy fewer overpriced products? Lost revenue.

At some point, “lost revenue” stops being an accounting term and becomes a political theology: the belief that corporations are entitled not only to sell you something, but to be compensated when you refuse.

That is why the rooftop solar fight matters beyond energy policy. It reveals the central fraud of modern Republican economics. The party wraps itself in free-enterprise rhetoric, but its actual policy instinct is often protection for incumbents, punishment for independence, and fees for anyone who dares to get ahead by a few pennies.

Florida homeowners did exactly what the market supposedly rewards: they invested private capital, reduced demand, and adopted a more efficient technology. The response from the utility-backed political machine was not admiration. It was retaliation.

The market, apparently, is only sacred when the customer loses.
