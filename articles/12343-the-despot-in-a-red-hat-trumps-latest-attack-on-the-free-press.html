The Despot in a Red Hat: Trump’s Latest Attack on the Free Press

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 27, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There are dog whistles, and then there are klaxons. Donald Trump’s recent outburst demanding the revocation of broadcast licenses from networks he accuses of siding with the Democratic Party isn’t just a temper tantrum. It’s a neon-lit sign reading: “Authoritarian ambitions, this way.”

“Networks aren’t allowed to be political pawns for the Democrat Party. It has become so outrageous that, in my opinion, their licenses could, and should, be revoked! MAGA.”

This isn’t hyperbole. This is the actual content of a post from the orange man that sits at the Resolute Desk. He is not merely suggesting criticism. He is advocating using the machinery of government to punish media outlets that dare to challenge him.

The fact he believes media licensing should hinge on political allegiance is grotesque enough. But what’s worse is how many of his followers cheer this as a show of strength rather than a betrayal of democratic principles. They see the free press not as a safeguard of liberty, but as a nuisance to be crushed.

Let’s get the law out of the way: the FCC licenses individual stations, not networks. And it cannot, under the Constitution, revoke a license just because a president doesn’t like their editorial stance. That’s not how the First Amendment works. This isn't a matter of opinion. It’s settled constitutional law. The government cannot retaliate against speech based on viewpoint. To suggest otherwise is to attack the very bedrock of American democracy.

But Trump has never let legality get in the way of a good purge. He has, time and again, made it abundantly clear that he sees government as a tool of personal vengeance. From the DOJ to the IRS, from immigration enforcement to military deployments, every institution becomes, in his hands, a cudgel to smash enemies and reward sycophants. This latest salvo at the media is simply another brick in his autocratic wall.

Let’s remember: the press is not a branch of government. It was never meant to be. Its role is to shine light into the shadows of power, not echo the throne. Trump's whining about media bias is a red herring; what he wants isn't fairness, it's fealty.

This is not about a biased report or a misleading headline. This is about silencing an entire sector of civil society. The kind of control Trump craves over the media is not democratic, it’s despotic. It's Viktor Orbán's Hungary. It's Erdogan's Turkey. It's Putin's Russia. You know, the strongmen he so admires.

And don’t think for a moment that this is harmless political theater. Words like this have consequences. The mob that stormed the Capitol believed it was defending truth. They believed the media was the enemy. They acted on the very rhetoric Trump is peddling again. This isn’t about policy differences. This is about incitement, intimidation, and authoritarian aspiration.

The press is messy, flawed, and occasionally maddening. But that’s the price of freedom. You don’t get to demand uncritical praise in a republic. You earn trust. You answer scrutiny. You don’t threaten to revoke licenses like a third-rate dictator.

Let’s be brutally clear: No president should ever threaten the press with state punishment. Not in jest. Not in a social media rant. Not ever. That’s not just bad optics. That’s a betrayal of the oath of office. It’s the political equivalent of shouting “fire” in a crowded theater, then locking the exits.

So, to the cowards in the GOP who continue to shrug and enable this nonsense, to the cynics who say “it’s just Trump being Trump,” and to the media outlets who treat this as just another news cycle blip: Shame on you. History has seen this movie before. The ending isn’t pretty.

Trump's call to silence the press isn't a gaffe. It's a threat. One we should take seriously. Because once the lights of a free press go out, we all stumble in the dark.
