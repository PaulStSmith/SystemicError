The Towing Tale That Was Too Good to Be True

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 26, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Why The Truth MattersThe Fantasy Is A SymptomWhat We Can Actually DoDon’t Settle For Symbolism

It spread like wildfire—a perfect blend of justice, audacity, and street-level resistance: a claim that a group of tow truck drivers in Los Angeles were stalking unmarked ICE vans, waiting for the agents to step out so they could legally tow the vehicles for illegal parking.

Cue applause. Cue memes. Cue collective sighs of relief.

But here’s the hard truth: it didn’t happen.

The image that accompanied the viral posts—of a tow truck hauling away a white van—was almost certainly AI-generated. The lighting was too polished, the cityscape too clean, and crucially, there was no driver in sight. The truck itself resembled an Isuzu cab-over model, not the kind you typically see in U.S. municipal towing fleets. There were no logos, no news reports, no witness statements. Just vibes and virality.

And yes, we wanted it to be true. In a country where ICE operates with near-impunity—detaining immigrants in unmarked vans, often without warrants or identification—the idea of someone flipping the script and using municipal parking laws to hobble their operations was undeniably satisfying.

But we, the side fighting for justice, can’t afford to build our momentum on fiction.

Why The Truth Matters

It’s tempting to let this one slide. It’s not a harmful lie, right? It’s not propaganda, it’s just a little fantasy. But this kind of viral mythmaking carries real risks:

  • It creates false hope. People may believe that resistance is already happening and thus feel less urgency to act themselves.

  • It erodes credibility. When supporters of immigrant justice share unverified or fake stories, it makes it easier for ICE apologists to dismiss real abuses as just more "social media exaggeration."

  • It distracts from action. Memes aren’t a substitute for organizing, witnessing, calling local representatives, or showing up at ICE checkpoints.

The Fantasy Is A Symptom

This story went viral because it tapped into something real: the hunger for accountability. ICE is a rogue agency. Its agents operate like paramilitary units with the barest thread of oversight. They intimidate communities. They sidestep due process. They tear families apart under the guise of enforcement.

And we’ve been told—implicitly and explicitly—that there’s nothing we can do about it. So when a meme suggests someone is doing something, we cling to it.

What We Can Actually Do

If you were inspired by the image of a tow truck driver reclaiming public space from ICE impunity, good. Keep that fire—but direct it toward something real:

  • Show up: Community defense groups exist across the U.S. to monitor ICE activity and support vulnerable neighbors. Find one. Join one.

  • Know your rights: ICE often banks on people not knowing what they can legally say or refuse. Know your rights and help others learn them.

  • Call for abolition: This isn’t just about rogue agents—it’s about a system built to terrorize and detain. ICE is beyond reform. It must be abolished.

Don’t Settle For Symbolism

The dream of tow truck drivers ambushing ICE is beautiful precisely because it flips the dynamic of power. But it’s just that—a dream. Let it remind you of the power we wish we had, and then channel that into real, collective action.

We don’t need memes. We need movement.

And we won’t dismantle ICE with Photoshop. We’ll do it with truth, solidarity, and relentless pressure.

ICE must be abolished. No amount of rebranding, restructuring, or reform can change the fact that it was built to hunt, detain, and dehumanize. The agency’s very foundation is rooted in fear and cruelty. We don’t want better cages. We want no cages. The only just future is one where ICE no longer exists.
