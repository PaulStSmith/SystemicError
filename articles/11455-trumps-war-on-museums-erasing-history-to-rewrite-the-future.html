Trump’s War on Museums: Erasing History to Rewrite the Future

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20256 min read

Donald Trump’s latest outburst is a roadmap to authoritarian revisionism. In one of his trademark Truth Social rants, he railed against the Smithsonian and other museums for daring to present America’s true history—particularly its history of slavery. According to Trump, these institutions are “out of control” for teaching how horrific slavery actually was. He claims they should focus instead on “success” and “brightness.” He’s even ordered his lawyers to find ways to police museums the same way he has attacked colleges and universities. This is more than a rant: it’s an attempt to seize control over the nation’s memory.

Let’s be crystal clear: museums aren’t being “woke.” They are doing the work of historians, curators, and educators—telling the story of this nation in its fullness, not in a sterilized Disney version of white innocence. And that, apparently, is what infuriates Trump and his movement: the idea that Americans might confront the brutal realities of their past.

Slavery Wasn’t a Footnote—It Was the Foundation

Slavery wasn’t an embarrassing side hustle for the early United States; it was the backbone of its economy. Enslaved people grew the cotton that fueled Northern textile mills. They built the roads, tilled the soil, laid the foundations for the very buildings in which laws were written and enforced. The White House itself was constructed by enslaved labor. Wall Street banks made fortunes financing the slave trade. The U.S. Constitution counted enslaved people as three-fifths of a human being to bolster white slaveholding states’ political power. This was not incidental—it was systemic.

And yet, Trump wants to erase this truth, to reduce it to a whisper or eliminate it altogether. Why? Because acknowledging slavery undermines the mythology of American innocence he and his followers cling to. It forces the uncomfortable recognition that the country’s wealth, power, and institutions were built on exploitation, theft, and violence. It demands that patriotism be grounded in honesty, not denial. And denial is precisely what Trump is selling.

The Smithsonian Doesn’t Preach Despair—It Preaches Truth and Triumph

Trump’s claim that museums only talk about how “bad slavery was” is not only dishonest, it reveals that he has either never set foot in the National Museum of African American History and Culture or simply doesn’t care what it actually contains. The museum’s very design takes visitors on a journey. Its lowest floors cover the horror—the transatlantic slave trade, the violence of bondage, the stripping away of humanity. But as you ascend, the narrative turns toward resilience, abolition, the Harlem Renaissance, the Civil Rights Movement, and the ongoing struggle for justice. By the top floors, you’re immersed in music, literature, sports, politics, and the undeniable brilliance of Black achievement.

Far from a one-note dirge, the museum is a living testament to survival and success against all odds. But Trump can’t stomach that, because even the celebration of triumph highlights the brutality that had to be overcome. He doesn’t want the story of resistance and resilience told; he wants a story with no villains, no victims—just a glossy brochure version of America that never existed.

Fascism 101: Control the Past, Control the Future

Trump’s promise to unleash his lawyers on museums is not some throwaway line. It’s part of a broader pattern: dismantling any institution that resists ideological conformity. He already launched attacks on schools and universities, branding them as “woke indoctrination camps.” He’s targeted libraries, egging on book bans. Now he’s aiming at museums. What’s next? Archives? Historical societies? Documentaries?

This is fascism in practice. Authoritarian regimes always start with history. They don’t just silence journalists; they silence historians. They don’t just burn books; they dictate curricula. In Trump’s imagined America, museums would no longer be spaces for truth-telling but propaganda factories, celebrating sanitized stories of greatness and ignoring the blood and chains beneath it.

His rhetoric—“WOKE IS BROKE”—isn’t just juvenile sloganeering. It’s a cudgel to delegitimize anything that challenges white grievance politics. For Trump, a museum that documents slavery is “anti-American.” A curriculum that acknowledges systemic racism is “Marxist.” A library that includes Toni Morrison or James Baldwin is “dangerous.” His goal is not to preserve history but to neuter it.

The Hypocrisy of Trump’s “Victimhood” Politics

Let’s not miss the irony: Trump and his followers constantly rail against “cancel culture,” painting themselves as defenders of free speech. But when faced with historical truths that challenge their fragile myths, they are the first to demand censorship. They are not opponents of censorship—they are its most eager practitioners. They just want it weaponized in their favor.

Trump wants to “cancel” museums that acknowledge slavery, “cancel” universities that teach critical thinking, “cancel” any media outlet that dares fact-check him. His entire political identity is built on grievance and erasure. He plays the eternal victim, convincing his followers that their very survival depends on silencing others.

That’s the heart of MAGA: not patriotism, but paranoia. Not freedom, but suppression. Not history, but mythology.

Why This Matters: Erasure Enables Repetition

When Trump rages that museums shouldn’t show “how bad slavery was,” he is not merely whining about exhibits. He is laying the groundwork for a dangerous ideology: one in which slavery is minimized, Jim Crow is whitewashed, lynchings are ignored, and civil rights struggles are reframed as “attacks on tradition.” When you erase the past, you make it easier to repeat.

The Smithsonian exists to ensure we don’t repeat. To remind us that freedom wasn’t handed out like candy—it was fought for, tooth and nail. To show that progress is not automatic, that rights can be lost as easily as they are won. Trump wants to gut that reminder because it threatens the fantasy world he’s selling—a world where oppression never happened and therefore doesn’t need addressing.

Museums as Battlegrounds of Memory

The fight over museums is not just about dusty exhibits. It’s about who controls America’s story. Do we tell the truth, or do we tell a lie? Do we honor those who suffered and resisted, or do we bury their memory under platitudes? Museums are sacred because they anchor national memory. If they are turned into propaganda halls, the anchor is cut loose, and the ship drifts into dangerous waters.

Trump understands this, even if his base doesn’t. That’s why he lashes out. He knows that memory is power. If he can control the story of the past, he can dictate the terms of the future.

The Danger of Applause

The most disturbing part of Trump’s rant isn’t his words. It’s the inevitable roar of approval from his supporters. These are people who don’t want truth; they want comfort. They don’t want to wrestle with America’s contradictions; they want to bask in delusions. They don’t want history—they want bedtime stories.

But democracies are not sustained by bedtime stories. They are sustained by a citizenry willing to face hard truths and act on them. Trump’s supporters are cheering their own infantilization, applauding ignorance as if it were strength. That’s not patriotism. That’s self-sabotage.

Conclusion: Museums Must Stand Firm

Trump’s war on museums is a war on history itself. It’s a declaration that truth is expendable, that memory can be sacrificed for political convenience. But museums are not just buildings with artifacts. They are guardians of collective memory, keepers of stories that authoritarianism depends on silencing.

The question isn’t whether Trump will rant. He will. The question is whether institutions, educators, curators, and citizens will bow to his threats—or stand firm. Because once you let a demagogue dictate history, you have already surrendered the future.

America doesn’t need less history. It needs more. More honesty. More reckoning. More truth. The Smithsonian and every museum like it are not “out of control.” They are doing the hardest, most patriotic work imaginable: forcing a nation to remember what it would rather forget.

And that is precisely why Trump wants to shut them up.
