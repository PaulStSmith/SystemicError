Trump couldn't wreck the White House without help from 'pathetic' Republicans: ex-lawmaker

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 24, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trumpian Demolition: A Metaphor for Republican Silence and Complicity

A demolition crew takes apart the facade of the East Wing of the White House, where President Donald Trump’s proposed ballroom is being built, in Washington, D.C. on Oct. 21, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump’s demolition of the White House East Wing to build a ballroom is more than just a metaphor, according to a former Republican congressman.

Demolition crews set to work this week pulling down the East Wing this week, just two days after nationwide No Kings protests against the president and his policies, and former GOP lawmaker Joe Walsh posed a question to Trump’s enablers on his “Social Contract” Substack page.

“If Donald Trump next week began demolishing the Washington Monument — because he wanted to change the structure of the Washington Monument and make it look more like Mar-a-Lago or the shape of a T for Trump or whatever — would any Republican say anything?” Walsh asked.

“If Trump took a backhoe to the Washington Monument, if Trump took a bulldozer to the Washington Monument, if Donald Trump on his own next week began tearing down part of the Washington Monument or tearing the whole monument down and restructuring it, would any of his supporters,” he added, “would any Republican say anything?”

That’s not as ludicrous as it might sound, Walsh argued, saying that no Republicans or anyone in right-wing media has objected to his destruction of the White House.

“The entire process that one must go through to restructure, to change the structure of the White House — Trump ignored all of it,” Walsh wrote. “Didn’t involve Congress at all. No vetting at all. No approval by any of the historic groups, the committees, the commissions, the National Park Service. No approval. No permits.”

“Trump ignored all of it,” the former Tea Party lawmaker added. “He’s doing what he’s doing to the White House all by himself. One man has taken a wrecking ball to the historic landmark that is the White House, the people’s house. And not one f------ Republican has said a thing. Not one Trump supporter has screamed and shouted. Nobody on Fox News has made a big deal of it.”

What he’s doing to the White House is the same thing he’s done to the nation since entering politics a decade ago, Walsh argued, and he said Republicans have stood aside and let him do whatever he wants.

“How sick and sad and scary and pathetic is that?” he wrote. “You see, Trump is a problem, a big problem. He’s everything our Founders feared. He’s an utterly corrupt, dishonest, cruel, ignorant, psychopathic, lawless fascist. That’s a big, big problem to have that in the White House.”

The Trumpian Demolition: A Metaphor for Republican Silence and Complicity

In an act that seems ripped straight from the pages of a dystopian novel, President Donald Trump has embarked on a physical and metaphorical demolition of the White House East Wing to construct a ballroom. This audacious move, coming hot on the heels of nationwide No Kings protests, is not just a testament to Trump’s unyielding vanity but also a glaring symbol of the Republican Party’s silent complicity in the face of his bulldozer governance.

Former GOP congressman Joe Walsh raises a chilling hypothetical in his “Social Contract” Substack page: if Trump were to set his sights on the Washington Monument, transforming it into a grotesque monument to his own ego, would there be any outcry from his enablers within the party? This is not mere hyperbole. The destruction of the East Wing, with nary a peep from the right, sets a precedent that is both dangerous and unprecedented.

“The entire process that one must go through to restructure, to change the structure of the White House — Trump ignored all of it,” writes Walsh. No Congressional involvement, no vetting, no approvals from historical preservation or regulatory bodies. Trump, in his typical fashion, bypassed all democratic and procedural safeguards, wielding power with the finesse of a tyrant rather than the responsibility of a democratically elected leader.

The silence from the Republican Party and its media mouthpieces is deafening. It’s not just the lack of outcry over the demolition of a part of America’s heritage that’s troubling; it’s the broader implication that Trump can do no wrong in the eyes of those who should be holding him accountable. Fox News, along with a chorus of Trump supporters, has seemingly lost its voice when it comes to critiquing the president’s more autocratic whims.

Walsh’s scathing critique of Trump as “an utterly corrupt, dishonest, cruel, ignorant, psychopathic, lawless fascist” is a stark reminder of the gravity of the situation. Trump’s actions, both physical and metaphorical, reflect a broader assault on American democracy and its foundational values. The demolition of the East Wing is not just about the construction of a ballroom; it’s a symbol of the erosion of democratic norms and the unchecked rise of autocracy in the land of the free.

The Republican Party’s silence in the face of Trump’s actions is not just complicity; it’s an endorsement of his tactics and an abdication of their constitutional duties. This is not merely a political issue; it’s a moral one. The question now is not if the GOP will stand up to Trump, but whether it can ever recover from the damage it has allowed him to inflict on the party, its principles, and the country.

As we delve deeper into this era of political surrealism, the destruction of the East Wing serves as a powerful metaphor for the current state of American politics: a once-sturdy edifice now compromised by the whims of a single man, and the silence of those who have the power to stop him but choose not to. How “sick and sad and scary and pathetic” indeed.
