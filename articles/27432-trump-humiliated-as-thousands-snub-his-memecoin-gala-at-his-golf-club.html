Trump humiliated as thousands snub his memecoin gala at his golf club

Based on an article by Frank Yemi

Paulo SantosNovember 24, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A presidential sales event with few buyers leftThe office is part of the productMusk replaces the missing reckoning

Donald Trump’s latest $TRUMP memecoin gala drew only a couple dozen investors to his golf club in Sterling, Virginia, on Sunday, according to AlterNet—despite invitations reportedly going to thousands. The collapse in attendance tracked the collapse in the product: a token that peaked near $73 after its January 2025 launch was worth roughly 85 cents at the time of the report, leaving investors with enormous losses after Trump had already earned more than $600 million from the venture.

A presidential sales event with few buyers left

The gala was billed as a VIP event for people holding $TRUMP tokens. But the threshold for entry had fallen to as little as $518 invested, AlterNet reported. At the first memecoin dinner in May 2025, attendees needed holdings worth at least $55,000 to qualify.

That decline measures more than waning enthusiasm. It shows how thoroughly the venture’s hierarchy had inverted. The presidency supplied the brand, Trump’s golf club supplied the venue, and investors supplied the money and market demand. Once the token had lost more than 98 percent of its peak value, the remaining holders were offered another opportunity to gather around the man who had already profited.

Many declined. AlterNet reported that most of the largest stakeholders did not attend, while those who did were initially left outside in the rain because of delays. The Daily Beast reported that guests eventually received bags containing Trump “Victory” perfume, a “Coin Club” bookmark and a $TRUMP-branded hat. The four largest token holders in attendance were awarded 18-karat gold watches; each reportedly had at least $1 million invested.

The gifts preserved the language of exclusivity after the market had stripped away much of its substance. An event once restricted to people with tens of thousands of dollars in tokens was now trying to fill seats with holders of a few hundred dollars’ worth.

The office is part of the product

Kevin O’Leary, former NBA player Tristan Thompson and boxer Floyd Mayweather appeared before Trump entered, according to AlterNet. Trump delivered prepared remarks and then opened the floor to questions.

That scene matters because this was not merely a celebrity promoter tending to a failed endorsement. It was the sitting president appearing at his own property to encourage interest in a financial asset bearing his name—an asset from which he had reportedly made hundreds of millions of dollars while buyers absorbed the crash.

The report does not establish what each attendee paid, when each bought or whether each sold. It does establish the venture’s basic allocation of power and consequence: Trump controlled the political brand and benefited from the token, while holders bore the exposure to its falling price. Calling them “investors” can obscure that asymmetry. They could buy access to the coin and, for some, a room with the president. They could not control the conduct or prestige that gave the token its speculative appeal.

The gala therefore functioned as another sales instrument. Access to presidential attention was not incidental to the commodity; it was among the commodity’s principal attractions.

Musk replaces the missing reckoning

When one attendee asked about Elon Musk—who had reportedly declared himself “not MAGA” after Republican midterm losses—Trump answered with a familiar mixture of praise and insult.

“He’s 80 percent super-genius and 20 percent I refuse to say what that is,” Trump said. “He’s a very unusual guy.”

The exchange gave the room a piece of political theater while diverting attention from the transaction surrounding it. The consequential question at a gathering of battered token holders was not Trump’s latest appraisal of another billionaire. It was why the president was using his office, name and property to sustain interest in an asset that had enriched him as its value disintegrated for buyers.

The sparse crowd supplied its own answer. Whatever loyalty, access or promotional spectacle once drove demand, thousands of invitees reportedly decided the offer was no longer worth accepting. Trump still had the presidency, the golf club and the proceeds. Many of his customers had an 85-cent token—and not even enough interest left to collect the hat.
