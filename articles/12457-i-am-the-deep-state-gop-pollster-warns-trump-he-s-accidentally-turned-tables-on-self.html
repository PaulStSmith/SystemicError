'I am the deep state': GOP pollster warns Trump he's accidentally 'turned tables' on self

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 24, 20254 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks at a dinner with Republican Senators, in the State Dining Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 18, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

President Donald Trump has positioned himself as the "deep state" by targeting his predecessor Barack Obama with possible prosecution, according to a Republican pollster.

Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard has dredged up previously investigated claims about the origins of the "Russiagate" probe and alleged that Obama and some of his high-ranking national security officials engaged in a "treasonous conspiracy" during the 2016 election, and panelists on "CNN This Morning" agreed that Trump was trying to distract from his Jeffrey Epstein scandal.

"What's really bizarre about this whole situation, the current president is saying we have to move on from the Epstein story because it's old, but now we keep obsessing about the 2016 election, and we know the the report said at the time that the Russians did, in fact, meddle," said former Republican congressman Charlie Dent. "They didn't mess with any tallies, they didn't affect the outcome, and that's what the Senate Intelligence Committee told us, and I think Marco Rubio was part of that report."

"So this is, again, it's a wild distraction just away from the Epstein story, so I don't think there's much here," Dent added. "I agree with you, I don't think that President Obama has much to worry about. But, again, it's, I think, this is just flailing around."

GOP pollster Kristen Soltis Anderson warned that Trump's threats against Obama and other political rivals had the potential to backfire on him politically.

"This is silly to reopen this now almost decade-old story," she said. "I'm unfamiliar with there being a great deal of new information [but] it does feed into Donald Trump's belief that there is a sort of 'deep state' against him, and part of what's made this Epstein story so sticky is that this is one where now the tables have been turned, and it feels like Donald Trump has put himself in the 'I am the deep state' position."

Our Analysis:

Trump's "Deep State" Distraction: A Critical Analysis

In a recent turn of events, President Donald Trump has cast himself as the victim of a so-called "deep state" conspiracy, spearheaded by none other than his predecessor, Barack Obama. At the heart of this narrative is the revival of the long-debunked "Russiagate" probe claims, now championed by Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard. The allegations suggest a "treasonous conspiracy" during the 2016 election involving Obama and high-ranking national security officials. However, this resurgence of conspiracy theories appears to be nothing more than a smoke screen, aiming to divert attention from Trump's own controversies, namely his connection to Jeffrey Epstein.

The Art of Distraction

It is no secret that President Trump is a master of diversion. His latest tactic? Rehashing a nearly decade-old story to overshadow his ties to the Epstein scandal. This is not merely a political maneuver but a deliberate attempt to manipulate public perception. The commentary from former Republican congressman Charlie Dent underscores this, highlighting the absurdity of fixating on the 2016 election when the Epstein scandal remains unresolved. The fact that Russian interference, as confirmed by the Senate Intelligence Committee, did not sway the election's outcome makes Trump's obsession with "Russiagate" even more bewildering and transparently self-serving.

The Irony of the "Deep State" Claim

What is particularly ironic about Trump's positioning is his portrayal as a victim of the "deep state" – a concept he has often derided as fiction when it suits his narrative. Yet, as noted by GOP pollster Kristen Soltis Anderson, Trump's revival of these baseless allegations against Obama not only feeds into his perennial narrative of being under siege by invisible forces but also inadvertently places him in the very role of the "deep state" he claims to oppose. This self-victimization tactic is a clever yet dangerous play that seeks to undermine the credibility of legitimate investigations and democratic processes.

A Potential Backfire

Anderson's warning that Trump's threats could backfire politically speaks volumes. By dredging up a "decade-old story" with little to no new evidence, Trump risks alienating moderate voters and further entrenching the polarization of American politics. This strategy of division and distraction might have short-term gains for Trump, particularly among his base, but it threatens the very fabric of American democracy by eroding trust in its institutions and leaders.

Conclusion

The recent developments, as discussed in the CNN panel, reveal a troubling pattern of behavior from President Trump. His attempt to deflect from his association with Jeffrey Epstein by attacking Obama and fabricating a "deep state" conspiracy is not only disingenuous but also potentially damaging to the political landscape. This tactic of distraction and division is a desperate bid for self-preservation that undermines the integrity of the presidency and insults the intelligence of the American public.

In an era where truth and facts are more important than ever, it is critical to remain vigilant and scrutinize the motives behind such political maneuvers. As this analysis shows, Trump's latest ploy is a transparent attempt to manipulate the narrative, one that should be met with skepticism and rigorous examination.
