How to Actually Reduce the U.S. Debt — No Spin, No Bullshit

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20254 min read

While Elon Musk was busy turning a joke cryptocurrency into a punchline that cost real people real money, the U.S. national debt ballooned past $34 trillion. That’s not meme-cash or internet points—that’s an economic powder keg with a slow-burning fuse.

Every election cycle, politicians across the spectrum vow to be "fiscally responsible"—and then proceed to spend, borrow, and deficit-finance like a teenager with their first credit card. The problem isn’t that we don’t know how to fix the debt. The problem is no one wants to do what’s necessary because it’s politically suicidal.

Here’s what it would actually take to reduce the U.S. debt—and why no one with power will touch it.

1. Cut Military Spending (Yes, Really)

The U.S. spends over $800 billion a year on its military—more than the next 10 countries combined. This isn’t about national security anymore; it’s about feeding the bloated beast of the military-industrial complex.

Trimming even 10–15% would save $80–120 billion annually. The U.S. wouldn’t suddenly be defenseless—it would still outspend every potential rival by a mile.

Why it won’t happen: Cutting defense means killing jobs in politically sensitive districts, and no politician wants to be labeled "soft on security" by a cable news attack ad.

2. Tax the Ultra-Wealthy—Actually Tax Them

Forget the theater of “wealth taxes” that die in committee. Here’s what works:

  • Close loopholes like carried interest

  • Treat capital gains like income

  • End offshoring tax avoidance schemes

  • Raise the top marginal income tax rate to Reagan-era levels

Yes, Reagan taxed the rich more than we do now.

Why it won’t happen: Billionaire donors write campaign checks. The same people who rigged the system are holding the pen when laws are written.

3. End Corporate Welfare

Every year, Uncle Sam hands out $100–200 billion in subsidies and tax breaks to industries that are wildly profitable on their own—Big Oil, Big Ag, Big Pharma.

It’s time to call this what it is: welfare for the wealthy. You can’t say you’re a free-market capitalist while suckling the government teat.

Why it won’t happen: These industries fund think tanks, PACs, and politicians who keep the gravy train rolling.

4. Lift the Payroll Tax Cap

In 2024, earnings above $168,600 aren’t taxed for Social Security. That means a nurse pays a higher effective rate than Jeff Bezos.

Remove the cap. Social Security becomes solvent. Revenue increases. Fairness improves.

Why it won’t happen: Rich people don’t like paying more taxes. Shocker.

5. Implement Universal Healthcare (Yes, It Saves Money)

The U.S. spends ~18% of GDP on healthcare. Countries with universal systems spend 10–12%—and get better results. A transition to Medicare for All could save $300–500 billion a year in aggregate costs.

The U.S. isn’t paying for healthcare—it’s hemorrhaging money into private middlemen who deny coverage and inflate prices.

Why it won’t happen: Insurance companies know how to scare people with the phrase “government-run.” And they’ve bought enough senators to keep it that way.

6. Means-Test Some Entitlements

Should billionaires collect Social Security or Medicare? No. Means-testing isn’t about punishing success—it’s about sustainability.

Preserve these programs for those who need them. Trim off the fat where it’s ridiculous.

Why it won’t happen: Any attempt to “touch entitlements” is painted as an attack on grandma—even if grandma is sitting on a $10 million portfolio.

7. Reverse the Bush and Trump Tax Cuts

Those tax cuts blew a multi-trillion-dollar hole in the federal budget. They didn’t trickle down. They bled out.

Letting them expire or reversing them earlier would restore critical revenue without raising taxes on the working class.

Why it won’t happen: "Cutting taxes" is the magic phrase that wins elections, even when it breaks the nation.

8. Invest Strategically to Grow GDP

Debt-to-GDP is the real metric that matters. If you grow the economy faster than the debt, you shrink the ratio.

Investing in infrastructure, green energy, universal broadband, and education produces long-term returns. You don’t fix a bleeding economy by starving it—you fix it by helping it heal and grow.

TL;DR: This Isn’t Just an Economic Problem—It’s Political Rot

The U.S. debt is a solvable problem—on paper. But paper doesn’t vote. Politicians do what pleases their donors, not what helps their constituents.

  • The wealthy are under-taxed.

  • The Pentagon is overfed.

  • Healthcare is a profit engine instead of a human right.

  • And voters are fed a steady diet of culture war garbage to keep them distracted.

Want to fix the debt? Start by breaking the stranglehold of money in politics. Because until we stop electing cowards and cronies, the debt isn’t going anywhere.

Just like Dogecoin—what started as a joke turned into a scam.

And the joke, once again, is on us.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.
