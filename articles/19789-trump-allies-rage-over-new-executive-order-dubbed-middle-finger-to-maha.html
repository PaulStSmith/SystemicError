Trump allies rage over new executive order dubbed 'middle finger' to MAHA

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 20, 20265 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump holds the hand of Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. in the Oval Office of the White House in Washington, D.C., January 29, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Donald Trump on Wednesday signed a controversial new executive order, with some of his key supporters telling the New York Times it was a “middle finger” to the “Make America Healthy Again“ movement.

The new order called for increasing domestic production of the prominent weedkiller, glyphosate. Troublingly for Trump’s supporters, that compound is one of the active ingredients in Roundup, a popular herbicide product that, according to the Times, “has been the target of tens of thousands of lawsuits that claim it causes non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma.” In fact, Trump’s own Health & Human Services Secretary, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., served as a lawyer for the plaintiffs in a “landmark” $289 million civil case against Monsanto, the previous makers of Roundup, in which a jury ruled that the company was aware of the product’s cancer-causing potential.

Now, however, the secretary appears to be singing a different tune, more in line with Trump’s official stance.

“Donald Trump’s executive order puts America first where it matters most — our defense readiness and our food supply,” Kennedy said in his own statement. “We must safeguard America’s national security first, because all of our priorities depend on it.”

“Lack of access to glyphosate-based herbicides would critically jeopardize agricultural productivity, adding pressure to the domestic food system,” Trump said in a statement about the executive order.

Speaking to the Times about the new order and Kennedy’s involvement with it, various figures in the “MAHA” movement cried foul.

“I can’t envision a bigger middle finger to every MAHA mom than this,” Ken Cook, president and co-founder of Environmental Working Group, told the Times. Cook’s group has, in the past, supported portions of Kennedy’s broader agenda as the head of HHS.

“MAHA voters were promised health reform, not chemical entrenchment,” Vani Hari, a Kennedy ally and “healthy eating advocate,” said. She also trashed Trump’s new order as “a direct assault on MAHA” and “a gift to pesticide and chemical industry lobbies at the expense of human health.”

Just last year, a commission led by Kennedy released a report on the potential causes of chronic disease, which singled out glyphosate and other herbicidal materials as potentially harmful to children. The Times also noted that MAHA movement activists were among the coalition that fought against a government funding bill provision that would have protected Bayer, the current producers of Roundup, and other similar companies from having to pay plaintiffs.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Executive Order: A Stark Contradiction to Health Advocacy

In a move that can only be described as bewilderingly contradictory, Donald Trump has signed an executive order poised to increase the domestic production of glyphosate—a decision that flies in the face of the health concerns raised by the very constituents who propelled him to power. This executive order, as reported, seems less about policy and more about sending a “middle finger” to the Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement, a clear betrayal cloaked in the guise of national security and agricultural productivity.

Glyphosate: The Controversial Heart of the Matter

At the center of this storm is glyphosate, an active ingredient in Roundup, a herbicide that has been the subject of tens of thousands of lawsuits for its alleged link to non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma. It’s not merely a subject of grassroots activism; it’s a compound that has been legally recognized as a potential health hazard, with a jury ruling against Monsanto, its previous manufacturer, in a landmark case. The twist? Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Trump’s own Health & Human Services Secretary, once fought against this very compound as a lawyer for the plaintiffs. Yet now, aligning with Trump’s stance, Kennedy heralds the executive order as a boon for national security and the food supply, a stark pivot from his previous advocacy against glyphosate’s dangers.

The MAHA Movement: Promises vs. Reality

The MAHA movement, initially bolstered by promises of health reform, now finds itself grappling with what appears to be a profound betrayal. Advocates and voters who once saw hope in Trump’s administration for health-conscious reforms are now met with policies that seemingly prioritize industrial agribusiness interests over public health concerns. Critics, including Ken Cook and Vani Hari, have not minced words, viewing this executive order as a direct assault on the principles of the MAHA movement and a gift to the chemical industry at the expense of human well-being.

Kennedy’s Role: A Puzzling Shift

The involvement of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. in this narrative adds a layer of complexity and confusion. Once a staunch opponent of glyphosate, his recent statements in support of Trump’s executive order contrast sharply with his past advocacies. This raises questions about the consistency of political figures in maintaining their stances on public health issues, especially when such stances become inconvenient within the framework of governmental policy-making.

Conclusion: The Cost of Political Contradictions

Trump’s executive order, under the guise of securing national defense and food supply, blatantly disregards the substantial health concerns associated with glyphosate—a compound linked to cancer and the subject of widespread legal and public outcry. The apparent flip-flopping of Robert F. Kennedy Jr. on an issue he once passionately opposed further muddies the waters, suggesting a concerning level of political expediency that overshadows the genuine health risks posed to the American public.

In essence, this scenario is a glaring example of political priorities gone awry, where the health of the populace is sidelined for broader, more ambiguous goals of “national security” and agricultural productivity. It’s a stark reminder of the systemic errors that continue to plague our political landscape, where the well-being of the many is often sacrificed at the altar of the interests of the few.
