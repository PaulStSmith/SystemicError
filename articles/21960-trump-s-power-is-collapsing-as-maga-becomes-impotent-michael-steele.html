Trump's power is collapsing as MAGA becomes impotent: Michael Steele

Trump’s Endorsements Are Not Power. They’re Damage Control.

The Myth of the Kingmaker

Michael Steele’s point was simple: Donald Trump keeps acting like his endorsement is a command, but the latest primary results say otherwise. In Minnesota and South Carolina, the candidates he backed did not deliver the clean victories he uses to project control. That matters because Trump’s real power inside the GOP has never been ideology or competence. It has been intimidation, spectacle, and the assumption that Republican politicians would rather kneel than risk his rage.

What Actually Holds Power

Trump still has influence, but influence is not the same as leverage over outcomes. The GOP keeps granting him a platform because too many Republicans built their careers on his base and his threats. That is the institutional fact here: Trump’s authority depends on party elites, donors, candidates, and elected officials who continue to treat him as the center of gravity. He is not a force of nature. He is a beneficiary of repeated Republican surrender.

The Misdirection Machine

Steele is right that Trump tries to cloud the airwaves and turn elections into noise. That is the operating method. When a candidate fails, the story gets redirected into personality theater, grievance, and manufactured confusion. The point is not to persuade voters with policy. The point is to keep everyone too distracted, too angry, or too exhausted to notice that the party is running on fraud, resentment, and cowardice. Trump’s “kingmaker” pose is propaganda for his own weakness.

Election Denial Is Corruption

Steele’s warning about putting a known election denier in office goes to the core of the problem. This is not a harmless factional disagreement. It is the normalization of officials who reject the legitimacy of votes unless they win. Once a party rewards that behavior, it turns government into an instrument for laundering anti-democratic conduct. The damage is not abstract. It lands in state offices, election administration, and the basic trust people need to believe their votes count.

The Party Feeding the Beast

Steele said the party is now about feeding chaos, and that is the most useful line in the segment. Trump does not govern the GOP from outside it. He is the product of its appetite for domination without accountability. Republican leaders enabled him, protected him, and kept moving the goalposts every time he broke something. Now they are trapped inside the machinery they built, forced to pretend a collapsing personality cult is still strategic strength.

The Larger Pattern

The real story is not that Trump is losing some endorsements. It is that the Republican Party has made itself structurally dependent on a man whose main political skill is sabotage. When a party rewards election denial, conspiracy, and personal loyalty over democratic legitimacy, it does not just drift into cynicism. It becomes an anti-democratic institution with ballots attached. Steele is describing a fracture, but the deeper truth is worse: this is what decay looks like when a major party confuses authoritarian theatrics for power.
