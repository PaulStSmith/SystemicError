A scandal-plagued Republican lawmaker got pushback from conservatives and liberals alike after he called for President Donald Trump to receive a third term, as well as a Nobel Peace Prize.

Rep. Andy Ogles, who has been reportedly put under scrutiny for financial scandals in the past, said Saturday on X, "Give Trump a third term, give him a Peace Prize, and let him run D.C. as long as he wants."

The pushback was almost instant.

Joe Walsh, a former Tea Party Republican lawmaker who recently switched parties, replied with, "225 days ago, you raised your right hand and swore to support & defend the Constitution of the United States. Shame on you."

Republicans against Trump asked Elon Musk's AI chatbot, Grok, "What’s wrong with this tweet?" The program provided the following answer:

"The tweet proposes unconstitutional actions: a third term violates the 22nd Amendment's two-term limit (Trump is serving his second). The Nobel Peace Prize is awarded by the Norwegian Committee, not 'given' by the US. Allowing indefinite rule undermines democracy and the Constitution."

Former youth pastor John Pavlovitz said, "Nah."

"Give him a polygraph test about r----- girls," the liberal author added.

Brian Mistrot, a self-described politically homeless conservative, chimed in:

"The Constitution limits a POTUS to 2 terms, which this numb-nuts knows. But s------- on the Constitution allows him to keep making $200k/year off taxpayers and stay in power."

"More proof that Republicans have given up on the Constitution & rule of law," he then added.

The popular MAGA Cult Slayer account said, "Ogles is a traitor to the United States of America."

Our Analysis:

A Dive into the Abyss of Political Absurdity: Rep. Andy Ogles' Controversial Call

In a recent spectacle that blurs the line between audacious political theater and a troubling disregard for constitutional principles, Rep. Andy Ogles has thrown the political sphere into a frenzy. His proposition? "Give Trump a third term, give him a Peace Prize, and let him run D.C. as long as he wants." This statement, not only a blatant affront to the U.S. Constitution but also a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism, has sparked a wildfire of criticism from all political corners.

The Constitution: A Mere Suggestion?

First, let’s address the elephant in the room: the 22nd Amendment. Ratified in 1951, this cornerstone of American democracy limits a president to two terms in office. Ogles, in his infinite wisdom, suggests we not only ignore this amendment but obliterate it entirely for the sake of Trumpian glorification. This isn’t just a political faux pas; it’s a brazen challenge to the very foundation of American governance.

The Nobel Peace Prize: A Political Party Favor?

Moving on to the Nobel Peace Prize — a prestigious award that, last I checked, was not a party favor to be doled out by U.S. politicians but rather a recognition bestowed by the Norwegian Nobel Committee. Ogles' suggestion that Trump be "given" this honor is as laughable as it is ignorant, reducing a symbol of peace and international cooperation to a mere token of partisan acclaim.

Permanent Power: Democracy’s Demise

The idea of letting anyone "run D.C. as long as he wants" is so antithetical to democracy, it’s astonishing that it was uttered by a sitting U.S. lawmaker. It reeks of an endorsement for indefinite rule, a notion so dangerously close to dictatorial aspirations that it should raise every American's hackles.

The Backlash: A Chorus of Disdain

The backlash was swift and scathing. Joe Walsh, a former Tea Party Republican turned critic, reminded Ogles of his constitutional oath, encapsulating the sentiment of many when he expressed his shame. The response from Elon Musk's AI chatbot, Grok, was particularly telling, offering a factual debunking of Ogles' proposition and highlighting its unconstitutionality and undemocratic essence.

Liberal voices and conservative critics alike have lambasted Ogles, with Brian Mistrot summing up the sentiment succinctly: Ogles knows better, yet chooses to defile the Constitution for personal gain and power. The "MAGA Cult Slayer" account didn't mince words, labeling Ogles a "traitor to the United States of America."

Conclusion: A Reflection of Our Times

This episode transcends mere political controversy; it is a reflection of a deeper, more systemic rot within certain factions of the political establishment. Ogles’ comments, and the support such ideas garner, underscore a disturbing trend toward authoritarianism and a flippant disregard for the principles that underpin American democracy.

Let this moment be a clarion call to those who cherish democracy and the rule of law. The fight to preserve the sanctity of our Constitution and democratic norms is far from over. It's a battle that demands vigilance, resilience, and an unwavering commitment to the foundational values of the United States. As for Ogles, history will undoubtedly remember his proposition, but not as he might hope. Instead, it will serve as a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy and the constant vigilance required to defend it.
