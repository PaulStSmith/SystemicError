'We would step in': Mike Johnson admits what could force Congress to take power from Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) admitted that an "imbalance" of powers between his legislative branch and President Donald Trump's executive branch could prompt Congress to take action on disruptive tariffs that have contributed to the weakening economy, according to a new report in Politico.

Johnson made the statement at an Axios "News Shapers" event in Washington, D.C., Wednesday.

“I think the executive has a broad array of authority that’s been recognized over the years” related to trade, Johnson said. “If it gets close to where the imbalance is there, then we would step in.”

But Johnson made clear he didn't think Trump's tariff strategy and the economic downturn warranted action right away, according to reporter Meredith Lee Hill.

“I mean, what are we, about three weeks into the tariff policy of this new administration? I don’t think it’s appropriate for Congress to jump in the middle of that and try to legislate," Johnson said, adding that he would “call the president and talk with him ... and tell him our concerns” before doing anything to curtail the tariffs.

“Right now, I think ultimately, this policy is going to achieve the desired outcome, and it’s going to be good for the country,” Johnson said.

Hill added that "Johnson...has undertaken a procedural move to effectively block any House effort to cancel Trump’s tariffs, despite several Republicans backing legislation to do so."

The Dow Jones fell 500 points, or 1.25%, Wednesday morning after news of the shrinking economy, prompting Trump to post on social media, “This is Biden’s Stock Market, not Trump’s. I didn’t take over until January 20th."

He continued, "Tariffs will soon start kicking in, and companies are starting to move into the USA in record numbers. Our Country will boom, but we have to get rid of the Biden ‘Overhang.’ This will take a while, has NOTHING TO DO WITH TARIFFS, only that he left us with bad numbers, but when the boom begins, it will be like no other. BE PATIENT!!!”

Our Analysis:

Economic Disruption: A Tale of Tariffs and Political Theater

In a recent spectacle of political maneuvering, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) has openly acknowledged what many have whispered in the hallowed halls of Congress: an "imbalance" of power between the legislative and executive branches, particularly in the realm of trade and tariffs. This admission, as reported by Politico, sheds light on the ongoing drama surrounding President Donald Trump's controversial tariff strategy, which has been blamed for exacerbating an already weakening economy.

The Delicate Dance of Power

Johnson's remarks at an Axios "News Shapers" event in Washington, D.C., illustrate a precarious balancing act. On one hand, he concedes the executive branch's broad authority over trade—a power that has been gradually expanded over decades, often at the expense of Congressional oversight. On the other hand, he hints at a threshold of tolerance that, if crossed, might compel Congress to intervene.

Yet, despite this acknowledgment of potential overreach, Johnson stops short of asserting Congressional authority. His assertion that it's too early to judge the impacts of Trump's tariff policy—barely three weeks old at the time of his statement—rings hollow in the face of immediate economic backlash, exemplified by a significant drop in the Dow Jones.

Rhetoric vs. Reality

Johnson's reluctance to confront the executive branch's tariff strategy is underscored by a procedural maneuver designed to stifle any House effort to repeal Trump's tariffs, despite the existence of bipartisan support for such action. This move not only highlights a troubling complicity in the face of economic turmoil but also raises questions about the true priorities of Congressional leadership.

Furthermore, Trump's response to the economic downturn—a defiant redirection of blame towards his predecessor, coupled with a plea for patience from the American public—exemplifies a disturbing trend of political deflection. His claim that the tariffs will eventually lead to economic prosperity, despite evidence to the contrary, underscores a disconcerting disconnect from the realities faced by American businesses and consumers.

Conclusion: The Cost of Inaction

As the economy teeters on the brink, the actions (or lack thereof) of political leaders such as Johnson and Trump serve as a stark reminder of the consequences of unchecked executive power and legislative inaction. The narrative spun by these figures—a mixture of denial, deflection, and unwarranted optimism—cannot obscure the tangible impacts of their policies on the American people.

In this moment of economic uncertainty, it is imperative that we demand more from our elected officials than mere lip service and procedural roadblocks. The balance of power, so crucial to the functioning of our democracy, must be restored, and it is the duty of Congress to step up and fulfill its role as a check on executive excess. Failure to do so not only undermines the legislative branch's authority but also jeopardizes the economic wellbeing of the nation.
