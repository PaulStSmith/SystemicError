'Keeping him up at night': CNN host mocks Elon Musk's late-night apology

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

CNN's Erica Hill needled tech mogul Elon Musk's late-night about-face in his feud against President Donald Trump.

The president and his billionaire benefactor publicly split last week after Musk lambasted the so-called "big beautiful bill" as a "disgusting abomination," and the break-up quickly escalated into a bitter war of words on social media that Musk now regrets.

“I regret some of my posts about President @realDonaldTrump last week," Musk said in a post on X. "They went too far."

He didn't specify which posts fall into that category, but over the weekend Musk appeared to delete a post threatening to "drop the really big bomb" on Trump by revealing his ties to convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, but the guest host for "CNN This Morning" noted the overnight timestamp for Musk's apparent mea culpa.

"Well, it turns out regrets, he's got a few," Hill said, paraphrasing Frank Sinatra's enduring hit, "My Way." "Elon Musk, second thoughts. It appears about all those unkind tweets he rattled off last week calling for the president to be impeached, suggesting without evidence that the name 'Donald Trump' appears in the unreleased Epstein files, in fact, the bromance breakup appears to be keeping Musk up at night. How do we know that? Well, we're just looking at the timestamp here, but at 3:04 a.m. so just about three-and-a-half hours ago this morning."

She read the Tesla CEO's walk-back of his angry outbursts, saying the statement sounded like a child's apology.

"That is probably like the most perfect response you could have from one of your kids when they realize they messed up like, 'Oh, hey – I went too far,'" Hill said.

Former Republican congressman Charlie Dent said Musk's entire stint in the government was regrettable.

"Of course, he has to have regrets, not just about the tweets, but about the whole DOGE process," Dent said. "Remember how this started off? He said he was going to find $2 trillion in savings, then they shaved it down to $1 trillion, and they said, 'Oh, we're not going to look at Social Security and Medicare and all the big areas where the federal government spends money,' and then they talk about bringing out wood chippers, and there's the chainsaw and, you know, and the images of the wealthiest man in the world, then denying food assistance and medical assistance to some of the most desperately poor people in the world. Then you wonder why people don't want to buy your cars."

"So I think he probably has a few regrets well beyond his latest fight with Donald Trump," Dent added, "so he's got a lot to think about."

Our Analysis:

The Musk-Trump Saga: A Study in Billionaire Whimsy and Political Theatre

In a recent turn of events that could only be described as a billionaire's late-night remorse, Elon Musk, the tech mogul known for his erratic Twitter (now X) musings, has expressed regret over his heated exchange with President Donald Trump. This saga, as reported by CNN's Erica Hill, encapsulates the volatile intersection of wealth, power, and social media in a way that's both bewildering and alarmingly indicative of our times.

The Regretful Tweet

Musk's regret, announced in a post on X, conveniently lacks specifics. He states, “I regret some of my posts about President @realDonaldTrump last week. They went too far." This vague admission follows Musk's earlier threats to reveal Trump's alleged ties with convicted sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein—a claim as explosive as it is unsubstantiated. The deletion of these posts raises questions not just about Musk's accusations but also about the strategic retraction of such serious allegations. Is the deletion an acknowledgment of baselessness, or merely an attempt to retreat from the backlash?

The Media's Midnight Musings

Hill's commentary, invoking Frank Sinatra's "My Way" to describe Musk's regrets, serves as a stark reminder of the trivialization of matters of grave significance. These are not mere celebrity spats; they involve individuals whose decisions and behaviors have far-reaching implications. The focus on the timestamp of Musk's tweet—3:04 a.m.—although seemingly trivial, underscores a culture of impulsivity and recklessness that seems to pervade the echelons of power. This isn't just about Musk keeping odd hours; it's about the erratic, sleepless whims of those in positions of influence.

A Child's Apology or a Billionaire's Remorse?

Hill's likening of Musk's statement to a "child's apology" is both apt and telling. It reflects a broader issue of accountability (or the lack thereof) among the elite. When the consequences of one’s actions are so far removed from the personal sphere, apologies can indeed seem childlike—minimal repercussions, a mere slap on the wrist, and then business as usual.

Beyond Tweets: The Real Regrets

Former Republican congressman Charlie Dent's comments illuminate the deeper, more systemic issues at play. Musk's regrets, as per Dent, should extend far beyond his social media tirades against Trump. Dent criticizes Musk's handling of the DOGE process, highlighting the stark contrast between Musk's billionaire antics—like threatening to bring out "wood chippers" and chainsaws—and the dire needs of "the most desperately poor people in the world." This juxtaposition isn't just stark; it's grotesque. Musk's behavior and policies, as Dent suggests, are emblematic of a broader disregard for societal welfare—a sentiment that seems to permeate much of today's billionaire class.

Conclusion: Reflection or Deflection?

As this saga unfolds, one can't help but wonder if Musk's regrets are a moment of genuine reflection or merely a strategic deflection. The issue at hand transcends Musk's late-night tweets or his feud with Trump. It's about the immense power wielded by individuals like Musk and the responsibility that should accompany it—a responsibility that seems all too often forgotten in the face of ego, power struggles, and the 24/7 news cycle.

The Musk-Trump debacle is a mirror to the times—a reflection of the complexities, the absurdities, and, most importantly, the systemic errors at the heart of our contemporary socio-political landscape. As observers, it's crucial we demand more than midnight apologies and childlike retractions. The stakes are simply too high.
