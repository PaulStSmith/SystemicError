They Swore an Oath. And Then They Watched It Burn.

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 26, 20252 min read

“To defend the Constitution of the United States against all enemies, foreign and domestic.”
That’s the oath. The sacred promise. The line in the sand.

And yet — when the enemy stood not across an ocean, but inside the White House, wearing a red tie and spitting venom at the Constitution, they did nothing.

Not a resignation. Not a mass statement. Not even a line in the sand.

The Uniform Is Not a Shield Against Accountability

We’re told that the military is the last line of defense. That the men and women in uniform aren’t political, but principled. That they don’t answer to parties — they answer to the flag, to the people, to the law.

But where were they?

Where were they when the President incited insurrection? When judges were arrested for respecting due process? When ICE was unleashed like a paramilitary force? When the press was demonized, the courts delegitimized, and truth itself turned into a punchline?

They watched.
They saluted.
They waited for someone else to do something.

An Enemy Within — and a Deafening Silence

Every speech they give, every medal they polish, every ceremony they attend is a performance unless it’s backed by the courage to uphold the one duty that matters most: defending the nation.

The Constitution doesn’t need to be protected when it’s safe. It needs to be protected when it’s under siege.

And right now? That siege is real.
It’s coming from inside.
And the military — the institution that boasts of courage, of conviction, of duty — has fallen quiet.

The Lie of “Enemies Within”

They love to say the words. “Foreign and domestic.” But it’s easy to say that when the enemies are imaginary. When they’re dressed in someone else’s skin, someone else’s language, someone else’s country.

It’s harder when the enemy is a man in your own chain of command.
Harder still when he’s surrounded by flags, by power, by supporters who scream “traitor” at anyone who questions him.

But that’s exactly why the oath includes “domestic.” Because the enemy within is always the most dangerous.
And the easiest to ignore.

The Oath Was Not a Costume

It was a contract.
And if you can’t uphold it when it matters most, then you were never worthy of it in the first place.

This isn’t about asking for a coup. This isn’t about demanding the military take sides. This is about demanding they remember which side they swore to protect.

When a judge is arrested…
When laws are weaponized against the innocent…
When authoritarianism no longer knocks, but kicks down the door…

Silence is betrayal.

And we will remember who stayed silent. We will remember who folded their oath into a drawer and turned their back.

Because when the republic fell to its knees, it wasn’t because the people failed to rise.
It was because those who swore to defend it chose to stand down.

And no uniform can cover that shame.
