'Let's be blunt — this is bad': MSNBC host alarmed by Trump's 'really dangerous' new order

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The White House has ordered the Smithsonian Institution to align its museum exhibits with President Donald Trump's vision of American history, which has set off alarms about how that will impact the accuracy of the story told to visitors.

Eight museums are covered under the order setting a 120-day deadline for "replacing divisive or ideologically driven language with unifying, historically accurate, and constructive descriptions," and MSNBC's Jonathan Lemire said that would necessarily corrupt how they presented the history of slavery and other difficult topics.

"I mean, let's be blunt: This is really bad and really, really dangerous," the "Morning Joe" co-host said. "A nation needs to know its history. It has to be honest about its history to learn from it, to honor it, and also to then grow and improve for the present and future. This is there's no way that rewriting a history to fit one president's vision is good for a nation's health or good for a nation's democracy."

"We've already seen the Smithsonian change its impeachment exhibit, you know, because, you know, to eliminate the references to President Trump," he added. "They've been restored, modified, edited [and] sanitized some, but at least they're back. But now it seems like that's just the first step to a sweeping revisiting of American history through the Smithsonian. We already know a little bit about what this how this president views this issue and his administration."

The administration has ordered the return of Confederate monuments to parks in Washington, D.C., and slapped the names of Confederate generals back onto military bases, and Lemire said those efforts distorted the historical narrative.

"This is this is not what a healthy democracy does," Lemire said. "You know, you can be patriotic, you can love your country – we have the 250th anniversary next year. Certainly, President Trump and his team are already gearing up towards really celebrating that. Some of that is good, but to honor a nation's history you have to be honest about it and to sanitize it, to whitewash it, to not be honest about it, right, that's not good."

Our Analysis:

A Distortion of Democracy: The White House's Dangerous Directive

In a move that reeks of authoritarian control over national narrative, the White House has issued a directive to the Smithsonian Institution demanding a reconfiguration of museum exhibits to conform to President Donald Trump's interpretation of American history. This stark mandate covers eight museums and imposes a tight 120-day deadline to scrub any "divisive or ideologically driven language" in favor of what it deems "unifying, historically accurate, and constructive descriptions." The implications of this are as alarming as they are clear: a deliberate manipulation of historical narrative to serve a political agenda.

Jonathan Lemire of MSNBC rightly labels this maneuver as "really bad and really, really dangerous." History, in its raw and unvarnished form, is essential for a nation's identity and growth. It is through the honest acknowledgment of its past that a country learns, honors its journey, and paves the way for genuine improvement. Lemire's concern that this order will corrupt the presentation of slavery and other contentious chapters of American history is not just valid but a forewarning of the systemic erosion of democratic values.

The precedent for this sort of historical revisionism by the Trump administration is already set, with alterations to the Smithsonian's impeachment exhibit to minimize references to Trump. Although these changes were somewhat reversed, the initial act of censorship reveals a disturbing trend towards sanitizing the past to fit a political narrative.

Moreover, Lemire highlights the administration's efforts to reinstate Confederate monuments and names of Confederate generals on military bases. This move not only distorts the historical narrative but glorifies a divisive chapter in American history, undermining the struggles and progress made in the fight against racism and inequality.

The underlying issue here transcends mere historical accuracy. It strikes at the very heart of democracy itself. A healthy democracy thrives on transparency, accountability, and an informed populace. By attempting to rewrite history, the administration is not just manipulating the past; it's undermining the principles of informed citizenship and open discourse that are foundational to democratic governance.

As the United States approaches its 250th anniversary, there's undoubtedly a need to celebrate and reflect on its history. However, true patriotism and honor lie not in the whitewashing of past atrocities but in the courageous confrontation of them. It's through this honest reckoning with history that a nation demonstrates its commitment to truth, justice, and democracy.

In summary, the White House's directive to the Smithsonian Institution represents a dangerous pivot towards authoritarianism, where control of historical narrative becomes a tool for political power. It is an affront to democratic values, intellectual freedom, and the very essence of what it means to learn from the past. As citizens and stewards of democracy, there must be a collective rejection of any attempt to sanitize history for political gain. The integrity of a nation's history and the health of its democracy depend on it.
