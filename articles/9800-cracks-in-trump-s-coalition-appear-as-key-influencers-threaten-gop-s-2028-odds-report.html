'Cracks in Trump's coalition' appear as key influencers threaten GOP's 2028 odds: report

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Analysts around the country have suggested it was the conservative male podcasting culture that helped propel Donald Trump into a second term, but those same performers are now turning on the president.

Joe Rogan and other podcaster/influencers were a hot topic in the 2024 election, and Trump enjoyed a shift in the polls among young males that was largely attributed to this new political paradigm. Now, however, the tide appears to be turning.

In an article called “Pod man out: Trump’s support with influential podcasters is waning,” NBC News reports that, “Some of the podcasters who hosted Trump ahead of last year’s election are breaking with him now on key issues like deportations and the war in Gaza.”

“Some of the internet’s most popular voices with young men — almost all of whom either hosted President Donald Trump or spoke highly of him last November — have some thoughts on what he’s doing wrong,” according to the outlet’s reporting. “An all-star lineup of podcasters and YouTube impresarios has taken Trump to task in recent months on everything from immigration and Israel to free speech and Jeffrey Epstein. The list includes Joe Rogan, Theo Von, Andrew Schulz and Shawn Ryan, a cast that Trump courted heavily to win access to their audiences during last year’s campaign.”

The report goes on to highlight how some individuals have led the charge.

“Rogan and Von have been particularly critical of the Trump administration’s mass deportation agenda, with Rogan calling some deportations ‘f------ crazy’ and Von questioning why the Department of Homeland Security has posted videos of immigration arrests online,” the NBC report states. “Schulz and his co-host, Akaash Singh, have criticized the president for not fulfilling his campaign promises.”

After noting that “Republican and Democratic strategists alike have acknowledged that Trump’s willingness to engage with them helped give him a crucial boost in a hard-fought election,” the outlet says too much of this shift could threaten Republicans in the next presidential election.

“The recent disagreements threaten to swallow some of Trump’s support — potentially with the less politically active, harder-to-reach podcast listeners — even if it does not translate into a bigger chunk of the electorate for Democrats. Trump is ineligible to seek re-election, but Republicans hope to keep hold of the new voters who turned out for him as they battle in midterm elections next year and to maintain the presidency in 2028,” according to the reporting. “During recent focus groups of 18-29-year-old Trump voters — observed by NBC News as part of the 2025 Deciders series, produced by Syracuse University and the research firms Engagious and Sago — a handful of voters said that people like Rogan and Von helped to persuade them to vote for Trump.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Influence: Podcasters, Politics, and the Perilous Support of Donald Trump

In an era where traditional media’s influence seems to wane in the face of digital insurgency, the 2024 election cycle has illuminated a stark reality: the conservative male podcasting culture, once an unyielding bastion of support for Donald Trump, is showing signs of fracture. This revelation, as detailed in NBC News’ “Pod man out: Trump’s support with influential podcasters is waning,” not only underscores the volatile nature of political allegiance but also exemplifies the precarious pedestal upon which modern political figures stand.

The Unlikely Kingmakers

It’s almost poetic justice that the same digital demagogues who helped hoist Trump to a second term are now the architects of his potential downfall. Figures like Joe Rogan, Theo Von, and Andrew Schulz, initially lauded as the vanguard of a new political paradigm, are now expressing disillusionment with Trump’s policies and promises. This isn’t merely a case of shifting political winds; it’s a testament to the inherent instability of relying on influencers for political gain.

The Deportation Debacle and Other Disasters

Rogan and Von, in particular, have taken issue with the Trump administration’s approach to immigration, with Rogan branding some deportations as “f------ crazy” and Von questioning the public display of immigration arrests. Meanwhile, Schulz and his co-host, Akaash Singh, lament Trump’s failure to fulfill his campaign promises. These criticisms highlight a broader issue: the gap between political rhetoric and reality, a chasm too wide for even the most eloquent of podcasters to bridge.

The Fickle Tide of Political Favor

The crux of the matter lies not just in the podcasters’ changing allegiances but in the potential ripple effects on the electorate. The NBC report suggests that Trump’s estrangement from these influential figures could erode his support among the less politically active, harder-to-reach podcast listeners. This demographic, once considered securely in Trump’s camp, now represents a significant uncertainty for Republicans eyeing the 2028 presidential election.

The Myth of the Influencer Voter

What this situation ultimately reveals is the folly of conflating media influence with political loyalty. The 18-29-year-old Trump voters, swayed by the likes of Rogan and Von, exemplify the transient nature of influencer-fueled convictions. As the NBC report indicates, the recent focus groups show a clear disaffection among these voters, hinting at a deeper, more systemic disillusionment with the political process.

Conclusion: A Harbinger of Political Instability

The narrative unfolding around Trump and his podcaster supporters is more than just a tale of political intrigue. It serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of building a political strategy on the shifting sands of influencer culture. As the 2024 election has shown, the allegiance of influencers and their followers is not a panacea for political challenges; it’s a mirage in the desert of modern politics. The real question now is not whether Trump can regain the support of these digital kingmakers, but whether future political candidates will heed the lessons of his precipitous fall.
