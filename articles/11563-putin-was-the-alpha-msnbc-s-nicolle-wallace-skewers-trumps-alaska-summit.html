'Putin was the alpha': MSNBC's Nicolle Wallace skewers Trump’s Alaska summit

MSNBC's Nicolle Wallace brutally panned President Donald Trump's summit with Russia's Vladimir Putin after nothing appeared to be accomplished — claiming that Trump essentially let Putin steamroll the whole process and ensured it would fail.

The event, which saw Trump giving literal red-carpet treatment to the autocratic Russian ruler as he tried to negotiate a "peace deal" out of Russia's invasion of Ukraine, ended with the two world leaders giving statements to the press, vaguely suggesting the outlines of a deal but giving no specifics, before walking off without taking any questions.

"I think the press conference underscored something that Ambassador McFaul and Ali Velshi and I've been talking about since 4 p.m. Eastern," said Wallace. "I mean, this was, for Trump, a television production that may have been rooted in different motives, but one of them was likely to change the topic of news coverage. It succeeded in that we've all been covering this all day, for better or for worse. But clearly Trump was not in control of anything."

"I mean, Putin was the alpha all day long, from Trump holding on his plane and waiting for him like a, you know, middle schooler waiting for their first date, the red carpet that rolled out and the choreography," she continued.

"I went to dozens of foreign trips with the president," Wallace continued. "It is very rare to have a red carpet and then an ascent to a platform, and then a walk down the stairs into a limo together. I won't say it's never happened, but it's exceedingly rare to the point that I can't think of it ever happening before."

The biggest tell that Putin was setting the agenda, Wallace added, is that reporters didn't get to ask anything at what was supposed to be a press conference.

"And then Donald Trump — you know, don't stand between Donald Trump and a scrum of reporters asking questions if you don't want to be stampeded, there's no way it was Donald Trump's decision or request to take no questions," she said. "He'd be talking until 9 p.m. Eastern if he'd been in control of anything that happened today."

Our Analysis:

A Performance, Not Policy: Trump's Summit with Putin

In a recent display that could only be described as theater of the absurd, MSNBC's Nicolle Wallace offered a scathing critique of President Donald Trump's summit with Russian President Vladimir Putin, a spectacle that unfolded with all the predictability of a poorly scripted reality TV show. The event, which ostensibly aimed to negotiate a "peace deal" regarding Russia's invasion of Ukraine, ended not with a bang, but with the whimper of ambiguity and a glaring absence of specifics. Let's dissect this moment of political theatre with the precision of a scalpel, shall we?

The Alpha in the Room

Wallace's analysis cuts to the heart of the matter, laying bare the uncomfortable truth: Trump was outmaneuvered at his own game. Describing the summit as nothing more than a "television production," Wallace points out the underlying tragedy of the spectacle—it was less about making tangible progress and more about shifting the news cycle. Trump, known for his love of the spotlight, instead played the uncharacteristic role of the waiting suitor, giving Putin not just the red-carpet treatment but, effectively, the keys to the castle.

The Devil in the Details (Or Lack Thereof)

One of the most glaring indicators of Putin's dominance in this choreographed dance was the so-called press conference, a term I use as loosely as Trump's grasp on the proceedings. A press conference without questions is like a king without a crown: utterly pointless. The fact that no questions were allowed speaks volumes about the true nature of the summit: a showcase without substance, a meeting devoid of meaningful dialogue.

The Spectacle of Silence

Wallace's assertion that Trump would normally leap at the chance to engage with reporters—painting a picture of him being restrained only by the sheer force of Putin's will—highlights a disturbing reality. It wasn't just that Trump was not in control; it was that he surrendered the stage to Putin with the ease of one conceding defeat. This isn't merely about optics; it's about the relinquishing of power, the absence of assertiveness from a leader who prides himself on his deal-making prowess.

Conclusion: A Summit of Shadows

In analyzing Wallace's critique and the summit itself, one thing becomes painfully clear: this was an exercise in futility, a meeting that moved no needles and shifted no paradigms. Instead, it was a masterclass in the art of appearance over substance, a stark reminder of the era of politics-as-entertainment we find ourselves mired in. The real casualty here, beyond the missed opportunity for genuine diplomatic progress, is the truth. In its place, we're left with the echoes of what could have been, a narrative controlled by the stronger hand, and a world watching as the stage is set for the next act of this ongoing farce.

In sum, Trump's summit with Putin was less a diplomatic endeavor and more a surrender to the spectacle, a capitulation cloaked in the guise of negotiation. As observers and analysts, we must demand more than just performances; we must hold our leaders to account, insisting on substance over showmanship, on action over rhetoric. Anything less is a disservice to the principles of democracy and a threat to the global order.
