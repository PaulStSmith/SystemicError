'This is bad stuff': Trump's DOJ flattened over yet another 'embarrassing' court fail

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 6, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Study in Desperation and Abuse: The DOJ’s Fruitless Crusade Against Letitia James

Donald Trump (Photo by Leah Mills for Reuters)

Reacting to another attempt to get a grand jury to issue an indictment of New York Attorney General Letitia James on fraud charges that fell flat on its face, the Department of Justice under Donald Trump appointee Pam Bondi was leveled on MS NOW on Friday morning.

Late Thursday, the DOJ once again came up empty-handed in its pursuit of one of Trump’s main antagonists, who, unlike the DOJ, was successful when she convicted the president of business fraud, only to have an appeals court rule the financial penalty was “excessive.”

Thursday’s failure to get a true bill from the grand jury comes just two weeks after a judge tossed similar mortgage fraud case against James because the prosecution by Trump appointee and real estate lawyer Lindsey Halligan was deemed unlawful.

Reporting on the latest blow to the DOJ, “Morning Joe” co-host Jonathan Lemire expressed disgust with the efforts to get James when there is little to no evidence of a crime.

“But the president and his team want to persist. They say they’re going to go back at Letitia James. We don’t know if they’ll go back yet,” Lemire explained before adding, “So it has been, to this point, pretty Keystone Cops. But yet at the same time, we shouldn’t lose sight of the fact that these things keep falling apart, at least for now, that this remains an extraordinarily dangerous abuse of power, I would argue, of presidential power and blurring the lines entirely between the West Wing and the Department of Justice to try to enact some sort of legal punishment against the president’s perceived foes.”

Bulwark editor-in-chief, and MS NOW contributor Sam Stein agreed.

“Yeah. I mean, this is embarrassing, obviously,” he replied. “Let’s just call it what it is, this failure to get an indictment from the grand jury.”

“Remember, they had already failed to get several other indictments in several different cases,” he recalled. “This isn’t like Major League Baseball, where if you go, you know, one for three, it’s a success, right? This is bad stuff.”

“On top of that, it’s a huge waste of resources,” he pointed out. “But obviously the problem here is that they completely gutted the Eastern District of Virginia’s department because they were pressing so hard to bring charges against Letitia James and [ex-FBI Director] James Comey that all the actual real lawyers in the office said no, and then they quit or were fired.”

“And I think we need to step back a little bit, because obviously this is an abuse of power, and it’s just using the Justice Department to go after your foes.”

Our Analysis:

A Study in Desperation and Abuse: The DOJ’s Fruitless Crusade Against Letitia James

In a saga that reads like a script torn from the pages of a political thriller, yet sadly devoid of any credible plot, the Department of Justice under the guidance—or more aptly, the misguidance—of Donald Trump appointee Pam Bondi, has once again stumbled in its quixotic quest to pin fraud charges on New York Attorney General Letitia James. This latest chapter, as reported with palpable disdain by “Morning Joe” co-host Jonathan Lemire on MS NOW, not only underscores a desperate vendetta but also highlights a disturbing abuse of power at the highest levels of government.

The DOJ’s Obsession: A Fruitless Endeavor

Letitia James, a figure who has proven to be a formidable adversary to Trump by successfully convicting him of business fraud—a conviction only partially dimmed by an appeals court deeming the financial penalty “excessive”—has once again emerged unscathed from the DOJ’s attempt to sully her reputation with baseless fraud charges. This latest failure to secure an indictment from the grand jury, following on the heels of a judge dismissing a similar mortgage fraud case against James as unlawful, is emblematic of an alarming pattern of behavior.

The Waste of Resources and Legal Talent

The relentless pursuit of James (and, as noted, former FBI Director James Comey) is not just an embarrassing misallocation of the DOJ’s focus; it’s a hemorrhaging of its resources and talent. The Eastern District of Virginia’s department, once presumably a bastion of legal integrity, has been so thoroughly compromised in its pursuit of these vendettas that, as reported, “all the actual real lawyers in the office said no,” leading to a mass exodus through resignation or termination. This isn’t merely a failure; it’s a self-inflicted wound with far-reaching consequences for the administration of justice.

A Dangerous Abuse of Power

What we’re witnessing is not just a series of legal missteps; it’s a concerted effort to weaponize the Department of Justice against perceived enemies—a tactic that should alarm anyone with a vested interest in the rule of law and democratic principles. The blurring of lines between the West Wing and the DOJ, as Lemire aptly notes, represents an “extraordinarily dangerous abuse of power,” transforming what should be an impartial arm of the government into a tool for personal vendettas.

The Verdict: A Damning Indictment of the Trump DOJ

The relentless campaign against Letitia James serves as a stark reminder of the lengths to which certain factions will go to undermine their adversaries, irrespective of the cost to the country’s legal framework or its democratic institutions. This isn’t a testament to the strength or tenacity of the DOJ under Trump’s appointees; it’s a damning indictment of a justice system being bent towards autocratic ends.

In a functioning democracy, the Department of Justice serves the people by upholding the law without fear or favor. But when it’s hijacked as a tool for political retribution, it not only fails in its primary duty—it undermines the very foundations of justice and accountability in the process. As this saga unfolds, it’s incumbent upon all who cherish those principles to speak out, to question, and to demand better. Because in the end, the true victim of these vendettas isn’t just Letitia James—it’s the integrity of the American justice system itself.
