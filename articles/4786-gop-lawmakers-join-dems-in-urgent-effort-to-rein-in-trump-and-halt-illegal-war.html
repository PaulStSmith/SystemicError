GOP lawmakers join Dems in urgent effort to rein in Trump and halt ‘illegal war'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 1, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump leaves the stage at the end of an event at the Port of Corpus Christi in Corpus Christi, Texas, U.S., February 27, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Multiple Republican lawmakers have joined their Democratic colleagues in working to rein in President Donald Trump after his administration launched a major military operation in Iran early Saturday morning.

Leading the charge are Reps. Ro Khanna (D-CA) and Thomas Massie (R-KY), who’ve introduced what’s known as a War Powers Resolution, a legislative tool to check the president’s power over declaring war and require congressional authorization before U.S. forces can engage in sustained military action.

“Donald Trump has launched a war on Iran – the Congress must reconvene on Monday to vote on Thomas Massie and my War Powers Resolution to stop this war!” Khanna said in a statement Saturday. “Every member of Congress must go on record today on how they will vote on Thomas Massie and my War Powers Resolution!”

Massie outright said that he was “opposed to this war” on Saturday, and was joined in his opposition by his fellow Republican lawmaker Rep. Warren Davidson (R-OH), who argued earlier this week that the Trump administration had failed to make a case for military action against Iran.

Also joining the effort to rein in Trump was Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX), who on Saturday called the Trump administration’s military action in Iran “illegal.”

“Congress must do everything in its power to stop Trump’s illegal war, including reconvening for an immediate vote on a War Powers Resolution,” Casar wrote in a social media post on X Saturday morning. “But we will also need millions of Americans to speak out and demand an end.”

Trump authorized aseries of strikeson Iran early Saturday morning as part of what has been called Operation Epic Fury, with the focus of the initial strikes being Iran’s military assets. Civilian casualties have already been reported, however, including the striking of a girls’ school that leftat least 40 dead, per theIndiaTimes

Our Analysis:

The Unchecked Aggression of Presidential War Powers

In an audacious move that underscores the perennial issue of executive overreach, President Donald Trump has propelled the United States into a de facto state of war with Iran. The operation, dubbed “Operation Epic Fury,” not only marks a significant escalation in U.S.-Iran relations but also highlights a dangerous precedent of bypassing congressional approval for military actions. The strikes, which have tragically included civilian targets, demand a moment of pause and critical examination.

Bipartisan Backlash

It’s both noteworthy and commendable that opposition to this unilateral military action transcends partisan lines, with figures like Reps. Ro Khanna (D-CA) and Thomas Massie (R-KY) leading a bipartisan charge. Their proposal of a War Powers Resolution is not just a legislative rebuke of Trump’s actions but a clarion call for the sanctity of congressional oversight in matters of war and peace.

Khanna and Massie’s resolution serves as a stark reminder of the constitutional provisions designed to prevent exactly this type of executive adventurism. The framers of the Constitution vested the power to declare war with Congress precisely to avoid the whims of a single individual dragging the nation into conflict. Thus, when Khanna demands that Congress “must reconvene on Monday to vote on Thomas Massie and my War Powers Resolution to stop this war,” he’s not just advocating for a procedural check; he’s fighting for the very principles upon which the United States was founded.

Massie’s outright opposition, coupled with Rep. Warren Davidson (R-OH)’s skepticism about the justification for the military action, underscores a growing concern within the Republican Party about the trajectory of U.S. foreign policy under Trump’s guidance. Their voices, often in the minority within their party when it comes to foreign intervention, are crucial in the discourse surrounding the legality and prudence of military engagements.

The Legal Quagmire

Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX) goes a step further, labeling the military action in Iran as “illegal.” This is a significant accusation, pointing to the broader implications of Trump’s decision on international law and the United States’ compliance with it. The unauthorized use of military force against a sovereign nation is a grave matter that risks not just immediate retaliation but long-term geopolitical instability.

The operation’s reported civilian casualties, including the heart-wrenching detail of a girls’ school being struck, leaving at least 40 dead, should give any proponent of the strikes pause. The moral and ethical ramifications of such collateral damage are immense, further complicating the already tenuous justification for the operation.

The Path Forward

The bipartisan effort to pass a War Powers Resolution in response to Trump’s actions is a critical step in reasserting congressional authority over military engagements. However, it also highlights the broader issue of executive power and the need for systemic reforms to prevent future unilateral military actions without clear congressional authorization.

Moreover, the call by Casar for millions of Americans to speak out against the “illegal war” is a poignant reminder of the role of public opinion and civic engagement in democratic governance. It’s not just Congress that has a say in these matters; the American people must also hold their leaders accountable for decisions made in their name.

In conclusion, the situation demands more than just a legislative response. It requires a collective reevaluation of how the United States approaches conflict and the mechanisms in place to ensure that such decisions are made responsibly, transparently, and in accordance with both domestic and international law. The actions taken by Trump and the ensuing bipartisan backlash should serve as a wake-up call for a nation at a crossroads, questioning the very essence of its democratic principles and commitment to the rule of law.
