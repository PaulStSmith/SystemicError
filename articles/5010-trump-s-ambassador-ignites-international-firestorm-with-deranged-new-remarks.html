Trump's ambassador ignites international firestorm with 'deranged' new remarks

U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee ignited an international firestorm this weekend after appearing to endorse the idea of Israel taking control of the entire Middle East, remarks that prompted a swift response from more than a dozen Arab nations, including the United States’ own allies.

Huckabee sat down with conservative media figure Tucker Carlson recently for a lengthy interview that was published on Saturday, during which, Carlson pressed the former Arkansas governor on specifically what regions in the Middle East he believed Israel to be Biblically entitled to.

“Does Israel have the right to that land?” Carlson asked, making reference to what he described as “basically the entire Middle East,” including Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, and sizable portions of Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Jordan.

“It would be fine if they took it all,” Huckabee responded.

The comments drew an immediate backlash from more than a dozen Arab nations in the Middle East and North Africa, all of which signed onto a joint statement condemning the remarks as “extremist and lacking any sound basis,” NBC News reported Sunday.

Saudi Arabia, a U.S. ally, described Huckabee’s remarks as “extremist rhetoric,” and Egypt called them a “flagrant breach” of international law, NBC News reported.

Huckabee has appeared to endorse the idea of “Greater Israel” in the past, with “Greater Israel” referring to the territorial aspirations of some Israelis to significantly expand the nation’s borders. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu – who was indicted by the International Criminal Court for war crimes last year – said as recently as last August that he “absolutely” subscribed to a “vision” for “Greater Israel.”

“I don’t think we have fully [realized] how deranged the people driving US policy truly are,” wrote Bruno Macaes, an author, writer and geopolitical analyst, in asocial media poston X Sunday in response to Huckabee’s remarks

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Firestorm: Mike Huckabee’s Controversial Endorsement

In a world teeming with diplomatic tightropes, U.S. Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee has opted for a sledgehammer over a scalpel. His recent endorsement of an expansive Israeli territory, covering “basically the entire Middle East,” during an interview with Tucker Carlson, isn’t just a faux pas; it’s a geopolitical blunder of epic proportions. Let’s dissect this debacle piece by piece, shall we?

The Comments That Shook the Middle East

Huckabee’s assertion that “It would be fine if they took it all” is nothing short of astonishing in its disregard for international law, sovereignty, and basic decency. This statement, endorsing a so-called “Greater Israel” that encompasses countries like Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, and significant parts of Saudi Arabia and Iraq, is a flagrant provocation. It’s akin to tossing a match into a powder keg and marveling at the explosion.

The Backlash: Swift and Severe

The response from over a dozen Arab nations was immediate and united, branding Huckabee’s remarks as “extremist and lacking any sound basis.” This is not mere diplomatic displeasure; it’s a chorus of outrage from nations that see these comments as a direct threat to their sovereignty and stability. Saudi Arabia and Egypt, both pivotal U.S. allies, have expressed their condemnation in no uncertain terms, highlighting the gravity of Huckabee’s blunder.

The Bigger Picture: Endorsing Extremism

What’s equally concerning is the broader endorsement of “Greater Israel,” a vision that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has expressed support for. This notion, coupled with Huckabee’s comments, signals a dangerous tilt towards extremist territorial aspirations that fly in the face of international norms and peace efforts in the region.

The Realization of a Deranged Policy Direction

Bruno Macaes’s assertion that we may have underestimated the “deranged” nature of those steering U.S. policy in the Middle East seems more accurate than ever. Huckabee’s remarks do not exist in a vacuum; they reflect a broader, more troubling trend of extreme policy positions gaining traction at the highest levels of government.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Game

Mike Huckabee’s endorsement of a “Greater Israel” that spans the Middle East is not just a diplomatic misstep; it’s a dangerous game that threatens to unravel decades of delicate negotiations and peace efforts in the region. His comments, and the ideology they represent, risk igniting further conflict in an already volatile region. As we witness the international fallout, one thing becomes clear: the path to peace is not through expansionist fantasies but through dialogue, respect, and mutual understanding. The sooner Huckabee and his ilk realize this, the better for the Middle East and the world at large.
