Immunity for Me, Jail for Thee: The Deadly Double Standard Behind Driver Protection Laws

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 1, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

This article was sparked by a recent discussion I had around laws passed in several Republican-led states granting immunity to drivers who strike protesters. We ask: would the same immunity apply if the victims were anti-abortion demonstrators outside a Planned Parenthood clinic?

A License to Kill—Selective Edition

In the wake of the George Floyd uprisings, Republican legislatures across the United States rushed to pass laws offering legal immunity to drivers who hit protesters with their vehicles. Framed as “public safety” or “anti-riot” bills, these laws claim to protect drivers from liability if they “unintentionally” injure or kill people while fleeing from a crowd—assuming they felt they were in danger.

But strip away the legalese, and you’ll find a far uglier truth: these laws are less about protecting the public and more about weaponizing cars against dissent—specifically, left-wing dissent.

Let’s be blunt: if a car plows through a crowd blocking the street during a Black Lives Matter protest, these laws kick in like airbags. But swap that crowd for anti-abortion protesters outside a Planned Parenthood clinic, and suddenly the legal machinery grinds to a halt. The exact same action, carried out under the exact same conditions, would not be treated the same. Because in red state America, who you hit—and why—matters more than what you did.

Who Counts as a “Protester”?

The selective application of these laws hinges on a deeply partisan view of protest. When progressives flood streets in outrage over police brutality, it’s called a “riot.” When conservatives swarm clinics harassing women seeking legal medical care, it’s a “prayer vigil.”

This is not accidental.

Laws like Oklahoma’s HB 1674 or Florida’s HB 1 were written in the shadow of racial justice protests, specifically designed to immunize backlash against them. They were not forged with ideological neutrality in mind. They weren’t asking, “How do we protect innocent drivers?” They were screaming, “How do we stop those people from making noise?”

And yet, they are framed with chilling vagueness. “Unintentionally” causing harm while fleeing a crowd? What defines “unintentional”? Who gets to decide what’s a riot and what’s a demonstration? Who gets to be the victim—and who’s seen as the threat?

Answer: the same people who think tiki torches in Charlottesville were “very fine people,” but kneeling during a national anthem is “un-American.”

Let's Imagine a Role Reversal

Picture this: it’s a sweltering Thursday morning. A driver, late for a medical appointment, pulls up to a Planned Parenthood clinic only to find a crowd of protestors—holding grotesque signs, yelling invectives, blocking the entrance and part of the road. She honks, waits, then decides to slowly inch forward, scared, angry, maybe desperate.

She bumps a protestor.

The police arrive. She’s arrested.

Suddenly the law doesn’t see a frightened driver. It sees an “abortion militant,” a “clinic escort,” a “left-wing radical.” News outlets rush to condemn her as violent. Social media explodes with calls for maximum sentencing. And you can bet your last dollar no red-state DA is invoking “driver immunity” here.

Why? Because she hit the wrong team.

Protest Laws as Culture War Ammunition

Let’s be clear: protest has never been universally popular. It makes people uncomfortable. It disrupts traffic, schedules, and illusions. But a democratic society understands that the disruption is the point. The goal is to be heard when no one’s listening.

Yet in red America, “protest” is now synonymous with “leftist threat.” It’s why standing in traffic with a BLM sign is criminalized, but storming the Capitol with a Confederate flag earns a Fox News gig. It’s why drag shows get banned, but Proud Boys get speaking slots.

The driver immunity laws are part of this escalating culture war. They exist not to protect the innocent but to punish the defiant. They elevate property over people, convenience over conscience, and ideology over equality.

The Car as a Political Weapon

Cars have long been symbols of freedom in American mythology—but lately, they’ve become blunt instruments of political retaliation. From Charlottesville to Minneapolis to Tacoma, vehicles have been used to mow down demonstrators. These aren’t isolated incidents. They’re a pattern.

So what happens when the law steps in—not to stop this violence, but to bless it?

We get a chilling message: in the battle of ideas, some lives are disposable. Some causes are targets. And some murder weapons come with four wheels and a get-out-of-jail-free card.

The Double Standard Isn’t Just Hypocrisy—It’s Policy

This isn’t just a case of inconsistent enforcement. It’s a feature, not a bug. The laws were built with the assumption that “legitimate” protest comes from one side of the aisle.

If an anti-abortion protestor is hit, the narrative is immediate: the sanctity of life was attacked. But if a racial justice protestor is hit? The driver “feared for their safety.” If a driver hits a MAGA caravan by accident, they’re a terrorist. But if someone barrels into climate activists on a freeway, it’s “just unfortunate.”

We don’t need hypotheticals. We already know what would happen. Because we’ve seen what happens. The law isn’t blind—it’s politically cross-eyed, gazing only in one direction.

We Deserve Better Than Vehicular Vigilantism

Protest is messy. Democracy is inconvenient. And justice doesn’t always arrive on time. But the answer is never to crush dissent—literally or figuratively—beneath the wheels of partisan lawmaking.

Laws that allow drivers to hit protestors are not just morally bankrupt; they are legally obscene. They turn fear into a legal loophole, outrage into roadkill, and justice into a rigged game of who’s considered human today.

We must demand better. Not just for the people in the streets, but for the soul of a country that seems increasingly willing to trade its moral compass for a bumper sticker and the right to run people down with impunity.

Because if we don’t, someday, someone will ask:
“Whose side of the road were you on?”
