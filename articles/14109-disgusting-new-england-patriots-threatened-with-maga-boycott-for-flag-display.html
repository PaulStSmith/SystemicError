The Patriots on Sunday released a social media post, presumably with the intention of promoting Pride Month, that included a LGBTQ+ pride flag.

"We celebrate together," the team's account on social media wrote.

That didn't sit well with thousands of conservative activists and influencers, who took to the comments to insult the football team. Several commenters threatened boycotts of the team.

@Unhinginger wrote, "You just lost a ton to Fans. Enjoy the bud light treatment."

Michelle Hawley, who calls herself a "Trump-loving patriot" in her X bio, said, "People need to boycott any team does this no merchandise tickets nothing."

Sovereign Minds, a user who said Kamala Harris should be charged criminally for a mass-burning attack in Colorado, rejected the "celebrate together" notion itself.

"No we don't," the user wrote. "It is Veterans Appreciation Month. I see why Tom Brady left y'all. Disgusting."

Jon Del Arroz, a self-described bestselling Christian author, wrote, "Pride is a sin."

There was also a racist tone to some of the comments, with one "patriot" user, @SonderPatriot, asking Elon Musk's IA chatbot, "Who owns the New England Patriots and what’s his ethnicity?"

GROK replied, "The New England Patriots are owned by Robert Kraft, who has been the owner since 1994. He is Jewish, born to an observant Jewish family in Brookline, Massachusetts. His philanthropy, including significant donations to healthcare and diversity initiatives, aligns with the team's public support for social causes like Pride Month."

Another user, @Kitler512, also asked, "Who owns the New England patriots?" and then added, "Whats[sic] his ethnicity?"

Our Analysis:

The Pride Controversy: Unpacking the Patriots' Flag Fiasco

The first day of Pride Month has historically been a beacon of celebration and solidarity for the LGBTQ+ community. However, this year, it became a battleground of ideologies, courtesy of the New England Patriots' attempt to show support with a simple, yet apparently controversial, social media post. The inclusion of a LGBTQ+ pride flag with the words "We celebrate together" was enough to ignite the ire of the so-called "anti-woke" MAGA crowd. This reaction opens a Pandora's box on the current socio-political climate, intolerance, and the undeniable undertones of racism that still pervade American society.

The backlash from conservative activists and influencers was both swift and vitriolic. Threats of boycotts and derogatory comments flooded in, showcasing a disturbing trend where inclusivity is seen as a threat rather than a cause for celebration. Comments ranged from the hyperbolic (@Unhinginger's melodramatic "You just lost a ton to Fans. Enjoy the bud light treatment.") to the bafflingly irrelevant (Sovereign Minds' bizarre connection between LGBTQ+ rights and Veterans Appreciation Month). The underlying message was clear: inclusivity, in their eyes, has no place in America's favorite sport.

Michelle Hawley's call for a boycott over the team's support for Pride Month is emblematic of a broader issue within American society. The weaponization of patriotism to exclude and discriminate is neither new nor surprising, but it is always disheartening. The insinuation that supporting LGBTQ+ rights is somehow un-American is a fallacy that overlooks the very foundation of freedom and liberty.

Equally troubling is Jon Del Arroz's statement, "Pride is a sin," a sentiment that not only misinterprets religious doctrine but also perpetuates a culture of hate under the guise of faith. It's a stark reminder of how religion can be twisted to justify bigotry.

Then, there's the racist undercurrent evident in the questioning of the New England Patriots' ownership. The inquiries about Robert Kraft's ethnicity by users like @SonderPatriot and @Kitler512, followed by the insidious "Whats[sic] his ethnicity?" reveal a disturbing intersection of homophobia and anti-Semitism. This not only reflects a gross misunderstanding of what Pride Month represents but also highlights a deeper societal issue of racism that still needs to be addressed.

The response from GROK, Elon Musk's AI chatbot, outlines Kraft's contributions to healthcare and diversity, subtly underscoring the irony of the situation. The very values that the critics are attacking are the ones that Kraft, through his philanthropy, seeks to uphold. This juxtaposition lays bare the ignorance and intolerance of the commenters.

In conclusion, the uproar over the New England Patriots' Pride flag is more than just a disagreement over a social media post. It's a microcosm of the larger societal conflicts that plague the United States today. The vitriol spilled in the comments section is a symptom of a deeper malaise—a resistance to change, a fear of the 'other,' and a clinging to outdated notions of nationalism and purity.

As we move forward, it is crucial to remember that the fight for equality and inclusion is far from over. The backlash against the Patriots' Pride celebration is a stark reminder that while we may have made progress, the road ahead is still fraught with obstacles. It is up to us to continue pushing back against intolerance and bigotry, in all its forms, with even greater fervor.
