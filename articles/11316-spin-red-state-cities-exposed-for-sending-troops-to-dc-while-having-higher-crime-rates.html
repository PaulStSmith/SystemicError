'Spin': Red state cities exposed for sending troops to DC while having higher crime rates

The U.S. Capitol is seen along Pennsylvania Avenue in Washington. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

President Donald Trump has received support in his effort to take military control of law enforcement in Washington, D.C., with Republican governors sending hundreds of their own troops to patrol the streets of the nation's capital, all in the name of addressing crime. However, the actual number of arrests related to this initiative remains questionable.

A bigger issue, CNN analyst Marshall Cohen told anchor Boris Sanchez, is that most of the governors who are sending in troops have higher crime rates in their own cities.

"Marshall, take us through the data," said Sanchez.

"Yeah, Boris, there's a lot of talk, there's a lot of spin, but the numbers don't lie," said Cohen. "So we pulled the latest FBI violent crime statistics to find out what's going on."

"You just mentioned the National Guard troops," he continued. "There are 2,000 of them here in our nation's capital, half of them from the D.C. National Guard. Half of them come from these six states. Republican governors in these states sent troops here to join the D.C. guard: Ohio, West Virginia, South Carolina, Tennessee, Mississippi, and Louisiana. And we looked at the data, and there's actually 10 cities in those states that have a higher violent crime rate than D.C."

He pointed to Cleveland and Toledo in Ohio, as well as Memphis, Tennessee.

"They were number one last year in the nation for violent crime. Nashville. Charleston, West Virginia, the capital city there. And then two down in Louisiana, Shreveport and Lafayette. They all had a higher violent crime rate last year," he said.

"But if that's not bad enough for us, look at this," Cohen added. "The homicide rate. The murder rate last year in D.C. — it was 27 per 100,000. Not saying that's good. That's not good. It's too high. But look at this. Seventy-seven last year in Jackson, the capital city in Mississippi. And that's why the critics of this are saying it's not really about public safety. Because if that was the case, you'd be looking at some of these other states. It's more to put on a show for Donald Trump."

Our Analysis:

A Critical Examination of the Military Policing Strategy in Washington, D.C.

In a move that seems ripped straight from the pages of a dystopian playbook, President Donald Trump has garnered support to militarize law enforcement in Washington, D.C., with the backing of Republican governors. These governors have dispatched hundreds of troops to patrol the streets under the guise of combating crime. Yet, this strategy's efficacy and motivations are now under intense scrutiny, revealing a scenario fraught with irony, hypocrisy, and a glaring disregard for statistical evidence.

The Irony of Imported Security

CNN analyst Marshall Cohen, during a conversation with anchor Boris Sanchez, sheds light on a paradox that can only be described as bewildering. Despite the fanfare surrounding the deployment of 2,000 National Guard troops—half from D.C. and half from states like Ohio, West Virginia, South Carolina, Tennessee, Mississippi, and Louisiana—the actual impact on crime remains nebulous at best. The ironic twist? Most of the governors dispatching these troops preside over states with cities that have higher violent crime rates than Washington, D.C. itself.

A Tale of Misplaced Priorities

Cohen's analysis, grounded in the latest FBI violent crime statistics, exposes a glaring misallocation of resources and attention. Cities such as Cleveland, Toledo, Memphis, Nashville, Charleston, Shreveport, and Lafayette not only surpass D.C. in violent crime rates but also highlight the absurdity of the current initiative. If the motive truly was public safety, these governors would be better served addressing the rampant crime within their own jurisdictions rather than parading their forces in the nation’s capital.

The Spectacle of Security

Perhaps the most damning revelation comes from the comparison of homicide rates. With D.C.’s homicide rate standing at 27 per 100,000, it is dwarfed by the staggering 77 per 100,000 in Jackson, Mississippi. This discrepancy starkly illustrates that the initiative is less about safeguarding citizens and more about orchestrating a political spectacle. The strategy seems to be a thinly veiled attempt to showcase power and allegiance to Donald Trump, rather than a genuine effort to tackle crime.

Conclusion: The Real Crime is the Charade

The deployment of military forces for law enforcement in D.C., supported by Republican governors, is a maneuver that raises serious questions about priorities, effectiveness, and true intentions. The evidence presented by Cohen not only undermines the purported goal of enhancing public safety but also points to a concerning trend of politicizing safety and security for optical gains.

In essence, the real crime here is not the one being purportedly fought on the streets of Washington, D.C., but rather the charade of security being played out at the expense of real issues plaguing various American cities. It's high time for a redirection of focus from these grandstanding gestures to genuine efforts aimed at combating crime where it hits hardest.
