White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt deflected when she was asked to prove President Donald Trump wasn't lying about "paid insurrectionists" in Los Angeles.

The press secretary was asked about Trump's remarks during Wednesday's White House briefing.

"The president said yesterday that paid insurrectionists were behind or involved in the protests in Los Angeles," a reporter noted. "Does the White House have any more details about who they believe is paying these insurrectionists?"

"Yeah, the president spoke about this last night when he returned from Fort Bragg because frankly, the president has a lot of common sense," Leavitt replied. "And if you look at some of the imagery that we have received from the Department of Homeland Security and ICE and law enforcement who are on the ground, you will see boxes and boxes of very professionalized masks and rioting equipment being dropped off for these protesters."

"So it's a good question the president is raising and one we are looking into about who is funding these insurrectionists and these rioters and these protesters and these illegal criminals," she continued. "So, who is funding these illegal aliens who are coming in with this very professional riot gear?"

"It's a question everyone in this room, who cares about truth, should be asking. And we encourage your help in trying to get to the bottom of that question."

Our Analysis:

Deconstructing the Deflection: Unpacking White House Rhetoric

In a recent spectacle of evasion, White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt sidestepped a critical inquiry regarding President Donald Trump's claims about "paid insurrectionists" in Los Angeles. Trump's allegations, lacking evidence as they were presented, suggested a premeditated orchestration behind the protests. The press secretary's response, rather than clarifying, muddied the waters further, revealing a troubling pattern of communication from the current administration.

The Question of Evidence

When pressed for details that could substantiate the president's serious accusations, Leavitt's reply was notably devoid of specifics. Instead of providing evidence, she pivoted to a general assertion that the President's "common sense" and unspecified "imagery" from the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), ICE, and law enforcement were sufficient to raise questions about the funding of these so-called insurrectionists. The leap from questioning the origin of "professionalized masks and rioting equipment" to implicating "illegal aliens" in a funded operation to incite riots is not only a gross simplification but a dangerous conflation of unrelated issues.

The Evasion Technique

Leavitt's deflection is a textbook example of a broader strategy employed by the administration: when confronted with a demand for evidence, shift the focus to a vaguely related concern, thus avoiding direct answers while appearing to engage with the query. This tactic serves multiple purposes: - It muddies the waters, making it harder for the public and the press to focus on the lack of evidence behind serious accusations. - It stigmatizes dissent, by labeling protesters indiscriminately as "insurrectionists" and "illegal criminals," thereby delegitimizing genuine protest. - It spreads disinformation, by suggesting, without evidence, a sinister conspiracy involving illegal funding and orchestration of protests.

The Implications of Such Rhetoric

The implications of the administration's rhetoric are deeply concerning. By casting aspersions without evidence, the administration not only undermines the democratic process but also sows division and mistrust among the populace. The reference to "illegal aliens" equipped with "very professional riot gear" is a particularly insidious attempt to xenophobically link immigration with civil unrest, despite the lack of evidence to support such a claim.

Furthermore, the appeal to the press to "help" in investigating these baseless claims is a misleading call to action. It suggests that the burden of proof lies not with the accuser—in this case, the President and his administration—but with the media and, by extension, the public. This is a reversal of the foundational principle that those making allegations should provide evidence to support them.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The response from the White House Press Secretary is emblematic of a troubling trend toward obfuscation and misinformation. It is critical that the press and the public demand higher standards of evidence and accountability from those in power. The dangerous implications of spreading unfounded accusations—particularly those that demonize protesters and immigrants—cannot be overstated. In an era where truth is increasingly under assault, it is the responsibility of every citizen to question, to demand evidence, and to hold those in power to account for their words and actions.
