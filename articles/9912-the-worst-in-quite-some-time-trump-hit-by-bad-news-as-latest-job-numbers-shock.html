'The worst in quite some time': Trump hit by bad news as latest job numbers shock

Analysts were left shocked Wednesday after ADP released its latest jobs report that showed private employers had shed 32,000 jobs in September, a stark contrast to the expected gain of around 50,000 jobs.

“Very disappointing, it was a weak number; this is private sector jobs for September, the total comes in negative 32,000,” said CNBC host Sara Eisen on Wednesday.

“And that was a surprise because economists were looking for an increase of 51,000. Also, the revision was not good. August was revised down sharply, the month now showing negative 3,000 compared to the initial read of positive 54,000... the labor market is softening.”

The ADP jobs report does not factor in government jobs, with the official jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics now likely to be delayed due to the ongoing government shutdown that kicked off Wednesday morning at midnight. Even so, the numbers spelled poorly for President Donald Trump’s economy, leading to a number of media personalities to express their shock.

Fox Business’ Maria Bartiromo called the numbers as “worse than expected,” and Bloomberg News’ Michael McKee described the numbers as “obviously the worst in quite some time.”

The latest job numbers follow a wave of bad economic news for Trump, who raged against previous reports that showed a sharp decline in job growth, going as far as to call job numbers coming out of his own administration “rigged,” and firing the BLS chief in retaliation.

“Despite the strong economic growth we saw in the second quarter, this month’s release further validates what we’ve been seeing in the labor market, that U.S. employers have been cautious with hiring,” said ADP chief economist Nela Richardson in a statement.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Reality of Job Losses: A Blow to the Trump Economy

In an unexpected twist that left analysts and media personalities alike in a state of shock, the latest ADP jobs report unveiled a grim picture of the American job market under President Donald Trump’s administration. Contrary to the anticipated increase of around 50,000 jobs, private employers shockingly shed 32,000 jobs in September. This revelation not only contradicts the optimistic forecasts but also paints a bleak narrative of a weakening economy—a narrative that Trump has vehemently fought against by labeling such reports as “rigged.”

The Numbers Speak Louder than Rhetoric

CNBC host Sara Eisen’s description of the report as “very disappointing” and “a weak number” barely scratches the surface of the issue. The stark deviation from the expected increase to a significant decrease is alarming, to say the least. Moreover, the revision of August’s figures from a positive 54,000 to a dismal negative 3,000 further emphasizes a distressing trend in the labor market’s direction. These figures are not merely statistics—they are indicative of a larger, systemic problem within the U.S. economy, one that the Trump administration seems either unwilling or unable to address effectively.

Fox Business’ Maria Bartiromo and Bloomberg News’ Michael McKee echoed the sentiment of surprise and concern, underscoring the severity of the situation. When such diverse voices within the financial media landscape unite in their assessment, it’s a clear signal that the issue at hand transcends partisan lines—it’s a matter of national economic health.

The Irony of “Rigged” Reports

The article highlights an ironic twist in Trump’s reaction to unfavorable economic reports. His dismissal of previous job growth declines as “rigged” and the extreme step of firing the BLS chief in retaliation reveal a troubling approach to governance. It’s one thing to dispute the accuracy of data, but another to undermine the institutions that produce it, especially when actions taken are seemingly out of spite rather than reasoned critique.

The Real Victims

As ADP chief economist Nela Richardson aptly noted, despite the economic growth observed in the second quarter, the labor market’s reality tells a different story. Employers are cautious, and jobs are being lost. Behind these numbers lie real people—families struggling to make ends meet, individuals uncertain about their future, and young professionals entering a volatile job market.

Concluding Thoughts

The ADP jobs report serves as a wake-up call to the dire state of the American economy under Trump’s watch. It’s a testament to the fact that despite any claimed economic successes, the foundation is cracking, and those cracks are starting to show. The labor market’s softening is a symptom of deeper economic issues that need to be addressed with urgency and care.

In the end, the numbers don’t lie. They tell a story of an economy that’s not only underperforming but is actively moving in the wrong direction. It’s a narrative that requires immediate action, not denial or deflection. As the government shutdown looms, delaying further reports, one can only hope that this serves as a wake-up call for the Trump administration to start facing the economic realities of their making head-on.
