Ugly accusations fly among Trump supporters over school bombing

The bombing of a school in Iran has already started to create cracks in the MAGA coalition, as commentators generally loyal to the president are demanding answers.

Reports have indicated that a U.S. strike destroyed the Shajarah Tayyebeh elementary school, leaving 175 dead. President Donald Trump has dismissed these claims, asserting that the Iranian government itself destroyed the school.

But experts aren’t buying this claim — and some MAGA allies are not either.

“Re-upping what I said last week: Admin must wrap its investigation and address head-on. Horrible unintended tragedy of this war,” wrote right-wing commentator Laura Ingraham on X.

“Seconded,” replied Megyn Kelly.

Other MAGA accounts, however, pushed back on them, with one saying, “People die in a war, and sometimes they are not the target. War is messy. If a war is just, and this one is, then responsibility automatically falls on the party responsible for the war in the first place. In this case that is Iran. Case closed,” and another saying, “Laura falling for Iran propaganda is very on brand for Fox News.”

This comes as the MAGA sphere remains divided over the wisdom of invading Iran in the first place, with some pro-Trump influencers blasting it as a “betrayal“ of his entire pledge to supporters to get America out of military adventurism.

Our Analysis:

The MAGA Coalition: A House Divided?

The recent bombing of the Shajarah Tayyebeh elementary school in Iran has thrown a wrench into the seemingly impenetrable armor of the MAGA coalition, revealing a fissure that could very well turn into a chasm. Reports of a U.S. strike resulting in the tragic deaths of 175 individuals have sparked a conflict not just on the international stage, but within the ranks of President Donald Trump’s most ardent supporters. The president’s claim that the Iranian government is behind the destruction of the school is, to put it mildly, met with skepticism—not just by experts, but by members of his own fan base.

A Schism Among Allies

Right-wing commentator Laura Ingraham’s call for a wrap-up of the investigation and a direct address of the tragedy is a telling sign that loyalty to Trump does not equate to blind acceptance of all actions taken under his administration. Megyn Kelly’s endorsement of Ingraham’s statement further underscores the emerging rift. The question is not just about the legitimacy of the strike, but about the very principles upon which the MAGA movement was built.

The Justification of War

The defense offered by some MAGA supporters, that “People die in a war, and sometimes they are not the target,” is a chilling reminder of the dehumanization inherent in wartime rhetoric. The assertion that “If a war is just, and this one is, then responsibility automatically falls on the party responsible for the war in the first place” is a dangerous abdication of accountability. It’s a logic that absolves perpetrators of targeted violence under the guise of wartime necessity, a slippery slope that leads to unchecked aggression.

Betrayal of a Pledge

Perhaps the most striking aspect of this internal conflict is the accusation of betrayal. The invasion of Iran stands in stark contrast to Trump’s pledge to extricate America from the quagmire of “military adventurism.” This divergence from a foundational promise of the MAGA platform is not just a policy shift; it’s a profound betrayal of the trust placed in him by his supporters.

The Propaganda Machine at Work

The claim that skepticism towards the official narrative is “very on brand for Fox News” is an ironic twist, considering the network’s role in propelling the MAGA movement to the forefront of American politics. It reveals a deep-seated paranoia and a refusal to engage with dissenting perspectives—a hallmark of authoritarianism.

Conclusion

The bombing in Iran and the subsequent fallout within the MAGA ranks is a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the movement. It lays bare the inherent contradictions of a coalition built on the promise of anti-interventionism, yet now rationalizing an act of war that has led to the loss of innocent lives. As the MAGA coalition grapples with these contradictions, the true cost of such policies becomes painfully clear—not just in terms of geopolitical stability, but in the erosion of moral clarity and the internal coherence of a political movement. The tragedy in Iran is not just a test of Trump’s leadership, but a litmus test for the soul of MAGA itself.
