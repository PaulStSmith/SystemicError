MAGA denigrated as the man it tried to destroy flips the tables

Based on an article by Ray Richmond

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Fauci Was Not the Target. Public Trust Was.

The Setup

The source story is simple enough: an 85-year-old public-health veteran who helped steer the COVID vaccine response was dragged into a hostile Republican spectacle, with Rand Paul leading the charge and a leaked diary turned into a political cudgel. The hearing did not clarify a real charge so much as stage a ritual of resentment around masks, closures, vaccines, and the discomfort of being governed by evidence.

Who Held the Gavel

The institutional power in this story sat with the Republican senators, not with Fauci. They controlled the hearing, the questioning, and the camera-ready outrage. Fauci was the defendant in a theater they wrote, even as they pretended to be mere seekers of truth.

That matters because this was never a neutral inquiry into pandemic response. It was a political extraction operation: take one public servant, strip his words for insult, and convert public health into a partisan grievance machine.

The Charge Was Never Coherent

The article notes the blur of accusations: Wuhan, deaths, masks, shutdowns, school closures, mandates, vaccine skepticism. That scatter is the point. When a movement cannot settle on a specific offense, it is usually because the real offense is symbolic: Fauci represented expertise, caution, and a state that sometimes asked people to sacrifice convenience to keep other people alive.

That is what the anti-science camp cannot forgive. Not one memo, not one sentence in a diary, not one imperfect judgment. They resent the existence of adult authority itself.

The Diary Was the Hook

Leaked private remarks about Trump being “rambling,” “crazy,” “desperate,” and worse were treated as if they were the core crime. They were not. They were evidence that a public official, in private, recognized the obvious: he had been forced to work under a president the article describes as incompetent and chaotic.

The diary was useful because it let Republicans pretend this was about decorum rather than power. It is always easier to prosecute tone than to answer for bodies, infections, and the wreckage of a pandemic response.

Misdirection as Method

This is what political misdirection looks like in practice. The people who turned public-health guidance into culture-war fuel now posture as aggrieved inquisitors. They want Fauci to stand for every inconvenience, every mistake, every fear that accompanied a once-in-a-century crisis. That lets them avoid the harder accounting: who spread confusion, who profited from denial, and who made lying feel like liberty.

The source is right to treat the spectacle as shameful. It is less convincing when it frames the episode as almost mystifying. There is nothing mysterious here. This is punishment politics: find a competent expert, blame him for the disruption caused by reality, and call the performance accountability.

The Larger Pattern

The deeper lesson is that American conservatism has learned to weaponize oversight against expertise while never surrendering real power. It manufactures enemies out of scientists, then calls the persecution evidence of its own seriousness. That is not democratic correction. It is institutional cowardice dressed up as courage.

A democratic politics worth the name does not hand public health to people who think ignorance is authenticity. It defends expertise, names sabotage as sabotage, and stops pretending that deliberate cruelty is just confusion with better lighting.
