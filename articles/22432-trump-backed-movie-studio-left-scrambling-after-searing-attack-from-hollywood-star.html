Trump-backed movie studio left scrambling after searing attack from Hollywood star

Oracle, Paramount, and the Thin Veneer of “Neutrality”

The Power Is Not On Instagram

The source story is not really about Mark Ruffalo’s tone. It is about who controls capital, media infrastructure, and the legal channels that move a merger forward. Larry Ellison co-founded Oracle, chairs it, and is the father of Paramount CEO David Ellison. Safra Catz sits on Paramount’s board. That is the power map. Ruffalo is reacting to it; he is not the one assembling a media-and-tech bloc with state power and surveillance-grade tools.

What Was Actually Said

The factual core is straightforward: a resurfaced video shows Safra Catz saying Oracle did things she “can’t talk about” to support the Israeli military after Oct. 7, and that Oracle has “profoundly scary technology.” Ruffalo then tied that corporate arsenal to the proposed Oracle/Paramount/Warner Bros. Discovery merger and warned that the technology could wind up inside one of the largest media conglomerates in the world. That is not a mystery. It is a disclosure of the kind of alliance that should alarm anyone who still thinks media consolidation and national-security capitalism live in separate boxes.

The Real Misdirection

Paramount’s response is the familiar corporate deflection: lower the temperature, condemn the critic, and avoid the substance. The company says Ruffalo crossed into “antisemitic tropes,” then tries to reroute the discussion into “legal merits” and “underlying bias.” That framing is convenient because it shifts attention away from the merger itself, away from Oracle’s role, and away from the documented comments about military support and “scary technology.”

There is a real difference between antisemitism and criticism of a billionaire-dominated corporate network. Paramount blurs that distinction for self-protection. It wants the public to argue about Ruffalo’s rhetoric instead of Ellison family power, boardroom alignment, and the merger politics around media consolidation.

Who Enabled This

President Donald Trump’s Justice Department greenlit the merger before a judge halted it after a lawsuit from a dozen state attorneys general. That is the institutional chain that matters. The executive branch opened the gate; the courts and state AGs slowed it down. This is not a story about one celebrity being impolite. It is a story about the normal functioning of oligarchic access: regulatory blessing up top, litigation as the remaining brake, and corporate public relations trying to launder the whole arrangement as standard business.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

The deeper pattern is the merger of media power, surveillance-adjacent technology, and political impunity. Oracle is not just another software company in this context; the source itself makes clear that the company’s technology is being discussed in relation to the Israeli military, then linked to a major media consolidation effort. That should be treated as a democratic danger in its own right. When a corporate family can span tech, media, and political influence while dismissing scrutiny as bias, the public is no longer dealing with a neutral marketplace. It is dealing with coordinated power.

The Point

The scandal is not that Ruffalo named oligarchs. The scandal is that the underlying structure he pointed to exists at all, and that Paramount’s first instinct was to protect it with a victimhood script. Corporate America loves “lowering the temperature” when the heat is on the people with money and boards and merger leverage. The democratic response is not politeness. It is clarity: identify the actors, follow the money, and refuse to let propaganda about tone obscure concentrated power.
