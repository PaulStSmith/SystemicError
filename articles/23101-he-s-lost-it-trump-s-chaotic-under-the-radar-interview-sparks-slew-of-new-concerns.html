'He's lost it': Trump's 'chaotic' under-the-radar interview sparks slew of new concerns

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosSeptember 6, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Source MaterialPower Without DisciplineThe Performance of ControlMisdirection as StrategyThe Real Pattern

Presidential Incoherence Is Not the Story. Power Is.

The Source Material

A British columnist says Trump gave GB News an erratic, meandering interview that wandered from immigration to Meghan Markle to Iran and the Falklands, and that the performance looked less like strength than an increasingly incoherent president trying to sound commanding. That is the surface. The deeper story is that he is still using the authority of the U.S. presidency to narrate wars, fears, and obligations that he did not cleanly authorize and cannot clearly explain.

Power Without Discipline

Trump still holds institutional power, and that is the problem. The office gives him a megaphone, diplomatic weight, and the ability to turn private obsessions into public doctrine. The interview shows a president who can still impose his confusion on foreign audiences, even when the content is rambling and the logic collapses under basic scrutiny.

That is not accidental. The presidency is not failing here because one man is merely old or unwell. It is failing because the institution has been built to absorb and normalize personal volatility as if it were statecraft.

The Performance of Control

The article’s most revealing detail is Trump’s demand for gratitude. That is the logic of strongman politics in miniature: make the audience treat the leader’s unilateral decisions as gifts, then frame skepticism as ingratitude. The problem is not just that he sounds incoherent. It is that incoherence is being paired with entitlement to obedience.

The source also notes that this war did not secure congressional approval and has no clearly stated goals. That matters. When the executive acts without a defined public mandate, the usual checks are not absent by accident. They are being bypassed by design, while the president still expects credit for the consequences.

Misdirection as Strategy

The article rightly mocks Trump’s Iran claims, but the larger danger is how easily this kind of rhetoric shifts attention away from decision-making power. If a president is portrayed mainly as “rambling,” the political system gets to treat the problem as a personality defect instead of an accountability failure.

That framing is convenient. It lets institutions avoid the harder question: who enabled this, who ratified it, and who benefits from presenting reckless state action as merely confused speech? Calling it incoherence can become a form of deodorant for deliberate harm.

The Real Pattern

This is the recurring authoritarian pattern: personal instability wrapped in the symbols of office, force without a clear public purpose, and spectacle used to blur responsibility. The interview is not notable because Trump said odd things about the Falklands. It is notable because a U.S. president can still speak like a man outside reality while remaining inside the machinery that makes reality for everyone else.

That is the system’s failure mode. It rewards domination, excuses chaos, and treats scrutiny as a curiosity rather than a duty. The crisis is not that Trump sounded lost. The crisis is that the office can keep operating while he does.
