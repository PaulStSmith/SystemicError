'It has gone horribly': CNN analyst finds Trump policy not 'changing people's minds'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Gulf Naming Fiasco: A Dive into the Absurd

CNN

House Republicans passed Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene's bill to rename the Gulf of Mexico as the "Gulf of America," but CNN's Harry Enten said the name change first advanced by president Donald Trump isn't breaking through with the public.

If passed by the Senate, the bill would codify the executive order Trump signed on his first day in office, although other countries would not be obligated to go along with the change, and Enten presented polling data that showed Americans don't see the point of the move.

"Let's just put it this way," Enten said. "It has bounced, it's like I'm wearing waterproof clothing, if the water was coming down on me and it was the Gulf of Mexico. It bounces right off of me. It has gone horribly. This bounce is terrible."

Google searches for the Gulf of Mexico have exceeded searches for the Gulf of America in all 50 states and the District of Columbia, which Enten said indicated that Trump's preferred name wasn't sticking.

"The bottom line is, despite whatever Trump wants to say at this particular point, when it comes to what Americans are actually using, they are sticking with the old name," Enten said. "They are sticking with the Gulf of Mexico. They are not in any state going with the Gulf of America. It is 50-to-none, a blowout in favor of the Gulf of Mexico over the Gulf of America. When you're going into Google to search for whatever that body of water is called underneath, you know, Alabama, Mississippi and by Florida, there are people in every state are typing in this instead of this. That is exactly right, and every single state, more folks are typing in Gulf of Mexico in order to search for that body of water underneath our southern border, compared to the Gulf of America – zero states."

Polling shows that 67 percent of registered voters oppose renaming the Gulf of Mexico, and even 36 percent of Republicans are against the move.

"When it comes to the American people, two-thirds oppose, and that is very rare, because in our politics, you rarely get two- thirds of Americans to agree upon anything," Enten said, "but they do oppose the idea of renaming the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America."

What's more, Enten said, the issue is actually motivating Trump's opponents, with 34 percent of Democrats saying the change makes them very motivated to vote, compared to just 18 percent of Republicans.

"The bottom line is Donald Trump has been trying over the last three months to sell this idea that we're going to make the Gulf of Mexico the Gulf of America, but there is no indication that it's actually seeping in to the American consciousness," Enten said. "You see it here. It motivates Democrats more than Republicans. You see it here. 67 percent of Americans oppose the idea, and you see it in the Google searches as well."

"You see that people, simply put, are not going along with Donald Trump, and every single gosh-darn state, more folks are googling Gulf of Mexico than Gulf of America," he added. "There is no sign that this is working out and Trump is changing people's minds."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Gulf Naming Fiasco: A Dive into the Absurd

In an era where political theater often trumps (pun intended) substantive policy debates, the latest act from the House Republicans takes the absurdity to a whole new level. The passage of Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene's bill to rename the Gulf of Mexico as the "Gulf of America" is a prime example of performative politics at its most bewildering. It's a move so outlandish, it begs the question: are we witnessing a legislative body at work or a satirical sketch in progress?

The Proposal's Lackluster Reception

CNN's Harry Enten's commentary paints a vivid picture of a nation collectively shrugging its shoulders at this quixotic quest to Americanize the Gulf of Mexico. Despite the fervor with which this bill was passed in the House, the American public seems to be firmly against the idea, with a whopping 67 percent of registered voters opposing the name change. This includes, notably, 36 percent of Republicans, showcasing a rare moment of bipartisan consensus in an otherwise polarized political climate.

The data speaks volumes - not only are Americans opposed to this change, but they also remain steadfast in their use of the original name. Google search data, a modern barometer of public opinion, shows an overwhelming preference for "Gulf of Mexico" over "Gulf of America" across all 50 states and the District of Columbia. This isn't just a defeat; it's a nationwide dismissal of an unwanted and unnecessary rebranding effort.

A Misguided Priority

In a time when the country faces myriad pressing issues, the focus on such a trivial matter is perplexing, to say the least. The resources and legislative energy expended on this endeavor could undoubtedly be better spent addressing the myriad of challenges facing Americans today. Climate change, healthcare, economic inequality - the list of priorities is long and urgent. Yet, here we are, discussing nomenclature for a body of water that, frankly, doesn't need a new name.

Motivating the Opposition

Interestingly, this issue seems to have done more to galvanize Trump's opponents than to rally his base. According to Enten, 34 percent of Democrats reported that this name change saga has made them very motivated to vote, compared to just 18 percent of Republicans. This suggests that the move could backfire spectacularly, serving to energize the very demographic it likely sought to sideline or demoralize.

The Verdict

The push to rename the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America is a stark reminder of the current state of American politics - where symbolism often eclipses substance, and the pursuit of ideological victories overshadows the real needs of the American people. It's a maneuver so disconnected from the public's concerns and desires that it borders on the farcical.

In sum, this episode is not just a failed attempt at renaming a geographic feature. It's a metaphor for a broader systemic failure - a diversionary tactic that prioritizes petty victories over genuine governance. As we move forward, one can only hope for a return to a political discourse grounded in reality, focused on addressing the actual issues that affect the daily lives of Americans. Until then, we remain adrift in a sea of absurdity, ironically, still known to us all as the Gulf of Mexico.
