Senior Trump official badmouths 'tedious' European leaders behind their backs

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. President Donald Trump meets French President Emmanuel Macron and Finland’s President Alexander Stubb, amid negotiations to end the Russian war in Ukraine, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 18, 2025. REUTERS/Al Drago

A senior Trump administration official ridiculed the several European leaders that attended the high-stakes meeting between President Donald Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on Monday, calling the heads of state “tedious” and “used to being in charge.”

Speaking with Politico on the condition of anonymity, the official said the goal of inviting the European leaders to the meeting was “to say ‘we’re in charge,” and to communicate to them that they’ll “sign on to anything we say.”

“The Europeans were positive today, but they are tedious,” the official said. “But they were really good. And if you just were an observer of the two hours today, you'd say, ‘wow, that’s like a family – a happy family.’ But they all have their own little thing that they want to have happen.”

Monday’s meeting with Zelenskyy was the second recent meeting initiated by Trump to bring about a quick end to the Russo-Ukraine War, and followed Trump’s Friday meeting in Alaska with Russian President Vladimir Putin.

And while some fears that Trump would fold in the negotiations to Putin’s demands have been quelled following Monday’s meeting, the official made clear that the United States was still calling the shots — despite European leaders’ pleas that the Trump administration commit to a few red lines in its negotiations with Putin.

“They're heads of state,” the official told Politico. “They’re used to being in charge. And when you put seven of them in one room, you get what you would think. But it wasn't bad.”

The fact that European leaders were invited at all to the meeting Monday in Washington, D.C. was due strictly to what the official described as the success of Trump’s meeting with Putin the preceding week, a second senior administration official said, who noted that Russia seemed open to concessions to end the war, and that that “opening is huge.”

“After Alaska, we were excited that Putin was at least talking and there were signs we could negotiate,” the second official said. “If Alaska was not successful and Putin didn’t give us a little bit of an opening, we wouldn’t have [had] the Europeans at the White House.”

European leaders are set to convene virtually Tuesday to debrief on the outcomes of Trump’s meeting with Zelenskyy, dubbed the “Coalition of the Willing”, co-chaired by Prime Minister Keir Starmer and French President Emmanuel Macron. The White House now has its hopes set on a trilateral summit, a meeting between Trump, Zelenskyy and Putin.

Dissecting the Arrogance: A Critique of the Trump Administration’s Approach to European Leaders and the Russo-Ukraine War Negotiations

The recent Politico article sheds light on what can only be described as a brazen display of egotism by a senior Trump administration official. This individual, who opted for the cowardly shield of anonymity, openly ridiculed European leaders who participated in the crucial meeting between President Donald Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Labeling these heads of state as “tedious” and accusing them of only being accustomed to leading—rather than following—underscores a disturbing undercurrent of disrespect within the Trump administration's foreign policy approach.

The official's assertion that the inclusion of European leaders was merely a power play—“to say ‘we’re in charge,’”—reveals a misguided superiority complex. The claim that these leaders would “sign on to anything we say” not only insults the intelligence and sovereignty of European nations but dangerously oversimplifies the delicate dynamics of international diplomacy.

It's particularly noteworthy that this meeting followed a previous one in Alaska between Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin, aimed at ending the Russo-Ukraine War. The senior official's comments suggest a disturbing satisfaction with merely the appearance of unity and progress, rather than any substantive negotiation outcomes. Describing the European leaders as part of a “happy family” trivializes the critical stakes of these discussions, which are, at their core, about the sovereignty of Ukraine and the stability of Europe.

Furthermore, the official's remarks inadvertently confirm fears regarding Trump's potential to "fold" in negotiations with Putin. While trying to assert the U.S.'s dominance in negotiations, the official instead highlights a jarring lack of commitment to essential diplomatic principles and red lines, as requested by European leaders. This cavalier attitude towards complex international negotiations does not inspire confidence but rather signals a dangerous propensity towards appeasement of authoritarian regimes.

The mention of the success of the Alaska meeting as the sole reason for involving European leaders in Washington discussions is both short-sighted and misleading. The suggestion that Russia’s "opening" for concessions was a direct result of this meeting ignores the broader context of international pressure and diplomacy at play. It also naively assumes that one meeting can pivot the course of an aggressive military conflict that has deep historical and geopolitical roots.

The upcoming virtual meeting of European leaders, dubbed the “Coalition of the Willing,” co-chaired by Prime Minister Keir Starmer and French President Emmanuel Macron, represents a critical counterbalance to the Trump administration's erratic negotiation style. It underscores the importance of a collective and principled European response to the Russo-Ukraine War, in stark contrast to the unilateral and dismissive approach suggested by the Trump officials.

In anticipation of a proposed trilateral summit between Trump, Zelenskyy, and Putin, one must question the efficacy and motives behind the U.S.'s current negotiation strategy. The administration's apparent prioritization of showmanship over substance—as evidenced by the senior official's comments—raises profound concerns about the future direction of U.S. foreign policy and its implications for global stability.

In conclusion, the Politico article reveals more than just a senior official’s disdain for European partners; it exposes a fundamental misunderstanding of the nature of diplomacy and international relations by the Trump administration. Such an approach not only jeopardizes the delicate balance of power and respect among nations but also emboldens adversaries by showcasing a divided and discordant Western alliance.
