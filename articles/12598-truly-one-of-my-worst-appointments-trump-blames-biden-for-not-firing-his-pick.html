'Truly one of my worst appointments': Trump blames Biden for not firing his pick

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 19, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Deflection: Trump's Critique of Powell

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Donald Trump on Friday called out a man the president said is "truly" one of his "worst appointments."

Trump has repeatedly prodded and attacked Fed Chair Jay Powell, whom Trump himself hired in his first term, for his purported interest rate inaction.

Ahead of the weekend, Trump slammed Powell, and blamed then-President Joe Biden for not firing Powell while Biden was in the White House.

Referring to his nickname for Powell, Trump said, "'Too Late,' and the Fed, are choking out the housing market with their high rate, making it difficult for people, especially the young, to buy a house."

"He is truly one of my worst appointments. Sleepy Joe saw how bad he was and reappointed him anyway - And the Fed Board has done nothing to stop this 'numbskull' from hurting so many people," Trump added. "In many ways the Board is equally to blame! The USA is Rockin’, there is VERY LOW INFLATION, and we deserve to be at 1%, saving One Trillion Dollars a year on Interest Costs. I can’t tell you how dumb Too Late is - So bad for our Country!"

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump's Critique of Powell

In a recent tirade that can only be described as vintage Trump, the former president has once again unleashed his fury on an all-too-familiar target: Jay Powell, the Chair of the Federal Reserve. Trump's penchant for scapegoating officials for broader systemic issues is nothing short of legendary, and this instance is no exception. Let's dissect this cacophony of blame and misdirection with the precision of a scalpel.

Firstly, Trump's assertion that Jay Powell is "truly one of my worst appointments" is a masterclass in avoiding accountability. It's crucial to remember that Powell, appointed by Trump himself, has been a central figure in the Fed's efforts to manage the economy. The irony of Trump criticizing his own choice is rich with the kind of self-unawareness that has become a hallmark of his public declarations.

Secondly, the attack on President Joe Biden for not firing Powell is a bewildering act of deflection. Trump's insinuation that Biden should have rectified his "mistake" is a bizarre expectation, especially considering the independence of the Federal Reserve is a foundational principle meant to insulate it from political whims. This attempt to shift blame to Biden for his own appointment decision is a convoluted logic that only Trump could attempt to sell with a straight face.

Moreover, Trump's criticism of the Fed's interest rate policies under Powell as "choking out the housing market" is an oversimplification of a complex issue. While it's true that higher interest rates can make mortgages more expensive, this is a tool used by the Fed to combat inflation — a nuance conveniently omitted from Trump's rant. His assertion of "VERY LOW INFLATION" is at odds with the realities faced by Americans, suggesting a disconnect from the economic challenges confronting the average citizen.

Trump's lament that the U.S. "deserves to be at 1%" interest, claiming it would save "One Trillion Dollars a year on Interest Costs," is a simplistic view of monetary policy that ignores the balancing act the Fed must perform to manage inflation and stimulate economic growth. This statement also glosses over the potential downsides of artificially low interest rates, such as the encouragement of excessive borrowing and the risk of creating asset bubbles.

Finally, Trump's personal attacks on Powell, calling him "Too Late" and a "numbskull," are an unfortunate diversion from substantive economic debate. This name-calling serves no purpose other than to deflect from the complexities of economic management and to scapegoat Powell for broader economic trends.

In summary, Trump's critique of Jay Powell and the Federal Reserve is a textbook example of his approach to politics: blame, deflect, and oversimplify. It's a reminder of the importance of understanding the nuanced realities of economic policy, rather than getting caught up in the whirlwind of Trump's blame game. The real issues at play — economic management, inflation control, and the independence of the Federal Reserve — deserve a serious conversation, not just a series of scathing tweets and sound bites.
