Speaker of the House Mike Johnson is coming under fire after a report revealed he expects to swear in this week the newest Republican elected to Congress, Matt Van Epps, for whom Johnson campaigned. Van Epps won a narrow victory Tuesday night in a deep red Tennessee district. The move comes after Johnson most recently delayed seating Democratic U.S. Rep. Adelita Grijalva of Arizona for 50 days.

Johnson offered an array of explanations for why he would not swear in Congresswoman Grijalva, who won her September election for a seat vacant since March but was not seated until November.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Speaker Mike Johnson: A Scathing Analysis

In the latest saga of political hypocrisy that would make even the most cynical observer blush, Speaker of the House Mike Johnson has embroiled himself in a controversy that reeks of partisanship and double standards. The crux of the matter lies in Johnson’s blatant preferential treatment in swearing in the newly elected Republican, Matt Van Epps, over the delayed seating of Democratic U.S. Rep. Adelita Grijalva.

Swift Actions for Friends

Matt Van Epps, fresh off a narrow victory in a staunchly red Tennessee district, is set to be sworn in this week with the enthusiastic support of Mike Johnson, who had actively campaigned for him. The speed at which Johnson is moving to seat Van Epps stands in stark contrast to his previous actions—or rather, inactions—when it came to seating a Democrat.

A Tale of Two Seatings

Democratic U.S. Rep. Adelita Grijalva of Arizona found herself in legislative limbo for 50 excruciating days after her election victory. Johnson, armed with a buffet of excuses, dragged his feet in swearing her in. His reasons ranged from the House not being in session, a federal government shutdown, and the need for official certification of Grijalva’s election. However, critics were quick to point out the glaring inconsistency, as other members-elect were sworn in under similar circumstances without delay.

The Ulterior Motive

The reason behind Johnson’s reluctance becomes even more suspect when considering Grijalva’s legislative intentions. She had plans to co-sponsor bills on pressing issues such as the environment and public education. More provocatively, she aimed to provide the crucial final signature on a petition demanding a vote on legislation to release files related to accused sex trafficker Jeffrey Epstein, a move opposed by both Johnson and Donald Trump. This revelation, first reported by The Guardian, paints a picture of political maneuvering designed to stifle initiatives that run counter to Johnson’s and his allies’ interests.

The Critics’ Chorus

The inconsistency in Johnson’s actions has not gone unnoticed, drawing ire and sarcasm from various quarters. Robbie Sherwood, communications director of the Arizona House Democratic Caucus, snarked about being misled on the customary waiting period for seating. Political commentators and strategists alike expressed disbelief and frustration over the apparent double standard, highlighting that the “speed of democracy” seemingly varies based on political affiliation and racial considerations.

Conclusion: Democracy on Demand?

Mike Johnson’s actions—or lack thereof—raise serious questions about the integrity of the democratic process in the House. By selectively delaying or expediting the swearing-in of elected representatives based on political convenience, Johnson not only undermines the democratic principles he is sworn to uphold but also erodes public trust in the institution he leads. It’s a stark reminder that in the corridors of power, justice and fairness are often contingent upon whom you know and which side of the aisle you sit on. This is not just a systemic error; it’s a blatant affront to the principles of democracy and equality that should concern us all.
