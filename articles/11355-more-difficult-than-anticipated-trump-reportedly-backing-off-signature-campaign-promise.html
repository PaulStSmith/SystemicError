'More difficult than anticipated': Trump reportedly backing off signature campaign promise

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 22, 20254 min read

Donald Trump listens at the White House. REUTERS/Alexander Drago

President Donald Trump is handing off responsibility for one of his signature campaign promises.

The U.S. president will leave Russian and Ukrainian officials to organize a meeting between Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskyy to discuss an end to hostilities without taking a direct role himself, reported The Guardian, citing officials familiar with the situation.

"Trump’s reluctance to push Putin and Zelenskyy to a meeting comes as he has acknowledged in recent days that ending the war in Ukraine has been more difficult than he had anticipated," the publication reported, "after saying on the campaign trail last year he could achieve it in 24 hours."

The president has reportedly told advisers in recent days that he plans to host a trilateral meeting involving the two leaders, but only after they've met first, but plans for that meeting are not set and Trump does not intend to get involved in planning those talks.

“I just want to see what happens at the meeting," Trump told talk radio host Mark Levin. "So they’re in the process of setting it up and we’re going to see what happens.”

The president has met separately with each leader in the past week after his deadline for Russia to end its invasion expired this month, and a senior administration official said he was taking a “wait-and-see approach” to a possible Putin-Zelenskyy meeting, although there haven't been any tangible signs of progress and the White House hasn't offered any suggestions for where those talks could take place.

“Trump and his national security team continue to engage with Russian and Ukranian officials towards a bilateral meeting to stop the killing and end the war," the White House said in a statement. "It is not in the national interest to further negotiate these issues publicly.”

Putin's foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said Trump and the Russian president agreed that senior negotiators would be selected for direct talks with Ukraine, which suggests direct talks are not imminent, and Russian foreign minister Sergei Lavrov complicated matters by suggesting that his country should help guarantee security for the country it has invaded.

“The Ukrainian side proposed, and our delegation at that time agreed, to work out security guarantees that would involve all permanent members of the UN security council – that is, Russia, the People’s Republic of China, the United States, France, and the United Kingdom,” Lavrov said.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump's Hands-Off Approach to Ukraine

In an almost satirical twist of fate, President Donald Trump seems to have realized that his campaign trail promise of ending the war in Ukraine within 24 hours was more of a fantastical pledge than a feasible policy. According to a report by The Guardian, Trump has decided to step back, leaving the heavy lifting of organizing a peace meeting between Vladimir Putin and Volodymyr Zelenskyy to the officials of Russia and Ukraine themselves. This move is not just a stark deviation from his initial bravado but underscores a broader theme of his administration: big talk, little action.

Trump's Reluctance: A Closer Look

Trump's reluctance to actively push for a meeting between Putin and Zelenskyy, as reported, reveals a profound underestimation of the complexities involved in international diplomacy and conflict resolution. After boldly claiming he could end the Ukraine conflict in a day, Trump now tells advisers he plans to host a trilateral meeting only after Putin and Zelenskyy have met first. However, this promise is as vague as it is non-committal, with no concrete plans set and Trump openly stating he does not intend to get involved in the planning of such talks. This hands-off approach is not just a display of diplomatic indifference but a testament to a lack of strategic foresight.

The Wait-and-See Approach: A Recipe for Inaction

Trump's "wait-and-see approach" to a possible Putin-Zelenskyy meeting, as a senior administration official described, might as well be a euphemism for inaction. Despite meeting separately with each leader, there have been no tangible signs of progress. The White House's silence on potential locations for these talks further amplifies the administration's reticence to take a clear stance or lead in any meaningful way towards peace.

The Russian Complication

The situation is further complicated by Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov's suggestion that Russia, the very nation that invaded Ukraine, should help guarantee security for the country. This proposition is as ironic as it is problematic, highlighting the perverse logic that seems to guide much of the diplomatic discourse surrounding the conflict. It's a classic case of the aggressor playing the peacemaker, a role that the international community should scrutinize with skepticism and caution.

Concluding Thoughts

Trump's admission of the difficulty in ending the war in Ukraine is perhaps one of the rare moments of candor in his presidency, albeit a candidness born out of failure to deliver on grandiose promises. His delegation of responsibility to Russian and Ukrainian officials, coupled with a nebulous promise of a future trilateral meeting, is a clear abdication of leadership. It reflects a troubling pattern of his administration: a preference for rhetoric over reality, spectacle over substance, and promises over progress.

In the grand scheme of things, Trump's approach to the Ukraine crisis is a microcosm of his broader foreign policy strategy—or lack thereof. It's a strategy characterized by a fundamental misunderstanding of international relations, a penchant for oversimplification of complex issues, and a disturbing willingness to sidestep America's role on the world stage. As the situation unfolds, it remains to be seen whether Trump's hands-off approach will lead to any meaningful resolution, or if it will serve as yet another testament to the systemic errors of his administration.
