'Come over here, weenie': New video shows MAGA lawmaker taunting protester before shove

A House Republican's story about a physical altercation he had with a protester outside a federal building in Washington, D.C., appeared to be undercut by videos of the incident, according to a new report.

Rep. Tim Burchett (R-TN) was confronted by shouting protesters Thursday outside the Longworth House Office Building last week. The confrontation revolved around the lawmaker's stance on the Israel-Hamas war in Gaza. At the time, his office claimed he was bumped into by the protester, leading Burchett to give the man a forceful shove.

“Everyone has a right to their opinion, and they can say all of the filthy stuff they want. But they don’t have the right to bump the Congressman,” Will Garrett, a spokesperson for Burchett, told Politico in a statement.

Burchett later said the man “had bad breath.”

But the story took a turn Monday night after Politico said it reviewed video footage that reveals what led up to the "bump" and subsequent shove.

A "recording of the incident, reviewed by POLITICO, shows that prior to the two of them coming face to face, Burchett taunted the man, saying 'come over here,'" the report said. Burchett calls the protester a "weenie."

The protester stepped closer to Burchett and called him a "dodo brain," and Burchett claimed he was paid by billionaire Democratic donor George Soros.

After the protester walked away, Burchett followed and said he was "quivering." The protester spun around and replied, "I'm quivering?" In doing so, the video showed his torso bumping Burchett, who shoved him with both hands. The protester then yells, "I didn't touch him!"

Capitol police intervened and separated the two, but Burchett chose not to press charges.

The incident comes after Burchett was kicked by a horse and treated for a broken rib in July.

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Rep. Tim Burchett and the Alleged Bump

In an episode that feels more like a scene from a low-budget political satire than real life, Rep. Tim Burchett (R-TN) has found himself at the center of a rather petty controversy. This incident, involving a protester, bad breath, and a supposed bump, reads like a playbook on how not to handle public confrontations, especially for a public servant. Let's dissect the layers of this absurdity with the precision it deserves, shall we?

First off, the claim that this altercation began because Rep. Burchett was bumped into by a protester seems to crumble faster than a stale cookie under the weight of video evidence. According to Politico, footage of the incident tells a different story—one where Burchett seemingly invites the confrontation by taunting the protester with a rather juvenile "come over here" followed by an even more childish insult: calling the protester a "weenie."

The Art of Escalation
Burchett's strategy, if we can call it that, appears to hinge on escalation. From taunting to name-calling, he didn't just engage with the protester; he actively sought to belittle him. When the protester retorts with "dodo brain" (are we in the third grade?), Burchett ups the ante by accusing him of being a Soros-funded operative—a baseless and tired conspiracy trope that's been thoroughly debunked yet continues to circulate in certain circles.

The Bump Heard 'Round the World
The supposed bump seems to be more of a light brush—a mere consequence of the protester turning in response to Burchett's provocations. Yet, Burchett's reaction, a forceful shove, is portrayed by his camp as a righteous defense of personal space. It's fascinating how quickly the narrative shifts from an aggressive lawmaker to a victim of unwarranted physical contact, especially when the video evidence suggests otherwise.

The Breath Comment: A Distraction or a Desperation?
Burchett later commenting on the protester's bad breath is a peculiar cherry on top of this farcical sundae. It's as if lacking substantial ground to stand on, he resorts to personal attacks that have no relevance to the altercation or the protester's stance on the Israel-Hamas conflict. This could be seen as a desperate attempt to divert attention from his own questionable behavior.

Capitol Police: The Unenviable Referees
The involvement of Capitol police, who had to separate the two, highlights the unnecessary escalation of a situation that could have been avoided with a modicum of self-control and professionalism from Burchett. Choosing not to press charges was perhaps the only wise decision made in the entire debacle, sparing us further embarrassment.

Conclusion: A Lesson in What Not to Do
This incident, while seemingly trivial, underscores a larger issue of decorum and responsibility in public office. Rep. Burchett's actions, from taunting to physical confrontation and subsequent trivializations of the event, paint a picture of a lawmaker who momentarily forgot the dignity his office demands. In a time when political tensions are high, and civility seems to be at an all-time low, one would hope for better from their representatives.

In essence, the Burchett vs. Protester episode should serve as a cautionary tale for public figures: the world is always watching, and now, more than ever, it's crucial to act with a level of respect and restraint befitting the office. Otherwise, you might just end up as the subject of a scathing critique, or worse, a viral internet meme.
