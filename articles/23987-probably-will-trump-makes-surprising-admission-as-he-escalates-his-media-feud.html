‘Probably will’: Trump makes surprising admission as he escalates his media feud

Based on an article by Erik De La Garza

The White House Calls Exclusion “National Security”

Power Sits Behind the Door

President Donald Trump barred CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House, according to CNN’s reporting. The three outlets sued, alleging violations of their First Amendment and due process rights. Trump said he would “probably” readmit them if the judge ruled against him, while defending the exclusion as both a response to “fake news” and a matter of national security.

The President Made the Decision

This is not a dispute among equally powerful combatants. The White House controls access to its premises and officials. News organizations can challenge that power in court, but they cannot issue their own credentials or compel the administration to answer questions.

Trump’s language identifies the operative standard: he believes the government has “a right to clean out fake news.” In the account provided, “fake” is not tied to a specified error, security breach, or misconduct by the barred organizations. It functions as a presidential label attached to disfavored press.

“National Security” Without a Stated Threat

Trump invoked national security, but the reporting supplies no description of classified information being exposed, restricted areas being compromised, or any other concrete security concern involving these outlets. That does not prove the rationale is fabricated. It does show that, in this account, an extraordinary justification appears without supporting particulars.

The distinction matters. A government can establish neutral security rules. What it cannot legitimately do is convert official displeasure with coverage into a security category merely by naming it one. On the facts presented, the administration’s explanation leaves that central question unanswered.

The Judge Is Cast as the Offender

Trump also called the judge “not particularly fair” while indicating that he would probably comply with an adverse ruling. CNN analyst Brian Stelter noted that the judge was appointed by Trump and had sided with CNN in the 2018 Jim Acosta litigation.

That history does not predetermine the current case. It does expose the weakness of treating judicial disagreement as evidence of unfairness. The framing shifts scrutiny away from the president’s decision to exclude journalists and toward the legitimacy of the official empowered to review it.

Compliance Is Not Restraint

A president’s expectation that a court may reject his action is not institutional humility. It means the judiciary may have to impose a boundary that the executive branch declined to observe voluntarily.

Stelter framed Trump’s remarks as a realistic assessment of his legal prospects. The more politically significant point is that the administration reportedly exercised the power first and left the affected organizations to seek relief afterward. Even a successful lawsuit imposes delay, expense, and uncertainty on the excluded parties.

Retaliatory Power, Sanitized as Controversy

Calling the ban “controversial” understates the structure of the conflict. The president used control over White House access against named news organizations, defended that decision by branding news “fake,” and invoked national security without a specific threat described in the reporting.

The broader pattern is government power presented as editorial quality control: the executive decides which reporting is legitimate, exclusion becomes “cleaning out” the press, and judicial review becomes evidence that the judge is unfair. The immediate dispute concerns three outlets. The systemic error is allowing official access to become a reward for favorable treatment—and forcing courts to police retaliation after the door has already been shut.
