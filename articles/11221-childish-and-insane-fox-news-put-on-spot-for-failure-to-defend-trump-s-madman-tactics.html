'Childish and insane': Fox News put on spot for failure to defend Trump's 'madman' tactics

The president's most ardent supporters seem to be having trouble coherently defending his latest moves, according to one analyst.

Before entering office, President Donald Trump was known as someone who used a madman negotiation style in his business deals. He has seemingly taken a similar approach to conducting foreign policy during both of his administrations, which became even more evident during his recent summit with Russian President Vladimir Putin, according to Emma Vigeland, co-host of the progressive podcast "The Majority Report."

Vigeland noted in a clip posted on Sunday that Trump's favorite news network, Fox News, has struggled to defend Trump's behavior during the summit, which some experts have described as capitulation.

"They spin it as how he's the ultimate dealmaker...It's just like these pop negotiation business tactics that you learn on TikTok or something like that," Vigeland said about Trump's negotiations with Putin over the Ukraine war. "I mean, it's just so childish and insane that he thinks he's going to achieve anything this way."

Vigeland added that Trump's negotiation style may have a different impact if he were educated about the issues he's negotiating.

"This is the problem: there is something to his madman theory to a degree," Vigeland said. "You never know what he's going to say next, and perhaps that could create some change. But it's insane because he doesn't know what he's even talking about, and he's so deteriorated."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Negotiation Style: A Blend of Madness and Ignorance

In a recent analysis that strikes at the heart of the tumultuous political landscape surrounding President Donald Trump's foreign policy antics, Emma Vigeland of "The Majority Report" podcast sheds light on the chaotic strategy Trump appears to have employed, particularly in his interactions with Russian President Vladimir Putin. Vigeland's insights provide a critical lens through which to view the precariousness of Trump's negotiation tactics, which, while not new to his repertoire, have seemingly reached a zenith of controversy and ineffectiveness.

Madman Theory or Mere Madness?

Trump's negotiation style, often described as akin to a 'madman theory,' ostensibly serves to keep opponents off-balance, unsure of his next move. This approach, theoretically, could leverage uncertainty to the negotiator's advantage. However, as Vigeland astutely points out, this presupposes a level of strategic acumen and issue familiarity that Trump conspicuously lacks. The 'madman' approach, when wielded by someone with a profound understanding of the geopolitical chessboard, might indeed be a tool of unpredictability that could unnerve adversaries and extract concessions. Yet, in Trump's hands, it degenerates into a farcical display of ignorance and bravado, devoid of coherence or depth.

Fox News: The Spin Machine at a Loss

It's particularly telling that Fox News, described by Vigeland as Trump's "favorite news network," struggles to mount a defense of his summit behavior. This network, long criticized for its unflagging loyalty to Trump, finds itself in the unenviable position of having to polish a turd. Their attempts to spin Trump's negotiation tactics as those of an "ultimate dealmaker" are laughably inadequate, betraying either a willful ignorance or a disingenuous attempt to prop up a failing narrative. The comparison to "pop negotiation business tactics" learned on TikTok not only underscores the superficiality of Trump's approach but also highlights the absurdity of expecting such tactics to yield meaningful geopolitical outcomes.

The Deterioration of Discourse and Strategy

Vigeland's observation that Trump's negotiation style might have been somewhat effective if he were actually educated on the issues at hand is a damning indictment of his presidency's foreign policy strategy. It suggests a fundamental misunderstanding of the role of a leader on the world stage, reducing complex international negotiations to the level of child's play. Trump's apparent cognitive and informational decline only exacerbates this problem, rendering his unpredictable pronouncements not as strategic maneuvers but as the confused babblings of a figure out of his depth.

Conclusion: The Emperor Has No Clues

In sum, Vigeland's critique of Trump's foreign policy approach reveals a troubling confluence of ignorance, arrogance, and ineptitude. The madman theory, in the absence of a guiding intelligence and a coherent understanding of global politics, is reduced to sheer madness. Trump's supporters, including his propaganda arm at Fox News, find themselves contorting into rhetorical pretzels trying to defend the indefensible. This is not the art of the deal; it's the art of the debacle. Trump's foreign policy, characterized by bluster and devoid of substance, is a dangerous game of roulette with the world as the stakes.
