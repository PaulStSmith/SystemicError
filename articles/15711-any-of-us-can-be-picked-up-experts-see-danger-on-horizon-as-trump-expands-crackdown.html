'Any of us can be picked up': Experts see danger on horizon as Trump expands crackdown

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 24, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Authoritarian Ambitions: A Closer LookConclusion

Protesters demonstrate ahead of a press conference, on the day of a hearing in the case related to Kilmar Abrego Garcia, the Salvadoran man who was deported without due process by the U.S. President Donald Trump administration to a prison in El Salvador, outside U.S. District Court in Greenbelt, Maryland, U.S., April 15, 2025. REUTERS/Leah Millis

U.S. citizens have been mistakenly detained in president Donald Trump's immigration crackdown, but civil rights lawyers are gearing up for a fight over expanding those operations to deliberately target American citizens.

The president has made clear he would like to rendition "homegrown" criminals to El Salvador, and Trump officials and allies have alarmed civil libertarians by suggesting that administration critics could be, as White House senior director for counterterrorism Seb Gorka said, "aiding and abetting criminals and terrorists" by standing in the way of administration priorities, reported Axios.

"They're trying to use terrorism laws to attack people for their speech and for their political activism, and that's an authoritarian effort," said Kerri Talbot, co-executive director of the Immigration Hub advocacy group.

The administration has refused to return Kilmar Abrego Garcia, who was legally living in the U.S. with a court order prohibiting his deportation back to El Salvador, and they have challenged a U.S. Supreme Court order to "facilitate" his return, arguing they did not have to "effectuate" it, and the resulting confusion could lay the groundwork to expand the deportations to include citizens.

"If they can send a noncitizen to a prison in El Salvador without due process ... why would a U.S. citizen be safer?" said Michelle Brané, former executive director of the Biden administration's Family Reunification Task Force.

Federal appeals court judge Harvie Wilkinson III wrote a scathing rebuke of the administration, saying their actions in the Abrego Garcia case were a dangerously slippery slope.

“If today the Executive claims the right to deport without due process and in disregard of court orders, what assurance will there be tomorrow that it will not deport American citizens and then disclaim responsibility to bring them home?” wrote Wilkinson, a Reagan appointee. “And what assurance shall there be that the Executive will not train its broad discretionary powers upon its political enemies?”

The White House did not respond to a request for comment, but Trump officials have argued that opposition to their policies goes against the will of voters, but Talbot sees the same dangers on the horizon as Wilkinson.

"People are realizing that this is going to impact all communities," Talbot said, "and that if one citizen can be picked up, then any of us can be picked up and put into proceedings, or labeled a terrorist, or removed to a foreign prison."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Authoritarian Ambitions: A Closer Look

In what can only be described as a disturbing escalation of the Trump administration's immigration policies, recent developments suggest a move towards not just targeting undocumented immigrants but also American citizens. The implications of such a shift are as alarming as they are unconstitutional, revealing a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism under the guise of national security.

Exploiting Terrorism Laws Against Political Dissent

The administration's rhetoric, as reported by Axios, dangerously conflates criticism of its policies with aiding and abetting terrorism. Seb Gorka's comments are particularly chilling, suggesting a willingness to use terrorism laws as weapons against political adversaries. This isn't just overreach; it's a blatant attack on the very foundations of free speech and political activism. Kerri Talbot's assessment hits the nail on the head, labeling these efforts as unequivocally authoritarian.

The Slippery Slope of Deporting Citizens

The case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a legal resident whose rights the Trump administration has blatantly disregarded, is a harbinger of the slippery slope we now find ourselves on. If the government can ignore court orders and due process for noncitizens, what's to stop them from extending such tyrannical behavior to citizens? Michelle Brané raises a critical point here: no one is safe. This isn't a matter of immigration policy; it's a fundamental threat to civil liberties.

Judge Harvie Wilkinson III's scathing rebuke of the administration's actions underscores the gravity of the situation. A Reagan appointee, Wilkinson's concern is not borne out of partisan bias but a genuine alarm at the erosion of judicial authority and due process. His rhetorical question about deporting American citizens without due process today, and potentially targeting political enemies tomorrow, is not just speculative—it's a dire warning.

Ignoring the Will of the People?

The Trump officials' defense, that opposition to their policies is anti-democratic, is laughably ironic. In what democracy does the government respond to dissent by threatening to label its citizens as terrorists and deport them? This is not governance; it's tyranny.

Talbot's warning that this could impact all communities and that any citizen could be targeted is not hyperbole. It's a realistic assessment of the trajectory we're on if these authoritarian impulses are not checked.

Conclusion

The Trump administration's efforts to expand its immigration crackdown to include American citizens under the pretext of counterterrorism is not just an overreach—it's an assault on the very principles of democracy and justice. When political dissent becomes grounds for rendition or deportation, we must ask ourselves: what kind of country are we becoming?

This isn't about immigration policy anymore; it's about protecting the fundamental rights and freedoms that define the United States. If we allow these authoritarian measures to stand, we risk losing the very essence of what makes America a beacon of freedom and democracy. It's time to sound the alarm, loud and clear.
