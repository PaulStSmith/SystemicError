Confused Trump can't keep his popes straight when asked about harsh criticism

During a filmed interview with Politico’s Dasha Burns, President Donald Trump became confused when confronted with comments made by the first American pope, Pope Leo XIV.

As part of the 45-minute interview, where the president professed, “I watch everything, yeah. I watch everything. I see a lot of things,” Trump seemed surprised the Chicago-born pope has criticized his administration for the forced roundup of immigrants by ICE on orders from the Department of Homeland Security.

Asked by Burns, ”Immigration, sir, critical to your agenda. I want to ask about what the Pope has said, which is, he has called your immigration actions inhumane. Do you take that seriously from the pope?”

“I haven’t seen that. I mean ... I mean, he ... maybe he has,” Trump stuttered. “I mean, he also didn’t like the wall. You know, they didn’t like the wall. The wall turned out to be great.”

That led Politico’s Seb Starcevic to write, “Trump was apparently referring to Pope Leo’s predecessor, Pope Francis, who slammed Trump’s U.S.-Mexico border wall as ‘not Christian.’ The Republican and the former Catholic leader — who, months before his death, also called Trump’s mass deportation plans a ‘disgrace’ — frequently clashed over immigration, with Trump branding him a ‘very political person.’”

Having confused the current pope with the one who died the day after meeting with Vice President JD Vance, Trump responded to a question of whether he would sit down with the living pontiff by talking about his controversial wall by remarking, “You know, I built a wall that, uh, stopped people from flooding into ... we ... I didn’t put up the wall. I built the wall. The wall, nobody talks about that. You know, it’s interesting, uh, two years ago, a year ago under Biden, this country was out of control; millions of people pouring through totally unchecked, totally unvetted. And now we have nobody coming in. We have them coming in only legally. They have to go through a legal process. Nobody ever talks about that anymore. They should talk about that.”

“But I haven’t heard any statements from the Pope. I’m sure he’s a lovely man. His brother’s a lovely man. You know, his brother ...” he continued.

“Yeah, you’ve spoken to him,” Burns interjected.

“Do you know his brother is serious MAGA? Lives in Florida. He’s like ...” Trump replied as he was asked again, “So will you meet with the other brother, with the Pope?”

“I already met with the brother. He came to —,” he continued as the Politico interviewer asked again, “But with the Pope, will you meet with him or speak with him?“

“Sure, I will. Why not?” Trump finally replied.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Confusion in the Oval Office: Trump’s Encounter with the Imaginary Pope Leo XIV

In an era where fact and fiction blur with alarming frequency, the recent interview with President Donald Trump conducted by Politico’s Dasha Burns stands as a testament to this troubling trend. The discussion, intended to shed light on Trump’s immigration policies, instead veered into an almost surreal exploration of confusion surrounding the Catholic Church’s leadership.

The Imaginary Pope Leo XIV: A Figment of Miscommunication

First and foremost, let’s address the elephant in the room: Pope Leo XIV does not exist. This glaring error, whether it originated from a slip of the tongue or a deeper misunderstanding, sets a concerning precedent for the accuracy of Trump’s statements and his awareness of global religious leaders.

Trump’s Immigration Policies and Papal Criticism

Trump’s immigration policies, particularly the forced roundup of immigrants by ICE, have been a point of contention since his administration’s inception. The interviewer’s reference to a non-existent Pope Leo XIV criticizing these actions might have been intended to probe Trump’s reaction to religious authority’s condemnation. Yet, Trump’s response, which conflated the imaginary criticism with that of Pope Francis, only muddles his stance further. His deflection to the success of the border wall serves as a classic maneuver to divert the conversation from the critical issue at hand—the humanitarian implications of his immigration policies.

A Wall of Confusion

Trump’s assertion that the border wall has effectively stemmed illegal immigration is a contentious claim, lacking in nuance and disregarding the complex reality of immigration dynamics. His statement, “nobody talks about that anymore,” ignores the ongoing debates surrounding the wall’s effectiveness and the ethical implications of such an exclusionary infrastructure.

A Meeting with the “Other Brother”?

The interview takes a turn for the bizarre with Trump’s references to the Pope’s non-existent MAGA-supporting brother in Florida. This claim, aside from being factually incorrect (Pope Francis does not have a brother living in Florida, much less a MAGA-supporting one), underscores a concerning detachment from reality. It reflects a broader pattern of misinformation that has characterized much of Trump’s rhetoric.

Conclusion: A Reflection on Leadership and Accountability

This interview should have been an opportunity for Trump to address significant criticisms of his immigration policy directly. Instead, it devolved into a bewildering mix of inaccuracies and deflections. The mention of an imaginary pope and a fictitious MAGA-supporting brother in Florida are not just simple gaffes; they are indicative of a deeper issue—a lack of accountability and a disregard for the truth.

The role of a leader, especially one with as much influence as the President of the United States, includes the responsibility to be informed, accurate, and transparent. This interview, unfortunately, was a missed opportunity to demonstrate these qualities. As citizens and global observers, we must demand better. We must hold our leaders to a higher standard of truth, for the sake of our shared reality and the integrity of our democratic institutions.
