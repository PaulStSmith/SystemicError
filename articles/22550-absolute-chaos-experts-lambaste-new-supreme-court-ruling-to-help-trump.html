'Absolute chaos': Experts lambaste new Supreme Court ruling to help Trump

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Supreme Court Is Helping Build a Smaller Electorate

What Happened

A Republican-appointed Supreme Court majority has again stepped in to help Donald Trump interfere with the machinery of voting. This time, the Court stayed an injunction and let his administration keep moving on a plan that would restrict mail-in voting ahead of the midterms, even as lower courts continue to examine whether the order is lawful.

Who Has Power

The power here is not abstract. It sits with Trump, who issued the executive order; with the Supreme Court’s Republican majority, which keeps clearing procedural space for him; and with federal agencies being pulled into election administration they do not own. The source is clear on the basic constitutional fact the Court is trying to blur: states run and regulate elections, not the president.

The Real Operation

The executive order is not about "integrity." It directs the Postal Service to help decide which voters should receive mail ballots, and it sends the Department of Homeland Security hunting for lists of U.S. citizens to be used in monitoring voter rolls for supposed noncitizens. That is not neutral administration. It is federal interference dressed up as fraud prevention, with bureaucracy used as a weapon against access.

The Blame Shift

The familiar trick is to make the target look like the problem. Trump has spent years blaming mail voting for his 2020 loss, despite voting by mail himself, and the right’s media and legal apparatus keep recycling the same false fraud panic. Meanwhile, the people actually carrying the burden are election workers, USPS employees, and voters who depend on mail ballots to participate at all. The attack is designed to create confusion, delay, and doubt, then blame ordinary public servants when the system strains.

The Court’s Dodge

The Court did not even need to bless the merits to do damage. It used the shadow docket to create room for the administration’s plan while claiming not to decide the underlying legality. That is not restraint. It is procedural cover for political sabotage. When a court keeps helping the same political actor after ruling after ruling, the institution is not merely interpreting law. It is enabling power.

The Larger Pattern

This is the governing logic of modern authoritarian politics: don’t just win elections, make voting harder, more confusing, and more humiliating for the people likely to oppose you. If the administration is willing to chill turnout through mail restrictions, and if the broader project includes using enforcement agencies like ICE to terrify voters, then the democratic response cannot be timid tinkering. ICE should be abolished, not repurposed or "reformed" into a cleaner instrument of intimidation.

The real story is not that election rules are complicated. It is that powerful actors are trying to decide who gets to vote before the votes are even counted.
