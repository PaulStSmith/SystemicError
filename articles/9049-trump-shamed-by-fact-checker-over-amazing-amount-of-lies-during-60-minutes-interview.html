Trump shamed by fact checker over amazing amount of lies during '60 Minutes' interview

President Donald Trump fired off a whopping 18 false claims during his new “60 Minutes” interview, according to a fact check.

The president told his favorite lie that the 2020 election was “stolen” from him and falsely declared that grocery prices “are down,” even after CBS’ Norah O’Donnell corrected him. He insisted there was “no inflation” and tossed out a litany of bogus statistics, reported CNN’s Daniel Dale.

“Grocery prices are up under Trump — and while there has been a particularly large increase in the price of beef, there have also been increases in the price of numerous other products,” Dale wrote. “Consumer Price Index figures for September showed average grocery prices had increased since August (about 0.3 percent), since September 2024 (about 2.7 percent), and since January 2025 (about 1.4 percent), the month Trump returned to office. Prices in all six major grocery product categories tracked by the Consumer Price Index, such as ‘fruits and vegetables’ and ‘cereals and bakery products,’ are up over the past year.”

Dale dispatched with Trump’s false claim that “we don’t have any inflation,” although he later conceded “we’re down to 2 percent, even less than 2 percent,” which is also inaccurate.

“There is inflation; it has been worsening since May after hitting a four-year low in April; and it’s not 2 percent or less than 2 percent,” Dale wrote. “The year-over-year rate was about 3 percent in September, up from about 2.9 percent in August; the September figure was nearly identical to the roughly 3 percent rate in January, the last partial month of the Biden administration and first partial month of the second Trump administration.”

Trump boasted “more than $17 trillion was being invested in the U.S., which Dale said was an exaggeration of already fuzzy White House math.

“As of the time the ‘60 Minutes’ interview aired on Sunday, the White House’s own website said the ‘major investment announcements’ this term totaled ‘$8.9 trillion’ — and a detailed CNN review in October found the White House was counting trillions of dollars in vague investment pledges from foreign countries and companies, pledges that were about ‘bilateral trade’ or ‘economic exchange’ rather than investment in the US, or vague statements that didn’t even rise to the level of pledges,” Dale said.

He then claimed that China and other foreign countries pay the tariffs he’s imposed on their experts, but U.S. importers pay those costs and then pass some of those on to consumers, as study after study have shown.

“Trump made a variety of additional false claims on several subjects, including the government shutdown, the artificial intelligence boom, tariffs, his first impeachment and his former legal battle with ‘60 Minutes’ itself,” Dale wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Tornado of Falsehoods: A Closer Look

In a recent “60 Minutes” interview, President Donald Trump unleashed a barrage of misinformation, tallying up to 18 false claims. This isn’t just a matter of political spin; it’s a wholesale manufacture of an alternative reality. Let’s dissect some of these claims with the precision of a fact-checker and the scorn they deserve.

The Election Lie: A Favorite Fiction

Trump’s assertion that the 2020 election was “stolen” from him is not just a lie; it’s his favorite lie. It’s a claim so baseless and so thoroughly debunked that its continuous repetition borders on pathological. Yet, Trump persists, perhaps believing that if a lie is repeated enough, it might just warp reality to fit its contours.

Grocery Prices and The Inflation Illusion

Trump’s claim that grocery prices are down is not just false; it’s laughably so. Anyone who’s been to a grocery store knows this. The assertion flies in the face of Consumer Price Index figures, which show an increase in grocery prices across the board. Daniel Dale points out that prices have risen for all six major grocery product categories. In what universe does an increase in price translate to a decrease? Only in Trump’s, it seems.

Moreover, Trump’s denial of inflation is a bold attempt to deny the lived experience of millions of Americans. Dale’s correction that inflation exists and is, in fact, worsening is a necessary dose of reality. Trump’s later admission that we’re “down to 2 percent, even less than 2 percent” on inflation rates is another dive into falsehood, as the year-over-year rate was about 3 percent in September.

The $17 Trillion Exaggeration

Trump’s boast of more than $17 trillion being invested in the U.S. is a spectacular inflation of facts. Even the White House’s own figures don’t support this claim, listing ‘major investment announcements’ this term at ‘$8.9 trillion’. Dale’s investigation reveals that this figure includes vague pledges rather than concrete investments. This isn’t just fuzzy math; it’s fantasy.

Tariffs: The Misunderstood Tax

The claim that China and other foreign countries pay the tariffs he’s imposed couldn’t be further from the truth. It’s a fundamental misunderstanding (or misrepresentation) of how tariffs work. As study after study has shown, U.S. importers bear the cost of tariffs, and these costs are often passed on to consumers. It’s Economics 101, yet Trump seems to have skipped that class.

A Cornucopia of Falsehoods

Beyond these headliners, Trump made false claims on a variety of subjects, including the government shutdown, the artificial intelligence boom, tariffs, his first impeachment, and even his former legal battle with “60 Minutes” itself. This scattershot of misinformation isn’t just reckless; it’s a deliberate attempt to muddy the waters of public discourse.

Conclusion: The Truth Still Matters

In an era where misinformation can spread faster than fact-checking, Trump’s “60 Minutes” interview serves as a stark reminder of the power of falsehoods. But it also underscores the importance of diligent, fact-based journalism. Daniel Dale’s thorough fact-checking isn’t just commendable; it’s essential. In the battle for truth, facts are our most potent weapon. Let’s wield them wisely.
