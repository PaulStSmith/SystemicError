'Chainsaw-wielding': Insiders reveal secret DOGE team lurks behind Trump duds

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

DOGE Did Not Die. It Got a Cleaner Logo.

Power Kept the Frame

The real story here is not “young technologists” or a “design studio.” It is that Trump’s White House still has the power to hide an ideological project inside a harmless-sounding bureaucracy. The National Design Studio may look like a branding shop, but according to the reporting it functions as a continuation of DOGE’s core mission: centralize control, dress it up as efficiency, and keep the public busy staring at the packaging.

That is not neutral administrative tinkering. It is political authorship. Trump’s administration gave the outfit legitimacy. Musk’s allies gave it the governing philosophy. Joe Gebbia gave it a softer vocabulary. The public gets the invoice.

The Aesthetic Is the Cover

The point of shifting from a “chainsaw-wielding approach” to “design” is not restraint. It is camouflage. When a project keeps the same mandate while changing the branding, the change is not substantive. It is rhetorical. The administration is swapping open vandalism for polished vandalism.

That matters because aesthetics can launder power. “Brand initiatives,” “government websites,” and “food pyramid” sound low-stakes until you look at what sits underneath: procurement influence, data exposure, disability access failures, and a federal government increasingly run by people who think governance is a UI problem.

Failure Is Doing the Work

The TrumpRx example gives the game away. It was sold as a cost-cutting tool and then exposed as offering fewer than 12 percent of the more than 800 brand-name drugs made by participating companies. That is not a victory with implementation issues. That is a promise reduced to theater.

The same pattern appears in the retirement portal redesign and in warnings that sleek federal sites could create security holes and violate disability law. The administration is not merely moving fast and breaking things. It is building things badly, then calling the wreckage innovation. The failures are not accidental side effects. They are part of the method.

Quiet Capture, Not Clean Reform

The reporting also shows how this machine survives pressure: it retreats into opacity. DOGE allies remain “quietly operating behind the scenes,” anonymous staffers say, because retaliation is the operating environment. Thomas Shedd reportedly still sits inside GSA in a special-government-employee limbo, even when he no longer appears to have an official job.

That is institutional capture wearing a contractor’s badge. The people responsible for the project can blur their status, obscure their authority, and keep moving while everyone else is left guessing who actually approved what. That is how unaccountable government survives: not by announcing itself, but by dissolving into job titles, task forces, and special statuses.

The Real Misdirection

The weakest framing in stories like this is the idea that the problem is a few overconfident technologists or an overambitious design team. That misses the structure. The structure is a political coalition that wants public institutions to serve private power while appearing modern and efficient. The names change. The tactic does not.

The harm is deliberate, even when it is packaged as confusion. The administration gets to claim modernization while exposing Americans to shoddy service, possible security failures, and bureaucratic opacity. Musk’s methods may have been rebranded, but the underlying agenda remains intact: concentrate authority, weaken scrutiny, and sell the damage as progress.

The Pattern

This is what oligarchic governance looks like in its cleaned-up phase. First comes the brutal version: layoffs, threats, and chainsaws. Then comes the refined version: studios, aesthetics, and “brand initiatives.” The second is not an improvement. It is the same project with better lighting.

Trump’s operation does not need to scream authoritarianism when it can subcontract it into a design office. That is the lesson here. The danger is not only the wrecking crew. It is the people who arrive afterward with a nicer font and ask the public to mistake disguise for reform.
