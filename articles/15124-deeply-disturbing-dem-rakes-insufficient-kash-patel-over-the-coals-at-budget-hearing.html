'Deeply disturbing': Dem rakes 'insufficient' Kash Patel over the coals at budget hearing

Senate Appropriations Committee/screen grab

Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA) grilled FBI Director Kash Patel after his agency purportedly violated the law by failing to provide a timely budget.

"As ranking member Van Hollen noted earlier, this hearing is being held without the FBI's fiscal year 2025 spend plan and a full budget request for fiscal year 2026," Murray told Patel at a Thursday hearing. "The spend plan is required by law. It was due to Congress over a week ago. We have not yet seen it. That is really absurd."

"So, Director Patel, when should we expect this FY 2025 spend plan for the FBI?" she asked.

"I'll get you an answer, ma'am," Patel stated. "I don't have a timeline on that."

"It was due last week by law," the senator noted.

"I understand," the FBI director replied.

"And your answer is you just understand," Murray observed. "You're not going to follow the law?"

The Democrat also pressed Patel on providing a full budget to Congress, "not a single paragraph full of wild talking points that we saw with the skinny budget proposal."

"It's being worked on, ma'am," Patel insisted.

"When will we get it?" Murray wondered. "Six months from now?"

"I don't know, ma'am," Patel stated. "I'm not going to make up the timeline."

"Well, how do we as Congress do our budget and our work without that request and without the spend plan?" Murray said.

"I'm doing the best I can," Patel opined.

"That is insufficient and deeply disturbing," Murray asserted. "No response?"

"I've given my response," Patel remarked.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: The FBI's Budget Blunder and Bureaucratic Ineptitude

In what appears to be a flagrant disregard for legal obligations and procedural norms, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), under the leadership of Director Kash Patel, has stumbled into a contentious confrontation with Congress, specifically with Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA). The crux of the issue? A failure to submit a timely budget for fiscal years 2025 and 2026, as mandated by law. This incident, as reported, not only highlights a troubling lapse in administrative responsibility but also underscores a deeper malaise within federal operations.

The Law Is Not a Suggestion

Sen. Murray's frustration during the Senate Appropriations Committee hearing is not just warranted; it's emblematic of a larger, systemic problem. The requirement for the FBI to submit a spend plan "over a week ago" is not a mere formality—it is a legal requirement, a critical component of congressional oversight, and a foundational aspect of transparent governance. Director Patel's responses, ranging from "I'll get you an answer, ma'am" to "I'm doing the best I can," are not just inadequate; they are insults to the legislative process itself.

A Budget in Limbo

The absence of a detailed budget and spend plan for the FBI does not exist in a vacuum. It directly impacts Congress's ability to perform its constitutional duties—budgeting and oversight. Murray's pointed critique, highlighting the receipt of "a single paragraph full of wild talking points" instead of a comprehensive budget, cuts to the heart of the issue. It's not just about numbers on a page; it's about accountability, transparency, and the capacity to plan and allocate resources effectively.

The Response (Or Lack Thereof)

Patel's noncommittal responses and apparent lack of urgency are troubling. His statement, "I'm not going to make up the timeline," while perhaps intended to avoid overpromising, instead comes off as dismissive of the gravity of the situation. The law does not afford the luxury of indefinite delays, and Patel's demeanor suggests a worrying detachment from the responsibilities of his office.

Conclusion: A Symptom of a Larger Disease

This episode is more than a bureaucratic slip-up; it's a symptom of a larger disease afflicting government agencies—a disease characterized by a lack of respect for legal mandates, a cavalier attitude towards accountability, and a disturbing comfort with opacity. Sen. Murray’s dogged pursuit of answers and her insistence on compliance with the law are commendable but the fact that such insistence is necessary is a damning indictment of the current state of affairs.

What we are witnessing is not just an administrative oversight; it is a bold illustration of systemic error, where the basic principles of governance are sidelined in favor of bureaucratic inertia. The FBI’s budget debacle should serve as a wake-up call, a rallying cry for a return to principled, law-abiding administration. Anything less is not just absurd; it's unacceptable.
