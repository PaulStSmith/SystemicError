Trump doubles down on revoking Hollywood star's citizenship: 'Not a great American!'

By Paulo Santos

Donald Trump (Photo by Kevin Lamarque for Reuters)

President Donald Trump on Wednesday doubled down on his threats to revoke the citizenship of a Hollywood star with whom he has feuded for several years.

Trump posted on Truth Social that his administration is considering revoking Rosie O’Donnell’s citizenship. O'Donnell and Trump have feuded since at least 2006, when O'Donnell criticized Trump on a daytime talk show, according to People.

O'Donnell has lived in Ireland since early 2025.

"As previously mentioned, we are giving serious thought to taking away Rosie O’Donnell’s Citizenship," Trump wrote. "She is not a Great American and is, in my opinion, incapable of being so!"

He threatened O'Donnell in July as well, writing at the time, “Because of the fact that Rosie O’Donnell is not in the best interests of our Great Country, I am giving serious consideration to taking away her Citizenship. She is a Threat to Humanity, and should remain in the wonderful Country of Ireland, if they want her. GOD BLESS AMERICA!”

Our Analysis:

The Trump-O'Donnell Feud: A New Chapter in Political Vendettas

In what appears to be a never-ending saga of personal vendettas masquerading as political discourse, President Donald Trump's recent declaration to revoke Rosie O'Donnell's citizenship takes the cake for pettiness cloaked in patriotism. The move, as outlined in Trump's Truth Social post, is not only unprecedented but also deeply troubling in its implications for freedom of speech and the right to dissent.

A Feud Fueled by Personal Disdain

The feud between Trump and O'Donnell, which dates back to at least 2006, has been nothing short of a public spectacle. However, the escalation to threats of revoking citizenship marks a dark turn in this drama. Let's be clear: O'Donnell's primary "crime" seems to be her vocal criticism of Trump, a right enshrined in the very fabric of American democracy. The irony of Trump's statement, "She is not a Great American and is, in my opinion, incapable of being so!" is palpable. Since when did dissenting opinions disqualify one from being considered a "Great American"?

The Threat to Democracy

Trump's rhetoric is not just an attack on O'Donnell; it's a blatant threat to the principles of democracy. Revoking citizenship as punishment for criticism is a tactic more aligned with authoritarian regimes than a country that prides itself on freedom of speech and expression. The fact that O'Donnell has resided in Ireland since early 2025 does nothing to diminish the rights she holds as an American citizen, nor should it make her a target for state-sanctioned retribution.

The Misuse of Presidential Power

"Because of the fact that Rosie O’Donnell is not in the best interests of our Great Country, I am giving serious consideration to taking away her Citizenship. She is a Threat to Humanity, and should remain in the wonderful Country of Ireland, if they want her. GOD BLESS AMERICA!” Trump's words are more than just hyperbolic; they're a misuse of presidential power. Labeling a private citizen as a "Threat to Humanity" for their political views and threatening their citizenship crosses a line from political rivalry into tyranny.

Conclusion: A Dangerous Precedent

Trump's threat to revoke Rosie O'Donnell's citizenship is not only unjust, it sets a dangerous precedent. If the administration can even consider such an action against a high-profile critic based on personal animosity, what stops them from using similar tactics against any citizen who dares to dissent? This move is a stark reminder of the fragility of our democratic norms under leaders who prioritize personal vendettas over constitutional rights.

In a democracy, criticism of political leaders should not only be expected but encouraged. It is a sign of a healthy, vibrant political system. The day we allow our leaders to punish dissent with threats to our very citizenship is the day we lose the essence of what makes America truly great. Trump's actions and rhetoric should alarm anyone who values their fundamental rights and the principles upon which the United States was founded.
