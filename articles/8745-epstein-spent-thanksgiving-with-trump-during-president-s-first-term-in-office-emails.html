President Donald Trump’s relationship with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein continued into his first term in the White House, so much so that Epstein spent Thanksgiving with Trump while the president was in office, according to newly released emails written by the late financier.

Democrats on the House Oversight Committee released a new batch of emails Wednesday obtained from Epstein’s estate, followed by the Republican-led panel’s release of 20,000 additional documents, and one of those exchanges shows that he claims to have spent Thanksgiving 2017 – Trump’s first as president – at his private Mar-a-Lago resort.

Epstein exchanged emails on Thanksgiving morning, Nov. 23, 2017, with Manhattan modeling management guru Faith Kates, who asked where he was spending the holiday.

The disgraced financier told her he would spend the day with “eva,” likely a reference to his former girlfriend Eva Andersson-Dubin, and Kates mentions Andersson-Dubin’s husband, Glenn Dubin, by name and asks “who else is down there?”

Epstein names Trump, hedge fund founder David Fiszel and someone else he referred to as Hanson.

White House records and contemporaneous media reports show Trump spent Thanksgiving 2017 at Mar-a-Lago, but the White House did not disclose a guest list.

Andersson-Dubin later testified at the 2021 trial of Ghislaine Maxwell, who was convicted for her role as a co-conspirator in Epstein’s sex trafficking network after he was found dead in prison while awaiting new charges two years earlier on similar allegations.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Thanksgiving with Epstein: A Bitter Feast of Controversy

In what reads like a script torn from the darkest corners of political satire, new revelations have surfaced that President Donald Trump, during his first term in the White House, not only maintained but seemingly flourished in his relationship with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. According to recently released emails from the late financier, this dubious friendship extended to sharing Thanksgiving dinner at Trump’s private Mar-a-Lago resort in 2017.

Democrats on the House Oversight Committee have thrown the spotlight on this association by releasing a batch of emails obtained from Epstein’s estate, a move promptly countered by the Republican-led panel’s release of 20,000 additional documents. However, it’s one particular exchange that cuts through the political noise: Epstein’s claim of spending Thanksgiving 2017 with Trump.

The email in question, exchanged on the morning of November 23, 2017, with Manhattan modeling management guru Faith Kates, is damning in its casual drop of high-profile names. Epstein reveals his plans to spend the day with “eva”, likely referring to his former girlfriend Eva Andersson-Dubin, and mentions the presence of Trump, hedge fund founder David Fiszel, and a mysterious figure known only as Hanson.

The evidence stacks up uncomfortably. White House records and contemporaneous media reports confirm Trump’s presence at Mar-a-Lago during that Thanksgiving, although, predictably, a guest list was never disclosed. This lack of transparency, a hallmark of Trump’s administration, only fuels speculation and concern.

What’s truly chilling is the context. Andersson-Dubin later testified at the 2021 trial of Ghislaine Maxwell, who was convicted for her role as a co-conspirator in Epstein’s sex trafficking network. Epstein, meanwhile, met a notorious end, found dead in prison while awaiting trial on similar charges.

Unpacking the Implications

This revelation is not just a mere blip in the relentless news cycle; it’s a glaring indictment of the moral and ethical bankruptcy that seemed to permeate Trump’s presidency. The fact that a convicted sex offender could not only boast of his close ties with the President of the United States but also claim to share in the intimacy of a holiday gathering is appalling.

The Silence is Deafening

The silence from Trump’s camp in the wake of these revelations speaks volumes. The absence of a guest list, the lack of denial or explanation, and the general opacity surrounding Trump’s interactions with Epstein are indicative of a broader issue: a disturbing disregard for accountability and transparency.

A Call to Action

This incident should not be brushed aside as yet another controversy in a presidency riddled with them. It demands a thorough investigation, not just for the sake of political accountability, but to uphold the basic standards of decency we expect from our leaders.

In a climate where partisan politics often dictates the narrative, it’s crucial that we, as a society, demand better from those in power. The association between Trump and Epstein, underscored by their shared Thanksgiving, is not merely a scandal; it’s a stark reminder of the systemic rot that can pervade the highest levels of government.

As this story unfolds, one thing is clear: the implications of Trump’s relationship with Epstein are as profound as they are disturbing. It’s a festering wound on the presidency, a stark illustration of how power can be abused and morality sidelined in the corridors of power. This isn’t just about politics; it’s about the fundamental values that underpin our society.
