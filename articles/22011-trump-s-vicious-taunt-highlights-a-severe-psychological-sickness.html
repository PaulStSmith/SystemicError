Trump's vicious taunt highlights a severe psychological sickness

Based on an article by Ray Richmond

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

Humiliation Is the GOP’s Actual Message

The Target Was the Point

The source is simple on the facts that matter: Joe Biden’s cancer had spread, his son described the pain, and Donald Trump chose that moment to post a mocking golf photo on Truth Social with a snide handicap line. That is the event. Everything else is ornament.

The political meaning is not complicated either. Trump did not stumble into cruelty. He selected a vulnerable target, timed the attack to land while the target was publicly suffering, and used humiliation as the payload. That is not bad manners. It is a deliberate display of dominance.

Power Does the Mocking

The key question is not whether Trump is personally grotesque. The key question is who gives grotesquerie political utility. Trump does not operate as an isolated insult machine. He operates inside a Republican ecosystem that keeps rewarding him for treating weakness as a cue to strike.

The source names that plainly: Republican members of Congress do not merely tolerate this behavior; they enable and applaud it. That is institutional power at work. When the party withholds consequences and offers cover, it turns cruelty into a credential. Trump supplies the sadism. The party supplies legitimacy.

This Is Not a Mistake

The article’s framing is strongest when it refuses the usual sanitizing language. This was not a misunderstanding, not a joke that went too far, not some accidental lapse in judgment. Trump knew exactly what he was doing. He has made a practice of turning another person’s pain into a punchline.

That matters because the “just Trump being Trump” defense is itself a political operation. It tries to turn repeated abuse into personality, then turn personality into inevitability. If cruelty is just his brand, then nobody has to answer for the incentives that keep elevating it. That is misdirection. It shifts responsibility away from the people who benefit from the performance and onto the performance itself.

Humiliation Is the Product

The source gets closest to the deeper pattern when it notes Trump’s obsession with status reversal: the respected person made pathetic, the powerful person brought low, the critic crushed, the rival degraded. That is not random vulgarity. It is a method.

Humiliation is useful because it reorganizes the room. It tells supporters what kind of authority they are meant to admire: not competence, not stewardship, not even self-command, but the ability to hurt someone weaker and call it strength. Once that standard is normalized, politics becomes a contest in licensed degradation. The cruelty is the proof of belonging.

The Framing Is Too Small

The article is right to condemn the act, but it still circles Trump’s psychology as if the real puzzle were what kind of damaged man could behave this way. That question is secondary. The larger issue is how a major political faction made room for this behavior, defended it, and trained its voters to treat it as ordinary.

This is the part that matters for democracy. A democratic coalition cannot afford to treat open sadism as a personality quirk. It is an organizing principle for authoritarian politics: select an enemy, strip away pity, and invite the audience to laugh at the wound. That is not drift. It is discipline.

The Pattern Beneath the Story

The story is not really about a golf photo or a nasty caption. It is about a political movement that treats empathy as weakness and cruelty as proof of strength. It is about a party apparatus that keeps granting institutional shelter to a man who turns another person’s illness into entertainment.

That is the system, not the exception. And once cruelty becomes routine, the next escalation is always easier to sell.
