Nick Fuentes, a far-right influencer who’s praised Adolf Hitler, called for a “holy war” against Jews and championed a “whites-only immigration policy” is well poised to take up the mantle of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk after his assasination in September, New York Times columnist Michelle Goldberg wrote Tuesday.

Fuentes has long been a staunch critic of Kirk, particularly over his support of Israel. Goldberg called the two ‘bitter enemies.”

And now, in the absence of the wildly influential Kirk, a growing number of Americans appear open to turning to Fuentes as a new thought leader for the right.

“His sneering, proudly transgressive attitude has made him a hero to legions of mostly young men who resent all forms of political gatekeeping,” Goldberg wrote.

In 2020, major social media platforms began banning and restricting Fuentes, a crackdown on right-wing extremism that accelerated in 2021 in the wake of the Jan. 6 Capitol riot. After Tesla CEO Elon Musk purchased Twitter in 2022, however, Fuentes was reinstated on the platform, a decision that jumpstarted his return to the mainstream.

Fuentes would go on to assist musician Ye – formally known as Kanye West – with his 2024 presidential campaign amid the artist’s series of antisemitic statements, including lavishing praise on Hitler. Fuentes and Ye would dine with President Dondal Trump amid the controversy; in 2023, Fuentes was hosted by a major Texas conservative political action committee; and last week, Fuentes was featured on conservative commentator Tucker Carlson’s show.

It’s in the wake of Kirk’s killing, and prominent conservatives’ reaction to it, Goldberg argued, that have set the stage for Fuentes to continue to ascend the right-wing ecosystem.

“Kirk, who came of age in the pre-Trump conservative movement, was still sometimes willing to police boundaries. But in the wake of his killing, there’s surprisingly little sense on the right that that part of his legacy should be upheld,” Goldberg wrote.

Our Analysis:

The Rise of Nick Fuentes: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

In a political landscape already fraught with divisiveness and extremism, the ascendance of Nick Fuentes, as chronicled by Michelle Goldberg in a recent New York Times column, is not just a cause for alarm but a glaring symptom of the systemic failures plaguing American political discourse. Fuentes, a far-right influencer known for his abhorrent views, including praising Adolf Hitler, advocating for a “whites-only immigration policy,” and calling for a “holy war” against Jews, is reportedly poised to fill the void left by the assassination of Charlie Kirk. This transition marks a disturbing shift towards mainstreaming what was once considered fringe extremism.

The Unlikely Ascension

Kirk, despite his own controversial conservatism, at least seemed to possess a modicum of restraint when it came to policing the boundaries of acceptable political discourse. His death, however, has seemingly opened the floodgates, with Goldberg noting a lack of interest on the right in upholding any semblance of Kirk’s legacy of boundary-setting. Instead, what we’re witnessing is a perverse rallying cry, epitomized by podcaster Matt Walsh’s declaration of unity with anyone on the right, irrespective of their views, in the wake of Kirk’s murder. This sentiment, while perhaps understandable in the context of grief and outrage, is recklessly shortsighted, serving only to embolden the likes of Fuentes.

The Role of Social Media and the Complicity of the Right

The trajectory of Fuentes’ rise is inextricably linked to the role of social media in amplifying extremist voices. His resurgence, particularly after being banned from major platforms for his extremist views, was significantly bolstered by Elon Musk’s decision to reinstate him on Twitter. This move, under the guise of free speech, has facilitated Fuentes’ return to the mainstream, granting him a platform that he has used to further entrench his hateful ideologies, even assisting Ye (formerly Kanye West) in his presidential campaign amidst a flurry of antisemitic remarks.

Moreover, Fuentes’ engagement with prominent figures and platforms on the right, including dining with former President Donald Trump and appearing on Tucker Carlson’s show, underscores a troubling trend of normalization and acceptance of extremist views within conservative circles. The mention by conservative writer Rod Dreher of a significant portion of young GOP staffers being fans of Fuentes is particularly alarming, indicating a deep-seated rot within the party that threatens to undermine the very foundations of democracy.

The Dangers of Unchecked Extremism

The acceptance and ascension of figures like Fuentes signal a dangerous shift in American politics, where the lines between right-wing conservatism and far-right extremism become increasingly blurred. This conflation not only emboldens individuals with dangerous ideologies but also fractures the political landscape, making unity and constructive dialogue nearly impossible.

The mainstreaming of extremism, as evidenced by the rise of Fuentes, should serve as a wake-up call to all those who value democracy and the principles upon which the United States was founded. It is incumbent upon not just political leaders but also the electorate to vehemently reject the normalization of hate and bigotry in all its forms. Failure to do so not only dishonors the legacy of figures like Charlie Kirk, who, for all their flaws, understood the importance of maintaining certain boundaries, but also imperils the very fabric of our society.

In conclusion, the ascent of Nick Fuentes is a stark reminder of the systemic errors within our political system. It underscores the need for a rigorous reevaluation of the mechanisms through which we allow voices to be amplified and the standards to which we hold our political figures. If unchecked, this trend towards extremism threatens to erode the foundational principles of democracy and plunge the nation into an abyss of division and hate from which recovery may be impossible.
