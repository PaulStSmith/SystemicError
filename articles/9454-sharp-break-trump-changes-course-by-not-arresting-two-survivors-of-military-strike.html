Donald Trump’s administration took a “sharp break” from its typical handling of military strike cases by choosing not to prosecute two survivors of an attack on a foreign suspected drug smuggling boat, according to the New York Times.

Trump over the weekend took the time to brag about the military strike, saying it was an “honor” while announcing that two were killed in the strike. The Times also released a report focusing on the two survivors, who were not arrested.

“The Trump administration has decided to repatriate two survivors of a deadly U.S. strike this week on suspected drug runners in the Caribbean Sea rather than prosecute them or hold them in military detention, people with knowledge of the matter said on Saturday,” according to the report. “The men who survived were being returned to their home countries, Colombia and Ecuador, the people with knowledge of the matter said. They spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss operational and diplomatic matters. It was not clear if the government of either nation would prosecute the men upon their return, or simply release them.”

The report continues:

“President Trump has previously described people aboard suspected drug-smuggling boats, which the United States has targeted in several deadly airstrikes since early September, as ‘unlawful combatants.’ He has claimed the authority, widely disputed by legal experts, to summarily kill such suspects in military strikes as if they were enemy soldiers in a war.”

According to the Times report, the move “was a sharp break from the traditional handling of maritime smuggling, in which the Coast Guard would intercept boats and arrest people if suspicions proved accurate.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s “Sharp Break” in Military Strike Protocol: A Closer Look

The New York Times has shed light on a controversial and, frankly, alarming approach by the Trump administration concerning the handling of military strikes against suspected drug smuggling operations. At the heart of this issue is the decision to not prosecute two survivors of such an attack, a move that deviates sharply from conventional U.S. policy. Let’s dissect the layers of this decision and its implications.

A Departure from Norms

Traditionally, when the U.S. Coast Guard intercepts boats suspected of drug smuggling, a due process is followed, involving arrest and prosecution if the suspicions are confirmed. This practice is grounded in the principles of justice and the rule of law, ensuring that those accused are given a fair chance to defend themselves. However, the Trump administration’s decision to repatriate two survivors of a deadly strike, without any form of prosecution or detention, represents a stark deviation from these principles.

The Bragging Rights of Conflict

Donald Trump’s public declaration that it was an “honor” to announce the deaths resulting from the military strike is not only macabre but also deeply troubling. It underscores a cavalier attitude towards human life and a dangerous glorification of military might over diplomatic and legal avenues. The decision to brag about such actions does not align with the values of accountability and humanity that should guide the actions of the U.S. on the global stage.

The Question of Legality and Morality

Labeling individuals on suspected drug-smuggling boats as “unlawful combatants” and asserting the authority to kill such suspects summarily in military strikes is a legal and moral quagmire. This stance, as rightfully pointed out by legal experts, is widely disputed and raises serious questions about the adherence to international law and human rights. The notion that anyone suspected of a crime can be deemed an “unlawful combatant” and targeted in such a manner without due process is a slippery slope towards lawlessness.

Operational and Diplomatic Secrecy

The secrecy surrounding the operational and diplomatic aspects of this decision, with officials speaking on the condition of anonymity, adds another layer of concern. The lack of transparency and accountability in these actions does not bode well for democratic oversight or for the U.S.’s reputation internationally. When actions are taken in the shadows, without clear justification or accountability, it erodes trust and undermines the principles upon which democratic societies are built.

The Bigger Picture

This incident is not an isolated aberration but a reflection of a broader trend of militarization and unilateral actions in international affairs under the Trump administration. Such a posture not only endangers lives but also risks escalating conflicts and diminishing the U.S.’s standing as a proponent of justice and human rights worldwide.

In conclusion, the Trump administration’s handling of this military strike and its aftermath represents a troubling departure from established norms and legal frameworks. It underscores a preference for might over rights, secrecy over transparency, and bravado over humanity. This approach not only undermines the United States’ moral authority but also poses a direct challenge to the principles of justice, accountability, and international law. As citizens and as a global community, we must demand better, advocating for policies that prioritize human rights, due process, and diplomatic engagement over unilateral military actions.
