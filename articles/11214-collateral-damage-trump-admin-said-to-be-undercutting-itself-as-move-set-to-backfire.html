'Collateral damage': Trump admin said to be 'undercutting' itself as move set to backfire

One of the several policy initiatives launched by President Donald Trump in his crackdown on illegal immigration is poised to backfire and may already be “undercutting its own objectives,” argued Politico columnist Ankush Khardori Monday morning, and set off a wave of “collateral damage.”

In his effort to make good on his campaign pledge to eradicate illegal immigration, Trump has bolstered the budget of Immigration and Customs Enforcement to $37.5 billion, instituted a daily migrant arrest quota of 3,000, and on his first day in office, signed an executive order designating drug cartels as “foreign terrorist organizations.”

And it’s that executive order, Khardori wrote in a column Monday, that could end up backfiring.

“The Trump administration’s decision could ultimately prevent the extradition of defendants to the United States who have been apprehended by European countries,” Khardori wrote. “That is because the ‘foreign terrorist organization’ designation could expose some defendants to the death penalty, and certain European countries will not extradite defendants to countries where they are eligible for the death penalty.”

Two European diplomats who spoke with Khardori on the condition of anonymity specifically named Trump’s executive order as being “on their radar,” and said that the policy had only created more “confusion” as to how to cooperate with the United States on such matters.

The European diplomats also named the behavior of top Justice Department officials – Attorney General Pam Bondi and FBI Director Kash Patel – as being particularly “counterproductive and unprofessional,” Khardori wrote.

“They worry that the standards for decorum among the nation’s top law enforcement officials have fallen, and that senior administration officials like Bondi and Patel – as well as DHS Secretary Kristi Noem – have politicized law enforcement in a way that may seriously compromise the integrity of the U.S. criminal justice system and make allied law enforcement agencies reluctant to lend support to Trump administration investigations or prosecutions they see as primarily political,” Khardori wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Immigration Crackdown: A Counterproductive Quagmire?

The irony is thick enough to cut with a knife in President Donald Trump's latest policy maneuver, a misstep so glaringly misguided it would be comedic if it weren't so tragically counterproductive. In a bid to fulfill his campaign promises of eradicating illegal immigration, Trump has not only beefed up the budget of Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to a staggering $37.5 billion but also instituted a daily migrant arrest quota of 3,000. Yet, it's his executive order designating drug cartels as "foreign terrorist organizations" that takes the cake for misguided policy decisions.

The Backfire

In an analysis that reads like a prelude to a policy disaster, Politico's Ankush Khardori highlights a glaring oversight that could very well undermine the very objectives Trump seeks to achieve. The executive order, far from being the silver bullet to stop illegal immigration, could inadvertently hamstring the U.S. in its efforts to extradite criminal defendants from Europe. The reason? A very straightforward and predictable one: the designation could expose defendants to the death penalty, a non-starter for extradition from countries where the death penalty is outlawed.

European Skepticism

Khardori's discussions with two European diplomats, albeit anonymous, shed light on the growing concern and "confusion" over how to cooperate with the U.S. on matters of extradition and criminal prosecution. The subtext here is clear: international law enforcement cooperation, a critical component of combating transnational crime, is at risk of being compromised by Trump's policy.

A Fall from Decorum

Khardori doesn't stop at policy critique. He plunges into the behavior of top Justice Department officials, namely Attorney General Pam Bondi, FBI Director Kash Patel, and DHS Secretary Kristi Noem, painting a picture of a justice department that has not only lost its way but seems to be actively undermining the principles it's supposed to uphold. The alleged politicization of law enforcement by these officials is not just an internal issue; it's causing international ripples. Our European allies are reportedly wary of engaging in collaborative efforts that appear to be driven more by political vendettas than by the pursuit of justice.

The Collateral Damage

The term "collateral damage" is aptly used here, as the ramifications of these policies and behaviors extend far beyond the immediate objectives. By potentially limiting extradition opportunities, the Trump administration is not just failing to crack down on illegal immigration and drug trafficking effectively; it's also eroding the international credibility and integrity of the U.S. criminal justice system. The irony? In attempting to appear tough on crime and illegal immigration, these actions may actually weaken the U.S.'s ability to prosecute and punish the very criminals they purport to target.

Conclusion: A Systemic Misstep

Trump's immigration policies, characterized by their aggressive stance and hefty funding, were ostensibly designed to protect the nation. However, by failing to consider the international implications of such policies, particularly in regards to cooperation with allies, the administration has not only undercut its own objectives but has also potentially compromised the effectiveness of the U.S. criminal justice system on the world stage. It's a classic case of a systemic error, where the pursuit of a hardline approach has led to outcomes diametrically opposed to those intended. The lesson? Policy decisions, especially those with international ramifications, require a nuanced understanding of global dynamics and a respect for the delicate balance of international relations. Anything less is not just ineffective; it's recklessly counterproductive.
