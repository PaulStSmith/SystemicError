Cursing Trump calls for war crime tribunals: 'They must be tried!'

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20262 min read

Trump’s Tribunal Theater, and the Politics of Invented Certainty

Tribunals as Theater

Donald Trump is not making a principled human-rights argument here. He is using the language of war crimes as a weapon of state power, even as he tosses out evidence-free claims to inflate the case. The source gives the essential context: Iran has carried out mass repression during unrest in 2026, but Trump’s claim that “as many as 100,000” protesters were killed is unsupported. That gap is not a footnote. It is the point.

The Number Is the Misdirection

The scale matters because the number is doing political work. A real atrocity does not need a fabricated supersize version to be horrifying. But Trump reaches for one anyway, because exaggeration turns condemnation into spectacle and spectacle into authority. He is not clarifying the violence in Iran; he is trying to own the narrative with a louder lie.

Power Is Not Confused

The decision-maker in this story is Trump, and the institutional power is American presidential power amplified through social media. He is not a detached observer. He is a political actor with a megaphone, turning unverified claims into an instrument of foreign-policy posturing. The article should not let that sound like mere bluster. It is a deliberate attempt to define reality first and evidence later.

Iran’s Repression, and the Convenient Silence Around Interference

The reporting also says Iran executed what is believed to be as many as 30,000 protesters amid civil unrest, and that the unrest began in January during an economic crisis. That is the actual crime scene: state violence against dissent. But the source also notes Iran’s accusation that Israel stoked dissent inside its borders, with Israel’s Mossad admitting agents were among protesters “in the field,” and figures like Amichai Eliyahu and Mike Pompeo acknowledging it as well. That does not erase Iran’s repression. It does expose the hypocrisy of selective outrage. The same U.S. political class that treats foreign destabilization as acceptable leverage suddenly discovers moral language only when it can be aimed at an enemy.

The Real Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics operates across borders: repression at home, interference abroad, and a permanent campaign to turn violence into a story that serves power. Trump’s call for tribunals is not democratic accountability. It is a display of punitive imperial posture built on inflated claims and strategic amnesia. The deeper pattern is not just that leaders lie. It is that they choose which suffering counts, which facts get scaled up, and which crimes are rendered useful instead of prosecutable.
