​Conservative lawyer flags evidence Trump is 'looking for excuses for failure'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 18, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Donald Trump's administration is already looking for excuses and scapegoats as it attempts to fulfill the president's massive promises, a conservative legal scholar said on Saturday.

Ed Whelan, who has a lengthy credentials list as a conservative legal scholar at the Ethics and Public Policy Center, and as a supporter of Trump's policies, took to social media over the weekend to float a theory about Trump's immigration plans.

Specifically drawing attention to Trump's attempts to use the Alien Enemies Act to deport people, Whelan said, "The due process required for deportations under Trump's Alien Enemies Act proclamation is much greater than due process for ordinary deportations."

"Any detainee facing deportation under AEA proclamation should be able to (1) challenge the lawfulness of the proclamation, and (2) contest factual basis for his designation as a member of TdA gang," he added before asking, "Is there any good reason that Trump administration continues to insist on AEA deportations?"

Whelan continued, questioning whether Trump is serious about his flagship political policy.

"If Trump administration were serious about deporting as many illegal aliens as possible, it would be doing everything it could to improve and expedite the ordinary deportation processes, not wasting its time on probably illegal Alien Enemies Act proclamation that would apply to 1/10,000 of the illegal aliens, all of whom could be deported through other means," he wrote. "It's almost as if it's looking for excuses for failure and scapegoats to blame."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Trump Administration's Immigration Strategy: A Scapegoat in the Making

In a remarkable turn of events, Ed Whelan, a conservative legal scholar known for his support of Trump's policies, has voiced critical concerns regarding the Trump administration's approach to immigration, specifically targeting the use of the Alien Enemies Act (AEA) for deportations. Whelan's critique offers a scathing rebuke of an administration already infamous for its chaotic and often controversial policy decisions. Here, we dissect his observations and the broader implications for Trump's immigration agenda.

Seeking Excuses Over Solutions

Whelan's analysis brings to light an administration seemingly more interested in finding excuses for its failure to deliver on grandiose promises rather than implementing effective and legal solutions. The use of the AEA—legislation that by Whelan's account, would impact an insignificantly small fraction of the undocumented population—highlights a perplexing strategy. Why indeed would the Trump administration focus on a complex, legally dubious method that affects a mere 1/10,000 of the illegal aliens in the U.S., as Whelan points out, when other more straightforward and lawful methods of deportation exist?

The Due Process Dilemma

Whelan's emphasis on the due process required under Trump's Alien Enemies Act proclamation is particularly telling. It underscores the legal and ethical quagmires inherent in Trump's immigration policies. According to Whelan, any detainee facing deportation under this proclamation should be able to challenge both the lawfulness of the proclamation itself and the factual basis for their designation as a threat. This requirement not only complicates the deportation process but also calls into question the administration's commitment to constitutional values and human rights.

A Strategy of Distraction?

The choice to pursue AEA deportations can be seen as a strategic move to distract from the administration's broader failures in immigration reform. By focusing on a high-profile, controversial approach, the Trump administration diverts attention from its inability to streamline and expedite the ordinary deportation processes. Whelan's critique suggests a deliberate obfuscation—an attempt to create a scapegoat in the form of the AEA while ignoring more practical and humane solutions to immigration enforcement.

A Critical Voice from Within

What makes Whelan's critique particularly damning is his position as a conservative legal scholar and a supporter of Trump's policies. His decision to publicly question the administration's strategy indicates a significant rift within conservative circles and highlights the extent to which Trump's immigration policies have become untenable, even among erstwhile allies.

Conclusion: A Path of Legal Entanglements and Ethical Quandaries

The Trump administration's insistence on using the Alien Enemies Act for deportations is a microcosm of its broader approach to governance: one characterized by legal shortcuts, ethical neglect, and a penchant for scapegoating. Whelan's critique is not just a condemnation of a specific policy decision but a scathing indictment of an administration that prioritizes political spectacle over substantive policy improvements and respect for the rule of law.

In the end, the administration's approach to immigration, as highlighted by Whelan, is a systemic error—a failure that speaks volumes about its disregard for effective governance, legal integrity, and the very principles upon which the United States was founded.
