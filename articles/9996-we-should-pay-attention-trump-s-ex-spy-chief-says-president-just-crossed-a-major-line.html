Former Homeland Security official Miles Taylor, who has claimed his home was targeted by a break-in shortly after he published a scathing “Anonymous” op-ed about Trump’s presidency, early Sunday morning published his interview with another Trump admin alum, the president’s former spy chief Sue Gordon. In that interview, she told Taylor that Trump is heading toward a “totalitarian regime” after calling for the prosecution of his perceived enemy, James Comey.

“When Donald Trump’s own former spy chief — someone who served under both Republican and Democratic presidents, by the way — says we’ve crossed the line into democratic decay, we should pay attention,” Taylor wrote. “In my Friday conversation with Sue Gordon, who served as Deputy Director of National Intelligence in the first Trump administration, she warned that this past week was that watershed moment.”

Taylor went on:

“Sue didn’t say it lightly. She’s spent her career safeguarding the American system from inside the government. So when she says this is one of the hardest weeks she’s ever experienced, it means we’re in a real national emergency.”

Taylor then quoted Gordon herself, who purportedly said, “I think we don’t talk nearly enough about historically about how totalitarian regimes start by using law for their own purposes.”

“We tend to think of them as just ‘not being lawful’ — but the first thing is…selectively applying it [for their own] purposes,” she then added. “And so you don’t have to see chaos and anarchy.”

Taylor went on to say we are in “grave danger” before quoting Gordon once again.

“So I’m worried because it feels like the institution hasn’t held…This was one of the control gates that I thought we wouldn’t be able to cross,” she said. “I think these may be the toughest days I’ve seen. But stay engaged. Don’t despair. This isn’t a ‘political’ fight. I only have one side, and that’s for America. So hold on to that. Hold that standard. Fight the fight that America needs fought. And then don’t shy away from participating.”

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump and the Path to Totalitarianism: A Dire Warning from a Former Spy Chief

In a world where the line between political theater and actual threats to democracy becomes ever blurrier, the latest revelations from within the Trump administration’s own ranks should set off alarm bells louder than anything we’ve encountered thus far. Miles Taylor, a former Homeland Security official who famously critiqued Trump’s presidency through an “Anonymous” op-ed, has brought to light an ominous warning from none other than Sue Gordon, the former Deputy Director of National Intelligence in the Trump administration. The message? We are teetering on the edge of a totalitarian regime.

Gordon’s Warning: It’s not every day that a figure of Gordon’s stature makes such a chilling diagnosis of the political climate. Having served under both Republican and Democratic presidents, her perspective carries an authority that cannot be easily dismissed. Her assessment that we have reached a “watershed moment” in democratic decay is a red flag of the highest order. Yet, what should truly terrify every American is her insight into the mechanisms of totalitarian regimes. They begin, not with overt lawlessness, but with the selective application of the law to serve the ruler’s ends. This subtlety is often overlooked until it’s too late, making her observations all the more urgent.

Trump’s Tactics: The focus of Gordon’s concern, the call for the prosecution of James Comey, Trump’s perceived enemy, exemplifies the kind of selective law enforcement typical of authoritarian leaders. This isn’t just about settling personal scores; it’s a strategic move to undermine the rule of law by weaponizing it against political adversaries. When the former president of the United States exhibits such tendencies without restraint, it’s not just a political problem; it’s a national crisis.

The Real National Emergency: The term “national emergency” is often thrown around in political discourse, but here, Taylor and Gordon articulate a genuine existential threat to the American democratic system. It’s a scenario where the safeguards of democracy are not just being eroded but are at risk of complete collapse. Gordon’s lament that “the institution hasn’t held” is a stark admission that the checks and balances we rely on to protect our democracy are failing.

A Call to Arms: Yet, all is not lost. Gordon’s parting message is one of resilience and engagement. This isn’t about partisan politics; it’s a battle for the soul of America. The call to “hold on to that. Hold that standard. Fight the fight that America needs fought” is a rallying cry for every citizen, regardless of political affiliation, to defend the principles that define the nation.

In conclusion, when someone with Gordon’s background and bipartisan respect warns of a slide into totalitarianism, it’s not just another opinion piece. It’s a clarion call to action. The path we’re on is fraught with danger, and complacency is not an option. The fight for America’s democratic soul is not over, but to win it, we must first acknowledge the magnitude of the threat we face. This isn’t hyperbole; it’s a reality check from those who know the stakes better than most. The question now is, will we listen?
