Judge throws Trump lawyers' own words back in their faces in angry slap down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Analyzing the Rejection of the Trump Administration's Request by Judge Murphy

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to reporters at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

A federal judge angrily rejected the Trump administration's request to reverse his order to give due process to migrants deported to South Sudan.

In a scathing 17-page order, U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy threw the administration's lawyers' words back into their faces to show they had argued in bad faith that meaningful immigration proceedings could be conducted overseas, and found the administration had violated his preliminary injunction.

"Since that hearing, merely five days ago, Defendants have changed their tune," Murphy wrote. "It turns out that having immigration proceedings on another continent is harder and more logistically cumbersome than Defendants anticipated."

The Trump administration flew immigrants to war-ravaged South Sudan earlier this month despite a court order requiring ICE to give people being deported to a country that's not their own a chance to argue their concerns in court. Trump administration lawyers claimed that could be done after arrival in the countries.

Murphy made clear the deportees should be given adequate notice and opportunity to raise fears of persecution or torture before they were taken to third countries, and he said the administration had raised red herrings to avoid complying with his order.

"However, the Court never said that Defendants had to convert their foreign military base into an immigration facility; it only left that as an option, again, at Defendants’ request," Murphy wrote. "The other option, of course, has always been to simply return to the status quo of roughly one week ago, or else choose any other location to complete the required process."

Only one of the eight deportees, who are currently being held in Djibouti, is a South Sudanese national. Another was expected to be returned to Myanmar.

"Defendants have mischaracterized this Court’s order, while at the same time manufacturing the very chaos they decry," Murphy wrote. "By racing to get six class members onto a plane to unstable South Sudan, clearly in breach of the law and this Court’s order, Defendants gave this Court no choice but to find that they were in violation of the Preliminary Injunction."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Rejection of the Trump Administration's Request by Judge Murphy

In a recent clash between the judiciary and the executive, U.S. District Judge Brian Murphy vehemently denied the Trump administration's attempt to circumvent his prior ruling that mandated due process for migrants deported to South Sudan. This confrontation underscores not just a legal battle, but a glaring example of the administration's disregard for human rights and judicial authority.

The Administration's Failed Argument

Judge Murphy's 17-page order is not just a legal document; it's a scathing rebuke of the Trump administration's half-baked plans to conduct immigration proceedings overseas. The administration's argument, which initially seemed to suggest a feasible, albeit flawed, plan, was quickly exposed as unworkable and in bad faith. The claim that immigration proceedings could effectively take place on another continent was not just optimistic; it was fundamentally detached from the logistical and humanitarian realities on the ground.

The Reality of Deportation to South Sudan

The decision to deport individuals to war-torn South Sudan, especially when the majority of those deported were not even South Sudanese nationals, is not just a bureaucratic oversight; it's a grave humanitarian concern. The administration's lawyers argued these proceedings could occur post-arrival in the deportees' supposed home countries—a notion Judge Murphy found not just impractical but inhumane. The requirement for adequate notice and the opportunity to raise fears of persecution or torture before deportation is not a bureaucratic hurdle; it's a fundamental aspect of due process and human rights.

The Manufacturing of Chaos

Judge Murphy's assertion that the administration manufactured the chaos it then decried is particularly telling. It reveals a strategy not of ineptitude, but of deliberate noncompliance with judicial orders. By rushing deportees onto a plane to South Sudan, the administration did not just breach the law and Judge Murphy's order; it acted in direct violation of international human rights principles.

The Mischaracterization of Judicial Orders

Perhaps one of the most egregious aspects of this situation is the administration's misinterpretation of the court's order. Judge Murphy clarified that he never mandated the conversion of a foreign military base into an immigration facility; this was merely presented as an option. The real crux of the matter is the administration's deliberate choice to ignore simpler, more humane alternatives in favor of a more draconian approach.

Conclusion

The saga of the Trump administration's attempt to deport migrants to South Sudan is not just a legal battle; it's a stark illustration of the administration's disregard for judicial authority, human rights, and the very concept of due process. Judge Murphy's order is a beacon of judicial integrity in the face of executive overreach and a reminder of the ongoing struggle against inhumane immigration policies. This episode should not be viewed in isolation but as part of a broader narrative of an administration consistently prioritizing expediency and political gain over human dignity and legal obligations.
