‘We’re underwater!’ GOP senator admits polling disaster – then blames Democrats

Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) admitted on Fox News Sunday night that the Republican Party was “underwater” with Americans on the issue of immigration – once one of the party’s strongest issues – but pivoted to blame Democrats for the GOP’s poor polling performance.

“How can we get the numbers switched on immigration?” asked Fox News host Trey Gowdy, a former Republican lawmaker himself. “Trump closed the border, and yet we’re underwater on that issue!”

The leader of the Republican Party, President Donald Trump, has seen his support on immigration tank among voters, particularly among independent voters. According to an NBC News poll from last month, 60% of Americans disapprove of Trump’s handling of immigration, a dramatic increase from the 49% who disapproved last June.

While the drop in support for the president on immigration has coincided with his administration’s aggressive and deadly immigration enforcement operations, Ernst instead moved to blame her Democratic colleagues.

“We are underwater on that issue, but we have to come together – both Democrats and Republicans – and find a way forward on this,” Ernst said.

“So much can be done on this if only Democrats will want to come to the table, but they’ve demonstrated – whether it’s DHS or anything else – that they just simply despise the president so much they don’t want to do the right thing for our country.”

Trump’s plummeting support on immigration was even reflected in a batch of private GOP polling data obtained by Axios in January, which revealed that 60% of independent voters disapproved of Trump’s immigration policy, polling that was conducted before the killings of Renee Good and Alex Pretti.

Our Analysis:

The Blame Game: GOP’s Immigration Woes

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of political gymnastics, Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) took to Fox News to perform an extraordinary act of deflection. With the Republican Party’s approval ratings on immigration sinking faster than the Titanic, Ernst’s solution wasn’t to address the root causes or to propose tangible policy changes. No, it was to pivot and place the blame squarely on the Democrats’ doorstep.

First off, let’s address the elephant in the room: President Donald Trump’s plummeting support on immigration issues. According to a recent NBC News poll, a whopping 60% of Americans disapprove of Trump’s handling of immigration. This isn’t just a slight dip in approval; it’s a nosedive from the 49% who disapproved last June. The drop is especially pronounced among independent voters, a demographic crucial to swinging elections. This group’s disapproval stands at a staggering 60%, according to private GOP polling data obtained by Axios. But why? Could it be, perhaps, due to the administration’s “aggressive and deadly immigration enforcement operations”? No, that couldn’t possibly be it, Ernst seems to imply, as she sidesteps this inconvenient truth.

Ernst’s argument that Democrats are to blame for the GOP’s poor polling on immigration because they “simply despise the president so much they don’t want to do the right thing for our country” is a masterclass in missing the point. It’s akin to blaming the fire department for water damage while ignoring the arsonist. The GOP’s stance on immigration, characterized by its support for Trump’s policies, is what’s under scrutiny by the American public.

Let’s not forget the human cost of these policies. More than 328,000 migrants have been arrested during the second Trump administration, and over 73% of them have no criminal record. This directly contradicts Trump’s pledge to target only the “worst of the worst.” It’s not just a policy failure; it’s a moral one. Yet, Ernst’s comments make no mention of this glaring issue.

In the face of such criticism, Ernst’s plea for bipartisan cooperation comes across as insincere at best. The call to “come together” sounds hollow when the GOP’s definition of cooperation seemingly involves the other party capitulating to its failed and unpopular policies. This isn’t a pathway to reform; it’s a roadblock.

In conclusion, Ernst’s attempt to shift the blame to Democrats for the GOP’s immigration woes is not just unconvincing; it’s insulting to the intelligence of the American electorate. The data is clear, the results of the policies are clear, and the disapproval from the public is crystal clear. It’s time for the GOP to look inward and confront the real reasons behind their sinking approval ratings on immigration, rather than pointing fingers across the aisle. Accountability starts at home, and in this case, it needs to start with the Republican Party and its leadership.
