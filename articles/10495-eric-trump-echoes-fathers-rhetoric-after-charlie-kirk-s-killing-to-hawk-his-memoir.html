Eric Trump echoes father’s rhetoric after Charlie Kirk's killing to hawk his memoir

Eric Trump railed against “radical left lunatics” in a tirade of grievances—parroting the same rhetoric his father used in the wake of the assassination of Charlie Kirk—as he hawked his new memoir, “Under Siege.”

“As to what the radical left lunatics were trying to do to our family, what the movement that we created, and it’s a movement of good. It’s exactly what Charlie epitomized,” the president’s son declared to podcaster Benny Johnson.

“You know, we’re all watching, and we see exactly what happened to Charlie,” he explained.

“That is us being under siege,” he told Johnson. “That is our party, that is our movement.”

“That is God being under siege,” he declared. “That’s our Constitution being under siege. That’s our American flag being under siege. That’s our family unit in this country being under siege. That’s our democracy being under siege. Everything that we love, everything that we fought for.”

“It’s every one of those college kids that’s indoctrinated,” he complained. “It’s all of them being under siege. It’s a revisionist history that they teach in our schools.”

Trump said he will donate part of the proceeds of the book to Kirk’s organization, Turning Point USA, “because here’s what you and I and all the other people can never allow happen: We cannot allow his mission, you know, to ever die, it won’t.”

He also claimed that “the bullets are only going one way,” suggesting that the left, not the right, is responsible for violence.

Trump went on to describe where he was when his father’s attempted assassination took place in Butler, Pennsylvania. He also invoked the 2017 shooting of Republican U.S. Rep. Steve Scalise, and “what almost happened to [Brett] Kavanaugh. And then we see what happens to Charlie Kirk.”

“I mean, the bullets are only flying one way, and yet I get to hear the nonsense, and I get to watch all these networks every night,” he said.

“I mean, how is it that you know, the decorum is only going one way in this country, and yet we’re the ones that are labeled as the ‘fascist’? Isn’t that kind of ironic?”

“You know, hey, the Republicans in this country are generally the ones that have the guns. We’re the ones that believe in the Second Amendment. You know, they’re not doing this shooting.”

On Friday morning, in a social media post with a photo of his book, Trump wrote, “Like all of you, I have been deeply impacted by Charlie’s life. His story is not over. This movement is not over, and there is still so much more to be done. In honor of Charlie’s life and his incredible legacy, I will be donating a portion of the proceeds from Under Siege to Turning Point USA to continue their amazing work.”

Pointing to that post, The Daily Beast wrote that Trump was using Kirk’s death “to sell his book.”

Our Analysis:

The Art of Opportunism: Eric Trump's Rhetoric Dissected

In a striking display of opportunism cloaked in grievance, Eric Trump leverages the assassination of Charlie Kirk to peddle his memoir, “Under Siege,” all while echoing the inflammatory rhetoric characteristic of his father. This maneuver is not merely a promotional tactic; it is a concerted effort to weaponize tragedy for political gain and to propagate a narrative of victimhood that is as strategic as it is disingenuous.

The Siege Mentality

Eric Trump’s tirade against the “radical left lunatics” is a textbook example of the siege mentality that the Trump family has long exploited. By claiming that everything from God to the American flag is “under siege,” he is not just rallying his base; he is attempting to monopolize the concept of patriotism. The audacity to suggest that a diverse and complex political landscape can be boiled down to a binary clash where one side embodies all virtue and the other all vice is not only simplistic; it's dangerously divisive.

The Irony of Violence

Perhaps the most egregious aspect of Trump’s commentary is his claim that “the bullets are only going one way,” a blatant attempt to absolve his side of any propensity for violence. This narrative conveniently ignores the fact that political violence in America is not the monopoly of any one ideology. By ignoring instances where the rhetoric of the right has led to violence, Trump is engaging in a selective amnesia that is both misleading and irresponsible.

The Commercialization of Tragedy

The Daily Beast's accusation that Trump is using Kirk’s death “to sell his book” is not without merit. Announcing a donation to Kirk’s organization, Turning Point USA, in the same breath as promoting his memoir does not signify magnanimity; it signifies marketing. This tactic is not just in poor taste; it’s exploitative, using the emotional weight of tragedy to bolster book sales. It’s a stark illustration of how personal gain is often entangled with public mourning in the Trump playbook.

The Misuse of History

Eric Trump’s lament about “revisionist history” being taught in schools and the indoctrination of college students is a familiar refrain used to undermine educational institutions that encourage critical thinking. By framing the academic exploration of America’s complex history as an attack, Trump is not defending the sanctity of education; he is promoting a sanitized, uncritical patriotism that refuses to acknowledge America's mistakes along with its triumphs.

The Irony of Accusations

The irony of Eric Trump complaining about being labeled as “fascist” while openly admitting that Republicans are “the ones that have the guns” and believe in the Second Amendment to an extreme degree cannot be overstated. This juxtaposition between claiming victimhood while boasting of armed readiness is not just ironic; it’s indicative of the cognitive dissonance that plagues much of Trump’s rhetoric.

In Conclusion

Eric Trump’s statements, as reported, are more than just the ramblings of a political figure; they are a calculated attempt to manipulate public sentiment by invoking fear, division, and patriotism, all while promoting his book. This strategy, while not new, continues to be effective in polarizing the public. It is crucial to recognize these tactics for what they are: a disingenuous blend of opportunism, manipulation, and commercial exploitation. The real siege here is not against a party or a movement; it’s against rational discourse and unity in the American political landscape.
