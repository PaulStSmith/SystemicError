'You cannot make this stuff up': Latest ICE recruitment pitch buried on MSNBC

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 10, 20255 min read

NBC/screen grab

Reacting to increasing efforts by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) to recruit as agents as fast as possible now that the agency is flush with cash, MSNBC's Ali Velshi was both stunned by the overt racist appeal in one ad and amused by a celebrity endorsement that has been widely mocked.

On Saturday morning, Velshi told his viewers, "I want to show you some recent recruitment ads from the Department of Homeland Security, specifically ICE," before singling out one and adding, "Take a look at this ad which reads: 'Serve your country. Defend your culture. No undergraduate degree required."

"How totally fun!" he added sarcastically. "Defend your culture and no undergraduate degree required."

"The immigration agency is lowering the bar when it comes to hiring requirements, too," he suggested. "This week, the Homeland Security Secretary, Kristi Noem, announced that the agency would be eliminating the age cap for new hires, allowing people older than 40 and as young as 18 to join the ranks of ICE."

Joking, "The recruitment push is working," he noted the use of former television star Dean Cain –– who starred as Superman almost 30 years ago –– in new ads.

"He [Cain] shared on social media that he plans to become an ICE officer, to quote, 'Save America.,'" Velshi stated before dryly pointing out, "Superman, a literal undocumented alien, like an actual alien from outer space from the planet Krypton, is now going to be an ICE agent. You cannot make this stuff up."

"Jokes aside, this drive to hire more personnel seems to be ideologically driven," he suggested. "ICE used to require employees to have an undergraduate degree, not anymore. You don't really need any experience in law enforcement either. Apparently, you don't even need a uniform, because so many of the arrests that we're witnessing are being carried out by masked plainclothes officers."

"Covid is on the rise, so maybe they're on to something," he joked.

''It seems like the Trump administration is building up ICE to be an alternative force that's bigger, more politically loyal, unencumbered by standards and largely shielded from democratic accountability," he accused.

Our Analysis:

ICE's Recruitment Frenzy: A Dive into Desperation and Dubiousness

In a move that reeks of desperation and not-so-subtle xenophobia, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has launched a recruitment campaign that can only be described as both laughable and deeply troubling. MSNBC's Ali Velshi has shed light on this with a blend of incredulity and necessary sarcasm, revealing an ad campaign that seems more rooted in promoting a narrow, exclusionary vision of American culture than in safeguarding the nation's security.

"Serve your country. Defend your culture. No undergraduate degree required." This slogan, so brazen in its appeal, would be comical if it weren't so dangerously close to the ethno-nationalist rhetoric that has no place in a modern, multicultural society. The implication here—that American culture is under siege and must be defended by any means necessary—plays directly into the hands of those seeking to divide rather than unite.

The Lowering of Standards: A Strategy or a Slippery Slope?

The revelation that ICE is lowering its hiring standards, eliminating the age cap, and no longer requiring an undergraduate degree for new hires is startling. This move, ostensibly aimed at widening the pool of potential recruits, raises serious questions about the quality and preparedness of the agents ICE intends to deploy. Velshi's sarcastic nod to the lack of need for a uniform, pointing to the increasingly common sight of arrests made by "masked plainclothes officers," underscores a chilling shift towards a more shadowy, unaccountable approach to immigration enforcement.

The Bizarre World of Celebrity Endorsements

Adding a layer of absurdity to the situation is the enlistment of former television Superman, Dean Cain, as a celebrity endorser for ICE. Velshi's observation that Superman—a literal alien—would now be working to deport undocumented immigrants is a stroke of dark irony that would be hilarious if the stakes weren't so high. This surreal twist highlights the lengths to which ICE is willing to go in order to glamorize and sanitize its increasingly controversial operations.

A Political Tool Sharpened for the Trump Era

Velshi doesn't shy away from calling out what many have observed: the Trump administration's efforts to build up ICE as a "bigger, more politically loyal" force. This accusation strikes at the heart of the matter. Under the guise of national security and border protection, ICE is being transformed into a tool of political power, unbound by the usual standards of accountability and transparency that are the hallmarks of a democratic society.

This recruitment drive, with its overt racist appeals and disregard for qualifications, is not just about filling the ranks—it's about reshaping ICE into an entity that serves a very specific ideological agenda. The implications for civil liberties, not to mention the very fabric of American society, are profound.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

As amusing as Velshi finds some aspects of this debacle, the underlying realities are no laughing matter. The efforts to militarize and politicize ICE, to turn it into an arm of cultural warfare, should alarm anyone who values the principles upon which the United States was founded. This is not just about who gets to wear the badge of an ICE agent; it's about the soul of American law enforcement and the direction in which it's headed.

In a time when the need for reform and accountability in law enforcement is being voiced louder than ever, ICE's recruitment campaign is a step back into the shadows. It represents a systemic error of judgment that threatens to undermine the very ideals of justice and equality that America purports to stand for.
