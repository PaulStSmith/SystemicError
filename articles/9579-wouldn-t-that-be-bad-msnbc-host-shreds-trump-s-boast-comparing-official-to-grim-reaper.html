'Wouldn't that be bad?' MSNBC host shreds Trump's boast comparing official to Grim Reaper

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 15, 20255 min read

FILE PHOTO: Director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Russell Vought speaks to reporters outside the West Wing of the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 17, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard/File Photo

President Donald Trump shared an AI-made video that used an image of Office of Management and Budget Russ Vought dressed as the Grim Reaper. It prompted a confused MSNBC host to question why Trump would use such an allegation that wasn’t a positive thing.

Speaking to Brendan Buck, a former strategist who worked under two House speakers, Paul Ryan (R-WI) and John Boehner (R-OH), Katy Tur read off the latest round of federal staffing cuts, which have been ongoing since Trump entered office.

“The president has depicted Russ Vought, his OMB director, as the Grim Reaper. It’s confusing, and I had to double-check this just to make sure, because you would think that depicting one of your officials as the Grim Reaper, in normal times, wouldn’t that be a bad thing?” Tur asked.

“In normal times, yes,” Buck said, chuckling.

“But I think that what that clearly tells you is that they’re using the threat or the follow-through of some of these RIFs, or layoffs, as a threat to Democrats. And I think Democrats are largely calling their bluff on it a bit,” Buck said. “Now, for all 4,000 of those people and their families that’s a crisis. And that’s a personal crisis for them, but it is still just a fraction of the amount of layoffs that we were hearing about, rumored about, that Russ Vought was going to take.”

He recalled people throwing around numbers, such as 100,000 people being cut from the government.

“And it took a really long time for them to get to this point. And I think what happened here was they kept threatening to do this, and didn’t do it, and eventually they had to do something or else it was going to be very clear that this was just an empty threat,” Buck said.

Tur returned to the phrasing of the reaper, asking as a communications expert, how it was helping to characterize Vought as the Grim Reaper or having Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT) saying Vought has been dreaming about firing people “since puberty.”

“They’re putting a positive message, or they’re making it sound like they really enjoy laying people off. Is that a good communications strategy?” Tur asked.

Buck agreed it wasn’t a good strategy and noted that Republicans seem to be failing in the messaging battle on the shutdown.

“I just wonder how people take it, Brendan, I mean, if you are trying to message that this is not your fault, that the government shutdown is the Democrats’ fault, that’s the messaging that I’m confused by,” Tur confessed.

Our Analysis:

The Grim Reaper of Government Efficiency or a Messaging Misstep?

In a recent, bewildering move, President Donald Trump shared an AI-made video that transformed Russ Vought, his Office of Management and Budget director, into none other than the Grim Reaper. This peculiar choice of allegory has left many scratching their heads, including MSNBC host Katy Tur, who found herself in a state of confusion over the president’s strategy – if it can even be called that.

Unpacking the Allegory

At first glance, depicting a high-ranking official as the harbinger of death seems like a communications blunder of the highest order. In what universe is the Grim Reaper a symbol of positive change or efficiency? The answer, it seems, is in the parallel universe where the Trump administration crafts its narrative.

Brendan Buck, a former strategist with ties to notable Republican figures, attempted to shed light on this bizarre scenario. According to Buck, this morbid representation is an attempt to weaponize federal staffing cuts against the Democrats. However, this is where the administration’s strategy begins to unravel. The threat of layoffs, while a reality for thousands and a personal crisis for each affected individual, was supposed to be a bargaining chip. Yet, the actual layoffs fell short of the catastrophic numbers initially floated around, revealing this strategy for what it was: a bluff.

A Crisis of Communication

Tur’s probing into the efficacy of such a grim depiction as a communications strategy only highlights the absurdity of the situation. The administration seems to be gloating over layoffs, painting Vought as a character who has been fantasizing about firing people since his teenage years. This is not just poor taste; it’s a catastrophic failure in messaging.

The government shutdown, a sore point for many Americans, is being spun by Republicans as the fault of the Democrats. Yet, here they are, effectively celebrating the very layoffs and cutbacks that contribute to the pain and frustration felt by the public. This mixed messaging doesn’t just fall flat; it backfires spectacularly.

The Bottom Line

In what could only be described as a systemic error in judgment, the Trump administration has fumbled the ball in the court of public opinion. Depicting a government official as the Grim Reaper and boasting about layoffs is not just tone-deaf; it’s a glaring indictment of a government disconnected from the consequences of its actions.

The real question here isn’t just about the optics of such a decision or the bizarre satisfaction derived from these layoffs. It’s about the underlying values it reveals. This administration, through its actions and its odd choice of symbols, has shown a disturbing lack of empathy and a gross misunderstanding of effective communication.

In the end, the Grim Reaper allegory serves as a perfect symbol, not of efficiency or necessary cuts, but of an administration that seems all too comfortable with the role of harbinger of doom for its own people. It’s a stark reminder that in these “normal times,” such a depiction is not only a bad thing; it’s a deeply troubling sign of how far from normal we’ve strayed.
