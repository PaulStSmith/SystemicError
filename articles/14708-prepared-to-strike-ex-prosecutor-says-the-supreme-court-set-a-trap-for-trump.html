​'Prepared to strike': Ex-prosecutor says the Supreme Court set a trap for Trump

The Supreme Court might just be giving Donald Trump enough rope to hang himself in a court of law, an ex-prosecutor said on Saturday.

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance over the weekend weighed in on the Supreme Court's recent hearing in a case involving a controversial law used to deport immigrants without due process.

According to Vance's Substack analysis, the Supreme Court "enjoined the government from summarily deporting alleged gang members under the Alien Enemies Act while litigation over the constitutionality of those deportations works its way through the courts."

"The decision is a per curiam opinion, which means no single justice signed it, but it represents the view of seven of them. You can read the full decision here. It runs to 24 pages, and is worth spending some time with, if only to get the Court’s tone. Suffice it to say, the majority is displeased with the government," she wrote.

Vance goes on to tug at a "couple of threads" from the ruling.

"First, the Supreme Court didn’t tell the government what it would have to do before deporting people in order to satisfy due process requirements," the attorney wrote. "Second, they emphasized that they are granting the injunction because if they didn’t, the government would deport these people and then claim, as they have in the Abrego Garcia case, that once someone is out of the United States, even when our government is responsible for that, the courts no longer have jurisdiction to hear their cases. That’s as close as the Supreme Court ever comes to accusing the government of perfidy." Perfidy, according to online dictionaries, is synonymous with deceit or perhaps with untrustworthiness.

Vance goes on to cover Trump lashing out at the Supreme Court itself, and then suggests the court is simply "biding its time."

"If, as it appears, the Supreme Court is biding its time, giving Trump every opportunity to comply with the law while preparing to strike if he doesn’t, then Trump is making the case against himself," the ex-prosecutor said. "Just as his solicitor general told Justice Barrett during oral argument in the birthright citizenship case, this is an administration that doesn’t think it always has to comply with court orders. Instead of contenting itself with appealing them, it engages in this extreme form of rhetoric designed to turn people against the courts. And although the Supreme Court hasn’t always come to the defense of other aspects of democracy, it does seem to understand the stakes when its authority is being contested."

Our Analysis:

The Supreme Court's Tightrope: Trump's Legal Perils and the Alien Enemies Act

In a recent turn of events that reads like a legal thriller, the Supreme Court has thrown a curveball that could very well lead to Donald Trump's legal entanglement. Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance has insightfully dissected this development, offering a glimpse into what might be a calculated move by the court to let Trump, in essence, dig his own grave. Let's unravel this intricate web of judicial decisions, governmental actions, and potential implications for Trump.

The Supreme Court's Displeasure

At the heart of this saga is the Supreme Court's recent per curiam opinion, a unanimous decision shrouded in collective anonymity, signaling a strong, unified front against the current administration's use of the Alien Enemies Act to summarily deport alleged gang members without due process. This decision not only halts these deportations but also sends a clear message of the court's displeasure with the government's actions.

Vance's analysis highlights two critical threads from the ruling: 1. The lack of guidance on what would satisfy due process requirements before deportation. 2. An emphasis on preventing the government from deporting individuals and then claiming a lack of jurisdiction to hear their cases, a tactic that reeks of deceit, or as Vance eloquently puts it, "perfidy."

Trump's Self-Inflicted Wounds

Trump's response to the Supreme Court's decision has been nothing short of bombastic, lashing out at the very institution that holds the reins of justice. Vance suggests that the Supreme Court is strategically "biding its time," giving Trump enough rope to hang himself by not complying with the law and openly challenging the court's authority. This is a president whose administration has been marked by a cavalier attitude towards judicial mandates, often preferring to engage in extreme rhetoric rather than respect the legal process.

The Bigger Picture

What this situation underscores is a troubling pattern of behavior from Trump and his administration: a flagrant disregard for the rule of law and a willingness to undermine judicial authority for political ends. The Supreme Court, though not always a defender of democracy's other aspects, recognizes the stakes when its own authority is contested.

Conclusion: A Judicial Checkmate?

The Supreme Court's decision could be seen as a masterstroke in judicial strategy, setting a trap for Trump that could lead to significant legal repercussions. By giving the administration enough leeway to comply with the law while preparing to act if it doesn't, the court is effectively allowing Trump to be the architect of his own downfall.

In this high-stakes legal chess game, the Supreme Court has made a decisive move. Whether it will lead to checkmate remains to be seen, but one thing is clear: the court is not willing to stand idly by as its authority is challenged, and Trump might just find himself ensnared in a legal quagmire of his own making.
