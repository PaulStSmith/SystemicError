'The numbers are startling': GOP strategists fret over electoral 'pattern' that's emerging

Republican strategists are increasingly worried about the enthusiasm gap that has been driving Democratic wins since the 2024 election.

Democratic candidates have outpaced Kamala Harris‘ 2024 performance by double digits in race after race, by an average of 10.5 percentage points in 20 state legislative special elections this year and 13.9 points in the 67 state House and Senate races last year, reported Axios.

“Many Republicans trace their troubles to Trump not being on the ballot this year or in 2028,” Axios reported. “But strategists acknowledge that some of his actions — including the administration’s reluctance to release more of the Epstein files — have turned off parts of his MAGA base, while energizing Democrats and anti-GOP independents.”

“The numbers are startling,” the report added. “In race after race, Democrats are outpacing their 2024 performance by double digits, a clear sign of a yawning enthusiasm gap.”

In the past four weeks, Democrat Taylor Rehmet won a plus-17 Trump district in north Texas by 14 points and Democrat Chasity Verret Martinez won a plus-13 Trump district in south Louisiana by a staggering 24 points, and while Republican Dillon Travis won a special election in north-central Oklahoma by 28 points, that was 30 points lower than Trump’s 58-point blowout in 2024.

“While it is tempting for many in our party to wish away these results,” a GOP operative told Axios, “the pattern is clear that there is at least a current 10-point Democratic over-performance from Trump 2024 — and it’s built on a fired-up Democratic base and a sleepy GOP base.”

Taylor Rehmet flipped the deep-red north Texas state Senate district Jan. 31 by focusing on affordability, rather than directly attacking President Donald Trump, he told Axios, and he believes he won over a “sizable” number of Trump voters, but some Republicans say the president will be able to turn out his base for Republicans in the midterms.

“Let’s not pretend a couple of low-turnout special elections suddenly signal a political earthquake,” said Mason Di Palma, communications director for the Republican State Leadership Committee. “They are unique, low-turnout contests driven by highly localized factors.”

Our Analysis:

The Republican Enthusiasm Gap: A Deep Dive into Democratic Wins

The political landscape is shifting beneath our feet, and the tremors are being felt most acutely within the Republican Party. A recent Axios report lays bare a phenomenon that’s been quietly brewing since the 2024 election: a significant enthusiasm gap favoring Democrats, a trend underscored by a series of startling electoral outcomes. Let’s dissect the findings and the somewhat desperate rationalizations orbiting this political black hole.

The Stark Numbers

The report cites an average outperformance of 10.5 percentage points by Democrats in this year’s 20 state legislative special elections, escalating from a 13.9 point average in last year’s 67 state House and Senate races. These aren’t marginal victories; they’re indicative of a seismic shift in voter engagement and preferences. The victories of Democrat Taylor Rehmet in a +17 Trump district and Chasity Verret Martinez in a +13 Trump district by 14 and 24 points respectively aren’t just wins; they’re resounding repudiations of the status quo.

Trump’s Diminishing Shadow

Attributing the GOP’s woes to Donald Trump’s absence from the ballot seems like a convenient excuse until you consider the broader context. The reluctance to release more of the Epstein files is mentioned as a specific turnoff for the MAGA base, yet it’s emblematic of a larger issue: a party struggling to move beyond the controversies and cult of personality that defined the Trump era. This isn’t merely about Trump not being on the ballot; it’s about the lingering aftereffects of his presidency and the divisive politics it championed.

The Enthusiasm Gap Explained

The GOP operative’s admission of a current 10-point Democratic overperformance since Trump’s 2024 campaign is telling. It speaks to a Democratic base galvanized by opposition to Trump-era policies and rhetoric, contrasted with a GOP base described as “sleepy.” This isn’t just about Democrats showing up in droves; it’s about Republicans failing to mobilize their base with the same fervor.

The Republican Rationalization

Mason Di Palma’s attempt to downplay these results as “unique, low-turnout contests driven by highly localized factors” is a masterclass in political obfuscation. While local factors always play a role in elections, dismissing such consistent overperformance across diverse districts as anomalies is wishful thinking at best. It smacks of a party in denial about the root causes of its declining appeal.

Conclusion: The GOP’s Crossroads

The Republican Party stands at a crossroads, confronted by a base disillusioned by unmet promises and controversies, and an opposition invigorated by the prospect of change. The lesson here isn’t just about Trump’s shadow or the specifics of the Epstein files; it’s about the broader challenge of defining what the Republican Party stands for in a post-Trump era.

The enthusiasm gap isn’t an aberration; it’s a wake-up call. The question for the GOP isn’t just how to win back the MAGA base, but how to address the broader existential crisis within its ranks. Ignoring these electoral tremors could very well lead to a political earthquake from which recovery could be arduously slow.
