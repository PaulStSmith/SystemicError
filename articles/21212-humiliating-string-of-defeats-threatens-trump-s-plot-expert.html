'Humiliating string of defeats' threatens Trump's plot: expert

Based on an article by Michael Waldman

Trump Is Not “Questioning” the Vote. He Is Weaponizing the State.

The Source Material

With Election Day still 112 days away, Trump is using federal power to destabilize voting: purging the Election Assistance Commission, pushing DOJ subpoenas into election workers, and sending state officials threats dressed up as election security. The visible campaign is legal aggression. The real campaign is fear.

Who Has Power

The key fact is institutional, not rhetorical. Trump is the president. The Justice Department is his instrument. The Election Assistance Commission is a tiny agency, but it still matters because it helps states administer elections and distribute resources. When Trump removes its commissioners, he is not merely “criticizing” election administration; he is stripping capacity from the system he claims to protect.

That matters more than his press quotes, more than the recycled 2020 lies, more than the rumors about some future revelation involving China or Iran. Those are the cover story. The power move is federal pressure against the people and offices that actually run elections.

The Method Is Fear

This is not confusion. It is choreography.

The DOJ letter warning state officials about criminal liability for noncitizens on the rolls is not an innocent reminder of law. The Utah Republican lieutenant governor said it plainly: the department sent a “love letter” full of threats while demanding voter data that courts have already ruled out of bounds. That is not civil-rights enforcement. It is intimidation with letterhead.

The same logic applies to the subpoenas in Fulton County. A judge called them “staggering” and described the DOJ’s conduct as a “fishing expedition.” That is the judicial record of what this is: a federal search for leverage, not evidence.

The Real Target Is Voter Confidence

Trump’s election lies are not just about relitigating 2020. They are about making ordinary voting feel dangerous, confusing, or futile.

The source gives away the mechanism. A voter worries that a name change will make her unable to vote. Another fears a Voting Rights Act ruling means she cannot vote at all. Latino voters and immigrants may hear the word “ICE” near polling places and stay home, even if the deployment would be illegal. That is the point. Fear does not have to be accurate to be effective.

This is why pretending the problem is simply “misinformation” is too soft. Misinformation is the smoke. The arson is coming from inside government.

The Enforcement State Should Stop Here

The article notes that public pressure and legal resistance are pushing back. Good. But the lesson is not that the system is healthy. The lesson is that democratic survival now depends on judges, voting rights groups, state officials, and voters resisting a president who treats elections as a hostile takeover target.

And yes, the ICE reference is not incidental. The fact that a federal immigration police force can be imagined as a polling-place menace is a sign of how deeply coercive this state has become. ICE should be abolished. A democracy does not need a domestic terror apparatus hovering over ballot access.

The Bigger Pattern

What this story reveals is not a series of isolated excesses. It is a governing method: use federal agencies to create uncertainty, use legal threats to chill participation, then call the resulting panic proof that the system is broken.

That is authoritarian politics in an American suit. It does not need to cancel the election outright when it can make voters doubt their own eligibility, officials fear compliance, and agencies lose the capacity to function. The damage is not only in the lawless acts themselves. It is in the deliberate conversion of public administration into a machine for democratic sabotage.
