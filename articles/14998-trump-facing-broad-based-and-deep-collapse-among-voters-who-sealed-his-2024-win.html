Trump facing 'broad-based and deep' collapse among voters who sealed his 2024 win

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Crumbling Trump Coalition: A Closer Look

Donald Trump (Photo by Nathan Howard for Reuters)

After over 100 days in office, support for Donald Trump among voters who normally are not found in the GOP camp but voted for him has already cratered.

According to a report from CNN, Trump got a boost in 2024 from " Latinos, younger men, non-White voters without a college degree, and, to some extent, Black men," but that support has withered away primarily due to a depressed economy.

According to longtime GOP analyst-turned-Trump critic Mike Madrid, "The collapse that he’s experiencing — I think that’s the right word to phrase it — is broad-based and it’s deep."

CNN's Ronald Brownstein is reporting, "Few strategists in either party believe the cooling toward Trump means Democrats have erased their long-term problems with these voter groups, which have generally drifted toward the GOP since the end of Barack Obama’s presidency. But the rapid erosion of Trump’s standing with them does suggest that their movement toward him in 2024 was driven less by a durable rightward shift on cultural issues than by immediate discontent with their economic situation."

Pointing out that Trump's "job approval rating among young people, Latinos and Black Americans" has dropped below falling below his 2024 vote levels, Brownstein suggested, "His ratings on the economy with those groups are even weaker. And while Trump still receives decent grades from Hispanic and young people for his handling of the border, ratings of his overall approach to immigration have consistently fallen into negative territory with them as well. Our Analysis:

The Crumbling Trump Coalition: A Closer Look

In a political landscape that often feels as though it's written by a team of overworked soap opera writers, the tale of Donald Trump's rapidly declining support among unconventional Republican voters reads like the latest twist in an ongoing saga. This isn't just the story of political allegiance shifting like sands in the wind; it's a narrative deeply rooted in the current economic malaise gripping the United States. But let's dive deeper, beyond the surface-level analysis, and explore what this really means.

The Illusion of a Diverse Coalition

The CNN report underscores a critical point: Trump's support among "Latinos, younger men, non-White voters without a college degree, and, to some extent, Black men" was never about a profound ideological alignment. No, this was a marriage of convenience, with economic discontent as the officiant. The notion that these groups had found a long-term home within the GOP under Trump's banner was always more fantasy than fact. As Mike Madrid, a GOP analyst turned Trump critic, succinctly puts it, the collapse of Trump's support is "broad-based and it's deep." This isn't merely a dip; it's a nosedive.

Economic Discontent: The Real MVP

The report hinges on a vital piece of context: the depressed economy is the primary catalyst for the erosion of support. Herein lies the crux of the matter. Trump's ersatz coalition was not galvanized by a sudden, collective rightward shift on cultural issues but by a pervasive dissatisfaction with their economic circumstances. This is a crucial distinction, suggesting that any Republican, Trump or otherwise, who fails to address these economic grievances will likely see their support evaporate just as quickly.

A False Dawn for Democrats?

However, it would be a grave mistake for Democrats to view this as a clear path to victory. Ronald Brownstein's analysis offers a sobering reminder: the Democrats' "long-term problems with these voter groups" have not magically disappeared. The cooling of enthusiasm for Trump does not equate to a warming toward the Democratic Party. Instead, it highlights a volatile electorate, one that's increasingly disillusioned with both parties.

The Economy, Stupid

Digging into the specifics, Trump's job approval ratings on the economy are in the gutter, especially among the demographic groups in question. This shouldn't come as a surprise. Economic downturns have a long history of souring public opinion on incumbent politicians. What's interesting, though, is the disparity between Trump's relatively better ratings on handling the border and his overall negative ratings on immigration. This suggests a nuanced voter base that cannot be easily pigeonholed or taken for granted by either party.

Conclusion: A Wake-Up Call

This development should serve as a wake-up call to both major parties. For Republicans, it's a stark reminder that economic policy cannot play second fiddle to cultural warfare if they hope to maintain a broad coalition. For Democrats, it's a warning not to be complacent, assuming disaffected Trump voters will automatically turn blue.

The political sands are shifting, and if the parties fail to adapt, they might just find themselves sinking. This isn't merely the story of Trump's declining support; it's a narrative about the volatile heart of American politics, where economic discontent reigns supreme.
