'Fragile' MAGA schooled on patriotism after 'meltdown' over US Olympians’ critiques

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 9, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Patriotism Paradox: Criticism as a Cornerstone of Democracy

Political commentator Tucker Carlson and political advisor Katie Miller in Washington, D.C., U.S., October 14, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

Pro-Donald Trump voices on the right erupted Saturday after American athletes voiced concern and disappointment over the state of affairs in the United States, including the ongoing unrest in Minnesota and militarized federal raids in Democratic-led cities around the country.

As a Daily Beast headline reads, “MAGA sent into full meltdown over ‘traitor’ U.S. Olympians.”

Team USA Olympic skier Hunter Hess told a group of reporters he’s experiencing “mixed emotions” while representing the U.S.

“Just because I wear the flag doesn’t mean I represent everything that’s going on in the U.S,” Hess said.

In response to Hess’ remarks, conspiracy theorist Jack Posobiec told the Olympic skier, that his frustration with the U.S. can be “Easily solved.”

“Get out,” Posobiec posted.

As AlterNet reported Saturday, “another American skier, Chris Liller, admitted he was ‘heartbroken’ over ‘what’s going on with ICE and the protests.’”

“I think that, as a country, we need to focus on respecting everybody’s rights and treating our citizens, as well as everybody, with love and respect,” Liller said. “I hope that, when people look at athletes competing in the Olympics, they realize that’s the America we’re trying to represent.”

“Politics affects us all,” American Figure skater Amber Glenn told reporters. “It is something I will not just be quiet about.”

Podcaster Megyn Kelly took particular offense to Glenn’s remarks, describing her as “another turncoat to root against.”

But MAGA critics are rallying around the Olympians, arguing it is in fact deeply patriotic to criticize the U.S. government.

“How dare you say something bad about America. I’m rooting against America now,” leftist @evanlovesworf humorously posted on X.

Political scientist Ian Bremmer argued that the ability to criticize the U.S. government is what makes the country stand out on the world stage.

“One of the things I most love about the United States is that it’s patriotic to criticize your government when you disagree with it,” Bremmer wrote. “Yes, even if you’re an athlete representing my country at the Olympics. Try that as a Russian or Chinese.”

“The political right remain the most fragile, pathetic snowflakes the world has ever encountered,” T.V. producer Franklin Leonard wrote.

Kate Miller, wife of top Trump aide Stephen Miller, appeared to summarize the far-right’s attitude towards U.S. athletes speaking out against the U.S., writing on X, “If you can’t say you love America while competing on behalf of our nation then you shouldn’t be at the Olympics.”

“That’s not how freedom works,” Emmy-winning reporter Mark Joyella reminded Miller.

Our Analysis:

The Patriotism Paradox: Criticism as a Cornerstone of Democracy

The recent uproar from pro-Donald Trump factions in response to American Olympians voicing concerns over the current socio-political landscape in the U.S. underscores a glaring hypocrisy and a fundamental misunderstanding of patriotism. As the Olympians express their dismay over issues like the unrest in Minnesota and militarized federal raids, MAGA proponents have launched into a predictable yet bewildering meltdown, labeling these athletes as traitors for daring to critique the very country they represent on an international stage.

“Just because I wear the flag doesn’t mean I represent everything that’s going on in the U.S,” stated Team USA Olympic skier Hunter Hess. This sentiment, a nuanced understanding of patriotism, has seemingly flown over the heads of MAGA supporters and their ilk. The knee-jerk reaction from figures like conspiracy theorist Jack Posobiec—who unhelpfully suggested Hess simply “Get out” if he’s frustrated with the U.S.—epitomizes the toxic “love it or leave it” nationalism that poisons rational discourse.

Another skier, Chris Liller, expressed being “heartbroken” over the actions of ICE and the nature of protests, a sentiment that should invoke empathy, not scorn. Liller’s hope that athletes can embody an America striving for respect and love for all its citizens is an admirable and genuinely patriotic stance. Yet, this call for basic human decency is met with disdain from certain quarters that view any criticism as betrayal.

Figure skater Amber Glenn’s refusal to remain silent on political matters drew particular ire from Megyn Kelly, who branded her a “turncoat.” This reaction starkly contrasts with the support from critics of the MAGA movement, who rightly argue that critiquing one’s government is not only patriotic but essential to democracy. Political scientist Ian Bremmer and others remind us that the freedom to dissent is what distinguishes the United States from authoritarian regimes like Russia or China.

The fundamental irony here is palpable: those who proclaim their patriotism the loudest seem to fail to grasp the core values that underpin American democracy. The right to dissent, to demand better from our leaders, and to strive for a country that lives up to its ideals of liberty and justice for all is the bedrock of genuine patriotism. The MAGA response to these Olympians is not just fragile; it’s antithetical to the principles of freedom and democracy.

Kate Miller’s stance that athletes must unequivocally love America or else forfeit their right to represent it at the Olympics is a dangerous proposition. It suggests a blind allegiance that is more akin to authoritarian loyalty tests than the democratic values of free expression and critique. Emmy-winning reporter Mark Joyella’s rebuttal, “That’s not how freedom works,” is a succinct reminder that patriotism and criticism are not mutually exclusive but are, in fact, intrinsically linked.

In this climate of heightened political polarization, the courage of these athletes to speak out, to represent an America that aspires to be better, is a reminder of what true patriotism looks like. It’s not about wearing a flag pin or chanting “USA” the loudest. It’s about striving for a country that all its citizens can be proud to call home—a task that requires introspection, criticism, and, most importantly, active engagement in the democratic process.

The backlash from the MAGA camp is not just an attack on these athletes; it’s an attack on the very ideals that make America great. In their zeal to defend what they perceive as an attack on their country, they trample on the principles of freedom and democracy that define it. It’s a sad irony that in their quest to preserve their vision of America, they undermine the very foundations upon which it stands.
