In the video, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche orders Newark, New Jersey, Mayor Ras Baraka arrested — a directive previously only alleged in court documents.

Former prosecutor Kristy Greenberg joined MSNBC’s “Deadline: White House“ to discuss the stunning video.

“I wish I could have this thing people talk about, being numb or not being shocked anymore. This is shocking to me,” said anchor Nicolle Wallace. “The deputy attorney general of the United States is on the phone with law enforcement, not just talking about an arrest, but stagecrafting it. What happened to Todd Blanche? Why is he involved at that point in the stagecraft?”

“I mean, that’s so far outside what he should be discussing and, yeah, where is the U.S. Attorney at that point?” said Greenberg. “It was Alina Habba. Like, why is Todd Blanche getting involved? It shows she kind of really wasn’t — wasn’t the one leading the show here. It’s coming all the way from the top, the number two at the Department of Justice.”

“I think it really speaks to — there’s an article in The New York Times, you know, recently that there was a passage about Todd Blanche that really stuck out to me and is really going to be cited, I think, by any defense attorney of any of these Democratic lawmakers,” Greenberg continued. “It talked about the fact that, according to the reporting, that Todd Blanche understands that Donald Trump still sees him as his personal attorney, and that he knows his client wanted revenge and not a legal lecture, and so he knew it would be futile to protest too much.”

Not only is this “a complete abdication of any responsibility that he has,” said Greenberg, but it could expose him to legal liability when the political officials he tries to order prosecuted come after him.

“This story, which is all captured on tape, is about law enforcement officers on the phone with the second most powerful law enforcement official in the United States of America, saying, quote, turn off your body cams, we’re walking out of the gates and arresting him. Per the deputy attorney general, right?” Greenberg continued. “And not only that, they then had to drop those charges because the whole thing was bogus. And now you have the mayor of Newark bringing a malicious, you know, vindictive prosecution lawsuit against ... Alina Habba. But maybe after seeing this, he has to add Todd Blanche to the lawsuit.”

Our Analysis:

A Spectacle of Misuse: The Arrest of Newark’s Mayor

The recent surfacing of a video capturing a top Justice Department official, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, in the act of ordering the arrest of Newark, New Jersey, Mayor Ras Baraka during a protest at an immigration detention facility has set the stage for a glaring examination of justice and power misuse at the highest levels. This incident, previously whispered in court corridors through documents, has now been thrust into the harsh light of public scrutiny, thanks to the hard evidence the video provides.

The Unsettling Revelation

In a dramatic reveal on MSNBC’s “Deadline: White House,” former New York prosecutor Kristy Greenberg and anchor Nicolle Wallace expressed their astonishment and concern over the actions of Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche. The video isn’t just troubling for its content—it’s a stark display of how the gears of power can be manipulated, showcasing Blanche not only discussing but also ‘stagecrafting’ the arrest of a Democratic mayor. This is a term that should never find relevance in legal procedures, yet here we are, dissecting its very application by a high-ranking official.

Where Does the Buck Stop?

The involvement of Todd Blanche, second in command at the Department of Justice, in such a blatantly political maneuver, raises immediate and pressing questions about the integrity of the justice system’s leadership. The role of Alina Habba, as highlighted by Greenberg, indicates a deeper issue of leadership and responsibility evasion at the Department of Justice. The fact that Blanche was involved to this degree suggests a troubling bypass of usual protocols and leadership channels, hinting at a potentially systemic issue within the department.

The Shadow of Personal Allegiance

What’s particularly damning in this scenario is the backdrop against which Todd Blanche’s actions are set. As noted in The New York Times, Blanche is perceived by Donald Trump as his personal attorney, a perception that evidently influences Blanche’s professional conduct to the extent that he prioritizes revenge over legal principle. This is not just a lapse in judgment; it’s a fundamental betrayal of the principles that govern legal practice and justice administration.

Legal and Ethical Ramifications

The implications of this incident are vast and multifaceted. Firstly, the directive to “turn off your body cams” is a direct affront to transparency and accountability, cornerstones of law enforcement integrity. The subsequent dropping of charges against Mayor Baraka only adds insult to injury, indicating the baselessness of the arrest in the first place. This entire saga culminates in the mayor’s justified pursuit of a lawsuit for malicious prosecution, a case that now has further ammunition with Todd Blanche’s potential addition as a defendant.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

This incident is a glaring symptom of a deeper malaise within the justice system, where political motivations can sometimes trump the rule of law. It underscores the urgent need for a reevaluation of leadership within the Justice Department, ensuring that those in power are held to the highest standards of integrity and impartiality. The misuse of power for political vendettas not only undermines the fabric of democracy but also erodes public trust in the institutions meant to protect it. This is a wake-up call, loud and clear, for systemic reform and accountability at all levels of government.
