White House 'livid' after MAGA senator blindsides with push to oust JD Vance: report

The White House was apparently “livid” after a MAGA senator launched a strategic move to potentially push forward a presidential bid to oust Vice President JD Vance.

The Trump administration was apparently blindsided by Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO), who launched a new anti-abortion group called the Love Life Initiative with his wife Erin, The Daily Beast reported.

“Clearly, Senator Hawley and his political team learned nothing from the 2022 elections, when the SCOTUS abortion ruling [overturning Roe v. Wade] resuscitated the Democrats in the midterms,” an unnamed, close Trump adviser told Axios.

The Trump administration sees the move as a way for Hawley to plan on challenging Vance for the 2028 presidential run. They apparently did not know about Hawley’s scheme until it went public.

President Donald Trump and other Republicans have said abortion rights are not a top concern after the Supreme Court overturned Roe v Wade in 2022.

Hawley, who been a vocal populist and often speaks to concerns among the working class, reportedly launched a dark money group to support the initiative, Axios reported. He and his wife have planned to reinitiate the conversation around abortion and have lined up a series of advertising campaigns, including an anticipated Super Bowl ad.

“We think that there needs to be a ... strong voice advocating for life,” Hawley told Axios.

Trump and other Republicans think that focusing on abortion could be divisive, especially among independent voters and suburban women.

Another Trump adviser told Axios that the 2026 midterm election strategy should have “aggressive action focused on positive gains in the economy.”

“That alone will be the driving force behind the next election,” the adviser told the outlet. “Picking a fight on an issue like abortion in a midterm is the height of asinine stupidity.”

Our Analysis:

The Hawley Gambit: A Strategic Misfire or Calculated Rebellion?

In a move that can only be described as boldly audacious or perhaps recklessly opportunistic, Senator Josh Hawley (R-MO) has thrown the proverbial political grenade into the already volatile arena of GOP internal dynamics. The establishment of the Love Life Initiative, under the guise of championing anti-abortion sentiments, is not merely a declaration of Hawley’s personal vendetta against abortion. It is, more accurately, a glaring beacon of his presidential ambitions, signaling a potential challenge to Vice President JD Vance for the 2028 presidential run. This maneuver has, unsurprisingly, left the White House and, more specifically, the Trump administration, in a state of rabid discontent.

The irony here is as thick as the proverbial political plot. The Trump administration, caught flat-footed by Hawley’s move, expressed their exasperation through the usual channels of anonymity—a “close Trump adviser” lamenting to Axios that Hawley and his team have apparently learned nothing from the 2022 elections. The Supreme Court’s decision to overturn Roe v. Wade, far from being the conservative victory lap it was intended to be, became a rallying cry for Democrats, providing an unexpected resuscitation in the midterm elections.

One must pause here to savor the delicious irony. The Trump administration, known for its own series of blindsides and political maneuvers, now finds itself on the receiving end of a surprise attack. The frustration expressed by the unnamed Trump adviser is not just about being outmaneuvered; it’s a realization that Hawley is playing a different game altogether.

Hawley, a vocal populist who has often expressed concern for the working class, seems to have taken a page out of the Trump playbook, launching a “dark money group” to support his initiative. This move, however, diverges from the current Republican strategy which downplays abortion rights as a central concern, post-Roe v. Wade overturn. The establishment of the Love Life Initiative and the planned advertising campaigns, including a Super Bowl ad, suggest that Hawley is not just trying to reinitiate the conversation around abortion; he’s attempting to reshape the political landscape to his advantage.

This strategy, however, is fraught with peril. Trump and other Republicans believe that focusing on abortion could alienate key voter demographics, particularly independent voters and suburban women. The unnamed Trump adviser’s comments to Axios about the 2026 midterm strategy—advocating for a focus on the economy rather than divisive issues like abortion—underscore the perceived political folly in Hawley’s approach. Describing the move as the “height of asinine stupidity,” the adviser highlights a fundamental rift within the party on how best to secure electoral victory.

Yet, what Hawley’s gambit underscores is a deeper, more systemic issue within the Republican Party. It’s not just about abortion or the 2028 presidential run; it’s about the very soul of the party. Hawley’s move represents a challenge to the status quo, a declaration that he believes the path to power lies not in economic populism alone, but in reigniting the culture wars that have long energized the Republican base.

In conclusion, while the White House may be “livid” and Trump advisers may decry the strategy as “asinine stupidity,” Josh Hawley’s establishment of the Love Life Initiative is a calculated risk. It’s a bid for relevance in a post-Trump GOP, a signal that he’s willing to defy party orthodoxy to forge his path to power. Whether this will pay off or prove to be a political miscalculation remains to be seen. However, one thing is certain: the GOP is in for a turbulent ride as it grapples with its identity in the lead-up to 2028.
