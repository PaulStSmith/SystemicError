Trump torn apart for passing the buck on manufacturing losses

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Tariffs, Blame, and the Old Trick of Governing by Excuse

The Power Is at the Pen

The article’s core fact is simple: Trump chose tariffs as the signature economic move of his second term, and his own Labor Department now reports 75,000 manufacturing jobs lost over that same period. That is not a mystery. It is a policy result. When a president has the authority to impose tariffs with a stroke of a pen, he also owns the economic fallout that follows.

The Blame Game Starts Immediately

Trump’s instinct is to blame Biden, which is the oldest dodge in the modern reactionary playbook: do harm, then insist the damage belonged to the last administration. But the reporting undercuts that spin by laying out the timeline. Trump returned to office in 2025 and made tariffs the defining economic initiative. If prices rise, investment stalls, and manufacturing employment drops after that decision, the causal chain points upward, not backward.

Even the Right Knows the Story

What makes this story revealing is not that a liberal economist criticizes tariffs. It is that Erick Erickson, speaking from the conservative side, rejects the fraud of blame-shifting. He says the public can see that things started to go bad when tariffs became policy and that Republicans cannot keep “passing the buck.” That matters because it shows the excuse has gotten weaker than the damage. When even allied commentators stop covering for the president, the propaganda has started to lose its grip.

Uncertainty as a Weapon

Robert Shapiro’s point is more technical and more damning: tariffs create uncertainty, and uncertainty chills investment, production, and trade. That is not accidental collateral damage; it is the predictable effect of making the economy hostage to impulsive executive power. Trump can reverse course tomorrow, but he will not, because the tariff is not just an economic tool. It is a political posture, a way to perform toughness while forcing workers and businesses to absorb the cost.

What the Story Really Shows

This is a case study in authoritarian economic theater: make a dramatic decision, take the credit if anything good happens, and outsource the pain to enemies, predecessors, or abstractions if it does not. The article’s framing is weak only when it pretends the central issue is whether blame can be politically reassigned. The real issue is that Trump used presidential power to impose a self-inflicted wound and then tried to lie his way out of the consequences.

The Pattern Beneath the Policy

Tariffs here are not just bad economics. They are governance by sabotage followed by narrative control. The point is to keep institutions and the public focused on blame-management instead of decision-making. That is the larger pattern: concentrated power in the executive, deliberate harm dressed up as strategy, and a permanent attempt to make accountability disappear into partisan fog.
