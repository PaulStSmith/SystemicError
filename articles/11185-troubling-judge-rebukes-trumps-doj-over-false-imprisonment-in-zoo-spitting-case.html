'Troubling': Judge rebukes Trump’s DOJ over ‘false imprisonment’ in zoo spitting case

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:A Kafkaesque Nightmare: The Bureaucratic Abyss of the American Justice System

FILE PHOTO: The seal of the U.S. Justice Department is seen on the podium in the Department’s headquarters briefing room before a news conference with the Attorney General in Washington, January 24, 2023. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque/File Photo

A woman accused of spitting on a zoo officer was ordered released from jail, but days later she remained locked up, caught in a bureaucratic tug-of-war that led her lawyer to plead at the end of a court filing: “HELP!!!!”

The Justice Department charged a woman who spat on a National Zoo police officer after she was removed from a staff-only area in the Bird House, wrote Reuters legal reporter Brad Heath. The woman was ordered to be released before trial, and the DOJ agreed with the request.

In the emergency motion for release, the woman's lawyer explained that the woman was detained Aug. 20 over the incident. She was charged two days later, brought to court on Monday, and ordered to be released.

But as of Tuesday, she remained behind bars.

New Mexico civil litigator Owen Barcala pointed to the motion detailing the outreach to the federal authorities to determine why she hasn't been released.

"The United States Marshal in U.S. District Court says they cannot remove a Superior Court Warrant from WALES," said the lawyer. "The US Marshal in D.C. Superior Court referred me to D.C. MPD. AUSA Nestor has been informed of the problem. At this time, Ms. Rios Esquivel remains incarcerated at DC DOC DCDC 394869 in contravention of the Court's release Order."

The lawyer ended the motion with the unusual request: "HELP!!!!"

"Not standard practice to end an emergency motion with 'HELP!!!!,' but I'll allow it in this case," Barcala joked.

The woman's lawyer confessed at one point in the filing, "But the undersigned naively believed that its judicial order compelled the appropriate outcome. This was not the case."

The city jail "has chosen not to comply with this Court’s Order," the lawyer added.

It prompted Barcala to comment, "Federal judges are really going to have to get over this thing with believing the feds will comply with their orders in good faith."

In the Order to Show Cause, U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui wrote, "The Court will not tolerate further delay, especially given there is no adequate remedy for false imprisonment. No later than 1:00 p.m. today, either the Department of Corrections should coordinate with the Department of Justice to withdraw that hold and release Ms. Rios-Esquivel, or else appear at 1:15 p.m. in-person in Courtroom 4 to explain why Ms. Rios-Esquivel has not been released."

"What is especially troubling is that this is not even the first time in the past four months that the Court has encountered this same problem of false imprisonment," wrote Faruqui. And still "the greater question of why it takes moving heaven and earth to ensure a person who is ordered to be released is actually released—in a timely manner—remains unanswered."

Barcala noted that the docket doesn't show any specific update. "So, hopefully, that means she was released after a certain AUSA changed his or her trousers."

Our Analysis:

A Kafkaesque Nightmare: The Bureaucratic Abyss of the American Justice System

In a recent unfolding that reads more like a dark comedy than a legal procedure, a woman accused of the minor crime of spitting on a zoo officer finds herself ensnared in the absurdities of the American justice system. This narrative, as reported by Reuters legal reporter Brad Heath, encapsulates a Kafkaesque ordeal where a simple directive for release transforms into a bureaucratic tug-of-war, leaving the accused languishing in jail despite a court order for her liberty.

The Incident and Its Aftermath

The core of this saga revolves around an incident at the National Zoo, where a woman, after being ousted from a staff-only area, allegedly spat on a zoo police officer. This act led to her being charged by the Justice Department. The narrative takes a turn towards the surreal when, despite an agreement for her release before trial—a consensus that included the DOJ itself—she remained imprisoned due to a convoluted mess of bureaucratic inefficiencies and jurisdictional finger-pointing.

A Plea for Help

The woman's lawyer, caught in this whirlwind of inefficacy, resorts to a dramatic plea in an emergency motion for release: “HELP!!!!” This unconventional cry for aid underscores the desperation and absurdity of navigating a system that seems almost designed to obfuscate and delay. It's a stark reminder of the human cost of bureaucratic paralysis, where the judicial order becomes more of a suggestion than a mandate.

The Heart of the Issue

At the heart of this debacle is a profound disconnection between the various components of the justice system. The U.S. Marshal, the D.C. Superior Court, the D.C. Metropolitan Police Department (MPD), and the Department of Corrections (DOC) become players in a farcical game of bureaucratic hot potato. The accused, Ms. Rios Esquivel, becomes a mere pawn, her freedom contingent on the system's ability to navigate its self-created labyrinth.

Judicial Frustration and Systemic Flaws

The frustration of the judiciary is palpable, with U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui issuing a stern Order to Show Cause. The order highlights a systemic issue that transcends this individual case—the recurrent nightmare of securing the release of a person from unjust incarceration. It's a damning indictment of a system where false imprisonment emerges not as an aberration but as a recurring motif.

The Unanswered Question

What remains troublingly unanswered is the "greater question" posed by Judge Faruqui: Why does it require Herculean efforts to enact something as straightforward as a court-ordered release? This situation exposes a systemic inertia and a disregard for judicial authority that borders on contempt.

The Bitter Irony

The irony that the legal system, ostensibly designed to uphold justice and protect rights, can itself become an instrument of injustice and oppression is bitter and stark. The ordeal of Ms. Rios Esquivel serves as a cautionary tale—a glaring beacon highlighting the need for systemic reform and a reminder of the ease with which individual rights can become ensnared in the web of bureaucratic inefficiency.

In Conclusion

The absurd saga of a woman trapped in the bureaucratic abyss of the American justice system over a minor infraction is a scathing indictment of that very system's failings. It's a narrative that demands reflection and action, for the sake of all those ensnared in similar Kafkaesque nightmares. The justice system, in its current form, seems more adept at perpetuating its own complexities and absurdities than delivering the swift, clear justice it purports to serve.
