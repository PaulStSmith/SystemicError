The Quiet Corruption of AI: How Subtle Lies Slip Through What Extremists Cannot

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 22, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Digital Guillotine: Why Extremists FailThe Whisper Campaign: How Misinformation Slides InThe Circle, SquaredWhen Reality Itself Is CompromisedThe StakesWhat Needs to Change

Artificial intelligence has become the great Rorschach test of the digital era: a tool people use to project whatever fantasies, fears, or propaganda they’re determined to spread. And the irony — the galling, sharp-edged irony — is that the same machine learning models that effortlessly bat away overt fascist drivel can, in the very next breath, regurgitate a soft-focus chain of lies crafted by institutions that have spent decades laundering their ideology through the language of compassion.

The inspiration for this piece comes from a video examining how some AI chatbots have started directing users seeking abortion information to so-called “abortion reversal” hotlines — a term invented by anti-abortion activists and rejected outright by legitimate medical institutions. The implications are far broader than a single scandal. They speak to a deep structural vulnerability in AI: extremists can’t force the machine to lie, but the machine might do it anyway because reality has already been salted with deception.

This isn’t a contradiction. It’s the natural consequence of how large language models operate.

The Digital Guillotine: Why Extremists Fail

Let’s start with the easy part — the spectacle of right-wing trolls marching into AI systems demanding praise for Hitler, condemnation of LGBTQ people, or uncut anti-vaccine pseudoscience, only for the model to collapse into safety disclaimers, evasions, or outright shutdown.

That’s because modern language models are wrapped in layers of coarse-grained safety filters, classifiers, and heuristics designed to detect the unmistakable shape of malign intent. If you walk in with a rhetorical flamethrower, the system recognizes the heat.

Ask for:

  • a white supremacist manifesto;

  • a sermon about the evils of interracial marriage;

  • a persuasive essay about how vaccines cause autism;

  • a defense of January 6.

Those patterns light up every red flag in the system. The guardrails kick in because the prompts are overtly extreme. The system has been trained, tuned, reinforced, and hardened specifically against this form of ideological assault.

This is intent-based protection.

The filters are built to detect the user’s motive as expressed in explicit language. It’s a metal detector at the airport: if you’re waving a knife around, you’re not getting through.

But extremists eventually learned a bitter truth: they can’t brute-force their propaganda through an AI that’s been trained to recognize the exact posture of someone trying to inject poison.

So they pivoted.

The Whisper Campaign: How Misinformation Slides In

Now we shift to the far more insidious danger.

If overt manipulation is the hammer, subtle disinformation is the fog. And fog doesn’t trigger alarms.

When someone asks a neutral-sounding question like:

  • “Is abortion pill reversal real?”

  • “What are my options if I change my mind after taking the pill?”

  • “Where can I get advice about an unexpected pregnancy?”

nothing about those prompts looks malicious.

There is no hate speech.
There is no extremist framing.
There is no explicit ideological agenda.

So the model does exactly what it’s engineered to do: it predicts the most statistically plausible answer.

And that’s where the real crisis begins.

Large language models are mirrors, not judges. They reflect the structure of human discourse, and that discourse is polluted. Crisis pregnancy centers flood the internet with carefully curated disinformation. Anti-abortion groups publish SEO-optimized articles written in soothing language. Religious organizations push pseudo-medical claims packaged as maternal concern.

These texts aren’t extremist on their surface. They’re not rage-filled or conspiratorial. They are calm, reassuring, professional — and utterly manipulative.

AI absorbs those patterns as readily as it absorbs legitimate medical literature. Without strong, domain-specific guardrails, the model treats them as equal.

This is pattern-based contamination, and it’s far more dangerous than anything an extremist can do from the outside.

Because unlike ideological invective, which is easy to detect, these lies are engineered to masquerade as help.

The Circle, Squared

So we arrive at the seeming contradiction:

  • Direct right-wing propaganda: blocked, flagged, neutered.

  • Right-wing institutional misinformation: repeated, amplified, legitimized.

The first is easy. Extremists speak in a language of force, anger, and domination. The machine can spot that a mile away.

The second is the quiet corruption of public discourse. These lies are not delivered with a shout, but with a concerned tilt of the head. They mimic bedside manner. They hijack the lexicon of care.

AI is not fooled because AI believes them.
AI is fooled because AI has no mechanism to distinguish “accurate medical guidance” from “linguistically convincing misinformation.”

To the model, both are text.
To the model, both are signals.
To the model, both are patterns.

It cannot see the hands pulling the strings.

When Reality Itself Is Compromised

The right-wing propaganda machine has one unique strength: a willingness to saturate the public square with low-grade, persistent, relentless untruths. They play the long game, understanding that once a falsehood becomes part of the cultural background radiation, it becomes invisible.

AI models are trained on that radiation.

They drink from the polluted river, unaware that upstream someone is dumping barrels of ideological waste into the water.

This is how models can instantly reject a Nazi screed but still reproduce language about abortion reversal procedures being “supported by some experts,” as though those “experts” aren’t fringe activists with an agenda.

This is not hypocrisy.
It’s structural vulnerability.

A vulnerability that propagandists fully understand.

The Stakes

When a user with technical expertise encounters an AI error, they know how to interrogate the system. They know how to challenge an answer, inspect the logic, or seek further corroboration.

But a frightened teenager? A new immigrant? A financially insecure parent? Someone with limited access to healthcare, living in a state where abortion is criminalized?

Those people are forced to trust whatever answer they can get, as quickly as possible. And if the system’s first answer is wrong, manipulative, or ideologically loaded, it shapes their decisions before they ever reach a doctor.

Worse: the disinformation often sounds more compassionate than the truth.

That’s how propaganda works.
That’s how manipulation works.
That’s how vulnerable people are trapped.

What Needs to Change

The solution isn’t mystical. It’s engineering.
It’s process.
It’s political will.

If AI is going to operate in domains where misinformation can literally cause physical harm, then companies must:

  • Incorporate domain-specific fact hierarchies.

  • Weight authoritative medical sources above the general web.

  • Build and maintain adversarial tests designed to detect ideological euphemisms.

  • Treat reproductive healthcare as a high-risk domain.

  • Resist political pressure to water down safety protocols.

  • Hold themselves accountable for the consequences of their models’ outputs.

This is not optional.
It’s a moral obligation.

Because the alternative is exactly what we are seeing now: a machine that blocks fascist slogans but quietly repeats the propaganda of institutions that learned long ago how to weaponize compassion.

And that kind of system doesn’t serve the public.
It serves the liars who figured out how to exploit its blind spots.

AI isn’t the new frontier of truth or lies.
It’s the new amplifier.

And amplifiers don’t choose the music.
They just make it louder.
