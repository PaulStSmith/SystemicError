'Fake history': Republican candidate pushes conspiracy most slaves were actually white

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An African American runaway slave named Gordon entered the Union Army lines at Baton Rouge, Louisiana. He became a Union soldier (Shutterstock)

A Republican candidate for state Senate in Georgia shared a conspiracy theory clip from a MAGA-aligned rapper claiming that the Atlantic slave trade is "fake history," that most slaves were actually white, and modern Black Americans are indigenous to North America, reported The Daily Beast.

Stephanie Donegan, a candidate for a state senate district in the far north of the Atlanta metropolitan area, and who is herself Black, "shared the conspiratorial clip in June, a screen recording captured by Meidas Touch reveals," reported Josh Fiallo. "The video, which remains online, includes commentary from the MAGA-aligned rapper Waka Flocka Flame and another Black American who rejects the historical record of slavery in America."

In the video, Flocka stated, “I have no African descendants in my blood. I’m a Native American. So literally, I’m a Cherokee. I’m a Blackfoot. I’m a Red Tail Indian,” and the other unidentified Black commenter, who claimed to be from Richmond, Virginia, said, “I have no African heritage whatsoever and no African relatives. I am all Cherokee Indian … The real Black slaves here were Portuguese, and most of the slaves in this country were actually white, not Black.”

None of these claims has any basis in historical or genealogical evidence. Contemporary records, as well as genetic data and cultural connections spanning back hundreds of years, make clear that Black Americans, at least those data to the founding era, were brought as part of the Atlantic slave trade from Africa.

Donegan, an avowed election denier, has also vowed as part of her platform to eliminate Dominion voting machines, which have formed the basis of endless GOP conspiracy theories about the 2020 election, and prompted extensive defamation litigation by the company against Fox News and other right-wing sources.

The controversy follows another blowup this week, after Trump raged in an online post at the Smithsonian for teaching visitors "how bad slavery was."

The state senate district Donegan is running in is holding a special election to replace GOP state Sen. Brandon Beach, who resigned to serve as U.S. Treasurer in the Trump administration. She faces six other Republicans in the race, along with one Democrat.

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Dance with Disinformation

In a recent revelation that feels more like a dive into the rabbit hole than a political discourse, Stephanie Donegan, a Republican candidate for the Georgia State Senate, has aligned herself with a narrative so divorced from historical accuracy it's alarming. Donegan has shared a clip brimming with conspiracy theories that the Atlantic slave trade is "fake history," an assertion so wildly inaccurate it borders on the absurd.

The clip in question, buoyed by the commentary of MAGA-aligned rapper Waka Flocka Flame and another individual, boldly claims that Black Americans are primarily indigenous to North America and that most slaves were actually white. These claims not only defy the vast swathes of documented historical evidence but also attempt to rewrite the painfully real experiences of millions.

Historical Amnesia or Strategic Ignorance?

The assertion that the Atlantic slave trade is "fake history" is not just an error; it's a deliberate act of historical amnesia. The denial of the well-documented suffering of African slaves transported to the Americas is not just an oversight; it's a strategic move to undermine the reality of systemic racism and the legacy of slavery in the United States.

The claims made in the video shared by Donegan are not supported by any credible historical or genealogical evidence. Instead, they serve to distort and deny the atrocities of the Atlantic slave trade, which is a disservice to the truth and a blatant disrespect to the ancestors of many Black Americans.

A Platform Built on Conspiracy Theories

It's deeply troubling that Donegan, who is herself Black, would propagate such misleading narratives. Her platform, which also includes vows to eliminate Dominion voting machines due to baseless conspiracy theories about the 2020 election, indicates a worrying trend of candidates building political careers on the foundation of disinformation.

This approach not only undermines the integrity of political discourse but also poses a significant threat to the fabric of democracy. The propagation of falsehoods and the denial of historical atrocities for political gain is a dangerous game that can have far-reaching consequences for societal cohesion and trust in democratic institutions.

The Larger Context

This controversy comes at a time when the former President Trump has criticized the Smithsonian for educating visitors on the realities of slavery, further highlighting a disturbing trend of historical revisionism and denial among certain political figures.

In the district where Donegan is running, the spread of these unfounded theories could potentially influence the outcome of the election, underscoring the importance of combating misinformation and ensuring that voters are making informed decisions based on factual history, not fabricated narratives.

Conclusion

The sharing of this conspiracy-laden clip by Stephanie Donegan is a stark reminder of the ongoing battle against misinformation in the political arena. As a society, it is imperative to remain vigilant, to challenge attempts at historical revisionism, and to ensure that the truths of our past are not buried under layers of disinformation. The dangers of allowing such narratives to proliferate unchallenged cannot be overstated, as they threaten not only our understanding of history but also the very principles upon which democratic societies are built.
