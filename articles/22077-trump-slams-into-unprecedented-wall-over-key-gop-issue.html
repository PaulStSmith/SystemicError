Trump’s Crime Brand Is Breaking

The Poll Numbers Aren’t the Story. The Power Is.

A recent YouGov Economist survey found 50 percent disapprove of Donald Trump’s handling of crime, with Trump at 13 percent among independents and only 24 percent saying the country is headed in the right direction. Mike Madrid’s reading is blunt: the old Republican crime advantage is weakening, and Trump’s credibility problem is dragging the party with it. That is the context. The political meaning is sharper.

The Same Machine, New Costume

Crime has long been one of the Republican Party’s most reliable fear engines. Trump is now trying to hold that line with the same tired staging: cops in promotional videos, immigration talk, and the usual performance of control. But performance is not power. The actual institutional power here sits with Trump and the Republican apparatus that keeps recycling punishment politics after the public has already seen the trick.

The Problem Is Not Confusion

Madrid is right to call this a credibility issue. That matters because it punctures the excuse that Trump’s failures are just bad communication or unfortunate timing. They are not. When a president is this weak with independents and still leaning on crime and immigration as his fallback, the message is not policy. It is panic. Republicans are not leading a governing project; they are trying to repackage authority they no longer command.

Optics for the Base, Damage for Everyone Else

The videos with cops are not evidence of order. They are evidence of dependence on symbols because the substance is collapsing. Trump’s own standing is now so shaky that even core Republican issue-framing is getting dragged down with him. Madrid notes that Republicans are reverting to “the oldies but goodies” because they have nothing else. That is what political depletion looks like: the resort to the oldest fear script in the book, even as it loses force.

The 2018 Warning Is Still There

Madrid’s comparison to 2018 matters because it places today’s deterioration in a larger pattern. Republicans lost 40 seats then while leaning on the same themes. Now the environment is worse. That suggests the problem is not just Trump’s personal brand. It is the party’s dependence on grievance politics that can mobilize fear but cannot sustain legitimacy. When crime becomes a slogan rather than a public-safety strategy, it eventually stops working and starts exposing the emptiness underneath.

The Larger Pattern

This story is not about one bad poll. It is about a ruling coalition that keeps trying to govern through threat narratives after its credibility has eroded. Trump still has the power of the presidency, but not the trust required to use it convincingly. Republicans still have the machinery of law-and-order politics, but less and less authority to make it believable. That gap is the real story: not a sudden lapse in messaging, but the collapse of a cynical political formula that depended on fear staying stronger than memory.
