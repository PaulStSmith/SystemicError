'Disconcerting': Ex-White House cardiologist deeply skeptical of 'weird' Trump MRI reveal

By Paulo Santos

A former Bush administration cardiologist questioned the Trump administration over its report Monday that the president is in “excellent health.”

Dr. Jonathan Reiner, the late former Vice President Dick Cheney‘s cardiologist for nearly 30 years, pushed back on White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt‘s claims that President Donald Trump‘s MRI was apparently “preventative” during an interview with CNN.

“Well, there really is no preventative cardiac MRI,” Reiner said. “This is not a standard test for, you know, an 80-year-old man to undergo advanced imaging. Of course, the whole note has kind of a weird, defensive, you know, evasive tone to it. First of all, this is not part of the president’s comprehensive physical examination.”

Following two physical exams, the White House revealed during the summer that the president was diagnosed with chronic venous insufficiency.

“He had that in April, and then he underwent some more testing in July,” Reiner said. “If you look at his first administration, the president, like most presidents, only underwent one comprehensive physical exam every year. So this comes completely off-cycle. Second, it’s filled with euphemisms. Again, Dr. Barbara Bell, the president’s physician, states that he underwent advanced imaging. Well, what specific advanced imaging did the president have? Was it an MRI, as the president said? Was it a CT [scan]? Did he have both? Why not just spell it out?”

Reiner explained how the administration could have his doctor or health official describe what happened.

“It’s as if a patient came in for a chest x-ray, and then I only told people that the patient underwent simple radiologic imaging. Just, you know, just spell it out,” he added. “This is, again, not part of a comprehensive screening, particularly, you know, abdominal imaging. Why would someone undergo a preventive, quote, preventative MRI imaging or CT imaging of the abdomen? This obviously was performed in response to some clinical concern, which is fine.”

The MRI could have been out of a health concern — and the American people deserve to know that, Reiner explained.

“Things happen to people as, you know, as we all get older. And the president is almost 80. So instead of this kind of evasive, almost laughable kind of note. Just spell out what happened. I hope the imaging is normal and great. That would be excellent news. But this kind of piece-by-piece, drip-by-drip release of information is disconcerting,” Reiner said.

Our Analysis:

The Evasive Art of Political Health Disclosures

In the latest theatrical installment of political opacity, the Trump administration’s report on the president’s health has sparked not just curiosity, but outright skepticism and criticism. Dr. Jonathan Reiner, a name with considerable heft in the cardiology realm, having served the late former Vice President Dick Cheney, has called into question the veracity and transparency of the administration’s claims regarding President Donald Trump’s health.

The Alleged “Preventative” MRI

The heart of the issue lies in the purportedly “preventative” cardiac MRI that Trump underwent. Dr. Reiner’s critique is as sharp as it is factual: “Well, there really is no preventative cardiac MRI.” This statement alone dismantles the administration’s narrative with the precision of a surgeon’s scalpel. It’s a clear signal that what’s being fed to the public is, at best, a diet of half-truths designed to obfuscate rather than illuminate the president’s health status.

A Pattern of Evasiveness

The administration’s handling of Trump’s health disclosures is a masterclass in evasiveness. Firstly, the timing and nature of the MRI scream irregularity. As Reiner points out, this sort of advanced imaging is far from standard for an octogenarian without specific clinical indications. Secondly, the language used in the health disclosures is riddled with euphemisms and vagueness. The term “advanced imaging” is so broad it’s essentially meaningless without further specification — was it an MRI, a CT scan, both, or something else?

The Issue of Transparency

The Trump administration’s reluctance to spell out the specifics of the president’s health examinations is not just a minor oversight; it’s a glaring red flag. The American people are not asking for a medical degree’s worth of detail, but rather, the basic transparency that comes with understanding the health of the nation’s leader. Dr. Reiner’s analogy of a patient undergoing a chest x-ray only to have the process described as “simple radiologic imaging” hits the nail on the head. It’s an almost comedic level of vagueness that serves no one.

Why It Matters

The handling of Trump’s health disclosures is emblematic of a broader issue within his administration: a chronic aversion to transparency. When it comes to the health of a sitting president, especially one of advanced age, the stakes are too high for evasive maneuvers and half-truths. Dr. Reiner’s call for straightforwardness is not just about medical ethics; it’s about the fundamental right of the American people to be informed about the wellbeing of their commander-in-chief.

Conclusion

In summary, the Trump administration’s report on the president’s health, as critiqued by Dr. Reiner, is a textbook example of how not to manage health disclosures for a public figure. It’s a mixture of evasion, half-truths, and a troubling lack of detail that does nothing but fuel speculation and concern. The American people deserve better. They deserve transparency, honesty, and respect. It’s high time the administration took a dose of its own medicine and started treating these disclosures with the seriousness they warrant.
