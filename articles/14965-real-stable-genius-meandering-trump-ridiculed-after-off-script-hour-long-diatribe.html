'Real stable genius?' Meandering Trump ridiculed after off-script hour-long diatribe

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks next to U.S. Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., during a press conference in the Roosevelt Room at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 12, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump spoke for a little under an hour on Monday morning about the ongoing trade negotiations with China, the costs of pharmaceuticals, and other matters that left some viewers with many questions.

One professor addressed Trump's claim on Obamacare: “I made it work. I could have let it die, but I chose to make it work.”

They posted a screen capture of the late Sen. John McCain (R-AZ) giving the deciding vote to stop the repeal of the Affordable Care Act in Aug. 2017.

Sports television producer Joe DiGiacomo responded by trashing Trump using a clown emoji and saying Trump "takes credit for John McCain saving the ACA in 2017. What a pathetic human being spitting on the grave of one of the best senators in our history."

The media monitoring group Patriot Takes also posted a fact-check, "No, Trump tried to kill Obamacare and John McCain saved it."

Advisor to Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT), Faiz Shakir, brought up Trump's discussion about "Big Pharma" and high drug prices in the United States. Trump said that a friend told him about the U.S. paying higher for drugs than other countries. Learning of the problem, Trump said he intends to do something about it.

"Some of you may have thought that Trump wanted lower drug prices for Americans. Wrong. He wants higher drug prices for Europeans and others — and higher profits for drug companies," wrote Shakir on X.

When Trump pointed to the "friend," he described him as a "businessman" who told him about the "fat shot drug" being significantly cheaper in Europe than the U.S. Trump said that the friend was a "very, very, very top guy" who is also "highly neurotic" and "brilliant."

Moms Demand Action founder Shannon Watts asked, "Does his name rhyme with Treelon Dusk?"

"Is he saying Elon takes Ozempic?" asked Patriot Takes.

"That Gay Guy Candle Co.," a liberal scented candle company known for trolling conservatives on X, asked, "Is the friend on our screen?"

Daily Beast columnist Julia Davis asked if the friend was "John Barron." It's a name that Trump frequently used as an alias when speaking to the press.

More Perfect Union senior director Josh Miller-Lewis posted, "Trump's executive order isn't designed to lower drug prices in America. It's designed to raise prices in other countries and boost pharma profits."

Trump told the press on drug prices, "We're getting them down 60, 70, 80, 90%. But actually, more than that, if you think about it in a way mathematically."

Tahra Hoops, director of economic analysis at the Progressive Chamber, keyed in on Trump's comment, saying, "I don't think we have thought about anything mathematically this entire admin."

"Real stable genius?" quipped content creator Mike S. Kim.

"Trump has rapidly advancing dementia, and I'm really thankful for that. Could you imagine if he had the kind of acumen he possessed back in the 1980s? So yeah, I'll enjoy the day now," said historian Daniel B. Kurz.

Our Analysis:

Dissecting Trump's Claims and the Public's Reaction

President Donald Trump's recent statements on a slew of issues, from the Affordable Care Act (ACA) to pharmaceutical pricing, have sparked a mixture of outrage, mockery, and skepticism. His remarks demonstrate a baffling blend of revisionist history and peculiar assertions that merit a closer examination.

Obamacare and the Ghost of McCain

Trump's audacious claim that he "made [Obamacare] work" is a stark revision of historical events that cannot be overlooked. The late Sen. John McCain's decisive vote against the repeal of the ACA is a well-documented moment of political courage that preserved health care for millions. To suggest that Trump somehow salvaged Obamacare is not only a distortion of facts but a glaring disrespect to McCain's legacy. This isn't just about taking undue credit; it's a complete rewriting of a critical chapter in America's healthcare saga.

Comments from figures like Joe DiGiacomo and organizations like Patriot Takes underscore the absurdity and perceived disrespect in Trump's statement. The public's reaction, ranging from disdain to outright mockery, reflects a broader awareness and rejection of Trump's attempt to warp reality.

Pharmaceuticals: A Misguided Approach

Trump's discourse on drug pricing further illuminates his administration's skewed priorities. His anecdote about a "very, very, very top guy" discussing drug prices reveals more than just a conversation. It exposes a fundamental misunderstanding of the pharmaceutical landscape and a perverse approach to "solving" the issue of high drug prices in the U.S.

The notion, as Trump suggests, that the goal is to increase drug prices abroad to level the playing field, rather than making medications more affordable for Americans, is both morally and economically misguided. This strategy doesn't aim to alleviate the burden on U.S. consumers but rather to inflate profits for pharmaceutical giants. Josh Miller-Lewis's critique hits the nail on the head, emphasizing the executive order's real intention: boosting pharma profits at the expense of global affordability.

Mathematical Fantasies and Questionable Acumen

Trump's claim of reducing drug prices by "60, 70, 80, 90%" is a puzzling boast that lacks any grounding in reality. Tahra Hoops's remark on the administration's failure to "think about anything mathematically" is a pointed critique of Trump's penchant for hyperbole over substantive policy analysis. This pattern of outlandish assertions without factual basis is emblematic of an administration more concerned with spectacle than with solving real issues.

The speculation around Trump's mental acuity, as mentioned by Daniel B. Kurz, adds another layer of complexity to the discussion. The suggestion that Trump's current state might be a silver lining, as it spares the nation from a more competent execution of his policies, is a darkly humorous yet troubling reflection on the state of American leadership.

Conclusion: The Theatre of the Absurd

In analyzing the reactions to Trump's statements, it's evident that public discourse has veered into the realm of the absurd. Figures like Shannon Watts and organizations such as That Gay Guy Candle Co. engage in a blend of satire and skepticism that underscores the bizarre nature of Trump's presidency. The reference to "John Barron" – a pseudonym Trump has used – by Julia Davis, serves as a metaphor for the fabricated reality Trump often resides in.

The overarching theme in the response to Trump's remarks is a collective incredulity and concern. The blend of factual distortion, ethical dubiousness, and economic folly presents a presidency that seems increasingly detached from the realities of governance and the needs of the American people. As we dissect Trump's claims and the public's reaction, it becomes clear that the current administration operates within a self-constructed narrative far removed from the pressing issues facing the nation.
