'Unprecedented': Ex-GOP insider flags 'evidence' Trump 'thinks his supporters are garbage'

According to Steve Schmidt, an independent political strategist who worked on former President George W. Bush's campaign, Trump recently showed his hate for MAGA.

"The 35-word presidential oath, first sworn by George Washington, obligates the president of the United States to 'preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of the United States.' Donald Trump has desecrated the US Constitution. He has been aided in his treachery by an unprecedented assemblage of sycophants, extremists, opportunists and appeasers, who have become detached from the responsibilities that come with the privileges of office in a democratic republic," he wrote. "Donald Trump has attacked dissent, facts, science and basic decency that have revealed him for what he is — and has always been."

After calling Trump a "fascist" and "buffoon," Schmidt said, "Trump is president of the United States. He has assaulted the cornerstones of the American civilization for over a decade. He has eradicated over that short time the attachment of scores of tens of millions of people to the basic conception of their rights and liberties."

He then added, "He has deluded his aggrieved and angry mobs into believing that the masked men have boundaries that protect them, but the truth is that Trump thinks his supporters are garbage."

"He disdains them," Schmidt said, pointing to the "evidence" for his claim.

"The evidence of this is Ghislaine Maxwell’s looming pardon. The abused girls were the anonymized and faceless children of the invisible people in America. They are the ones who get paper straws, lectures from the media, 32% interest rates on their credit cards, absurd tuitions, higher prices and less opportunity. Trump promised to stand up for those people, but of course, like every deal that he has ever made it was, well, bull----," Schmidt wrote. "What Trump has assembled around him are the most absurd and dangerous group of American people who have ever held political power in America."

The political strategist alleges, "They have opened concentration camps. They are torturing human beings behind the walls on direct orders from government officials. They have sold human beings to a foreign dictator, who has abused them with cruel and inhumane punishments."

"They have lied to the American people about big things and small. They have targeted political opponents, and abused their power to seek revenge against their enemies," he then added. "They have used their positions to enrich themselves with corrupt acts all over the world that have sold out the national interest for personal gain."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Accusations Against Trump: A Deep Dive into "Unprecedented Treachery"

In a recent tirade that can only be described as both a scathing indictment and a dire warning, Steve Schmidt, an independent political strategist with credentials from the conservative camp, has leveled charges against Donald Trump that, if true, paint a picture of a presidency and a political movement in deep moral and constitutional crisis. Schmidt's accusations are broad, encompassing, and if one is to analyze them critically, alarmingly reflective of deeper systemic issues within the current political landscape of the United States.

Trump's Alleged Disdain for His Own Supporters

Schmidt's central thesis—that Trump exhibits a "disdain" for his own supporters—is a bold claim, yet it's substantiated with what he considers to be compelling evidence. The potential pardon of Ghislaine Maxwell, against the backdrop of her alleged crimes, serves as a stark illustration of this disdain. Schmidt argues that the victims of Maxwell's crimes represent the "invisible people" in America, the very demographic Trump professed to champion. The implication here is not just of hypocrisy but of a profound betrayal, where the most vulnerable were used as pawns in a larger game of power and influence. This isn't just political opportunism; it's moral bankruptcy at its most blatant.

The Constitution: A Casualty of Convenience

Schmidt’s accusation of Trump's desecration of the US Constitution is a grave charge, one that echoes the fears of many observers about the erosion of democratic norms. The presidential oath to "preserve, protect, and defend the Constitution of the United States" is not merely ceremonial; it's a sacred vow to uphold the country's foundational principles. Yet, according to Schmidt, Trump's tenure has been marked by an unprecedented assault on these very cornerstones—dissent, facts, science, and basic decency. This isn't just failure; it's active destruction, a willful dismantling of the guardrails of democracy.

A Coterie of Complicity

Perhaps what's more disturbing than the actions of Trump, according to Schmidt, is the ecosystem of "sycophants, extremists, opportunists, and appeasers" that has enabled and even encouraged this behavior. This isn't merely about one man's actions but about a broader system that has allowed, and in some cases, facilitated, these transgressions. The allegations of opening concentration camps, torturing human beings, and selling out national interests for personal gain point to a regime that not only flouts ethical norms but potentially engages in criminal behavior. This isn't politics as usual; it's a descent into authoritarianism, cloaked in the guise of nationalistic fervor.

Conclusion: A Call to Action or a Descent into Despair?

Steve Schmidt's portrayal of the Trump presidency is not just an indictment of one man but a lament for the state of American democracy. The charges of "unprecedented treachery" are a clarion call for introspection and action. Yet, one must ask: Where do we go from here?

The implications of Schmidt's accusations are far-reaching. If even a fraction of these allegations are true, they demand not just condemnation but decisive action. This goes beyond partisan politics; it's about the soul of the American experiment. The real tragedy, perhaps, is the portrayal of a populace so divided, so ensnared in the machinations of political tribalism, that it may very well be incapable of the collective action necessary to address these fundamental breaches of trust and decency.

In sum, Schmidt's essay isn't just an attack on Trump; it's a somber reflection on the state of American democracy—a democracy that appears to be teetering on the brink. The real question it raises is not just about the legacy of one presidency, but about the resilience of American democratic institutions. Will they bend, or will they break? Only time will tell, but the clock, undoubtedly, is ticking.
