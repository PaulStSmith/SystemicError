'It's so cringe': Critics say Trump has made US a 'banana republic' with one economic move

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 12, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Trump Gold Card: A Gilded Path to American Residency

FILE PHOTO: President Donald Trump attends a celebration of the Army’s 250th birthday on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., U.S,, June, 14, 2025. Doug Mills/Pool via REUTERS/File Photo

Donald Trump’s recent economic decisions have turned the US into a “banana republic” according to notable critics of the administration.

The introduction of the Trump Gold Card, an investor visa which would grant a residency permit to any individual who donates $1million to the government, has been roundly criticized by members of the public. A donation to the Department of Commerce of $1million is required for individuals while a $2million donation is required for a corporation or a donation made on behalf of another person.

Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had claimed in June that there was a waiting list of over 70,000 people. The White House has since made the Trump Gold Card official, announcing the scheme Wednesday. A post to X from the White House account reads, “THE TRUMP GOLD CARD. Unlock life in America.”

The X account Republicans Against Trump wrote, “The United States under Trump is officially a banana republic. Great job, everyone.” USA Polling also weighed in on the card, adding, “It’s all so cringe.”

Political commentator Ron Filipkowski also aired his concerns on the Trump Gold Card. He wrote, “Everything in the US is for sale to the wealthy all over the planet, whether friend or foe. Citizenship, work visas, security guarantees, key industries, advanced military capabilities, AI and sensitive tech. For the right price, anyone can have whatever they want from us.”

A description of the Trump Gold Card on the official application website reads, “For a $15,000 DHS processing fee and, after background approval, a contribution of $1million, receive U.S. residency in record time with the Trump Gold Card.”

A “tax exemption” card named Trump Platinum Card is also available on the official website for Gold Card applications. The Platinum Card is listed as “coming soon”.

The Trump Platinum Card description reads, “Foreign nationals can sign up now and secure their places on the waiting list for the Trump Platinum Card. When launched, and upon receipt of a $15,000 DHS processing fee and $5 million contribution, they will have the ability to spend up to 270 days in the United States without being subject to U.S. taxes on non-U.S. income.”

Application times have been cited as taking just “weeks” according to the trumpcard.gov website. It reads, “Once an applicant’s processing fee and application are received, the process should take weeks. The applicant will need to attend a visa interview and submit any additional documents in a timely manner.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Gold Card: A Gilded Path to American Residency

In what reads like a plot lifted straight from a dystopian novel, the Trump administration has unveiled the Trump Gold Card, effectively commodifying American residency. The move, which astonishingly transforms U.S. citizenship into a high-stakes auction item, has prompted notable critics to label the United States a “banana republic.” A term historically reserved for politically unstable countries where a significant portion of the economy is controlled by a small elite, it seems more fitting now than ever.

The Scheme: For a mere $1 million donation to the Department of Commerce, or $2 million if it’s a corporate contribution or on behalf of someone else, the wealthy can bypass the traditional immigration pathways that millions wrestle with annually. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s announcement of a waiting list of over 70,000 people is as shocking as it is revealing. It underscores a blatant disregard for the principles of equality and fairness, hallmarks of the American Dream, now seemingly up for sale to the highest bidder.

The Backlash: Critics, from political commentators like Ron Filipkowski to groups such as Republicans Against Trump, have voiced their concerns loud and clear. Filipkowski’s assertion that “Everything in the US is for sale” encapsulates the gravity of this development. By placing a price tag on residency (and soon, a tax-free existence via the Trump Platinum Card), the administration has unabashedly prioritized wealth over merit, background, or character.

The Trump Platinum Card: The introduction of a “tax exemption” card is perhaps even more alarming. For $5 million, foreign nationals can enjoy up to 270 days in the U.S. without paying taxes on non-U.S. income. This not only undermines the tax system but also opens floodgates for potential misuse and exploitation, setting a dangerous precedent that could further erode public trust in the immigration system and the principles of fiscal responsibility.

A Banana Republic? The term might seem hyperbolic to some, but the actions of the Trump administration in this instance bear striking similarities to the characteristics of such a regime. Prioritizing the ultra-wealthy, eroding public trust in critical institutions, and commodifying what should be a rigorous and fair pathway to residency are indicative of a system that values money over democratic principles.

The Bottom Line: The Trump Gold and Platinum Cards are not just controversial policy decisions; they are indicative of a broader shift towards oligarchy. They stand in stark contrast to the ideals of fairness, equality, and opportunity that have long defined the American narrative. It’s a shift that should concern not just political analysts and critics but every American who believes in the foundational principles upon which the country was built.

In a world where American residency and tax exemptions can be bought, we must ask ourselves what remains sacred. The integrity of the American immigration system, the fairness of its tax laws, and the accountability of its leaders are at stake. As the Trump administration marches on with its gilded immigration pathway, the echo of the term “banana republic” grows louder. It’s a stark reminder of the systemic error at the heart of these decisions, and a call to action for those who wish to preserve the values that are supposed to make America great.
