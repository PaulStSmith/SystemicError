“I Don’t Read the News”: The Last Refuge of the Complicit

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 28, 20252 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

There’s a curious refrain echoing across America, a soft mumble from people who once chanted “Lock her up!” with chest-thumping pride:
“I don’t read the news anymore.”

It’s not that they don’t care. It’s that they can’t afford to. Not emotionally, not morally. Because to know—to really see the consequences of the monster they empowered—is to be forced into one of two uncomfortable outcomes: own the guilt, or admit you were conned. And let's face it, both are bitter pills for those who once draped themselves in red hats and blind certainty.

Apost from Quora captures it neatly. A Canadian asks her Georgia friend what she thinks of Trump now. The answer? “I wouldn’t know, I don’t read the news.” That’s not ignorance. That’s cowardice wrapped in plausible deniability.

Let’s call this what it is: learned helplessness weaponized as self-defense.

You see, early on, it was all about “draining the swamp,” “bringing back jobs,” “owning the libs.” But when the con became undeniable—when the jobs didn’t return, when the swamp got deeper, when grocery bills rose while billionaires got richer—the faithful didn’t storm the gates demanding answers. They just… unplugged.

Because reality became too sharp, too accusatory. Every headline is a reminder. Every news report, a mirror.

So instead of changing their minds, they changed the channel.
Instead of reading, they retreated.
Instead of reckoning, they recoiled.

This isn’t just political disengagement. It’s a strategic self-blinding. If you don’t look, you can’t see the harm. If you don’t read, you can’t be responsible. And if you don’t acknowledge, you don’t have to apologize.

It’s not that they’ve stopped believing in Trump. It’s that they’ve stopped believing in themselves as competent participants in democracy. The fantasy they bought into has rotted, and now they pretend the stink isn’t there by holding their breath and looking the other way.

And we let them get away with it.

We treat this feigned ignorance with kid gloves. We say, “Well, it’s hard to keep up with the news,” or “Politics is overwhelming.” Sure. But somehow they weren’t overwhelmed when chanting at rallies or sharing conspiracy memes at 3am. The truth didn’t get harder—it just got inconvenient.

So here’s the question we need to start asking:

When does willful ignorance stop being a personal failing and become a civic crime?

Because we don’t need everyone to be political junkies. But we do need people to care enough about the truth to not actively look away when their choices begin to harm others. We need citizens, not ostriches.

And to those now hiding behind “I don’t read the news,” here’s a newsflash:
You don’t get to wash your hands and walk away. You built this. You voted this in. You cheered it on.

You don’t get a mulligan because you closed your eyes halfway through the game.
