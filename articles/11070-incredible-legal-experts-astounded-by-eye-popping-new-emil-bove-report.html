'Incredible': Legal experts astounded by eye-popping new Emil Bove report

A prominent national security lawyer slammed Emil Bove, who was recently confirmed to a lifetime judicial position despite broad concerns from attorneys and former judges, after the embattled Trump loyalist found himself in more hot water.

In the days since Bove was narrowly confirmed to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit despite allegations of judicial malpractice, he has attended meetings at the Justice Department, The New York Times reported, adding the move "raised eyebrows" among legal experts.

Bove should have cut ties with the Justice Department, where he served as associate deputy attorney general beginning in January. By attending functions in the building, he may be forced to recuse himself at a later date after he's sworn in, according to the report.

“The code of conduct for federal judges does not appear to apply to Mr. Bove,” The Times wrote. “Some legal experts said that working for the administration after being confirmed could undermine faith in the court system. Others expressed worry that Mr. Bove could expose himself to potential conflicts, advising Justice Department officials on matters that may eventually land before him as a federal judge.”

National security attorney Mark S. Zaid blasted the Trump administration on X over the report.

"One would have previously ascribed these unforced errors as just mistakes but we're far past that now. This Administration and its senior officials simply don't care about norms, ethics or laws. This is a spiral to the bottom for individual gains, benefits and power," he wrote.

Many others also took issue.

"The only reason for Emil Bove to be still showing up for Trump administration photo opps (in this photo from Trump’s D.C. takeover, peeking out at top right) is that Emil Bove understands himself to be part of the Trump administration on an ongoing basis," wrote Jay Willis, editor in chief of Balls and Strikes, a nonprofit media outlet that covers the courts.

Leah Litman, a University of Michigan law professor, called the report "incredible."

"10/10 no notes. Emil Bove continued to work at DOJ - for the administration- after he was confirmed as a judge. Almost as if he doesn’t think his role, defending stuff Trump did, changed once he became a judge! (sure he wasn’t sworn in but AYFKM???)"

‪Jacob T. Levy‬, a professor of political theory at McGill University, wrote on Bluesky, "'Has raised eyebrows' is the 'racially tinged' of 'critics say.'"

Anthony Michael Kreis‬, a constitutional law professor, wrote on Bluesky, "If he had been sworn in, by definition under federal law, this would be a 'high misdemeanor.' Still, this is egregiously unbecoming for a soon-to-be seated federal judge."

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Confirmation of Emil Bove: A Dive into Judicial Malpractice and Ethical Bankruptcy

In a recent unfolding that could only be described as a circus of judicial malfeasance, Emil Bove, a figure who has clung to the coattails of the Trump administration with the tenacity of a barnacle to a ship's hull, has been confirmed to a lifetime position on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit. This decision, rife with controversy and opposition, sends a chilling message about the current state of our judiciary and the political machinations that undermine its integrity.

The Allegations and the Eyebrows They Raised

Despite the cloud of allegations of judicial malpractice hanging over his head, Bove's confirmation was pushed through, highlighting a blatant disregard for the ethical standards traditionally upheld in such proceedings. The New York Times reports that Bove, in a move that can only be described as audaciously unapologetic, attended meetings at the Justice Department post-confirmation but pre-swearing-in, a decision that 'raised eyebrows' among legal experts. This phrase, however, barely scratches the surface of the impropriety at play. The more fitting description would be 'jaw-droppingly inappropriate.'

A Spiral to the Bottom

Mark S. Zaid, a national security attorney, took to X to voice his outrage, suggesting that these actions are not mere oversights but a deliberate flaunting of norms, ethics, and laws by the administration. Zaid's comments underscore a terrifying descent into a realm where power and individual gains trump the foundational principles of our legal system. The fact that Bove continues to embed himself within the Trump administration, as pointed out by Jay Willis, editor in chief of Balls and Strikes, further cements the perception that Bove views his allegiance to Trump as superseding his duties as a federal judge.

The Scholars' Verdict

The academic community has not held back its disdain and alarm over Bove's actions. Leah Litman's incredulity at Bove's continued work for the administration post-confirmation captures the absurdity of the situation. It's as if Bove believes his new role as a federal judge is merely a title, rather than a position that demands a severance of previous partisan affiliations.

Jacob T. Levy's comparison of the tepid phrase 'has raised eyebrows' to the understated 'racially tinged' illustrates the media's failure to capture the gravity of the situation. Meanwhile, Anthony Michael Kreis points out that, while technically not a 'high misdemeanor' due to Bove not yet being sworn in, the behavior is egregiously unbecoming for a soon-to-be federal judge.

Conclusion: The Erosion of Judicial Integrity

The confirmation of Emil Bove is not just a misstep; it is a glaring testament to the erosion of ethical standards and integrity in our judiciary. It reflects a system increasingly influenced by political loyalties rather than the rule of law and the principles of justice. As the boundary between the administration and the judiciary blurs, one must question the future of impartial justice in America. The actions of Bove and the administration's support of his confirmation are not just 'unforced errors' but calculated moves in a game where power is the ultimate prize, and the judicial system is the playing field. This is not merely a 'spiral to the bottom' but a freefall into an abyss of ethical bankruptcy.
