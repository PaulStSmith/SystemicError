Whistleblowers find $1.7B in payments to Iran from crypto firm whose CEO Trump pardoned

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 24, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: Binance founder Changpeng Zhao, also known as CZ, speaks at the Bitcoin Asia conference, in Hong Kong, China, August 29, 2025. REUTERS/Tyrone Siu/File Photo

A cryptocurrency firm accused of involvement in shady overseas dealings had internal whistleblowers flag $1.7 billion in payments to Iran before President Donald Trump pardoned its CEO, The New York Times reported on Monday.

“People in Iran had gained access to more than 1,500 accounts on the Binance platform over the previous year. About $1.7 billion had flowed from two Binance accounts to Iranian entities with links to terrorist groups, a possible violation of global sanctions. And one of those accounts belonged to a Binance vendor,” said the report. “After uncovering the transactions, the investigators reported them to top executives, according to company records and other documents.”

Last October, Trump granted a full pardon to Binance founder Chanpeng Zhao, who had pleaded guilty to money laundering charges in 2023. Trump claimed, without any evidence, that Zhao was a political target of the Biden administration.

“Internal warnings about the Iranian transactions surfaced last year, in the months before President Trump granted a pardon to Binance’s founder ... who had spent four months in federal prison in 2024 for his role in the firm’s crimes,” the report continued. “The Trump family’s crypto start-up, World Liberty Financial, has forged close business ties with Binance, and Mr. Zhao was a guest this month at a conference at Mar-a-Lago, Mr. Trump’s club in Palm Beach, Fla.”

World Liberty Financial has become a lightning rod for criticism over its conflicts of interest, as the Trump administration and its allies in Congress have moved to establish new regulatory frameworks for cryptocurrency that do little to nothing to oversee the Trump family’s venture.

This is not the only questionable activity Binance has been accused of. A lawsuit filed in November alleged that Binance “knowingly enabl[ed] Hamas, Hezbollah, Palestinian Islamic Jihad and Iran’s Revolutionary Guard to move more than $1 billion through its platform, including more than $50 million after the October 7 attack.”

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Binance Controversy: Unpacking the Allegations and Implications

The recent exposé by The New York Times on a cryptocurrency firm, specifically Binance, and its alleged murky transactions, is not just a story about financial misdealings. It is a glaring spotlight on the intersection of politics, pardon power, and the perilous oversight of the burgeoning cryptocurrency sector. Let’s dissect the key elements of this convoluted saga.

Shady Dealings and the $1.7 Billion Red Flag

At the heart of this controversy is the accusation that Binance facilitated payments to Iranian entities linked to terrorist groups, amounting to a staggering $1.7 billion. This wasn’t just a small oversight or a minor compliance hiccup; it was a potential breach of global sanctions on a massive scale. The fact that internal whistleblowers raised the alarm internally underscores the severity of the situation. Yet, what’s more alarming is the timing and the subsequent actions (or lack thereof) taken by those at the helm of Binance.

The Pardon: A Political Lifeline?

Enter President Donald Trump, who, in a move that was as controversial as it was unexpected, pardoned Binance founder Chanpeng Zhao. Zhao, having pleaded guilty to money laundering charges, found himself a free man, thanks to a presidential pardon granted under dubious claims of political targeting by the Biden administration. This pardon reeks of political maneuvering, especially considering the lack of evidence to support Trump’s allegations.

The Trump-Binance Nexus

The ties between the Trump family’s crypto venture, World Liberty Financial, and Binance are particularly troubling. With the Trump administration pushing for cryptocurrency regulatory frameworks that conveniently sidestep rigorous scrutiny of their own ventures, the conflict of interest is as blatant as it is egregious. The coziness between Zhao and the Trump family, highlighted by Zhao’s presence at a conference at Trump’s Mar-a-Lago club, only adds to the suspicion.

The Bigger Picture: Regulatory Failure and Conflicts of Interest

This saga is emblematic of a larger, systemic issue within the cryptocurrency industry and its regulation—or the lack thereof. The allegations against Binance and the involvement of high-profile political figures illustrate a disturbing nexus that could undermine the integrity of financial regulations and sanctions. The purported ease with which significant amounts of money were moved to entities linked to terrorism is a stark reminder of the cryptocurrency sector’s vulnerabilities to misuse.

Moreover, the close ties between a major cryptocurrency firm and influential political figures raise profound questions about the impartiality and effectiveness of regulatory frameworks. When those in power have vested interests in the very industries they’re supposed to regulate, the potential for conflict of interest is not just a possibility; it’s a certainty.

Conclusion: A Call for Transparency and Accountability

The Binance controversy is not just a scandal; it’s a wake-up call. It underscores the urgent need for transparent, rigorous, and impartial regulatory frameworks for the cryptocurrency sector. The intertwining of political power, financial interests, and legal oversight poses a significant threat to not just the integrity of the financial system but to the very fabric of democratic governance.

As the cryptocurrency industry continues to evolve, the need for stringent oversight cannot be overstated. It’s high time for a systemic overhaul that ensures accountability, transparency, and the safeguarding of the financial system against exploitation by the powerful and the privileged. Without it, the cycle of scandal, controversy, and systemic risk will continue unabated.
