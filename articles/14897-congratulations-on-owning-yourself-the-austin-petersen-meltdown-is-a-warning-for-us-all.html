Congratulations on Owning Yourself: The Austin Petersen Meltdown Is a Warning for Us All

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 15, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A Political Suicide Note in Tweets

In October 2024, Austin Petersen tweeted with chest-thumping pride:

“I'm voting for Donald Trump, to own the libs.”

Less than a year later, in May 2025, he tweeted again—this time, in anguish:

Help fuel the work. Independent analysis doesn’t fund itself:
☕ Buy us a coffee

“Well there went my shoe business. I don't understand why the tariffs have to eliminate the de minimis exemption. Why does government punish small businesses...?”

This isn’t just irony. It’s not even just hypocrisy. It’s the tragic implosion of a man who confused partisan spite with economic wisdom. Petersen’s public unraveling offers a perfect example of how performative politics can backfire—not just metaphorically, but financially, personally, and with brutal finality. When the mission is to "own the libs" instead of protect your own interests, the only person getting owned is you.

Who Is Austin Petersen?

Austin Petersen is a former Libertarian presidential candidate, talk radio personality, and self-styled champion of limited government. He’s long cultivated an image as a provocative disruptor, someone unafraid to speak against the grain. But somewhere along the way, he traded intellectual consistency for Twitter virality. In 2024, rather than advocating for coherent free-market policy, he endorsed Donald Trump with the political depth of a YouTube comment: to provoke imaginary liberal tears.

He cheered Trump's win as a cultural victory, not a strategic one. There was no nuanced economic defense of Trump’s record—just vague allusions to triggering "the left" and reclaiming America from shadowy elites. And it worked, for a while. The likes, retweets, and MAGA kudos came rolling in. But attention is not protection. Clout is not capital. And tweets do not insulate your business from supply chain disruptions or tariff shocks.

The Policy That Torched His Business

So what actually happened to Petersen’s shoe business? The answer lies in a single policy decision with massive implications: the Trump administration's elimination of the de minimis exemption.

This rule allowed small businesses to import goods valued under a certain threshold without triggering heavy customs duties. It was the lifeblood for thousands of e-commerce entrepreneurs and boutique sellers—like Petersen—who relied on low-cost international sourcing to compete with corporate giants.

Eliminating it meant:

  • Multinational giants like Nike and Adidas survive, because they have the legal teams, logistics networks, and deep pockets to adapt.

  • Small businesses get crushed, burdened with new costs, delays, and bureaucratic chaos they are not structured to endure.

The rationale behind the change was old-school protectionism dressed up as patriotism: punish China, support American manufacturers, project economic dominance. But like most populist economics, it lacked precision. The policy didn't just hit Chinese conglomerates—it hit American sellers who happened to import from China, or anywhere else.

This wasn’t an unforeseeable consequence. Experts warned about it. Opponents raised flags. But Petersen, like many others, tuned them out. The cost of owning the libs just showed up on his profit-and-loss statement.

Reaping What Was Sown

Now, Petersen is being ratioed into oblivion.

He’s getting hit from every angle:

  • Leftists are reminding him that economic nationalism isn’t a meme—it’s a mallet.

  • Never Trump conservatives are saying, "Told you so."

  • MAGA trolls are mocking him for whining.

  • Libertarians are calling him a sellout who abandoned principle for clout.

To cope, he’s flailing. He’s trying to portray himself as a tragic martyr, a victim of tribal hostility from both sides. But the internet has receipts. He wasn’t tricked into supporting Trump—he celebrated it. He didn’t get steamrolled by surprise—he rode shotgun on the bulldozer.

Even his attempts to find a scapegoat have failed. In one tweet, he blames leftists and Trumpers for "joining arms" to attack him. In another, he tries to argue he’s more libertarian than Trumpers on economics. But that ship sailed when he supported Trump’s candidacy over a supposedly more economically consistent rival.

This isn’t bullying. This is the democratic equivalent of a feedback loop. When you stoke a fire and it burns your house down, don’t expect firefighters to apologize for not stopping you from lighting the match.

What Does “Own the Libs” Even Mean?

This entire meltdown hinges on the poisonous phrase that has come to define modern conservative politics: "own the libs."

The expression means voting, speaking, or enacting policy not to improve society, but to inflict emotional pain on ideological opponents. It is anti-governance disguised as victory. It rewards performative cruelty, not practical results. It transforms democratic responsibility into internet theater.

People who subscribe to this mindset don’t want debate—they want domination. They don’t want infrastructure or reform—they want viral dunks. The entire point is to watch someone else react, not to build anything meaningful.

But this strategy is a double-edged sword. Governments that rule by spite inevitably target the wrong people. The economic pain doesn’t stay neatly confined to "libs" in coastal cities. It hits red-state farmers, small-town merchants, and self-employed libertarians trying to run a shoe brand.

When you make politics about emotional wins instead of policy outcomes, you get exactly what Petersen is living through: short-term satisfaction, long-term ruin.

Final Thoughts – You Break It, You Buy It

Austin Petersen wanted to troll his way through democracy. Now he's being trolled in return, not by enemies, but by consequences. The tariffs he supported helped destroy his business. The coalition he championed has abandoned him. And the ideology he flirted with has turned into the mob mocking his downfall.

He helped elect a man who ran on economic belligerence, cheered him on when it was abstract, and then cried foul when it became personal.

Let this be a cautionary tale: when your vote is a punchline, don’t be surprised when the joke’s on you. When your political compass is tuned solely to the volume of liberal tears, you’ll never see the wreckage in your own backyard.

And if you’re going to light a fire just to "own" someone else, make damn sure your own house isn’t the first one engulfed in flames.
