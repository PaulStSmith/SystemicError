'Everyone is watching': Analyst fears Trump's readying major Kirk-related purge

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 16, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A person wears a t-shirt with the words, “We are Charlie”, during a vigil in Provo, after U.S. right-wing activist, commentator, Charlie Kirk, an ally of U.S. President Donald Trump, was fatally shot during an event at Utah Valley University, in Utah, U.S. September 12, 2025. REUTERS/Jim Urquhart TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

The nation — and the world — is waiting with bated breath as it expects President Donald Trump to use Charlie Kirk's death to launch imminent crackdowns, a CNN analyst wrote Monday.

"Everyone is watching how Trump and his administration respond to see whether the president will build on his blame for the so-called radical left by moving against specific organizations or political figures," wrote the network's Stephen Collinson.

"This is an administration that never worried about crossing constitutional barriers with its questionable national emergencies that have unlocked vast powers. And Trump has turned the Justice Department into an engine for his own political grievances.

"Anger among conservatives over Kirk’s killing could be channeled into more intensity for Trump’s existing policy goals, like his crackdown on crime in Democratic cities, his push for mid-cycle redistricting to try to stave off GOP losses in the midterm elections, and a federal funding confrontation with Democrats that could shut down the government at the end of the month."

The assassination of Kirk last Wednesday has plunged the United States into a moment of profound political tension, exposing the deep fractures in America's increasingly volatile social landscape, he wrote.

Senator Lindsey Graham (R-SC) over the weekend described it as "an attack on a political movement," noting that "Charlie Kirk is one of the top three people in the country that allowed President Trump to win in 2024 by his efforts."

President Trump's response has been characteristically divisive. "The problem is on the left. It's not on the right," he declared, further inflaming tensions.

This approach stands in stark contrast to calls for unity from other political figures, even including House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA), who urged Americans to "remember that you can disagree with someone vehemently on policy and not hate them as a person," Collinson wrote.

The potential for escalation is palpable, the analyst wrote. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem warned on Fox News Sunday, "It feels like a grief has settled on not just the country, but the entire world. Something has changed." Meanwhile, Trump administration officials like Stephen Miller have claimed there's a "vast, organized ecosystem of indoctrination" by the left.

The investigation continues, with authorities in Utah probing the motives of the alleged shooter, Tyler Robinson. Utah Gov. Spencer Cox has claimed that investigators are examining whether the suspect's personal relationships with a transgender person might have played a role in the killing, though many reports claimed he came from a Republican family and had been radicalized by right-wing ideology.

Social media has come under intense scrutiny in the aftermath. Cox criticized platforms for "hijacking our free will with these dopamine hits," comparing the effect to fentanyl addiction. Elon Musk escalated rhetoric by declaring, "The left is the party of murder" during a far-right rally.

The tragedy has exposed the fragile state of American political discourse, Collinson wrote. As Cox poignantly concluded, "Every one of us has to look in the mirror and decide: Are we going to try to make it better or are we going to make it worse?"

Our Analysis:

A Cloak of Tragedy: The Political Exploitation of Charlie Kirk's Death

In the recent CNN analysis by Stephen Collinson, a stark narrative unfolds, revealing the potential for President Donald Trump to exploit the tragic death of Charlie Kirk as a pretext for draconian measures that threaten to deepen the chasm within the already fractured American social and political landscape. The article, while attempting to navigate these complex waters, inadvertently sheds light on the disturbing trend of political figures using tragedy as a fulcrum for advancing divisive agendas.

Trump's Reaction: A Divisive Strategy

Trump’s immediate response to the assassination, pinning the blame squarely on the "left," starkly contrasts with calls for unity from other quarters, including Republican House Speaker Mike Johnson. This dichotomy not only underscores Trump's characteristic divisiveness but also his seeming indifference to the potential consequences of such polarizing rhetoric. Trump's approach is not merely about assigning blame; it's a calculated move to galvanize his base by demonizing the opposition, a tactic as transparent as it is reckless.

The Blame Game and Its Implications

The CNN analysis rightly points out the administration's history of crossing constitutional boundaries under the guise of national emergencies, a concerning precedent that suggests a readiness to bypass legal and ethical constraints for political gain. The insinuation that Trump might leverage Kirk's death to intensify his crackdown on crime in Democratic cities, push for advantageous redistricting, or instigate a government shutdown, speaks volumes about the administration's opportunistic approach to governance.

A Moment of Profound Political Tension

Collinson aptly describes the assassination as a moment of profound political tension, a reflection of the "deep fractures" in American society. Yet, the analysis falls short of fully dissecting the implications of such a moment. Senator Lindsey Graham's description of the assassination as "an attack on a political movement" and the subsequent portrayal of Kirk as instrumental to Trump's victory are emblematic of the martyrdom narrative being constructed around Kirk—a narrative that risks further entrenching partisan lines at a time when unity is most needed.

The Dangerous Rhetoric of "Us vs. Them"

The article highlights the dangerous escalation of rhetoric, from Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem's somber reflections to Elon Musk's incendiary declaration that "The left is the party of murder." This "us vs. them" mentality, perpetuated by figures of authority and influence, serves only to deepen divisions, making the path to reconciliation increasingly arduous.

The Role of Social Media: A Double-Edged Sword

While the article touches on the role of social media in the aftermath of Kirk's death, it fails to delve into the responsibility these platforms bear in curating content and the impact of their algorithms in amplifying divisive rhetoric. Governor Spencer Cox’s comparison of social media's addictive nature to fentanyl addiction is a poignant observation, yet it barely scratches the surface of the systemic changes needed to address this digital malaise.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Responsibility

As the investigation into Tyler Robinson's motives continues, with unsettling insinuations about his personal relationships and ideological leanings, the tragedy of Charlie Kirk's death becomes a mirror reflecting the ugliest facets of our political discourse. The article, for all its insights, stops short of demanding accountability from those who would use this moment for political gain. It is incumbent upon us, the readers and the electorate, to see through the veneer of opportunism and demand a more responsible, unifying approach from our leaders. In a time of profound grief and division, the true test of our nation's character is not how we assign blame, but how we come together to heal.
