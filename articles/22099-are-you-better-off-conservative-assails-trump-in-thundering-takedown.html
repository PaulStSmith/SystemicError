'Are you better off?' Conservative assails Trump in thundering takedown

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20262 min read

Trump’s Second-Term Ask Is for More Damage

Power, Not Performance

S.E. Cupp’s column does one useful thing: it stops pretending Trump’s failures are accidental. The basic fact is simple. Republicans want to keep control of the federal government after a first stretch defined by inflation, higher gas prices, a foreign-policy crisis with Iran, collapsing confidence, and poll numbers in the low 30s. That is not a case for continuity. It is an admission of institutional capture.

The relevant question is not whether Americans are “better off” in some generic campaign sense. It is who has authority, who used it, and who is asking to keep it after the damage is already visible.

The Damage Is Chosen

The source gives away the governing method. Measles elimination status is on the verge of being lost because of administration vaccine misinformation. Food safety is worsening after oversight agencies were downsized, inspections were cut, and mandatory reporting was stripped back. This is not drift. It is policy.

When a government weakens disease surveillance and food inspection, the result is not an unfortunate side effect. The result is preventable harm. Treating that as mere administrative misfortune launders responsibility for the people who made the decisions.

Blame Is Being Laundered

The usual political trick is to translate deliberate wreckage into a story about disorder, fatigue, or bad luck. Cupp does not fully fall into that trap, but the broader media frame often does. It talks about “messes” and “crises” as if they arrive from nowhere, when the source itself points to concrete actors and concrete acts: vaccine misinformation, reduced oversight, stripped reporting requirements, depleted munitions, and a military and public-health system visibly under strain.

That matters because misdirection protects power. If the public is taught to see sabotage as confusion, the saboteurs get to keep the keys.

Republicans Are Not Asking for Credit

The most revealing line in the source is the simplest one: Republicans are asking for two more years of control of it all. That is the real scandal. Not just that Trump has presided over worsening conditions, but that the party aligned with him still expects voters to hand over more authority after seeing what that authority has produced.

This is not stewardship. It is entitlement. The demand is not for accountability but for permission to continue.

The Real Pattern

The pattern here is authoritarian governance by degradation: hollow out oversight, flood the zone with misinformation, accept the human cost, then ask for another term because the alternative is admitting failure. That is how institutional decay becomes normalized. Not through a single dramatic rupture, but through repeated decisions that make the state less capable, less truthful, and less protective.

The article’s real subject is not whether Americans are “better off.” It is whether a political movement that has already damaged public health, public trust, and state capacity should be rewarded with more control. The answer is no, and pretending that question is even close is its own form of political cowardice.
