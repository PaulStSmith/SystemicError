'This will not work': Trump's idea to expand DC takeover elsewhere flatly knocked down

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 13, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. military personnel walk outside the D.C. Armory after U.S. President Donald Trump’s announcement to deploy the National Guard and federalize the Metropolitan Police Department in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 12, 2025. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

A CNN senior legal analyst on Tuesday afternoon flatly rejected any notion that President Donald Trump could expand his takeover of Washington, D.C.'s law enforcement.

Trump ordered a federal takeover of Washington, D.C.'s police department. On Monday, he said he was placing the city's police force under direct federal control and was deploying National Guard troops to the nation's capital to address crime and homelessness. City officials have pushed back on the move, noting that crime has declined in recent years.

Former federal prosecutor Elie Honig joined CNN anchor Boris Sanchez to discuss the move, with Sanchez asking whether Honig believed Trump could expand his takeover to other cities.

"I wonder what you view as the legal strategy here from the White House as President Trump threatens to replicate this intervention in other major cities like Chicago," said Sanchez.

Honig called this a "very common tactic of this White House."

"The president has declared either emergencies or rebellions or insurrections. In that instance, you talk about in California, in various instances relating to deportation and the Alien Enemies Act, even in instituting the tariffs, he declared an international economic emergency," Honig said.,

"With respect, though, it’s important to understand when we hear the administration talking about using a similar model in Chicago or New York or L.A., this will not work in other cities," Honig warned. "D.C. is legally unique. There’s a specific law that gives the president the ability to take over the Metro police force, and also he can control the National Guard in D.C., unlike in the states, except for an emergency."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Federal Takeover of D.C.'s Police: A Dangerous Precedent?

In a move that has raised more than a few eyebrows, President Donald Trump has ordered a federal takeover of Washington, D.C.'s police department, citing concerns over crime and homelessness. This unprecedented action has sparked intense debate and concern over the potential for similar interventions in cities across the United States. But let's dissect the facts and implications of this maneuver with the precision of a scalpel cutting through the fog of political rhetoric.

Firstly, the legality of Trump's actions in D.C.. Unlike states, Washington, D.C. occupies a unique legal position that apparently allows for such federal overreach. According to former federal prosecutor Elie Honig, speaking on CNN, there exists a specific law that empowers the president to assume control over the Metro police force. This is attributed to D.C.'s lack of the same sovereignty that states enjoy, also reflected in how the National Guard operates under different rules within the district.

However, the notion that this could serve as a blueprint for other cities is, to put it mildly, legally dubious. Honig was quick to point out that such a strategy "will not work in other cities," due to the unique legal status of D.C. The president's authority doesn't stretch as far in states, where he cannot as easily commandeer local law enforcement or deploy the National Guard without declaring an emergency.

Yet, the broader implication of Trump's move cannot be understated. It's a very common tactic of this White House to declare emergencies or insurrections to justify extraordinary measures, from deportation policies to tariff impositions. The danger here is not just in the act itself, but in the precedent it sets and the potential it reveals for the exploitation of executive power.

The pushback from city officials against the federal takeover highlights a critical point: crime in D.C. has been on the decline. This fact raises questions about the true motives behind such a drastic step. Is it genuinely about addressing crime and homelessness, or is there a darker political calculus at play, one aimed at demonstrating a tough stance on law and order ahead of elections?

Moreover, this move by Trump is an alarming display of centralized power. The idea that a president can, with the stroke of a pen, take control of a city's police force should send shivers down the spine of anyone who values democratic principles and local autonomy. It echoes the actions of authoritarian regimes, not a democracy that prides itself on checks and balances.

In summary, while Trump's takeover of Washington, D.C.'s law enforcement may be legally permissible due to the district's unique status, it sets a dangerous precedent. It underscores a willingness to use federal power to intervene in local affairs, potentially undermining state rights and the autonomy of cities. Furthermore, it exemplifies this administration's strategy of declaring emergencies to justify extraordinary actions, a tactic that should concern anyone who values the rule of law and democratic governance.

As we watch this situation unfold, one must question: where does the line between ensuring safety and preserving freedom lie? And more importantly, who gets to draw it?
