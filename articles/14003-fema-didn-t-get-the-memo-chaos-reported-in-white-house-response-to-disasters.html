'FEMA didn't get the memo': Chaos reported in White House response to disasters

Sources revealed a troubling pattern in Donald Trump's second term that has resulted in chaos and delays in approving disaster relief funds.

Current and former officials with the Federal Emergency Management Agency say communication and coordination with the White House and agency leaders has dwindled, which has thrown up obstacles as local managers work to deliver assistance to states hit by winter storms, flooding and other natural disasters, reported CNN.

“This is more than just who gets to tell who," said one longtime FEMA official. "There are regulatory timelines, especially for individual assistance, that are in play, and these delays do affect the delivery of assistance. It is very frustrating to the state and local partners because they think we should be doing things, but without the paperwork we cannot execute on the declaration.”

Arkansas Gov. Sarah Huckabee Sanders, who had been Trump's press secretary during his first term, was forced to publicly and then personally appeal to the president to reverse his decision to deny her state assistance to help recover from tornados and storms that killed more than 40 people in April, and he finally relented and gave his approval on May 8.

"But, again, FEMA did not get the memo," CNN reported. "The agency was not notified by the White House of the approval until May 13 – five days later."

That five-day lag is "unheard of," said another longtime FEMA official.

"It prevents FEMA from fulfilling its statutory roles,” that official told CNN. “It feels like a way to make it look like FEMA is being slow when we’re not yet authorized to act.”

Acting FEMA administrator David Richardson, a Marine combat veteran and martial arts instructor with no prior experience managing natural disasters, insisted he was joking when he said at a meeting Monday that he was not aware the U.S. had a hurricane season, but he said the agency's plan for that would not be publicly released and would instead revert to last year's response plan.

“The most critical currency during response is time," a FEMA official told CNN. "You never get it back, and we just wasted a whole boatload of it on this goose chase."

Homeland security secretary Kristi Noem, whose department oversees FEMA, told lawmakers last month there was "no formalized, final plan” for disaster response during this summer's hurricane season but insisted the administration would be prepared, which a DHS spokesperson told CNN would involve "empowering states to further lead their own disaster response."

But the administration's response to disasters thus far has FEMA officials concerned about the upcoming hurricane season, especially after the White House notified the governors of several red states that assistance had been approved for them long before FEMA was notified.

“It’s one thing for this to happen during these small disasters,” a third FEMA official said. “What if there’s a big one where we are waiting for the green light to mission assign partners to start response? There’s a process for a reason, and if we are left in the dark people are going to suffer.”

Our Analysis:

Dissecting the Disaster: Trump's FEMA Fiasco

In a stunning revelation that should shock no one but somehow still does, reports have emerged depicting a harrowing picture of inefficiency, miscommunication, and outright neglect within the Trump administration's handling of disaster relief efforts. Let's break down this debacle, piece by piece, and shine a light on the systemic failures at play.

FEMA in Disarray: A Symphony of Miscommunication

At the heart of this chaos lies the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), which, under Trump's second term, has become synonymous with delays, confusion, and frustration. Sources paint a bleak picture: communication between the White House, agency leaders, and FEMA itself has not just dwindled—it's practically evaporated. The consequences? States battered by natural disasters are left hanging by a thread, waiting for the go-ahead to receive the assistance desperately needed by their citizens.

One FEMA official put it succinctly, noting that the regulatory timelines for individual assistance are not just numbers on a page; they're lifelines for those affected by disasters. The delays aren't just inconvenient; they're harmful, leaving state and local partners in a lurch, unable to execute crucial relief efforts due to bureaucratic inertia.

The Sanders Saga: A Tale of Tornadoes and Tardiness

Arkansas Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders, formerly Trump's press secretary, found herself in a Kafkaesque nightmare, forced to appeal personally to the president for disaster relief after tornadoes ravaged her state. Trump's eventual approval came too late for many, and even then, FEMA was left out of the loop, notified five days after the fact—an "unheard of" delay that speaks volumes about the administration's disregard for protocol and efficiency.

Richardson's Remarkable Admission

Acting FEMA administrator David Richardson, whose resume apparently lacked any notable experience in disaster management before this role, made a flippant remark about being unaware of the U.S. hurricane season. Joke or not, this admission is emblematic of a broader issue: a staggering level of unpreparedness at the highest levels of disaster response leadership.

The Illusion of Preparedness

Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem's assurances to lawmakers that there was no "formalized, final plan" for the upcoming hurricane season but that the administration would somehow be prepared is as reassuring as a cardboard dam holding back a flood. The strategy, it seems, is to "empower states to further lead their own disaster response," a euphemism for passing the buck and hoping for the best.

The Dire Consequences of Inaction

FEMA officials are sounding the alarm, warning that the administration's failure to effectively coordinate and communicate could have catastrophic consequences, especially with the hurricane season looming. The message is clear: the process exists for a reason, and when those in charge choose to ignore it, people suffer.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure

The Trump administration's handling of disaster relief efforts is a case study in systemic failure. From the top down, there's a blatant disregard for the mechanisms of effective government, a disdain for expertise, and a disturbing willingness to gamble with American lives. It's not just about paperwork or bureaucracy; it's about ensuring that when disaster strikes, the response is swift, coordinated, and effective. Anything less is unacceptable.
