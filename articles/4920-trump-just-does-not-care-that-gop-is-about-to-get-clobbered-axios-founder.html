Trump 'just does not care' that GOP is about to get 'clobbered': Axios founder

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 25, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump attends a meeting in the Oval Office. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

As Donald Trump prepares to give his State of the Union speech, he will do it under a cloud of record-low approval numbers that he seemingly refuses to acknowledge — and that has Republicans living in fear.

But the president doesn’t care, reported Axios’s founder Jim VandeHei on MS NOW just hours before the national address.

Appearing on “Morning Joe,” VandeHei told the hosts that the president has convinced himself that he is doing a great job and will try and make that case in his speech, regardless of the sour mood in the country.

With co-host Jonathan Lemire claiming that it appears inevitable that Republicans will lose control of the House and that Democrats plan to make Trump’s life “miserable” after assuming power, VandeHei said things are far worse for Trump and Republicans than the polls show.

“You guys have painted a pretty bleak picture,” the Axios founder told the hosts. “For Republicans, I actually think it’s worse than what you painted, because it’s not just that he’s losing independents. It’s not just that he’s underwater in terms of his popularity, it’s that you see a massive gap in the enthusiasm among Democrats, which is high, and Republicans, which is low.”

“So he’s losing independents,” he elaborated. “He has an unmotivated Republican Party and a highly motivated Democratic Party. If you look at all of the local and state races over the last couple of months, Democrats are routinely outperforming their performance two years ago. That is a bad, bad sign.”

“I can’t find a single Republican I talked to that doesn’t think they’re not going to get clobbered in November, that maybe even the Senate, which looked like it wasn’t in play, could be in play,” he reported. “And here’s the thing: Trump does not seem to care.

“When I talk to his aides, when I talk to Republicans who are going in there, they’re like, ‘Please, please, please stop talking about tariffs. Stop talking about Venezuela, stop talking about Greenland. Please talk about affordability. We’ve got to win the House. We’ve got to win the Senate.’ And nothing! He just does not care. I think he thinks he’s killing it. And I think you’re going to see that in the State of the Union where he’s going to say we’re crushing it.”

Our Analysis:

The Delusion of Triumph: Trump’s Misguided State of the Union Confidence

In an era where political reality seems more malleable than ever, Donald Trump’s preparation for his State of the Union speech amidst record-low approval ratings is a masterclass in denial and detachment. According to Axios’s Jim VandeHei, speaking on MS NOW, the President is wearing a cloak of ignorance so thick, it’s practically bulletproof. He’s not just ignoring the storm clouds; he’s pretending it’s sunny.

Trump’s refusal to acknowledge his plummeting approval is not just an act of self-deception; it’s an affront to reality. Republicans, it seems, are shackled to the mast of a sinking ship, watching in horror as the captain steers into the storm with a grin. The stark disconnect between Trump’s perception of his presidency and the palpable dread within his own party is more than just political discord—it’s a full-blown identity crisis.

VandeHei’s conversation on “Morning Joe” paints a grim picture for the Republicans, one that’s even more dire than the already bleak outlook many have resigned themselves to. It’s not just a loss of independents or lukewarm approval ratings; it’s about a fundamental lack of enthusiasm within the Republican base, juxtaposed against a highly motivated Democratic opposition. This isn’t just bad news; it’s practically an obituary for the GOP’s 2020 ambitions.

Democrats are not just eyeing the prize; they’re sprinting towards it. With local and state races showing significant overperformances compared to two years ago, the writing isn’t just on the wall—it’s been neon-lit for anyone paying attention. Yet, Trump’s response to this impending tsunami? A shrug of the shoulders and a continuation of policies and rhetoric that only serve to deepen his party’s grave.

The advice from aides and Republican allies falls on deaf ears as Trump continues to harp on about tariffs, Venezuela, and, bewilderingly, Greenland. It’s as if the President is playing his greatest hits to an empty stadium, unaware that the crowd has long since left. “Please talk about affordability,” they plead, hoping to salvage what remains of their electoral chances. But no, Trump is convinced he’s “killing it.”

The delusion reaches its crescendo in the anticipated State of the Union address, where Trump is expected to declare victory in a war everyone else can see he’s losing. It’s not just political spin; it’s a dangerous detachment from the very realities that govern the lives of American citizens.

As Republicans brace for impact, one can’t help but wonder about the long-term consequences of such blatant disregard for public sentiment and internal party health. Trump’s State of the Union might be his swan song, a final act of defiance against a world that refuses to conform to his narrow vision of success. But it’s a song that could very well spell disaster for his party and the nation.

In summary, Trump’s approach to the State of the Union amidst record-low approval ratings is not just a study in stubbornness; it’s a cautionary tale of what happens when leaders become so entangled in their own narrative that they lose sight of the ground beneath them. The GOP’s fear is palpable, and rightly so. They’re not just facing an election; they’re facing an identity crisis with Trump at the helm, steering the ship with blinders on. The storm is here, and denial is no longer an option.
