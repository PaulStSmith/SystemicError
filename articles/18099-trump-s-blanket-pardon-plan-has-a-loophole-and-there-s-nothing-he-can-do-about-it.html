Trump's blanket pardon plan has a loophole — and there's nothing he can do about it

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 16, 20262 min read

Pardoning Corruption: Trump’s Misuse of Executive Power and the Supreme Court’s Complicity

Pardoning Corruption: Trump’s Misuse of Executive Power and the Supreme Court’s Complicity

Unmasking “Pardon-Palooza”

President Donald Trump’s abuse of the presidential pardon authority isn’t just a series of isolated incidents; it’s a strategic exploitation of executive power. Reports indicate he has floated the idea of preemptive pardons for anyone close to him, a promise that reeks of anticipatory obstruction of justice. This isn’t merely a protective measure; it’s an invitation to loyalty through the promise of impunity.

The Legal Shield: Supreme Court’s Role

The Supreme Court, with its Trump-appointed majority, has played a pivotal role in fortifying the president’s pardon power, effectively placing him above the law for certain actions. The ruling in Trump v. U.S. carved out a doctrine of criminal immunity for presidents that, as legal scholar Kimberly Wehle points out, has given Trump a green light to engage in what she describes as an “organized crime spree” within the Oval Office. This decision not only shields Trump from federal prosecution but also emboldens him to extend corrupt offers of legal safety to his allies.

The Joke that Wasn’t

Trump’s suggestion that he might pardon anyone who has been “within 200 feet of the Oval Office” was dismissed by his Press Secretary as a joke. However, the underlying truth is starkly serious. This flippant remark underscores a broader strategy of using humor to downplay corrupt practices, simultaneously testing public reaction and desensitizing his audience to the abnormality and severity of his actions.

State-Level Prosecutions: A Narrow Path

While the Supreme Court has armored Trump against certain federal legal actions, state-level prosecutions remain a viable avenue for holding him accountable. Wehle notes that Trump’s presidential pardons do not extend to state crimes—like those he was convicted of in Manhattan—nor do they shield him from civil liabilities. This is a crucial, if narrow, pathway to justice, contingent on state governments prioritizing law over political allegiance.

A Systemic Insight: Beyond One Presidency

The pattern revealed here extends beyond Trump’s presidency and into the structural vulnerabilities of the U.S. political system. The Supreme Court’s decisions have significant implications, potentially setting dangerous precedents for future presidents. This isn’t just about one man’s corruption; it’s about how institutional powers are wielded and the profound consequences of their misuse. The issue at hand exposes a critical need for robust mechanisms that can effectively check and balance entrenched powers, ensuring that no individual, not even the President, is above the law.

This situation illustrates not only the personal manipulation of power by Trump but also the institutional enablers that provide the framework allowing such corruption to flourish. It’s a stark reminder of the importance of vigilance and active engagement in political processes to safeguard democratic principles from being undermined by those meant to uphold them.
