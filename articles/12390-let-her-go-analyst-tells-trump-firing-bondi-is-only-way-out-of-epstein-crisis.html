'Let her go': Analyst tells Trump firing Bondi is only way out of Epstein crisis

U.S. President Donald Trump, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Attorney General Pam Bondi attend a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 8, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

Donald Trump's decision to prioritize personal loyalty over credibility at the Department of Justice has come back to haunt him in the most embarrassing way possible, a Washington Post columnist wrote.

And he added the only way out of his latest crisis is to let Attorney General Pam Bondi go.

The problem stems from Trump's calculated shift away from the traditional model of independent attorneys general, Jason Willick wrote.

After being "burned by Mueller's Russia investigation” in his first term, Trump deliberately installed "law enforcement leaders who would be not just politically aligned but also personally loyal — even if that meant abdicating norms of honesty and professional responsibility,” the columnist went on.

That strategy has now exploded in Trump's face with the Jeffrey Epstein document fiasco. Unlike former Attorney General William Barr, who possessed "enough independent heft and credibility" to effectively shut down Russia collusion theories, Bondi "has no such credibility in the current Jeffrey Epstein imbroglio," Willick wrote.

The damage was self-inflicted. In February, Bondi turned the Justice Department into a reality TV show, teasing on national television that incriminating Epstein information was "sitting on my desk." She then handed out theatrical binders labeled "The Epstein Files: Phase 1" to right-wing media personalities at the White House — only to later admit there was "nothing new in the public interest to release," Willick recalled.

"This absolute control over the department [Trump] has wanted and has now achieved in Trump 2.0 cuts in various ways, not all helpful to a president in conditions of scandal," observed Bob Bauer, former White House counsel under Barack Obama. A Justice Department "functioning in this way, under his thumb, cannot provide him with the cover a president may need."

The irony is staggering, Willick wrote. While Barr successfully "helped wind down Russiagate," Bondi's status as "a White House lackey makes it hard for her to wind down Epstein conspiracies, especially because she stoked them in the first place."

Political observers suggest Trump faces a brutal choice: "If Trump wants to move on from Epstein, he might need to let go of his attorney general,” Willick wrote.

The alternative — keeping someone who "changed her tune on Epstein" — amounts to maintaining "the cover-up" itself.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Justice Department Debacle: A Tale of Loyalty Over Logic

Donald Trump's penchant for prioritizing personal loyalty over credibility within the Department of Justice (DOJ) has spiraled into what can only be described as a farcical implosion, emblematic of his administration's broader disregard for the principles of justice and transparency. As outlined by a Washington Post columnist, this self-inflicted debacle centers around Attorney General Pam Bondi and the mishandling of the Jeffrey Epstein documents—an issue that starkly illustrates the consequences of Trump's leadership style.

The Strategy of Loyalty

Trump's strategy, post-Mueller investigation, to populate the DOJ with figures not just politically aligned, but personally loyal, marks a deliberate departure from the traditional model of independent attorneys general. This approach not only undermines the foundational norms of honesty and professional responsibility but also sets the stage for the current crisis. The comparison between Bondi and her predecessor, William Barr, is particularly telling. Barr, despite his controversies, had the "independent heft and credibility" to navigate the treacherous waters of the Russia collusion theories. Bondi, on the other hand, lacks such credibility, especially in the wake of the Epstein document fiasco.

The Epstein Embarrassment

Bondi's transformation of the DOJ into a veritable "reality TV show," complete with teased revelations and theatrical presentations of "The Epstein Files: Phase 1" to right-wing media personalities, not only failed to produce anything of "public interest" but also blatantly contradicted her later admission of having "nothing new" to release. This charade is a textbook example of the Trump administration's manipulation of justice for political spectacle, sacrificing substance for show.

The Irony and the Aftermath

The irony of this situation is as staggering as it is damning. While Barr's maneuvers could arguably be seen as having helped "wind down Russiagate," Bondi's overt role as a "White House lackey" and her initial fueling of Epstein conspiracies have rendered her ineffectual in quelling the very rumors she helped propagate. The notion, as suggested by Bob Bauer, that a Justice Department "functioning in this way, under his thumb, cannot provide him with the cover a president may need," underscores the self-defeating nature of Trump's approach.

A Brutal Choice

Trump now stands at a crossroads, facing a "brutal choice": either to dismiss Bondi, thereby acknowledging the failure of his loyalty-first strategy, or to maintain the status quo, effectively perpetuating what has been labeled a "cover-up." The latter option not only exacerbates the DOJ's credibility crisis but also deepens the ethical quagmire in which the administration finds itself.

Conclusion

In essence, the debacle surrounding Pam Bondi and the Epstein documents is not merely an isolated incident but a symptom of a larger, more systemic issue within the Trump administration: a willful disregard for the tenets of justice and professionalism in favor of loyalty and spectacle. This episode serves as a stark reminder of the dangers inherent in politicizing the Justice Department, where the quest for personal loyalty ultimately undermines the very essence of justice itself.
