Loyalty as a Weapon: The Dangerous Rhetoric of Rep. Sheri Biggs

The Power Dynamics at Play

When Rep. Sheri Biggs (R-SC) told Americans who criticize President Trump’s Iran policy to “get out” of the country, it wasn’t just a slip of the tongue or a hyperbolic throwaway line—it was an explicit use of patriotic rhetoric as a cudgel against dissent. In her interview with Newsmax, Biggs capitalized on her platform, not to articulate a reasoned defense of Trump’s strategies, but to delegitimize opposition by questioning the patriotism of critics. This tactic reveals a disturbing trend within certain political echelons where loyalty to leadership is conflated with loyalty to country.

Identifying the Decision-Makers

Biggs, leveraging her status as a Congress member, chose to frame political dissent as un-American, thus positioning herself and, by extension, her party as the sole arbiters of patriotism. This wasn’t a rogue act but a reflection of a broader strategic pattern within her party, aimed at stifling critical discourse and rallying the base by painting the opposition as enemies of the state. Such rhetoric has the potential to influence public opinion and policy, steering the national conversation away from substantive critique and towards divisive identity politics.

Misdirection and Consequences

Biggs’ statement redirects the blame for the escalating tensions with Iran from the policymakers, including Trump, to the American people who exercise their right to criticize. This kind of misdirection serves a dual purpose: it diverts attention from the administration’s geopolitical decisions and suppresses meaningful debate about the implications of those decisions. The consequences are twofold: it not only undermines democratic engagement but also polarizes the public discourse, reducing complex international issues to oversimplified narratives of loyalty and treason.

Authoritarian Impulses and Scapegoating

The rhetoric used by Biggs echoes authoritarian impulses, where dissent is not only discouraged but is also seen as a betrayal of the nation. By telling dissenters to leave the country, she effectively scapegoats those who exercise their democratic rights, thus contributing to an environment where political opposition is not just unwelcome, but potentially unpatriotic. This approach is deeply corrosive to democratic norms and principles, which rely on vigorous debate and the peaceful contestation of ideas.

Broader Implications: The Echoes of Past Demagogues

Rep. Biggs’ comments are not an isolated incident but rather part of a larger, more alarming trend where extreme loyalty to political leaders is demanded, and deviation from party lines is treated as heretical. History is replete with examples where leaders have demanded absolute allegiance, often at the cost of freedom and democracy. The rhetoric of “love it or leave it” is not merely about defending specific policy decisions on Iran; it’s about cementing a political culture that equates critical dissent with disloyalty.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

As we analyze the implications of Biggs’ statements, it becomes crucial to recognize and challenge the conflation of loyalty with uncritical acceptance of leadership. The health of a democracy can be measured by the robustness of its public discourse. In times of political tension, particularly concerning international conflicts, the necessity for a diverse range of voices and opinions becomes even more pressing. It is the responsibility of both the media and the public to resist authoritarian narratives and to defend the fundamental democratic right to dissent. This isn’t simply about one lawmaker’s inflammatory remarks; it’s about defending the very principles upon which democratic engagement is built.
