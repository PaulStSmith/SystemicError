Bruce Springsteen successfully baited Trump into handing him a 'personal gift': analyst

On Friday, during seemingly endless burst of Truth Social ranting over a myriad of topics, Trump took exception to Springsteen criticizing him during overseas concerts, which led to an over-the-top response from the aggrieved Trump.

On Truth Social, he snarled: "I see that Highly Overrated Bruce Springsteen goes to a Foreign Country to speak badly about the President of the United States. Never liked him, never liked his music, or his Radical Left Politics and, importantly, he’s not a talented guy — Just a pushy, obnoxious JERK, who fervently supported Crooked Joe Biden.”

Not content with that, he continued, "This dried out ‘prune’ of a rocker (his skin is all atrophied!) ought to KEEP HIS MOUTH SHUT until he gets back into the Country, that’s just ‘standard fare.’ Then we’ll all see how it goes for him!”

According to music writer and analyst Jeff Slate, the timing of Trump's latest jihad against the musician could not have come at a better time –– for Springsteen.

Writing for MSNBC, Slate explained, "Springsteen is 75 now and not making new music of any real impact. His forthcoming release, 'Tracks II: The Lost Albums,' the long-awaited follow-up to his 'Tracks' box set of studio outtakes from 1998, includes seven unreleased albums, the bulk of which weren’t recorded in this century."

Slate suggested that Trump's freak-out will likely, and inadvertently, give Springsteen a boost in sales.

"Certainly he knew there was a good chance Trump would react angrily. The pristine video posted to his [Springsteen's] website (and the transcript of his polemic) definitely telegraphed that. By taunting Trump and getting him to punch down, Springsteen gets to play the hero again to his legions of fans. It also gives him a jolt of cultural relevance — or at least a news cycle or two — at a good time," Slate predicted.

The music journalist then noted Trump also took aim at billionaire pop music performer Taylor Swift, who is more culturally relevant at the moment.

"Could it be that Trump was having second thoughts about taking Springsteen’s bait? Maybe. But probably not," Slate wrote.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tirade Against Springsteen: A Masterclass in Misguided Attention

In a spectacle that could only emerge from the convoluted corridors of Truth Social, Donald Trump's latest outburst targets none other than Bruce Springsteen, the 75-year-old rock icon. Springsteen, known for his blue-collar anthems and unwavering political stances, seems to have struck a chord with Trump, leading to a tirade that reeks of pettiness and misplaced priorities.

The Art of the Attack

Trump's attack on Springsteen is as predictable as it is petulant. His post on Truth Social is a classic example of his tendency to lash out at critics, regardless of their stature or relevance to his political career. Describing Springsteen as a "Highly Overrated...pushy, obnoxious JERK," Trump not only underlines his incapacity to handle criticism but also his eagerness to engage in battles of insignificance. The former president's rant, filled with personal jabs and devoid of substance, underscores a fragile ego rather than a strategic political mind.

An Unwitting Boost

Music writer Jeff Slate's analysis, cited in the article, points to an ironic twist: Trump's diatribe may actually benefit Springsteen. At a time when Springsteen is releasing a new album—Tracks II: The Lost Albums—Trump's spotlight on the musician could inadvertently boost sales. This incident is a textbook example of how Trump's attempts to belittle his critics often backfire, serving instead to amplify their voice and relevance.

The Misdirection

While Trump's focus on Springsteen (and Taylor Swift, as Slate mentions) might entertain or enrage his base, it's a glaring example of misdirected energy. At a time when the United States faces myriad challenges—ranging from healthcare crises to environmental disasters—the former president's preoccupation with celebrity feuds is not just trivial; it's irresponsibly diversionary.

Cultural Relevance vs. Political Irrelevance

Trump's assertion that Springsteen's criticism of him in a foreign country is somehow unpatriotic or unwarranted is a tired trope, one that fails to hold water. Artists like Springsteen and Swift, who have platforms and audiences spanning the globe, have every right to express their political views. That Trump feels the need to respond with such vitriol not only highlights his own insecurities but also his misunderstanding of freedom of speech.

Furthermore, Trump's comments about Springsteen's talent and relevance are laughably misguided. Springsteen's legacy as a musician and an activist is cemented in the annals of American culture—something that cannot be said for Trump's own legacy, which remains mired in controversy and division.

Conclusion: The Irony of It All

In attempting to demean Springsteen, Trump has inadvertently given him a platform and a sales boost. This episode is a microcosm of Trump's tenure in the public eye: a series of self-inflicted wounds, born out of a need to assert dominance and a penchant for getting embroiled in petty squabbles. It's a reminder that, in the arena of public opinion, Trump remains his own worst enemy.
