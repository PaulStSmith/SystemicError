'Grandpa needs a nap': Trump mocked as he can't keep eyes open during peace signing

President Donald Trump amused onlookers when he appeared to nod off — again — Thursday at a peace-themed signing ceremony for a U.S.-brokered pact between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda.

The event was being held at the U.S. Institute of Peace in Washington, D.C., where Trump was hosting Presidents Félix Tshisekedi of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Paul Kagame of Rwanda.

But as one speaker took the stage, Trump appeared to look down and struggled to keep his eyes open. The 79-year-old president’s eyes briefly shut before he forced them open and looked at the speaker.

The internet mocked Trump, who has appeared to doze off in other meetings recently.

Shannon Watts, founder of the Moms Demand Action grassroots movement to combat gun violence, joked on X, “Listening intently.”

Former CNN anchor Jim Acost jabbed on X, “More like the Institute of PeaZzzz.”

The Seneca Project, a women-led organization dedicated to preserving democracy, chided on X, “Looks like grandpa needs a nap…again.”

Former MSNBC host Keith Olbermann joked on X, “Donald, with 50 zzzzzzzzzzzzzz’s.”

Evan Rosenfeld, deputy digital director at The Bulwark, wrote on X, “Trump now consistently falling asleep during meetings is perhaps the most relatable thing he’s done this term.”

Ty McEachern, a former communications staffer for New Hampshire and North Carolina Democrats, wrote on X, “He’s falling asleep in public daily and all I can assume is the journalists who spent an entire election cycle hitting me and my colleagues up for quotes about Biden’s stamina must be sleeping on the f---in job too.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Nap Diplomacy: A Sleepy Foray into Peace Talks

In an era where the absurd has become the norm, President Donald Trump’s latest performance at a peace-themed signing ceremony for a U.S.-brokered pact between the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Rwanda seems to toe the line between farcical and worryingly negligent. The spectacle of the President dozing off, not for the first time, but in a setting that demanded the utmost attention and respect, is emblematic of a larger issue that plagues his administration: a glaring disregard for the gravity of international relations and diplomacy.

The Incident in Question

As onlookers watched, and the internet later devoured with a mix of amusement and disbelief, Trump’s effort to stay awake was as feeble as his administration’s attempts at coherent foreign policy. The 79-year-old’s eyelids fluttered shut in a room filled with individuals who had come together in the spirit of peace and cooperation, at the U.S. Institute of Peace, no less. This irony writes itself, but the implications are far from humorous.

Public Response: Laughter and Concern

The reaction on X (formerly Twitter) ranged from mocking to insightful. Shannon Watts and Jim Acost’s comments, while humorous, underline a disturbing reality: the leader of the free world cannot seem to muster the energy to stay awake during critical diplomatic engagements. The quips from Keith Olbermann and The Seneca Project further highlight this as a recurring issue rather than a one-off incident.

Evan Rosenfeld’s observation that Trump’s napping is “perhaps the most relatable thing he’s done this term” is a darkly comic reflection on the president’s disconnection from the responsibilities of his office. Yet, it’s Ty McEachern’s critique that strikes the deepest chord, pointing out the blatant double standard in media coverage of presidential fitness and alertness. Where are the fervent inquiries into Trump’s stamina, akin to those directed at Biden during the election cycle?

The Deeper Issue: A Disregard for Diplomacy

Beyond the immediate ridicule lies a profound disrespect for the diplomatic process and the individuals involved in it. Presidents Félix Tshisekedi of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Paul Kagame of Rwanda, who were present at the ceremony, deserved more than an audience with the inside of Trump’s eyelids. Their countries’ efforts towards peace, and by extension, the people of the Congo and Rwanda, were reduced to background noise for a presidential nap.

Conclusion: Wake-Up Call Needed

This incident should serve as a wake-up call, both literally and metaphorically, for Trump and his administration. The job at hand requires a leader who is not only awake but also fully engaged with the complexities of global diplomacy. Trump’s somnolent diplomacy is a stark reminder of the systemic error at the heart of his presidency: a failure to recognize and meet the demands of his role with the seriousness and respect it deserves.

The world watches, increasingly bemused and concerned, as the U.S. grapples with the reality of a leader who seems more comfortable with his eyes closed. Perhaps it’s time for Trump to realize that peace and diplomacy require more than just showing up; they demand full consciousness.
