'Never felt more betrayed': MAGA rebels over Trump's 'treasonous' Qatar base in Idaho

After years of advocating “America First,” President Donald Trump’s administration, the Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth announced on Friday, “I’m also proud that today we’re signing a letter of acceptance to build a Qatari Emiri Air Force facility at the Mountain Home Airbase in Idaho.”

It led to a swift meltdown from some of the president’s top allies.

Constitutionalist and MAGA influencer “The General” was furious, calling it outright “treason.”

“We are in the middle of rolling out military across the entire USA and then bringing in a non-NATO country military into the USA is TREASON. U.S. and Qatar sign deal to open a Qatari ‘air force facility,’ in the U.S., at Mountain Home Air Force Base, Idaho,” he wrote on X.

“Is this what ‘shared defense goals’ means now — or just the latest way our politicians get paid to sell out our country?” asked Amy Mek, the editor-in-chief of RAIR, an organization that advocates for the U.S. to return to a country run by Judeo-Christian values. “Twenty-four years after foreign nationals trained in our flight schools flew planes into our buildings, our leaders are inviting their financiers to train inside our bases. This is what happens when you gut national-security training, scrub every mention of Islam, jihad, and Sharia from the manuals, and let Obama- and Biden-era bureaucrats turn counterterrorism into cultural sensitivity class. We’re being led by officials who no longer recognize or refuse to name the enemy they’re inviting into our own backyard.’

Close ally to President Trump, Laura Loomer, lamented the news after advocating that the administration declare the Muslim Brotherhood an international terrorist organization.

“Well, I guess this isn’t going to happen since we just gave the Muslim Brotherhood an air base in Idaho. So much for my decade worth of hard work trying to protect Americans from the threat of Islamic terror,” said Loomer about the new base.

“No foreign country should have a military base on U.S. soil,” she also said. “Especially Islamic countries. I have never felt more betrayed by the GOP than I do now watching Islamic jihadists get away with implementing Sharia law in the US and now they are getting their own airbase where they will train to kill Americans.”

She went on to warn that it would make America less safe by setting up “for America to be attacked by Islamic savages from Qatar, the biggest funders of Islamic terror in the entire world. So much so, the Saudis and Emiratis find Qatar to be TOXIC. I need to see how much more of my life I am going to dedicate to a party that won’t address the threat of Islam in the West. The betrayal stings. WE ARE LOSING OUR COUNTRY!”

Content creator and influencer Red Eagle Politics denied the reporting.

“We aren’t giving Argentina a free $20 Billion handout, and we aren’t building an Air Force Base for Qatar in Idaho. The amount of dishonest lunacy on this app is reaching new heights,” he wrote on X.

Utah state Sen. Nate Blouin, a Democrat, pointed out that Idaho Republicans “have been crowing about” legislation similar to that his state enacted “blocking foreign ownership of land in their state.”

Dan Caldwell, former senior advisor to Hegseth, wrote on X that it wasn’t that big of a deal.

“The freak out around this is of course totally unwarranted since this is actually a pretty common practice with countries that buy and operate a lot of U.S. military aircraft. Singapore has a similar facility and detachment for its F-15 training unit at this very same airbase,” he said.

Caldwell is one of the DOD aides who was forced out amid Hegseth’s Signalgate scandal. He has denied any wrongdoing.

Our Analysis:

The “America First” Paradox: Unpacking the Fallout from the Qatar Air Force Facility Announcement

In an announcement that can only be described as a seismic shock to the core principles of the “America First” doctrine, Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth declared the signing of a letter of acceptance for constructing a Qatari Emiri Air Force facility at Mountain Home Airbase in Idaho. This move not only contradicts the isolationist rhetoric championed by President Donald Trump’s administration but also ignites a firestorm among its staunchest allies.

The Fury of the MAGA Influencers

The backlash was instantaneous and fierce. Constitutionalist and MAGA influencer “The General” branded the decision as “treason,” a term that, while charged, reflects the depth of betrayal felt by a segment of Trump’s base. The argument hinges on the incorporation of a non-NATO military presence on U.S. soil, which, in their view, undermines national sovereignty and security. The notion that this could be seen as selling out the country for undisclosed benefits is not just a whisper in dark corners but a loud accusation from voices like Amy Mek, the editor-in-chief of RAIR.

Mek’s criticism extends beyond the immediate issue to a broader condemnation of what she perceives as a systematic dismantling of national security protocols. Her assertion that this move is the culmination of years of eroding counterterrorism efforts in favor of “cultural sensitivity” is a stark commentary on the perceived priorities of the Obama and Biden administrations. However, it’s crucial to dissect these claims with a critical eye. The amalgamation of Qatar’s involvement with sweeping allegations about the erosion of counterterrorism efforts lacks nuance and a clear understanding of the multifaceted nature of global diplomacy and security.

Laura Loomer’s Lament

Further amplifying the outcry is Laura Loomer, known for her vehement opposition to Islamic influence in Western politics. Her comments reflect a deep-seated fear of Islamic terrorism, linking the establishment of the Qatari base directly to an increased risk of attacks on American soil. Loomer’s remarks, however, skate dangerously close to the edge of Islamophobia, generalizing the threat to encompass all Islamic countries and by extension, all Muslims. This leap from a specific security concern to broad-brush cultural condemnation is not only unhelpful but also exacerbates divisions and misunderstandings.

The Voice of Reason?

Amid the uproar, voices like Dan Caldwell, a former senior advisor to Hegseth, attempt to introduce a measure of perspective, noting the commonality of such arrangements with other nations, such as Singapore. Caldwell’s comparison is meant to underscore the routine nature of international military cooperation, hinting at an overreaction from critics. However, Caldwell’s past entanglement in the Signalgate scandal complicates his position, potentially coloring the reception of his arguments.

The Political Irony

The irony of a Republican administration, which has vocally opposed foreign influence and championed national sovereignty, facilitating the establishment of a foreign military base on U.S. soil, cannot be overstated. It highlights a fundamental inconsistency in the “America First” ideology, where geopolitical realities often necessitate actions that contradict isolationist rhetoric. Furthermore, the reaction from MAGA influencers and their followers underscores a significant rift within the Republican base—a divergence between ideological purity and the pragmatic demands of governing.

Conclusion

The announcement of the Qatari Emiri Air Force facility at Mountain Home Airbase is more than a policy decision; it’s a litmus test for the “America First” movement. As the fallout continues, one thing is clear: the path forward for this administration is fraught with ideological landmines, internal divisions, and the daunting task of reconciling its actions with its promises. The saga of the Qatari base in Idaho is not just about military strategy or international relations; it’s a mirror reflecting the complex, often contradictory nature of political identity and allegiance in today’s America.
