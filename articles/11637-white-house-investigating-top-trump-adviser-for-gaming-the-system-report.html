White House investigating top Trump adviser for 'gaming the system': report

Corey Lewandowski, (right) arrives with Eric Trump ahead of Trump’s debate with Democratic presidential nominee and U.S. Vice President Kamala Harris, in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S., September 10, 2024. REUTERS/Eduardo Munoz

A top advisor to President Donald Trump is now under investigation from the White House for having allegedly “gamed the system” in undercounting their work hours, Axios reported Thursday.

White House officials told Axios that Corey Lewandowski is being investigated for “failing to swipe in” to work, dodging a limitation imposed on him as a special government employee, which limits him to working 130 days a year.

"We're hearing that Corey's doing everything – failing to swipe in, working from home, whatever – to undercount his days,” a White House official told Axios, speaking on the condition of anonymity. “It's fair to say his work is being watched now."

As a special government employee, Lewandowski is exempt from the traditional requirements of a full-time employee of the federal government, such as having to file financial disclosures and forgo private employment and business dealings. But according to four White House officials, Lewandowski has likely already “exceeded his allowed time as a temporary employee,” despite records suggesting he’s only worked 69 days this year.

Lewandowski’s role in the Trump administration has been murky. While he was described by Trump as being appointed as his personal envoy, he has introduced himself as the “chief of staff” to Homeland Security Secretary Kirsti Noem, only for DHS to later clarify that he was actually just an adviser to DHS.

Two “sources with Knowledge of Lewandowski’s work” also told Axios that the Trump advisor has been “seen entering government buildings with other employees to avoid swiping his own badge.”

Lewandowski maintains a significant influence at DHS, and is frequently seen accompanying Noem, amid rumors of an alleged affair between the two, both of whom are married. His influence at the agency is also causing fear among many employees, according to a former DHS official.

“Everybody's scared s—less" a former DHS official told Axios, speaking on the condition of anonymity. “The feeling is that if they go up against Corey, they're going to lose. And I would have to say they're right."

Our Analysis:

The Lewandowski Labyrinth: Navigating the Murky Waters of White House Work Ethics

In an era where the lines between ethical practice and political maneuvering blur more with each passing day, the case of Corey Lewandowski, as reported by Axios, emerges as a glaring beacon of the systemic rot festering within the corridors of power. The allegations against this top advisor to President Donald Trump paint a picture of a political landscape where gaming the system isn't just a sport but a survival strategy. Let's dissect the layers of this sordid saga, shall we?

The Alleged Time Theft

At the heart of this controversy is the claim that Lewandowski, a special government employee limited to 130 working days a year, has been undercounting his work hours. The audacity of "failing to swipe in" to dodge this limitation not only mocks the very essence of accountability but also raises a significant question: How deep does the disdain for procedural integrity run among those in the highest echelons of power?

The Murkiness of Role

Lewandowski's role within the Trump administration is as clear as mud. Described by Trump as his personal envoy and self-touted as the “chief of staff” to Homeland Security Secretary Kirsti Noem, he was later demoted in public perception to merely an adviser to DHS. This chameleon-like ability to shift titles, combined with the DHS's later clarification, reveals a troubling lack of transparency and consistency in the administration's appointment and portrayal of roles.

Dodging Disclosure and Business Limitations

The position of a special government employee comes with its exemptions, notably from the requirements to file financial disclosures and abstain from private employment. Yet, the allegations suggest Lewandowski has exploited these loopholes beyond their intended purpose, likely exceeding his permissible time as a temporary employee while maintaining significant private influence. This raises another red flag about the erosion of ethical boundaries intended to prevent conflicts of interest within the government.

Influence and Intimidation

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of this saga is the climate of fear Lewandowski's influence at DHS has reportedly engendered among employees. The notion that challenging him could lead to professional ruin is antithetical to the principles of a democratic and transparent government. It underscores a culture where power is wielded as a weapon rather than a responsibility, leaving little room for accountability and dissent.

Conclusion: A Systemic Symptom

The case of Corey Lewandowski is not an isolated incident but a symptom of a broader disease plaguing our political system. It exemplifies how the mechanisms intended to ensure accountability and ethical governance can be manipulated by those with enough influence and audacity. As we navigate these murky waters, one must ask: When will the tide turn towards a genuine commitment to integrity and transparency in governance?

Until such questions are not only asked but answered and acted upon, tales like Lewandowski's will remain a staple in the diet of political discourse, serving as bitter reminders of the systemic error that undermines the very foundation of our democratic institutions.
