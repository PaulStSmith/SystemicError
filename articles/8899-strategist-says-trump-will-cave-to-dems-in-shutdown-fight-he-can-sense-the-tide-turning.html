Strategist says Trump will cave to Dems in shutdown fight: ‘He can sense the tide turning’

Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) and House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) have reportedly been opposed to Trump meeting with Democratic leaders Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY) and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) to discuss a pathway to re-opening the government, with the shutdown having been sparked over disagreements on health care policy.

“One of the reasons why Thune and Johnson didn’t want the president – still doesn’t want the president to meet with Jeffries and Schumer is because they’re afraid that Trump will make a deal with Schumer and Jeffries on the Obamacare subsidies,” said MSNBC’s Jonathan Capehart Saturday.

“Do you think that next week, we’re going to see the president get involved and make a deal with those two?”

Smikle said that he believed it was “more likely than not” that Trump would end up caving to Democrats’ demands, and largely due to the political blowback he and his party may receive from millions of Americans losing health care coverage and having premiums more than double.

“I think it’s more likely than not at this point because [Trump] can sense the tide turning,” Smikle said. “Everything that Republicans have tried to do, they didn’t think Democrats would last this long, and they have.”

The dispute centers around funding for Obamacare subsidies, which are set to expire at the end of this year. Democrats have refused to back any funding measure that doesn’t include an extension of the subsidies, which, if allowed to expire, would see nearly 4 million Americans lose health insurance and premiums for Obamacare enrollees increase by 114%.

Smikle also predicted that Democrats would hold strong in refusing to re-open the government without securing funding for the subsidies to be extended, arguing that if Democrats voted to re-open the government without getting the subsidies extended, they could face their own political backlash.

“If the Democrats make any deal, if they can’t go back to the voter and say ‘we brought your prices down,’ that’s a problem,” he said.

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Government Shutdown Over Obamacare Subsidies

In a recent political gambit that has left millions of Americans teetering on the edge of losing essential health care coverage, the ongoing government shutdown, now dragging into its 39th day, has become a battleground over Obamacare subsidies. Political strategist and policy advisor Basil Smikle’s bold prediction that President Donald Trump will capitulate to Democrats’ demands cuts to the heart of a political showdown that epitomizes the dysfunction at the core of current American politics.

The Crux of the Dispute

At the center of this standoff is the funding for Obamacare subsidies, crucial for nearly 4 million Americans who stand to lose health insurance and face a staggering 114% increase in premiums for Obamacare enrollees if these subsidies are allowed to expire. The Democrats’ refusal to ink any funding measure devoid of an extension for these subsidies is not just a political maneuver; it’s a stand for basic human rights—access to affordable healthcare.

Republican Reluctance: A Strategy of Obstruction

The resistance from Senate Majority Leader John Thune and House Speaker Mike Johnson to President Trump meeting with Democratic leaders Sen. Chuck Schumer and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries is telling. Their fear? That Trump might actually reach an agreement with Schumer and Jeffries on the Obamacare subsidies. This reflects a broader GOP strategy that appears more invested in political victory than in the welfare of the American people. Jonathan Capehart’s insight that this opposition stems from a fear of Trump making a deal underscores a perverse prioritization of party ideology over public good.

Political Blowback: A Tide Turning

Smikle’s assertion that Trump is likely to cave due to the potential political blowback highlights a critical aspect of this debacle: the tangible impact on American lives is becoming too significant to ignore. The prospect of millions losing healthcare coverage and facing doubled premiums presents not just a moral crisis but a political one for Republicans. The fact that “everything Republicans have tried to do” has not broken the Democrats’ resolve shows a miscalculation of the Democrats’ tenacity and the public’s tolerance for political brinkmanship.

Democrats’ Stance: A Risky yet Principled Position

The Democrats’ refusal to re-open the government without ensuring the extension of Obamacare subsidies is a risky political play, but it’s grounded in a principled stance. They are acutely aware that any concession that does not address the skyrocketing costs of health insurance would not only be a betrayal of their constituents but could also spell political suicide. Smikle’s warning that failing to secure these subsidies could lead to backlash against the Democrats underscores the high stakes involved. It’s not just about political victories but ensuring the American people can afford their next doctor’s visit.

Conclusion: A Systemic Dysfunction

This entire episode lays bare the systemic dysfunction at the heart of American politics, where the health and well-being of millions are used as leverage in political negotiations. The fact that such a fundamental issue as healthcare affordability has become a pawn in a broader political game is a damning indictment of the current state of affairs. Whether Trump will indeed cave to the Democrats or not, the larger issue remains: a political system that gambles with the lives of its citizens is in dire need of reform. The bravery to stand for healthcare subsidies should not be exceptional but expected. This shutdown is not just a battle over policy but a stark reflection of the systemic errors plaguing American democracy.
