Trump complains 'you need 185 IQ to turn on a lawnmower' in bizarre meeting

President Donald Trump vowed Monday afternoon to deregulate farming equipment, complaining that “you need 185 IQ to turn on a lawnmower.”

In a Monday meeting with farmers, Trump revealed that he would remove environmental regulations from John Deere and other tractor manufacturers.

“We’re going to also give the tractor companies, John Deere and all of the companies that make the equipment, we’re going to take off a lot of the environmental restrictions,” the president said. “It’s ridiculous. I know because I buy a lot of that machinery for different things.”

“We have a lot of big clubs with hundreds, thousands of acres,” he continued. “And you buy it, it’s got so much equipment on it for the environmental. It doesn’t do anything, except it makes the equipment much more expensive and much more complicated to work. And it’s not as good as the old days.”

“And a lot of the reason is because they put these environmental excesses on the equipment, which don’t do a damn thing except make it complicated, make it impractical. And you really have to be, in many cases, you need about 185 IQ to turn on a lawnmower.”

Trump placed the blame for the complications on former President Joe Biden.

“It’s crazy,” he said. “The machines, they’re always under repair because they’re so complicated that you can’t fix them. The old days used to fix it yourself. Now you can’t do that. You have to be a PhD from, let’s say, MIT.”

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Crusade Against Environmental Regulations

In an era where the absurd often masquerades as policy, the recent declarations by President Donald Trump regarding the deregulation of farming equipment take the proverbial cake. Trump’s lamentation over the complexity of modern lawnmowers—asserting that one needs a “185 IQ” to operate them—delves not into the realm of reasoned argument but plunges headfirst into the pool of the ludicrous.

The Misguided Assault on Environmental Protections

Trump’s pledge to strip away environmental regulations from companies like John Deere is not just a misguided venture; it’s a dangerous one. The president’s claim, that environmental safeguards make machinery “more expensive and much more complicated to work,” misses the forest for the trees. The “environmental excesses” he so eagerly vilifies serve a crucial purpose: they are in place to mitigate the substantial environmental footprint of agricultural machinery, from reducing emissions to curbing pollutants that can devastate ecosystems.

It’s not about the complexity; it’s about the planet. To frame environmental protections as nothing but an inconvenience is to ignore the vast body of scientific evidence pointing to the urgent need for sustainable practices in every sector, agriculture included.

Blame Game and Nostalgia Politics

Attributing the supposed over-complication of farming equipment to former President Joe Biden is another maneuver in Trump’s playbook of deflection and blame. This tactic, while predictable, is inherently flawed. The regulations in question span administrations and are part of a global effort to confront environmental challenges. Moreover, the yearning for the “old days” of simpler machinery overlooks the advancements that have been made in safety and efficiency, alongside environmental considerations.

A Reality Check

The notion that one needs a “PhD from, let’s say, MIT” to fix modern machinery is not just an exaggeration; it’s an insult to the intelligence and adaptability of farmers and technicians across the country. These individuals, who work tirelessly to feed the nation, are more than capable of mastering the technology that Trump deems too complex. It’s a false narrative, one that underestimates the skill of the agricultural community and misrepresents the nature of the problem.

The Bigger Picture

In the grand scheme of things, Trump’s diatribe against environmental regulations in farming equipment is a disservice to the serious conversations we need to be having about sustainability, technology, and agriculture. It’s a distraction from the pressing environmental crises facing our planet, wrapped in a veneer of populist rhetoric.

In conclusion, the deregulation crusade, as proposed by Trump, is a step backward in our collective responsibility to steward the Earth. It’s a move that prioritizes perceived convenience over the health of our planet and future generations. The real issue at hand isn’t the IQ required to operate a lawnmower; it’s the wisdom to understand the importance of safeguarding our environment, even if it requires a bit more effort and adaptation.
