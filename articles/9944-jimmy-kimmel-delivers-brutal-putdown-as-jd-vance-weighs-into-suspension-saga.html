Jimmy Kimmel delivers brutal putdown as JD Vance weighs into suspension saga

FILE PHOTO: Jimmy Kimmel arrives at the 74th Primetime Emmy Awards held at the Microsoft Theater in Los Angeles, U.S., September 12, 2022. REUTERS/Aude Guerrucci/File Photo

Late night host Jimmy Kimmel clapped back at JD Vance Monday after the vice president attempted to claim Kimmel’s short-lived ousting was a result of poor ratings and not his own administration’s threats — a claim Kimmel called a “fairytale even a five-year-old wouldn’t believe.”

Kimmel’s ABC show was briefly suspended this month after the late-night host made comments about President Donald Trump’s reaction to the killing of right-wing influencer Charlie Kirk, and after Federal Communications Chair Brendan Carr threatened to pull ABC’s license: “We could do this the easy way or the hard way,” Carr said.

Last Friday, Vance appeared on Fox News to defend his administration’s role in Kimmel’s ousting, claiming Carr had no involvement in the show’s suspension and blaming the incident on Kimmel himself for being “not funny because his ratings aren’t very good.”

Calling Vance’s comments “bull----,” Kimmel proceeded to rip into Vance for what he characterized as his hypocrisy in bringing up ratings.

“My ratings aren’t very good? Last time I checked, your ratings were somewhere between a hair in your salad and chlamydia,” Kimmel said.

“In three-and-a-half years, I’m not the one who’s going to be doing mascara tutorials on YouTube. How did we wind up with a president and a vice president who wear more makeup than Kylie Jenner and Lady Gaga combined?”

Indeed, Vance’s favorability among Americans has continued to sour, having started off his tenure with a net approval rating of -8, with 43% of voters viewing him favorably and 51% unfavorably. As of Aug. 1, Vance’s favorability tanked further, dropping to a net approval of -12.

Despite Kimmel’s show being restored, and broadcast now, he said, to “100% of the country,” the impacts of the incident continue to linger. Disney shareholders havestaged a revolt of sorts, demanding answers for Kimmel’s ousting and alleging the suspension may have been a violation of their rights as shareholders.

Our Analysis:

The Kimmel-Vance Showdown: More Than Just Late Night Banter

In the latest episode of what can only be described as political theater meets late-night comedy, Jimmy Kimmel finds himself at the center of a swirling controversy involving none other than Vice President JD Vance. The dispute, ostensibly over Kimmel’s brief suspension from ABC following his comments on President Donald Trump and the overt threat from Federal Communications Chair Brendan Carr to pull ABC’s license, has morphed into a full-blown spectacle. But let’s peel back the layers of this absurdity, shall we?

The Thinly Veiled Threat

At the heart of this debacle is a blatant misuse of power by Federal Communications Chair Brendan Carr, whose words, “We could do this the easy way or the hard way,” sound more like a line from a mafia movie than a statement from a government official. This isn’t just about regulating the airwaves; it’s about silencing dissent. When a late-night host’s joke becomes the pretext for such an overreach, it’s clear that the issue at hand is not about content, but control.

Vance’s Ratings Deflection: A Misguided Missile

Enter JD Vance, who took to Fox News with claims so outlandish they’d be comical if they weren’t so concerning. Vance’s attempt to blame Kimmel’s “poor ratings” for the suspension is not just a deflection; it’s an outright fabrication. It’s akin to blaming the weather for a traffic jam caused by a car accident—irrelevant and misleading.

Kimmel’s retort, comparing Vance’s favorability to “a hair in your salad and chlamydia,” might be crass, but it’s decidedly apt. It’s a vivid illustration of the popularity contest this political era has devolved into, where the measure of a person’s worth or credibility is likened to ratings on a TV show.

The Real Losers: The American Public

The squabble between Kimmel and Vance, while entertaining, masks a more sinister reality. The threat to free speech and the abuse of governmental power should concern everyone, regardless of where one falls on the political spectrum. This isn’t just about a late-night show; it’s about the principles that underpin a democratic society. When government officials can threaten media outlets over content they dislike, we’re treading on dangerous ground.

The reaction from Disney shareholders is a subplot worth noting. Their “revolt,” as it’s been termed, signals a growing awareness and unwillingness to stand by as corporate decisions—possibly influenced by political pressures—threaten to undermine their rights. This is not merely a question of Kimmel’s suspension but of corporate governance and shareholder rights.

Conclusion: A Call for Vigilance

The Kimmel-Vance tussle is more than a war of words; it’s a stark reminder of the fragility of our freedoms. The ease with which a comedic comment can become a national controversy, leading to governmental threats and corporate capitulation, should alarm us all.

As we navigate these turbulent waters, let us not be distracted by the smoke and mirrors of ratings and retorts. The essence of this conflict lies in the defense of free expression and the resistance against those who would seek to curtail it for political gain. In this scenario, laughter is not just the best medicine; it’s a weapon against the encroachment of authoritarianism.
