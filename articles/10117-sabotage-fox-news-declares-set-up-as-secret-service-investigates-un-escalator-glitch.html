'Sabotage?' Fox News declares 'set up' as Secret Service investigates UN escalator glitch

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 25, 20255 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump ride an escalator as they arrive to attend the 80th United Nations General Assembly, in New York City, New York, U.S., September 23, 2025. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

“Fox & Friends” hosts agreed the U.S. Secret Service should investigate an apparent escalator malfunction that forced President Donald Trump and his wife Melania to walk upstairs to his widely criticized United Nations speech.

Trump complained about the escalator and a glitchy teleprompter, among many other topics, during his admittedly “braggadocious,” 55-minute harangue, and Fox News broadcaster Ainsley Earhardt said she’d heard from viewers and acquaintances who said the malfunctions had overshadowed a speech that drew condemnation from veteran diplomats.

“This is what people are talking about, not his speech,” Earhardt said. “The Secret Service is now investigating.”

“There were talks in the press that UN workers in advance were joking they were going to turn off the escalator before the meeting, and so the Secret Service is investigating whether or not Trump was deliberately sabotaged by the UN staff,” she added.

The show’s producers played a 26-second clip of Trump suggesting during the speech that the first lady could have been injured when the escalator ground to a halt, and co-host Brian Kilmeade delighted in the president’s complaints.

“Think about this,” Kilmeade said, grinning. “He also in the speech, and he was going to bring this up anyway, said, ‘I bid on refurbishing this place when I was a contractor in the ‘90s, you went with somebody else and ended up with plastic and Lucite or something, instead of marble.’ So, meanwhile, they want to get to the bottom of this.”

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt confirmed the Secret Service was looking into whether the escalator or teleprompter had been tampered with, but UN spokesperson Stephane Dujarric said an examination showed the escalator’s central processing unit stopped after a built-in safety mechanism was activated.

“If you’re trying to mock the president by screwing with his teleprompter and the escalator, it’s not going to go unpunished,” Kilmeade said, paraphrasing the White House press secretary.

Kilmeade read the UN spokesperson’s statement aloud, pronouncing the technical terms for the escalator’s components as if incredulous they had individual names, and both he and co-host Lawrence Jones cast doubt on the explanation.

“No way, no way,” Jones said. “By the way, the intent may have been to annoy the president or embarrass the president, but that’s a major security failure.”

“I know,” Kilmeade agreed. “It could have been a setup.”

“I was more stunned at the reaction,” Jones added. “Like, the leader of the free world is on the escalator, and the Secret Service is looking, like, what do we do?”

Our Analysis:

Unpacking the Escalator Saga: A Masterclass in Deflection

In an episode that feels torn from the pages of a political satire, the hosts of “Fox & Friends” have managed to divert attention from President Donald Trump’s controversial United Nations speech to an escalator malfunction and a glitchy teleprompter. The incident, as reported, spiraled into a conspiracy theory so outlandish that it would be comedic if it weren’t presented with such earnestness.

The Misdirection Play

Firstly, let’s address the elephant in the room: the claim that the malfunction of an escalator and a teleprompter overshadowed the content of Trump’s speech is a misdirection of epic proportions. Veteran diplomats condemned the speech, but according to Ainsley Earhardt, the real scandal here is... an escalator malfunction? This is akin to focusing on the color of the Titanic’s deck chairs as it sinks into the icy waters of the North Atlantic.

The Secret Service Investigation

The notion that the U.S. Secret Service should investigate an escalator malfunction at the United Nations is a dramatic escalation of what is, at most, a maintenance issue. It’s framed as if there were a deliberate sabotage attempt by UN staff, based on hearsay and offhand jokes. This leap to conspiracy is not only a gross misuse of resources but also a distraction from more pressing national and international concerns.

Skepticism Over Safety Mechanisms

The skepticism displayed by the hosts towards the UN spokesperson’s explanation is particularly telling. Technical failures are not uncommon, and the existence of safety mechanisms in escalators is standard, not suspicious. The portrayal of the escalator’s CPU stopping due to a safety mechanism as some sort of elaborate ruse is baseless and borders on farcical.

Security Failure or Convenient Distraction?

Labeling the incident as a “major security failure” is an exaggeration. Lawrence Jones and Brian Kilmeade’s speculation that this could have been a setup is unfounded and irresponsible. The real question should be why such a minor incident is being blown out of proportion. Is it possibly a convenient distraction from the content of Trump’s “braggadocious” speech, which drew widespread condemnation?

The Real Issue at Hand

In the final analysis, this focus on an escalator and teleprompter instead of the substance of a speech at the United Nations speaks volumes about the priorities of “Fox & Friends”. It’s a clear attempt to deflect from the content of Trump’s address, which, by many accounts, was problematic enough to warrant critical examination.

The emphasis on trivial technical malfunctions over substantive diplomatic discourse is a disservice to the viewers and to the public discourse. It’s a stark reminder of the power of media framing and the need for viewers to remain vigilant and critical of the narratives being presented to them.

In essence, the escalator saga is a microcosm of a much larger issue: the prioritization of sensationalism and conspiracy over substance and factual reporting. As viewers and citizens, we must demand better, seeking out the full story rather than succumbing to the allure of distraction and diversion.
