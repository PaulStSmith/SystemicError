The Illusion of Judicial Restraint: The Supreme Court’s Complicity in Trump’s Power Grab

Power Consolidation Masquerading as Independence

Despite the popular narrative of Chief Justice John Roberts as a moderating force on the Supreme Court, recent developments starkly contradict this image. The reality, as discussed by legal analysts Mark Joseph Stern and Dahlia Lithwick, is that the Court under Roberts has not only failed to check President Donald Trump’s expansive view of executive power but has actively enabled it. This has been evident both in high-profile public rulings and through the less scrutinized shadow docket—a mechanism by which the Court handles emergency decisions and other orders without full briefing and oral argument.

The Shadow Docket: A Tool for Quiet Compliance

The shadow docket has emerged as a critical yet opaque tool in the Supreme Court’s arsenal, allowing it to make significant legal decisions quietly and without the usual public scrutiny. Stern points out that for every overt decision that appears to align with Trump, there are numerous others in the shadows helping to solidify his control. This secretive process not only undermines the transparency expected of the highest court but also raises questions about the integrity and impartiality of its decisions.

The Roberts Project: Reshaping American Politics

It would be a mistake to view Roberts’ actions through a lens of judicial restraint or independence. On the contrary, both Stern and Lithwick argue that Roberts has long been engaged in a project to reshape American politics from the bench. His strategy appears to involve making incremental decisions that pave the way for more significant shifts, reducing the shock and increasing the acceptance of his approach. This methodical strategy reveals a clear and deliberate reshaping of the Court’s role in governance, aligning more with political objectives than with an impartial arbiter of the law.

Complicity and Consequences

The Supreme Court’s alignment with Trump has not only expanded executive power but also, paradoxically, expanded its own role in the final say of U.S. governance. However, this comes at a cost. By repeatedly siding with Trump, the Court risks depleting its moral and institutional authority to act independently in future critical situations. Stern expresses a cynical view that the Court has already achieved much of what it wanted in terms of empowering Trump, suggesting a strategic pullback from the aggressive use of the shadow docket now that certain objectives have been accomplished.

Institutional Decay and the Future of Governance

The complicity of the Supreme Court in Trump’s presidency is not just a story of legal decisions but a broader narrative of institutional decay. The Court, through its actions, has shown a willingness to be an enabler of executive overreach, potentially setting dangerous precedents for the balance of power in American governance. The real test, as Stern hints, will come when the Court decides to oppose Trump, and he chooses to defy it—a scenario that could lead to a constitutional crisis given the precedents set.

Conclusion: The Perils of Judicial Overreach

The Supreme Court’s role in enabling Trump’s authoritarian tendencies reflects a broader trend of judicial overreach that compromises its ability to function as an impartial guardian of the Constitution. By blurring the lines between judicial independence and political maneuvering, the Court not only undermines its credibility but also imperils the very structure of balanced governance it is meant to protect. As this saga unfolds, the long-term implications for American democracy remain deeply concerning, with the Court’s integrity and independence hanging in the balance.
