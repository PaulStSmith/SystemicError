'Flunky': Trump's 'lap dog' prosecutor mocked for big mistake in case against Dems

A former federal prosecutor mocked one of President Donald Trump's favorite attorneys on Sunday because of the way he's handled a pair of high-profile cases.

Glenn Kirchner, a former Assistant U.S. Attorney, appeared on "The Legal Breakdown" with progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen to discuss how Ed Martin, Trump's pardon attorney and leader of the Weaponization of Government task force, has handed his mortgage fraud investigation into Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and New York Attorney GeneralLetitia James.

"There are all sorts of reasons we don't announce criminal investigations," Kirschner said. "But Ed 'Stop the Steal' Martin is the antithesis of the responsible prosecutor. He is nothing more than a mouthpiece, a flunky, a lap dog for Donald Trump."

Martin announced last week that he was opening an investigation into Schiff and James for allegedly committing mortgage fraud. Schiff and James have both denied wrongdoing.

Kirschner described the cases as "vindictive prosecutions."

The former prosecutor added that announcing that kind of investigation was one of Martin's biggest mistakes.

"First of all, you don't want to tip off somebody you are about to investigate because they could destroy evidence," Kirschner said. "They could tamper with witnesses. They could flee to avoid prosecution.

"Plus, you don't want to announce that you're launching a criminal probe into somebody because that person may not have done anything wrong," he added.

Kirschner suggested that there may be a higher purpose to Martin's investigations, one that is not based on law at all.

"He is taking a page out of Donald Trump's dirty playbook," Kirschner said. "Just announce the investigations and poison the well of public opinion against Adam Schiff or whoever Trump's enemy is."

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Trump's Legal Circus

In a spectacle that could only be conceived in the fever dreams of a political satire, Ed Martin, a figurehead in the Trump legal menagerie, has been publicly skewered by former federal prosecutor Glenn Kirschner. The stage for this legal mockery? None other than "The Legal Breakdown," hosted by progressive YouTuber Brian Tyler Cohen. Here, Kirschner, with the kind of exasperation reserved for watching a clown car collision, dissected Martin's handling of high-profile cases against Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and New York Attorney General Letitia James. The charge? Mortgage fraud. The evidence? As substantial as a soap bubble, it appears.

Ed Martin: Trump's Legal Lapdog

Let's not mince words. Kirschner's portrayal of Ed Martin as a "mouthpiece," a "flunky," and a "lap dog" for Donald Trump is not just a scathing critique; it’s an indictment of the charade that passes for legal strategy within Trump's orbit. Martin, who wears the moniker 'Stop the Steal' as if it were a badge of honor, seems to have forgotten the first rule of legal proceedings: don't announce your moves before you make them.

Why? Because, as Kirschner points out, announcing investigations prematurely can lead to destroyed evidence, tampered witnesses, or suspects conveniently finding themselves on a long-term vacation to avoid prosecution. But far more damning is the assertion that these announcements could target individuals who "may not have done anything wrong." This isn't just irresponsible; it's potentially destructive, both to the individuals involved and to the integrity of the legal system.

A Page from Trump's "Dirty Playbook"

The strategy, if one can call it that, behind Martin's announcements seems less about legal prudence and more about political theater. According to Kirschner, Martin is taking "a page out of Donald Trump's dirty playbook" by using the announcement of investigations to "poison the well of public opinion" against Trump's adversaries. This isn't justice; it's character assassination by press release.

Calling these actions "vindictive prosecutions" doesn't just suggest a misuse of legal resources; it highlights a perverse use of the justice system as a tool for political retribution. This isn't the behavior of a responsible prosecutor or a legitimate legal task force. It's the modus operandi of a regime that views the legal system as a weapon to be wielded against its enemies, rather than a mechanism for the impartial administration of justice.

The Mockery of Justice

In essence, what we're witnessing is a mockery of the legal process, a spectacle that undermines the principles of justice and accountability. The fact that such antics are being entertained at the highest levels of political discourse is not just disappointing; it's alarming.

As observers of this legal charade, we must ask ourselves: What does it say about the state of our political and legal systems when such transparently partisan and vindictive maneuvers are not only contemplated but executed with such brazenness?

In the final analysis, Ed Martin's actions, as described by Glenn Kirschner, aren't just an indictment of Martin himself or even of Donald Trump. They are a symptom of a broader systemic illness—a disregard for the rule of law and a disdain for the norms that sustain a functioning democracy.

So, as we watch this spectacle unfold, let us not be distracted by the absurdity of the theater. Instead, let us focus on the dangerous implications of allowing such a mockery of justice to proceed unchecked. The integrity of our legal system, and indeed our democracy, depends on it.
