As Swift takes over the news media with her new album announcement, Alex Galbraith, a news and politics editor for Salon, explains why he thinks Trump is jealous of her.

In a segment called "The tortured president’s society," the writer declared, "Donald Trump wants stans."

"The president may seem unpredictable and brash, but he’s easy to understand when you realize he wants a fan army to love him uncritically. All of the nicknames, all of the attempts to force a catchphrase, the focus on merchandising and branding, it’s all a push to create fan interest," Galbraith wrote. "Trump’s angriest moments come when something stands between him and the IV drip of love from the MAGA mob. As the Epstein-based schism among his base has shown, Trump truly spins out when he fails to control the narrative."

The editor continues, noting that, "In that light, it’s easy to explain his hate for Taylor Swift. He wants what she has."

"The pop star has an iron grip on her own story. Her every move is calculated to stir up speculation from the horde of Swifties, whose devotion is typically rewarded thanks to Swift’s own attention to detail," he wrote. "Swift announced her 12th album earlier this week. Her ultra-calculated announcement dropped at 12 minutes after midnight. The announcement itself was teased in an Instagram post of 12 photos of Swift wearing orange on her Eras tour. A simultaneous announcement was teased by her boyfriend Travis Kelce’s podcast in a clip with an orange background."

According to Galbraith, "Trump is far too impulsive, and the people around him too sloppy, to ever pull off something so meticulous. His strongarming of data-producing agencies, museums and libraries is an attempt to make history show that he was always adored."

Our Analysis:

Taylor Swift vs. Donald Trump: A Battle for Adoration

In the realm of public adoration, few rivalries seem as asymmetric yet intriguing as the one between pop megastar Taylor Swift and former President Donald Trump. According to Alex Galbraith of Salon, Trump's apparent ire towards Swift isn't just simple celebrity squabble—it's envy. Galbraith's analysis, titled "The tortured president’s society," posits that Trump's unpredictable and brash nature masks a simple truth: he yearns for the kind of unwavering fan devotion that Swift commands.

Trump's Quest for a "Stan" Army

Galbraith argues that Trump's antics—from his nickname creations to his merchandising efforts—are all aimed at cultivating an uncritical fan base, a "MAGA mob" to offer him an "IV drip of love". This is where Swift, with her meticulous management of her public image and her ability to mobilize her "Swifties," becomes an object of envy for Trump. Unlike Trump, Swift has mastered the art of narrative control, turning every album release, every public appearance, and every social media post into a carefully orchestrated event that galvanizes her fan base.

Swift's Calculated Dominance

Take, for example, Swift's announcement of her 12th album. The precision timing at 12 minutes after midnight, the thematic cohesion of 12 photos wearing orange, and the synchronized announcement via her boyfriend's podcast—all these elements showcase Swift's iron grip on her narrative. Each move is calculated to not just make waves but to reward her fans' devotion with meticulously crafted breadcrumbs.

Trump's Impulsive Approach

In stark contrast, Trump's approach is painted as impulsive and sloppy. His attempts to control the narrative often involve strong-arming data-producing agencies and rewriting history to paint himself in a favorable light. Yet, as Galbraith points out, these efforts fall short of achieving the kind of seamless narrative control Swift wields with seemingly effortless grace.

Analysis: Beyond Envy, A Tale of Two Brands

Galbraith's assessment, while intriguing, barely scratches the surface of what truly differentiates Swift's and Trump's approaches to their public personas. It's not just about the envy of a fan base; it's about the fundamental difference in how they view and interact with their audiences.

Swift treats her fans as partners in a shared journey, rewarding their devotion with personal attention and carefully crafted art. Trump, on the other hand, views his followers more as a monolithic bloc to be mobilized for support, often through divisive rhetoric and sensationalism.

Furthermore, Swift's strategy underscores the power of authenticity and consistency in building a brand. Trump's strategy, while undeniably effective in its own right, often relies on controversy and confrontation—a testament to his reality TV roots but a stark contrast to Swift's polished pop persona.

Conclusion: The Power of Narrative Control

Ultimately, Galbraith's piece highlights a crucial aspect of modern celebrity: the power of narrative control. Swift's success in this arena isn't just a matter of jealousy for Trump; it's a case study in the importance of brand management, audience engagement, and the meticulous crafting of public image. While Trump certainly has his own brand of celebrity, his inability to emulate Swift's level of narrative control and fan devotion is perhaps a reminder that in the age of social media, the pen (or the tweet) might be mightier than the rally.
