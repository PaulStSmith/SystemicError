'Dear God': Trump’s 'patriotic' AI slop art exhibit goes down in flames

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20262 min read

Patriotic Wallpaper for an Authoritarian State

The Exhibit Is the Point

A State Department exhibition at the Art Museum of the Americas is presenting AI-scrubbed Founding Fathers, flag paintings, and shallow nationalist kitsch as if this were cultural policy rather than political decoration. The reporting is useful because it shows what the administration is actually funding: not art, but a rehearsed fantasy of uncomplicated national pride.

Power Picked the Show

This is not a neutral museum mistake. The decision chain runs through Trump’s government, which has already pressed museum leaders into canceling shows that featured international Black and queer artists after the administration’s DEI ban. That is institutional power doing what it always does when it is confident enough: censoring one set of artists, then replacing them with safer propaganda that flatters the state.

Nepotism With a Flag on It

The exhibition’s organizers tell the real story. Erin Scavino, the Art in Embassies director, comes from the Trump inner circle as the wife of the deputy chief of staff and a former Apprentice contestant. Another organizer was pulled from running a pet-food store into a top national arts post. The reporting says there was no historian or art curator involved. That is not an accident of staffing. It is the governing principle: loyalty first, competence last, aesthetics as a social club for insiders.

The Misdirection Is Intentional

The article’s surface topic is bad art. The actual subject is state misdirection. When officials stage an exhibition like this, they are trying to launder power through patriotic language and visual noise. “Passport to Patriotism” sounds like a cultural program, but it functions like a compliance ritual. The AI portraits, the red-white-and-blue abstraction, the amateurish symbolism, all of it narrows American identity down to a decorative screen for executive authority.

Culture as Permission Slip

This is the same pattern that drives authoritarian politics elsewhere in the administration: defund or exclude the people who complicate the story, then elevate loyal mediocrities who will dress up the regime in innocent-looking forms. The result is not just embarrassing art. It is an arts apparatus trained to obey the president’s prejudices and call that national culture.

The System Behind the Trash

What looks like crude, forgettable exhibition curation is really a portrait of how power works under Trump: purge institutional independence, install personal allies, and convert public platforms into loyalty theater. The bad art matters less than the governing logic behind it. A state that fears nuance will always prefer propaganda with a frame.
