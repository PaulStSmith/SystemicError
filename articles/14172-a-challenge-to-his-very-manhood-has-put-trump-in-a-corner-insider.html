'A challenge to his very manhood' has put Trump in a corner: insider

Two setbacks suffered by Donald Trump this past week –– one legal, the other reputational –– has put him in a corner where he may have to double down on his tariff war to save face despite the possibility it could further s cripple his second term.

That is the opinion of one ally of the president who doesn't work in the White House and believes the present feels mortally wounded.

On the heels of losing a tariff case reviewed by the U.S. Court of International Trade, the president was made aware that he has become a joke among Wall Street traders who freely use "Trump Always Chickens Out" (TACO) when it comes to market bets of his tariff threats.

EXCLUSIVE: Trump accused of new grift that puts Qatari plane in shade

According to a report from Politico, the president and his inner circle see the setbacks and the mockery jeopardizing a central part of his economic policies which may lead him to double down instead of easing off on his constant tariff threats to calm both the markets and voters as his approval numbers tumble.

As Politcio's Megan Messerly, Eli Stokols and Adam Cancryn wrote, "Trump is digging in on his vow to impose steep tariffs by any means necessary — and stick it to those who question his strength and think he’s bound to 'chicken out.' He and administration officials have said that negotiations with other countries will continue, are insisting they’ll win their current tariff battle in court and are even preparing back-up strategies for new tariffs in case they don’t."

According to the insider, the president's fear of looking weak will likely force him to go down the wrong path.

“I don’t think Trump can back down now, mainly because of this TACO theme,” they explained. “He’s clearly super irritated by it and it’s like a challenge to his very manhood now.”

Former Trump adviser Marc Short is of the same mind, remarking, "The president is not one to accept defeat. He certainly didn’t in 2020. It’s not like because he had a bad court ruling he’s going to turn his back on this.”

The report goes on to note that Trump allies outside the White House worry his inner circle isn't counseling him to reconsider his options.

“Whether you’re for tariffs or not, it’s pretty clear the president doesn’t have unilateral authority to raise taxes,” cautioned longtime Trump advocate Stephen Moore.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Descent into Policy Paralysis

In a week that could only be described as disastrous for Donald Trump, the former president has managed to corner himself into a policy position that not only exacerbates his legal and reputational woes but also threatens to further destabilize his hypothetical second term. The essence of these setbacks, as reported, paints a picture of a leader whose decision-making process is not only flawed but fundamentally driven by ego and the fear of appearing weak.

Legal Losses and Laughing Stocks

First, let's dissect the legal defeat Trump faced at the U.S. Court of International Trade. Losing a tariff case is no minor setback, especially for a president who has touted tariffs as a cornerstone of his economic policy. This decision undermines his authority and questions the legality of his approach to trade and tariffs.

Then there's the reputational hit, which, in many ways, is far more damaging. The revelation that Wall Street traders have coined the acronym "TACO" (Trump Always Chickens Out) in reference to his tariff threats is a stark indicator of how his bluster is perceived in financial circles. This mockery not only chips away at Trump's self-styled image of a tough negotiator but also signals a lack of confidence in his economic policies.

Doubling Down on Disaster

The response from Trump and his inner circle to these setbacks is as predictable as it is misguided. Instead of reassessing their approach in light of clear signals from both the legal system and the market, they seem prepared to double down on the very policies that have led to these defeats. This stubbornness, driven by a fear of appearing weak, is nothing short of political self-sabotage.

The insider's comment about the TACO theme being a challenge to Trump's "very manhood" is particularly telling. It suggests that Trump's decisions are not being guided by sound policy considerations but by an obsession with personal image and strength. This is a dangerous basis for decision-making, especially when the economic well-being of the country is at stake.

The Echo Chamber Effect

The concerns raised by Trump allies outside the White House are noteworthy. They highlight a significant issue within Trump's administration: an echo chamber effect, where dissenting voices and critical advice are sidelined. Stephen Moore's caution that the president doesn't have unilateral authority to raise taxes is a critical reminder of the limits of executive power and the necessity of adhering to legal and procedural norms.

Conclusion: A Path Paved with Peril

Trump's refusal to accept defeat, as noted by former adviser Marc Short, is not a sign of strength; it's a manifestation of weakness. A strong leader would recognize the folly of their ways and adapt. Instead, Trump's insistence on pursuing a failed policy, in the face of overwhelming evidence of its ineffectiveness, is a testament to a flawed leadership style that prioritizes personal pride over public good.

In sum, Trump's tariff turmoil is more than just a series of setbacks; it's a case study in how not to govern. It underscores the dangers of a leadership style that is reactive, ego-driven, and resistant to critical feedback. As Trump seemingly prepares to further entrench himself in these failed policies, one can only hope that clearer heads will prevail, though history suggests we shouldn't hold our breath.
