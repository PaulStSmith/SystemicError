‘Routine training!’ JD Vance defends plan to fire live artillery during No Kings protest

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. Vice President JD Vance boards Air Force Two at Concord–Padgett Regional Airport, Concord, North Carolina, U.S. September 24, 2025. (Alex Brandon/Pool via REUTERS)

A spokesperson for Vice President JD Vance defended the Trump administration’s plan to fire artillery shells over a major California freeway on Saturday, the same day as the nationwide No Kings protests, and refuted concerns expressed by California Gov. Gavin Newsom as unfounded.

Newsom’s office first received reports earlier this week that Marines at Camp Pendleton near San Diego were planning to fire artillery shells Saturday in a training exercise, and as part of a celebration of the Marine Corps’ 250th birthday, as reported by The Los Angeles Times on Wednesday. At the time, Newsom said the unconfirmed reports, if true, were not “the right way to go about” honoring the Marines’ anniversary.

The New York Times confirmed the reports to be accurate on Saturday, and in a statement to the outlet, Newsom condemned the plan as a “profoundly absurd show of force that could put Californians directly in harm’s way.”

“Using our military to intimidate people you disagree with isn’t strength – it’s reckless, it’s disrespectful, and it’s beneath the office the president holds,” Newsom told the Times.

William Martin, Vance’s communications director, however, fired back at Newsom over his concerns, and invited the California governor to try and oppose the military exercise.

“Gavin Newsom wants people to think this exercise is dangerous; the Marine Corps says it’s an established and safe practice,” Martin said in a statement as reported by the Times Saturday.

“Newsom wants people to think this is an absurd show of force. The Marine Corps says it’s part of routine training at Camp Pendleton. If Gavin Newsom wants to oppose the training exercises that ensure our Armed Forces are the deadliest and most lethal fighting force in the world, then he can go right ahead.”

The announcement has already led to a temporary suspension of airspace around Camp Pendleton, the Times reported, along with the passenger rail Amtrakcancelling rail servicesin the area on Saturday from 12 p.m. to 3 p.m. Vance is expected to attend the event.

Our Analysis:

A Critical Analysis: The Artillery Exercise Over California Freeway

In a display that could only be concocted in the most surreal corners of a political strategist’s mind, the Trump administration has endorsed a plan to fire artillery shells over a major California freeway. This decision, ostensibly to honor the Marine Corps’ 250th birthday, intersects alarmingly with the nationwide No Kings protests scheduled for the same day. This move has sparked a fiery exchange between California Governor Gavin Newsom and Vice President JD Vance’s spokesperson, William Martin. Let’s dissect the layers of absurdity and potential recklessness this plan entails.

The Provocation

Firstly, the timing of this military exercise is, at best, provocatively tone-deaf and, at worst, an intentional display of power against the backdrop of civil unrest. The No Kings protests, symbolizing a widespread outcry against authoritarian tendencies, will now have the added backdrop of live artillery shells. This imagery, ripe for authoritarian regimes, seems misplaced in a democratic republic.

The Response

Governor Newsom’s condemnation of the plan as a “profoundly absurd show of force” is not an overstatement. To utilize military exercises in such a manner, especially over populated areas, is to blur the lines between demonstrating military might and intimidating civilian populations. Newsom’s concerns about the safety and appropriateness of this event are legitimate, grounded in a responsibility to safeguard Californian lives and public peace.

William Martin’s rebuttal, suggesting that opposition to the exercise is akin to opposing the readiness and lethality of the armed forces, is a gross mischaracterization of the issue at hand. The debate is not about the necessity of military preparedness but about the appropriateness of the time, place, and manner of this demonstration.

The Risks

Highlighting the potential risks, the article notes that the announcement has led to a temporary suspension of airspace around Camp Pendleton and the cancellation of Amtrak rail services in the area. These disruptions underscore the impact of the exercise on civilian life and raise questions about the unforeseen consequences of such a display. What’s deemed an “established and safe practice” in military contexts does not automatically translate to safety in densely populated civilian areas, especially when factoring in the unpredictability of public reactions and potential for misinterpretation.

The Underlying Issues

This situation exemplifies a broader issue within the current administration’s approach: a penchant for dramatic displays of force and a disregard for public concern in the pursuit of political objectives. It’s a stark reminder of the importance of civilian oversight of the military and the need for political leaders to engage in thoughtful deliberation before endorsing actions that could escalate tensions or endanger public safety.

In conclusion, the decision to conduct an artillery exercise over a major California freeway, coinciding with nationwide protests, is not just a “show of force.” It’s a misguided show of priorities that risks public safety and civil peace for the sake of political theater. Governor Newsom’s stand, far from opposing military readiness, is a call for rationality and restraint in times of domestic unrest. The administration would do well to heed such calls, rather than dismissing them in the pursuit of spectacle.
