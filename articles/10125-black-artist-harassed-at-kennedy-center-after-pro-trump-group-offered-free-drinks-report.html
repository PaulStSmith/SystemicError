Black artist harassed at Kennedy Center after pro-Trump group offered free drinks: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 25, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Grenell Gambit: A Symphony of Pettiness at the Kennedy Center

Yasmin Williams at the Kennedy Center (YouTube screenshot)

In what appears to be an act of retaliation by Kennedy Center head Richard Grenell, the Donald Trump appointee made available passes to an antagonist pro-Trump group to come to a performance of a Black artist he has been arguing with on social media.

Despite the efforts of Grenell to stop artists from cancelling at the cultural institution that has been reeling since the Trump takeover, the Washingtonian is reporting that he offered tickets to the Log Cabin Republicans, with the report stating, “A member of Grenell’s staff even distributed passes and welcomed the men as they arrived, a current staffer confirmed for Washingtonian.”

According to a report from USA Today, guitarist and composer Yasmin Williams shared screenshots on Instagram as evidence of a fight she had with Grenell last week where she wrote, “I hope posting this email thread illuminates the terrible folks who are now leading the Kennedy Center. I’ve had so many amazing career and life highlights at the Kennedy Center and I hate to think that the establishment could be run down permanently by this man.”

RELATED: Artists facing ‘80% empty seats’ or more at Kennedy Center after Trump takeover: report

On Wednesday, the Washington Post reported that her performance was immediately interrupted with cat-calling.

“The brief boos, which can be heard in a live-streamed video posted by the center, were the only disruption of the performance, Williams said, and the hecklers left after about 15 minutes into the show. Williams said she saw a few MAGA hats in the audience. Moments before taking the stage, she added, a Kennedy Center staffer told her she would need to be escorted out of the venue through a ‘secret exit’ by security,” the report notes.

“The heckling, she said, came from Trump supporters and members of the local chapter of Log Cabin Republicans, a nonprofit organization that represents LGBTQ conservatives and allies. In an email from the group Williams posted to Instagram, the organization encouraged its members to attend her show ahead of their monthly meeting that day on the center’s rooftop terrace,” the Post is reporting adding that Andrew Minik, president of the Log Cabin Republicans of D.C. promised drinks would be provided.

“She is apparently vocal opponent of President Trump and The Kennedy Center’s decision to hold a vigil for Charlie Kirk. Let’s make sure the audience is filled with patriots!” he wrote and then added drink tickets would be available and for members to “arrive early for the performance to take advantage!”

Despite video evidence of booing, according to the Washington Post, Kennedy Center spokesperson Roma Daravi complained, “This is an absolutely ridiculous claim. There was no coordinated effort by the Kennedy Center. Grenell had no involvement. We did not even know they were coming,” adding, “They did not heckle and frankly it is defamation of character for her to say that — she however bashed Grenell and the Center from the Kennedy Center stage. Republicans are patrons too and they are welcome at the Kennedy Center just like anyone else.”

Our Analysis:

The Grenell Gambit: A Symphony of Pettiness at the Kennedy Center

In a move that reads like a subplot ripped from the most melodramatic of political thrillers, Richard Grenell, a Donald Trump appointee and current head of the Kennedy Center, has orchestrated what can only be described as a petty act of retaliation. His weapon of choice? Passes to a performance of a Black artist he’s been sparring with on social media, generously gifted to an antagonist pro-Trump group, the Log Cabin Republicans. This isn’t just a story about tickets; it’s a narrative that lays bare the ongoing politicization of cultural institutions under the Trump administration, and the emboldenment of its appointees to wield power in the pettiest ways imaginable.

The Incident: A Concert Turned Confrontation

The artist at the center of this controversy, guitarist and composer Yasmin Williams, found herself facing off not just against Grenell but against an audience peppered with MAGA hats and emboldened hecklers. The Washingtonian and USA Today reports paint a vivid picture: Williams, a vocal critic of Trump and Grenell, was subjected to cat-calling by Trump supporters and members of the Log Cabin Republicans during her performance. This wasn’t just a group of individuals who happened upon tickets; this was a coordinated effort, with the Log Cabin Republicans being encouraged to “make sure the audience is filled with patriots!” courtesy of an email blast that even promised drink tickets for early birds.

The Kennedy Center’s Response: Denial and Deflection

In what can only be described as a masterclass in denial, the Kennedy Center’s spokesperson, Roma Daravi, rejected any suggestion of coordinated heckling or Grenell’s involvement as “ridiculous” and “defamation of character.” This response is as laughable as it is transparent. Video evidence and eyewitness accounts corroborate Williams’ experience, yet the Kennedy Center’s stance is to bury its head in the sand, dismissing legitimate concerns about the politicization of its space and the safety of its performers.

The Bigger Picture: The Politicization of Cultural Spaces

This incident isn’t an isolated skirmish; it’s symptomatic of a larger, more disturbing trend of cultural institutions being co-opted for political agendas. The Kennedy Center, a beacon of the performing arts, has been thrust into the political arena, weaponized by appointees like Grenell who seem more interested in settling personal scores than in upholding the Center’s mission to celebrate and foster the arts.

Republicans Are Patrons Too? A Misguided Justification

The Kennedy Center’s claim that “Republicans are patrons too” and thus should be welcome misses the point entirely. The issue isn’t about political affiliations of patrons but about the weaponization of those affiliations to intimidate and silence artists. The claim attempts to redirect the narrative, framing it as an issue of inclusivity, when in reality, it’s about the abuse of power and the undermining of artistic freedom.

In Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The Grenell gambit at the Kennedy Center is more than a mere spat between a political appointee and an artist; it’s a glaring example of how cultural institutions can be turned into battlegrounds for petty political vendettas. It’s incumbent upon those who value artistic freedom and the integrity of cultural spaces to call out and hold accountable those who would use their positions of power to undermine both. The Kennedy Center should serve as a sanctuary for the arts, not a stage for political retribution.
