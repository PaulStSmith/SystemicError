Trump lobs new threats at pro football team: 'I may put a restriction on them'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 21, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Political Coercion Disguised as Nostalgia

Republican presidential nominee former President Donald Trump sits with Susie Wiles as he attends the New York Jets football game against the Pittsburgh Steelers at Acrisure Stadium, Sunday, Oct. 20, 2024, in Pittsburgh. Evan Vucci/Pool via REUTERS

President Donald Trump is threatening to derail a stadium deal if a professional football team doesn't change its name, according to a post from the president on Truth Social.

Trump posted that he won't approve of the Washington Commanders building a new stadium in Washington, D.C. unless the team changes its name back to the "Redskins." The team was nicknamed the "Redskins" from 1933 through 2022. The team changed its name after growing public outcry and threats of losing its stadium sponsor, FedEx.

"I may put a restriction on them that if they don’t change the name back to the original 'Washington Redskins,' and get rid of the ridiculous moniker, 'Washington Commanders,' Trump said in the post. "I won’t make a deal for them to build a Stadium in Washington. The Team would be much more valuable, and the Deal would be more exciting for everyone."

Some professional sports teams have moved away from using Native American imagery or names over the last several years. For instance, the Cleveland Indians MLB franchise changed its name to the Cleveland Guardians in 2021.

However, other franchises like the Atlanta Braves, Chicago Blackhawks, and Kansas City Chiefs did not change their names.

Trump added that the Cleveland Guardians should also change their name back to the Indians, and claimed that Matt Dolan, who is a member of the family that owns the team, had lost three elections because of the name change.

Dolan is a Republican state Senator in Ohio and recently challenged Sen. Sherrod Brown (D-OH) in the 2024 senatorial election.

"What he doesn’t understand is that if he changed the name back to the Cleveland Indians, he might actually win an Election," Trump wrote. "Indians are being treated very unfairly. MAKE INDIANS GREAT AGAIN (MIGA)!"

Our Analysis:

The Art of Political Coercion Disguised as Nostalgia

In a recent eruption from the social media volcano that is Truth Social, former President Donald Trump has once again showcased his unique blend of nostalgia and coercion. Threatening to block the construction of a new stadium for the Washington Commanders unless they revert to their previous name, the "Redskins," Trump's post is a masterclass in how not to engage in civic dialogue.

The Power Play: Trump’s threat to derail the stadium deal unless the team name is changed back to the Redskins is a blatant display of political muscle-flexing, albeit without the subtlety or understanding of the underlying issues. The team's name was changed from the Redskins—a term widely recognized as a racial slur against Native Americans—after significant public outcry and pressure from sponsors, including FedEx. Trump’s disregard for this progress in favor of personal preference or political strategy is as alarming as it is revealing.

The Misguided Nostalgia: Trump's call for a return to the "original" name under the guise of value and excitement is a classic move in the playbook of those resisting change. However, he fails to acknowledge the societal shift towards greater recognition of and respect for cultural sensitivities. The renaming of the Washington Commanders and the Cleveland Guardians were steps towards rectifying historical wrongs and insensitivities—a concept Trump seems eager to dismiss for the sake of nostalgia.

Fact Check: Trump’s claim that the Cleveland Guardians' owner, Matt Dolan, lost three elections because of the team's name change is not just incorrect—it's a deliberate misinformation. Dolan, a Republican state Senator in Ohio, has not lost three elections post-name change. Furthermore, linking the success of political campaigns to sports team names is a bizarre leap that undermines the intelligence of the electorate and ignores the numerous factors that contribute to electoral outcomes.

The Misuse of MAGA: Trump's attempt to repurpose his "Make America Great Again" slogan into "MAKE INDIANS GREAT AGAIN" (MIGA) is not only a trivialization of the genuine concerns Native Americans have with such mascots and team names, but it is also a gross misunderstanding of the term "Indians" in this context. The original MAGA slogan, while controversial, at least attempted to speak to a broader national sentiment. MIGA, on the other hand, seems to miss the point entirely—opting for a catchy acronym over meaningful discourse on racial sensitivity and respect.

In conclusion, Trump's latest outburst is a potent reminder of the divisive rhetoric that characterized his presidency. It underscores a refusal to acknowledge societal progress and a preference for political strategies that stoke division rather than foster understanding. The insistence on reverting to outdated and offensive team names under the guise of enhancing value and excitement is a misguided nostalgia trip that we can, and should, do without. The path forward should be paved with respect, understanding, and an appreciation for the evolving societal norms that guide us toward a more inclusive future.
