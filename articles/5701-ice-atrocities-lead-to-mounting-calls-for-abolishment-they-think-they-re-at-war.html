ICE 'atrocities' lead to mounting calls for abolishment: 'They think they're at war!'

By Paulo Santos

Some Democrats sounded the alarm Wednesday and broke with their party over what they describe as an out-of-control Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency that has become a magnet for bad actors.

As some Democrats call for reforming ICE, some Democrats struck a more extreme tone, including Reps. Shri Thanedar (D-MI) and Rep. Juan Vargas (D-CA), who called for the agency to be eliminated entirely.

Thanedar swatted away any notion that a deal with President Donald Trump and Republicans could assuage his concerns after federal immigration agents had deployed brutal tactics on U.S. citizens as part of a nationwide crackdown on immigration.

“Well, with what’s going on, across our cities, the atrocities, the killing, the murdering of citizens, arresting little kids. What can they do? You know, there is nothing there for me to change my vote. I’m hell-bent on voting no. Strong no on this bill,” Thanedar declared to Raw Story.

“We need to abolish ICE and restart over. ICE is totally out of control,” he declared.

His blistering critique didn’t stop there. Thanedar pointed to reporting that ICE is recruiting “racist” and “power-hungry” recruits who lack basic training.

“They have no infrastructure to train them adequately. And with this $50,000 signing bonus, they’re attracting the wrong people,” he added.

Thanedar said the Trump administration is spreading hate and attracting the “saddest” candidates.

Vargas painted an even darker picture, comparing ICE agents’ conduct to military operations on domestic soil. He also wants to abolish ICE.

“I’m not supporting them in any way,” he told Raw Story, noting ICE agents are “invading homes” and using flash grenades, masks and more.

“All this s---. They think that they’re at war in our own country,” Vargas said.

Vargas, who served on the San Diego City Council for nearly 8 years, drew a stark contrast with professional law enforcement.

“I know what good law enforcement is like, and this is the worst, worst I’ve ever seen,” he said.

Rep. Mark Pocan (D-WI) said ICE has “ruined” its reputation with Americans.

“I think we’re using dismantle, or I use ‘F--- ICE,’ for language,” he told Raw Story about Democrats’ messaging on ICE’s future.

Their remarks notably break from other Democrats, including Rep. Bennie Johnson, who told Raw Story, “ICE has a purpose,” but needs to be reined in.

“But you got to fix it. Overall, it has to stand the same scrutiny of any other federal law enforcement agents. You know, nothing special,” he said.

Our Analysis:

The Great ICE Divide: A Democratic Schism

In a political landscape fraught with division, even within its own ranks, the Democratic Party finds itself at a crossroads over the future of the Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agency. A faction of Democrats, led by voices like Reps. Shri Thanedar (D-MI) and Juan Vargas (D-CA), is not just pushing the envelope but tearing it to shreds with calls for the outright abolition of ICE. This stance starkly contrasts with more measured calls for reform from other quarters of the party. The divide is not just about policy but the soul of the Democratic response to an agency that has, in the eyes of some, gone rogue.

The Abolitionist Stance

Thanedar’s comments to Raw Story are nothing short of incendiary. With a passion fueled by reports of “atrocities” and “brutal tactics” against U.S. citizens, Thanedar’s stance is a blistering indictment of ICE under the Trump administration. His rhetoric, peppered with references to “the killing, the murdering of citizens, arresting little kids,” is a clarion call to action. It’s not just the actions of ICE that Thanedar condemns but the very essence of its recruitment strategy, highlighting the alleged $50,000 signing bonuses attracting what he sees as “racist” and “power-hungry” individuals.

Vargas, drawing on his experience in local government, paints ICE’s operations as militaristic invasions, likening their tactics to “war in our own country.” This comparison to military operations on domestic soil is not just hyperbole; it’s a deliberate framing meant to shock the conscience. Vargas’s experience in law enforcement adds a layer of credibility to his critique, positioning him as a witness to the degradation of standards in federal law enforcement under ICE’s current modus operandi.

The Call for Reform

Contrasting with the abolitionist fervor of Thanedar and Vargas, other Democrats are navigating a more cautious path. Rep. Bennie Johnson’s remarks exemplify this approach, acknowledging ICE’s “purpose” while emphasizing the need for stringent oversight. This stance, while less fiery, suggests a belief in the possibility of redemption for ICE through reform and restructuring.

Analysis: Between a Rock and a Hard Place

The Democratic schism over ICE underscores a broader struggle within the party: how to address systemic issues in law enforcement without alienating moderate constituents who might be wary of radical change. The abolitionists, with their stark, uncompromising rhetoric, are laying bare a fundamental dissatisfaction with not just ICE but the broader system of immigration enforcement in the United States. Their radical solution—abolishing and starting afresh—speaks to a deep-seated belief that the agency’s issues are not just superficial but structural.

However, this approach raises critical questions about feasibility and the aftermath of such a drastic move. Abolishing ICE without a clear plan for its replacement or the reassignment of its duties could lead to operational chaos and a vacuum in federal law enforcement capabilities, particularly in areas critical to national security and immigration control.

In contrast, the reformist approach, while potentially more palatable to a broader audience, risks being seen as too incremental or superficial, especially by those who have lost faith in the agency’s ability to change. This middle-of-the-road strategy may also fail to address the root causes of the issues at hand, offering only cosmetic changes to a fundamentally flawed system.

Conclusion: A Defining Moment

The Democratic Party’s internal debate over ICE represents a defining moment in its approach to immigration and law enforcement. As the 2020 elections loom, the party must reconcile these divergent views into a cohesive policy stance. The outcome of this debate will not only shape the future of ICE but also signal the Democratic Party’s commitment to systemic change versus incremental reform. In this context, the voices of Thanedar, Vargas, and others are not just dissenting opinions but a challenge to the party’s conscience and direction. Will the Democrats choose radical surgery or opt for a more conservative treatment? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: the status quo is no longer tenable.
