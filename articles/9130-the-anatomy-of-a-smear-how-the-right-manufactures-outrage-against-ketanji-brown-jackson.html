The Anatomy of a Smear: How the Right Manufactures Outrage Against Ketanji Brown Jackson

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 1, 20256 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

When propaganda dresses itself in civility, it smells even worse. The Western Journal’s recent screed — “Even Fellow Liberal Supreme Court Justices Are Getting Fed Up with Ketanji Brown Jackson” — reprinted by MSN for mass consumption, is a case study in how racial resentment masquerades as political commentary.

It begins with the oldest of American lies: that diversity equals mediocrity. “When you use skin color and chromosomes as criteria,” the author lectures, “you will not get the best people for the job.” Translation: a Black woman on the Supreme Court must be there by accident. The statement doesn’t just insult Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson; it exposes the writer’s inability to grasp the simplest facts.

Justice Jackson didn’t appear out of a diversity program or an act of charity. She was valedictorian, Harvard Law graduate, editor of the Harvard Law Review, federal public defender, vice chair of the U.S. Sentencing Commission, and a federal judge on the D.C. Circuit. That résumé isn’t “DEI.” It’s constitutional literacy, institutional experience, and moral spine — three things the Western Journal hasn’t demonstrated in decades.

But the attack isn’t meant to inform. It’s meant to frame: to recycle the trope of the “angry, unqualified Black woman” as a political weapon. The author lifts selective quotes from a New York Times piece describing internal dynamics among the Court’s liberals and inflates them into a fantasy of “liberal infighting.”

In reality, the Times article was a sober analysis of how Justices Kagan, Sotomayor, and Jackson navigate a Court dominated by an aggressive conservative supermajority. It explored their differences in tone, not principle — a reflection of strategy, not schism. To anyone literate in institutional politics, this would be obvious. To the Western Journal, it’s an opportunity to manufacture conflict where none exists, because “Democrats in Disarray” is the only tune they know how to play.

The piece then performs a grotesque rhetorical pivot. Having painted Jackson as abrasive and alienating, it declares that she “threatened” relationships with Justices Kagan and Barrett — as if women working together must be measured by their ability to please others. The sexism is baked right into the syntax: Jackson’s independence isn’t conviction; it’s attitude. Her dissent isn’t jurisprudence; it’s insolence.

Then comes the constitutional gaslighting. The author proclaims that “nothing threatens the constitutional order more than an all-powerful judiciary,” accusing the Court’s liberals of embracing an “imperial Judiciary.” Yet, somehow, this concern never seems to apply to the conservative majority that dismantled Roe v. Wade, gutted the Voting Rights Act, and handed corporations more personhood than actual people.

The cognitive dissonance is staggering. The same crowd that cheers when the Court strikes down gun regulations or environmental protections suddenly pretends to fear “judicial overreach” when the justices defend civil rights. It’s not principle. It’s power — and who gets to wield it.

The author’s historical illiteracy then blossoms into full absurdity: “The Constitution does not authorize SCOTUS to interpret the Constitution.” That sentence alone could disqualify him from teaching civics to a fifth-grade class. Judicial review has been the Supreme Court’s duty since Marbury v. Madison in 1803 — the foundation of checks and balances. To deny that is to deny the entire structure of American government. But, of course, that’s precisely the point. For the MAGA right, the Constitution is not a framework; it’s a weapon — something to swing at perceived enemies while ignoring its actual text.

To cement the propaganda, the writer casts Donald Trump as a martyr of judicial tyranny. The Court, we are told, has been “distorting the Constitution and undermining Trump.” This is the theological core of modern conservatism: the belief that any institution not subservient to their chosen leader is illegitimate. Never mind that Trump tried to overthrow an election. Never mind that federal courts, including Trump-appointed judges, rejected his legal farce dozens of times. In the revisionist gospel of the far right, the judiciary’s sin isn’t corruption — it’s independence.

And when the writer cites Justice Amy Coney Barrett’s majority opinion in a case limiting nationwide injunctions, he frames it as a personal rebuke of Jackson’s intellect. It wasn’t. It was a disagreement over administrative procedure — the kind of debate that happens every term between justices of every ideological stripe. But the author can’t resist transforming legal nuance into playground drama, because nuance doesn’t feed outrage clicks.

Even worse, he implies that liberal justices themselves leaked to the New York Times out of frustration with Jackson — a baseless smear that would never be leveled at male colleagues. In conservative media, when white men disagree, it’s called “vigorous debate.” When women of color assert themselves, it’s “friction.”

What’s truly “damning” here isn’t the Times report; it’s how the Western Journal weaponizes it. The message is simple: Black women should know their place, even on the highest court in the land. Stay quiet. Don’t dissent too much. Don’t outshine your colleagues. Don’t be smarter than the men who insult you.

Justice Jackson’s mere presence on the bench terrifies these people, because she embodies something they can’t corrupt — merit grounded in empathy, intellect tempered by lived experience. Her questions during oral arguments are razor-sharp dissections of power and history. She doesn’t grandstand; she educates. Her dissents read less like partisan broadsides and more like lessons in constitutional morality. She is what a justice should be — and that’s what the propagandists can’t stand.

So they project. They accuse her of “abrasiveness” while defending men who leak draft opinions and accept luxury vacations from billionaires. They cry about “judicial imperialism” while cheering decisions that strip Americans of bodily autonomy. They pretend to protect the Constitution while desecrating its core promise: equal justice under law.

The Western Journal’s article isn’t journalism. It’s vandalism — the intellectual equivalent of spray-painting slurs on the courthouse steps and calling it commentary. MSN, by republishing it, lent oxygen to a fire that should have suffocated in obscurity.

There’s a grim irony in watching people who once screamed “respect the courts!” during Bush v. Gore now treat the judiciary as a punching bag whenever it refuses to bend the knee to Trumpism. But the deeper danger lies in the normalization of this rot. When mainstream platforms amplify such garbage, they don’t just misinform — they corrode the public’s trust in fact itself.

Ketanji Brown Jackson doesn’t need anyone’s permission to dissent. The Constitution protects her right — and, more importantly, her duty — to do so. The men who wrote it never imagined that a Black woman would one day interpret their words, but that’s the beauty of a living democracy: it expands. It learns. It corrects. That’s what terrifies reactionaries — that the American story keeps rewriting itself to include those they’d rather erase.

The Western Journal’s tantrum won’t change that. History will not remember who wrote that smear. But it will remember Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson — for her intellect, her integrity, and her insistence that equality is not a “liberal” ideal, but the American one.

So yes, sprinkle lime over this pile of rot. Let it stop stinking up the discourse. Because beneath all the noise, one truth remains: the people screaming loudest about “merit” are almost always the ones most afraid of being measured by it.

Thanks to MSN for reminding us how low “journalism” can sink.
