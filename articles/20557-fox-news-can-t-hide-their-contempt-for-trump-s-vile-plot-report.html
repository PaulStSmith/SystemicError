Fox News can't hide their contempt for Trump's 'vile' plot: report

Based on an article by Thomas Kika

Paulo SantosJuly 8, 20263 min read

Trump Lost on Birthright Citizenship. The Right Is Still Trying to Cheat the Constitution.

A Defeat, Not a Detour

The source story is simple enough: Trump tried to erase birthright citizenship by executive order, the Supreme Court stopped him by a 5-4 vote, and now he and House Speaker Mike Johnson are floating the same outcome through legislation instead. That is the whole game. The constitutional route failed, so they are searching for a procedural side door.

Power Is Still the Point

The people with real institutional power here are not the voters Fox News poll-tested, and not the babies whose citizenship is being treated like a policy inconvenience. It is Trump, who set the attack in motion; Johnson, who is laundering it through Congress; and the bloc of justices the source notes would likely uphold a narrower statutory version of the same result.

This is not confusion. It is coordinated escalation. The executive order failed, so the next move is to see whether Congress can be dragged into doing what the Constitution would not allow by decree. That is how anti-democratic politics works when it meets a hard limit: it changes costumes, not goals.

Johnson’s Language Is the Cover Story

Johnson’s “legislative fix” framing is doing a lot of work. It turns a direct assault on citizenship into an administrative nuisance, as if the right is just tidying up a technical glitch. That language matters because it drains moral clarity from the issue. Citizenship is not a software bug. It is the legal status the right wants to make conditional, racialized, and politically disposable.

The phrase “birth tourism,” which the source notes has long been a right-wing rallying cry, is the tell. It is propaganda disguised as policy vocabulary. The goal is to make a constitutional right sound like a loophole exploited by outsiders, so the public sees exclusion as housekeeping.

The Public Is Not Where the Right Thinks It Is

The most damaging fact for Trump’s side is not the Supreme Court loss. It is that the politics are bad even on Fox’s own terrain. The source cites a Fox News poll showing 69 percent of Americans think a child born to an undocumented immigrant should automatically become a U.S. citizen, including majorities of noncollege white voters, rural whites, and white evangelicals.

That should end the conversation. Instead, the right keeps pressing because it is not governing for consensus. It is governing for grievance. When a movement has spent years training its base to treat immigration as contamination, it can keep losing the public and still keep winning access to power.

The Real Misdirection

What the article gets right is that the frame itself is part of the fraud. This is not a serious debate about a technical ambiguity in the law. It is an attempt to convert anger about immigration into a legal attack on the children of people the right has already marked for exclusion.

The misdirection is also political. The story asks whether Republicans might find a “legislative fix,” as if the question is constitutional craftsmanship. The deeper question is whether a major political faction can keep using every branch of government to narrow who counts as American while pretending it is merely responding to a problem.

The Pattern

This is the same authoritarian habit in a cleaner suit: fail in one venue, repackage the attack in another, and rely on institutions to normalize the next version of the harm. First comes the decree. Then comes the bill. Then comes the punditry that describes the effort as pragmatic.

Birthright citizenship is a useful target for that politics because it forces the fight over belonging into the open. The right wants a nation where citizenship is granted by power, not by birth. The source shows that Trump and Johnson are still trying to build that regime, even after the Court blocked their first draft.

The pattern is not legal hardball. It is the steady effort to turn democratic membership into a privilege that can be revoked by the people already in charge.
