GOP lawmakers put on notice Trump just put their futures in jeopardy with 'dangerous' move

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

The decision by the Donald Trump administration to force through an indictment against former FBI Director James Comey could come back to haunt Republican lawmakers and ruin their careers, according to some media analysts.

Addressing the baffling success of newly appointed U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan in presenting her case before a grand jury that no other prosecutor in her office would touch, because Trump instructed her to, MSNBC host Joe Scarborough claimed the door has now been opened for future presidents to use the same tactics against their own enemies.

The animated Scarborough told co-host Willie Geist, “As we’ve been saying about the FCC, as we’ve been saying about so many things that Donald Trump has been doing, especially over the past few weeks, Republicans must understand that what goes around comes around.”

“They must understand that if there’s a billionaire independent who decides to be president of the United States, gets elected and then decides to start going after Republicans, Republican senators, Republican House members, Republican judges, Republican political operatives, all that a president will have to do is just pick up the phone, call his or her attorney general and say, ‘Arrest Republican senator A, arrest Republican political opponent, B go after conservative think tank C.’”

“I mean, it’s again, extraordinarily dangerous,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Dangerous Precedent Set by Trump’s Indictment Tactics

In a recent development that reads like a page torn from a dystopian playbook, the Donald Trump administration’s decision to push for an indictment against former FBI Director James Comey has not just raised eyebrows but set off alarm bells regarding the health of American democracy. This maneuver, as dissected by media analysts and fervently discussed by MSNBC host Joe Scarborough, underscores a harrowing precedent that could spell disaster for the very fabric of American political life.

The Unprecedented Move

Let’s break down the core of this issue. The Trump administration, leveraging the power of newly appointed U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan, managed to present a case against James Comey to a grand jury—a task that, until now, no prosecutor in her office dared to touch. This wasn’t just a bold move; it was an audacious play that reeks of political vendettas being prioritized over justice. The fact that this was done at the behest of Trump himself, as Scarborough points out, opens a Pandora’s box of future political weaponization of the justice system.

The Warning Bell of Reciprocity

Scarborough’s analysis brings to light a chilling future scenario where this tactic becomes the norm, not the exception. The message is clear: what goes around comes around. The idea that a future president could replicate Trump’s actions against their political adversaries is not just a theoretical threat—it’s a looming reality. Scarborough vividly paints a picture where the mere dislike or political rivalry could lead to arrests and indictments, facilitated by a single phone call to the attorney general. This scenario, where political retribution is just a call away, is a stark departure from democratic norms and a step towards autocratic rule.

The Dangerous Path Ahead

The implications of this development cannot be overstressed. If the action taken against Comey is normalized or goes unchallenged, it sets a dangerous precedent that undermines the foundational principle of the rule of law. It signals a shift towards a more personalized form of justice, where legal actions are subject to the whims of those in power, rather than the impartial and fair application of the law. This is not just “extraordinarily dangerous,” as Scarborough puts it; it’s a potential death knell for democracy as we know it.

A Call to Action for Republicans

The most piercing part of Scarborough’s commentary is the direct call to Republican lawmakers to wake up to the monster they are helping to create. This isn’t just about the potential for future abuse against Republicans by a hypothetical independent billionaire president; it’s about the integrity of the American political system. Republicans must recognize that by supporting or even tacitly condoning such actions, they are not just undermining their political opponents; they are undermining the very system that upholds American democracy.

In conclusion, the Trump administration’s push to indict James Comey is not merely a political skirmish; it’s a harbinger of a possible future where political power is wielded like a weapon against personal and political enemies alike. This is a dangerous path, and it’s incumbent upon all, especially those within the Republican Party, to recognize the long-term implications of such actions. The health of American democracy depends on it.
