MAGA-friendly 'CBS Evening News' sees viewership plummet to alarming levels: report

By Paulo Santos

“CBS Evening News” crashed back below the dreaded 4 million viewer threshold following its MAGA-friendly makeover, a red line that previously triggered major shake-ups at the network, Variety reported Tuesday.

For the week ending March 13, the program drew just 3.83 million viewers, with a measly 468,000 in the advertiser-coveted 25-54 demographic, according to Nielsen data. The stark freefall comes after anchor Tony Dokoupil’s start in January, when he debuted with 4.17 million viewers. Shortly thereafter, he brought in 4.6 million.

Meanwhile, the competition is lapping CBS.

ABC’s “World News Tonight” commanded an average 8.48 million viewers, while NBC’s “Nightly News” pulled 6.51 million. In the key demographic, both networks crushed CBS with 1.03 million and 946,000 viewers, respectively.

“Quarter to date as of March 12, ‘CBS Evening News’ has shed 15% of its viewership in the critical 25-to-54 demo, the audience coveted by advertisers in news programming, over the year-earlier period. In comparison, NBC’s ‘NBC Nightly News’ is up 8% in the demo, while ABC’s ‘World News Tonight’ is off 4%,” the report said.

Former “CBS Evening News” anchor Norah O’Donnell, meanwhile, ended her tenure in 2024 with an audience of nearly 5.4 million.

New CBS News editor in chief, Bari Weiss, has faced widespread criticism from observers across the political spectrum since her installation and the network’s MAGA-friendly coverage.

Our Analysis:

The Misdirection in Viewer Metrics: A Closer Look at CBS’s Strategy

As CBS Evening News witnesses a concerning drop in viewership, the narrative quickly shifts towards the network’s recent editorial changes and the supposed MAGA-friendly makeover. However, to understand the true dynamics at play, we must dissect the situation through the lenses of institutional power, decision-making, and the accuracy of the blame placed by the narrative.

Institutional Power

The institutional power in this scenario lies with the CBS News hierarchy, specifically with the new editor in chief, Bari Weiss. Weiss’s editorial direction and decisions significantly influence the content and tone of the news. This power to shape the narrative and potentially alter the target audience is crucial in analyzing the outcome.

Decision-Making

The decision to adopt a MAGA-friendly approach was presumably made to capture a broader or different segment of viewers. This strategic pivot, led by Weiss and her team, aimed at revitalizing the program’s viewership. The introduction of Tony Dokoupil as the anchor and the subsequent editorial changes were calculated moves within this strategy.

Misdirected Blame?

The article frames the viewership decline as a direct consequence of the MAGA-friendly makeover. However, this oversimplifies and potentially misdirects responsibility. Several factors must be considered:

  1. Audience Fragmentation: The media consumption landscape is increasingly fragmented, with audiences dispersing across cable news, streaming platforms, and digital news. This broader trend affects all traditional news broadcasts, not just CBS.

  2. Competitive Positioning: The comparison with ABC’s “World News Tonight” and NBC’s “Nightly News” highlights a significant viewership gap. However, it fails to account for long-standing viewer loyalty and the distinct market positions of each network. CBS’s strategy may be an attempt to carve out a unique space in a competitive market, rather than a direct cause of the decline.

  3. Short-Term vs. Long-Term Effects: Strategic shifts in media often result in short-term losses before any potential long-term gains can be realized. Immediate declines might not accurately reflect the effectiveness or eventual outcome of the new editorial direction.

  4. External Factors: The article does not account for external factors such as news cycles, major events, or changes in viewer behavior that might influence ratings independently of CBS’s editorial choices.

Conclusion

While CBS Evening News’s drop in viewership is concerning, the narrative that exclusively attributes this decline to a MAGA-friendly makeover oversimplifies the situation. It places undue blame on the editorial direction while ignoring broader industry trends, competitive dynamics, and the inherent risks of strategic realignment.

Criticism of Bari Weiss and the network’s editorial choices may hold merit but must be contextualized within the complexities of the media landscape. It is essential to question whether the framing of the situation misdirects responsibility toward actors with limited control over broader viewer trends and preferences.

In assessing blame or praise, a nuanced understanding of the decision-making processes, the influence of institutional power, and the potential for narrative misdirection is crucial. Only by acknowledging these factors can we have a comprehensive and fair analysis of CBS Evening News’s current predicament.
