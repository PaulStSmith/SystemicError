Trump ridiculed by columnist's plan to reward him with what he wants

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump gestures during a visit to the Kennedy Center. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

New York Times opinion writer Frank Bruni offered an unorthodox solution Monday to what he described as President Donald Trump’s compulsion to “assert his dominance,” proposing a way to exploit what he said was the president’s endless “need for validation.”

“If we keep him busy with award ceremonies and bury him in gleaming trophies, glittering medallions and gaudily framed certificates, he might not be so free or feel so compelled to assert his dominance in other ways, such as stripping poor people of their health insurance, immigrants of their humanity, the judiciary of its integrity, academia of its autonomy, Democrats of winnable congressional districts and America of democracy,” Bruni wrote in an op-ed published Monday in The New York Times.

For well over a decade, Trump has complained about having not received one award or another.

“I should have gotten it,” Trump said in 2016 after his show on NBC “The Apprentice” failed to win the Emmy. He also complained on numerous occasions about not being nominated for a Nobel Peace Prize.

Bruni argued that Trump’s “obsession with tributes – or at least with tributes to him” could be harnessed to blunt his policy agenda, suggesting there should be no limit to the awards showered on the president.

“I figure that every second he’s on the Oscars stage is a second he’s not at the Resolute Desk, pursuing or setting some record for executive orders signed,” Bruni wrote. “That’s a big, beautiful trade-off, the likes of which the world has never seen.”

Beyond an Emmy and a Nobel Peace Prize nomination, Bruni floated the idea of presenting Trump with a Grammy, an Oscar and a Tony. Furthermore, Bruni proposed – perhaps in jest – that Trump should be presented with a Fields Medal, the highest honor for mathematicians under the age of 40.

“While it’s currently reserved for brainiacs under 40, such norms are for chumps, not Trumps, and matter less than the singular brilliance of this president, whose novel formula for calculating trade imbalances and tariffs captivated economists,” Bruni wrote.

“His analysis of election margins was equally innovative. In 2016, he converted the slenderest of triumphs in the Electoral College and a sizable loss of the popular vote into proof of ‘a historic movement, the likes of which the world has never seen,’ in the words of his own inauguration speech. (Is there a Nobel for self-congratulation?)”

Our Analysis:

The Glittering Distraction Strategy: A Satirical Masterpiece or a Dangerous Underestimation?

In the realm of political commentary, Frank Bruni’s recent op-ed in The New York Times takes a dive into the absurd with a proposal that is as laughable as it is concerning. Bruni suggests a strategy to mitigate President Donald Trump's destructive policies by drowning him in awards and recognition. This proposal, while clearly tongue-in-cheek, underscores a deeper misunderstanding of the gravity of Trump's actions and the seriousness with which they should be addressed.

The Flawed Premise: Awards as Pacifiers

Bruni argues that Trump’s "obsession with tributes" could be exploited to distract him from his more harmful policies. This suggestion not only trivializes the significant consequences of Trump's presidency but also dangerously underestimates the resilience of his policy agenda. To imply that “every second he’s on the Oscars stage is a second he’s not at the Resolute Desk” is to ignore the systemic and far-reaching impact of the administration's actions, which extend well beyond the president's immediate presence or attention.

The Misguided Solution: Satire or Insensitivity?

While Bruni’s proposal might be intended as satire, the tone-deafness of suggesting we can simply pacify a presidency with "gleaming trophies, glittering medallions, and gaudily framed certificates" is startling. It’s akin to suggesting that a child throwing a tantrum could be appeased with candy; except, in this case, the “tantrum” involves stripping away healthcare, dehumanizing immigrants, and undermining democratic institutions.

The Underlying Issue: A Distraction from Real Solutions

Perhaps the most glaring issue with Bruni's piece is not its fanciful nature but its potential to distract from the search for genuine solutions. The focus on Trump's vanity, while a valid criticism, overshadows the broader systemic problems that allow such a presidency to enact harmful policies in the first place. It’s a momentary chuckle that fades to the sobering reality of an administration’s actions that have real and lasting impacts on society.

The Satirical Edge: A Double-edged Sword

Satire can be a powerful tool in political commentary, serving to highlight absurdities and criticize through humor. However, when the subject is as consequential as the presidency of Donald Trump, there’s a fine line between illuminating the issues and trivializing them. Bruni's piece, for all its cleverness, leans dangerously toward the latter, offering a moment of levity at the cost of diluting the discourse with implausible solutions to serious problems.

Conclusion: Beyond the Laughter

In essence, Bruni's op-ed is a stark reminder of the challenges facing political satire in today's hyper-partisan, high-stakes environment. While it’s essential to find humor amidst the chaos, it’s equally crucial to recognize when laughter obscures the gravity of the situation. Trump's presidency, with its profound implications for American democracy and global stability, is no laughing matter. It demands a response grounded in strategic, thoughtful opposition — not just the fleeting satisfaction of imagining a president distracted by a shiny medal.
