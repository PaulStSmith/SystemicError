'Going in the wrong direction': Wall Street analysts raise red flag over Trump's economy

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Masterclass in Economic Mismanagement

With President Donald Trump’s tariffs already being felt across the American economy, the president has told Americans to be patient, and that his trade policy would soon usher in a resurgence of domestic manufacturing jobs.

And yet, as job growth slows and prices tick up, Trump’s promise to reshore domestic manufacturing has not only not come to fruition, it appears to be “going in the wrong direction,” according to one analyst who spoke with the Washington Post Saturday.

“We aren’t even seeing the beginnings of a tariff-related recovery in manufacturing,” said Den Baker, an economist and co-founder of the Center for Economic Policy Research in Washington, speaking with the Washington Post. “You don’t expect to see it overnight. But it’s going in the wrong direction.”

Trump’s tariff policy has already begun to take its toll on the American economy according to a number of economists; prices have risen, job growth has slowed dramatically, and inflation has ticked up.

Trump has told Americans to be patient, that his tariff policy would see the economy rebound by next year, and at the very least, the stiff tariffs would ignite an explosion of domestic manufacturing jobs, with the policies incentivizing companies to reshore their operations to avoid paying steep penalties.

However, the tariffs now appear to be having the opposite effect, with import taxes impacting supply chains, and the uncertainty of Trump’s tariff policy – with tariff rates being adjusted for specific countries multiple times, unpredictably – giving cause for companies to be hesitant with their investments.

“Uncertainty around tariff policy is limiting activity,” wrote Wells Fargo economists Shannon Grein and Tim Quinlan this week in ajoint analysis. “While the higher costs associated with tariffs are a challenge, the uncertainty around where tariffs ultimately land is likely more so limiting current activity today.”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Masterclass in Economic Mismanagement

In what can only be described as a catastrophic misreading of basic economics, President Donald Trump’s trade policy, heavily anchored in tariffs, has not only failed to deliver on its grand promises but has actively contributed to the downturn of the American economy. Despite Trump's rallying cry for patience, claiming that a resurgence of domestic manufacturing jobs is on the horizon, the reality unfolding is starkly different. Let's dissect this economic debacle with the precision it deserves, highlighting the chasm between political rhetoric and economic reality.

The Empty Promise of Reshoring Jobs

Trump's narrative, seductive in its simplicity, pledged to bring manufacturing jobs back to American soil through the imposition of stiff tariffs. However, the reality is starkly different, and as Den Baker, an economist and co-founder of the Center for Economic Policy Research, aptly puts it, we are not even witnessing "the beginnings of a tariff-related recovery in manufacturing." Far from it, the situation is "going in the wrong direction."

This isn't just a minor hiccup in an otherwise flawless plan. It's a glaring testament to the failure of understanding how modern economies and global supply chains operate. Tariffs, rather than encouraging domestic production, have led to increased prices, slowed job growth, and contributed to inflation—a triple threat to economic stability.

Economic Self-Sabotage: The High Cost of Tariffs

The logic—or lack thereof—behind Trump's tariff policy seems to have missed a crucial economic lesson: tariffs are taxes that are often ultimately borne by the consumer in the form of higher prices. This is not a theoretical outcome but a reality that's unfolding before our eyes, with economists pointing out the tangible toll these policies are taking on the American economy.

Moreover, the constant adjustments and unpredictability of tariff rates have done more than just raise prices. They have injected a potent dose of uncertainty into the economy, causing companies to hold back on investments. Wells Fargo economists Shannon Grein and Tim Quinlan hit the nail on the head, stating that "uncertainty around tariff policy is limiting activity." This isn't just an inconvenience; it's a self-inflicted wound on the economy's ability to grow and create jobs.

The Real Victims: American Workers and Consumers

At the heart of this issue are the American workers and consumers, who have been sold a bill of goods with the promise of a manufacturing renaissance. Instead, they are left navigating an economy saddled with the consequences of these misguided policies—higher prices, slower job growth, and the looming specter of inflation.

The stark dichotomy between Trump's promises and the economic fallout of his tariff policy serves as a poignant reminder of the dangers of prioritizing political rhetoric over economic reality. As we move forward, it's imperative that policy decisions be grounded in economic sense rather than political expediency. The American economy, and indeed its people, deserve no less.
