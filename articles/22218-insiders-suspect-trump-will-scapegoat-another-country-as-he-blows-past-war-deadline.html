Insiders suspect Trump will 'scapegoat' another country as he blows past war deadline

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosAugust 20, 20263 min read

Trump’s Threat to Bomb Oman Is Not Diplomacy. It’s Blame Management.

President Trump threatened to bomb Oman after accusing the small Gulf state of blocking an Iran nuclear deal, even though the reporting makes clear he did not specify what Oman had actually done. Inside his own administration, officials are already divided: some treat Oman as a bad actor, others say Trump is using it as a scapegoat for a deal that is stalled on his watch.

Power, Not Confusion

This is not a story about a misunderstood ally. It is a story about who has the power to threaten violence and then invent a rationale after the fact. Trump is the one issuing the threat. Trump is the one turning a diplomatic problem into a public accusation. And Trump is the one trying to turn a negotiation failure into somebody else’s moral defect.

That matters because the asymmetry is the entire point. Oman is a small state bordering the Strait of Hormuz. The United States is the country with sanctions, troops, strike capabilities, and the political habit of calling coercion strategy. When Trump says he would handle Oman “very easily,” he is not making a policy argument. He is advertising domination.

The Scapegoat Is the Message

The article’s most revealing detail is not the threat itself but the vagueness surrounding it. Oman is apparently being blamed for “standing in the way” of a deal while the reporting also notes that Omani talks with Iran could ease pressure on global energy prices. In other words, Oman is both mediator and target, depending on what Trump needs that hour.

That is classic scapegoating. A stalled process needs a villain, so the weaker actor gets cast as duplicitous, obstructive, or secretly aligned with the real adversary. The point is not accuracy. The point is to externalize failure and preserve the image of strength.

Allies as Disposable Props

Trump’s pattern is not limited to Oman. He has reportedly already cut a U.S.-South Korea military drill after Seoul declined to help reopen Hormuz, while also complaining that the U.K. and Spain are not doing enough. The through line is obvious: allies are treated as instruments, not partners, and their value is measured by how much they can be made to absorb America’s unresolved problems.

That is a brittle form of power. It substitutes public shaming for statecraft and assumes smaller governments exist to clean up the diplomatic mess created in Washington. The result is predictable: the administration lashes outward, then calls the reaction evidence that everyone else is unreliable.

War Talk as Cover for Diplomatic Failure

The administration had already threatened sanctions and even strikes after intelligence assessments claimed Oman planned to let Iran impose tolls on Hormuz shipping, allegations Oman denied. That earlier escalation matters because it shows how quickly accusation becomes pretext in this kind of politics. Once the machinery of coercion is in motion, the target’s denial no longer matters much.

Omani Foreign Minister Badr al-Busaidi’s remark that “whatever your view of Iran, this war is not of their making” cuts through the fog. It names the basic dodge: when powerful governments choose escalation, they rarely admit the choice is theirs. They frame it as reaction, necessity, or grievance. That is how responsibility gets laundered.

The Larger Pattern

The deeper pattern here is authoritarian politics dressed up as dealmaking. When a leader cannot close the bargain, he does not examine his own leverage, his own deadlines, or his own failure. He hunts for a lesser target, inflates it into a menace, and uses the threat of violence to restore the theater of control.

That is what this Oman episode reveals: not confusion, but a governing style built on intimidation, scapegoating, and performative toughness. The substance of the policy is secondary to the spectacle of punishment. And once that becomes normal, diplomacy is just another stage for coercion.
