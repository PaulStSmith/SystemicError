In an article published on Monday, April 28, The New Republic's Michael Tomasky described the first 100 days of President Donald Trump's second term as "clown-show fascism." Tomasky argued that the buffoonish qualities of Trump's administration doesn't make it any less dangerous.

Tomasky discussed his article the next day during an appearance on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," attacking Trump's second presidency as a combination of incompetence and "authoritarianism."

Tomasky told "Morning Joe" hosts Joe Scarborough and Mika Brzezinski, "Well, as I was sitting down to write the piece, Mika, I was thinking about the Trump administration's actions over the first 100 days and thinking, 'You know, can I classify them or categorize them?' And I think you can. Most of the actions fall under the category of what I see as fascism. That word may be too strong for some people, but authoritarianism, it's not that different. A defiant lawlessness, a challenge to existing laws, a challenge to democratic — small d — democratic values and norms and institutions, ceaselessly and relentlessly and much more forcefully than I imagined and virtually everyone I talked to imagined that they would be, from the firings of the inspectors general, which was, if you read that statute, probably illegal, to the treatment of people like Kilmar Omar Ábrego García, and other things."

Tomasky continued, "So there's that category. Then there's another category of actions that, frankly, are just kind of laughably incompetent, the tariffs being Exhibit A on that. But certain other things too."

Tomasky cited "certain activities by" the Elon Musk-led Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) as examples of incompetence — including "firing and then rehiring people."

The New Republic editor told Scarborough and Brzezinski, "So, you put those together, and you have what I call clown-show fascism."

Our Analysis:

The Duality of Danger: Analyzing "Clown-Show Fascism"

In a recent piece for The New Republic, Michael Tomasky coined the term "clown-show fascism" to describe the first 100 days of President Donald Trump's second term, and frankly, it's as sharp as it is scathing. Tomasky's analysis, which he further elaborated on MSNBC's "Morning Joe," slices through the facade of buffoonery that has characterized Trump's administration, revealing a far more sinister underbelly that is as dangerous as it is incompetent.

The Facade of Incompetence

Tomasky identifies a slew of actions that could be dismissed as mere incompetence — with tariffs and the antics of the Elon Musk-led Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) serving as prime examples. The image of firing and then re-hiring employees conjures a sense of disarray that would be comical if it weren't so deeply troubling in the context of governance. This isn't just poor management; it's a destabilizing force against the institutions that uphold democratic governance.

A Sinister Undercurrent of Authoritarianism

However, to focus solely on the administration's incompetence would be to miss the forest for the trees. Tomasky dares to use the word fascism — a term loaded with historical weight and implications — to describe the Trump administration's actions. He outlines a pattern of defiant lawlessness, from the potentially illegal firings of inspectors general to the treatment of individuals like Kilmar Omar Ábrego García. These actions aren't just challenges to the law; they're direct assaults on the democratic values, norms, and institutions that form the backbone of American democracy.

The Danger of Underestimation

One of the most critical points Tomasky makes is the collective underestimation of the Trump administration's willingness to push its authoritarian agenda. The term "clown-show fascism" encapsulates this duality perfectly — the ludicrous veneer masks a relentless and forceful challenge to democracy that many did not anticipate.

The Bigger Picture

Tomasky's commentary on "clown-show fascism" serves as a dire warning. It's easy to laugh off incompetence until you realize that it's just one piece of a larger, far more dangerous puzzle. The combination of laughable inefficiency and authoritarian ambitions poses a unique threat to democratic governance — one that cannot be ignored.

In essence, Tomasky's analysis is a clarion call to take seriously the dual threats posed by the Trump administration: not just its overt challenges to democracy, but also the insidious danger posed by its incompetence. As we navigate these tumultuous times, it's crucial to remain vigilant against all forms of authoritarianism — whether they come in the guise of a clown show or not.
