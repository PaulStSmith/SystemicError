Trump jammed together McDonald's sandwiches to create 'hideous Franken-burger': RNC chair

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 16, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Fast Food Presidency: A Digest of Distraction

Republican presidential nominee and former U.S. President Donald Trump works behind the counter during a visit to McDonalds in Feasterville-Trevose, Pennsylvania, U.S. October 20, 2024. Doug Mills/Pool via REUTERS

The chairman of the Republican National Committee dished out disgusting details about President Donald Trump’s fast food fixation.

RNC chair Joe Gruters, who’s also a Florida state senator, appeared on a local government podcast and was asked to comment on one of the funniest things that had happened to him on the campaign trail, and he recounted the president’s “unique” order from McDonald’s, which The Daily Beast described as “a hideous Franken-burger.”

“The amount, and what the president eats,” Gruters told the Chambers, Changes, & Conversations. “He had hot fries waiting for him from McDonald’s. Then he had a Filet-O-Fish, a Quarter Pounder, and a Big Mac, and I think he combined two of them.”

Gruters didn’t say which two of the sandwiches the president jammed together, but he added that Trump ordered an orange soda to wash down the glutounous amalgamation.

“I’m thinking to myself, how does the guy that’s as senior as him get away with eating all this McDonald’s on a consistent basis?” Gruters said.

The president’s order, according to McDonald’s own nutrition guide, tallies up to a whopping 2,372 calories, well above the 2,000 calories older adult men are advised to consume in a day, and Gruters said eating just two of the eatery’s sandwiches made him “sick as a dog.”

“Yeah, he loves McDonald’s brand,” Gruters said. “McDonald’s should be paying that guy – no kidding.”

The president famously staged a photo opportunity last year at a McDonald’s restaurant in Pennsylvania, where he dunked fries into oil and answered reporters’ questions through the drive-through window.

The notorious germaphobe expressed amazement during that stunt that he didn’t have to touch the fries with his hands, and biographer Michael Wolff wrote 2018 book “Fire and Fury: Inside the Trump White House” that Trump likes eating at McDonald’s because, in his view, food made in advance is less vulnerable to tampering.

Our Analysis:

The Fast Food Presidency: A Digest of Distraction

In a political landscape often mired in complexity and controversy, it is both bewildering and somehow fitting that discussions around the sitting president’s diet find their way into the news cycle. The recent revelation by Joe Gruters, the chairman of the Republican National Committee, about President Donald Trump’s McDonald’s mania is a case in point. According to Gruters, Trump’s fast food fetish is not just a matter of convenience or taste but borders on the grotesque, with combinations that would make a nutritionist’s head spin.

The Gluttonous Gastronomy

The chairman’s tale of Trump’s “hideous Franken-burger” – a concoction involving a Filet-O-Fish, a Quarter Pounder, and a Big Mac, with an orange soda for good measure – is more than a quirky anecdote. It’s a metaphor for excess and a glaring display of the president’s disregard for health guidelines. The meal, tallying up to a staggering 2,372 calories, far exceeds the daily recommended intake for adult men. This isn’t just a meal; it’s a feast of folly, a testament to Trump’s indulgence.

A Misplaced Marvel

Gruters’ recounting of the president’s fast food escapades, including a stunt at a Pennsylvania McDonald’s, is not just a story about dietary preferences; it’s a glimpse into Trump’s reality distortion field. The president, known for his germaphobic tendencies, finds solace in the pre-made meals of McDonald’s, believing them to be less susceptible to tampering. This paranoia-driven preference for McDonald’s, as reported by biographer Michael Wolff, is less about the food and more about the fear – a sad commentary on the state of the presidency.

A Brand’s Best Friend

Gruters’ offhand comment that “McDonald’s should be paying that guy” speaks volumes about the intertwining of presidency and brand endorsement. Trump’s unabashed promotion of McDonald’s, whether through public stunts or personal anecdotes, blurs the line between personal preference and public influence. It’s a stark reminder of the commercialization of the presidency, where fast food becomes a fast track to free advertising.

The Bigger Picture

While the focus on Trump’s diet might seem trivial in the grand scheme of political discourse, it’s emblematic of a larger issue: the trivialization of the presidency. At a time when there are pressing national and global issues to be addressed, the fixation on the president’s fast food consumption is a distraction – a sideshow that diverts attention from matters of substantive importance.

In essence, the revelation of Trump’s McDonald’s obsession is more than just a tale of dietary disaster; it’s a reflection of a presidency mired in the superficial, the excessive, and the unhealthy. It’s a snapshot of a leader whose priorities, much like his diet, are in dire need of a healthier, more balanced approach.
