'This is a warning sign!' Fox & Friends freaks over Dem victory in GOP stronghold

Brian Kilmeade (right) speaks on Fox News’ “Fox & Friends,” Dec. 10, 2025. (Screengrab / Fox News)

Fox News’ Brian Kilmeade is sounding the alarm Wednesday morning after Republicans faced yet another election defeat Tuesday after a Democrat won the mayoral race in Miami, Florida, a seat that has been held by Republicans for nearly three decades.

Democratic candidate Eileen Higgins won Tuesday’s election handily, defeating her GOP opponent, Emilio Gonzalez, who was officially endorsed by President Donald Trump, with nearly 60% of the vote. And on Tuesday, “Fox and Friends” host Brian Kilmeade issued a grave warning to the Trump administration, begging the president to “examine” his “aggressive” deportation policy that he argued may have put off Hispanic voters.

“If the numbers show the Hispanic vote went heavily for the Democrat, that is a warning sign for next year,” Kilmeade said.

“They love him at the border – they don’t necessarily love the aggressive tactics going to Home Depot or landscape trucks dragging people off. The perception is that they’re being grabbed because they’re Hispanics. They need to examine that position when it comes to illegal immigration. Yes, it got him elected, but the way you implement it is not grading over 50%.”

Higgins’ victory marks just the latest in a series of election losses for the GOP, which includes last week’s congressional race in Tennessee, which, while the GOP Trump-backed candidate came out on top, was far closer than what experts expected from a district that supported Trump by double digits in 2024.

Kilmeade also expressed fear that Miami’s election upset may bode poorly for Trump’s redistricting effort in Texas, which was designed to give Republicans five additional House seats, but hinges on Trump maintaining his standing among Hispanic voters.

“Now I heard in many ways you look at Miami as an anomaly – there’s a lot of conservative Hispanics in the area – but, if the administration does not win the Hispanic vote, the Texas redistricting will not work because it’s all based on the president continuing to get 47% of the Hispanic vote!” Kilmeade warned.

“[Trump] got the job really because he promised to crack down at the border, which he did. When it comes to the 12 million illegals that are here, that’s going to be the key: how you round them up.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Echoes of Alarm in Fox News’ Latest Election Post-Mortem

In a recent outpouring of concern, Fox News’ Brian Kilmeade has illuminated the Republican Party’s dimming star in the political firmament, particularly highlighted by a Democratic victory in Miami’s mayoral race—a position not held by a Democrat for nearly three decades. Eileen Higgins’ triumph over Emilio Gonzalez, a candidate backed by none other than President Donald Trump, is not just a loss but a harbinger of potential doom for the GOP, according to Kilmeade. Let’s dissect the layers of desperation, misinterpretation, and the glaring omissions in Kilmeade’s analysis.

A Grave Warning Ignored by Many

Kilmeade’s urgent plea to the Trump administration to reassess its “aggressive” deportation policy comes as a reaction to the Hispanic electorate’s overwhelming support for Higgins. This demographic shift is interpreted as a direct consequence of the administration’s immigration tactics, which, according to Kilmeade, may have alienated Hispanic voters. “If the numbers show the Hispanic vote went heavily for the Democrat, that is a warning sign for next year,” Kilmeade asserts. However, what’s striking here is not the observation itself but the underlying surprise at such a predictable outcome. The Hispanic community’s response is not a sudden shift but a gradual estrangement fostered by years of hostile policies and rhetoric.

Misinterpreting the Message

Kilmeade’s analysis borders on the myopic, focusing on the superficial mechanics of electioneering rather than the substantive issues at stake. His advice to the Trump administration to modify its implementation of immigration policies to regain Hispanic support misses a critical point: the problem isn’t just how the policies are implemented but the fundamental nature of these policies themselves. The suggestion that a softer approach to the same aggressive policies could sway Hispanic voters underestimates the intelligence and political awareness of this demographic.

The Anomaly Argument: A Convenient Excuse

The labeling of Miami’s election as an “anomaly” due to its conservative Hispanic population is an attempt to downplay the significance of Higgins’ victory. This narrative is not only reductionist but also dangerously complacent. It overlooks the broader implications of shifting voter loyalties and the increasing rejection of divisive politics. Kilmeade’s anxiety over the implications for Texas redistricting efforts further underscores a strategic panic: the recognition that demographic shifts and political awakenings could derail carefully laid plans to consolidate GOP power.

The Elephant in the Room: Addressing the Root Cause

At the heart of Kilmeade’s commentary is an acknowledgment of Trump’s original sin: the promise to “crack down at the border.” Yet, there’s an avoidance of the critical reflection required to understand why such policies have backfired. The discussion about rounding up the 12 million undocumented individuals residing in the U.S. skirts around the core issue of humanity and justice in immigration discourse. The GOP’s loss, framed as a tactical error in policy implementation, is indicative of a deeper moral and ethical misalignment with an increasingly diverse and politically engaged electorate.

Conclusion: A Reflection Long Overdue

Brian Kilmeade’s warnings to the GOP and the Trump administration serve as a microcosm of a party at a crossroads, confronted by the consequences of its divisive strategies. Yet, the analysis falls short by not addressing the fundamental injustices and the rhetoric of division that have alienated not just Hispanic voters but a wider segment of the American populace. The real alarm should not be over the loss of a mayoral seat in Miami or the potential fallout in Texas redistricting efforts. Instead, it should be over the erosion of the GOP’s moral standing, a predicament no amount of tactical repositioning can rectify. As the Republican Party grapples with these electoral setbacks, one wonders if this moment will spark genuine introspection or if it will continue down a path of strategic adjustments devoid of substantive change.
