National Guard 'compromised' in massive Chinese hack on Hegseth's Pentagon

An elite group of Chinese hackers infiltrated the National Guard over nine months, according to a report from NBC News.

The Department of Homeland Security initially acknowledged the hack, known by its nickname "Salt Typhoon," in a June memo. It says the hacking group “extensively compromised a U.S. state’s Army National Guard network” but does not specify which state.

The attack occurred between March and December of last year, according to the memo.

It "likely provided Beijing with data that could facilitate the hacking of other states’ Army National Guard units, and possibly many of their state-level cybersecurity partners," the report continued.

This is not the first time that Salt Typhoon has hacked a major U.S. organization, NBC News reported. Last year, the group hacked eight large telecom companies to spy on Vice President Kamala Harris' presidential campaign as well as the office of Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY) when he was Majority Leader.

While the report doesn't mention Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth by name, it speaks to the cybersecurity issues that have plagued his agency since he took over earlier this year.

For example, Hegseth was one of the cabinet members implicated in the Signalgate scandal. That scandal involved cabinet officials discussing a bombing campaign against the Houthis in Yemen using a commercial encrypted messaging app. Government policy prohibits the use of commercial messaging apps for official business because of their propensity to be hacked.

Two of Hegseth's closest advisors are also under investigation for their roles in helping Hegseth skirt government cybersecurity protocols.

The "Salt Typhoon" Saga: A Cybersecurity Whirlwind

In a revelation that reads like a plot straight out of a cyber espionage thriller, NBC News has unfurled a chilling narrative involving an elite group of Chinese hackers, codenamed "Salt Typhoon", and their extensive breach into the heart of America's military cybersecurity. This group's audacious infiltration of the National Guard over a span of nine months not only exposes glaring vulnerabilities but also sets a dangerous precedent for the safety of national security data. Let's dissect this digital disaster, piece by precarious piece.

The Chronology of a Cyber Catastrophe

From March to December of the preceding year, this not-so-mysterious anymore "Salt Typhoon" wreaked havoc, extensively compromising the network of a U.S. state’s Army National Guard. The Department of Homeland Security's June memo, while being as elusive as a politician's promise by not specifying the state, does underline a grave concern: the potential facilitation of further hacks into other states’ Army National Guard units and their cybersecurity partners. This isn't just a breach; it's a domino effect waiting to devastate.

A History of Hacking Havoc

The audacity of Salt Typhoon isn't newfound. Their portfolio of disruption includes spying on high-profile targets such as Vice President Kamala Harris' presidential campaign and the office of Sen. Chuck Schumer (D-NY). This pattern of targeting significant political figures and entities isn't just alarming; it's a blatant act of cyber warfare.

Systemic Failures and the Hegseth Headache

The narrative takes a more personal turn with the mention of Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth. The cybersecurity woes plaguing his agency since his tenure began earlier this year are not merely unfortunate coincidences but symptomatic of a larger, systemic failure. The Signalgate scandal, which involved cabinet officials (including Hegseth) discussing sensitive military operations over a commercial encrypted messaging app, is a testament to a reckless disregard for security protocols. The investigation into two of Hegseth's closest advisors for aiding and abetting this disregard only adds layers to this onion of incompetence.

The Bottom Line

This article, while shedding light on the Salt Typhoon operation, paints a broader picture of a cybersecurity infrastructure that's as robust as a house of cards in a hurricane. The infiltration of the National Guard by a foreign hacking group is not just a breach; it's a blaring siren for a systemic overhaul. The recurring theme of high-profile hacks, coupled with internal security laxity (à la Hegseth and Co.), signifies a double-edged sword of external threats and internal weaknesses.

The implications of such breaches are far-reaching. Beyond the immediate risk to national security and the integrity of sensitive data, there's a profound erosion of trust in the institutions designed to protect us. When those at the helm treat cybersecurity protocols with a cavalier attitude, it not only emboldens adversaries but also undermines the very fabric of national defense.

In conclusion, the saga of Salt Typhoon isn't just a series of unfortunate events; it's a stark reminder of the cybersecurity quagmire that needs immediate and decisive action. The question isn't just about patching up the leaks; it's about reevaluating and reinforcing the entire ship. And as for the officials who prioritize convenience over security? Perhaps it's time for a reckoning. Because in the realm of national security, ignorance isn't bliss—it's a liability.
