Trump launches knock-out assault on dying honeybees

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 22, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Cuts That StingA Domino Effect of NeglectAn Unnecessary Crisis

A honeybee. (Shutterstock)

Already decimated honeybee colonies are the latest victims of President Donald Trump’s policies, a new report revealed Wednesday.

Since June last year, the U.S.’s honeybee industry has seen its worst period ever, The Atlantic reported. More than 60 percent of the insects mysteriously perished.

It threatened devastation for the industry that ships millions of colonies of the pollinators to farms and orchards across the country.

The government stepped in, sending samples to the Department of Agriculture’s five bee-research laboratories in a desperate effort to find the cause.

But, in just a few weeks, Trump has virtually killed that research, the Atlantic reported. Massive funding cuts across the department — along with the rest of government — crippled the ongoing work.

“Now scientists, farmers, and beekeepers alike are racing to recover and prevent the next massive die-off before it’s too late,” The Atlantic reported.

The cuts put the whole industry is in limbo, unable to rebuild wiped out colonies or treat surviving bees because beekeepers have no idea of the cause of the deaths, the magazine stated.

“Until they have results from the samples that were taken, they don’t know if it’s safe to rebuild with that equipment,” said Danielle Downey, executive director of the non-profit bee group Project Apis m.

“It’s a little frightening,” said California beekeeper Russell Heitkam.

The Atlantic reported that the Trump cuts had resulted in 800 Agricultural Research Service workers being fired. That’s the branch of the USDA that runs the honeybee labs.

“More than 90 commercial crops in the U.S. are pollinated by bees, including staples such as apples and squash,” The Atlantic reported.

“Even a modest reduction in crop yields, courtesy of honeybees dying off or beekeepers quitting the business, would force the U.S. to import more produce — which, with tariffs looming, is unlikely to come cheap.”

Our Analysis:

Honeybee Havoc: Unpacking the Impact of Trump's Policies

In a recent exposé by The Atlantic, the dire consequences of President Donald Trump’s policies on the United States' honeybee colonies have been laid bare, painting a bleak picture for the future of agriculture and biodiversity. The report uncovers a disturbing trend: since June last year, the honeybee industry has experienced its "worst period ever" with a staggering more than 60 percent of the insects perishing under mysterious circumstances. This is not just a minor setback but a catastrophic blow to an industry integral to the pollination of numerous crops across the country.

The Cuts That Sting

At the heart of this crisis are the massive funding cuts orchestrated by the Trump administration, which have essentially neutered the Department of Agriculture’s capacity to combat this issue. The article points out that these cuts have jeopardized critical research by slashing the budget and firing 800 Agricultural Research Service workers. This is not just a reduction in numbers; it's a decimation of a collective brain trust essential for safeguarding the country's agricultural backbone. The irony is palpable – in an attempt to streamline government spending, the administration has compromised an industry that is foundational to the nation's food security and economic health.

A Domino Effect of Neglect

The consequences of such policy decisions are far-reaching and multifaceted. First and foremost, the inability to pinpoint the cause of the honeybee die-offs leaves beekeepers and farmers in a precarious position, unable to rebuild colonies or treat surviving bees without the fear of repeating history. This uncertainty cripples an entire industry, suspending it in a state of limbo where every decision is a gamble against the odds.

Moreover, the article highlights a critical, often overlooked aspect of agriculture – the dependency of "more than 90 commercial crops in the U.S." on bee pollination. This isn't just about the bees or the honey; it's about apples, squash, and countless other staples that form the bedrock of the American diet. The threat of reduced crop yields and the subsequent need to import produce paints a grim picture of food security and economic stability, especially with looming tariffs escalating costs further.

An Unnecessary Crisis

What emerges from The Atlantic's report is a narrative of unnecessary crisis, a testament to the short-sightedness of policy decisions that prioritize immediate fiscal savings over long-term sustainability and prosperity. The Trump administration's approach, as detailed in the article, exemplifies a grave misunderstanding of the intricate balance between nature and economy. By undermining the honeybee research, the administration has not only endangered a species but has also jeopardized the agricultural industry, food security, and the economic well-being of the nation.

In essence, the plight of the honeybees under Trump’s policies is a stark reminder of the interconnectedness of environmental stewardship and economic health. It's a cautionary tale of how policy decisions can have far-reaching consequences beyond their immediate scope, affecting not just the present but the future of the nation's agricultural and ecological landscape. The article from The Atlantic serves as a critical, necessary spotlight on an issue that demands immediate attention and action before the damage becomes irrevocable.
