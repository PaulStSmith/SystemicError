'So bad for Republicans!' Trump rages at Fox after guest says shutdown is about healthcare

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump lashed out at Fox News and correspondent Peter Doocy for allowing a Democratic senator to point out Republicans had shut down the government instead of negotiating for lower health care costs.

“This is about the price that Americans have to pay for their health care,” Sen. Mark Kelly (D-AZ) told Doocy during the Sunday interview. “We could sort this out next week sometime or as early as Monday if Mike Johnson would bring back the Republican House, we get together and have a real negotiation about how to bring down these high costs for the American people.”

Moments later, the president unloaded in a Truth Social post.

“Why is FoxNews and Peter Doocy putting on Democrat Senator Mark Kelly to talk about, totally unabated or challenged, Healthcare?” Trump wrote. “The FAKE SPIN is so bad for Republicans that it is hard to believe that we WIN. It will be very unfair, in the future, when they don’t have ‘TRUMP’ to fight for them.”

“I’m watching this Interview. It just doesn’t end. Fox should either get on board, or get off board, NOW, but at this point, it just doesn’t make any difference to me,” he continued. “They suck up the Ratings because of us, and then spin them in the Democrats’ direction.”

Trump went on to complain about Fox News polls showing that many Americans disapproved of the job he was doing.

“If I have a fake bad ‘Poll,’ many of which are done by Fox (One of the worst ‘Pollsters’ out there is the FoxNews Poll), they put them up immediately, and with gusto,” he claimed. “Republicans are so tired of this fight with Fox always trying to be so ‘politically correct!’”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Tirade: A Dissection of Discontent

In a recent eruption of vexation, President Donald Trump took to Truth Social to voice his disgruntlement with Fox News and correspondent Peter Doocy. His ire was sparked by their coverage of Democratic Senator Mark Kelly’s comments on the Republican-induced government shutdown and the ongoing health care cost debacle. Here, we dissect the layers of Trump’s tirade, revealing not just a personal vendetta against perceived media bias but also the underlying tensions within the Republican party and its uneasy alliance with conservative media.

The Unabashed Outburst

Trump’s outburst was not just a simple critique but a blistering attack on Fox News for providing a platform to Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ), who dared to challenge the Republican narrative by attributing the government shutdown to their refusal to negotiate on health care costs. Kelly’s proposition for negotiation was not just a call for policy discussion; it was a direct challenge to the Republican strategy, something Trump apparently found unpalatable.

Fox News: Friend or Foe?

Trump’s relationship with Fox News, once perceived as a bastion of unwavering support for him, has soured. His accusation that Fox News is spinning narratives in favor of Democrats is both ironic and indicative of his expectation for unwavering loyalty from the network. This expectation underscores a concerning trend where political figures seek not just coverage but endorsement from media outlets.

The Poll Problem

Trump’s disdain for Fox News polls, which he claims portray him unfavorably, reveals a deeper issue: an aversion to unfavorable news or data. Labeling Fox News as “one of the worst ‘Pollsters’” because its findings do not align with his perceived popularity exposes a preference for echo chambers over objective analysis. This aversion to critical feedback is a dangerous precedent for democratic discourse.

The Ratings Rant

Trump’s assertion that Fox News owes its ratings to him, and his subsequent complaint that the network then “spins them in the Democrats’ direction,” is a remarkable claim. It suggests a transactional view of media relationships, where coverage is expected to be reciprocated with loyalty. This perspective is not just flawed but fundamentally undermines the principle of independent journalism.

The Politically Correct Conundrum

Finally, Trump’s lament that Republicans are tired of Fox News trying to be “politically correct” is a telling sign of the times. The term “politically correct,” once a descriptor for language and policies intended to avoid offense, has become a pejorative in conservative circles, used to deride any attempt at balanced reporting or inclusive dialogue. This disdain for political correctness is less about advocating for free speech and more about resisting accountability.

Conclusion

Trump’s tirade against Fox News and Peter Doocy is more than a mere moment of frustration; it’s a microcosm of the current political climate within the Republican Party and its complex relationship with conservative media. It reflects a broader struggle over the soul of the party and the future of conservative discourse. As the GOP grapples with these internal tensions, the role of the media—whether as a critical observer or an unquestioning ally—will remain a contentious issue. What is clear, however, is that democracy thrives on diversity of thought and media independence, principles that seem to be in peril in the current political landscape.
