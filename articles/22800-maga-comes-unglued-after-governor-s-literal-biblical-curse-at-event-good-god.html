MAGA comes unglued after governor's 'literal biblical curse' at event: 'Good God'

A Hijab Became a Crisis Because MAGA Needed One

The Actual Event

Kathy Hochul attended an event at the Islamic Cultural Center in New York and wore a hijab while speaking to Muslim men. That is the entire factual core. The rest is reaction: MAGA commentators and influencers treated a gesture of basic cultural respect as a scandal, a betrayal, and in one case a “biblical curse.”

Who Holds Power

This is not a story about a governor losing her mind in public. Hochul is the elected official here, and she chose to appear in a setting where modest dress is customary. The louder power belongs elsewhere: to an outrage machine that can turn a routine act of civic courtesy into a loyalty test, and to officials like Leo Terrell, a senior counsel at the Department of Justice, who help launder that outrage through state authority.

That matters. When a federal appointee joins the mob against a Democratic governor for respecting Muslim etiquette, the line between propaganda and governance gets thinner than it should be.

The Real Target

The hijab is not what they are really mad about. It is a proxy for three things they cannot tolerate: women in authority, Muslims as legitimate participants in public life, and non-Christian customs treated as normal instead of suspect.

That is why the language went straight to contamination and submission. “Ruled by women and foreigners.” “Foreign government.” “Suicidal empathy.” This is not confusion. It is hierarchy talking to itself.

Misdirection as Method

The framing tries to make Hochul the problem, as if the offense were that she showed too much courtesy. That is backwards. The offense, in MAGA terms, is that she did not perform hostility. She did not flinch from Muslim Americans. She did not treat their customs as a threat. For a movement built on racialized grievance and Christian nationalist coding, that is enough to trigger a panic.

The point of the outrage is to punish any public figure who refuses to mirror the movement’s contempt. Respect gets recoded as weakness. Inclusion gets recoded as submission. A governor in a hijab becomes evidence that the right cannot survive in a pluralist country without calling pluralism treason.

The Pattern Beneath It

This is the same old authoritarian reflex: convert ordinary civic coexistence into a cultural emergency, then use that emergency to justify harder exclusion. Muslims are cast as outsiders. Women are cast as illegitimate. Democrats are cast as collaborators whenever they acknowledge either group as fully part of the polity.

The story is not that Hochul wore a hijab. The story is that a large part of the right can no longer distinguish public respect from surrender because it needs every gesture to serve tribal discipline.

The System

What this episode reveals is not a momentary social-media tantrum. It is a working political system: grievance producers on one side, institutional amplifiers on the other, and a public space steadily narrowed by people who profit from turning coexistence into offense. The hijab was never the issue. The issue is that MAGA cannot govern a democratic society without translating diversity into insult and courtesy into corruption.
