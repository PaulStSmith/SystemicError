'Cruelty for cruelty's sake': Expert slams Trump's response to Boulder attack

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 6, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Donald Trump (Reuters)

Following a firebombing terror attack in Boulder, Colorado of suspected antisemitic motivation, the Trump administration moved quickly, vowing that the accused undocumented attacker, 45-year-old Mohamed Sabry Soliman, could be deported by tonight on an expedited basis.

However, the White House social media account went further than that, suggesting his entire family could be punished over it. "Six One-Way Tickets for Mohamed’s Wife and Five Kids. Final Boarding Call Coming Soon," said the post.

This collective punishment is unsettling, American Immigration Council expert attorney Aaron Reichlin-Melnick posted — and possibly against the law.

"What the guy did was absolutely heinous and he should rot in prison for it," said Reichlin-Melnick. "But without any evidence that his wife and kids had anything to do with this, this is just cruelty for cruelty's sake. You can't have an actual investigation of the family's role in this amount of time."

Moreover, he noted, if the family really is being taken away under "expedited removal," that is a plain violation of federal law.

"It's possible that the White House is lying about using expedited removal and the family agreed to take 'stipulated removal' orders," said Reichlin-Melnick. "If they are actually using 'expedited removal,' that would be ILLEGAL — the law forbids it for anyone admitted on a visa."

The Trump administration has made a show of force in ramping up mass deportation efforts, at times clashing with federal courts as judges find their summary removals with little or no due process to be against the law.

Our Analysis:

A Deep Dive into the Depths of Punitive Politics: The Case of Mohamed Sabry Soliman

In a recent development that reads like a dystopian playbook, the Trump administration has once again stirred the pot of controversy with its response to a firebombing terror attack in Boulder, Colorado. The accused, 45-year-old Mohamed Sabry Soliman, an undocumented immigrant of suspected antisemitic motivation, is at the center of this storm. However, the whirlwind doesn't stop with Soliman; it engulfs his entire family, courtesy of a chilling social media post from the White House.

"Six One-Way Tickets for Mohamed’s Wife and Five Kids. Final Boarding Call Coming Soon," reads the post, a statement that reeks of collective punishment and blatant disregard for the principles of justice and human rights. Aaron Reichlin-Melnick, an expert attorney at the American Immigration Council, labels this move as "unsettling" and potentially illegal—a critique that barely scratches the surface of this administration's audacity.

Expedited Removal: A Legal Quagmire

Reichlin-Melnick points out that the use of "expedited removal" for Soliman's family, without any evidence implicating them in the attack, could be a gross violation of federal law. Expedited removal, as dictated by law, is designed for individuals caught at the border or having recently entered the U.S. without proper documentation. It was never meant to be a tool for collective punishment or a knee-jerk reaction to criminal acts.

The attorney's speculation that the White House might be fibbing about the use of expedited removal only adds another layer of concern. If the family agreed to "stipulated removal," a possibility suggested by Reichlin-Melnick, it raises questions about the pressures and fears they might have been subjected to, especially given the high-stakes environment they find themselves in.

The Trump Administration's Deportation Frenzy

This incident is but a microcosm of the Trump administration's broader approach to immigration and deportation. The administration has frequently clashed with federal courts over its aggressive and often legally dubious mass deportation efforts. Judges have repeatedly flagged these summary removals for what they are: a trampling of due process and a disregard for the rule of law.

The administration's penchant for "show of force" in its immigration policy does not just undermine the judicial system; it plays fast and loose with human lives. The case of Mohamed Sabry Soliman and his family is indicative of a dangerous precedent where individuals are not just denied their rights but are also subjected to a form of collective punishment that harkens back to darker times.

Cruelty for Cruelty's Sake

Reichlin-Melnick's assertion that this act of collective punishment is "just cruelty for cruelty's sake" hits the nail on the head. The notion that a family could be punished for the alleged crimes of one member without any evidence of their complicity is not just legally questionable; it's morally reprehensible. It's a stark reminder of the depths to which political agendas can stoop, leveraging human lives as pawns in a larger game of power and control.

In conclusion, the situation surrounding Mohamed Sabry Soliman and his family is a glaring example of how far we have strayed from the principles of justice, due process, and basic human decency. As this case unfolds, it serves as a litmus test for the nation's commitment to its foundational ideals. Will we allow fear, prejudice, and political expediency to dictate the course of justice, or will we stand firm on the principles that are supposed to define us?

The answer to that question will reveal much about the character of our nation and the direction in which we are headed.
