'Very bad facts' emerge as Comey fights Trump prosecutors over seashells: reporter

Based on an article by Travis Gettys

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

The State as a Personal Grudge Machine

Retaliation in Legal Clothing

James Comey is not being treated like a defendant in a normal criminal case. He is being treated like a political target, and the source text says the quiet part out loud: Trump and his aides pushed surveillance and prosecution over a social media post, even after agents concluded there was no threat.

That is the political core here. A former president with institutional reach is using federal machinery to turn annoyance into accusation. The issue is not whether “86 47” was wise, inflammatory, or stupid. The issue is that the state appears to have been mobilized to criminalize a disputed phrase after the fact, against a man Trump has wanted punished for years.

Who Actually Made This Happen

The power in this story sits with Trump, the aides who carried out his pressure, and the prosecutors who translated that pressure into charges. Comey’s lawyers say the Secret Service surveillance was directed by a senior official, and they tie the prosecution to Trump’s involvement in pushing the case forward.

That matters because it strips away the comforting fiction that institutions self-correct automatically. Institutions do what powerful people inside them tell them to do, especially when those people are willing to bend process toward revenge. The first indictment was already dismissed because the prosecutor had been unlawfully appointed. That did not end the campaign. It just forced a reroute.

The Crime Narrative Is the Cover Story

The government is trying to sell “86 47” as a true threat against Trump. The defense says the phrase is ordinary slang, widely used on protest signs and merchandise, and a Washington federal judge has already found that usage protected. Comey also deleted the post within hours and said he did not realize some people read it as violent.

The article’s framing is weak where it pretends this is a close interpretive dispute rather than a selective prosecution problem. When the state goes after one man over a phrase that circulates broadly, the legal theory is doing political work. The charge is not just about a post. It is about picking a target and manufacturing the seriousness afterward.

Selective Prosecution Is the Point

The source notes that prosecutors sought warrants after allegedly misleading a judge about witness interviews. It also describes a witness who hated Comey, wanted him jailed, admitted to speculation, and then shifted after hearing a Rudy Giuliani podcast. That is not a clean evidentiary record. That is a prosecution built on desire, narrative, and contamination.

And Comey is not alone in making the selective-prosecution argument; the filing says he is singled out while others use the same phrase without criminal consequences. That is the kind of uneven enforcement that reveals the real purpose of the case. Law is being used as a sorting mechanism: not to apply a neutral rule, but to punish the politically disfavored.

The Institutional Cowardice Is Familiar

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in a procedural democracy: not tanks in the street, but agencies that allow a boss’s vendetta to masquerade as public safety. The Secret Service, prosecutors, and the broader federal apparatus are being described here not as neutral guardians but as instruments that can be aimed when the right person applies pressure.

The cowardice is not only in the original push. It is in the willingness to keep going after the first case collapsed. A lawful outcome was inconvenient, so the effort was repackaged. That is how state power behaves when its purpose shifts from justice to punishment.

The Larger Pattern

The larger pattern is simple: powerful men do not always need to break institutions when they can commandeer them. They can turn law enforcement into a memory bank for grudges, then dress retaliation up as public order. That is what this story shows, and it is why the proper question is not whether Comey used a careless phrase. The proper question is why the federal government was ever conscripted into making that phrase a case.

This is not a system reaching for truth. It is a system revealing who can make it move.
