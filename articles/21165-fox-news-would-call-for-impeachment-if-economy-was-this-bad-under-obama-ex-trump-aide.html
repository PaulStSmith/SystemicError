Fox News would call for impeachment if economy was this bad under Obama: ex-Trump aide

Trump’s Economic Failure Is Not a Mystery

Anthony Scaramucci, a former Trump aide, is now saying out loud what Trump’s record has been telegraphing for months: prices are up, tariffs are distorting the economy, debt is ballooning, and the president is entangled in one scandal after another while claiming competence he does not have. That is the surface story. The political story is simpler: Trump holds the office, Trump makes the decisions, and Trump’s allies and enablers keep pretending the damage is some kind of weather event.

Power Has a Name

Trump is not suffering from an uncontrollable economy. He is exercising power inside it. The source makes that plain enough: he imposed tariffs, he pushed the country into a “quagmire” in Iran, and he continues to try to bury his connection to Epstein. These are not accidents of governance. They are choices made by the man at the top, then defended by the people who still depend on him.

That matters because Trumpism thrives on a lie of diffusion. Everything bad is supposedly inherited, unavoidable, or someone else’s fault. But institutional power in this story is not abstract. It sits in the White House, and the consequences attach to the man occupying it.

The Economy Is the Cover

Scaramucci’s comments about affordability, debt, and inflation are not just economic complaints. They are a map of how Trump governs: feed the rich, squeeze everyone else, and then sell the pain as strength. When prices rise across the board, that is not a branding problem. It is a political outcome.

The most revealing part is the logic Scaramucci describes: debt climbs, then politicians reach for inflation as the easiest way to pay for their recklessness. That is not technocratic drift. It is class warfare with a spreadsheet. Lower- and middle-income people absorb the cost while the powerful keep their options open.

Tariffs Are Not Populism

Trump’s tariffs are the kind of policy that sound muscular on a rally stage and act like a tax on the people least able to absorb it. Scaramucci says as much: they raised the “pricing umbrella” over lower-middle-income Americans who voted for Trump. That is the core fraud of Trump’s economic nationalism. It performs protection while transferring pain downward.

This is where the misdirection matters. The damage is often framed as volatility, uncertainty, or market unease, as if no one chose this. But Trump chose it. The point was never to build broad prosperity. The point was to make the state look punitive enough that authoritarian posturing could pass for competence.

Scandal Is Not Noise

The Epstein reference is not a side issue. It is part of the same pattern: a political figure who survives by demanding deference while surrounding himself with corruption, abuse, and concealment. Trump’s defenders want the public to treat that as separate from the economy, separate from governance, separate from everything that matters. It is not separate.

A presidency that lies about its alliances, its obligations, and its conduct will also lie about prices, jobs, and the future. The deceit is the method. The policy failures are the result.

The Real Comparison

The Hoover comparison is useful only if it is read correctly. The point is not nostalgia for some cleaner Republican past. The point is that a collapsing political order always tries to rename its own failures as bad luck. Scaramucci is not suddenly a reliable truth-teller; he is an insider describing a system that is visibly rotting under the weight of Trump’s choices.

The larger pattern is plain enough: Trump turns public office into private impunity, then uses economic pain to keep his coalition angry, confused, and dependent. That is not leadership in crisis. It is a governing style built on extraction, denial, and the expectation that other people will pay for the wreckage.
