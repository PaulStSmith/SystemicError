'Be ready': Expert warns Trump's 'boyhood pal' on verge of enacting major midterm 'scheme'

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosJuly 14, 20262 min read

Trump’s Real Scheme Is Not the Quackery. It’s the Power.

The Source of the Alarm

Miles Taylor says Donald Trump and Peter Ticktin are edging toward a manufactured “national emergency” to justify meddling with the midterm vote. Ticktin, a longtime Trump associate, is pushing a script about 2020 “foreign election interference,” claims about Maduro, and a federal takeover of the elections. The reporting matters because it points at a real mechanism: not gossip, but executive power being readied for abuse.

Who Actually Holds Power

The important fact is not that Ticktin sounds absurd. It is that he has access. According to the report, he was in the Oval Office as recently as last week, and he has already helped draft an executive order built around this emergency theory. That is the center of gravity here. An 80-year-old lawyer does not threaten an election by himself. A president with the machinery of the state does.

The Quack Is the Cover

The article treats Ticktin’s ideas as outlandish, then circles back to the possibility that they matter anyway. That is the right instinct, but the story still risks letting the absurdity do too much work. “Quack” is not a defense when the president is willing to turn quackery into policy. Authoritarian politics often arrives dressed as paranoia, then gets translated into executive language by people close enough to power to make it stick.

The Real Operation: Pretext

What is being described is not confusion, and not some spontaneous panic about election integrity. It is a pretext. The source says Trump has already tried to impose limits on mail-in voting and may use a national emergency to put the midterms “under federal control.” That is the point: manufacture a crisis, claim emergency authority, then use the state to narrow or override democratic choice. If the plan is to hold power by corrupting the rules of the vote, the language of “security” is just the wrapper.

Misdirection as Method

The blame trail in this story should not end with Ticktin’s eccentricity or with vague talk about “concerns” over election interference. The decision-maker is the president. The enablers are the lawyers, aides, and political loyalists who convert his grievance into legal theater. And the institutional failure is broader than one man: an executive branch that can still be bent toward anti-democratic ends if enough insiders decide the risk is tolerable.

The Pattern Beneath It

This is the old Trump pattern with a fresh costume: lose legitimacy, invent crisis, weaponize the state, then cast resistance as hysteria. The democratic response cannot be to marvel at how unserious the agents look. The danger is that unserious people are often exactly how serious abuses are laundered into official action. The lesson is not that the plot sounds ridiculous. It is that American institutions keep leaving the door open for a president who treats elections as property to be managed, not contests to be accepted.
