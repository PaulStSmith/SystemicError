‘The decline is real’: Ex-Trump official spills on president’s ‘frontal lobe illness’

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Diagnosis Is a DistractionTrump Has the PowerThe Useful Spectacle of “Concern”Corruption Is the ConstantThe Pattern Beneath the Panic

The Problem Is Not Trump’s Mood. It’s Trump’s Power.

Ty Cobb, a former Trump White House lawyer, told Mehdi Hasan that Trump shows signs of “frontal lobe illness,” pointing to erratic posts, late-night behavior, and public slippage. The piece uses that claim to frame Trump as cognitively unraveling and to suggest democracy may be “screwed” in 2026. That is the hook. It is not the story.

The Diagnosis Is a Distraction

This is what Washington does when it wants to look alarmed without getting specific. It turns political damage into a personality puzzle. Is he declining? Is he sleeping in meetings? Is he posting grotesque memes at 3 a.m.? Maybe. Cobb says so. But none of that answers the real question: what does a man with control over the executive branch do with that power?

The answer, from the source itself, is plain. He weaponizes the office. He degrades it. He uses the presidency as a stage for humiliation, retaliation, and spectacle.

Trump Has the Power

Trump is not a bystander in his own administration. He is the institutional actor. He holds the authority. He sets the tone. He decides what the executive branch becomes.

That matters because the article’s gravity tends to settle on his alleged decline, as if the danger comes from impairment rather than intent. But the office does not become corrosive only when the occupant is mentally unstable. It becomes corrosive when the occupant is allowed to turn the state into an instrument of personal grievance. The source describes exactly that: a president whose public behavior drains dignity from the office and demeans the country.

That is not merely a neurological question. It is a political one.

The Useful Spectacle of “Concern”

Cobb is a revealing witness. He once represented the Trump White House during the Mueller investigation. He knows the machinery. He has already lived through the ritual in which insiders describe the damage after the damage is underway.

That makes his alarm useful and limited. Useful, because he can name the rot from inside the Republican orbit. Limited, because it still centers Trump as a singular abnormality instead of a party-state project that keeps producing excuses, enablers, and late-arriving warnings. The issue is not that one man has become strange. The issue is that institutions keep adapting to make his strangeness governable.

Corruption Is the Constant

Cobb also says Trump is engaged in “unprecedented” corruption. That is closer to the core of the matter than the medical speculation. Corruption is not a side effect of erratic behavior; it is the operating system. When the presidency becomes a vehicle for self-dealing, revenge, and degradation, the republic does not need a diagnosis. It needs resistance.

The article’s frame risks treating deliberate abuse as confusion. Trump’s memes, insults, and theatrics are not random noise that accidentally surrounds policy. They are the method. They flood the zone, lower the standard, and train everyone else to debate tone while power is exercised without restraint.

The Pattern Beneath the Panic

This is the broader pattern: authoritarian politics rarely arrives wearing a uniform and announcing itself as authoritarian politics. It arrives through normalization, through elite nervous laughter, through former insiders reciting warnings after they helped stabilize the system.

That is what makes pieces like this incomplete. They identify the symptoms and still hesitate at the cause. The cause is not one man’s alleged decline. It is a political order that keeps handing power to a movement built on humiliation, corruption, and institutional cowardice, then acts surprised when the results look exactly like that.
