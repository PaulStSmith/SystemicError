'That is not true': Reporter debunks GOP senator's 'very strange' Chuck Schumer claim

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 10, 20253 min read

U.S. Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) walks to attend a press conference, following a Senate Democrats weekly policy lunch, on Capitol Hill in Washington, U.S., November 13, 2024. REUTERS/Leah Millis

A reporter flagged what he says is a “very strange” false claim about Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) made by a Republican senator on the Senate floor on Saturday.

Jake Sherman, founder of Punchbowl News, over the weekend took to social media to highlight the purported falsehood. The fact check comes amid a flurry of activity on the Senate floor over the weekend, as lawmakers on both sides of the aisle give speeches largely about the shutdown and healthcare.

Sherman specifically noted a comment made by U.S. Senator Bernie Moreno (R-OH), who was recently accused by Sen. Jacky Rosen (D-NV) of tracking the VINs of the vehicles for her Democratic campaign.

“Bernie Moreno said something very strange on the Senate floor today,” according to Sherman. “He said that financial disclosures show that Chuck Schumer has a higher net worth than he does.”

“That is not true,” Sherman added. “Schumer’s net worth is shy of $2M. Moreno is worth many, many millions.”

Asked by a fellow reporter, “Does he have huge mortgages or other debts though?” Sherman replied, “Still not even close....”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fabrication: Moreno vs. Schumer

In an era where political discourse often teeters on the brink of absurdity, the claim made by U.S. Senator Bernie Moreno (R-OH) about Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) stands out not for its originality in falsehood but for its blatant disregard for easily verifiable facts. As Jake Sherman, the founder of Punchbowl News, pointed out, Moreno’s assertion that Schumer has a higher net worth than his “many, many millions” is not just incorrect—it’s laughably so.

The Facts vs. Fiction

Sherman’s fact-checking reveals that Schumer’s net worth is under $2M, a figure that pales in comparison to Moreno’s wealth. The claim is not just a simple exaggeration; it’s a gross distortion of reality. What’s particularly baffling is Moreno’s decision to fabricate such a claim when financial disclosures are public and easily fact-checked. It speaks to a broader issue within political rhetoric today: the belief that one can say anything, regardless of its veracity, without consequence.

The Implications of Moreno’s Claim

This incident is not merely about the misrepresentation of financial statuses; it’s indicative of a deeper, more systemic issue in political discourse: the erosion of truth. When a senator can stand on the Senate floor and make a blatantly false claim, it undermines the integrity of the institution and erodes public trust. It’s a direct reflection of how the line between fact and fiction has not just been blurred but obliterated in some political quarters.

The Role of Fact-Checking

Sherman’s quick fact-check serves as a crucial bulwark against the spread of misinformation. However, it also highlights a worrying trend: the onus is increasingly on journalists and the public to decipher truth from falsehood. The expectation that politicians will engage in truth-telling seems to be dwindling, placing democracy in a precarious position. When leaders are unmoored from the truth, the very foundation of democratic discourse is threatened.

Conclusion: The Need for Accountability

Moreno’s strange and false claim about Schumer’s net worth is more than an oddity; it’s a symptom of a larger disease plaguing political discourse. It underscores the necessity for not just fact-checking but also accountability. Politicians must be held to account for the statements they make, especially when those statements are easily disproved. The integrity of political debate and the health of democracy depend on it.

In the end, one must ponder: if a senator can fabricate something as trivial as net worth, what else is being distorted in the halls of power? The incident is a stark reminder that in today’s political climate, vigilance and skepticism are not just valuable but vital.
