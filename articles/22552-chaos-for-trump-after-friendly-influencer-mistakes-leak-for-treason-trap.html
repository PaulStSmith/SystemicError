Chaos for Trump after friendly influencer mistakes leak for 'treason trap'

Based on an article by Matthew Rozsa

Paulo SantosAugust 26, 20263 min read

When the State Needs a Sucker

The source story is about Jennica Pounds, a pro-Trump influencer and Special Government Employee at the Defense Department, panicking over a proposed document handoff from a State Department official, Sarah B. Rogers. One side called it an entrapment plot; Rogers called it a FOIA-redacted document dump; the whole episode ended with the leak collapsing into public farce. That is the surface. The substance is uglier: the Trump administration is trying to use government power to feed its propaganda machine, and it is doing so through people selected for loyalty, not competence.

The Power Is Not on X

The real institutional power sits with the Pentagon, the State Department, and the White House that directs them. Pounds may have a large audience, but she does not control budgets, agencies, document flow, or classification rules. Rogers does not either, at least not in the way the administration’s critics like to imagine. The force behind the story is the state itself: officials with access, offices with authority, and a governing team that thinks public perception can be managed through backchannel leaks to friendly internet personalities.

That is the central fact here. The administration is not merely being covered by media figures. It is cultivating them.

Message Laundering, Not Journalism

Sommer’s point, as relayed in the source, is that Pounds is being used for propaganda work she is too clumsy to handle. That is true, but incomplete. The more important issue is that this job exists at all. The Trump operation appears to want a social-media intermediary who can launder government material into partisan talking points while preserving the illusion of outsider credibility.

That is not a communications strategy. It is institutional degradation. A serious government does not need to recruit a “dunce” to spread its message. It uses professional channels because it expects to justify its actions in daylight. When an administration instead reaches for influencers, it is admitting that persuasion has become subordinate to manipulation.

The Scandal Is Bigger Than the Blunder

The source frames the episode partly as comic incompetence: the wrong person, the wrong documents, the wrong reaction. But the blunder is not the core scandal. The core scandal is that the administration’s political culture is so rotten it normalizes using a Defense Department employee as a content node and a State Department official as a prospective source for a staged “exclusive.”

That is what deliberate state propaganda looks like in the era of personalized media: not a grand censorship apparatus, but a swarm of semi-official brokers, loyalists, and opportunists trying to convert public institutions into partisan content farms. If this administration lacks “talented media figures,” as the source puts it, that is not the main problem. The problem is that it still wants propaganda capacity inside the state.

Far-Right Ecosystems Feed Themselves

The source also notes that some far-right figures defended Rogers because of her work boosting European extremists and far-right Twitter personalities. That detail matters. This is not a clean ideological project with clear institutional boundaries. It is an ecosystem of mutual promotion, where government access, online clout, extremist brand-building, and partisan loyalty feed one another.

That ecosystem rewards performative outrage and punishes seriousness. It elevates people who can amplify, not analyze. It turns public office into raw material for spectacle. And it makes every failed stunt look like an accident instead of what it usually is: a predictable consequence of staffing the state with people chosen for ideological usefulness and online reach.

The Lesson

The story is not really about whether Pounds was “entrapped.” It is about a governing faction that treats propaganda as a substitute for legitimacy and recruits unserious actors to make that work. The administration is not being sabotaged by a deep state. It is revealing its own dependence on sloppy operatives, redactions, and performative leaks to keep its message alive.

That is the pattern. When power cannot defend itself through policy, it tries to survive through theater. When institutions are treated as instruments for partisan messaging, incompetence is not an exception. It is the operating system.
