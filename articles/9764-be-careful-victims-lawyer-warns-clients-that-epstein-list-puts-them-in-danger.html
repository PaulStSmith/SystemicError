'Be careful': Victims' lawyer warns clients that Epstein list puts them in danger

When a group of Jeffrey Epstein survivors spoke out last month on the steps of the U.S. Capitol, a speech by one of them seemed to grab the headlines with her announcement that they could compile their own list of the late sex offender’s clients if the government would not release their names.

Lisa Phillips, who says she endured years of abuse by Epstein and others in his sex trafficking network, ended her brief remarks Sept. 3 with an announcement that capped off a dramatic afternoon, as Reps. Thomas Massie (R-KY) and Ro Khanna (D-CA) pushed for the release of the investigation records, reported CNN.

“Us Epstein survivors have been discussing creating our own list,” Phillips said to cheers. “We know the names. Many of us were abused by them. Now, together as survivors, we will confidentially compile the names we all know who were regularly in the Epstein world, and it will be done by survivors and for survivors. No one else is involved. Stay tuned for more details.”

However, a month later there seems to be no coordinated effort to compile such a list, and several survivors and lawyers involved with the case say that the undertaking would put Epstein’s victims in greater danger by publicly accusing his powerful friends and accomplices.

“Be careful,” said Spencer Kuvin, a Florida-based lawyer who has represented dozens of Epstein victims. “Because these men that they may name have a lot of money and resources behind them to be able to potentially litigate against them and sue them and cause all kinds of problems and issues.”

“This should not be something that the victims have to do,” Kuvin added. “This should be something that the federal government does because it’s right and because it stands for justice for these victims.”

Another Epstein survivor, Liz Stein, told CNN that she doesn’t believe Phillips intended harm or mislead anyone with her announcement, but she worries about the additional pressure placed on victims already in a “precarious” position.

“We’re seeing a lot of pressure put on to us directly for things like, for us to release names, and for us to expose these people. But I don’t think that anyone understands the risks to us in doing that,” Stein said. “We’re being asked by every journalist about that question. It is not our job.”

Jennifer Freeman, another lawyer who has represented victims of Epstein’s co-conspirator Ghislaine Maxwell said she was not aware of anyone compiling a list of their clients, but she said such an effort would be “dangerous.”

“It puts them at risk and puts the burden of action on them, when it should be the government and institutions that are sworn to protect us,” Freeman said.

Phillips explained to CNN that her message at the news conference was intended to be a show of support to other survivors, but she agreed that compiling a list put them at risk.

“Survivors making a list, and just putting a list out there, which is read by us, is very dangerous,” Phillips said.

Attorney Brad Edwards, who has represented numerous Epstein victims, told reporters at the Capitol event that some attorneys had in the past “created somewhat of a list,” but he did not respond to a request for additional comment, and Phillips said she’s trying to figure out what to do next.

“What is the right way to go about it? The legal way? The most protective way for all of us?” Phillips said.

Our Analysis:

The Empty Promises and Real Dangers Facing Epstein Survivors

In a moment that could have been plucked from a thriller novel, Lisa Phillips stood on the steps of the U.S. Capitol last month, flanked by a bipartisan duo of lawmakers, and delivered a line that would reverberate across the media landscape. She, along with other survivors of Jeffrey Epstein’s notorious sex trafficking ring, proposed to do what the U.S. government has so far failed to accomplish: expose the high-profile clientele of Epstein. This announcement was not just a declaration of defiance but a spotlight on the systemic failure to protect and seek justice for victims.

However, the aftermath of this bold proclamation has been nothing short of anticlimactic. The supposed collective effort to compile a list of Epstein’s clients has fizzled out, revealing a stark and uncomfortable truth: the survivors are still very much alone in their fight for justice.

The Reality Behind the Headlines

CNN’s reporting sheds light on the complexities and dangers associated with naming Epstein’s powerful accomplices. Spencer Kuvin, a lawyer representing dozens of Epstein victims, encapsulates the crux of the issue: the immense risk of litigation and retaliation from those accused. His words, “Be careful,” serve as a grim reminder of the David vs. Goliath battle these survivors are up against.

Liz Stein, another survivor, highlights a critical point: the undue pressure placed on victims to take action that could jeopardize their safety. The expectation for survivors to expose their abusers is not just unreasonable but perilous. The narrative that victims must shoulder the burden of outing their perpetrators is a twisted perversion of justice.

Jennifer Freeman and Brad Edwards, attorneys with deep involvement in cases against Epstein and his co-conspirators, echo these sentiments, stressing the dangers and legal complexities of making such a list public. It’s evident that the system has placed an unfair burden on the shoulders of those it should be protecting.

The Bigger Picture: A Systemic Failure

What this situation underscores is a systemic failure at multiple levels. The government, with its vast resources and power, should be at the forefront of bringing Epstein’s clients to justice. Instead, survivors, who have already endured unimaginable trauma, are left to ponder the daunting task of exposing some of the world’s most powerful individuals on their own.

Phillips’ announcement and the subsequent inaction speak volumes about the lack of support and protection for survivors of sex trafficking. The rallying cry on the Capitol steps has morphed into a sobering reminder of the solitude and danger that survivors face when the spotlight fades and the media moves on to the next story.

The Path Forward

The question that looms large now is: What is the correct, legal, and safe way for survivors to seek justice? The answer is as complex as the problem itself. It is a multifaceted issue that requires a concerted effort from the government, legal system, society, and media.

  • Government Responsibility: The failure to release the names of Epstein’s clients is a glaring omission in the pursuit of justice. The government must take decisive action to investigate, expose, and prosecute those involved in Epstein’s network.

  • Legal Protections: Survivors should be afforded the highest level of legal protection and support when coming forward. This includes protection from retaliation, defamation lawsuits, and any form of harassment.

  • Societal Change: Society must reckon with the uncomfortable truth that power and wealth can shield predators from justice. A collective shift in mindset is needed to support and believe survivors rather than vilify them.

  • Media’s Role: The media must tread carefully, balancing the public’s right to know with the safety and dignity of survivors. Sensationalizing survivors’ plights or pressuring them for information only exacerbates their trauma.

In conclusion, the saga of the Epstein survivors’ list is a microcosm of the broader systemic issues plaguing our society’s approach to justice for sex trafficking victims. It’s a stark reminder that without systemic change, the scales of justice will remain woefully unbalanced. Survivors deserve more than empty promises and fleeting headlines; they deserve a system that fights for them with the same fervor with which they’ve fought to reclaim their lives.
