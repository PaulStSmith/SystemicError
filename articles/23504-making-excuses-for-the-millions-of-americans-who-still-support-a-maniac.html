Making excuses for the millions of Americans who still support a maniac

Based on an article by D. Earl Stephens

The Scapegoat Coalition Is the Governing Coalition

Hate Is Not a Distraction

Donald Trump’s political strength does not survive despite his bigotry. It depends on it.

The source describes an administration producing economic pain, institutional decay, environmental damage, military dysfunction, and attacks on voting rights. Yet Trump retains fierce loyalty because he offers supporters a politically useful explanation for every failure: someone weaker, less white, less wealthy, less Christian, or less protected supposedly took what belonged to them.

That is not confusion. It is scapegoating as statecraft.

Power Sits at the Top

The decisive actors are not voters with the least social power. They are the president, the wealthy interests benefiting from his government, Republican officials sustaining his authority, and institutions choosing accommodation over confrontation.

Trump controls the executive branch. His allies can direct agencies, intimidate universities, attack diversity programs, shape public memory through schools and museums, and weaken protections for political participation. Billionaires do not merely share his prejudices; according to the source’s framing, they benefit while public resources and institutions are stripped for parts.

Personal corruption and racial politics reinforce each other. The spectacle keeps supporters looking sideways while wealth and authority move upward.

The Black-Male-Voter Story Is a Convenient Detour

Coverage obsessing over the reported 25 percent of Black men who supported Trump in 2024 performs a familiar maneuver: it places explanatory weight on a minority subgroup while reducing the white electorate to background scenery.

Other constituencies also moved toward Trump. Black women did not. And white men remained central to his coalition. Treating Black male defections as the defining mystery turns a marginal shift into a morality play and obscures the larger bloc that repeatedly supplies Trumpism with electoral power.

Black voters are not beyond scrutiny. But scrutiny without proportion is misdirection. The question is not why every member of a disadvantaged group failed to resist authoritarian politics perfectly. It is why the electorate with the greatest accumulated wealth and institutional advantage remains the indispensable base for a politics built on exclusion.

Inequality Is the Evidence

The source cites a Federal Reserve finding that median Black family wealth stood at $44,890, compared with $285,010 for white families, and federal data showing Black men imprisoned at roughly 5.5 times the rate of white men.

Those disparities do not prove that every unequal outcome has a single cause. They do destroy the fantasy that race has become politically irrelevant. A government attacking diversity and equity initiatives amid inequalities of that scale is not restoring neutrality. It is protecting an unequal baseline and prohibiting institutions from naming it.

“Colorblindness” becomes camouflage when power insists that existing advantages are natural but corrective policies are discriminatory.

Normalization Is Institutional Cowardice

The press weakens public understanding when it converts deliberate conduct into style, controversy, or voter mood. Birtherism was not merely provocative branding. It was a racist campaign to portray the first Black president as an illegitimate foreign presence who had to produce papers to establish his belonging.

That campaign helped make Trump nationally powerful. Journalism that treats his subsequent rise as an inexplicable bond with disaffected voters erases the mechanism in plain sight.

The same failure appears when institutions describe attacks on voting, education, and historical inquiry as ordinary partisan disputes. Euphemism does not create neutrality. It conceals agency.

The System Rewards the Scapegoat

Trumpism reveals a durable political arrangement: concentrated power delivers material advantages upward while distributing humiliation downward. Wealthy beneficiaries receive policy. Supporters receive targets. Institutions receive threats. Marginalized people receive the consequences.

The system persists because each participant gets something: oligarchs get access, Republican officials get office, media organizations get spectacle, and voters animated by hierarchy get official permission to blame people with less power than themselves.

The central error is therefore not that Americans have failed to notice Trump’s cruelty. It is that too many political and media institutions still treat that cruelty as incidental to his appeal when it is one of the principal instruments through which power is acquired, defended, and used.
