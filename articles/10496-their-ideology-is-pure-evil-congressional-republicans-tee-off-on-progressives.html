U.S. Rep. Bob Onder (R-MO), in a speech Thursday on the floor of the House of Representatives, attacked the left, charging that even those who are “well-meaning” abide by an ideology of “hate” that mirrors communist authoritarians and revolutionaries.

“Well, everything has changed,” Onder claimed. “If we didn’t know it already, there is no longer any middle ground.”

“Some on the American left are undoubtedly well-meaning people. But their ideology is pure evil,” he charged. “They hate the good, the truth, and the beautiful, and embrace the evil, the false and the ugly.”

“And they literally will kill those with whom they disagree just as their predecessor leftists—Marx, and Stalin, and Lenin, and Pol Pot, and Fidel Castro, did. “We must know that.”

Onder is not the only GOP lawmaker on Capitol Hill attacking the left in the wake of Kirk’s killing, despite the killer having not yet been identified or captured.

Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) told Newsmax on Thursday morning that “what we are finding is that when you do have those strong conservative voices speaking truth to power you will find that when the left can no longer debate it, they can’t deny the truth, the only way they can silence those voices is through violence.”

U.S. Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) went even further, vowing to use “Congressional authority” to “cancel” anyone who “belittled the assassination of Charlie Kirk.”

Our Analysis:

The Blame Game: A Closer Look at the GOP's Response to Charlie Kirk's Assassination

In the wake of the tragic assassination of conservative activist Charlie Kirk, House and Senate Republicans have launched a scathing attack on Democrats, accusing them of fostering rhetoric that ostensibly led to this violent act. This article delves into the inflammatory statements made by GOP lawmakers, revealing a stark and dangerous polarization that merits a critical examination.

U.S. Rep. Bob Onder (R-MO) and the Ideology of Hate

Onder's speech is a case study in the art of political hyperbole. His assertion that the left's ideology is "pure evil" and akin to that of history's most notorious dictators is not only absurd but dangerously misleading. Onder’s sweeping generalization that the left "literally will kill those with whom they disagree" is a reckless statement that lacks any grounding in reality. The fact that he chose to make such claims without a suspect being identified in Kirk's assassination is particularly troubling. It's a classic move straight out of the playbook of political fear-mongering: broad-brush the opponent as not just wrong, but mortally dangerous.

Sen. Joni Ernst (R-IA) and the Silencing of Conservative Voices

Ernst's comments to Newsmax further this narrative of victimization, suggesting that violence is the left's method of silencing conservative voices. This claim is not only unsubstantiated but also ignores the myriad of ways in which political discourse occurs across the spectrum. To insinuate that an entire political ideology resorts to violence when unable to debate is to ignore the complex reality of political dialogue in America and to trivialize the tragic nature of Kirk's death.

U.S. Rep. Clay Higgins (R-LA) and the Threat of Cancellation

Higgins' vow to use "Congressional authority" to "cancel" those who "belittled the assassination of Charlie Kirk" is alarming. The irony of a Republican lawmaker threatening to "cancel with extreme prejudice" those he disagrees with cannot be overstated, especially in an era where the GOP has criticized "cancel culture." His use of dehumanizing language to describe his opponents as "evil, sick animals" only serves to deepen the divide and escalate tensions.

U.S. Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) and the Ownership of Violence

Mace's declaration that "Democrats own what happened today" is perhaps the most egregious example of blame-shifting in this saga. It's a statement that not only demonstrates a profound misunderstanding of the complexity of political violence but also irresponsibly points fingers without evidence. The assertion that the killer was a "raging leftist lunatic" before any suspect has been identified is not just premature; it's dangerously inflammatory.

In Conclusion: The Dangers of Polarization

The rhetoric employed by these GOP lawmakers in the wake of Charlie Kirk's assassination is not just divisive; it's potentially destructive. By painting the entire left as inherently evil and violent, these individuals are not just misrepresenting reality; they're contributing to the very polarization and hatred they claim to abhor. In a time of tragedy, the focus should be on unity, understanding, and the pursuit of justice—not unfounded accusations and political point-scoring.

It's crucial that we demand more from our political leaders. Escalating rhetoric and baseless accusations do nothing to solve the real issues at hand. They only serve to deepen the systemic errors plaguing our political discourse.
