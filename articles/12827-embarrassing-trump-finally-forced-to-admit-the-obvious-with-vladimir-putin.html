'Embarrassing': Trump finally forced to 'admit the obvious' with Vladimir Putin

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 12, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Rocky Relationship with Putin: An Analysis

U.S. President Donald Trump reacts after disembarking Marine One at Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., June 6, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

President Donald Trump's relationship with Russian President Vladimir Putin, someone Trump claims to know "very well," seems to be on the rocks.

During a cabinet meeting earlier this week, Trump said Putin has given the U.S. "a lot of bull----" regarding the war in Ukraine. The comment was a sharp departure from Trump's previous overtures to the Russian president. To one analyst, it seems like Trump is "admitting the obvious" by being so frustrated with his Russian counterpart.

Susan Glaser, a staff writer at The New Yorker, wrote in a recent essay that Trump has finally been forced to admit the "embarrassing reality" that Putin has no interest in ending the war in Ukraine.

"It sure did take Trump a while to admit the obvious, that the peace deal he promised to deliver within twenty-four hours of returning to office does not exist—a hundred and seventy days later," Glaser wrote.

Trump has flip-flopped sides in the Ukraine war several times throughout his second administration. Earlier this year, he invited Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to the White House to discuss the war, only for the meeting to devolve into a lecture from Trump and Vice President JD Vance.

Glaser noted that Trump's recent embrace of Zelensky is a welcome development, but there are still several issues to sort out.

"This is the play that many foreign-policy hands expected Trump might run back in January—it would be a smart bid for leverage in forcing Putin to the negotiating table, they figured, and would have the added benefit of shattering the conventional wisdom that Trump was willing to sell out to Moscow," Glaser wrote. "But not only did that not happen; Trump leaned hard in the other direction, fawning over Putin, voting with Russia at the U.N. Security Council, berating Ukraine’s President in the Oval Office."

Trump's Rocky Relationship with Putin: An Analysis

In what appears to be a significant shift in the geopolitical drama that is U.S.-Russia relations, President Donald Trump's once-cozy stance towards Russian President Vladimir Putin has hit a notable rough patch. This development, as reported, underscores a turbulent and unpredictable foreign policy that has defined Trump's administration. Let's dissect the layers of this evolving narrative with the precision of a scalpel and the skepticism it rightfully deserves.

Trump's Abrupt Shift

It's almost cinematic – the protagonist, once enamored by the charm of a dubious ally, finally sees the light. But this isn't fiction; it's the mess of international politics. Trump's declaration that Putin has fed the U.S. "a lot of bull----" regarding Ukraine is a stark departure from his previously documented admiration. This isn't just a change of heart; it's a political pirouette that would make a ballerina dizzy. The question is, why now? Susan Glaser's insights suggest Trump's realization of an "embarrassing reality" – that his Russian counterpart has no intention of bringing the war in Ukraine to a close. It's a realization that's as belated as it is blatantly obvious to the rest of the world.

Promises, Promises

Trump's promise of a peace deal "within twenty-four hours of returning to office" now reads like a hollow pledge, a political maneuver lost in the wind of his administration's erratic foreign policy. One hundred and seventy days later, the absence of this peace deal is not just a failure; it's a testament to the folly of believing that complex international conflicts can be resolved through sheer bravado and personal rapport.

Flip-Flopping Allegiances

The article highlights Trump's vacillating stance on the Ukraine war, noting a prior invitation extended to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, which devolved into what was described as a lecture. This inconsistency is emblematic of a larger pattern of unpredictability that has plagued Trump's foreign policy approach. To invite dialogue and then demean your guest does not diplomacy make; it breeds mistrust and confusion.

A Missed Opportunity for Leverage

Glaser's commentary points to a missed strategic opportunity. The expectation that Trump might leverage his relationship with Zelenskyy to pressure Putin reveals a potential path not taken. Instead, Trump's actions, including "fawning over Putin" and "voting with Russia at the U.N. Security Council," speak to a disconcerting alignment with Moscow over Kyiv. This alignment not only contradicts the principles of democratic solidarity but also undermines the U.S.'s standing on the global stage.

Conclusion

In summary, this article sheds light on a dramatic shift in the Trump-Putin dynamic, one that reflects broader inconsistencies and strategic missteps within Trump's foreign policy playbook. The realization that Putin is not a reliable partner in peace, albeit delayed, is a crucial acknowledgment. However, the path to rectifying this relationship and its implications for global politics remains fraught with uncertainty. As observers, we must remain vigilant, critical, and unafraid to call out the systemic errors that jeopardize not just national interests, but the fabric of international diplomacy and peace.
