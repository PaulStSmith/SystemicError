The board recalled the words of Congressman John Jennings Jr. (R-TN), uttered in 1947. After President Franklin D. Roosevelt’s fourth term election, Jennings was promoting legislation which would limit a president to two sessions in office.

While advocating for what is now the 22nd Amendment, Jennings warned that a president “backed by a ‘subservient Congress’ and a compliant Supreme Court' could ‘sweep aside and overthrow the safeguards of the Constitution,’" the Times wrote.

Jennings left office in 1951 and, later that year, the country ratified the 22nd Amendment.

Today, the Times echoed Jennings' warning, applying his words to Trump: “President Trump is a man of vaulting ambition,” the board wrote. “Congress is largely subservient to his agenda. And he keeps mentioning the idea of a third term.”

The 22nd Amendment says no person shall be “elected” to the office of president more than twice. However, the Times points out, it doesn’t say that no president shall serve more than twice.

This opens up the question — can a term-limited president run as a vice president and then be handed the Oval Office when his running mate resigns?

“I suspect I won’t be running again unless you say, ‘He’s so good, we’ve got to figure something else out,’” Trump said after being re-elected in November.

In March, Trump noted that he was “not joking” and that “there are methods which you could do it.”

But the Times concluded with the belief that the 22nd amendment loophole could blocked by “another amendment — the 12th — [that] appears to rule out that possibility.”

The board's final sentence declares that “no person constitutionally ineligible to the office of president shall be eligible to that of vice president of the United States.”

“Together, the two amendments make clear that Mr. Trump’s time in office cannot extend beyond his current term,” the Times wrote. “Mr. Trump’s third-term fantasizing is more dangerous than this response suggests, and it deserves more forceful pushback.”

Our Analysis:

The Trump Third Term Fantasy: A Dangerous Delusion

The New York Times Editorial Board has once again pierced through the fog of political discourse with a piece that brings to light the perilous musings of former President Donald Trump regarding a potential third term in office. This is not just idle chatter; it's a manifestation of a deeper, more insidious threat to the democratic fabric of the United States. Let's dissect the dangerous implications and the constitutional safeguards against such a political maneuver.

The Misinterpretation of the 22nd Amendment

At the heart of this discussion is a glaring misconception about the 22nd Amendment, which was ratified in 1951 in response to Franklin D. Roosevelt's unprecedented four-term presidency. The amendment explicitly limits individuals to being elected to the presidency no more than twice. However, Trump and his acolytes seem to cling to the loophole that it doesn't explicitly prohibit a president from serving more than two terms.

The idea that Trump could sidestep this constitutional safeguard by running as vice president and then ascending to the presidency is not just legally dubious; it's a flagrant disregard for the spirit of the Constitution. This isn't about legal technicalities; it's about the fundamental principles of democratic governance and the clear intent of the 22nd Amendment to prevent any individual from monopolizing the executive power of the United States.

The 12th Amendment Checkmate

The New York Times Editorial Board astutely points out that any third-term machinations by Trump are effectively checkmated by the 12th Amendment. This amendment, ratified in 1804, states that no person constitutionally ineligible to the office of president shall be eligible to that of vice president. This is a clear and unambiguous safeguard against the kind of constitutional gymnastics Trump suggests.

It's disheartening that this even needs to be stated. The fact that a former president is openly contemplating ways to circumvent the Constitution's clear intent is a testament to the erosion of democratic norms and values that his presidency represented. The 12th Amendment's provision is not just a technicality; it's a fundamental bulwark against the concentration of power and a key component of the checks and balances that preserve our democracy.

The Real Danger: Eroding Democratic Norms

The most alarming aspect of Trump's third-term fantasy isn't the legal feasibility; it's the fact that he's willing to entertain such ideas publicly, thus normalizing the concept of undermining democratic principles and constitutional safeguards. This is how democracies die: not in the swift coup of a dictator, but in the slow erosion of norms and the steady drumbeat of authoritarian ideas.

The Times is correct in asserting that Trump's musings deserve a more forceful pushback. This isn't merely a constitutional debate; it's a fight for the soul of American democracy. The suggestion that a president could serve more than two terms, regardless of the method, is an affront to the democratic ideals enshrined in our Constitution and a dangerous flirtation with authoritarianism.

Conclusion: Vigilance is Our Safeguard

In the end, the discussion about Trump's potential third term is not just about legal technicalities or constitutional interpretations. It's a stark reminder of the fragility of democracy and the importance of vigilance in protecting it from those who would seek to undermine it for their own power.

The New York Times Editorial Board has once again served as a crucial voice of reason in a time of political tumult. As citizens, it's our duty to remain informed, engaged, and ready to defend the democratic principles that define our nation. The specter of a Trump third term, no matter how legally implausible, should be a wake-up call to all who cherish democracy.
