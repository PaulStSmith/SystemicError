Trump official recommended for severe discipline as DC Bar seeks to 'send a message'

The Washington, D.C. Bar's board of professional responsibility submitted its recommendation that a former senior official for President Donald Trump be disbarred.

Jeffrey Clark, Trump's acting administrator of the Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs at the Office of Management and Budget, was handcuffed outside his home in the summer of 2022. Then-special counsel Jack Smith found he was among six co-conspirators who attempted to illegally overturn the results of the 2020 election and block a peaceful transfer of power.

In the final weeks of the first Trump administration, he nearly appointed Clark to serve as the acting attorney general, but other officials threatened to publicly resign if he did.

Clark was also indicted in Georgia in 2023 on allegations of violating state racketeering laws and making false statements. He pleaded not guilty.

Clark was indicted in Georgia last summer, where he’s charged with violating the state’s racketeering law and attempting to make false statements. He has pleaded not guilty.

The letter from the D.C. Bar said that a majority of the board supported the recommendation that Clark be disbarred as a consequence of his actions.

"We recognize that there are no factually comparable prior disciplinary cases. But that is not surprising given the underlying facts," the ruling says. "In making this recommendation, we are mindful of the need to maintain the integrity of the legal profession and deter the respondent and other attorneys from engaging in similar misconduct."

The recommendation explained that lawyers must "observe the highest standard of professional conduct" and, at a bare minimum, "they must be honest."

"While dishonesty is always intolerable, the facts here are significantly aggravating to warrant disbarment: Respondent was prepared to cause the Justice Department to tell a lie about the status of its investigation of an important national issue (the integrity of the 2020 Presidential election)," the recommendation continued. "Lawyers cannot advocate for any outcome based on false statements and they certainly cannot urge others to do so. Respondent persistently and energetically sought to do just that on an important national issue. He should be disbarred as a consequence and to send a message to the rest of the Bar and to the public that this behavior will not be tolerated."

When appearing before the Bar for the hearing, Clark pleaded his Fifth Amendment privilege "repeatedly," NBC News reported last year.

The Bar previously ruled that Clark's election scheme violated attorney ethics rules, Politico reported in 2024.

Our Analysis:

The Case of Jeffrey Clark: A Stark Reminder of Legal and Ethical Boundaries

In a move that reverberates through the corridors of power and the halls of justice, the Washington, D.C. Bar's board of professional responsibility has made a recommendation that cuts to the very core of legal integrity and professional ethics: the disbarment of Jeffrey Clark, a former senior official in President Donald Trump's administration. This recommendation is not just a procedural footnote; it is a thunderous declaration regarding the boundaries of legal conduct and the consequences of their breach.

The Charges Against Clark

Jeffrey Clark finds himself in the eye of a legal and ethical storm, accused of engaging in activities aimed at overturning the results of the 2020 election and obstructing the peaceful transfer of power. The implications of such actions are grave, striking at the heart of democratic principles and legal ethics. Clark's alleged readiness to manipulate the Department of Justice into disseminating falsehoods about the 2020 Presidential election's integrity is a glaring affront to the legal profession's foundational standards.

The Legal Profession's Ethical Backbone

The D.C. Bar's ruling underscores a fundamental expectation of lawyers: to uphold the highest standards of professional conduct, with honesty being paramount. The Bar's recommendation eloquently articulates the aggravating nature of Clark's actions, highlighting the unacceptable leap from advocacy to the promotion of falsehoods on a matter of national significance. This is not just about bending the rules of advocacy; it is about shattering those rules entirely.

The Implications of Disbarment

Disbarment is the legal profession's ultimate sanction, reserved for conduct so egregious that it warrants the severest of responses. In recommending Clark's disbarment, the D.C. Bar is sounding a clarion call to all attorneys about the sanctity of legal ethics and the severe consequences of their violation. This is a message that transcends the individual case of Jeffrey Clark, serving as a stark reminder to the legal community at large.

A Wider Context of Accountability

Clark's indictment in Georgia on charges of violating state racketeering laws and attempting to make false statements adds another layer to the narrative of accountability. His plea of not guilty in these matters does nothing to mitigate the severity of the allegations against him, nor does it detract from the D.C. Bar's recommendation. If anything, it reinforces the need for a robust response from the legal profession and the justice system to safeguard the principles they stand for.

Conclusion: A Line in the Sand

The D.C. Bar's recommendation to disbar Jeffrey Clark is not just about the fate of one man; it is a powerful statement about the values and standards of the legal profession. It draws a clear line in the sand, beyond which lies the realm of professional misconduct too severe to be tolerated. As we reflect on the implications of this case, let us remember that the integrity of the legal profession and the justice system as a whole depends on the willingness to confront and address such transgressions head-on. In the end, it is about protecting the very pillars of democracy and justice that define our society.
