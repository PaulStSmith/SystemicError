Red flags as Trump ignites the irony nobody expected

Based on an article by The Conversation

Paulo SantosJuly 7, 20262 min read

FIFA Didn’t Protect Fair Play. It Proved It Can Be Bought by Influence.

The Incident

FIFA has suspended Folarin Balogun’s automatic one-match ban after his red card against Bosnia and Herzegovina, and multiple reports say the reversal followed a personal call from Donald Trump to FIFA president Gianni Infantino. Belgium called the decision “astonishing.” The source article treats this as a dispute about a hard tackle and a disciplinary code. That is too small. The real story is who had enough power to bend the process.

The Decision Was Made Above the Field

The referee did not create this mess by himself, and neither did Balogun’s challenge. Raphael Claus issued the red; VAR Juan Ernesto Soto Arévalo pushed the review; FIFA then chose to override the consequence. That is where the power sits. Once a head of state can reportedly lean on FIFA’s top official and get a suspension softened, the institution is no longer describing a rule. It is advertising who can make the rules wobble.

The Star Exception Is the Point

FIFA dresses this up as discretion under Article 27, but discretion without a transparent appeal process is just selective mercy for people with leverage. The source notes this was done before for Cristiano Ronaldo. That is not reassurance. It is the pattern: famous names and powerful patrons receive procedural flexibility while ordinary players and teams are left with rigid enforcement. The law is not being refined. It is being rationed.

Misdirection Masquerading as Principle

The article spends time on whether the tackle was “too harsh,” whether the VAR overreached, and whether the red card was defensible. That frame is convenient because it keeps attention on the footage instead of the institution. The decisive issue is not whether the original call was perfect. It is that FIFA appears willing to alter a live tournament’s discipline after political intervention. That is not integrity. That is governance by pressure.

A Tournament Run by Prestige

Belgium’s outrage is easy to understand because the message is blunt: rules apply until a powerful enough actor decides they should not. FIFA claims to defend the “spirit of the game” while leaving itself room to privilege stars, presidents, and backchannel influence. The more openly it treats elite access as a legitimate route to relief, the more it turns competition into a hierarchy of access rather than a contest governed by shared rules.

What This Reveals

This is not a sports story with a little politics attached. It is a politics story wearing a jersey. Institutions that claim neutrality often collapse first not through open abolition, but through selective exceptions for the powerful. When FIFA lets influence rewrite discipline midstream, it teaches the same lesson authoritarian systems always teach: rules are for everyone else.
