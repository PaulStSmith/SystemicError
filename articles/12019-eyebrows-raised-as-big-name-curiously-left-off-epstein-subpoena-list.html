'Eyebrows raised' as big name curiously left off Epstein subpoena list

The House Oversight Committee subpoenaed all of the Justice Department documents involved in the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, and subpoenaed 10 people it wants to hear from. But that list has sparked questions about a number of people who are not on that list.

CNN reported that the House Oversight Committee wants to speak to former Attorneys General Merrick Garland, William Barr, Jeff Sessions, Loretta Lynch, Eric Holder and Alberto Gonzales; former FBI Director James Comey; former special counsel and FBI Director Robert Mueller; former Secretary of State and first lady Hillary Clinton; and former President Bill Clinton.

Axios reported after the list was released that the former prosecutor who gave Epstein a sweetheart sentencing deal was absent from the list.

Alex Acosta offered Epstein a non-prosecution deal, which handed Epstein a minor prison sentence and shut down further federal investigations into his sex trafficking ring of teen girls. Acosta did this under the George W. Bush administration, while Gonzales was Acosta's boss at the time.

Axios noted that leaving Acosta off the list "raised eyebrows from reporters and followers of the Epstein probe, given Acosta's role in negotiating the deal at the heart of the scandal."

After the bombshell New York Times photos of Epstein's Manhattan house, showing framed photos of him with notable individuals. Among those pictured who are still alive are Donald and Melania Trump, Larry Summers, Elon Musk, Mick Jagger, and Richard Branson. They were not subpoenaed.

The Times also revealed that in 2016, he was given some of the Epstein birthday book letters. Among those who included letters were media mogul Mortimer Zuckerman, former Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak, Woody Allen, Joichi Ito, Lawrence M. Krauss, Martin Nowak, and Steve Bannon. None of those individuals were subpoenaed either.

Bannon has bragged that he has 15 hours of footage of interviews with Epstein, which he claims is for a documentary. Neither Bannon nor the footage was subpoenaed.

Huffington Post reporter Yashar Ali was among those who pointed out Acosta's absence from the list.

The conservative Daily Caller also pointed out the omission.

Legal Analyst Bill Moore attacked the subpoena as "idiotic bulls--- beggars belief."

Acosta was previously subpoenaed by Democrats when they were in charge of Congress in 2019.

Our Analysis:

The House Oversight Committee's Selective Blindness in the Epstein Saga

In the latest chapter of the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, the House Oversight Committee has embarked on a questionable quest, serving up a menu of subpoenas that is as notable for its omissions as it is for its inclusions. The glaring absence of key figures from this list not only undermines the integrity of the investigation but also exposes a potential reluctance to confront the full spectrum of Epstein's nefarious network.

The List: A Curated Selection of the Usual Suspects

The committee's decision to subpoena a roster of high-profile names, including former Attorneys General (Merrick Garland, William Barr, Jeff Sessions, Loretta Lynch, Eric Holder, and Alberto Gonzales), former FBI Directors (James Comey and Robert Mueller), and political heavyweights (Hillary Clinton and Bill Clinton), reads like a who's who of Washington's elite. However, this star-studded lineup distracts from the absence of crucial figures directly implicated in the Epstein scandal.

The Most Glaring Omission: Alex Acosta

The most eyebrow-raising exclusion from the subpoena list is undoubtedly Alex Acosta, the former prosecutor who engineered Epstein's "sweetheart deal." Under his watch, Epstein received a pitifully lenient sentence, effectively a slap on the wrist, for his heinous crimes. This deal not only allowed Epstein to evade true justice but also precluded further investigation into his extensive sex trafficking ring. Acosta's exclusion from the list is nothing short of a systemic failure to address the core elements of Epstein's protection racket.

The Unsubpoenaed Gallery: A Collection of Influential Figures

Further diluting the investigation's credibility is the committee's failure to subpoena individuals with known ties to Epstein, captured in the now-infamous New York Times photos of Epstein's Manhattan mansion. The list of unsummoned associates includes Donald and Melania Trump, Larry Summers, Elon Musk, Mick Jagger, and Richard Branson. This selective oversight is a stark reminder of the potential for bias in legislative investigations, where connections and influence might shield one from scrutiny.

The Curious Case of Unsubpoenaed Correspondence and Documentary Evidence

Adding to the investigation's inadequacies is the oversight of Epstein's "birthday book" letters and Steve Bannon's claimed 15 hours of interview footage with Epstein. These pieces of evidence could provide invaluable insights into Epstein's network and operations. Their exclusion from the subpoena list is a missed opportunity to deepen the investigation's scope and uncover the full extent of Epstein's connections.

Conclusion: A Flawed Investigation Marred by Selective Oversight

The House Oversight Committee's investigation into the Jeffrey Epstein saga is fundamentally flawed by its selective approach to subpoenas. By omitting key figures like Alex Acosta and ignoring potential evidence from Epstein's associates and correspondents, the committee has demonstrated a lack of commitment to thoroughly unraveling the Epstein network. This selective blindness not only compromises the investigation's integrity but also perpetuates the cycle of impunity that shielded Epstein for so long. As the public grapples with the fallout of this investigation, one cannot help but echo the sentiment of legal analyst Bill Moore, who criticized the subpoena effort as "idiotic bulls--- beggars belief." In the quest for justice and accountability, partiality should have no place.
