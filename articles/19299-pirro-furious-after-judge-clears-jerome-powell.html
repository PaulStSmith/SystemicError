Pirro furious after judge clears Jerome Powell

A judge has quashed a set of subpoenas tied to a criminal probe against Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, writing that “the Government has offered no evidence whatsoever that Powell committed any crime other than displeasing the President.” Now as a result, US Attorney Jeanine Pirro is displeased and raging about it.

The subpoenas date back to January, when Powell revealed that he was being targeted by the White House for his resistance to lowering federal interest rates. For its part, the administration claimed the probe was launched due to purported misleading statements made by Powell regarding renovation costs at the Federal Reserve office buildings. A federal judge, however, didn’t buy it.

“There is abundant evidence that the subpoenas’ dominant (if not sole) purpose is to harass and pressure Powell either to yield to the President or to resign and make way for a Fed Chair who will,” the judge declared, effectively putting an end to the investigation.

Pirro was not happy with the outcome.

Speaking immediately after the judge’s decision, she complained that the “activist judge” James Boasberg — who was unanimously confirmed by the Senate in 2011 — had “neutered the grand jury’s ability to investigate crime,” leaving Powell “bathed in immunity” in a Washington, DC, “where no one is held accountable.” She offered little evidence to support such claims.

Boasberg, however, cited a number of statements by Trump as proof of the president’s real intentions, such as a social media post where he ranted, “Jerome ‘Too Late’ Powell has done it again!!! He is TOO LATE, and actually, TOO ANGRY, TOO STUPID, & TOO POLITICAL, to have the job of Fed Chair.”

“That is one of at least 100 statements that the President or his deputies have made attacking the Chair of the Federal Reserve and pressuring him to lower interest rates,” wrote Boasberg in his decision.

This is another in a growing string of defeats for Pirro since being appointed US Attorney by Trump one year ago. She failed to indict six Democratic lawmakers who made a video urging members of the military to refuse illegal orders. Her cases against former FBI director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James were thrown out. She was unable to secure charges against a man accused of assaulting federal agents with a sandwich. And the list goes on.

What links all of these targets is the blatant political motivation for leveling prosecution against them.

As former federal prosecutor Ankush Khardori wrote, “It is now clear that Pirro…is willing to use her power to try to intimidate and punish Trump’s political opponents — even if that means degrading herself, the office and DOJ, and wasting taxpayers’ money all at the same time.”

Our Analysis:

Analysis of the Judge’s Decision to Quash Subpoenas Against Jerome Powell

Institutional Power Dynamics

At the heart of this situation is a complex power struggle involving several key players: Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, US Attorney Jeanine Pirro, President Trump, and Federal Judge James Boasberg. Each holds a significant but distinct type of institutional power.

  • Jerome Powell possesses the economic influence inherent in leading the Federal Reserve, an institution crucial for U.S. monetary policy.

  • Jeanine Pirro, as US Attorney appointed by Trump, wields legal power, specifically the capacity to initiate investigations and press charges.

  • President Trump has the executive power of the presidency, which includes the authority to appoint individuals like Pirro but not direct control over the Federal Reserve.

  • Judge James Boasberg exercises judicial power, providing a check on the actions of both the executive branch and its appointees.

Decision-Making and Outcome

The decision to quash the subpoenas was made by Judge Boasberg, based on a lack of evidence for any crime committed by Powell and significant evidence suggesting the subpoenas were aimed at pressuring Powell for political reasons. This outcome underscores the judiciary’s role in curbing potential abuses of power by the executive branch or its appointees.

Framing and Responsibility

The article frames the situation as a conflict between an overreaching executive branch, represented by Pirro and implicitly Trump, and the judicial system’s efforts to maintain checks and balances, represented by Boasberg. This framing correctly places responsibility for the subpoenas and the probe on Pirro and, by extension, Trump, who are portrayed as acting out of political motivations rather than genuine legal concerns. The criticism of the judge by Pirro, especially her use of the term “activist judge,” attempts to shift blame to Boasberg for simply performing his duty to uphold the law and constitution.

Misdirection and False Equivalence

The narrative pushed by Pirro and, indirectly, by Trump, aims to misdirect responsibility by attacking the judiciary for blocking what they suggest are legitimate efforts to investigate crime. However, the lack of evidence presented for any actual wrongdoing by Powell, as noted by Boasberg, and the political context provided by Trump’s own statements, render such claims baseless. Thus, any attempt to equate the judge’s decision with an abuse of power or to paint Powell as somehow beyond accountability is misleading and unfounded.

Conclusion

The article does not fall into the traps of false equivalence, lazy narratives, or convenient scapegoats. It accurately identifies the institutional powers at play, clarifies the decision-making process, and rightly points out the misdirection and misuse of power by Pirro and, by extension, Trump. The judiciary, through Judge Boasberg, acted within its role to check potential executive overreach, highlighting the importance of separation of powers in safeguarding democratic governance. Criticism of this judicial action as obstructive or biased is misplaced and serves to distract from the actual misuse of prosecutorial power for political ends.
