Fox News issues 'rare on-air apology' after comments from MAGA Shark Tank star

Fox News Apologized for the Lie It Helped Air

Context

Fox News issued a rare on-air apology after Kevin O’Leary used the network to claim his Utah data center opponents were acting on behalf of China. Fox later said there was no evidence for that accusation and publicly distanced itself from the remark. The apology followed reported legal threats from the people O’Leary attacked.

Power Was Not With the Neighbors

The story is not really about a television correction. It is about who gets to build first, speak loudest, and then smear dissent as foreign sabotage. O’Leary is backing a 9-gigawatt data center on a 40,000-acre parcel in Utah. That is institutional power: capital, media access, and the ability to turn a local environmental fight into a national propaganda stunt.

The opponents are local residents, state leaders, and scientists warning that a natural-gas-powered facility near the Great Salt Lake could increase emissions, strain resources, and worsen an ecosystem already in decline. They are the ones raising costs, risks, and consequences. O’Leary tried to recast them as agents of China. That is not confusion. It is a smear tactic.

Fox Did Not Correct the Record Until It Was Forced To

Fox’s apology matters because it shows the network understood the problem only when legal exposure entered the picture. That is not editorial accountability. That is risk management.

The network gave O’Leary a platform, then had to publicly separate itself from his claim that his critics were working for China. The correction was unusual precisely because it was so plainly necessary. Fox did not discover truth. It discovered liability.

Scapegoating as Project Defense

When a large project faces backlash, there are two basic responses: answer the criticism or attack the critics. O’Leary chose the latter. Alleging foreign manipulation is a classic move because it converts ordinary opposition into suspected disloyalty. It shifts attention away from emissions, land use, and ecological damage and toward a manufactured enemy.

That is the deeper pattern here. Environmental and community objections are treated not as political resistance but as something illegitimate, even treasonous. The point is to make the public debate smaller, meaner, and easier to control.

The Network Enabled the Smear

Fox is not a passive stage in this story. It amplified a guest’s claim, then only backed away after the claim became untenable. That matters because this is how elite misinformation works now: not through explicit newsroom endorsement, but through strategic hosting, platforming, and post hoc denial.

The result is familiar. A powerful figure makes a reckless accusation. The network launders it through airtime. The targets are forced to defend themselves. If they push back hard enough, the broadcaster retreats and calls that prudence.

The Real Lesson

This is a small case study in how institutional power protects itself. Big projects produce local damage. Their champions demand obedience. When resistance appears, they reach for foreign-influence narratives, legal intimidation, and media access to blur the source of harm. Fox’s apology is not evidence of restraint. It is evidence that the machinery briefly ran into a wall.

The pattern is the point: power causes the disruption, then tries to make the people living with the consequences look like the problem.
