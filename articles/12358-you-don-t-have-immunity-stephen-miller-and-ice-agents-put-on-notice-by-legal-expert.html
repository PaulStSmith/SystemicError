'You don't have immunity': Stephen Miller and ICE agents put on notice by legal expert

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

U.S. White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller walks away after speaking to reporters at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Government employees who have been doing Donald Trump's dirty work during his second term should take note that the president recently admitted they may not have immunity for their actions.

And that includes White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller and ICE agents who have been assaulting and snatching immigrants off U.S. streets whether they are in the country legally or not.

That was a warning given by conservative attorney George Conway on MSNBC on Saturday morning where he talked about, among other topics, Trump's appointees going up to the line and sometimes over thereby breaking the law.

Speaking with the hosts of "The Weekend," Conway stated, "There's no check against him anymore, there's no checks. He actually, by the way, he was a lot more coherent a few years ago than yesterday, but let's set that aside."

"His mental acuity," co-host Eugene Daniels interjected.

"Yeah, no," Conway replied. "Everything is –– he's a narcissist. He's the most profoundly narcissistic individual we've ever seen in American politics. It's all me, me, me, me, me, there is no other to him. The government belongs to him, he talks about his generals. It was always going to be his Justice Department and he was always going to view the Justice Department and the attorney general and everybody who works for the attorney general to the lowest US assistant, US attorney in the smallest district in the country."

"And let me just say, I mean, it's working for all these people who want these nice jobs, right, that they're not qualified for, like Alina Habba and the guy in the Northern District of New York," he added.

"But you know, Trump said something before the break in his incoherent way that, actually, the people who work for him now should remember," he pointed out. "He [Trump] said that the people who work for Obama, they're not protected by the immunity decision. Well, all you people who are getting those jobs right now working for Donald Trump, whether you be the lowliest ICE agent or Stephen Miller himself? You better watch it because you don't have immunity."

That earned him a "Wow!" from Jonathan Capehart.

Our Analysis:

The Stark Reality of Trump's Tenure: A Closer Examination

In an era where political discourse often descends into chaos, the recent remarks by conservative attorney George Conway on MSNBC shed a glaring light on the perilous path tread by government employees under Donald Trump's administration. Conway's observations are not merely a critique but a dire warning to those entangled in Trump's web of questionable directives, especially highlighting figures like White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller and ICE agents involved in the contentious handling of immigrants.

Stephen Miller and ICE Agents: Pawns in Trump's Game?

Conway's comments underscore a grave reality: the individuals executing Trump's contentious policies, particularly those targeting immigrants, are dangerously close to, if not already beyond, legal and ethical boundaries. The mention of Stephen Miller alongside ICE agents snatching immigrants off U.S. streets, irrespective of their legal status, paints a vivid picture of an administration willing to trample on rights and decency in pursuit of its agenda.

Trump's Warning: An Admission of Vulnerability?

Perhaps the most striking revelation from Conway's discourse is Trump's own admission regarding the lack of immunity for his aides and appointees. This candid acknowledgment, as highlighted by Conway, should serve as a wake-up call to those still operating under the illusion of protection within Trump's circle. The implication is clear: involvement in executing or endorsing questionable policies could have long-lasting legal and moral consequences.

The Danger of Narcissism in Leadership

Conway's portrayal of Trump as the "most profoundly narcissistic individual we've ever seen in American politics" is not just a character assassination; it's a critical analysis of how narcissism at the helm can distort governance into a one-man show, disregarding checks, balances, and the very essence of democratic accountability. This narcissism, as Conway suggests, transforms the government into an extension of Trump's ego, with every action and decision orbiting around his whims.

The Illusion of Immunity and the Reality of Accountability

The crux of Conway's warning lies in the illusion of immunity that Trump's associates might feel enveloped in. The stark reminder that Trump himself pointed out the lack of immunity for Obama's aides, only to apply the same precarious situation to his own staff, underscores a universal truth in governance: actions have consequences, and positions of power do not equate to a carte blanche for unlawful or unethical behavior.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Caution

The insights provided by George Conway, albeit scathing, serve as a necessary critique of the current state of affairs under Trump's administration. For those within Trump's orbit, it is a moment for serious reflection on the ethical and legal ramifications of their actions. For the broader public, it is a call to remain vigilant and demand accountability from those in power.

In dissecting the implications of Trump's governance and the potential pitfalls for his aides and appointees, it becomes evident that the line between serving the public and serving Trump is not just blurred but perilously non-existent. The message from Conway is clear: those who align themselves too closely with Trump's controversial policies may find themselves in a quagmire of legal and ethical challenges. It's a sobering reminder that in the end, the law and moral accountability may very well catch up, leaving behind a trail of cautionary tales for future administrations.
