Of all the legal problems Donald Trump faced, from accusations of sexual assault to business fraud, he most feared the Florida stolen government documents investigation by special counsel Jack Smith that ended up being dropped when he won re-election.

That is according to “Morning Joe” co-host Jonathan Lemire, who reported on the president’s terror at being convicted during a discussion on U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon’s Monday ruling that permanently blocked the Justice Department from releasing Smith’s final report.

With MS NOW legal analyst Lisa Rubin once again saying “all bets” would be off if the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals decides to intervene, Lemire pointed out that Trump knew he was fortunate that his appointee covered up for him.

After noting there was considerable second-guessing going on after Smith elected to pursue the case in Florida, which led to Cannon’s courtroom, Lemire reported, ”Judge Cannon ended up putting a thumb on the scale significantly for Trump down the line. And you’re right, Joe [Scarborough], we’ve talked about it on this show, Trump has privately told people, you know, more or less that he [Smith] was dead to rights on that one.”

“That was the case, the classified documents case that he feared the most,” he added. “And I know there will be second-guessing for all time in terms of the sequencing of those cases. The one in Manhattan went first, got a conviction, but it seemed to many to be the least serious, and we will never know if things had gone quicker or if the order was changed, what would have happened.“

“But you’re right, Cannon, certainly at every opportunity, seems to be deferential to Trump and Trump’s wishes,” he added.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Teflon Presidency: A Closer Look at the Stolen Government Documents Investigation

In a recent revelation, “Morning Joe” co-host Jonathan Lemire peeled back another layer of the seemingly impervious legal armor of Donald Trump. Amidst the cacophony of legal challenges ranging from accusations of sexual assault to business fraud, Trump’s greatest fear was reportedly the investigation into stolen government documents in Florida—a case that could have significantly marred his re-election campaign, had it not been for the intervention of U.S. District Judge Aileen Cannon.

Trump’s Legal Lifeline: A Judge’s Bias?

The narrative takes a darker turn with the involvement of Judge Cannon, who is depicted as having “put a thumb on the scale significantly for Trump”. This isn’t just a minor judicial discrepancy; it’s a colossal failure in the justice system, where impartiality should be the bedrock. Lisa Rubin, an MS NOW legal analyst, hinted at the precarious nature of this situation, suggesting that if the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals were to intervene, it could potentially unravel this thread of judicial partisanship.

The Fear Factor: The Classified Documents Case

Lemire’s commentary sheds light on Trump’s personal fears regarding the classified documents case, which he believed had him “dead to rights.” This admission, albeit indirect, provides a rare glimpse into the former president’s psyche, revealing that even he acknowledges the gravity of the allegations against him—allegations that, in a just world, could have led to significant legal repercussions.

A Sequence of Questionable Decisions

The sequencing of the cases against Trump is another point of contention. The Manhattan case, which led to a conviction, was perceived by many as the least serious of the charges Trump faced. This raises critical questions about the strategic choices made by the legal entities pursuing these cases. Could a different sequence have led to a different outcome? We’re left to speculate on what might have been, had the more severe charges been prioritized.

Conclusion: The Scales of Justice or the Scale of Injustice?

As Lemire points out, Judge Cannon’s deference to Trump at “every opportunity” not only highlights a deeply troubling bias but also underscores the systemic flaws within our judicial system. When a judge’s allegiance to a political figure outweighs their commitment to impartial justice, it erodes public trust in our legal institutions.

In analyzing these developments, one can’t help but wonder about the state of democracy and the rule of law in America. If those in power can manipulate the legal system to serve their interests, what hope do ordinary citizens have for justice? The case of Donald Trump and the stolen government documents investigation is more than a legal battle; it’s a stark reminder of the work that lies ahead in ensuring that justice is truly blind and that no individual is above the law.
