Trump-appointed judge skeptical of DOJ's bid to bury damning info on MAGA rioter

A Trump-appointed federal judge has ordered Justice Department prosecutors to explain why they want the original sentencing memo for a pardoned Jan. 6 rioter to remain under seal.

In the court order, obtained by Lawfare’s Roger Parloff, U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols wrote, “The government is to file a memo stating why document [155] should remain sealed and a response regarding Motion [142].”

The controversy stems from the sentencing memo written against Tyler Taranto by the federal prosecutors originally overseeing the case.

In 2023, after President Donald Trump posted the address of former President Barack Obama’s house on his Truth Social platform, Taranto went to that location armed with illegal guns, live-streamed himself, and issued a false threat to bomb a government building. He was subsequently arrested.

In the original sentencing memo written by Assistant U.S. Attorneys Carlos Valdivia and Samuel White, the government made references to all these details and sought a 27-month prison sentence.

But earlier this week, the DOJ abruptly placed Valdivia and White on leave, removed the sentencing memo from the docket, and filed a revised one that removed all references to him being a Jan. 6 rioter or Trump having publicly revealed the address shortly before he went to it.

Nichols, who had originally convicted Taranto in a bench trial, ultimately sentenced him to 21 months in prison — which releases him on time served — and an additional three years of supervised release.

The judge also went out of his way to praise the “excellent“ work of the two removed prosecutors during proceedings this week.

Our Analysis:

The Masked Prejudices in Judicial Discretion

In a move that would make Kafka blush, a Trump-appointed federal judge, U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols, demands transparency from the Justice Department, a plea as ironical as it is audacious. The case in question orbits around Tyler Taranto, a pardoned Jan. 6 rioter whose original sentencing memo has become the eye of a legal storm.

The Sealed Sentencing Memo: A Symphony of Silence

At the heart of this controversy is the push to keep the original sentencing memo for Taranto under wraps. Authored by Assistant U.S. Attorneys Carlos Valdivia and Samuel White, this document is no mere piece of paper but a testament to Taranto’s alarming actions post-Jan. 6. After Trump, with reckless abandon, posted former President Barack Obama’s address on his Truth Social platform, Taranto, armed and dangerous, made his way to Obama’s residence, live-streaming his intentions and threatening to bomb a government building. The sentencing memo, originally advocating for a 27-month prison sentence, highlighted these points before it was unceremoniously plucked from the docket and sanitized of all mentions tying Taranto to the Jan. 6 riot or Trump’s social media antics.

The DOJ’s Sudden Amnesia

The Department of Justice’s abrupt decision to sideline Valdivia and White, coupled with the redaction of significant details from the sentencing memo, reeks of political interference at its most blatant. The revised memo, stripped of its context, presents a neutered narrative, conspicuously omitting any reference to Taranto’s involvement in the Jan. 6 insurrection or the incendiary role played by Trump’s social media behavior. This act of selective memory loss not only undermines the gravity of Taranto’s actions but also casts a shadow over the DOJ’s commitment to impartial justice.

Nichols’ Curious Applause

Judge Nichols, meanwhile, appears to play the part of the concerned jurist with a performance worthy of an Oscar. While he sentenced Taranto to 21 months in prison, effectively time served, and an additional three years of supervised release, Nichols made it a point to laud the “excellent” work of the now-removed prosecutors. This applause, however hollow, cannot mask the underlying dissonance of the situation. One must ask: If their work was so “excellent,” why were their original findings and recommendations scrubbed clean from the record?

A Judicial System at a Crossroads

This episode is emblematic of a judicial system grappling with its own identity in the face of political pressures. The sealing of the sentencing memo, the sidelining of federal prosecutors, and the subsequent scrubbing of inconvenient truths from official documents are not mere procedural footnotes. They are symptomatic of a larger, more troubling trend towards opacity and manipulation within the legal processes that are supposed to uphold justice and accountability.

As this saga unfolds, it serves as a stark reminder of the systemic errors that plague our judicial system. It’s not just about a sealed document or a revised sentencing memo; it’s about the integrity of our institutions and the public’s trust in them. The handling of Taranto’s case is a litmus test for the justice system’s resilience against the forces of political expediency and obfuscation. Sadly, it’s a test that, thus far, we seem to be failing.
