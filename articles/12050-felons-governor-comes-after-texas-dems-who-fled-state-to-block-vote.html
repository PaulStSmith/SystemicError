'Felons!' Governor comes after Texas Dems who fled state to block vote

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 5, 20256 min read

Gov. Greg Abbott informed Texas House Democrats late Sunday that he would attempt to have them removed from office if they do not return to Austin to pass the GOP’s proposed new congressional maps.

The Republican governor’s late-night missive came after more than 50 Democrats left the state Sunday afternoon so the Texas House would not have a quorum — the number of lawmakers needed to consider and pass legislation under chamber rules — aiming to grind all legislative activity to a halt for the remainder of the special session, slated to end later this month They are hoping to stop the passage of a new congressional map, drawn at the direction of President Donald Trump, that could net five additional seats for Republicans in the 2026 midterms.

“This truancy ends now,” Abbott said in a letter sent to each of the departed members. “The derelict Democrat House members must return to Texas and be in attendance when the House reconvenes at 3:00 PM on Monday, August 4, 2025.”

If they are not back by then, Abbott said, he would initiate legal action to remove them from office. He cited a nonbinding 2021 legal opinion from Attorney General Ken Paxton, who said it would be up to a court to decide whether a lawmaker who had left the state to deny quorum had forfeited their office. If a court were to decide that the legislators had vacated their offices, Abbott would be permitted to fill those seats with appointees of his choosing, Paxton’s opinion stated.

Abbott further alleged members could face felony charges for fundraising to pay the $500 fine they will each accrue every day they are away from Austin during the session. Lawmakers who are “soliciting funds to evade the fines they will incur under House rules” may be violating bribery laws, Abbott said, adding that anyone who donates to the cause could also be liable.

The governor said he would move to extradite “any potential out-of-state felons.” Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker said at Sunday’s press conference that he would do whatever he could to help Texas lawmakers while they were on the lam.

“We’re going to do everything we can to protect every single one of them,” Pritzker said.

The Texas House Democratic Caucus, the organizing group for House Democrats, responded to Abbott's threats with a four-word statement that read, "Come and take it."

Democrats have condemned the mid-cycle redistricting as a racist and partisan attack on Black and Hispanic voters, as well as an unnecessary distraction from passing legislation to address the devastating Hill Country floods that left 130 people dead. The proposed map, which is expected to be brought to the House floor for a vote Monday, carves up existing districts in Houston, Dallas and Austin, and is expected to net five additional seats for Republicans.

GOP state lawmakers have defended the process, arguing they are permitted to redraw maps for partisan gain between the usual once-a-decade redistricting process that happens after each U.S. census.

Our Analysis:

Governor's Gambit: Abbott's Threats Against Democracy

In a move that reeks of desperation and autocratic tendencies, Texas Governor Greg Abbott has threatened to remove Democrats from office for employing a time-honored legislative tactic: breaking quorum. This act of political theater, detailed in a late-night correspondence that can only be described as draconian, highlights a dangerous escalation in the battle for democratic representation.

The Chessboard: More than 50 Texas House Democrats took a bold stand against a controversial redistricting plan, purportedly drawn at the behest of President Donald Trump, designed to unfairly net Republicans five additional seats in the 2026 midterms. This plan, a blatant power grab, serves as the backdrop for Abbott's threats.

Abbott's letter, infused with a tone of authoritarianism, demands the return of these "derelict" Democrats by a specified deadline or face legal action to forcibly remove them from office. This threat, based on a nonbinding 2021 legal opinion by Attorney General Ken Paxton, a figure not known for his impartiality, sets a dangerous precedent. What Abbott proposes is not just a threat to these lawmakers but a threat to the very fabric of democratic governance.

The Irony of 'Truancy': Labeling elected officials as truant for adopting a strategic, albeit drastic, legislative tactic is both ironic and hypocritical. These Democrats have not abandoned their duty but have chosen a path of resistance to an undemocratic process that threatens the integrity of Texas's electoral landscape.

The Threat of Felony Charges: Adding insult to injury, Abbott suggests that fundraising efforts to cover the fines incurred by this act of protest could be construed as bribery, further threatening to criminalize the Democrats' actions. This move to financially and legally hamstring the opposition is not just underhanded; it's a gross misuse of legal authority.

The Shield of Solidarity: Illinois Governor JB Pritzker’s pledge to protect these lawmakers underscores the national significance of this standoff. It's a stark reminder that the fight for fair representation transcends state lines, becoming a rallying cry for defenders of democracy everywhere.

The Response: "Come and take it." With these four words, the Texas House Democratic Caucus has not only rebuffed Abbott's threats but also encapsulated the spirit of defiance necessary in the face of such authoritarian tactics. This isn't just about Texas; it's a microcosm of the broader struggle for democratic principles in America.

The Real Distraction: Amid this spectacle, let's not lose sight of the real issues at stake. The proposed redistricting is a direct assault on the voting rights of Black and Hispanic communities, framed by the GOP as a legitimate exercise of partisan power. Furthermore, Abbott's focus on this political maneuvering, rather than addressing the recent Hill Country floods that claimed 130 lives, is a testament to his misplaced priorities.

Conclusion: Governor Abbott's threats against Texas House Democrats are not just an attack on a few individuals but an assault on the democratic process itself. It's a chilling reminder of the lengths to which some will go to cling to power. As this drama unfolds, it's crucial that we stand in solidarity with those fighting for fair and representative democracy, lest we allow the erosion of our freedoms under the guise of legal and legislative authority.
