‘Horrifying’: JD Vance ignites firestorm with one word

Vice President JD Vance is facing strong criticism from legal and political experts after commenting on the FBI’s seven-hour raid on Friday morning on former Trump National Security Advisor John Bolton’s Maryland home and Washington, D.C. office, reportedly in a search for classified documents.

“The information that provided the basis for the warrant to search John Bolton’s home was based on intelligence collected overseas by the C.I.A., according to people who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss an ongoing criminal investigation,” The New York Times reported. “It involved the mishandling of classified material by Bolton, the people said.”

But some critics charge that the raid is retribution for Bolton’s past and present criticism of the Trump administration. Indeed, reportedly during the raid, Bolton tweeted: “Russia has not changed its goal: drag Ukraine into a new Russian Empire. Moscow has demanded that Ukraine cede territory it already holds and the remainder of Donetsk, which it has been unable to conquer. Zelensky will never do so. Meanwhile, meetings will continue because Trump wants a Nobel Peace Prize, but I don’t see these talks making any progress.”

Speaking to NBC News (video below), Vice President Vance delivered remarks that suggested the wall constructed after Watergate to separate the White House — including both the president and vice president — from the U.S. Department of Justice may have been compromised, some suggested.

“We’re in the very early stages of an ongoing investigation into John Bolton,” Vance told NBC News’ Kristen Welker, drawing criticism.

“And so if we think that Ambassador Bolton has committed a crime, of course, eventually prosecutions will come,” he also said. “And like I said, if there’s no crime here, we’re not gonna prosecute it.”

Experts responded swiftly.

Harvard Kennedy School Professor Juliette Kayyem is a CNN senior national security analyst and the former U.S. Assistant Secretary of Homeland Security for Intergovernmental Affairs.

“’We’re’ is the tell,” she wrote. “Who is ‘we’ when the Department of Justice is supposed to be free of political influence,” Kayyem asked.

“‘We’??” wrote U.S. Rep. Daniel Goldman (D-NY). “JD Vance (and the WH) are clearly involved in — and likely directing — the DOJ investigation of Trump nemesis John Bolton.”

“Everyone is focusing on the ‘early stages’ remark, but the telling word he used was ‘we’re.’ If this were any other administration, the FBI would be acting independently of the executive branch,” noted The Philadelphia Inquirer’s Rob Tornoe.

“So is the VP @JDVance part of the ‘we’ who are directing an investigation into one of his most vocal critics? Why is that not the natural and horrifying follow up question here?” asked Professor of Law Joseph Mastrosimone.

“It’s notable his response isn’t ‘The Justice Department is operating independently and I don’t have any firsthand knowledge about the investigation,'” wrote Business Insider legal correspondent Jacob Shamsian.

“The Vice President has absolutely no place in the ‘We’ who are investigating a private citizen. Totally corrupt,” declared former Biden White House official Jesse Lee.

Our Analysis:

JD Vance's Dubious Involvement: A Critical Analysis

In a recent unfolding that reads like a script ripped from the annals of political vendettas, Vice President JD Vance has found himself at the eye of a storm, one that threatens not only the sanctity of political independence but also the foundational principles of justice in the United States. The controversy stems from Vance's comments on the FBI's raid on former Trump National Security Advisor John Bolton’s properties, a raid reportedly in pursuit of classified documents. The narrative, as reported by The New York Times, implicates overseas intelligence collected by the C.I.A. as the basis for this legal action, a detail that sows the first seeds of intrigue in what is to become a thorny thicket of political machination.

The Raid as Retribution?

Critics argue that the raid on Bolton's properties is not a straightforward legal maneuver but rather a politically motivated act of retribution. Bolton, known for his caustic critiques of the Trump administration, both during and after his tenure as National Security Advisor, represents a figure of defiance against a regime that is notoriously intolerant of dissent. His tweet amidst the raid—a commentary on Russia's imperial ambitions in Ukraine—underscores his continued engagement in political discourse, refusing silence even in the face of federal scrutiny.

Vance's Incendiary Remarks

It is Vice President JD Vance's remarks to NBC News, however, that fan the flames of controversy. Vance suggests an intimate involvement of the White House in the ongoing investigation into Bolton, a statement that shatters the purported wall of separation between the executive branch and the Department of Justice—a safeguard erected in the aftermath of Watergate to prevent political meddling in legal affairs. The use of "we" by Vance has not gone unnoticed, drawing fierce criticism from legal and political experts who view this as a blatant encroachment of political influence into the realm of justice.

Harvard Kennedy School Professor Juliette Kayyem and U.S. Rep. Daniel Goldman (D-NY) are among the many voices raising alarm over Vance's lexical choice, interpreting it as a direct indication of the White House's involvement in the Justice Department's proceedings. This breach of independence not only undermines the credibility of the investigation but also sets a dangerous precedent for future administrations.

The Chorus of Condemnation

The chorus of condemnation resonates with calls for accountability and transparency. Critics, including Professor of Law Joseph Mastrosimone and Business Insider legal correspondent Jacob Shamsian, question why Vance's immediate response was not to affirm the Justice Department's autonomy. Instead, his comments imply a disturbing level of executive oversight and potential direction of the investigation into Bolton, a critic of Vance's political allies.

Former Biden White House official Jesse Lee's denouncement of Vance's involvement as "totally corrupt" encapsulates the broader sentiment of those concerned with the integrity of the American justice system. The critique is not merely about the potential politicization of one investigation but about the erosion of democratic norms and the rule of law.

Conclusion: A Call for Integrity and Independence

Vance's remarks, whether offhand or indicative of a deeper truth, have spotlighted the fragile barrier between political power and judicial independence. In an era where political divisions run deep and the sanctity of democratic institutions is under constant scrutiny, the controversy surrounding the raid on John Bolton's properties is a stark reminder of the importance of maintaining clear demarcations between the realms of politics and justice.

The unfolding narrative offers more than just a glimpse into a singular event of political intrigue; it serves as a critical juncture at which the principles of justice, independence, and the rule of law must be vehemently defended. As the situation develops, it is incumbent upon all stakeholders—politicians, legal experts, and the public—to vigilantly guard against the encroachment of political interests into the sacred halls of justice.
