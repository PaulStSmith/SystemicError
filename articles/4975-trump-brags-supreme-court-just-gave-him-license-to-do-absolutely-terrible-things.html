Trump brags Supreme Court just gave him 'license to do absolutely terrible things'

U.S. President Donald Trump looks on as he speaks to reporters onboard Air Force One, on travel from West Palm Beach, Florida, to Joint Base Andrews, Maryland, U.S., February 16, 2026. REUTERS/Elizabeth Frantz

Donald Trump continued his war on the Supreme Court early Monday, apparently still smarting from the bruising landmark 6-3 decision limiting his ability to levy tariffs that was handed down on Friday.

After lashing out at the six justices who did not bend to his will at an impromptu White House press conference, calling the ruling a “disgrace to our nation,” he opened up a new front and insisted the nation’s highest court had already given him free rein to do as he pleases.

On Truth Social, he wrote Monday, “The supreme court (will be using lower case letters for a while based on a complete lack of respect!) of the United States accidentally and unwittingly gave me, as President of the United States, far more powers and strength than I had prior to their ridiculous, dumb, and very internationally divisive ruling.”

He added, “For one thing, I can use Licenses to do absolutely ‘terrible’ things to foreign countries, especially those countries that have been RIPPING US OFF for many decades, but incomprehensibly, according to the ruling, can’t charge them a License fee - BUT ALL LICENSES CHARGE FEES, why can’t the United States do so? You do a license to get a fee! The opinion doesn’t explain that, but I know the answer! The court has also approved all other Tariffs, of which their [sic] are many, and they can all be used in a much more powerful and obnoxious way, with legal certainty, than the Tariffs as initially used.”

He then fired off another smear, writing, “Our incompetent supreme court did a great job for the wrong people, and for that they should be ashamed of themselves (but not the Great Three!). The next thing you know they will rule in favor of China and others, who are making an absolute fortune on Birthright Citizenship, by saying the 14th Amendment was NOT written to take care of the ‘babies of slaves,’ which it was as proven by the EXACT TIMING of its construction, filing, and ratification, which perfectly coincided with the END OF THE CIVIL WAR. How much better can you do than that?”

“But this supreme court will find a way to come to the wrong conclusion, one that again will make China, and various other Nations, happy and rich. Let our supreme court keep making decisions that are so bad and deleterious to the future of our Nation - I have a job to do. MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN! President DONALD J. TRUMP,” he concluded.

Our Analysis:

The Latest Tirade: Trump vs. The Supreme Court

In an episode that feels ripped straight from a political satire, former President Donald Trump has once again taken to social media to air his grievances. This time, his target is none other than the Supreme Court of the United States—or, as he pettily demeans it, the “supreme court,” intentionally stripped of capitalization in a juvenile display of disrespect.

The Root of the Fury

Trump’s latest outburst can be traced back to a 6-3 decision that curtailed his proclaimed authority to impose tariffs. This ruling, which Trump deems a “disgrace to our nation,” has sparked a series of inflammatory reactions that range from the absurd to the downright dangerous.

A Deluded Interpretation of Power

Trump’s rant on Truth Social is a bewildering journey through a landscape of delusion and misunderstanding. He claims, bizarrely, that the Supreme Court’s decision has inadvertently granted him “far more powers and strength” than before. The logic here is as flawed as it is perplexing, with Trump railing against the inability to charge license fees to foreign countries—a complaint that reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of both the decision and how tariffs actually work.

His assertion that the Court has “approved all other Tariffs” and that they can be wielded in a “much more powerful and obnoxious way” not only distorts the ruling but also underscores a worrying penchant for authoritarianism. The use of tariffs as a weapon, rather than a tool of economic policy, is a dangerous proposition that betrays a lack of understanding of international trade dynamics.

A Smear Campaign Against the Judiciary

Trump’s subsequent attacks on the Supreme Court are both personal and political. He accuses the justices of incompetence and of acting in the interests of “the wrong people”—a vague accusation that seems to hint at a conspiracy against his administration. His fear-mongering about the Court possibly favoring China in future rulings further illustrates his tendency to view complex legal issues through a simplistic, us-vs-them lens.

His reference to the 14th Amendment and birthright citizenship is a particularly egregious example of historical revisionism. Trump’s claim that the amendment was solely intended to address the issue of “babies of slaves” ignores its broader implications and the foundational principle of equal protection under the law.

The Bigger Picture

Trump’s tirade is not just a temper tantrum thrown on social media; it’s a direct assault on the credibility and independence of the judiciary. By undermining the Supreme Court with baseless claims and personal vendettas, he threatens to erode public trust in one of the pillars of American democracy.

Moreover, his rhetoric of making America “great” and “powerful” through aggressive and unilateral actions like imposing tariffs reveals a misguided view of leadership and governance. True greatness comes from cooperation, respect for democratic institutions, and adherence to the rule of law—concepts that seem foreign to Trump’s brand of politics.

In Conclusion

The recent spectacle of Trump vs. the Supreme Court is more than just another chapter in the saga of a former president who refuses to fade into the background. It’s a stark reminder of the dangers posed by leaders who put their egos before the principles of democracy and governance. Trump’s unyielding assault on the judiciary is not just an affront to the Supreme Court—it’s an attack on the very foundations of democratic governance. As observers and participants in this democracy, it is our duty to remain vigilant and to call out such baseless attacks for what they are: the desperate flailings of a man unable to come to terms with his power being checked by the very system that upholds our nation’s values.
