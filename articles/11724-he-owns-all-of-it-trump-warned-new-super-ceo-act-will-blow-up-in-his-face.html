According to a report from the Washington Post, the president's decision to take federal control of the policing in Washington, D.C. is just another item he placed on his to-do list that includes negotiating with China on AI chip sales and brokering peace between Russia and Ukraine -- all of which could blow up in his face.

As the Post's Naftali Bendavid and Abha Bhattarai wrote, "Trump’s personal involvement has been most striking when it comes to the economy, where he acts as a sort of super-CEO, becoming involved in companies’ individual deals and personnel decisions."

That led Douglas Holtz-Eakin, chief economist of the Council of Economic Advisers under President George W. Bush, to warn, "He owns all of it. He doesn’t even delegate it to Cabinet secretaries that he can fire if it doesn’t go well. He now owns crime in D.C. He owns peace in the Middle East. He owns a Ukrainian ceasefire … I would never advise a president to take ownership of so many things.”

One Trump adviser also urged caution.

“I’m all in favor of the things President Trump is doing to make America’s economy strong again, but we want the government and the business community to be at arm’s length from each other,” Stephen Moore admitted. "With good economic policy, you want to put into place policies that will benefit all businesses, without creating winners and losers. The White House needs to steer clear of any idea that they’re picking favorites.”

The report added, "The ultimate success or failure of Trump’s approach remains uncertain, although many economists say his aggressive tactics on tariffs and unpredictable approach generally are creating a drag on the economy that is likely to intensify."

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Trump's Centralized Leadership: A Recipe for Disaster?

In the political theater, where the line between leading and micromanaging blurs, former President Donald Trump's approach to governance stands out—not necessarily for its brilliance, but for its brazen centralization. According to a Washington Post report, Trump's penchant for making himself the main character in every major effort by his administration is a high-stakes gamble that could have easily backfired, painting a target on his back for any and all failures.

The Super-CEO Syndrome

Trump's personal involvement in matters typically delegated to experts or subordinates, especially those related to the economy, is astonishing. Described as acting like a "super-CEO," Trump's foray into individual company deals and personnel decisions is not only unprecedented but borderline reckless. This approach led Douglas Holtz-Eakin, a chief economist under President George W. Bush, to caution against such a monopolization of responsibility. “He owns all of it,” Holtz-Eakin warned, highlighting the impossibility of distancing oneself from failure when one is so deeply entrenched.

The High Risk of Personalized Politics

Trump’s decision to take federal control of policing in Washington, D.C., negotiate with China on AI chip sales, and broker peace between Russia and Ukraine are prime examples of his all-in strategy. This personal involvement was so pronounced that one Trump adviser, Stephen Moore, expressed concern over the close ties between the government and the business community, emphasizing the need for policies that benefit all businesses rather than picking winners and losers.

The Economic Drag of Unpredictability

The report underscores the uncertain outcome of Trump's aggressive tactics, particularly his fondness for tariffs and an overall unpredictable approach. Many economists believe that these tactics are likely to exert a drag on the economy, intensifying over time. Trump's strategy, while bold, could be likened to steering a ship through a storm without a compass—where the destination is as uncertain as the journey itself.

In Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

Trump's approach to leadership, characterized by its centralized, hands-on nature, serves as a cautionary tale for future administrations. The risks associated with such a strategy are manifold, not least of which is the potential for significant economic and diplomatic fallout. The mixture of politics and personal ego, as demonstrated by Trump's tenure, is a volatile one, with consequences that reach far beyond the Oval Office.

As the debate over the efficacy of his methods continues, one thing remains clear: the legacy of Trump's presidency will be inexorably tied to the successes and failures of his direct interventions. Whether this will be seen as a bold move by a visionary leader or a cautionary example of overreach remains to be seen. However, the lessons from this approach—about the dangers of too much centralization and the value of delegation—should not be ignored by current and future leaders.
