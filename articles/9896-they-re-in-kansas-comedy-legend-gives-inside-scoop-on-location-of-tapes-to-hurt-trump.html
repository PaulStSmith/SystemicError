'They're in Kansas': Comedy legend gives inside scoop on location of tapes to hurt Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

Tom Arnold has been claiming for years to know about recordings so shocking they could end President Donald Trump’s political career, and he recently pinpointed their whereabouts.

The comedian and actor produced a show called “The Hunt for the Trump Tapes” back in 2018, early in the president’s first term in office, about his search for the fabled recordings from “The Apprentice” reality TV show, and he spoke about them to journalist Brian Karem and attorney Mark Zaid on their “Just Ask The Press” podcast.

“Billy Bush was in the news, they were talking about the ‘Access Hollywood’ thing,” Arnold said. “I’ve always liked Billy Bush, good guy, and he actually said something that got some play. There’s a set of secret recordings that are everything bad Trump ever said on ‘The Apprentice,’ because they have to keep the outtakes because of a legal thing.”

Bush was with Trump when was infamously bragging about sexually assaulting women by grabbing their genitals and getting away with the crime because he’s a celebrity, but Arnold said his recollection of “The Apprentice” tapes was faulty.

“Well, here’s the thing,” Arnold said. “He said they’re in Idaho, but they are not. They are in Kansas, outside of Hutchinson, Kansas, where MGM has their underground, salt mine vault, and there was a time when a guy, Bryan Fogel, who won the Oscar for ‘Icarus,’ the best documentary, ‘Icarus,’ about the Russian doping scandal. He’s a buddy of mine, and we were talking about, well, how do you get in there and get those.”

“We had a whole elaborate thing where I’ve met the forklift driver, I know a lot of people,” Arnold said. “He thought I should be the one that goes in and gets those. They’re not called cassettes, they way they store them on this digital – bring out one at a time, but if they put it there, until we get them out. It’s like the guy who works at a car plant, steals one piece at a time and builds [a car over 20 years]. But that just seemed like it would be too time-consuming.”

Our Analysis:

The Ever-Elusive Trump Tapes: A Wild Goose Chase or a Quest with Merit?

Tom Arnold’s pursuit of the so-called Trump tapes has been nothing short of a modern-day political odyssey, replete with claims that could easily be mistaken for the plot of a Hollywood thriller. Arnold’s assertions, laid out in a recent episode of the “Just Ask The Press” podcast, not only reignite the fervor around President Donald Trump’s alleged unguarded moments on “The Apprentice” but also underscore a broader narrative about the lengths to which individuals are willing to go to unearth potentially career-ending material on political figures. Yet, beneath the surface of this sensational quest lies a tale of misinformation, misdirection, and, quite possibly, a wild goose chase of epic proportions.

The Misdirection: From Idaho to Kansas

Arnold’s claim that the tapes are not, in fact, stashed away in Idaho but are instead hidden in an underground salt mine vault in Kansas, managed by MGM, is a narrative twist worthy of a spy novel. The specificity of the location, alongside the mention of Bryan Fogel, an Oscar-winning documentarian, adds a layer of credibility to Arnold’s tale. However, it’s essential to approach these claims with a healthy dose of skepticism. This isn’t the first time we’ve been led on a merry dance by those claiming to have the silver bullet to end Trump’s political career. The fact that these tapes, if they indeed exist, have remained out of the public’s reach for so long raises questions about their existence and the veracity of Arnold’s claims.

A Quest Fueled by Speculation

Arnold’s account of his elaborate plan to retrieve the tapes, involving forklift drivers and insider assistance, reads more like a heist movie plot than a credible journalistic endeavor. This raises a critical point about the nature of Arnold’s quest: is it a genuine attempt to bring to light damning evidence against Trump, or is it a quixotic adventure fueled by speculation and a desire for public attention? The comparison to a worker stealing parts from a car plant to build a car over 20 years underscores the seemingly Sisyphean nature of Arnold’s pursuit. It’s an endeavor marked by its time-consuming and seemingly impossible odds of success.

The Larger Implication: A Distraction from Substantive Critiques

While Arnold’s quest for the Trump tapes is undoubtedly captivating, it distracts from more substantive, fact-based critiques of Trump’s tenure in office. The focus on potentially sensational yet elusive recordings diverts attention from the well-documented actions and policies of the Trump administration that warrant scrutiny and debate. This pursuit of a silver bullet in the form of incriminating tapes exemplifies the pitfalls of sensationalism in political discourse. It encourages a focus on the scandalous and speculative, rather than engaging with the tangible and often more complex realities of political leadership and accountability.

Conclusion: A Quest with Uncertain Merit

Tom Arnold’s pursuit of the Trump tapes is a saga that continues to captivate and confound in equal measure. While the prospect of uncovering recordings that could potentially end Trump’s political career is undeniably intriguing, the lack of tangible progress and the increasingly fantastical nature of the quest raise significant questions about its merit and ultimate goal. In the end, the focus should perhaps shift from chasing shadows in underground vaults to addressing the clear and present challenges posed by political figures in the harsh light of day. Only then can we hope to engage in a political discourse grounded in fact, rather than being led astray by the tantalizing mirage of career-ending tapes.
