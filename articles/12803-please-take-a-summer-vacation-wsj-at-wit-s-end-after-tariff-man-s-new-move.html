'Please take a summer vacation': WSJ at wit's end after 'Tariff Man's' new move

"Would Tariff Man please take a summer vacation for the good of the nation?" wrote the board. "Stocks tumbled on Friday after President Trump announced he will raise tariffs on Canada to 35%, starting Aug. 1. Then in an interview with NBC, Mr. Trump floated increasing his current 10% across-the-board tariffs on many countries to 15% or 20%."

This follows months of chaos and uncertainty, in which Trump initially agreed to exempt all Canadian imports covered by the USMCA trade deal he negotiated in his first term — which itself had essentially just been the Trans-Pacific Partnership he canceled upon taking office, but only covering countries under the earlier NAFTA trade deal.

"For the record, since Mr. Trump has a fixation with trade deficits, the U.S. had a surplus with Canada last year when excluding $141 billion in crude oil imports," the board wrote. "Canadian crude feeds refineries in the Midwest and some on the Gulf Coast that aren’t equipped to process U.S. shale blends. Crimping that flow would eliminate the overall trade deficit with Canada, but it would also raise costs for U.S. consumers, while sending dollars to the Middle East instead."

Ultimately, the board wrote, Trump is just making the world economy worse even as he believes he's being a shrewd negotiator.

"Mr. Trump seems to think that his unpredictability is a negotiating advantage," the board concluded. "But keeping trading partners guessing — along with investors and U.S. companies with global supply chains — isn’t a recipe for economic strength."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tariff Turmoil: A Dissection of Economic Chaos

In an era where political decisions often seem more aligned with the whims of reality TV drama than the sober realities of global economics, President Donald Trump's latest maneuver in the realm of tariffs is both a head-scratcher and a face-palmer. The Wall Street Journal's editorial board, a bastion of conservative economic thought and no stranger to Trump's often bewildering fiscal policies, has unleashed a scathing critique of Trump's recent decision to impose a new round of tariffs on Canada. This move, described with a palpable sense of frustration and disbelief, merits a deeper dive.

Tariff Man's Latest Act

"Would Tariff Man please take a summer vacation for the good of the nation?" This plea from the Wall Street Journal's editorial board encapsulates the exasperation felt by many observers of the Trump administration's erratic trade policy. The decision to slap a 35% tariff on Canadian imports, starting August 1, has sent stocks tumbling and raised eyebrows across the globe. Trump's suggestion of possibly escalating his current 10% across-the-board tariffs on many countries to 15% or 20% adds further uncertainty to an already volatile situation.

A Cycle of Chaos and Contradiction

Trump's trade policy has been a rollercoaster of reversals and contradictions. Initially, he exempted all Canadian imports covered by the USMCA trade deal — a pact that was essentially the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP) in new clothing, covering countries under the earlier NAFTA agreement — which Trump had scrapped upon taking office. This latest round of tariffs not only undermines the spirit of these agreements but also highlights a fundamental misunderstanding of trade dynamics.

The Mirage of Trade Deficits

The editorial board rightly points out the fallacy in Trump's fixation on trade deficits. In 2022, the U.S. actually had a surplus with Canada when excluding $141 billion in crude oil imports. Trump's tariff policy threatens to disrupt this balance, potentially raising costs for U.S. consumers and driving dollars to the Middle East instead of maintaining them within North American trade flows. It's a classic case of cutting off one's nose to spite their face, driven by a misguided perception of economic leverage.

Economic Strength or Global Uncertainty?

The board concludes, with a tone of weary resignation, that Trump sees his unpredictability as a negotiating tool. However, this approach confuses capriciousness with strategy. The real casualties of this unpredictability are not just trading partners but also investors and U.S. companies reliant on global supply chains. Far from being a recipe for economic strength, Trump's tariffs threaten to undermine the very foundations of international trade stability.

Conclusion: A Stark Warning

The Wall Street Journal's editorial is more than just a critique of a single policy decision; it's a dire warning about the consequences of navigating global economics with a bluster-over-substance approach. As the board eloquently argues, Trump's tariff schemes are not just making the world economy worse under the guise of shrewd negotiation; they're also sowing a harvest of chaos that will ultimately be reaped by American consumers and workers.

In the grand tapestry of Trump's presidency, these latest tariffs are yet another thread of discord in an already tangled web of policy. The question that remains is not just how this will affect the global economy, but how long the world is willing to endure the capricious whims of the Tariff Man.
