Witch Hunts and ICE Raids: The DOJ’s War on Dissent Hits Home

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 23, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Digital Paul RevereThe MAGA Mob Demands BloodICE: The Machine That Devours the ConstitutionConstitution for Me, Not for TheeA Chilling PrecedentWhy ICE Must Be AbolishedResistance Isn’t Optional—It’s Necessary

In an era where authoritarianism dresses in bureaucracy and retribution comes wrapped in “ethics reviews,” the Department of Justice has crossed a new line. A federal employee was recently fired—not for what she did, not for leaking secrets or failing her duties—but for the simple fact that she’s married to a man who dared to challenge one of the government’s most unaccountable and fear-driven agencies: ICE.

Carolyn Feinstein, a forensic accountant at the DOJ in Austin, Texas, was abruptly terminated after an internal investigation found no misconduct, no security breach, no ethical lapse—only a marriage to Joshua Aaron, the creator of ICEBlock, a community-powered app that helps people track the movements of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents.

This isn’t just bureaucratic overreach. It’s targeted retaliation—a state-sponsored purge of anyone even adjacent to dissent. And it should terrify anyone who still believes in the Bill of Rights.

The Digital Paul Revere

Let’s be clear about what ICEBlock does. It’s a mobile app, now downloaded more than a million times, that allows users to report and receive notifications about ICE activity in their area. That’s it. No hacking, no access to restricted databases, no classified information. Just civilians sharing public observations in real time—like a digital neighborhood watch, but for immigration enforcement.

ICEBlock doesn’t just help undocumented immigrants avoid raids. It helps entire communities—citizens, legal residents, neighbors—know when armed, militarized agents are prowling their streets. In many of these communities, ICE agents are indistinguishable from a SWAT team: armored, armed to the teeth, and empowered to detain on suspicion.

In a country with any moral compass left, ICEBlock would be seen for what it is: a civic tool, a pushback against secretive policing, a line of defense against the unchecked terror ICE continues to inflict on marginalized communities.

Instead, it triggered a political vendetta.

The MAGA Mob Demands Blood

Shortly after Joshua Aaron appeared on CNN to talk about ICEBlock, the right-wing outrage machine revved up. Far-right figures like Laura Loomer, Tom Homan, and Pam Bondi launched coordinated attacks, calling for both Joshua and anyone connected to him to be investigated, prosecuted, or purged.

And they got what they wanted.

Under mounting pressure, the DOJ opened an ethics review into Feinstein—again, not for anything she did, but because of who she married. According to the DOJ, Feinstein had a “sizeable interest” in her husband’s company, All U Chart Inc., which holds the IP for ICEBlock. That “interest” consisted of a technical role on paper: as a precautionary legal move, Aaron had made his wife an officer so she could shut the app down in his absence, should anything happen to him.

Let that sink in: her potential ability to shut down an app was used as evidence of a conflict of interest worthy of firing her from a job she’d held with no blemishes.

The message was crystal clear: if you marry dissent, you inherit the punishment.

ICE: The Machine That Devours the Constitution

The bigger story here, the one hiding in plain sight, is just how far the United States government will go to shield ICE from criticism. ICE is a rogue agency born from post-9/11 paranoia, bloated with budget, shielded from oversight, and addicted to cruelty. From separating children at the border to building secret databases and deploying facial recognition in public spaces, ICE has become a dystopian parody of immigration enforcement.

ICE doesn’t keep America safe. It keeps America afraid.

So when a grassroots app like ICEBlock dares to pull back the curtain—when it exposes the movements of agents behaving like a domestic military force—it threatens not national security, but the illusion of necessity that ICE relies on. And in response, the government lashes out—not at the app’s creator directly, but at his wife.

Because it’s easier to make an example out of a bureaucrat than defend why ICE should remain unaccountable.

Constitution for Me, Not for Thee

Feinstein’s firing isn’t just cruel. It’s unconstitutional.

The First Amendment guarantees freedom of speech, press, and association. It does not say, “unless your husband made the government mad.” Legal experts, including those interviewed by Wired and The Daily Beast, confirm that ICEBlock’s function is protected speech. It reports public events, relies on user submissions, and even explicitly warns users not to interfere with ICE operations or incite violence.

If Feinstein had, in any way, assisted in the app’s creation or maintenance, the DOJ might have had a leg to stand on. But she didn’t. She didn’t write code. She didn’t push updates. She didn’t leak information. She didn’t even publicly endorse the app.

Her “crime” was proximity. Her husband exercised his right to protest tyranny, and she got caught in the blast radius.

This isn’t justice. This is scorched-earth governance.

A Chilling Precedent

Imagine if this precedent were applied broadly.

  • A teacher fired because her spouse organized a Black Lives Matter protest.

  • A postal worker terminated because their partner runs a local police watchdog group.

  • A federal scientist let go because their husband writes environmental exposés.

Where does it end?

Feinstein’s case sets a dangerous standard. It says that federal employment is conditional—not just on your conduct, but on your relationships. That speaking out, even by proxy, can cost you your livelihood.

This isn’t just about Feinstein. This is about whether anyone is safe from the retribution of a government increasingly intolerant of dissent.

Why ICE Must Be Abolished

Let’s not mince words: this would not have happened if ICE didn’t exist.

ICE is the root of this rot. An agency built on fear, operating in shadows, now flexing enough power to get someone fired just for being married to a critic. This is the behavior of secret police, not a democracy.

ICE must be abolished, not reformed. Not renamed. Not "retrained."
Abolished.

Because the longer it exists, the more it corrupts everything around it—law, policy, institutions, and now, marriages.

Resistance Isn’t Optional—It’s Necessary

Joshua Aaron built a tool that helps people avoid being dragged out of their homes in the dead of night by ICE agents with assault rifles. His wife lost her job because of it.

The government has shown its hand: it is not just targeting immigrants, not just activists—but anyone adjacent to resistance.

That’s why we must fight back. Not just to support Feinstein, not just to defend ICEBlock, but to protect our right to dissent.

Because if we stay silent, we’re next.
