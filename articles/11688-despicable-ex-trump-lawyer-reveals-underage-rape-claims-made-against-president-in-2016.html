'Despicable': Ex-Trump lawyer reveals underage rape claims made against president in 2016

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unsettling Dance Between Power and Silence

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks with reporters, as he departs for travel to Pennsylvania from the South Lawn at the White House in Washington, D.C. U.S., July 15, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

President Donald Trump's former fixer Michael Cohen revealed in a new interview that he handled a rape complaint against him and the late financier Jeffrey Epstein just weeks before the 2016 election.

Cohen mentioned the Jane Doe complaint almost in passing during an interview with journalist Tara Palmieri, who wrote on her "Red Letter" website that the details revealed by the former attorney resembled allegations made at that time by a woman known as Katie Johnson.

"She was a Jane Doe who accused President Trump in three different lawsuits of raping her in Jeffrey Epstein’s Manhattan townhouse in 1994, when she was a 13-year-old aspiring model," Palmieri wrote. "She was planning to hold a press conference in Los Angeles days before the 2016 election, but instead, she dropped her suit and vanished. Her attorney Lisa Bloom cited death threats. Her lead lawyer, Thomas Meagher, filed a one-page dismissal in Manhattan federal court."

"For years, I’ve wondered why," the journalist added.

Palmieri has been thinking about that accuser as the Epstein case has been foregrounded by the Justice Department's announcement that no new details would be released, and she decided to speak to Cohen after he seemingly cleared Trump's involvement in the disgraced financier's sex trafficking network.

"If anyone knew what happened to Katie Johnson’s case, it would be Cohen," Palmieri wrote. "He was in Trump’s orbit in September 2016 when her final suit was filed in the Southern District of New York. He was simultaneously arranging payouts for Stormy Daniels and Karen McDougal. This was exactly the kind of mess Trump’s fixer would 'fix.'"

Cohen insisted he had never met Epstein, who'd had a falling out with Trump a year or so before Cohen joined the Trump Organization, and he also said he had never spoken to co-conspirator Ghislaine Maxwell – although he said lawmakers asked him about the pair during his February 2019 testimony about Russia collusion.

“Do you really think that of all of these members of Congress that asked me questions for a total of 63 hours, do you think they didn't bring up this sort of stuff with Jeffrey Epstein?” Cohen said. "And I turn around and I say the same thing. I have no knowledge of anything with Jeffrey Epstein. Zero, I didn't handle it.”

But he did bring up work he had done for Trump on one Epstein-related case, which Palmieri said had a similar timeline and details from Katie Johnson's rape complaint, which was filed right before the election and then withdrawn on November 4, 2016.

"As far as the only case that I was involved with was a Jane Doe, an infant, by and through her mom, Mary Jane Doe, right, as legal guardian," Cohen said, "and the allegations in it are awful. They're despicable. It talks about, basically, rape of an underage female, claiming and alleging that Donald was involved in it and all that other nonsense."

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Dance Between Power and Silence

In an era where the truth is often stranger than fiction, the revelations made by Michael Cohen, President Donald Trump's former fixer, to journalist Tara Palmieri are both harrowing and alarmingly indicative of the murky depths of political powerplay. The discussion centers around a rape complaint involving Trump and the late financier Jeffrey Epstein, dredged up from the shadows just weeks before the 2016 election. This isn't just a story—it's a stark exposé on the intersections of power, intimidation, and the chilling effects they have on justice.

The Allegations: A Dark Nexus

At the heart of this narrative is a complaint by a Jane Doe, later identified as Katie Johnson, who accused Trump of raping her in Epstein's Manhattan townhouse in 1994 when she was merely a 13-year-old aspiring model. This accusation, buried under layers of power and threat, resurfaced momentarily before the 2016 election, only to be smothered into silence as the accuser vanished, citing death threats, and her lawsuit was abruptly dismissed. The mere fact that such serious allegations could be so effectively muted speaks volumes about the systemic rot within our corridors of power.

Cohen's Convenient Amnesia

Michael Cohen's role—or, as he claims, lack thereof—in this sordid affair is particularly illuminating. Despite his proximity to Trump during the relevant period and his known involvement in silencing other inconvenient stories for Trump, Cohen asserts a baffling ignorance regarding the Epstein-related cases. His claim of having "no knowledge" of anything related to Epstein sounds less like a genuine denial and more like a carefully crafted shield against culpability. This is the man who was once Trump’s go-to for making unpleasant legal and public relations messes disappear, yet we are to believe he remained blissfully unaware of accusations linking Trump to Epstein's notorious activities?

The Systemic Failure

What is profoundly disturbing about Cohen's revelations and the subsequent handling (or mishandling) of the Jane Doe complaint is the glaring systemic failure it represents. It's a textbook example of how wealth and status can not only intimidate victims into silence but also seemingly erase their grievances from public and legal scrutiny. The case's dismissal and the accuser's disappearance, driven by fear of retribution, underscore a grotesque reality: in the face of power, even the most serious allegations can be suffocated into nonexistence.

A Reflection on Journalism and Justice

Tara Palmieri's pursuit of the truth, juxtaposed with the broader media's and justice system's handling of such allegations, raises critical questions about accountability in the age of power politics. The very fact that Palmieri has to piece together these revelations years after the fact is a damning commentary on the collective failure to hold influential figures accountable. It's a stark reminder that in the battle for truth and justice, journalism often stands as the last line of defense against the tyranny of power.

In Conclusion

The narrative woven by Cohen's revelations and the subsequent analysis isn't just a tale of political scandal; it's a grim reminder of the pervasive influence of power in silencing voices. The allegations against Trump, entwined with Epstein's notorious legacy, should have been a clarion call for thorough investigation and justice. Instead, they've become yet another footnote in the annals of "alleged incidents" that somehow never see the light of day. This isn't just a failure of the individuals involved; it's a systemic error that speaks to the heart of our society's deepest flaws.
