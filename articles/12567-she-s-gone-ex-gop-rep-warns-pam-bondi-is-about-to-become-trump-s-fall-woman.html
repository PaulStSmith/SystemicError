'She's gone': Ex-GOP rep warns Pam Bondi is about to become Trump's 'fall woman'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJuly 20, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Epstein Controversy: A Tangled Web of Denial, Delay, and Desperation

U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi testifies before a Senate Appropriations Subcommittee hearing on U.S. President Donald Trump’s budget request for the Department of Justice, on Capitol Hill in Washington, D.C., U.S., June 25, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Attorney General Pam Bondi could be on the chopping block as President Donald Trump and his inner circle scramble for any way to contain the fallout of their base's revolt over their delay and obfuscation in turning over the Jeffrey Epstein case files — but it won't be enough, former Rep. Denver Riggleman (R-VA) told MSNBC on Friday evening.

"Here's the thing. You know what they say, far-right conspiracy theorists: nothing to hide, nothing to fear," said Riggleman, a frequent critic of the MAGA movement. "So right now my question is, why don't they — why didn't Congress, just like, you know, Comer did with Hunter Biden, why wouldn't they want to call [Epstein associate Ghislaine] Maxwell to actually talk about the 50th birthday? Why wouldn't Congress want to have her to come there? I mean, you want the facts out, right, Mike? That's what we want to do, right?"

"The other thing, too, is that — and it's something that's been bothering me for a long time on this — how in the heck, right, do you blame other people for creating the own conspiracy theory yourself?" said Riggleman, referencing Trump's more recent claims that the whole scandal was fabricated by Democrats. "And when you're looking at the base, what they're going to see with the grand jury reports, right? We're going to see if anything is redacted. If they don't see all the evidence, they're going to spiral. And a lot of people have seen the far right trying to message out of this."

As far as Bondi goes, Riggleman added, "It's the beginning of the end. She's gone soon. She's not going to last much longer in this administration. I think they're going to have to have a fall woman. I think it's going to be Pam Bondi."

Getting rid of Bondi, however, won't solve the problem, he continued.

"It's going to be very difficult, right, for the Republicans and the MAGA base to message out of this," said Riggleman. "And I think the Democrats can hit on this for years. I just don't see any way out for them, regardless of what they release. As long as they waited to release this, this fever, the conspiratorial fever rises and rises and rises and rises and rises and rises until crazy starts to eat its own tail. And I've told people for a long time, crazy has so much more energy than sanity. And if crazy starts to turn that ship towards you, you better watch out."

"Even if it's Donald Trump, if it looks like that he is protecting a pedophile and it looks like he is the one that QAnon warned about, there could be huge issues," he said.

The Epstein Controversy: A Tangled Web of Denial, Delay, and Desperation

In a recent MSNBC interview, former Representative Denver Riggleman (R-VA) offered a scathing critique of the Trump administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein case files, predicting dire political consequences for Attorney General Pam Bondi and the wider Republican establishment. Riggleman's analysis, peppered with a clear disdain for the MAGA movement's conspiracy theories, cuts to the heart of a scandal that refuses to be silenced by obfuscation and delay.

Pam Bondi: A Sacrificial Lamb in a Losing Battle

The suggestion that Pam Bondi could be on her way out of the Trump administration is not just an isolated personnel prediction; it is emblematic of a broader strategy of scapegoating to deflect accountability. Riggleman's assertion that Bondi might soon be cast as the "fall woman" is a telling indication of a desperate scramble to contain a scandal that has spiraled out of control. Yet, as Riggleman rightly points out, removing Bondi from the equation will hardly address the root of the issue. It is a superficial band-aid on a festering wound, a move that underestimates the intelligence and engagement of the public.

The Inevitability of Truth: Confronting the Conspiracy

Riggleman's challenge to the administration and his former colleagues in Congress to bring Ghislaine Maxwell before a congressional hearing is a pointed critique of their reluctance to confront uncomfortable truths. His rhetorical question, "You want the facts out, right?" underscores a fundamental principle of justice and accountability that seems to have been lost in the political maneuvering surrounding this case. The unwillingness to pursue a transparent investigation into the Epstein scandal not only feeds into the conspiratorial frenzy but also raises serious questions about complicity and cover-up.

A Self-Inflicted Conspiracy: The Irony of Trump's Claims

Perhaps most striking is Riggleman's commentary on the irony of Trump's claims that the scandal is a fabrication by Democrats. This assertion is a masterclass in deflective politics, turning the lens of conspiracy back onto its creators. However, as Riggleman astutely observes, this tactic only serves to exacerbate the problem, fueling the very "conspiratorial fever" the administration seeks to quell. In a landscape where the far right has proven adept at messaging, even the most convoluted narratives find fertile ground.

The Futility of Denial: A Warning to the MAGA Base

Riggleman's warning that "crazy has so much more energy than sanity" is a chilling reminder of the power of unfounded conspiracy theories to shape political discourse. His prediction of the MAGA base turning against Trump if it appears he is "protecting a pedophile" is a stark illustration of the precariousness of political loyalty built on a foundation of misinformation and hyperbole. The Epstein case, with its sordid details and high-profile connections, presents a litmus test for the integrity of political leaders and the resilience of truth in the face of relentless attempts to distort it.

Conclusion: A Systemic Failure of Accountability

In the final analysis, the Epstein scandal and the Trump administration's response to it reveal a systemic failure of accountability and transparency in government. The reluctance to confront the facts, the scapegoating of individuals like Pam Bondi, and the reliance on conspiracy theories as a defensive strategy are symptomatic of a deeper malaise. Denver Riggleman's critique is not just an indictment of a particular administration or political movement; it is a wake-up call to all who value justice and democracy.
