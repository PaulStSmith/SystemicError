'My dad's not a quitter!' RFK Jr's job hangs by a thread as Trump eyes replacement

Based on an article by Tom Boggioni

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump Is Replacing One Health Grifter With Another

Power Is Moving Up, Not Out

The reporting says Trump has lost patience with Robert F. Kennedy Jr. because Kennedy is not “doing his job” on vaccines and autism, and that Dr. Mehmet Oz is already being groomed as the next occupant of HHS. That is the whole story in miniature: the president wants a secretary who will carry out his health politics, and when one loyalist becomes inconvenient, he gets swapped for another.

Kennedy Was Never the Point

Kennedy is being treated as a problem only because he is failing at the specific assignment Trump appears to have given him. The issue is not “infighting” at HHS in the abstract. The issue is that a Cabinet secretary was brought in as a vessel for anti-vaccine politics and is now under pressure to produce the desired propaganda fast enough for the White House.

That is not management failure in the neutral, technocratic sense. It is political damage control around an administration that wants the institutions of public health bent toward a conclusion it has already chosen.

Oz Is the Compliance Candidate

Oz is not being described as a policy independent. He is described as someone Trump calls and texts on health matters that have nothing to do with Oz’s agency, someone who “takes responsibility for ensuring the president gets what he wants.” That is the job description, stripped of the fake dignity.

The White House is apparently drawn to Oz because he is useful: a TV-ready surrogate, a congressional performer, a man who can translate presidential whim into bureaucratic action without pausing to ask whether any of it serves the public. His value is obedience with a medical aesthetic.

The Misdirection Is the Point

The story is already being packaged as a personnel drama, with rumors, reassignment, social-media chaos, and a Cabinet shuffle maybe after the midterms. That framing softens the reality. The central fact is not who gets seated in the HHS chair next. The central fact is that the president is pressuring health agencies to chase a predetermined narrative about vaccines and autism.

That is how institutional harm gets laundered. The public is invited to watch factional theater while the machinery keeps running underneath it. By the time the press turns the issue into a succession contest, the original abuse of power has already been normalized as “department management.”

A Cabinet Built for Obedience

The White House already launched a “top-to-bottom review” of Kennedy’s department, reassigned top leaders, and restructured his advisory team. That is not oversight. It is capture. The chain of command is being tightened so the secretary is not accountable to public health standards, but to presidential preference.

This is what authoritarian governance looks like in a suit and tie: not a dictator shouting from a balcony, but a president measuring whether subordinates are sufficiently useful, sufficiently pliable, sufficiently willing to turn public institutions into personal instruments. Kennedy may be on the way out, but the mechanism that put him there is still intact.

The Pattern Beneath The Shuffle

The deeper pattern is a party-state instinct that treats expertise as decoration and public institutions as props for a political project. Trump does not need health leadership in the democratic sense. He needs enforcers, surrogates, and stage managers who can keep the presidential line moving. Kennedy failed to do that cleanly; Oz is being positioned to do it better.

That is the real political meaning here. The story is not about a messy Cabinet. It is about a White House that sees governance as the management of obedience, and public health as another domain to be bent, if necessary, until it says what power wants to hear.
