'Long shot' upset taunts GOP as senator faces candidate she rejected for lifetime seat

U.S. Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith (R-MS) at the State of the Union address. REUTERS/Kylie Cooper

A Mississippi GOP Senator may be challenged for her seat by a Democrat she once blocked from a federal judge appointment, according to a Semafor story,

Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith is set to face Scott Colom, who was the subject of Hyde-Smith’s 2023 veto as he sought a seat as a federal judge. The irony is that if he won appointment for life, he would likely not be seeking her seat.

Colom is expected to win the Democratic Senate race this week in the state’s primary.

While Colom is an admitted longshot as a progressive Democrat running in a deep-red state, he has done research into a key talking point for the campaign trail.

When his federal bench appointment was denied by Hyde-Smith, who cited his progressive views, Colom “dug into her voting record,” according to Semafor. He plans to cite her failure to bring the state federal funding for infrastructure investments and the domestic chip-building industry. Both would bolster the economy in one of the poorest U.S. states.

“Mississippi has a rich history of electing senators that understand that Mississippi and our needs have to be first. ... Sen. Hyde-Smith is the first one we’ve had in a long time that’s totally betrayed that,” Colom said.

The campaign rhetoric is already heating up. Jake Morissen, Hyde-Smith’s campaign manager, brought up Colom’s prior support of the transgender community. Colom signed a letter five years ago from an organization representing prosecutors and law enforcement to “condemn the ongoing efforts to criminalize transgender people.”

Mississippi has long been a Republican stronghold. The last Democrat to win the state was Sen. John Stennis in 1982. President Donald Trump won the state by 22 points in 2024.

However, things may be changing. In the 2018 senatorial race, former Rep. Mike Espy lost to Hyde-Smith by only seven points. Democrats also came with three points of winning the governor’s race in 2023, and took several state legislative seats in 2025, breaking a GOP supermajority.

Our Analysis:

The Irony of Political Retribution: Colom vs. Hyde-Smith

The political stage is set in Mississippi for what could be described as the ultimate showdown of irony and retribution. Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, a Mississippi GOP stalwart, finds herself potentially challenged for her Senate seat by none other than Scott Colom, a Democrat whose aspirations for a federal judge position she once squashed. This narrative, ripe with political intrigue, unfolds a tale of ambition, betrayal, and the long shadows cast by past actions.

The Background: In a move that can only be described as politically poetic, Colom’s denied federal bench appointment by Hyde-Smith in 2023 has spurred him to challenge her directly in the political arena. Had Hyde-Smith not vetoed his appointment, citing his progressive views as the reason, Colom would likely not be in the position of seeking her seat today. The irony is thick, almost to the point of absurdity.

The Campaign Trail: Colom, despite being an admitted longshot in the deep-red stronghold of Mississippi, has not taken his campaign lightly. He has meticulously “dug into” Hyde-Smith’s voting record, pinpointing her failures in securing federal funding for crucial infrastructure investments and the domestic chip-building industry—areas that would significantly benefit Mississippi, one of the poorest U.S. states. Colom’s critique of Hyde-Smith is not just a political maneuver; it is a factual indictment of her tenure as a senator who has, in his words, “totally betrayed” the needs of Mississippians.

Hyde-Smith’s Counterattack: In response to Colom’s campaign, Hyde-Smith’s camp has resorted to highlighting Colom’s past support of the transgender community, aiming to distract and polarize voters on social issues rather than addressing the substantive economic criticisms levied against her. This tactic, while predictable, is a clear indication of Hyde-Smith’s strategy to deflect from her record and appeal to Mississippi’s conservative base by invoking divisive social issues.

The Democratic Surge: The political landscape in Mississippi is showing signs of change, albeit slowly. With Democrats inching closer to victory in recent elections, including a notably narrow loss by former Rep. Mike Espy to Hyde-Smith in 2018 and significant gains in state legislative seats, the GOP’s grip on the state appears to be loosening. Colom’s challenge to Hyde-Smith, therefore, is not just a personal vendetta—it signifies the broader Democratic effort to reclaim political ground in Mississippi.

The Final Analysis

Colom’s campaign against Hyde-Smith is more than just a political battle; it’s a symbol of the evolving dynamics in Mississippi politics and a testament to the long-lasting impact of political decisions. Hyde-Smith’s decision to block Colom’s judge appointment has inadvertently created her challenger, one that comes armed with a deep understanding of her failures and a personal vendetta to settle.

While Colom faces an uphill battle in the deeply Republican state, his campaign is a glaring spotlight on Hyde-Smith’s record and a reminder that political actions have consequences, often manifesting in ways one least expects. The unfolding drama of Colom vs. Hyde-Smith is not just a political contest; it’s a narrative of irony, ambition, and perhaps, eventual retribution.
