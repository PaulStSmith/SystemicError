'Bamboozled' MAGA tears are drowning the internet as Trump 'chokes'

Based on an article by Adam Lynch

Paulo SantosJune 21, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Trump’s Iran Fiasco Exposed the Right’s Favorite Lie: That Power Can Be Cleaned Up Afterward

Power Was the Point

The source describes a political tantrum inside MAGA after Donald Trump, having unilaterally attacked Iran, then moved toward restitution payments and backed away from disarmament demands. The immediate story is not confusion. It is collapse: commentators who wanted escalation for their own strategic fantasies suddenly discovered that Trump’s version of dominance did not produce the clean victory they imagined.

Who Actually Held the Lever

Trump held the institutional power. Not the podcasters. Not the pundits. Not the men performing outrage from microphones. He made the decision to attack, and he controlled the policy reversal that followed. That matters because much of the reaction in the source tries to present the failure as a betrayal of principle, when it is really the predictable result of concentrating war-making power in one erratic political boss and then outsourcing the moral commentary to his hangers-on.

The Complaint Is Not Principle

John Podhoretz and the other voices quoted here are not objecting to the logic of coercion itself. The source makes that plain. They wanted war because they thought it would help Israel and force the Middle East into a shape they preferred. Their complaint is not that Trump acted aggressively. Their complaint is that he did not carry aggression to the finish line they wanted.

That is the rotten core of this story: the same people who cheer spectacle, punishment, and “exerting our will” suddenly talk like guardians of strategy once the spectacle stops paying off.

Corruption as a Convenient Discovery

The source also shows how corruption becomes a moral issue only when it stops being useful. The commentary ecosystem allegedly laughed off bribery and influence-buying when Trump was still delivering the geopolitical outcome they liked. Once he stopped serving that agenda, the same people found their voices and called him corrupt.

That is not ethics. That is a delayed invoice.

The right-wing media class is not exposing corruption here. It is exposing itself as a market for selective outrage, where bribery is tolerable if the policy output flatters the buyer’s ideology.

Misdirection and Scapegoats

The article’s framing is weak in one important way: it treats the meltdown as a surprising split among Trump’s allies, when the real story is how eagerly they enabled the path to war in the first place. Their public grief now tries to relocate blame onto Trump’s personal weakness, his supposed capitulation, or his corruption. Those may be part of the picture, but the deeper failure belongs to the ecosystem that wanted imperial pressure, excused dishonesty, and only objected when the machine produced embarrassment instead of victory.

The weaker actors in this story are not the ones speaking into podcasts. But they are the ones being used as cover while the powerful keep rewriting their own responsibility.

The Pattern

This is what modern political decadence looks like: war sold as strength, corruption tolerated as long as it is useful, and commentators who confuse access with insight until the deal goes bad. The source captures a familiar arrangement. Leaders make dangerous decisions; elite media blesses them; and when the outcome turns ugly, the same people perform moral shock as if they were bystanders.

That is the system. Not a failure of messaging. A coalition of power, grievance, and opportunism that can only recognize its own rot after the money, the war, or the narrative stops working.
