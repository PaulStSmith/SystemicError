House Democrats have launched a new effort to compel Andrew Mountbatten Windsor — formerly known as Prince Andrew before being stripped of his title — to testify before a congressional committee over his past ties with Jeffrey Epstein, BBC reported Saturday.

The disgraced British royal to appear voluntarily before facing a possible subpoena.

“If he wants to clear his name, if he wants to do right by the victims, he will come forward,” said Rep. Suhas Subramanyam (D-VA), speaking with the BBC. “Frankly, Andrew’s name has come up many times from the victims, so he clearly has knowledge of what happened and we just want him to come forward and tell us what he knows.”

Mountbatten Windsor has faced renewed scrutiny over his past relationship with Epstein, who died in 2019 while awaiting trial on sex-trafficking charges and is alleged to have operated a blackmail operation targeting powerful figures. In October, the British royal was booted from royal life after newly published emails revealed that he had maintained contact with Epstein following his initial 2008 arrest on child sex crimes.

A member of the House Oversight Committee, Subramanyam has been among the more prominent lawmakers pushing for transparency around Epstein and his alleged crimes, having pledged back in September that his committee would continue to push for more files related to Epstein to be released, and taunting President Donald Trump – who had a close relationship with Epstein – in the process.

“No matter who it is – American or not – everyone should be looked at,” Subramanyam told BBC.

Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) is another member of the House Oversight Committee – which holds the authority to subpoena individuals – and told the BBC that his committee would be willing to force Mountbatten Windsor to testify should he refuse to speak with lawmakers willingly.

“If Andrew wishes to come to the United States or he’s here, then he’s subject to the jurisdiction of the US Congress, and I would expect him to testify,” Krishnamoorthi told BBC. “Come clean. Come before the US Congress, voluntarily testify. Don’t wait for a subpoena. Come and testify and tell us what you know.”

House Democrats’ Bold Move: The Quest to Compel Andrew Mountbatten Windsor

In a recent development that reads like a high-stakes political thriller, House Democrats have embarked on a crusade to drag Andrew Mountbatten Windsor — the royal formerly known as Prince Andrew, now stripped of his title and shrouded in disgrace — into the glaring spotlight of a congressional committee. Their mission? To dissect his connections with the notorious Jeffrey Epstein. This isn’t just a story; it’s a saga of power, privilege, and the pursuit of accountability.

The Epstein Connection: A Tangled Web

Jeffrey Epstein, whose 2019 death in a jail cell has sparked endless conspiracy theories, remains a figure of immense interest, not just for his heinous crimes but for the elite circle he entangled. Among these is Andrew Mountbatten Windsor, a name that has surfaced repeatedly, like a bad penny, in discussions about Epstein’s sordid network. The BBC’s report on this new congressional effort highlights not just the persistence of Epstein’s shadow but the lengths to which it stretches.

A Royal in the Hot Seat

The idea of compelling a figure like Mountbatten Windsor to testify before a U.S. congressional committee is audacious, to say the least. Rep. Suhas Subramanyam’s (D-VA) call for the disgraced royal to “come forward” and “do right by the victims” isn’t just a quest for transparency; it’s a pointed challenge to the very notion of royal impunity. It’s worth noting that Mountbatten Windsor’s fall from grace, accelerated by revelations of his continued association with Epstein post-2008 arrest, paints a damning picture of indifference to moral and legal accountability.

The Role of the House Oversight Committee

The House Oversight Committee, armed with the power to subpoena, emerges as a central player in this drama. With figures like Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi (D-IL) asserting the committee’s readiness to compel testimony, the stage is set for a potential showdown that straddles the line between legal authority and diplomatic delicacy. The committee’s determination to shine a light on Epstein’s “alleged crimes” — with a special focus on those in his orbit, regardless of nationality — signals a broader commitment to unraveling the full extent of Epstein’s influence.

The Political Undercurrents

This episode is laden with political implications, not least of which is the veiled jab at former President Donald Trump, noted for his past association with Epstein. Rep. Subramanyam’s pledge to push for the release of more files related to Epstein, coupled with his taunting of Trump, underscores the charged atmosphere in which this inquiry unfolds. It’s a reminder that in the corridors of power, the battle lines are often drawn not just around legal or ethical principles but around allegiances and animosities.

Conclusion: A Test of Will and Power

As this saga continues to unfold, several questions loom large. Will Andrew Mountbatten Windsor heed the call to testify, or will he seek refuge in the remnants of his royal status? More importantly, what revelations might his testimony bring? The effort to bring him before Congress is more than a quest for answers; it’s a bold statement on the limits of privilege and the imperatives of justice.

In the end, this isn’t just about the misdeeds of a disgraced royal or the dark legacy of Jeffrey Epstein. It’s about the broader struggle to ensure that no one — no matter how powerful or well-connected — is beyond the reach of accountability.
