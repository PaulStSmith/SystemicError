'Major red flag': Trump's two 'greatest assets' are reportedly 'becoming liabilities'

Paulo SantosApril 25, 20254 min read

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

President Donald Trump has already fumbled two issues that were crucial to his re-election after less than 100 days on the job, according to a report on voter sentiments.

The president's approval rating on the economy and immigration suggest that voters agreed with his campaign pitch but have been alarmed with his actions on both issues, and an Axios analysis found that this disconnect could collapse the fundamental pillars of his political brand.

"Trump's greatest assets — tough on borders, sharp on business — are becoming liabilities," Axios reported. "That's a major red flag for a president whose chaotic style is only tolerated when it delivers results."

Trump's polling on the economy tumbled to his lowest ratings ever, with just 37 percent approval in a Reuters/Ipsos poll and 40 percent approval in a Pew Research Center survey, and a majority of Americans told Gallup pollsters that their own economic situation was worsening.

"Despite a sealed-off border and a wave of high-profile deportations," Axios reported, "Trump is now barely above water on his best issue, according to an average of polls by data journalist G. Elliott Morris."

Trump's immigration approval rating has dropped 10 points in the last week, down to 45 percent in a new YouGov/Economist poll, and he has lost virtually all the support he gained on the issue during Joe Biden's presidency.

Half of respondents in the YouGov poll agreed Trump should return Kilmar Abrego Garcia, an immigrant living legally in Maryland who was mistakenly renditioned to El Salvador, while just 28 percent said he should remain there despite court orders requiring the administration to "facilitate" his return.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Tumbling Approval: A Tale of Broken Promises and Political Missteps

In what can only be described as a spectacular mishandling of key issues, President Donald Trump has managed to alienate the very base that catapulted him to power. With less than 100 days into his tenure, it appears the pillars supporting his political brand are not just cracking; they are crumbling. Two crucial areas where Trump promised to excel—the economy and immigration—have become his Achilles' heel, as per an Axios analysis. This isn't just a stumble; it's a fall face-first into the quicksand of voter disillusionment.

Economic Downfall: A Sharp Decline in Approval

Trump, who prided himself on being the ultimate businessman, has seen his approval ratings on economic management plummet to depths unseen. With a mere 37 percent approval rating in a Reuters/Ipsos poll and a slightly better, yet dismal, 40 percent in a Pew Research Center survey, the message from the American public is loud and clear. They are not buying what Trump is selling anymore. The Gallup pollsters bring another blow, revealing a majority of Americans perceive their economic situation as worsening. This is not just a red flag; it's a blaring siren that Trump's promises of economic prosperity are far from being realized.

Immigration Inefficacy: Losing Ground Fast

On the immigration front, Trump's performance is equally disheartening for his supporters. A YouGov/Economist poll indicates a 10-point drop in approval, with Trump's immigration policies now barely treading water. This stark decline is noteworthy, especially considering the brief surge in support he enjoyed during Joe Biden's presidency. The case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a legal immigrant mistakenly deported to El Salvador, serves as a poignant example of the administration's haphazard approach to immigration. The public's preference for Garcia's return, despite the administration's reluctance, underscores a significant disconnect between Trump's policies and voter expectations.

The Crux of the Matter: A Brand in Jeopardy

Axios hits the nail on the head: Trump's greatest assets—his tough stance on borders and his business acumen—are morphing into his greatest liabilities. This isn't just about numbers dwindling on a chart; it's about a fundamental erosion of trust in what Trump stands for. His chaotic style, once tolerated, perhaps even admired by some for its departure from the norm, is now seen as a liability rather than a strength. When chaos fails to deliver results, it loses its allure.

The overarching narrative here is not just about a president failing to meet expectations; it's about a brand—the Trump brand—facing an existential crisis. If the bedrock of his political identity continues to fracture at this rate, Trump's journey to re-election will be fraught with insurmountable challenges. Voters agreed with his pitch, but the reality of his policies has left them more than just disappointed. They feel betrayed.

In summary, Trump's tenure, marked by these critical blunders in handling the economy and immigration, paints a picture of a leader out of sync with the nation's heartbeat. The fallout from these missteps is not merely statistical; it's a telling sign of a presidency struggling to uphold the very promises that defined its inception. The question now is not if but how Trump plans to navigate this tumultuous sea of disapproval. Can he recalibrate and regain the trust of the American public, or is it too late? Only time will tell, but as things stand, the forecast is anything but sunny.
