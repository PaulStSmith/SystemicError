Far-right frenzy as conservative's damning exposé ties Nazi-aligned network to White House

The Authoritarian Right Isn’t Infiltrating Power. It Is Being Invited In.

A Warning From Inside the House

Erick Erickson’s account describes a funded network advancing white nationalism, antisemitism, patriarchal rule, and dictatorship through conservative institutions. Its operatives are not merely posting from the political fringe. According to Erickson, they are entering think tanks, church organizations, governing pipelines, and the vice president’s orbit.

That distinction matters. This is not principally a story about extremists having ugly beliefs. It is about institutions with money, access, and personnel systems giving those beliefs somewhere to operate.

Follow the Money—and the Jobs

Charles Haywood reportedly turned a shampoo fortune into political leverage. His foundation has given $390,500 to the Claremont Institute since 2022, while also funding American Moment, a pipeline designed to place young conservatives in administration posts.

The institutional power here belongs to donors, think-tank executives, political patrons, and officials who can convert ideology into staffing decisions. A manifesto is rhetoric. A funded organization placing loyalists inside government is infrastructure.

Calling this “influence” risks understating the transaction. Money supports organizations; organizations cultivate personnel; personnel enter government; government exercises coercive power. That is how a private authoritarian vision acquires public machinery.

Vance’s Proximity Is a Choice

Erickson’s sharpest allegation is not that Vice President JD Vance secretly shares every belief in this network. It is that Vance keeps its participants and products within reach.

The evidence cited is concrete: Vance blurbed Unhumans, which Erickson says presents Francisco Franco and Augusto Pinochet as models for confronting the left; American Moment was reportedly founded with Vance’s support; and Vance has declined, in Erickson’s view, to forcefully repudiate antisemites associated with Nick Fuentes. Rod Dreher further claims Vance ended their friendship after Dreher pressed him to draw that boundary.

These facts do not establish Vance’s private convictions. They do establish repeated political choices about affiliation, endorsement, and distance. Refusing to draw a line is not the absence of a decision. For a vice president, it is a decision with institutional consequences.

The Fringe Has Powerful Gatekeepers

The phrase “a group of white guys” gave the accused movement’s defenders a convenient diversion. Rather than address the money, organizations, staffing networks, or praise for dictators, figures tied to white-nationalist politics answered with racial triumphalism, mockery of Erickson’s weight, and complaints that he attacks the wrong faction.

That response is revealing because it substitutes grievance for rebuttal. The serious charge is not that white men gathered together. It is that specific men allegedly used wealth and organizational access to normalize political exclusion and authoritarian government. Pretending the accusation concerns their race allows them to evade the conduct.

But “infiltration” can also misdirect responsibility. It suggests unsuspecting institutions were quietly breached by outsiders. Erickson’s own evidence points toward something less comforting: established conservative organizations accepted funding, board members, endorsements, and personnel connections. Gatekeepers opened those gates.

Churches and Think Tanks Are Political Machinery

The reported targeting of churches is not incidental. Religious communities offer moral authority, trusted social networks, and access to families. Think tanks offer policy language and elite legitimacy. Staffing organizations provide the final conversion mechanism: turning doctrine into appointments.

If Erickson’s characterization is accurate, the project is not simply electoral. It is an attempt to shape what conservatives regard as morally permissible before those ideas appear in official policy. Restrictions on women’s autonomy, racial nationalism, and admiration for dictatorship become easier to implement once institutions have repackaged them as tradition, order, or resistance to the left.

That is propaganda’s institutional form: not a single lie repeated loudly, but a network that decides which cruelties become respectable.

Authoritarianism Advances Through Accommodation

The larger pattern is not that extremists occasionally wander too close to power. It is that powerful actors repeatedly discover uses for them. Wealthy patrons supply resources. Institutions supply legitimacy. Political pipelines supply personnel. Senior officials supply proximity and strategic ambiguity.

The resulting danger does not depend on proving a secret conspiracy or reading anyone’s mind. It lies in the visible structure Erickson describes: anti-democratic ideas receiving money, access, and career paths while those capable of excluding them decline to do so.

Authoritarian politics rarely arrives labeled as authoritarian politics. It advances through sponsorship, appointments, euphemism, and the calculated refusal to say who is unacceptable. By the time the institutions admit what they enabled, the people they welcomed are no longer outside the gates.
