Explosive leak reveals shocking new use of AI in Trump's Pentagon: 'Monumental'

U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth attends a cabinet meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder TPX IMAGES OF THE DAY

The Department of Defense has launched a new generative artificial intelligence tool that will help the agency identify and engage military targets, according to a DOD source and an explosive leak reported on by journalist Jessica Burbank and Drop Site News.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the new AI tool – dubbed “Gemini” – in a Tuesday memo obtained by Burbank, informing all DOD personnel that they are expected to incorporate the technology into their “workflows immediately.” Hegseth wrote that Gemini would help the agency “out-think, out-decide, and out-pace any adversary.

According to a source within the DOD who spoke with Burbank on the condition of anonymity, the rollout of Gemini also included the distribution of a “Do and Don’t list.”

“[One] example of don’t was, don’t use GenAI for decisions involving attribution, targeting, or threat evaluation without human validation; I read that as someone can read what the AI said and be like ‘yep it’s good to go shoot that missile,’” the source told Burbank, who published the report on her Substack, “Ka’s Counterprogramming.” “They are legit going full force into AI.”

Hegseth would officially announce the technology Tuesday morning in a social media post on X, though the details – including its use for helping to identify military targets – were not revealed until Burbank and Drop Site News’ reporting later Tuesday morning. Instead, Hegseth highlighted the technology’s ability to “conduct deep research, format documents and even analyze video or imagery at unprecedented speed.”

“Victory belongs to those who embrace real innovation,” wrote Hegseth in the internal memo, issued to all DOD personnel.

“Rather than being reliant on the dusty, antiquated systems of a bygone era, we are thinking ahead here in the Department of War. [Gemini] is part of this monumental transformation. It removes wasted time and focuses more of our energy into decisive results for the warfighter.”

Our Analysis:

The Dubious Dawn of “Gemini”: A Critical Analysis

The Department of Defense (DOD) has unveiled a new generative artificial intelligence tool, “Gemini,” with the lofty ambition of revolutionizing military target identification and engagement. This announcement, as reported by journalist Jessica Burbank and Drop Site News, reveals a concerning pivot towards an AI-dependent military strategy under the guise of innovation and efficiency. The internal memo from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, coupled with the clandestine whispers of a DOD insider, paints a picture of a future fraught with ethical quandaries and potential missteps in the march towards AI militarization.

The Premise of “Gemini”

Hegseth’s memo to DOD personnel heralds Gemini as the next leap forward in military strategy, a tool designed to “out-think, out-decide, and out-pace any adversary.” This rhetoric, awash with the optimism of technological triumphalism, masks the deeper implications of entrusting AI with the power to shape military decision-making.

One cannot help but question the prudence of such a wholesale embrace of AI in warfare. The DOD insider’s comments to Burbank unveil a “Do and Don’t list” accompanying Gemini’s rollout, which notably includes a caution against using the AI for critical decisions involving “attribution, targeting, or threat evaluation without human validation.” This caveat starkly contradicts the memo’s promise, suggesting a reality where Gemini’s recommendations require little more than a perfunctory human nod to precipitate potentially lethal actions.

The Illusion of Control

The narrative spun by Hegseth and the DOD’s leadership is one of control and precision. Yet, the underlying message conveyed through the insider’s revelations is one of recklessness. The notion that a mere “yep, it’s good to go shoot that missile” could suffice as oversight in AI-facilitated military operations is not just alarming; it’s a glaring red flag. It underscores a disturbing readiness to sideline critical ethical considerations in favor of expediency.

Technological Hubris

Hegseth’s assertion in the memo that victory belongs to the innovators, while dismissing “the dusty, antiquated systems of a bygone era,” smacks of technological hubris. This mindset, which prioritizes innovation at the cost of potentially overlooking the nuanced, unpredictable nature of human conflict, could lead to dire consequences. The emphasis on “decisive results for the warfighter” neglects the inherent unpredictability of war, where the fog of conflict often renders the most sophisticated technologies fallible.

Conclusion: A Call for Caution and Ethics

The introduction of Gemini is a testament to the DOD’s ambition to lead in the AI arms race. However, the eagerness to deploy such technology without a robust, transparent framework for ethical oversight and human accountability is a recipe for disaster. The reliance on AI for critical aspects of warfare, as depicted in the reporting by Burbank and Drop Site News, should not be celebrated without scrutinizing the potential for catastrophic errors and ethical breaches.

Innovation in warfare, while necessary, should not outpace our capacity for ethical consideration and control. The DOD’s venture into AI-assisted military operations with Gemini demands a cautious approach, grounded in the principles of human rights and international law. Without this, the promise of technological advancement rings hollow, overshadowed by the specter of unchecked, algorithm-driven warfare.
