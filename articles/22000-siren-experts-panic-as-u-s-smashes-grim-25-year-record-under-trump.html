'Siren!' Experts panic as U.S. smashes grim 25-year record under Trump

The Price at the Pump Is a War Receipt

The Fact Pattern

Gasoline has stayed above $4 a gallon this deep into the year, with AAA putting the national average at $4.03 on Wednesday. That is the part of the story the market obsessives can count. The political part is harder to dodge: prices are elevated amid Trump’s war in Iran, and they have remained stubborn because the conflict has not been resolved.

Who Owns This

The people who actually hold power here are not drivers, nor analysts, nor the officials offering solemn commentary about “pressure” and “uncertainty.” Power sits with the Trump administration, which chose the war footing that has kept energy markets pinned. The bill is being paid by everyone who buys fuel, but the decision was made at the top.

That matters because this is not a weather event or a mysterious malfunction of the invisible hand. The source text describes a military and political choice with economic consequences. That choice belongs to the White House, not to the commuter filling a tank.

The Useful Distraction

The easier story is the one about a “dubious milestone,” as if the calendar itself were the culprit. That framing softens responsibility. It turns policy into atmosphere and administration into bystander. It invites readers to stare at the price board instead of asking who forced the market into this position.

Even the expert quotes, while useful, mostly describe the symptoms. Jim Bianco warns that prices could stay high. Larry Sabato flashes a warning label. Fine. But the underlying mechanism is not subtle: war raises risk, risk raises prices, and the political class then pretends the pain arrived from nowhere.

What The Story Reveals

This is what governing by crisis looks like in practice. The administration gets to project strength, then quietly externalizes the cost onto households. The damage is not abstract. It is paid in weekly grocery budgets, commute costs, and the humiliating ritual of watching a basic necessity become a political hostage.

The administration’s branding about a “Golden Age” is not just tacky. It is a tell. When the rhetoric is triumphalist and the outcome is a prolonged fuel squeeze, the gap between image and reality is the point. Propaganda is not only falsehood; it is the effort to make harm look like national destiny.

Democrats Should Not Pretend This Is Normal

The right answer is not to celebrate pain as some cleansing market discipline. It is to name the governing failure plainly and treat energy insecurity as a political consequence of reckless executive power. Democrats do not need to mirror Trump’s fantasy politics to oppose it. They need to show, again and again, that the costs of authoritarian bluster land on ordinary people first.

That means refusing the lazy language that depersonalizes responsibility. A war has political authors. So do the prices it leaves behind.

The Larger Pattern

This story is bigger than gasoline. It is the recurring arrangement where concentrated power creates instability, then tells the public to absorb it as inevitability. The administration chooses confrontation, markets react, and officials hope voters will blame “conditions” instead of leadership.

That is the real record here: not a strange late-summer price milestone, but another demonstration that authoritarian politics privatize the damage and nationalize the excuse.
