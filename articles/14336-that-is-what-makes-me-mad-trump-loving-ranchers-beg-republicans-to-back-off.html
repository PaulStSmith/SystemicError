'That is what makes me mad': Trump-loving ranchers beg Republicans to back off

Former Republican state lawmaker, rancher and a steadfast Donald Trump supporter John Davis says he’s become horrified at his party’s assault upon clean energy, which is exploding in Texas as local oil fields run dry. The Guardian reports a volley of state bills pushed by the legislature’s Republican majority is threatening to reverse the state’s position as a clean energy superpower. Many of these bills, like SB819, call for the “police power of the state” to suppress new wind and solar projects by requiring onerous permits not required for gas, coal or nuclear plants.

“I’ve heard from many constituents with valid concerns regarding the reckless installation of these facilities,” said Lois Kolkhorst, a Texas state senator and a SB819 sponsor who considers herself a “lifelong wildlife conservationist.”

Using Trump’s claims of extensive damage to birds and wildlife, the legislation imposes meticulous “wildlife assessments” around the turbine site, and it requires builders “adapt operations … to minimize facility effects on bats, birds, and other wildlife.”

Davis is dubious of the claims. “See any dead birds here? I’ve never seen one,” he told the Guardian while standing beneath one of seven wind turbines on his property churning out new revenue for his ranch.

“We’re supposed to be the party of common sense but we’ve shifted to be like the Democrats and that is what makes me mad. When you tell me what to do on my own property that sounds more like a Democrat,” Davis added. “Some of the bills they are trying to pass would kill renewables.”

The Texas bills hit just as Trump is tamping down renewable on the federal level, barring major clean energy projects from federal lands and waters. “We’re going to try to have a policy where no windmills are being built,” said Trump.

“It’s not productive or helpful when [Trump] spouts off like that,” said Brandon Corbin, a local Republican county judge who supports a proposed battery facility in the county that could add as much as 30 percent new revenue to Menard County’s overstretched budget. “We are the ones on the ground here and he says and does things that puts fear and uncertainty out.”

Our Analysis:

The Texas GOP's Clean Energy Turmoil: A Deep Dive

In a revelation that sounds almost like a plot twist from a political thriller, The Guardian reports a schism within the Texas Republican party. This isn't your typical political squabble; it's a full-blown intraparty battle that pits traditional energy moguls against the burgeoning clean energy sector. At the heart of this conflict are struggling Texas ranchers and the imposing influence of big oil, a scenario that sounds all too familiar yet increasingly paradoxical in today's energy climate.

Former Republican state lawmaker, rancher, and unabashed Donald Trump supporter, John Davis, finds himself at odds with his party's increasingly hostile stance against clean energy. This is particularly jarring given Texas's status as a clean energy titan, a position now jeopardized by a slew of state bills aimed at curtailing the growth of this sector. The irony? These measures are being pushed under the guise of environmental protection and wildlife conservation, a reasoning that seems as thin as the Texas air in the face of factual scrutiny.

The Dubious Crusade Against Clean Energy

Senate Bill 819 (SB819) emerges as the poster child for this misguided legislative effort. Championed by State Senator Lois Kolkhorst, a self-described "lifelong wildlife conservationist," the bill seeks to impose draconian permits on new wind and solar projects—requirements conspicuously absent for gas, coal, or nuclear plants. Kolkhorst and her ilk wield Trump’s exaggerated claims of wildlife damage like a cudgel against clean energy, mandating exhaustive “wildlife assessments” and operational adjustments purportedly to safeguard bats, birds, and other wildlife. Yet, as Davis astutely points out while surveying his wind turbine-laden property, the alleged avian apocalypse is nowhere to be seen.

The Hypocrisy of Control

Davis's frustration is palpable and justified. His rebuke of the GOP's shift towards regulatory overreach—a behavior traditionally criticized when exhibited by Democrats—highlights a glaring inconsistency in the party's ethos. "We're supposed to be the party of common sense," Davis laments, yet the party's actions betray a bewildering departure from this principle. The proposed legislation is not just an affront to clean energy; it's an attack on the autonomy of property owners and the economic prospects of rural communities.

The Economic and Political Fallout

Beyond the ideological implications, the practical impact of stifling clean energy development in Texas cannot be overstated. With Trump at the federal helm, pushing an anti-renewable agenda that bars major clean energy projects from federal lands and waters, the local pushback against renewables in Texas adds insult to injury. Trump's blunt assertion, "We're going to try to have a policy where no windmills are being built," not only undermines the global shift towards sustainable energy but also threatens the financial lifeline of areas like Menard County.

Brandon Corbin, a Republican county judge, underscores the disconnect between Trump's rhetoric and local realities. The proposed battery facility in his county, potentially boosting its budget by 30 percent, stands as a testament to the economic boon that renewables can offer. Yet, the shadow of fear and uncertainty cast by Trump's declarations looms large, jeopardizing not just environmental progress but economic revitalization in regions that stand to benefit the most.

Conclusion: A Call for Reason

The unfolding drama in Texas serves as a microcosm of the broader ideological battle within the Republican party and the nation at large. It's a cautionary tale of what happens when political allegiances and outdated allegiances to fossil fuels overshadow common sense and the public good. The resistance against clean energy, underpinned by dubious environmental concerns and blatant economic self-interest, not only contravenes the principles of freedom and property rights but also hampers the United States' progress towards a sustainable future.

In this era of systemic error, where the lines between conservation and conservatism are increasingly blurred, the Texas GOP's clean energy turmoil is not just a state issue—it's a national crisis in waiting. The question remains: Will reason prevail, or will the party continue to undermine its own principles, to the detriment of the country and the planet?
