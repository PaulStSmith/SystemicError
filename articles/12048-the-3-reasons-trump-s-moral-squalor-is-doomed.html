The 3 reasons Trump's moral squalor is doomed​

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 5, 20256 min read

People take part in a protest against Donald Trump. REUTERS/Kent Nishimura

It gets bleaker and bleaker. He’s eviscerating environmental protections. He accuses Barack Obama of treason. He’s ripping up labor protections. He wants to privatize Social Security. He fires the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics because he doesn’t like the job numbers. He forces the Smithsonian to take down an exhibit that includes his two impeachments. The European Union, Japan, Columbia University, and CBS are all surrendering to him.

Many of you ask me where I get my hope from, notwithstanding.

Three sources.

First, from all the young people I work with every day. They’re enormously dedicated, committed to making the world better. They’ll inherit this mess, and they’re ready to clean it up and strengthen our democracy. They also have extraordinary energy. And they’re very funny. It is impossible not to be hopeful around them.

Second, from history. We are now in a second Gilded Age that, like the first one (from the late 19th century to the start of the 20th), features wide inequalities of income and wealth, abuses of power by the oligarchs (then called “robber barons”), and a bullied and abused working class.

What happened then? The great pendulum of America swung back. The first Gilded Age was followed by what historians call the Progressive Era. Taxes were raised on the wealthy. Antitrust laws were enacted. Regulations stopped corporate malfeasance. Big money was barred from politics. And reformers — starting with Teddy Roosevelt in 1901 and extending through his fifth cousin, FDR, in 1933 — made life better for average working people.

I don’t know exactly how or when the pendulum will swing back this time, but I am certain it will. And the regressive moral squalor of Trump and his lackeys will be swept into the dustbin of history.

My third source of optimism comes from people I meet all over America, including self-described Republicans in so-called “red” states and “red” cities, who detest what’s happening to the nation and to the world under Donald Trump (as well as under Benjamin Netanyahu and Vladimir Putin).

There’s a profound decency in the sinews of America. Most Americans are generous and kind.

Opinion polls show the vast majority don’t want ICE agents disappearing their neighbors off the streets and into detention camps. They reject Trump prosecuting his so-called enemies. They think it’s wrong for him to pocket billions from crypto and other pay-to-play schemes. They don’t like him or his lackeys verbally attacking federal judges, or silencing critics.

Over 80 percent believe the minimum wage should be raised, that no full-time worker should be in poverty, that corporations should share their profits with their employees, that working people should get paid family leave, and that child care and elder care should be affordable.

I don’t want to minimize the repugnance of Trump and his sycophants. Like you, I wince when I read the news. Some days I despair.

But there are sources of hope all around us. Find them. Cling to them. Never give up.

Our Analysis:

The Bleak Reality and Glimmers of Hope: A Critical Analysis

In an era where the political landscape often seems irreparably polarized, the recent article by Robert Reich, a professor emeritus of public policy at Berkeley and former secretary of labor, stands out as both a stark reminder of current challenges and a beacon of potential hope. Reich's analysis, while grounded in the grim realities of today's political and environmental degradation under the leadership of Donald Trump, also seeks to offer solace and motivation by pinpointing the sources of optimism in these turbulent times.

The Dismantling of Protections and the Assault on Decency

Reich's article paints a vivid picture of a United States under siege by its own leadership. Trump's administration is accused of eviscerating environmental protections, a move that not only threatens the immediate well-being of the planet but also sets a dangerous precedent for future governance. The accusation against Barack Obama of treason, the dismantling of labor protections, and the audacious attempts to privatize Social Security are laid bare as examples of a broader agenda to undermine the foundations of democratic governance and social welfare.

The firing of the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics over unfavorable job numbers and the manipulation of national institutions to erase the president's impeachments from public memory are highlighted as particularly egregious acts of authoritarianism. Moreover, the capitulation of international and national entities, including the European Union and major media outlets, signals a worrying trend of eroding resistance against Trump's tactics.

Sources of Hope amidst Despair

Despite the dire situation, Reich identifies three pillars of hope: the youth, historical precedents, and the inherent decency of the American populace. The dedication and readiness of young people to address and rectify the current crisis offer a glimmer of hope for the future. Their humor and energy are presented as infectious forces that can invigorate the weary and bolster the fight for a better tomorrow.

Drawing parallels with the first Gilded Age, Reich reminds us that history is cyclical and transformative change is possible. The Progressive Era that followed the Gilded Age brought about significant reforms that improved the lives of average working people and curbed the excesses of the wealthy. This historical perspective serves as a powerful reminder that no matter how entrenched the current system may seem, change is within reach.

Perhaps most importantly, Reich underscores the fundamental decency of the American people. Despite the polarization and the barrage of divisive rhetoric, he points to the overwhelming majority who oppose the draconian policies and actions of the Trump administration. This collective sense of morality and justice is a potent force that can drive societal change.

Final Thoughts

While Reich's analysis does not shy away from depicting the bleakness of our current reality, it also refuses to succumb to despair. The article serves as a clarion call to recognize the sources of hope that exist even in the darkest of times. It's a reminder that change is not only necessary but also possible through collective action and the relentless pursuit of justice.

However, it's crucial to remember that hope alone is not sufficient. As much as the youth, historical cycles, and inherent decency are sources of optimism, they must be coupled with concrete actions and political engagement. The battle for the soul of the nation and the future of our planet is ongoing, and complacency is the enemy of progress.

In conclusion, Reich's article is a compelling blend of stark realism and cautious optimism. It challenges us to confront the harsh truths of our time while also holding onto the belief that a better world is possible. As we navigate these tumultuous waters, let's remember that the arc of history bends towards justice, but it requires our hands to guide it.
