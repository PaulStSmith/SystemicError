On MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” co-host Joe Scarborough was unsparing in calling out Johnson, who is notable for citing his Christianity and adherence to the Bible. The host repeatedly called him a “liar.”

After hammering Vice President J.D. Vance for his continuing lies over the past week when he blamed the left for violence, Scarborough went after Johnson for continually stating undocumented immigrants would be handed medical benefits under the Affordable Care Act, known as “Obamacare.

”‘Now it’s happening again,” he began. “This time the lie from the speaker of the House, Mister ‘I ruled by the Bible,’ spreading an unambiguous lie going into this government shutdown. They’re lying to their own supporters. They’re lying to their own constituents. They’re lying to their own followers, saying Democrats want to shut down the government because they want to give great health care benefits to illegal immigrants. “

“It’s a total lie,” he exclaimed. “So I’m wondering where in the Bible does Mike Johnson read Jesus saying, ‘You know what, tell the truth. Except, well, when it might serve you well politically. And then just lie. And why don’t you, while you’re lying, why don’t you spread lies about those who are on the outskirts of society? Those who are suffering right now the most? Why don’t you lie about the weakest among us, Mikey?’”

“I mean that, but that’s what he’s doing — he’s spreading the lie,” he added.

Our Analysis:

The Gospel According to Mike Johnson: A Tale of Hypocrisy and Political Expedience

In the latest episode of Political Theatre, House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-LA) finds himself at the center of a storm, not of divine origin, but of his own making. With the government at a standstill, Johnson’s proclivity for bending the truth has caught the unforgiving eye of MSNBC’s “Morning Joe,” where co-host Joe Scarborough delivers a sermon that spares no rod on the Louisiana representative.

The Sin: Spreading “Unambiguous Lies”

It’s one thing to engage in political spin; it’s an entirely different ballgame when you’re accused of spreading “unambiguous lies.” Scarborough’s accusation isn’t lightweight. It’s a heavyweight punch that lands squarely on Johnson’s jaw, challenging the very foundation of his public persona — that of a man guided by his Christian faith and adherence to the Bible. The lie in question? That Democrats are orchestrating a government shutdown to provide “great health care benefits to illegal immigrants” under the Affordable Care Act, or as it’s colloquially known, “Obamacare.”

The Irony: A Biblical Paradox

What’s striking here isn’t just the lie, but the audacity of its framework. Johnson, who prides himself on ruling by the Bible, seems to have missed a few key verses. Scarborough’s rhetorical question, “Where in the Bible does Mike Johnson read Jesus saying, ‘You know what, tell the truth. Except, well, when it might serve you well politically. And then just lie,’” cuts deep. It exposes a fundamental hypocrisy at play — the willingness to abandon core moral principles in pursuit of political gain.

The Victims: The “Weakest Among Us”

Scarborough’s commentary doesn’t just stop at calling out Johnson’s mendacity; it goes further, highlighting the insidious nature of his lies. By spreading falsehoods about those “on the outskirts of society,” Johnson isn’t merely lying; he’s weaponizing his platform to target the most vulnerable. It’s an act of political malfeasance that seeks to demonize the marginalized for partisan points.

The Verdict: A Call for Accountability

What we witness here is a tale as old as time, yet no less damning. Mike Johnson, in his quest for political dominance, has chosen a path littered with deceit and moral compromise. The question posed by Scarborough isn’t just rhetorical; it’s a clarion call for accountability. It challenges not only Johnson but his constituents and followers to reckon with the reality of their representation.

In a world where political expediency often trumps ethical considerations, Johnson’s actions serve as a cautionary tale. The gospel according to Mike Johnson — one of lies, hypocrisy, and political expedience — stands in stark contrast to the core values he professes to hold. As the government grinds to a halt, one can’t help but wonder: At what point does the cost of political victory outweigh the soul’s price?

In conclusion, the spectacle of Johnson’s duplicity is more than just political fodder; it’s a reflection of a deeper systemic rot that threatens the very fabric of our democratic institutions. As the dust settles, the challenge remains — will there be a reckoning, or will this episode be relegated to a footnote in the annals of political chicanery? Only time will tell, but one thing is clear: The gospel according to Mike Johnson is one sermon that many would rather not hear again.
