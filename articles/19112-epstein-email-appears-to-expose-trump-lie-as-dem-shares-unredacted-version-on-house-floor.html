Epstein email appears to expose Trump lie as Dem shares unredacted version on House floor

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMarch 20, 20265 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An undated photo of Jeffrey Epstein released by the Justice Department. (DOJ)

An unredacted email appears to reveal testimony that contradicts President Donald Trump’s longstanding narrative about Jeffrey Epstein’s relationship to Mar-a-Lago. The 2009 correspondence from Epstein’s attorney Jack Goldberger was substantially redacted in the Justice Department’s initial Epstein files release.

Representative Dan Goldman displayed the complete email on the House floor Wednesday, exposing its contents. The correspondence documents a telephone conference involving Trump, his attorney Alan Garten, and a person presumed to be Brad Edwards, representing Epstein’s victims.

According to Goldberger’s summary, when asked whether Epstein was ever expelled from Mar-a-Lago, Garten stated, “No he was not a member. May have been his guest. Never asked to leave.” A manager at the Florida estate confirmed to Edwards that Epstein was “never asked to leave Mar-a-Lago.”

That directly contradicts repeated claims by Trump and the White House that Epstein had been kicked out of the club.

The email, reported on by The Daily Beast, documents Trump’s responses regarding his relationship with Epstein. When questioned about flying on Epstein’s plane, Trump stated, “I’ve been on a lot of planes. May have been on his plane. No young girls on plane.” Regarding visits to Epstein’s residence, Trump said, “I may have been there with my wife,” adding, “May have been children of guests but that’s it.”

Trump has publicly maintained different positions. He stated he was not “friendly” with Epstein and was “never” on his plane. White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt asserted, “President Trump did nothing wrong and he kicked Jeffrey Epstein out of Mar-a-Lago for being a creep.”

However, the new evidence contradicts these claims. Trump appears thousands of times throughout Epstein files. Photographs document Trump and Epstein socializing together from the 1980s through the 2000s. Flight logs place Trump on Epstein’s aircraft. Membership documents indicate Epstein maintained Mar-a-Lago membership until October 2007, more than one year after his indictment for soliciting prostitution.

Goldman accused Trump of making “false statements over the past quarter century about Jeffrey Epstein.” He criticized Attorney General Pam Bondi’s department for withholding information, stating: “The reason why this matters is because we don’t have half of the Epstein files. We identified 6 million Epstein files for potential release but have only disclosed about 3.5 million.”

Goldman questioned Bondi’s motives, asking, “If the attorney general is covering up this information that she then reveals to Congress, what else is she covering up about Donald Trump’s involvement in the Epstein files?”

The Justice Department and White House did not immediately respond to the Beast’s requests for comment.

Our Analysis:

Unveiling the Shadows: Trump, Epstein, and the Mar-a-Lago Controversy

In the swirling vortex of political scandal and public scrutiny, the recent revelations about Donald Trump’s relationship with Jeffrey Epstein, especially regarding Mar-a-Lago, demand a meticulous dissection. The article in question sheds light on an unredacted email that appears to contradict Trump’s claims about Epstein’s association with his Florida estate. Let’s peel back the layers with precision, focusing on who holds power, who made crucial decisions, and whether the narrative misdirects responsibility.

Institutional Power and Decision-Making

Who Holds Institutional Power?

The central figures in this unfolding drama are Donald Trump, former President and owner of Mar-a-Lago, and the U.S. Justice Department, tasked with the release and redaction of Epstein’s files. Representative Dan Goldman also emerges as a key player, leveraging legislative power to expose information on the House floor.

Decision Makers:

  1. Alan Garten and Mar-a-Lago Management: Statements regarding Epstein’s membership and presence at Mar-a-Lago directly impact Trump’s narrative. Garten, Trump’s attorney, and the estate’s manager made decisions on membership status and disclosures about Epstein’s expulsion—or lack thereof.

  2. Justice Department: The decision to initially redact significant portions of the email implicates the Department in controlling the flow of information. Attorney General Pam Bondi’s actions—or inactions—regarding the release of Epstein files are central.

  3. Donald Trump: His responses to inquiries about his relationship with Epstein and the discrepancies in his public statements position him as a key decision-maker in shaping the narrative.

Misdirection and Responsibility

The article suggests a concerted effort to misdirect responsibility and obscure the truth. The framing, however, doesn’t misplace blame on actors with limited control; rather, it highlights the discrepancies in Trump’s statements versus documented evidence (e.g., flight logs, photographs, and now, unredacted emails).

False Equivalence and Scapegoating:

Rejecting false equivalence means acknowledging that the power dynamics at play are not evenly distributed. Trump, as both a former President and a businessman with significant influence, holds substantial power in this scenario. The Justice Department, under Attorney General Bondi’s leadership, also wields considerable authority over the dissemination of information. The narrative correctly places responsibility on these actors, avoiding undue blame on peripheral figures.

Analysis

Trump’s Contradictions: The documented evidence of Trump’s interactions with Epstein starkly contrasts his public denials. The unredacted email and other materials (flight logs, membership documents) provide tangible proof, challenging Trump’s narrative.

Justice Department’s Role: The initial decision to redact crucial information and the delay in releasing the full scope of Epstein’s files raise questions about transparency and potential cover-up. Goldman’s accusations against Bondi suggest a deliberate withholding of information that could clarify Trump’s involvement.

Misplaced Criticism: Criticism in this context appears well-placed. The focus on Trump’s contradictory statements and the Justice Department’s handling of Epstein’s files illuminates the central issues without diverting blame to less influential parties.

Conclusion

The controversy surrounding Donald Trump’s relationship with Jeffrey Epstein, particularly the narrative about Mar-a-Lago, underscores a complex interplay of power, decision-making, and accountability. The evidence, including the unredacted email, contradicts Trump’s longstanding claims, implicating him in a narrative of denial and revisionism. The Justice Department’s role in redacting and releasing information further complicates the picture, suggesting a need for greater transparency and scrutiny. In dissecting these narratives, it’s essential to focus on those who hold the reins of power and their decisions that shape our understanding of the truth.
