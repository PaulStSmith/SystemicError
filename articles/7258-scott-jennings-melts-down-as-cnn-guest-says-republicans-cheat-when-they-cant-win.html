Scott Jennings melts down as CNN guest says Republicans ‘cheat’ when they can’t win

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 28, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Art of Dodging Accountability: A CNN Spectacle

(Screengrab via CNN)

A tense exchange on CNN played out Friday night when podcast host Tezlyn Figaro accused Republicans of rigging the system whenever they face electoral trouble – prompting immediate pushback from conservative commentator Scott Jennings.

The fireworks began during a discussion about the growing number of retirements on Capitol Hill when Figaro argued that GOP-led map changes in states like Texas amounted to cheating.

“If Republicans can’t win, they cheat,” Figaro said flatly, repeating the accusation multiple times as a clearly agitated Jennings attempted to interrupt – even as he appeared to battle a hoarse voice.

“How?” he asked, objecting to the use of the word “cheating” and insisting Republicans had followed the legal process.

“What is cheating about it?” he demanded, arguing that redistricting disputes are routinely settled through courts and accusing Democrats of the same thing. Figaro, who reminded the longtime Republican insider that she was an independent, was unmoved, countering that Republicans often rely on GOP-friendly judges and drawn-out legal battles to get their way – which she insisted, “is also called cheating.”

As the exchange continued, Jennings accused Figaro of painting the entire U.S. political system as corrupt. “No, that’s not what I said,” she replied. “I said if Republicans can’t win, they cheat. Not the entire political system – just Republicans.”

The back-and-forth quickly turned personal, with the two talking over one another. “You talk in circles,” Jennings said, adding, “Are you dizzy?”

“No, actually, I’m not. We can keep going,” Figaro, a political consultant, said. And the tension didn’t end there. After a commercial break, the pair clashed again during a separate segment about President Donald Trump renaming the Kennedy Center.

“You’re still in the circle, you’re still on the track, keep going!” Jennings said with a laugh.

“And I’m going to keep going…keep following me. We’re going to get it. That’s why I’m sitting right next to you,” Figaro replied. “I flew in just for you.”

“I’m worn out already,” Jennings added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Dodging Accountability: A CNN Spectacle

In a recent CNN showdown that could easily be mistaken for a poorly scripted reality TV episode, we witnessed a classic example of political evasion and the art of turning a critical debate into a circus. Tezlyn Figaro, a podcast host with the audacity to speak the unspeakable, accused Republicans of essentially rigging the electoral system in their favor. This accusation, grounded in the tangible evidence of GOP-led map changes in states like Texas, was met with fierce opposition by conservative commentator Scott Jennings. Let’s dissect this exchange with the precision of a surgeon and the skepticism of a seasoned cynic.

“If Republicans can’t win, they cheat,” Figaro stated, a comment that seemed to strike a nerve with Jennings, leading to an immediate and predictable reaction. Jennings, battling a hoarse voice and perhaps a fragile argumentative position, retorted by questioning the use of the word “cheating” and insisted that Republicans simply followed the legal process. This response is a classic example of political obfuscation — the art of hiding behind the supposed sanctity of legal procedures to justify actions that, ethically speaking, linger in the murky waters of moral ambiguity.

Jennings’ defense is a page straight out of the political playbook: when confronted, redirect and accuse the other side of similar practices. However, Figaro’s point about Republicans relying on “GOP-friendly judges and drawn-out legal battles” to secure electoral gains is not just an accusation; it’s an observation of a pattern that many independent analysts and legal experts have noted over the years. Labeling this strategy as cheating might seem harsh to some, but it’s hard to argue that it doesn’t at least flirt with the boundaries of ethical conduct.

The exchange devolved further as Jennings accused Figaro of painting the entire U.S. political system as corrupt, a classic straw man tactic to shift the focus from the specific allegations at hand. Figaro’s clarification that her criticism was directed specifically at Republican strategies, not the entire system, seemed to fall on deaf ears. The back-and-forth quickly spiraled into personal jabs, with Jennings questioning Figaro’s coherence and Figaro asserting her determination to continue the debate — a debate that, by this point, had lost any semblance of constructive discourse.

The tension didn’t ease after a commercial break, as the pair clashed again during a discussion about President Donald Trump renaming the Kennedy Center. Jennings’ taunts and Figaro’s retorts might make for entertaining television, but they do little to advance a meaningful conversation about the integrity of our electoral processes.

This CNN exchange is emblematic of a broader issue in political discourse: the tendency to prioritize scoring points over addressing the substantive concerns at stake. Figaro’s allegations about Republican gerrymandering and judicial manipulation deserve serious scrutiny, not dismissive retorts or accusations of bias. The fact that these discussions quickly devolve into personal attacks and circular arguments is a disservice to the electorate, who deserve clear, fact-based analyses of the tactics used by all political parties to secure power.

In the end, this spectacle on CNN did little more than entertain and perhaps infuriate viewers. It’s a missed opportunity to engage in a critical examination of our electoral system’s vulnerabilities and the ways in which those in power, regardless of party affiliation, might manipulate those vulnerabilities to their advantage. It’s high time we demand more from our political commentators and the networks that host them — less circus, more substance.
