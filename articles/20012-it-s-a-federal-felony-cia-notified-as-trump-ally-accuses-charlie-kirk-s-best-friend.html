'It's a federal felony!' CIA notified as Trump ally accuses Charlie Kirk's 'best friend'

A current ally of Donald Trump is lobbing allegations at a prominent conservative commentator, leading to the CIA being notified.

Candace Owens, a fringe conservative and active podcaster who formerly worked as a communications director for Charlie Kirk's Turning Point USA, has since Kirk's death spread various conspiracy theories about the Utah killing.

Enter Laura Loomer, a purported "Trump whisperer" who has the presidents ear when it comes to hiring choices.

According to Loomer, Owens is trying to "grift" her way through Kirk's death, and in the process has broken the law with her merch.

"As part of her grift to monetize the murder or her 'best friend Charlie Kirk', Candace Owens is selling a 'CIA line' of merchandise called the 'Candace Intelligence Agency' which rips off the CIA name and LOGO. It is a FEDERAL FELONY to use the CIA @CIA seal without license," Loomer alleged as she tagged the CIA's X account and cited 50 U.S. Code § 3513 - Misuse of Agency name, initials, or seal.

Loomer then added, "You are likely breaking the law with your CIA merch, Candace."

"The CIA can technically sue to get Candace to stop using their logo. It’s really disrespectful to Charlie Kirk and the CIA," she then added.

Owens replied, "This tweet is fantastic content for the show next week. Do you think I should go to prison? If so, for how long? Have you considered tagging Pam Bondi? Please respond."

Loomer then said, "I think you should find a new grift. That’s what I think."

"I think you should stop pretending like you aren’t a product of jewish money and jewish grace, rich Jews who paid your salary your entire career and gave you every opportunity you ever had," according to Loomer. "I think you should stop…"

Our Analysis:

The Spectacle of Conservatism Eating Its Own

In the latest chapter of the conservative movement's ongoing drama, we find ourselves witnessing a peculiar feud that underscores the deep fractures within its ranks. Candace Owens, a figure who flirts with the edges of mainstream conservatism, is now at the center of a controversy that not only highlights the opportunistic nature of political commentary but also the dangerous game of leveraging tragedy for personal gain.

Candace Owens vs. Laura Loomer: A Battle of Accusations

Laura Loomer, dubiously dubbed the "Trump whisperer," has taken it upon herself to expose what she sees as a gross exploitation of Charlie Kirk's death by Candace Owens. Owens, not one to shy away from controversy, has apparently launched a "CIA line" of merchandise, cleverly acronymized from "Candace Intelligence Agency," yet dangerously skirting the line of legality by mimicking the CIA's name and logo. Loomer's accusation isn't just a petty jab; it's grounded in the federal law outlined in 50 U.S. Code § 3513, which prohibits the unauthorized use of the CIA's insignia.

The Merchandising of Tragedy

Owens's response to the allegations is a masterclass in deflective mockery. Instead of addressing the potential legal and ethical implications of her actions, she opts to treat the issue as fodder for her show, exemplifying a disturbing trend among political commentators: the monetization of tragedy. Owens's cavalier attitude towards the potential disrespect of not only Kirk's memory but also a federal agency is emblematic of a larger problem within political discourse — the sacrifice of respect and decency at the altar of personal gain.

A Deeper Malaise Within Conservatism

Loomer's subsequent attacks on Owens, which veer into personal and anti-Semitic territory, reveal a bitterness and division that transcends mere ideological differences. This isn't just about whether Owens crossed a legal line with her merchandise; it's a manifestation of the toxicity and infighting that plagues the conservative movement. The exchange degenerates further into base personal attacks, with Loomer accusing Owens of being a "product of Jewish money and Jewish grace." This language not only detracts from the original, somewhat valid, criticism but also exposes a dark undercurrent of anti-Semitism within certain conservative factions.

Conclusion: The Tragedy of Opportunism

This debacle between Owens and Loomer, while may appear as a mere squabble on the surface, is a poignant reminder of the ethical erosion within political commentary. The eagerness to exploit tragedies, engage in legal dubiousness, and descend into hate-fueled personal attacks points to a broader systemic failure. The conservative movement, once a bastion of certain principles and values, now finds itself mired in opportunism, division, and a distressing departure from those very ideals it purports to champion.

In the end, this episode isn't just about a dispute over merchandise; it's a stark illustration of how far some are willing to go in their quest for relevance and profit, even if it means sacrificing integrity and decency on the public stage. As observers, we must question the direction in which political discourse is headed and consider the long-term implications of this relentless pursuit of controversy and clicks over substantive, respectful dialogue.
