Ducey’s Ghost Campaign: A Mirage of Traditional Conservatism in the MAGA Era

The Illusion of a Moderate Revival

Former Arizona Governor Doug Ducey, as painted by columnist George Will, represents an almost wistful figment of traditional conservatism, in stark contrast to the ultra-MAGA fervor that has seized the Republican Party. Will’s articulation of a hypothetical 2028 presidential campaign for Ducey is less a realistic political maneuver and more a desperate plea from the ‘Never Trump’ faction within the GOP. The imagery of Ducey’s non-MAGA conservatism holds a mirror to the internal strife and ideological schisms fracturing the party.

The Power Dynamics of Republican Ideology

Ducey’s real power, or lack thereof, within the Republican Party is evident in his ostracization following his certification of the 2020 presidential election results. This act, adhering to constitutional duty rather than party pressure, has made him persona non grata among MAGA adherents, who continue to wield substantial influence over the GOP’s direction and narrative. The portrayal of Ducey as a potential presidential candidate is thus not just speculative but indicative of the marginalization of traditional conservative values in the current Republican landscape.

Misdirection and the Blame Game

Will’s column, while ostensibly promoting a return to less confrontational politics, inadvertently highlights the ongoing blame game within the GOP. By positioning Ducey as a foil to MAGA Republicans, it perpetuates the internal conflict and deflects from the broader issue: the party’s complicity in enabling and sustaining the divisive politics it now seeks to temper. This misdirection serves to absolve the party’s structural complicity, focusing criticism on surface-level animosities rather than the underlying shifts towards authoritarianism and populism.

The Fictional Promise of Republican Simplicity

The fictional Ducey campaign promises a presidency that would be “out of sight,” focusing on “taking care that the laws are faithfully executed” and reducing executive overreach. However, this idealized vision of presidency, with its emphasis on congressional revival and constitutional fidelity, feels increasingly out of touch with the reality of current Republican politics, where executive power and unilateral actions (often via executive orders) have been celebrated, rather than scrutinized, under Trump’s administration.

Broader Implications: The Struggle for the GOP’s Soul

This narrative around Ducey and the envisioned shift back to a more moderate, less combative political stance reflects a deeper ideological battle for the soul of the Republican Party. Will’s endorsement of Ducey symbolizes the yearning for a pre-Trump normalcy, but it also underscores the significant hurdles that traditional conservatives face in reclaiming influence within a party transformed by populism and personality cults.

Conclusion: The Mirage of Change

Ultimately, the political saga of Doug Ducey and the speculative nature of his presidential candidacy as depicted by George Will reveal more about the fractures within the Republican Party than about Ducey’s actual prospects. It serves as a stark reminder of the challenges facing traditional conservatives in a party reshaped by extremist elements. The story is less about Ducey’s potential for leadership and more about the GOP’s ongoing existential crisis, struggling between the allure of authoritarian populism and the principles of traditional conservatism. As the party continues to grapple with these tensions, the prospect of a Ducey-like figure emerging victorious seems more like a mirage than a foreseeable reality.
