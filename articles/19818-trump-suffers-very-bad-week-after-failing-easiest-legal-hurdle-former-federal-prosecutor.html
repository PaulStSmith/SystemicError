Trump suffers very bad week after failing easiest legal hurdle: former federal prosecutor

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosFebruary 18, 20265 min read

Former federal prosecutor James D. Zirin on Tuesday lambasted Donald Trump for failing to clear a legal hurdle deemed so easy by the legal profession that it is the subject of a famous joke.

Writing for The Hill, Zirin said that Trump is coming off of “a bad week” after a grand jury in Washington D.C., declined to level criminal charges against six Democratic lawmakers who took part in a video reminding military members of their obligation to ignore illegal orders. Despite Trump declaring the video “SEDITIOUS BEHAVIOR, punishable by DEATH,” in a social media post, the jury found that there was no probable cause to charge the Democrats under a law that targets individuals who “interfere with, impair, or influence the loyalty, morale, or discipline of the military or naval forces of the United States.”

Zirin spent three years as an assistant U.S. Attorney in Manhattan years ago, and said that during his time in the office, grand juries had already become somewhat infamous as a “rubber stamp” for the government’s legal pursuits. He shared a famous joke about them, which claims that “a prosecutor could get a grand jury to indict a ham sandwich,” due to the threshold for an indictment being low and because prosecutors do not have to share conflicting evidence during the grand jury process.

Even with this low legal bar, Trump’s administration has, for months now, been dealing with a trend of grand juries refusing to sign off on its desired criminal charges. Former judge Jon E. Jones III called the idea “aberrational” and “unprecedented” in the world of law. Zirin, conversely, said that grand juries have “frequently” declined indictments since Sol Wachtler coined the “ham sandwich” joke in the 50s, even if it is remarkably rare in modern times.

“A grand jury has great power. It is one of the few institutions that can tell the government to go to hell,” Zirin explained. “If a grand jury fails to indict, a criminal prosecution cannot proceed. Of course, prosecutors are free to present the evidence again to another grand jury if they do so within the statute of limitations.”

He later added: “Although the bar is so low that grand juries almost always return the indictment the prosecutor desires, the Trump administration’s vengeful attempts to harass his political opponents have been so shockingly anti-democratic that grand juries have repeatedly refused to go along.”

Both the most recent slate of failed indictments and the headline-grabbing failure to indict a man for throwing a sandwich at a law enforcement official have occurred under the jurisdiction of Trump’s U.S. Attorney for Washington D.C., former Fox News host Jeanine Pirro. Her office’s escalation in charges has also been accused of clogging the city’s court system, with sources close to the matter calling it a “nightmare” and a “real mess” last summer.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Legal Follies: A Spectacle of Failure and Hypocrisy

In an era where political theater often trumps (pun intended) rational discourse, the recent developments concerning Donald Trump and his administration’s legal battles are both a farce and a stark illustration of their relentless pursuit of vengeance over justice. James D. Zirin’s analysis in The Hill not only sheds light on the absurdity of Trump’s legal maneuvers but also highlights a fundamental breakdown in the administration’s understanding of the law and democratic principles. Let’s dissect this tragicomedy, piece by piece.

First and foremost, the grand jury’s refusal to indict six Democratic lawmakers, despite Trump’s bombastic claims of “SEDITION BEHAVIOR,” underscores a profound misapprehension of what constitutes illegal orders within a military context. Trump’s rhetoric, alarmingly detached from legal reality, was met with the cold hard truth of the law—a truth that does not bend to the will of capricious political agendas.

Zirin, leveraging his experience as a former federal prosecutor, reminds us of a longstanding joke within the legal community that highlights the low threshold for indictments, stating that “a prosecutor could get a grand jury to indict a ham sandwich.” The irony here is palpable. Despite this low bar, Trump’s administration has faced an unprecedented series of rebukes from grand juries. This isn’t just a minor hiccup in their legal strategies; it’s a blaring siren signaling their profound incompetence and ethical bankruptcy.

Former judge Jon E. Jones III’s description of this trend as “aberrational” and “unprecedented” might seem like an understatement when considering the broader implications. It’s not just about failing to secure indictments; it’s about the erosion of democratic principles and the rule of law. Trump’s attempts to weaponize the legal system against political adversaries are not only “shockingly anti-democratic” as Zirin points out, but they also represent a dangerous drift towards autocracy.

The role of Jeanine Pirro, former Fox News host turned U.S. Attorney for Washington D.C., in this debacle cannot be overstated. Her office’s aggressive escalation of charges, which has reportedly turned the city’s court system into a “nightmare,” is indicative of a deeper malaise. This isn’t just about legal incompetence; it’s about a deliberate strategy to harass and intimidate political opponents—a tactic that reeks of authoritarian regimes, not a democratic republic.

In conclusion, the article paints a damning picture of an administration that is not only failing to grasp the basics of legal procedure but is also actively undermining the very foundations of democracy. The grand juries’ repeated rejections of Trump’s desired indictments are not mere legal setbacks; they are a resounding affirmation of the resilience of democratic institutions in the face of authoritarian impulses.

As we navigate these tumultuous waters, it’s essential to remain vigilant. The dangers of political vendettas masquerading as legal proceedings are not to be taken lightly. Today, it’s a failed indictment against Democratic lawmakers; tomorrow, it could be an erosion of civil liberties and the rule of law. We must not let that happen.
