U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi speaks during the announcement of a law enforcement action during a press conference at the Department of Justice in Washington, D.C., U.S., November 19, 2025. REUTERS/Tom Brenner

A massive list of names discovered in the Jeffrey Epstein files, which was released over the weekend by Attorney General Pam Bondi, contained a handful of celebrities, including one who died when the accused sex trafficker was still a child.

That led to rolled eyes from MS NOW host Alex Witt on Monday afternoon when she joined the pile-on of the Donald Trump appointee for yet another effort to muddy the waters as she drags her feet on doing anything for the Epstein victims.

Less than a week after Bondi turned in a nationally-televised abrasive performance before the House Judiciary Committee, where she avoided eye contact with victims who have yet to be interviewed by the DOJ, her office released the list that curiously included, among others, Kurt Cobain, Elvis Presley, Marilyn Monroe and Janis Joplin.

Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA) complained, “To have Janis Joplin, who died when Epstein was 17, in the same list as Larry Nassar, who went to prison for the sexual abuse of hundreds of young women and child pornography, with no clarification of how either was mentioned in the files is absurd. Release the full files. Stop protecting predators. Redact only the survivor’s names.”

MS NOW’s Ken Dilanian explained, “They’re doing what commercial litigants do all the time. They dump people in, they dump information on their opponents and say, ‘You guys sort it out.’ So we are left not knowing the meaning of some of these names, why they’re included. They didn’t have to do that, they could have been more specific, they chose not to.”

“Nonetheless, the Justice Department is arguing that they have been fully transparent here, and they’re doing the best they can under some very difficult circumstances,” he added.

“I don’t know, man,” the obviously skeptical Witt replied. “Marilyn Monroe? She died in 1962. I mean, Jeffrey Epstein was 9 years old. Really? That one is really quite extraordinary, that one.”

Our Analysis:

A Critical Dissection of the Epstein Files Debacle

In an astonishing revelation that could only be described as a bewildering circus of incompetence, the Attorney General Pam Bondi’s office has fumbled into the absurd by releasing a list of names associated with the Jeffrey Epstein files that reads more like a guest list for a séance than a serious investigative document. Among the names listed were Kurt Cobain, Elvis Presley, Marilyn Monroe, and Janis Joplin—icons who have long since departed this mortal coil, some long before Epstein’s heinous activities came to light. This move has not only sparked outrage but has also raised serious questions about the motives and competency of those at the helm of this investigation.

The Absurdity of Including Deceased Celebrities

The inclusion of these deceased celebrities in the Epstein files is not just a clerical error; it’s a grotesque oversight that borders on the surreal. Marilyn Monroe, who died in 1962 when Epstein was a mere child, and Janis Joplin, who passed away when Epstein was 17, have no conceivable connection to Epstein’s criminal undertakings. This blatant disregard for factual accuracy not only undermines the credibility of the investigation but also insults the intelligence of the public.

Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna rightly pointed out the absurdity, emphasizing the gross injustice of lumping legitimate predators with individuals who could not possibly be involved. This smacks of an attempt to “muddy the waters” and distract from the real issues at hand. Khanna’s call for the full release of the Epstein files, with only the victims’ names redacted, is a demand for transparency and accountability that seems to be falling on deaf ears.

The DOJ’s Dubious Claim of Transparency

The Justice Department’s assertion of being “fully transparent” in this debacle is, to put it mildly, a farce. Transparency does not involve throwing a mishmash of names into the public domain without context or clarification. Ken Dilanian’s commentary that this tactic resembles the practices of commercial litigants dumping overwhelming and irrelevant information on opponents only further illustrates the lack of due diligence and respect for the gravity of the situation.

The Media’s Role in the Spectacle

The media, represented by figures like MS NOW’s Alex Witt, plays a crucial role in holding those in power accountable. Witt’s skepticism towards the DOJ’s actions is a beacon of common sense in an otherwise murky narrative. It’s essential that the media continues to question, probe, and not accept nonsensical explanations at face value.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

The release of the Epstein files in their current form is a slap in the face to the victims and a mockery of the justice system. It’s evident that those responsible for this investigation are either woefully incompetent or deliberately obfuscating the truth. The demand for the full release of the Epstein files, with appropriate redactions to protect the victims, is not just a request—it’s a mandate for justice. Anything less is an endorsement of the systemic failures that allowed Epstein’s crimes to go unchecked for so long.

In the end, this isn’t just about the names of long-dead celebrities being wrongly included; it’s about a systemic disregard for the severity of Epstein’s crimes and a blatant disrespect for the victims. The time for obfuscation and excuses is over. It’s time for the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth.
