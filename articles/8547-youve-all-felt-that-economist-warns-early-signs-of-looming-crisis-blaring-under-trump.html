'You’ve all felt that': Economist warns early signs of looming crisis blaring under Trump

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosNovember 20, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

President Donald Trump rolled back tariffs on a number of grocery staples, like beef, coffee, and bananas, late last week in what is being called a “sharp reversal” of his economic policy. He even admitted that tariffs do increase prices “in some cases,” but he also insisted there is “virtually no inflation” in America.

Inflation, as many grocery store shoppers and consumers in general have seen, is “persistent,” according to economist Justin Wolfers. Officially, U.S. inflation rose to 3.0 percent in September, about the same level it was at when President Trump took office. Trump campaigned on a promise to reduce prices on day one.

Wolfers spoke to ABC News, which showed that prices on some grocery items have risen by double-digit percentages: for example, coffee is up 18.9 percent, beef is up 14.7 percent, and bananas are up 6.9 percent.

“So, so far we’ve seen rising inflation — well, not really rising, sorry, persistently high inflation,” Wolfers told ABC News. “That’s one of the things that’s kept interest rates high.”

President Trump has repeatedly criticized Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell for not lowering interest rates more. Economists warn that lowering interest rates could increase inflation.

“The other thing that happens when you’ve got across-the-board, tariffs, is it reorganizes supply chains. Something that we sometimes call a ‘supply shock,’ Wolfers explained.

The New York Times on Wednesday reported that “President Trump’s sweeping tariffs took a toll on trade in August, as imports dropped 5.1 percent.”

Wolfers then warned about seeing signs of “stagflation,” a term popularized in the 1960s and 1970s.

“We’re seeing the early stages of what economists call ‘stagflation’ — the ‘flation’ part is inflation, and you’ve all felt that at the grocery store,” he explained.

“The ‘stag’ part is stagnation, which is, we’ve got rising unemployment and slower economic growth than we otherwise would have.”

Trump’s tariffs have largely been blamed for slower economic growth.

Bloomberg News on Tuesday also warned on stagflation, reporting that Trump’s fight against the Federal Reserve — the president for months has said he wants Chairman Powell out, and on Tuesday said he already knows who he wants to succeed Powell — could trigger stagflation

Our Analysis:

The Tariff Tango: Trump’s Policy Pirouette

In a move that can only be described as a dramatic shift, President Trump has decided to roll back tariffs on essential grocery items such as beef, coffee, and bananas. This decision, dubbed a “sharp reversal” of his previous economic stance, shines a spotlight on the tumultuous and often contradictory nature of his administration’s policies. The fact that Trump has conceded that tariffs do indeed increase prices “in some cases” while simultaneously claiming that there’s “virtually no inflation” in America is a bewildering display of cognitive dissonance.

The Inflation Illusion

Despite Trump’s assertions, the specter of inflation is far from illusory. Economist Justin Wolfers provides a reality check, pointing out the “persistent” nature of inflation that grocery store shoppers are painfully aware of. With U.S. inflation ticking up to 3.0 percent in September, mirroring the levels at the beginning of Trump’s tenure, the promise of reduced prices “on day one” seems like a distant fantasy. The stark figures presented by ABC News, highlighting significant price hikes in coffee (18.9 percent), beef (14.7 percent), and bananas (6.9 percent), further dismantle any claims of nonexistent inflation.

The Interest Rate Quagmire

Trump’s vocal criticisms of Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell for not slashing interest rates further add another layer of complexity to this economic conundrum. Yet, economists are right to caution that such a move could fan the flames of inflation, potentially exacerbating the very issue Trump purports to combat. The president’s misunderstanding of basic economic principles is not just alarming; it’s dangerous.

Tariffs, Trade, and Stagflation

The broader implications of Trump’s tariff policy cannot be overstated. As Wolfers articulates, the imposition of tariffs triggers a “supply shock,” disrupting supply chains and contributing to economic inefficiency. This point is underscored by The New York Times, which reported a significant 5.1 percent drop in imports in August, a direct consequence of Trump’s “sweeping tariffs.”

The specter of stagflation looms large over this discourse. Wolfers’ warning of the early stages of stagflation, characterized by the toxic combination of inflation and economic stagnation, should be a wake-up call for the administration. Yet, Bloomberg News’s report on the potential for stagflation, exacerbated by Trump’s ongoing feud with the Federal Reserve, suggests that such warnings are likely to fall on deaf ears.

Conclusion: A Policy of Contradictions

President Trump’s tariff rollback is a testament to the erratic nature of his economic policy. While the move may provide temporary relief to consumers, it does little to address the underlying issues plaguing the U.S. economy—namely, the president’s fundamental misunderstanding of economics. The administration’s approach, marked by flip-flops and contradictions, not only undermines the nation’s economic stability but also erodes confidence in its leadership.

As we navigate these turbulent economic waters, one thing becomes clear: the need for coherent, consistent policy has never been greater. Unfortunately, with Trump at the helm, such clarity appears to be in short supply.
