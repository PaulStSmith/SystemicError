Trump resorts to 'nonsense' as telling pattern reveals desperation

Based on an article by Nick Hilden

Paulo SantosSeptember 1, 20262 min read

AI Slop Is What Power Looks Like When It Has Nothing to Show

The Bragging Has Stopped

Trump’s social feed is doing something more revealing than his supporters want to admit: it is advertising failure. According to the source article, he is posting a steady stream of fake, often AI-generated images and distractions because his administration has nothing real to sell. The promises were simple: lower prices, no new war, better outcomes. The record is the opposite.

The People With Power Own the Damage

This is not a story about a noisy president surrounded by bad content. It is a story about who controls the machinery of government and what they have done with it. Trump and Republican lawmakers, by the source’s account, are in complete control of the federal government, and the results are inflationary pressure, a war with Iran, and added pain for farmers under tariffs and a new trade war with Canada.

That is the core political fact. The governing coalition made the decisions. The governing coalition owns the consequences.

Distraction Is the Message

The AI images are not a sideshow. They are cover. Fake Lake Ontario patriotism, vanity-project visuals, invented encounters with dead celebrities, all of it performs a basic function: keep attention away from the absence of achievements. It is easier to flood the feed with nonsense than to explain why promised gains never arrived.

This is propaganda in the most banal form. Not grand ideology. Not coherent policy. Just noise deployed to create the impression of motion while ordinary people absorb the cost.

Poll Attacks Are a Tell

Trump’s attack on polling matters because it exposes the weakness under the bluster. When he calls the polls fake after posting his own fake content, he is not making an argument. He is trying to discredit the only instruments still recording his unpopularity.

That matters because the polls are not the problem. The governing record is. If the numbers are bad, the answer is not to scream at the mirror. It is to admit that the administration has delivered neither the economic relief nor the restraint it promised.

The Framing Problem

The source article gets one thing right: this is about what Trump is not bragging about. That absence is the story. The weaker framing would be to treat the AI garbage as a quirky symptom of internet-age politics, as if nonsense were the main event. It is not. Nonsense is what a failing power structure uses when it cannot defend results.

The larger pattern is familiar. Authoritarian politics does not need competence; it needs spectacle, blame-shifting, and enough media oxygen to blur responsibility. When the government is in the hands of people who cannot deliver, they manufacture a fake world around themselves and demand that everyone else live inside it.

The Real Record

The fake images are disposable. The consequences are not. Food prices, war, tariffs, and farm distress are the real record here, and they belong to the people who seized power and then tried to replace accountability with digital confetti.

This is what collapse-by-performance looks like: not silence, but constant fabrication; not leadership, but a torrent of self-advertising that only works if the public forgets who actually governs.
