'It’s not tariffs!' White House bristles over gloomy new economic report

One of President Donald Trump’s top economic advisors is scrambling to blame anything but tariffs after a new jobs report indicated that they might be ‘hammering” small business jobs, a move the New Republic dismissed as “only damage control.”

“No, no, it’s not tariffs,” Lutnik told CNBC’s Sara Eisen. “Remember, you had the Democratic shutdown, right, and what do you think happens to small businesses, the people who do business with the U.S. government? They know they’re not getting paid, so they slow down their projects.

“Remember, as you deport people, that’s gonna suppress private job numbers of small businesses,” he added. “But they’ll rebalance and they’ll regrow, so I think this is just a near-term event, and you’ll see as the numbers come through over the next couple of months, you’ll see that all pass.”

Reacting to Lutnick’s claims, a report from the New Republic dismissed them as “damage control” and cited numerous other factors to back up the claim that tariffs are harming the U.S. economy in ways Trump said they would not.

“Domestic manufacturing, as measured by the Institute for Supply Management Manufacturing index, fell for the ninth month in a row, showing that the tariffs are hurting an area that Trump boasts they will improve,” the piece explained. “And private companies, including wholesale retailer Costco, are suing the government to get a refund of the tariffs they’ve paid.

The report from ADP found that, for November, private sector businesses employing at most 50 people saw job losses of nearly 32,000. This was notably off from the initial prediction that these businesses would add 10,000 new jobs. Small businesses, those that employ at most 50 people, were hit the hardest, losing 120,000 jobs. Medium-sized businesses, meanwhile, added 51,000 jobs, and the largest businesses added 39,000, all of which was still not enough to create net positive job growth.

Reporting on these new numbers, CNBC host Steve Liesman said that Trump’s tariffs may be to blame for some of the pain felt by small businesses.

“That’s the fourth negative number in the past six months,” Liesman said. “The estimate was for plus 40,000, so the street was off on this one... Small business getting hammered and there is some information that some of this may be coming from being hammered by the tariffs.”

During an appearance on CNBC later in the day, Trump’s Commerce Secretary denied the theory that tariffs were to blame, instead putting the onus on the administration’s preferred target: Democrats.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump’s Economic Advisors and the Tariff Blame Game

In a display of political theater worthy of Broadway, one of President Donald Trump’s top economic advisors, Lutnik, embarked on a desperate scramble to deflect blame from the administration’s tariffs, following a damning jobs report. Despite the evidence piling up like a car crash in slow motion, Lutnik’s attempts to point fingers elsewhere—be it at Democrats or the deportation of workers—reek of desperation and a tenuous grasp on the economic realities facing small businesses in America.

The Blame Game: Democrats and Deportations

Lutnik’s performance on CNBC was nothing short of a masterclass in deflection. With the finesse of a seasoned politician, he attempted to shift the blame onto the “Democratic shutdown” and the deportation of individuals, suggesting these factors were responsible for the downturn in small business employment. This maneuver is as transparent as it is ineffective, serving only to highlight the administration’s inability to own up to the consequences of its policies.

The notion that these factors alone could be responsible for the significant job losses reported is laughable at best and dangerously misleading at worst. Small businesses, the backbone of the American economy, are clearly under siege, but not from the sources Lutnik would have us believe.

Tariffs: The Elephant in the Room

The New Republic’s dismissal of Lutnik’s claims as “only damage control” hits the nail on the head. The evidence is overwhelming: the tariffs, hailed by Trump as a winning strategy, are in fact a millstone around the neck of domestic manufacturing and small businesses. The Institute for Supply Management Manufacturing index’s continued decline is a testament to this, contradicting Trump’s boasts and exposing the tariffs as a self-inflicted wound on the American economy.

Furthermore, the lawsuit from Costco seeking a refund for tariffs paid is a glaring indicator of the broader discontent within the private sector. This isn’t just about numbers on a page; it’s about real companies bleeding real money, thanks to a misguided policy.

The Reality: Small Businesses Bear the Brunt

The ADP report’s findings are a cold splash of reality. While Lutnik and company engage in verbal gymnastics, small businesses are facing job losses that starkly contrast with the administration’s rosy projections. The loss of nearly 32,000 jobs in November alone is a dire warning sign, particularly when these businesses were expected to add jobs, not hemorrhage them.

CNBC host Steve Liesman’s analysis that tariffs may be to blame adds a voice of reason to the cacophony of deflection. Despite the administration’s best efforts to muddy the waters, the connection between the tariffs and the plight of small businesses is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore.

Conclusion: A Deflection Too Far

In their response to the economic fallout of their tariff policy, Trump’s advisors, including the Commerce Secretary, have chosen a path of deflection over accountability, blaming Democrats for their own policy failures. This tactic is not only intellectually dishonest but also a disservice to the American people, particularly those small business owners and workers bearing the brunt of these policies.

As the evidence mounts, the administration’s efforts to shift blame are becoming increasingly untenable. The truth, as is often the case, is simple: the tariffs are harming the U.S. economy in ways Trump promised they wouldn’t. No amount of blame-shifting can conceal this reality. It’s time for the administration to face the music and reckon with the consequences of its actions.
