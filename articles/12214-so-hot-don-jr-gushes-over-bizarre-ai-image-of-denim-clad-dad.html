Donald Trump Jr. posted a bizarre gushing over his dad as he shared a denim-clad AI image of the 79-year-old president with the comment, “so hot!”

"That Hanse…. Um, Donald is so hot right now!!!" he posted on Instagram, apparently referencing Owen Wilson's Zoolander character while promoting an eye-popping artificial intelligence rendering of Trump lying across a floor in double denim.

The post capitalizes on a racism controversy surrounding American Eagle's campaign featuring “White Lotus” actress Sweeney. The brand's tagline "Sydney Sweeney has great jeans"—a play on "good genes"—sparked outrage over apparent eugenic messaging.

"The American Eagles ad wasn't just a commercial. It was a love letter to white nationalism and eugenic fantasies, and Sydney Sweeney knew it," activist Zellie Imani wrote on X, triggering a MAGA meltdown.

Sen. Ted Cruz predictably jumped in to defend Sweeney, posting: "Wow. Now the crazy Left has come out against beautiful women,” the Daily Beast reported.

Trump's communications director Steven Cheung escalated the rhetoric, calling criticism "warped, moronic, and dense liberal thinking" and a "big reason why Americans voted the way they did in 2024."

"Cancel culture run amok," Cheung declared, sharing an MSNBC article titled "Sydney Sweeney's ad shows an unbridled cultural shift towards whiteness."

Our Analysis:

The Trump Dynasty: A Masterclass in Distraction and the Weaponization of Media

Donald Trump Jr.'s latest social media stunt—posting an AI-generated image of his father in double denim, accompanied by a flippant, "so hot!"—is a bizarre spectacle that underscores the Trump family's relentless pursuit of media attention at any cost. This move, ostensibly a nod to the Sydney Sweeney advertising controversy, is a masterstroke in distraction and manipulation, diverting attention from substantive issues to trivial, inflammatory content.

The Art of the Distraction

At its core, Trump Jr.'s post is nothing short of a calculated diversion. By engaging in the Sydney Sweeney "jeans vs. genes" debacle, the Trumps once again hijack the news cycle, steering the conversation away from policy or their own controversies. It's a classic move in the Trump playbook: when in doubt, redirect. The post's absurdity—lauding a digitally altered image of the elder Trump as "so hot"—is a testament to their ability to generate buzz over nothing, eclipsing far more pressing matters with the digital equivalent of a sideshow.

Weaponizing Controversy

The Trumps have long thrived on controversy, but this incident reveals a deeper, more insidious strategy: the weaponization of cultural wars. By inserting themselves into the American Eagle racism row, they not only stir the pot but also exploit the situation for their own gain. The post capitalizes on the outrage over the ad's perceived eugenic messaging, turning a serious conversation about racism and advertising ethics into fodder for their relentless self-promotion.

The Echo Chamber Reacts

Predictably, the Trump echo chamber sprang into action. Ted Cruz's defense of Sweeney under the guise of attacking the "crazy Left" and Steven Cheung's dismissal of legitimate criticism as "warped, moronic, and dense liberal thinking" are par for the course. These reactions serve a dual purpose: rallying the base and perpetuating the us-vs-them narrative that the Trumps so effectively exploit. Cheung's declaration of "cancel culture run amok" is particularly rich, considering the Trump administration's own attempts to silence dissent and control the narrative.

The Bigger Picture

Beyond the immediate spectacle, this episode reflects a troubling trend in American politics: the commodification of outrage and the erosion of public discourse. The Trumps, ever the opportunists, have mastered the art of turning every controversy, no matter how trivial or serious, into a spectacle that benefits them, often at the expense of meaningful conversation.

In essence, Donald Trump Jr.'s latest Instagram escapade is not just a frivolous post in the digital ether. It's a stark reminder of the Trump strategy: distract, divide, and conquer. As we inch closer to another election cycle, one thing is clear—the Trumps remain committed to their brand of chaos politics, with no sign of changing course. And that, perhaps, is the most disturbing takeaway of all.
