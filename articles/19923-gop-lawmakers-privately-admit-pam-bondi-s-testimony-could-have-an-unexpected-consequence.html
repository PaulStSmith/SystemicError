Lawmakers — both Democrats and Republicans — have told an analyst that Attorney General Pam Bondi‘s contentious performance this week could ultimately backfire on President Donald Trump and the GOP.

Salon‘s White House columnist Brian Karem revealed how Bondi’s conduct could lead to an unexpected consequence ahead of the midterms in November.

“Still, nothing was as odd as Bondi’s performance, which was unprofessional and combative,” Karem wrote. “She lashed out at legislators, telling them they had no right to accuse her of breaking the law, and called Rep. Jamie Raskin, D-Md., a ‘washed up loser lawyer.’ Many Democrats and even some Republicans told me privately that her testimony will probably help the Democrats during the fall midterm elections.”

One Republican staffer from Texas described Bondi as “the worst attorney general in history,” Karem wrote.

Bondi wasn’t the only Trump administration official this week facing questions from congressional leaders.

Customs Enforcement Acting Director Todd Lyons and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick also testified before House and Senate committees the day before Bondi, responding to questions about immigration enforcement and questionable ties to deceased convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

“For three hours, Lyons had a hard time defending ICE actions in Minneapolis before a House oversight hearing on immigration enforcement,” Karem wrote. “Across Capitol Hill, Lutnick floundered while testifying before a Senate Appropriations subcommittee overseeing broadband funding and budget issues that ended up diving into the cancerous Jeffrey Epstein scandal following detailed revelations about the secretary’s connections to the convicted sex offender. Democrats, and even many Republicans, were left stunned when Lutnick admitted that he took his underage children and his wife to Epstein’s Caribbean island for lunch.”

Karem described what Trump could be feeling following the week:

“And after two days of contentious hearings on Capitol Hill, Trump must be jealous,” Karem wrote. “For once, since he returned to office, the president is not the center of attention. His staff, however, is overwhelmingly thankful for the respite. They know the danger of Trump talking. Besides, many members of Congress and Trump appointees are as shallow and demeaning as the president, so their rhetoric sounds the same.”

The impact of this week could be telling come November, the writer explained.

“But every one of Trump’s people so far this week have probably helped Democrats. Republicans are wallowing in overconfidence, ignoring reality and are their own worst enemies,” Karem wrote.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fallout of Bondi’s Performance and Its Ripple Effects

In a week marked by political theatre at its most absurd, Attorney General Pam Bondi’s performance has emerged as a spectacle of unprofessionalism and combativeness, setting the stage for a potentially self-inflicted wound on the GOP and President Trump ahead of the crucial midterm elections. Brian Karem’s report in Salon paints a vivid picture of a political apparatus in turmoil, with Bondi’s antics at the forefront.

Bondi’s Unhinged Testimony: A Gift to Democrats?

Bondi’s approach—attacking legislators and labelling Rep. Jamie Raskin a “washed up loser lawyer”—is not just unbecoming of her office; it’s political ammunition handed on a silver platter to the Democrats. It’s one thing to defend one’s record and quite another to descend into personal attacks that do nothing but highlight a defensive and deranged state of mind. The private admissions from both Democrats and Republicans that Bondi’s testimony could swing the midterms in favor of the Democrats underscore a broader recognition of her failure to maintain even a veneer of professionalism.

The GOP’s Broader Crisis

However, let’s not pretend Bondi is an anomaly within the Trump administration. The same week saw other officials, including Customs Enforcement Acting Director Todd Lyons and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, grappling with their own controversies—from questionable immigration enforcement actions to deeply troubling ties with Jeffrey Epstein. Lutnick’s admission of taking his family to Epstein’s Caribbean island is not just a personal error in judgment; it’s a public relations nightmare that feeds into the narrative of a morally bankrupt administration.

Trump’s Isolated Relief Amidst the Chaos

Interestingly, Trump, usually the epicenter of controversy, finds himself sidelined by the calamities engulfing his staff. This ironic twist might offer him a momentary escape from the spotlight, but it’s hardly a victory. The sheer volume of scandals and missteps by his appointees only serves to reinforce the image of a presidency in constant chaos, driven by individuals who mirror Trump’s own propensity for shallow and demeaning rhetoric.

Republicans: Ignorance Is Not Bliss

Karem’s assertion that the GOP is “wallowing in overconfidence, ignoring reality and are their own worst enemies” is a stark warning that cannot be overstated. The Republican Party’s failure to acknowledge and rectify the damages wrought by such unprofessional conduct—not just by Bondi but by the administration at large—could very well be their undoing in the upcoming elections. The idea that these events “probably helped Democrats” is not just speculation; it’s a reflection of a party so consumed by its own hubris that it fails to see the iceberg ahead.

In sum, the events of this week lay bare not just the individual failings of people like Bondi, but a systemic rot within the GOP under Trump’s leadership—a rot characterized by defiance in the face of decency, and combativeness instead of constructive dialogue. As the midterms approach, the Republican Party finds itself at a crossroads, and the path it chooses could determine not just the outcome of the elections, but the future of the party itself.
