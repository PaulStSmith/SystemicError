Government Shutdown: Everything Must Go

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 5, 20253 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The Death of Governance by DesignThe House Always Loses“Trump Auctions” as National MetaphorEverything Must Go

If there were ever an image that distilled the modern American tragedy into one absurd tableau, this is it. A “Trump Auctions” sign at the gates of the nation’s seat of power, promising an “absolute auction” of federal property, and solemnly assuring that “all proceeds will go to the Trump Foundation.” It’s a parody—but not by much.

Because let’s be honest: America’s government has already been for sale for decades. What began as the genteel corruption of lobbyists and corporate PACs has metastasized into something brazen, transactional, and proudly grotesque. The image of a liquidation sale at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue doesn’t feel far-fetched—it feels overdue.

The Death of Governance by Design

When shutdowns happen now, they’re not accidents of negotiation—they’re strategy. A weaponized collapse. The modern Republican Party, hijacked by Trumpism, discovered that breaking government is more profitable than running it. Why bother passing budgets when you can hold the entire system hostage for cable airtime and campaign donations? Why govern when you can grift?

Shutdowns used to be political failures. Today, they’re performance art—proof of ideological purity before a base that’s been trained to see “government” as the enemy. The cruelty isn’t a side effect; it’s the product. Federal workers unpaid, services halted, veterans and families left twisting in the wind—these are not accidents. They are applause lines.

The “Everything Must Go” mentality isn’t just rhetorical. National parks, environmental protections, public education, healthcare oversight—all treated like clutter in a garage sale of democracy. A once-functional republic has become a liquidation event where public good is priced below scrap value.

The House Always Loses

The Trump presidency made this philosophy explicit. His empire of bankruptcies and bad debt became the nation’s operating model. Deregulate, defund, dismantle—then brag about efficiency while selling the furniture. Every hollowed-out agency, every unfilled post, every public service starved to death by “small government” zealotry—each is another lot on the auction block.

And when the proceeds flow, they don’t go “to the people.” They go to the donor class, to private contractors, to cronies and cousins with LLCs registered in Delaware. Or, in the Trumpian spirit, they go to a “foundation” that does little but launder self-interest into a tax write-off.

The genius of the con lies in convincing working Americans that this dismantling is freedom. That burning down the state is patriotism. That the government’s bankruptcy somehow restores their dignity. It’s a lie so colossal it would make Goebbels blush, and yet, it sells—again and again—like beachfront property in Kansas.

“Trump Auctions” as National Metaphor

The fictional sign’s fine print—“All proceeds will go to the Trump Foundation”—isn’t just a punchline; it’s prophecy. Every scandal, every graft scheme, every post-office grift is another reminder that Trump didn’t invent American corruption—he just stripped away the pretense. He sold the illusion of greatness while pawning off the country’s moral furniture.

The America of 2025 doesn’t need an actual “Trump Auctions” to see how far we’ve fallen. The auction’s already happened. The public square sold to social media monopolies. The news sold to outrage algorithms. The judiciary mortgaged to billionaires. Even faith itself—sold by megachurch televangelists who trade Jesus for jet fuel.

We’ve reached the part of the auction where the auctioneer’s voice cracks from repetition, and only the ghosts of bidders remain.

Everything Must Go

What makes the image so haunting isn’t the absurdity—it’s the familiarity. We’ve seen versions of this before: the foreclosure signs after 2008, the “For Sale” placards on factories and farms, the boarded-up Main Streets in small towns stripped for corporate parts.

This is just the final evolution: the foreclosure of the republic itself. The White House as collateral. Democracy as inventory. “Freedom” as brand merchandise, heavily discounted for quick liquidation.

And while the political class plays musical chairs with shutdown deadlines, the American public remains the unpaid staff—told to keep sweeping the floors while the lights go out.

The photo’s satire hits because it doesn’t exaggerate—it documents. The government isn’t being sold tomorrow. It was sold yesterday.

All that’s left now is the auctioneer’s gavel, echoing off marble walls that once housed ideals.
Lot 1: Integrity. Sold. Lot 2: Compassion. Sold. Lot 3: Truth. Sold—cheap.

When the sign reads “Everything Must Go,” it isn’t a joke anymore.
It’s an epitaph.
