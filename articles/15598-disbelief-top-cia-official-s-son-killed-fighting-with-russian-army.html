'Disbelief': Top CIA official's son killed fighting with Russian army

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 27, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

FILE PHOTO: The seal of the Central Intelligence Agency is shown at the entrance of the CIA headquarters in McLean, Virginia, U.S., September 24, 2022. REUTERS/Evelyn Hockstein/File Photo

The CIA on Friday broke its silence over the death of Michael Gloss, the son of a senior national security official and an Iraq War veteran, who was killed last April while fighting with Russian forces in Ukraine – one of the few Americans known to have done so, the Washington Post reported.

While Gloss, 21, was killed in Donetsk on April 4, 2024, of “massive blood loss” in an artillery barrage, that he was killed on the battlefield in Ukraine fighting alongside the Russian army was not publicly revealed until Friday, according to the Post, which described Gloss as “a Dylan-loving peacenik who wanted to save the environment.”

His parents told the publication that their son struggled with mental illness for the majority of his life.

“It was with ‘disbelief and devastation’ that his parents, Juliane Gallina — a CIA deputy director for digital innovation — and Larry Gloss, received the tragic news last June, delivered in person by State Department consular affairs official, that their son had been killed in Donetsk province in eastern Ukraine, where Russia backed a separatist insurgency in 2014 and invaded outright in 2022, escalating its control of the territory,” the Post reported Friday.

Until that time, the couple had no idea their son was in Ukraine fighting with the Russian army.

“It was absolutely news to us that he was involved in any military relationship with Russia,” Larry Gloss said in an interview with the publication.

The CIA released a brief statement on Friday. “CIA considers Michael’s passing to be a private family matter — and not a national security issue. The entire CIA family is heartbroken for their loss.”

Ralph Goff, a former senior CIA official who oversaw operations in Europe and Eurasia and has spent significant time in Ukraine, also expressed sadness for the family.

“For the parents, it’s just a great tragedy,” he is quoted as saying.

Michael Gloss’s remains were cremated the week his body was returned, according to the Post. His November obituary made no mention of the war or Russia, the publication reported. It simply said: “With his noble heart and warrior spirit Michael was forging his own hero’s journey when he was tragically killed in Eastern Europe.”

Our Analysis:

The CIA, Michael Gloss, and the Unraveling Threads of National Security

In a recent revelation that has the aroma of a poorly scripted tragedy, the CIA has finally broken its silence over the death of Michael Gloss, a figure whose life story reads like the footnotes of a geopolitical thriller gone awry. The Washington Post has brought to light the demise of Gloss, a 21-year-old Iraq War veteran and the progeny of Juliane Gallina, a CIA deputy director for digital innovation. His death, occurring in the tumultuous terrains of Donetsk on April 4, 2024, represents not just a personal tragedy for his family, but a glaring spotlight on the intricate web of national security, mental health, and the inexplicable draw of foreign conflicts on American citizens.

Michael Gloss: A Peacenik's Paradox

Described by the Post as a "Dylan-loving peacenik with aspirations to save the environment," Michael Gloss's trajectory from an advocate for peace to a combatant on the side of Russian forces in Ukraine is a narrative fraught with contradictions. That Gloss, struggling with mental illness for the majority of his life, found himself in the throes of a conflict aligned against American interests raises questions far beyond the personal. It underscores a profound failure in systems meant to protect, guide, and heal.

The CIA's Stance: A Private Matter or a Public Concern?

The CIA's response to the tragedy, deeming it a "private family matter — and not a national security issue," is a stance that oscillates between dismissive and disconcerting. While the personal loss of the Gallina-Gloss family is indeed profound, the implications of a CIA deputy director's son fighting alongside Russian forces cannot be neatly categorized into the realm of personal grief. It beckons a broader discussion about national security, the vetting processes within our intelligence agencies, and the potential for conflicts of interest or breaches of sensitive information.

Silence and Omissions: The Shadow over National Security

The narrative, as spun by the CIA and the State Department, seems to conveniently sidestep the glaring red flags that Michael Gloss's situation hoists over the apparatus of national security. The delayed acknowledgment of his whereabouts and activities, coupled with the subdued response, paints a picture of either a startling oversight or a deliberate minimization of potential fallout. Furthermore, the fact that Gloss's parents were unaware of his military engagements with Russia until after his death raises alarming questions about communication breakdowns, both within families and governmental structures.

Conclusion: The Uncomfortable Questions We Must Face

Michael Gloss's story is a tragic confluence of personal battles and geopolitical entanglements. It forces us to confront uncomfortable questions about the nature of allegiance, the oversight of individuals with close ties to national security, and the mechanisms in place to prevent such bewildering scenarios. While the CIA may consider this a private matter, the implications ripple far beyond the confines of personal grief. It challenges us to scrutinize the systems we trust to safeguard our nation's secrets and the individuals within them. In the end, the story of Michael Gloss is a stark reminder of the complexities of modern warfare, where the battle lines are not just drawn on the ground, but within the very fabric of our societies and the human psyche.
