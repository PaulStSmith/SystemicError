'Toxic': Vietnam vet so sickened by Trump he can't bear to fly American flag

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJune 3, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

An 84-year-old Vietnam veteran is taking a stand against President Donald Trump by taking down the American flag that used to hang outside his California home, according to a report from the Guardian.

“The American flag won’t fly again until things get straightened out down the line and administrations change,” said Morgan Akin.

Originally from Texas, Akin claims the country is becoming increasingly unrecognizable to him and he is concerned for his family.

“What happens to me is immaterial because I’m 84 years old. My lifespan is decreasing daily, so it’s probably not going to have a big impact on me overall. But I have two children. I have three grandchildren. I’ve got now four great grandchildren,” he said. “How is that impacting their future?”

The Guardian reported, “Older Americans such as Akin are among the most disapproving of Donald Trump’s performance as president.”

A poll from NPR, PBS News, and Marist found that 37% of people born before 1947 approve of the job Trump has done as president.

Roughly six in 10 veterans say they supported Trump in 2024, but Akin does not. He is a lifelong Democrat, dating back to his time growing up in segregated Texas.

According to the Guardian, “Akin loved his home state but found it deeply prejudiced and never could shake his association with the Republican Party and racism.”

At 17, he joined the Marine Corps — and discovered it was essential to get on with his fellow Marines.

“You all bled the same way,” he said. “From that point, my whole attitude changed about a lot of things. So I have been a Democrat [since] the first time I registered.”

Today, Akin believes Trump is “just tearing the country apart. The whole fabric of the country is just being ripped apart,” Akin said. “The worst part is the people that are getting hurt – the migrants that came here in earnest.”

The outlet wrote, "Outside Akin’s home, a U.S. Marine's flag still flies alongside a Ukrainian flag. The American flag remains put away.

“I still get upset if I see someone flying it upside down. And my wife gets really upset when she sees an American flag with Trump’s face on it,” he said. “Because we’ve always been respectful of the flag. It’s just much more toxic now.”

Akin is worried about the care disabled veterans may receive in the future, and that there will be a day when the US government might use the military against citizens. The Guardian said, "Now Akin is focused on advocating how he can. He and his wife plan to keep protesting."

Our Analysis:

A Veteran's Silent Protest: A Reflection of America's Deep Divisions

In an era where the American flag often finds itself at the center of heated political debates and cultural wars, the story of Morgan Akin, an 84-year-old Vietnam veteran, becomes more than just a personal anecdote—it symbolizes the profound disquiet stirring within segments of American society. Akin's decision to remove the American flag from outside his California home as a stand against President Donald Trump is a poignant illustration of how deeply polarized the United States has become.

The Gravity of Akin's Actions

It's essential to underscore the weight of Akin's protest. Here is a man who served his country, who bled for the ideals that the American flag purports to represent, now feeling compelled to stow away this symbol because, in his eyes, those ideals are under siege. The flag, for Akin, has become a casualty—a symbol tarnished by the actions and rhetoric of a leadership he vehemently opposes. This is not a decision made lightly, especially by someone of Akin's background and generation, for whom the flag often holds a sacrosanct significance.

Dissecting the Disapproval

The article mentions that "Older Americans such as Akin are among the most disapproving of Donald Trump’s performance as president," with a poll from NPR, PBS News, and Marist highlighting that 37% of people born before 1947 approve of Trump's job as president. This statistic is particularly striking, given that roughly six in 10 veterans reportedly supported Trump in 2024. Akin's stance, therefore, is not just a personal rebuke but a mirror to a broader discontent festering within segments of the older population and veteran community—a demographic often perceived as a solid Trump support base.

A Lifelong Democrat in a Changing Landscape

Akin's disillusionment with the Republican Party, stemming from his upbringing in segregated Texas and solidified by his experiences in the Marine Corps, speaks volumes about the shifts in American political and social landscapes over the decades. His observation that "The whole fabric of the country is just being ripped apart" underscores a sentiment felt by many Americans who view the current political climate as not just divisive but destructively so.

The Toxicity Surrounding the Flag

What's perhaps most telling in this narrative is the transformation of the American flag from a universal symbol of freedom and democracy to a contentious emblem that can evoke anger, division, and even hatred. Akin's discomfort with seeing the flag flown upside down or adorned with Trump's face reflects a broader issue: the politicization of national symbols to the point where they no longer unite but divide.

Veterans and Future Uncertainties

Akin's concerns for disabled veterans and the potential misuse of the military against citizens reveal deep-seated anxieties about the direction in which the country is headed. These fears, whether one deems them justified or not, point to a crisis of confidence in the institutions meant to protect and serve the American people.

Conclusion: More Than Just a Flag

At its core, Akin's protest is a clarion call for reflection—a demand that Americans consider what the flag should represent and the realities it currently does. It's a plea for a return to the values that once made the United States a beacon of hope and freedom, viewed through the lens of a veteran who fears for the future of the country he once fought for. The question now is, will America listen?
