On Tuesday, Mace tweeted, “Adam and Eve, not Adam and Steve,“ a popular refrain from the religious right that belittles gay marriage. Her post earned a Community Note on X, which provided examples of when Mace previously voted in favor of protecting gay marriage.

Miller threw a few more of Mace’s words back at her during a new episode of “Bulwark Takes” on Wednesday.

“This is a deeply sad woman just looking for attention,” Miller said.

He then went through a short montage of Mace’s previous posts on X where she claimed to support gay rights. For instance, a post from 2021 reads, “I support LGBTQ rights. Nobody should be discriminated against.”

“Religious liberty, gay rights, and transgender equality can all coexist,” Mace’s post added.

“At this point, she’s even pro-trans,” Miller said. “ Noting also it’s 2021, so Biden is president. This is not during the Romney campaign, and this is after Trump 1.0, and she is talking about how she wants dignity for everybody, trans, gays, straights, and religious people.”

Miller also displayed a tweet where Mace shared a South Carolina Pride flag.

He also noted that Mace’s tone about marriage began to change after one of her friends got divorced. That was when she became more self-deprecating about marriage, having been through two divorces herself, Miller noted.

“That’s Nancy’s trajectory from ‘Equality for everybody, miss pride, love trans people,’ to ‘Go ahead and get married if you want, gays, it’s miserable.’” Miller said.

Our Analysis:

The Hypocrisy of Rep. Nancy Mace: A Tale of Flip-Flops and Fabrications

In the latest episode of political theater that could only be described as both bewildering and disheartening, Rep. Nancy Mace (R-SC) seems to have taken a page out of the age-old playbook of political flip-flopping. This time, it’s about an issue that’s not just political fodder but deeply personal to many: gay marriage.

Former GOP analyst Tim Miller, wielding the unyielding sword of past statements and social media posts, has masterfully highlighted the glaring inconsistencies in Mace’s stance on LGBTQ+ rights. Mace, who once proudly proclaimed, “I support LGBTQ rights. Nobody should be discriminated against,” has now seemingly regressed to echoing the tired and derogatory mantra of the religious right: “Adam and Eve, not Adam and Steve.”

The Schizophrenic Stance of Rep. Mace

What makes Mace’s recent outburst on social media not just perplexing but deeply troubling is the stark contrast it presents to her previous public pronouncements. Mace, who once draped herself in the colors of South Carolina Pride and championed the coexistence of “religious liberty, gay rights, and transgender equality,” has now seemingly abandoned this inclusive stance for a more divisive and attention-seeking rhetoric.

Tim Miller’s scathing critique, branding Mace as a “deeply sad woman just looking for attention,” may seem harsh. However, it underscores a critical and systemic issue in contemporary politics: the blatant and unabashed hypocrisy of politicians who switch stances as easily as changing outfits, depending on what they perceive to be politically expedient at the moment.

The Tragic Comedy of Political Expediency

Mace’s transformation from an advocate for “dignity for everybody, trans, gays, straights, and religious people” to someone who belittles the concept of gay marriage is not just a personal failing; it’s a symptom of a larger, more malignant disease plaguing our political system. It’s the disease of political expediency, where principles are malleable, and convictions are as steadfast as a leaf in the wind.

Miller’s observation that Mace’s tone shift came after a personal experience of divorce is particularly telling. It suggests that personal grievances and experiences, rather than principled stances or ideological convictions, may be driving her public pronouncements. This is not just disappointing; it’s a betrayal of the trust that the electorate places in their representatives.

Conclusion: A Call for Authenticity and Integrity

The saga of Rep. Nancy Mace is a stark reminder of the importance of authenticity and integrity in public service. As constituents, we must demand more from our elected officials than mere lip service to the ideals of equality and non-discrimination. We must hold them accountable for their words and, more importantly, for their actions.

Let Tim Miller’s unmasking of Rep. Mace serve as a wake-up call, not just to politicians but to all of us, about the kind of leadership we deserve and should demand. In a world rife with challenges and divisions, the last thing we need is leaders who say one thing and do another. The time for empty rhetoric has passed. What we need now, more than ever, are leaders of conviction, leaders who stand unwaveringly for what is right, even when it’s not convenient.
