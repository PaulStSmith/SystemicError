Trump's 'mental decline' put on display with 'creepy obsession': House lawmaker

WASHINGTON — A pair of top Democrats in the House of Representatives slammed President Donald Trump’s “deranged obsession” with attacking Somali-Americans on Wednesday.

On Tuesday, Trump said he does not want Somalis in the United States because “they contribute nothing,” the AP reported. His most recent attack follows a report by the conservative outlet City Journal that accused Somali Americans of committing fraud in Minnesota, the report added.

Speaking exclusively with Raw Story on Wednesday, Reps. Ilhan Omar (D-MN) and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) pushed back against the president’s remarks.

“He’s a bigoted fool,” Omar said. “There’s nothing surprising about the president using racist, xenophobic, and Islamophobic rhetoric to attack an entire community.”

Omar added that Trump’s comments make him look weak to the Somali community she represents.

“They’re all mockingly wondering if he’s ok, and so am I,” she said. “Even the reporters were asking, ‘Why are you bringing them up?’ It just seems like he has a very deranged and creepy obsession with me and, by extension, the Somali Americans, and it’s really off-putting. It puts his mental decline on display in a way that I don’t think he’s smart enough to recognize.”

The Trump administration has since stepped up its immigration enforcement activities against Somali-Americans since the president made his remarks, officials told the AP.

Ocasio-Cortez said the immigration raids show Trump is not aware of the legal complexities of his actions. She warned that his actions could leave him vulnerable to legal action.

“There are so many legal exemptions, from libel laws to slander, that, as an elected official, there are very few protections,” she said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Attack on Somali-Americans: A Closer Look

In a recent and deeply troubling development from Washington, President Donald Trump’s unabashed and continuous assault on Somali-Americans has taken a sharp and ugly turn. Labeling an entire community as contributors of “nothing” not only showcases a monumental ignorance but also a dangerous, deranged obsession that sits at the heart of his administration’s policies.

The Accusation and Its Implications

Trump’s baseless accusation comes on the heels of a report by the conservative outlet City Journal, which accused Somali Americans of committing fraud in Minnesota. Such reports, often lacking in nuance and understanding, serve as fodder for Trump’s inflammatory rhetoric. The consequential broad-brushing of an entire community based on unverified accusations is not just irresponsible; it’s reprehensible.

The Response from Omar and Ocasio-Cortez

Reps. Ilhan Omar (D-MN) and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY), two of the most outspoken critics of Trump’s policies and rhetoric, did not hold back in their condemnation. Omar’s characterization of Trump as a “bigoted fool” is not just a personal rebuke but a reflection of the sentiments of a community constantly under siege by his rhetoric. The fact that Somali-Americans, including those Omar represents, are mockingly questioning the president’s mental stability is a testament to their resilience but also highlights the absurdity and danger of his obsession.

Ocasio-Cortez’s warning about the legal vulnerabilities Trump exposes himself to with his actions is a critical aspect that often goes overlooked. The legal complexities and potential repercussions of sweeping immigration raids, fueled by baseless claims and xenophobic fervor, cannot be overstated.

The Bigger Picture

Trump’s comments and the administration’s subsequent actions reflect a broader, more alarming trend of xenophobia, Islamophobia, and racism that has been normalized and institutionalized in certain corridors of power. The targeting of Somali-Americans, or any community based on their ethnic background or religion, under the guise of national security or immigration enforcement, is a slippery slope towards authoritarianism.

Conclusion

The situation demands a response not just from Somali-Americans or the communities directly impacted but from all who value the principles of justice, equity, and the American dream. The narrative spun by Trump and his allies, rooted in prejudice and fear-mongering, must be met with facts, fierce resistance, and a commitment to the ideals that truly make America great. The mockery and disdain Trump has shown towards Somali-Americans reveal more about his character and the deranged priorities of his administration than about the community he so vehemently attacks.

In a time when leadership is needed more than ever, Trump’s actions and words are not just a systemic error but a fundamental failure of moral and ethical responsibility. The resilience of those targeted, and the voices of reason and resistance that rise in opposition, offer a glimmer of hope in these dark times.
