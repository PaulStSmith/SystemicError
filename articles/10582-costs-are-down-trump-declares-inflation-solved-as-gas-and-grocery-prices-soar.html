“I’ve already solved inflation,” Trump told “Fox & Friends” on Friday morning. “Costs are down.”

Inflation is now the highest it’s been since January, according to CNBC.

“Look at the energy costs,” he added. “You’re gonna have $2 gasoline pretty soon.”

The national average price of gas as of Friday is $3.19, according to AAA. Nationwide, prices are as high as $4.65 and as low as $2.71.

The president went on to boast, “I’ve solved just about every problem.”

But statistics from the U.S. Department of Labor show Americans are paying more—in some cases far more—at the pump and at the checkout line.

“Inflation inched higher last month as Americans closed out the summer paying more for both groceries and gasoline,” NPR reported on Thursday. “Consumer prices in August were up 2.9% from a year ago, according to a report Thursday from the Labor Department. That’s a sharper annual increase than the previous month, when inflation was clocked at 2.7%.”

During the 2024 presidential campaign, Donald Trump vowed to lower prices “on day one.”

Yet, due in part to his sweeping tariffs, “Virtually all major grocery categories are now more expensive than they were a year ago, some substantially so,” according to Axios. “This was the biggest month-over-month increase since August 2022, the tail end of a year of huge monthly increases in grocery prices.”

The price of coffee is up nearly 21%, the price of uncooked beef steaks is up nearly 17%, apples are up nearly 10%, and bananas are up almost 7%.

Overall, the cost of groceries in August was up 0.6% over July’s prices, and the price of gas jumped 1.9% in just one month, according to NPR.

Our Analysis:

Trump's "Inflation Solved" Fantasy: A Reality Check

In a display of what can only be described as staggering disconnect from reality, President Donald Trump has boldly declared inflation a non-issue under his administration, asserting that costs are on a downward trajectory. This claim, made during an appearance on "Fox & Friends," would be laughable if it weren't so misleading and detached from the financial pressures facing millions of Americans.

Inflation's Stubborn Reality

Despite Trump's triumphant proclamation, the facts stubbornly tell a different story. CNBC reports that inflation has hit its highest level since January, directly contradicting the president's narrative. The national average price of gas stands at $3.19, far from the $2 utopia Trump envisions "pretty soon." This number alone dismantles his assertion with the precision of a sledgehammer to a house of cards.

Energy Costs: A Mirage of Savings

Trump's specific reference to energy costs and the promise of $2 gasoline is not just overly optimistic; it's a mirage. With prices fluctuating between $2.71 and $4.65 nationwide, the reality at the pump is a far cry from his rosy predictions. This disparity isn't just a matter of cents; it's a chasm that reveals a profound misunderstanding or, worse, a deliberate misrepresentation of the economic landscape.

The Groceries Gouge

The president's claim that he has "solved just about every problem" flies in the face of the harsh truth that Americans are paying more, sometimes significantly more, for basic necessities. The U.S. Department of Labor's statistics show an unmistakable trend of rising costs, with grocery prices witnessing the "biggest month-over-month increase since August 2022." Items like coffee, beef steaks, apples, and bananas have seen substantial price jumps. These aren't luxury goods but staples in the average American diet, underscoring the impact of inflation on everyday life.

The Tariff Trap

Trump's much-vaunted tariffs, a centerpiece of his economic strategy, have backfired spectacularly. Axios highlights that these tariffs are a contributing factor to the spike in grocery prices. This policy, intended to bolster American industry, has instead acted as a tax on American consumers, exacerbating the inflation Trump claims to have conquered.

Conclusion: A Reality Check Overdue

In the realm of economic policy, facts are stubborn things. They refuse to bend to the will of optimistic declarations or wishful thinking. President Trump's assertion that he has "solved inflation" is not just wrong; it's a stark illustration of his administration's failure to grasp the economic realities facing ordinary Americans. The gap between Trump's rhetoric and the lived experience of citizens is wide and growing wider, a chasm filled with the real cost of his policies.

In a world where political leaders are expected to tackle challenges with honesty and integrity, Trump's approach serves as a cautionary tale. It's a reminder that in the face of hard truths, no amount of bluster can serve as a substitute for substantive policy solutions. As Americans continue to feel the pinch at the grocery store and the gas pump, one thing is clear: inflation is far from solved, and wishful thinking won't fill anyone's tank or pantry.
