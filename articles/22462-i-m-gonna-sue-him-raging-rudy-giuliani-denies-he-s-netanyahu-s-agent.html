'I'm gonna sue him!' Raging Rudy Giuliani denies he's Netanyahu's 'agent'

Based on an article by Alexander Willis

Paulo SantosAugust 24, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

When Operatives Fight Over the Scandal They Built

Rudy Giuliani went on Newsmax threatening to sue Tucker Carlson after Carlson aired a long interview with Hunter Biden and repeated claims about Giuliani’s role in pushing the laptop scandal. Carlson also claimed, without proof, that allegations about underage images on the laptop “came from the Israelis,” and Giuliani blasted him for implying he had been acting as an “agent for Bibi” Netanyahu.

Power, Not Noise

The real story is not Giuliani’s tantrum. It is the ecosystem of political operatives, media hucksters, and foreign-policy name-dropping that turns rumor into weaponry. Giuliani was not some random commentator drifting through the chaos. He was an actor with institutional reach, using his proximity to power to seed a story that could be laundered into partisan ammunition.

Carlson is playing the same game from another angle. He does not need proof because proof is not the product. The product is confusion, grievance, and the ongoing conversion of political accountability into spectacle.

The Framing Is Rotten

This story is built on misdirection. The article treats the dispute like a bizarre feud between egotists. That is the weak frame. The more serious reality is that one former mayor and one cable provocateur helped drag unverified allegations through the public sphere and then started accusing each other of foreign manipulation when the arrangement became inconvenient.

That is not a misunderstanding. It is a method. When an operation gets too ugly to defend, the operatives rebrand it as a mystery. When the story looks indefensible, they claim someone else was really behind it.

Netanyahu as a Prop

Netanyahu’s name is being used here less as a reference to policy than as a shield, a loyalty test, and a way to insinuate foreign control without having to prove anything. Giuliani says he has been close to Netanyahu for 25 years and even described personal ritual around visits to Israel. Carlson, meanwhile, tossed out a claim that “the Israelis” were pushing the minor-images allegation but offered no evidence.

That is classic political contamination. Foreign policy becomes a prop in a domestic smear campaign. Israel is not being discussed as a state with interests; it is being used as an alibi, a bogeyman, or a prestige badge depending on which lie is most useful at the moment.

The Laptop Was Always a Vehicle

The laptop story was never just about a laptop. It was a delivery system for suspicion: smear by implication, then amplify by repetition, then let the audience do the rest. Giuliani’s role in feeding that machine matters because it shows how power moved through informal channels when formal institutions were inconvenient.

Carlson’s performance adds another layer of rot. He is not correcting the record. He is escalating the same tactic with a different costume, pretending that his unsupported allegation somehow clarifies the damage his side already helped spread. It does not. It only reveals that the people trafficking in scandal are the first to cry foul when the scandal starts reflecting back on them.

The Pattern Is the Point

This is what authoritarian politics looks like in a media environment that rewards insinuation over evidence. Operatives manufacture accusations, media figures recycle them, and when the lie begins to splinter, everyone reaches for a more dramatic enemy. Foreign influence. Deep state. Treachery. Betrayal. Anything but responsibility.

The lesson is not that Giuliani and Carlson are feuding. The lesson is that they are both products of the same degraded system: one where democratic accountability is replaced by theatrical accusation, and where the people doing the most damage insist they are the ones being wronged.

If a political order depends on this kind of permanent smear engine, the problem is not the latest fight inside it. The problem is the machinery itself.
