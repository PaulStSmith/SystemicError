'Is there a smell?' Trump biographer confronts rumor that comes up 'all the time'

Based on an article by Bennito L. Kelty

Trump’s Body Is Not the Crisis

Michael Wolff was asked, again, to confirm a bathroom rumor about Donald Trump. He said there is no evidence for it and that he has not noticed any odor when he was around Trump before the 2024 campaign. That is the entire factual payload here. Everything else is a symptom of the attention economy eating its own tail.

The Real Subject Is Power

Trump is not a private citizen being gossiped about for sport. He is the president. The relevant fact is not whether online speculation about adult diapers is true; it is that a man with immense institutional authority has turned his own aging body into a perpetual side show while holding the apparatus of government.

That matters because power does not become harmless when it gets ridiculous. It becomes harder to monitor. The costume, the rumors, the cruelty, the aesthetic of decay all function as cover for actual decisions made by actual officials with actual consequences.

Gossip Is Doing the Work of Analysis

This kind of story offers the illusion of scrutiny while dodging the substance. It invites readers to obsess over hygiene, smell, and embarrassment instead of the machinery Trump commands. That is misdirection, not investigation.

The article’s frame treats bodily speculation as a kind of proxy for political judgment. But a president’s suspected frailty is not the same thing as accountability. Decline does not cancel authoritarianism. It can intensify it, because frightened leaders often lean harder on spectacle, loyalists, and intimidation.

The Court Around Him

The detail about Natalie Harp being mocked as his “diaper nurse” tells you something more useful than the rumor itself. Trump’s operation is not a normal administration. It is a court. Aides orbit him, manage the theater, and absorb the absurdity so the central figure can remain politically usable.

That is the deeper scandal: not that people speculate about his body, but that the White House is structured around protecting the myth of his vitality no matter how degraded the reality becomes. The system adapts to him instead of restraining him.

Weak Framing, Useful Evidence

If the article is trying to say something serious about age, health, and fitness, it chooses the dumbest possible route. It collapses governance into gossip and pretends embarrassment is analysis. That is weak framing.

The useful evidence is not the rumor. It is the fact that a sitting president’s physical state has become a cottage industry of whispers because official politics has offered so little honest disclosure, and because Trump’s entire brand thrives on forcing everyone else to talk around the obvious.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics degrades public life: the leader’s body becomes a spectacle, the press chases the spectacle, and the state keeps operating in the background. The noise is not accidental. It is a method.

Trump’s vulnerability, if it exists, is not primarily a medical question. It is an institutional one. A politics that cannot distinguish bodily rumor from democratic accountability has already been trained to look away from the real crime scene.
