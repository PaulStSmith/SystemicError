'The state is me': Historian warns Trump just gave clearest authoritarian signal yet

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20257 min read

U.S. President Donald Trump holds a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., August 26, 2025. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

After militarized federal occupations of Los Angeles and Washington, D.C., President Donald Trump is threatening to do the same thing to other U.S. cities with Democratic mayors, including Chicago and Baltimore. But Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker and Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson are making it clear that they don't want to see U.S. troops marching up Michigan Avenue, and Maryland Gov. Wes Moore is stressing that law enforcement in Baltimore should be left up to the Baltimore Police Department — not the U.S. military or federalized National Guard troops.

Philadelphia District Attorney Larry Krasner told CNN that Trump has no legal grounds for federalizing the city's police department, adding, "He better not try it in Philly."

Historian/author Garrett Graff discussed Trump's militarized occupations of U.S. cities during an appearance on The New Republic's podcast "The Daily Blast" posted on Aug. 26. And Graff, seven months into Trump's second presidency, argued that these occupations are part of a much broader pattern. Trump, according to Graff, is disregarding experts in a variety of areas — from law enforcement to museums to education to libraries.

Trump, Graff warned, is showing his authoritarianism by casting aside what experts have to say on a variety of matters and views himself as the sole authority.

"Well, it's not even just political opponents," Graff told host Greg Sargent, a former Washington Post columnist. "And this is, I think, an important part of this. It is part and parcel again of this worldview that Trump has brought into office this time where 'the state is me' — the old French king line — where it's not just that Donald Trump doesn’t like being criticized by domestic political opponents."

Trump, Graff observed, is even trying to micromanage sports.

"His craziest tweet of the weekend to me was him trying to dictate that Major League Baseball immediately induct Roger Clemens into the Hall of Fame because he played golf with Roger Clemens and, boy, Roger Clemens is just a great guy," Graff told Sargent. "This idea that there’s one true vision for America — and it is Donald Trump’s personal vision for what our life and our culture and our society should be — is to me the most clear example of authoritarianism that we could see."

Graff told Sargent that Trump is "ratcheting up… the hostility and aggression and posture of the D.C. federal takeover."

"What I think we saw over the course of last week was this rising level of aggression of the federal interference in D.C. — up to and including ICE officers and agents of this weird amalgamated federal task force of various agencies coming together to fight nondescript crime of some kind in D.C. literally shouting, 'Papers, please' to people getting off the metro in D.C. on their way home from work and forcing people to show ID to walk out of a transit station," Graff explained. "We saw a 14-ton up-armored MRAP patrolling the streets of D.C. slam into and t-bone a civilian car. And now, as of this weekend — as of Sunday night — the National Guard are now authorized to be carrying weapons."

Graff continued, "We can see now that this is an occupation by the U.S. military on domestic soil that is being done to a population and not in support of them. They've tried to have this fig leaf of: We're out there protecting D.C. But what you actually see in DC is deserted streets, deserted monuments, restaurant reservations cratering — because people don't want to go out on the street and risk these interactions with the armed agents of the state that are there."

Trump, Graff argued, is embracing the mentality of French King Louis XIV, which was embodied in the expression "l'état, c'est moi" — which, in French, means, "I am the state."

"It's that Donald Trump thinks that he personally and his taste should dictate what is in our museums with his attacks on the Smithsonian," Graff told Sargent. "He believes that his taste should dictate what is performed in our theaters with his takeover of the Kennedy Center. He believes that his taste and his personal preferences should dictate what books people should read as we have seen him go after things like the Naval Academy library…. This idea that there's one true vision for America — and it is Donald Trump's personal vision for what our life and our culture and our society should be — is, to me, the most clear example of authoritarianism that we could see."

Our Analysis:

Analysis of "After Militarized Federal Occupations" Article

In an era where the line between authoritarian impulses and democratic governance grows ever thinner, the recent actions and rhetoric from President Donald Trump, as detailed in the examined article, serve as a stark, chilling reminder of the former. The militarized federal occupations of American cities like Los Angeles and Washington, D.C., and the threats to extend these operations to other Democrat-led cities like Chicago and Baltimore, evoke images more synonymous with military dictatorships than with the United States' storied democracy.

Trump's Disregard for Local Governance

The article highlights the vocal opposition from local leaders such as Illinois Governor JB Pritzker and Chicago Mayor Brandon Johnson, who have made their stance against the militarization of their streets crystal clear. Maryland Governor Wes Moore's insistence on leaving law enforcement to local authorities underscores a fundamental principle of American democracy: the value of localized, community-based governance. Trump's blatant disregard for these pleas showcases a disturbing trend towards centralization of power that is antithetical to the nation's democratic ideals.

The Legal Quagmire

Philadelphia District Attorney Larry Krasner's assertion that Trump has no legal grounds for federalizing the city's police department is a crucial point that cannot be overstated. This legal overreach, should it proceed, would set a dangerous precedent, potentially eroding the very foundations of American democracy by undermining the autonomy of local jurisdictions. It's a stark reminder that the rule of law is under threat, and with it, the checks and balances that prevent the consolidation of autocratic power.

Authoritarianism on Display

Historian Garrett Graff's observations, shared on The New Republic's podcast "The Daily Blast," paint a broader picture of Trump's governance as one marked by a flagrant disregard for expertise and a disturbing embrace of authoritarianism. Trump's actions, as Graff notes, are not limited to political adversaries but extend to any form of critique or dissent, embodying the dangerous adage of l'état, c'est moi ("I am the state"). The invocation of such a historically loaded phrase cannot be taken lightly, as it speaks volumes about Trump's perception of his role and power.

Cultural Dictatorship

Trump's attempts to dictate cultural and societal norms, from museum exhibits to the induction of sports figures into halls of fame, represent an unprecedented overreach into the personal and cultural lives of Americans. This imposition of a singular vision upon the diverse tapestry of American culture is not only antithetical to the nation's democratic values but also a direct assault on the freedom of expression.

Militarization as Occupation

The militarized occupation of American cities, with its increased aggression and hostility towards civil liberties, is perhaps the most alarming development. Graff's description of armed National Guard troops and federal agents demanding identification from civilians in D.C. is a scene more befitting a police state than a democracy. The transformation of public spaces into zones of fear and suspicion, where citizens are viewed as potential threats rather than constituents, is a direct attack on the principles of freedom and security.

Conclusion

The article lays bare the stark reality of Trump's presidency and its leanings towards authoritarianism. The militarization of American cities, disregard for legal and local governance, and attempts to dictate cultural norms are not only alarming but a clarion call for vigilance. It's imperative that these actions are not normalized but seen for what they are: a systemic erosion of democratic principles. As observers and participants in this democracy, it is our duty to question, critique, and challenge the encroachment of authoritarianism in any form. The stakes are too high, and the price of silence too great.
