'It’s going to be really hard': Trump aide panics as voters feel rising costs

As the number of Americans who say the economy is worsening continues to rise, one aide to President Donald Trump is sounding the alarm over the impending midterm elections.

“At the end of the day, what will define the midterms is the administration’s economic success and the perception of it,” said an adviser to Trump, speaking with Axios in its report Friday on the condition of anonymity.

“If the economy is booming and is perceived as booming, it creates a headwind that will be hard for Democrats to overcome. But if it’s booming and it’s not perceived as booming, it’s going to be really hard.”

According to a recent poll conducted by the data analytics firm YouGov, the number of Americans who say the economy is getting worse skyrocketed to 54% as of Nov. 1, an increase of 18 percentage points from when Trump took office in January. The number of Americans who described the economy as poor also increased since Trump took office, from 31% in January to 39% in November.

And yet, despite Trump insisting Thursday that the economy was in good shape, the recent Democratic sweep in Tuesday’s election contests, where Democratic candidates largely campaigned on improving affordability, has caused fear in the White House, Axios reported.

The perception of a worsening economy among Americans has grown so severe that even staunch Trump allies have begun to sound the alarm on how many of Trump’s actions appear to the average voter, actions such as throwing a lavish party while food assistance lapsed for millions of Americans, or flying all over the globe to mingle with world leaders.

“It’s a revolving door at the White House of foreign leaders when Americans are, you know, screaming from their lungs,” said Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), speaking with Axios recently.

Nevertheless, Trump has continued to insist in the wake of Democrats’ victories on Tuesday running on affordability, Republicans still have the upper hand on the issue, and that his party’s only blind spot is that “Republicans don’t talk about it.”

“Affordability is much better with the Republicans,” Trump said Thursday night,ABC News reported. “The only problem is the Republicans don’t talk about it.”

Our Analysis:

The Illusion of Prosperity: Trump’s Economic Mirage

In a striking revelation that underscores the widening chasm between political rhetoric and reality, a Trump aide’s anonymous comments to Axios bring to light the precarious position in which the Trump administration finds itself as the midterm elections loom. The unnamed adviser’s assertion that the administration’s economic success, or more critically, the perception of it, will define the upcoming midterms, unveils a troubling reliance on optics over substance.

The YouGov poll’s findings are a damning indictment of the administration’s economic stewardship, with 54% of Americans now believing the economy is deteriorating—an 18 percentage point increase since Trump’s inauguration. This stark uptick is not just a number; it’s a reflection of the growing disillusionment among the American populace. When 39% of Americans describe the economy as poor, up from 31% in January, it’s a clear signal that the administration’s narrative of a booming economy is increasingly at odds with the lived experiences of its citizens.

Despite Trump’s assertions of economic vitality, the Democratic victories in recent elections, propelled by campaigns focused on improving affordability, signal a potent dissatisfaction and a yearning for change among the electorate. Trump’s insistence on the economy’s good health in the face of these results not only demonstrates a disconcerting disconnect but also hints at a deeper malaise within the administration’s approach to economic policy.

The critique by Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) encapsulates the growing concern within Trump’s own ranks. The imagery of lavish parties and global mingling amid widespread economic disenchantment paints a picture of an administration ensconced in a bubble of privilege, detached from the struggles of the very people it purports to serve. This is not just a failure of policy but a failure of empathy.

Trump’s claim that “affordability is much better with the Republicans” is a masterclass in missing the point. The issue isn’t merely about talking the talk on affordability; it’s about walking the walk. The administration’s inability to recognize the glaring discrepancy between its economic narratives and the harsh realities facing many Americans is not just a “blind spot;” it’s a gaping chasm.

The assertion that Republicans have the upper hand on economic issues, juxtaposed with the administration’s apparent blindness to its disconnect with the populace, is not just ironic—it’s emblematic of a systemic failure to grasp the essence of governance. It’s not enough to claim economic success; the benefits of said success must be tangible to those whom the administration serves.

In conclusion, the Trump aide’s comments and the subsequent analysis reveal a troubling disconnect between the administration’s economic rhetoric and the stark realities facing many Americans. As the midterms approach, this disconnect could very well be the administration’s Achilles’ heel, challenging the notion that perception can trump reality. In the end, it’s not just about the economy booming; it’s about who it’s booming for. The administration would do well to remember that optics can only take you so far when the foundation is crumbling.
