CNN analyst brutally mocks Trump after jobs failure: 'Buck stops way, way over there!'

U.S. President Donald Trump speaks to the press as he arrives at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., July 13, 2025. REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

CNN data analyst Harry Enten raked President Donald Trump over the coals on Friday following the dismal new jobs report, taking the president to task for taking every possible opportunity to blame someone else for his woes while pushing ahead with aggressive tariffs and other policies the business world warns are constraining growth.

"Let's dig into those numbers a little bit, take a closer look at what the jobs report tells us," said anchor Erica Hill. "So, Harry, first of all, give me the big takeaways, my friend."

"Well, I think one of the big takeaways, at least according to the Trump administration, is the buck stops way, way over there whenever there's bad news. It's not their fault. It's somebody else's fault!" said Enten, roasting Trump. "But when you take a look at what we're seeing here, I mean, here's the big takeaway. It's not just the 22,000 jobs that are added in August. It's just been a very weak four months, 79,000 jobs in July. You know, we mentioned the downward number that we saw in June, losing 13,000 jobs, and then 19,000 jobs added in May."

Previously, the president responded to the weak numbers in July by firing the Bureau of Labor Statistics chief, causing a massive outcry among experts who fear a chilling effect on honest statistics-gathering by the federal government.

"I went back through the record books," Enten continued. "The last time there were four months, at least four months in a row where you had less than 100,000 jobs gained in each of those months, you got to go all the way back to 2010, back when I was in college, back during the Great Recession. You know, that's a long time ago. So it's not just this month, it's the last four months. It's an ugly duckling, Erica."

"It certainly is," said Hill. "And how does this compare to President Trump's first term, Harry?"

"So, you know, they were blaming the Biden administration, et cetera, et cetera, et cetera, you couldn't expect a change," said Enten. "But let's compare Trump versus Trump, okay. Median monthly jobs added during Trump so far during the second term, you got 91,000 jobs dating back since January. In his first term, look at this, it was more than double that number, more than 200,000 in the median month added. So Trump is doing, get this, get this, he's adding less than 100,000 jobs this month. He was adding two during this term. During the first term, over 200,000 jobs. So less than half the level that we're talking about."

"So versus himself, he's doing horribly," Enten added.

Our Analysis:

The Art of Deflection: Trump's Economic Quagmire

In a recent scorching analysis by CNN data analyst Harry Enten, the Trump administration's handling—or rather, mishandling—of the economy was laid bare with a precision that leaves no room for doubt or excuse. Enten's critique wasn't just a superficial jab at President Trump's notorious blame-shifting tactics; it was a deep dive into the dismal job growth figures that have characterized the recent months of his presidency. Let's dissect this with the unforgiving clarity it deserves.

The Blame Game

Enten's observations kick off with a pointed commentary on Trump's default strategy: when the going gets tough, the buck gets going—far away from him. This isn't just political banter; it's a critical insight into how leadership, or the lack thereof, shapes economic narratives. Trump's penchant for pointing fingers elsewhere while his policies, particularly his aggressive tariffs, stifle economic growth, is not just a failure of economic policy but a failure of leadership.

The Job Growth Fiasco

The numbers speak louder than any of Trump's tweets or deflections. With only 22,000 jobs added in August and a preceding four months of lackluster job growth, the situation is dire. Enten points out that we haven't seen such a prolonged period of weak job growth since 2010, during the Great Recession. This isn't just a bad month or an off-quarter; it's a trend indicative of systemic economic weakness.

Enten's comparison of Trump's job growth record in his first term versus the current term is particularly damning. A drop from an average of over 200,000 jobs per month to less than 100,000 is not just a downturn; it's a freefall. And yet, the Trump administration seems more focused on rewriting the narrative than addressing the underlying issues.

The Sacking of the Bureau of Labor Statistics Chief

Perhaps the most alarming aspect of this saga is Trump's response to the weak job numbers in July: firing the Bureau of Labor Statistics chief. This move is not just about a change in personnel; it's a direct attack on the integrity of statistical governance. It signals a disturbing trend of undermining professional and objective analysis in favor of politically expedient narratives. The chilling effect on honest statistics-gathering cannot be overstated.

Conclusion

Harry Enten's analysis is a stark reminder of the consequences of leadership that refuses to confront reality. The economic woes facing the United States are not just numbers on a report; they represent real struggles for millions of Americans. When the response to such challenges is deflection, denial, and the dismantling of institutional integrity, the path to recovery becomes all the more arduous.

The Trump administration's economic policies, characterized by aggressive tariffs and a disregard for professional analysis, are not just failing by historical standards—they are failing by Trump's own previous standards. In the face of such evidence, continued attempts to shift blame are not just ineffective; they are inexcusable.

In the end, the question isn't just about how many jobs were lost or gained in a month. It's about leadership, accountability, and the courage to face facts. On all these counts, Trump's record is as dismal as the job growth figures his policies have produced.
