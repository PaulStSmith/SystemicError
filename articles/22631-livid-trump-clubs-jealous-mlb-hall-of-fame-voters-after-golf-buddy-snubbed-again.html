Livid Trump clubs 'jealous' MLB Hall of Fame voters after golf buddy snubbed again

Based on an article by Daniel Hampton

Paulo SantosAugust 27, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Celebrity Grievance as Political Cover

Donald Trump is using Roger Clemens’s Hall of Fame denial as a prop. The immediate story is simple enough: Trump posted on Truth Social demanding that Clemens be enshrined in Cooperstown, attacking the voters who rejected him, while also reviving the Pete Rose complaint and blaming baseball for the mess. The larger story is not baseball. It is power, diversion, and the habit of turning institutional failure into personal grievance.

Power, Not Taste

The people with actual authority in this story are not random Hall of Fame voters and not Trump’s golfing preferences. The relevant institutions are Major League Baseball, the Hall’s voting bodies, and commissioner Rob Manfred, who removed Pete Rose from the permanently ineligible list in May 2025, after Rose’s death, making him eligible for consideration.

Trump’s post pretends this is about merit being insulted by “jealous people.” That is a political trick, not an argument. Clemens was blocked because of the steroid allegations tied to the Mitchell Report and Brian McNamee’s claims, not because anonymous voters woke up and decided to spite talent. Trump strips out the institutional history so he can replace it with ego theater.

The Convenient Scapegoat

The writers and committee members become the perfect target because they are weak enough to blame and faceless enough to attack. That is the move: redirect anger at the gatekeepers while erasing the actual controversy that shaped the decision.

Clemens has never tested positive and was acquitted in his perjury trial, which is exactly why this issue remains politically useful to Trump. The facts are complicated enough to keep the grievance alive and simple enough for a demagogue to flatten into a morality play about unfair elites. That flattening is the point.

Clemens as Political Mirror

Clemens is not being defended here as much as recruited. Trump is not making a case for a baseball player; he is making a case for himself as the kind of man who always believes the system wronged his allies and, by extension, him.

That is why the post matters politically. It converts a sports dispute into a loyalty test. If the institutions reject the star, then the institutions are corrupt. If the commissioner waits until after Rose dies to change his status, then the commissioner is sneaky. In Trump’s world, every decision is persecution unless it flatters the strongman.

The Distraction Works Because It Is Small

The final line of the source says the quiet part out loud: this meltdown lands against Trump and the GOP floundering in approval ratings, a trade war with Canada that “spiraled out of control,” and a war in Iran with no end in sight.

That is the real function of the baseball rant. It is not a detour from politics. It is a method for escaping accountability inside politics. When the governing party is under pressure, it reaches for culture-war garbage, celebrity grievances, and invented enemies because those are easier to stage than competence. The baseball post is not a sideshow accidentally attached to the presidency. It is what a failing political operation sounds like when it needs noise more than results.

The Pattern

This is how authoritarian politics degrades institutions: not always through grand decrees, but through constant contamination. Everything becomes a loyalty drama. Every decision becomes a betrayal. Every gatekeeper becomes a villain. The actual record, the actual process, and the actual consequences get buried under one more performative outburst from a man who treats public office like a personal grievance machine.

The point is not that baseball is important. The point is that Trump’s instincts are always the same: personalize power, weaponize resentment, and make every institution answer to his wounded vanity.
