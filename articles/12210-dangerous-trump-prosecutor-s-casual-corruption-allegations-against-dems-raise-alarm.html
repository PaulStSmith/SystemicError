'Dangerous': Trump prosecutor's 'casual' corruption allegations against Dems raise alarm

In a post on X, Sigal Chattah alleged that the U.S. senators for Nevada were somehow scamming the state because they want fiber optic internet connecting rural parts of the state. Chattah says that the state already has high-speed internet.

"It took me two minutes to understand why our illustrious NV Senators were apoplectic about their $400 million BEAD money being cut when 90% of Nevadans already had broadband high-speed internet," Chattah wrote. "#47 crushed their fiber optic scam in a minute. Nobody makes cheddar on the back end better than Nevada’s politicians. The fruit never falls far from the tree."

She tagged the post with "public corruption."

The funds, which were allocated from the previous administrations, would have connected 43,715 households and businesses, a January release said. The winners of the contracts were named in December 2024, one report explained. Trump has not released the funds, however. Now, Chattah is implying that the funding for the fiber is dead.

Ralston pointed out that "one day after Trump extended her tenure as NV's U.S. attorney and one day after both Democratic senators criticized her as unfit (again), the state's highest federal law enforcement officer casually and publicly accuses @SenJackyRosen and @SenCortezMasto of corruption."

"By the way, this program to provide broadband in Nevada and elsewhere has bipartisan support and has been touted by...the Lombardo Administration," Ralston added, referencing Gov. Joe Lombardo (R-NV). "Maybe the gov should speak up and tell Chattah her allegations of corruption are insane, especially without evidence?"

On Nov. 13, 2023, Chattah accused the same lawmakers of making "their cheddar" off of the Formula One Grand Prix, Ralston pointed out.

She mentioned the "cheddar" again as part of what she claimed is a "stadium scam" on June 14, 2023. She alleged that lawmakers were somehow profiting from a campaign to bring the Oakland A's to Las Vegas. Funding for building a new stadium cost $36 million, which many, including Chattah, opposed. There was no evidence provided to confirm the accusations in any of the social media posts.

"Let’s be frank here… #stadiumscam is all about how much cheddar is made on the back end by those who bring it to town and 'insider' deals on the backs of honest hard working Nevadans. Never have I hoped harder for #NVDems to torpedo an agenda like this one. Stop the #Uniparty perks!" she wrote at the time

Ralston noted it was one thing when Chattah was serving as the National Committeewoman for the Nevada Republican Party, but now she's a federal official while also still holding that political role and continuing to make political accusations.

"It was one thing, albeit outrageous, when Chattah was GOP National Committeewoman, and publicly accused Nevada officials of taking money. But now that she is U.S. attorney (retaining her RNC role), it's much more dangerous. Some past examples include the F1 race and A's stadium," wrote Ralston on X.

In her post, Chattah could use her power to announce she will prosecute both lawmakers, with or without evidence. The same happened in New Jersey, where interim U.S. Attorney Alina Habba indicted the mayor of Newark and a congresswoman. She later dropped the charges against the mayor, however.

Our Analysis:

The Chattah Conundrum: Blurring the Lines of Political and Governmental Ethics

In what appears to be a troubling trend of political figures intertwining their roles with governmental duties, Sigal Chattah, a U.S. attorney appointed by President Donald Trump, has boldly stepped into the limelight with accusations that reek more of partisan politics than of judicial prudence. Veteran journalist Jon Ralston's insights bring to surface a concerning narrative where Chattah, seemingly unfazed by the ethical boundaries her position mandates, has used her platform to launch baseless accusations against Nevada's Democratic senators.

Accusations Without Evidence: A Dangerous Precedent

Chattah's allegations against Senators Jacky Rosen and Catherine Cortez Masto, claiming they are involved in a "fiber optic scam" related to the allocation of $400 million for broadband expansion in Nevada, is a striking example of political vendetta overshadowing governmental responsibility. Her claims, devoid of evidence, not only undermine the integrity of her office but also potentially jeopardize the bipartisan efforts aimed at enhancing Nevada's connectivity infrastructure.

The essence of Chattah's argument, that 90% of Nevadans already have high-speed internet and thus do not require the BEAD (Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment) program, ignores the critical need for rural connectivity. The program's goal to connect 43,715 households and businesses is not a trivial number; it represents a significant step towards closing the digital divide, a goal that transcends political affiliations and benefits all Nevadans.

The Role of a U.S. Attorney: A Misunderstood Concept?

As a U.S. attorney, Chattah's primary responsibility is to prosecute federal crimes and represent the United States in legal matters. However, her actions, as highlighted by Ralston, seem to pivot more towards political activism than legal oversight. Her continued role as the National Committeewoman for the Nevada Republican Party while serving as a U.S. attorney blurs the lines between political partisanship and governmental duty, raising questions about her ability to remain impartial and focused on her judicial obligations.

The Bigger Picture: A Threat to Democratic Norms

What stands out in Chattah's conduct is not just the misuse of her platform but the broader implications it has on democratic norms and the rule of law. When federal officials use their positions to advance political vendettas or engage in baseless accusations, it erodes public trust in governmental institutions and diminishes the perceived integrity of the legal system. This behavior, if unchecked, could set a dangerous precedent where evidence and truth become secondary to political objectives.

Moreover, the silence or lack of strong rebuke from other political figures, including Governor Joe Lombardo, only serves to normalize this intertwining of political rhetoric and governmental responsibility. It is imperative for leaders, irrespective of their political affiliations, to uphold the principles of justice and integrity, ensuring that governmental roles are not exploited for political gains.

Conclusion: The Need for Ethical Clarity

The saga of Sigal Chattah, as dissected by Jon Ralston, is a stark reminder of the fragile boundaries between political activism and governmental duty. It underscores the urgent need for ethical clarity and stringent adherence to the principles that govern public officials. As citizens and stewards of democracy, it is our collective responsibility to hold our leaders accountable, demanding that they fulfill their roles with the utmost integrity, away from the shadows of political biases and unfounded accusations.

In an age where the truth is often a casualty of political warfare, Chattah's actions serve as a cautionary tale of the perils that lie when political agendas overshadow the solemn duties entrusted to public officials. It is a wake-up call for all, demanding a reevaluation of our expectations from those in power and a reaffirmation of our commitment to the principles of justice, fairness, and the rule of law.
