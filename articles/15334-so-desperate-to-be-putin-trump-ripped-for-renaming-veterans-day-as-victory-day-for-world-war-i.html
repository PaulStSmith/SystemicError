President Donald Trump announced Thursday his plan to rename Veterans Day, observed on Nov. 11, as “Victory Day for World War I,” and May 8, commonly recognized as V-E Day, as “Victory Day for World War II." He said the move is meant to enable the United States to honor its accomplishments in those wars.

"Many of our allies and friends are celebrating May 8th as Victory Day, but we did more than any other Country, by far, in producing a victorious result on World War II. I am hereby renaming May 8th as Victory Day for World War II and November 11th as Victory Day for World War I," he wrote in a late night post on his Truth Social platform.

"We won both Wars, nobody was close to us in terms of strength, bravery, or military brilliance, but we never celebrate anything — That’s because we don’t have leaders anymore, that know how to do so! We are going to start celebrating our victories again," the president added.

Victory Day is celebrated annually on May 9 in Russia, and it commemorates the Soviet Union’s triumph over Nazi Germany in World War II. The national holiday pays tribute to the sacrifices made by Soviet soldiers and civilians during what Joseph Stalin called the "Great Patriotic War."

Trump's announcement elicited a strong reaction on social media, with political commentators and journalists expressing surprise that the president has chosen to follow Russian practices.

Political commentator and The Atlantic writer David Frum wrote: "Nazi Germany surrendered in May 1945. For Americans, the Second World War would rage for three more months, until VJ Day in August. But for Russians and those influenced by them, 'Victory Day' falls in May. Trump follows the Russian practice, not the American."

Russian author Nina Byzantina said: "Russia’s Victory Day celebrates the Soviet victory in WWII, in which the Red Army was responsible for approximately 75% of Nazi German losses. As they say, imitation is the best form of flattery!"

Our Analysis:

A Dissection of Trump's Historical Revisionism and Nationalistic Posturing

President Donald Trump's recent announcement to rename Veterans Day as “Victory Day for World War I” and May 8 as “Victory Day for World War II” is a bewildering maneuver that dances on the fine line between national pride and egregious historical revisionism. This decision, purportedly aimed at honoring the United States' "accomplishments" in these wars, is as misguided as it is unnecessary.

The Misguided Renaming of Veterans Day

Let's start with the obvious. Veterans Day, observed on November 11th, is not just another holiday. It is a day of remembrance and respect for all those who have served in the U.S. armed forces. The day marks the armistice signed between the Allies of World War I and Germany in 1918, effectively ending hostilities on the Western Front of WWI. To repurpose this day as a mere celebration of military victory is not only a disservice to the nuanced history behind the holiday but also diminishes the personal sacrifices of millions of veterans.

Ignoring the Allies: A Solo Victory Narrative

Trump's assertion that the U.S. "did more than any other country, by far, in producing a victorious result on World War II" is a stark oversimplification of history. This statement egregiously overlooks the immense contributions and sacrifices of the Allied nations, particularly the Soviet Union, which bore the brunt of Nazi aggression. The Soviet Union's role in the defeat of Nazi Germany, responsible for approximately 75% of Nazi German losses, cannot be overstated. To frame World War II as a unilateral American victory is not just historically inaccurate; it's an affront to the concept of allied cooperation in the face of tyranny.

The Imitation Game: Trump’s Fascination with Russian Practice

The decision to rename these days, aligning more closely with Russian practices, raises eyebrows, especially considering Trump's controversial admiration for Vladimir Putin. The alignment with Russia's Victory Day is peculiar and, as pointed out by political commentator David Frum, deviates from American tradition. It's hard not to see this move as another instance of Trump's peculiar affinity for Russian leadership styles and practices, potentially at the expense of American historical context and values.

The Reaction: A United Front of Disapproval

The backlash against Trump's announcement was swift and severe, spanning a wide spectrum of political and public opinion. Critics have pointed out the absurdity of renaming a day of solemn remembrance to celebrate military victories, highlighting Trump's history of avoiding military service and his contentious relationship with the military community. This decision has been lambasted as an attempt to rewrite history, with a notable emphasis on glorifying war rather than honoring the individual sacrifices made by countless servicemen and women.

Conclusion: A Step Backwards in Historical Understanding

In conclusion, Trump's decision to rename Veterans Day and May 8th as "Victory Days" for World Wars I and II is a perplexing blend of historical revisionism, nationalistic posturing, and a seemingly desperate bid for validation. This move not only misinterprets the essence of these commemorations but also risks alienating allies and veterans alike. It is a stark reminder of the importance of historical accuracy and respect in national discourse—a reminder that seems sorely needed.
