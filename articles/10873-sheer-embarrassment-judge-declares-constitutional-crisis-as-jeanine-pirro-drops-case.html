'Sheer embarrassment': Judge declares 'constitutional crisis' as Jeanine Pirro drops case

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 5, 20254 min read

REUTERS/Annabelle Gordon

Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui said Thursday the country was "past the point of constitutional crisis" after U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro's office dropped the case against a man accused of threatening to kill President Donald Trump.

"Why is the government not out of sheer embarrassment and shame seeking to dismiss with prejudice and expunge the record?" Faruqui asked prosecutors on Thursday after they dropped charges against Edward Dana.

Dana was held in jail for a week after the government alleged he broke a light fixture and threatened to kill Trump while drunk.

"We're past the point of constitutional crisis," Faruqui said, according to CBS correspondent Scott MacFarlane. "We're acting like this is all normal."

The judge accused the Justice Department of "just a rush to get stats on Twitter or Truth Social."

"What's to prevent people from just getting rounded up off the streets?" he wondered. "These are people with names and rights!"

The judge said that "people like Mr. Dana are suffering the consequences" as the DOJ was "losing credibility."

"You created this mess," he told prosecutors, accusing Pirro's office of "too many misfires."

"The government's message to people who look like Mr. Dana is … 'be very afraid!'" Faruqui exclaimed. "I'm afraid right now."

The judge gave prosecutors until 5 p.m. Thursday to file a document explaining how they would prevent the same mistake from happening in the future. Grand juries have declined to provide indictments in at least nine cases brought by Pirro's office since Trump ordered a federal takeover of policing in Washington, D.C.

Our Analysis:

The Theater of the Absurd: Justice in the Era of Social Media Metrics

In a scene that reads more like a Kafkaesque dystopia than a chapter from the annals of American jurisprudence, Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui's courtroom became the stage for a shocking indictment not just of a failed prosecution, but of the entire justice system under the stewardship of U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro's office. The dismissal of charges against Edward Dana, accused of threatening the life of President Donald Trump, morphs from a procedural footnote into a scorching critique of a justice department seemingly more invested in likes and retweets than in upholding the pillars of justice and constitutional rights.

Past the Point of Constitutional Crisis

Faruqui’s assertion that the country is "past the point of constitutional crisis" is no mere hyperbole. It's a chilling diagnosis of a justice system in peril, teetering on the edge of a precipice where the rule of law is sacrificed at the altar of political expediency and public relations. The term "constitutional crisis" is often bandied about with reckless abandon in political discourse, yet here it seems not only apt but perhaps an understatement.

The Spectacle of Shame

The judge’s incredulity at the government's failure to dismiss the charges against Dana "with prejudice and expunge the record" lays bare a spectacle of shame. It's a spectacle where the machinery of justice is not just malfunctioning but is being actively misdirected to serve insidious ends. The question posed by Faruqui, "Why is the government not out of sheer embarrassment and shame seeking to dismiss with prejudice and expunge the record?" speaks volumes about the erosion of ethical standards at the highest levels of prosecutorial power.

A Rush for Stats

Faruqui’s accusation that the Justice Department is engaged in "just a rush to get stats on Twitter or Truth Social" is a scathing indictment of a culture that prioritizes the performative over the principled. This is not justice as envisioned by the framers of the Constitution; this is justice reimagined as a reality TV show, where the number of indictments serves as a scorecard, devoid of any consideration for the human lives entangled in its grasp.

The Cost of Credibility

The judge's lament that "people like Mr. Dana are suffering the consequences" as the DOJ "loses credibility" highlights a fundamental truth: the erosion of trust in one of the most critical institutions of democracy does not occur in a vacuum. Every misstep, every "misfire" as Faruqui puts it, reverberates through the fabric of society, undermining the very foundations upon which the justice system stands.

The Fear Factor

When Faruqui exclaims that "The government's message to people who look like Mr. Dana is … 'be very afraid!'" he is articulating a pervasive and pernicious fear that infects the heart of American democracy. It's a fear of a government that appears increasingly arbitrary in its exercise of power, a government where the whims of those in high office dictate the direction of justice, leaving citizens like Dana in a perpetual state of vulnerability.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability

Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui's courtroom confrontation is more than a moment of legal drama; it's a clarion call for accountability, a demand for a return to principles over politics, and a sobering reminder of the fragility of justice in an age dominated by the court of public opinion. As the judge gave prosecutors a deadline to outline preventive measures against future misfires, one cannot help but wonder: Can the system correct its course, or have we ventured too far into the theater of the absurd? The answer to that question will shape the future of justice and democracy in America.
