Musk has made sweeping cuts to the federal government from his perch as the head of the Department of Government Efficiency, but The Atlantic's David A. Graham told MSNBC's "Morning Joe" that White House budget chief Russell Vought and other authors of Project 2025 were happy to let him take the blame for the deeply unpopular cuts they had recommended themselves.

"You look at the layoffs of federal workers, you see attempts to close the Education Department, it's just point-by-point right from here, and I think the thing that gets missed is people talk about the policies, but it's so much, you know, a scheme for how to rework the entire shape of government, and that's something that I think maybe didn't come through during the campaign," Graham said.

Musk's cuts have proven to be unpopular so far, and host Joe Scarborough asked whether that had caught the administration off guard.

"Washington always wins in the end because we've all seen every administration, we're going to come in, we're going to change everything – the people before us were idiots, we're smarter than everybody else," Scarborough said, "and, like, you go see them a year later, go how you doing? Was the Bush administration really as dumb as you thought they were? Were the Obamas really as dumb as you thought they were? You know, like, are they do you think they're surprised at the pushback that they've gotten from all angles?"

Graham said the pushback did seem to catch the Trump administration off guard, and he said Vought and the other authors were happy to let Musk and his Department of Government Efficiency shoulder the blame.

"I don't know the exact numbers, so I think he is surprised," Graham said, "because he clearly doesn't understand how Washington works, and, you know, Vought is happy to use him as a vessel, get that as far as he can, and then he'll move on to the next thing."

"And cost him $100 billion, $150 billion in the process," Scarborough said, laughing.

Our Analysis:

Elon Musk: The Unwitting Pawn in the Right-Wing Blueprint?

In an era where the lines between political maneuvering and corporate power blur more aggressively than ever, the recent revelations surrounding Elon Musk's role in the Trump administration's deeply unpopular federal cuts unveil a narrative rich in irony, ambition, and, perhaps, naivety. According to David A. Graham of The Atlantic, Musk, the tech mogul turned head of the Department of Government Efficiency, may have been outplayed by the architects of Project 2025, a right-wing blueprint purportedly guiding Donald Trump's second presidency.

Musk's Unpopular Gambit

Elon Musk, a figure synonymous with technological innovation and boundary-pushing ventures, has seemingly embarked on a new, less favorable odyssey. As the spearhead of sweeping cuts across the federal government, Musk's actions have stirred significant unrest and disapproval. But the plot thickens. Graham suggests that Musk's drastic measures are not merely the whims of a tech billionaire trying his hand at government reform. Instead, they are point-by-point recommendations from the playbook of Project 2025, orchestrated by White House budget chief Russell Vought and his cohorts.

The Scheme Behind the Curtain

The essence of this scheme, as Graham points out, is not merely about policy changes. It's a comprehensive strategy aimed at reshaping the entire structure of the U.S. government—a detail that might have slipped through the cracks amidst campaign tumult. The fallout from these measures, including layoffs and attempts to dismantle the Education Department, has placed Musk in the eye of the storm, bearing the brunt of widespread backlash.

Washington's Immutable Law

Joe Scarborough's commentary on Morning Joe underscores a timeless truth about the Washington machine: it invariably emerges victorious. Administrations come and go, each claiming to possess the elixir for America's ailments, yet the establishment's inertia remains unshaken. The pushback Musk has encountered appears to have caught the Trump administration off guard, a miscalculation that Graham attributes to a fundamental misunderstanding of Washington's workings.

Musk: The Expensive Vessel

The narrative takes a darker turn with the suggestion that Musk, despite his vast resources and intellect, has been reduced to a mere instrument in the hands of more seasoned political operators like Vought. Musk's foray into government, costing him billions, is painted as a costly lesson in the unforgiving nature of political machinations.

Conclusion: A Cautionary Tale

This episode serves as a stark reminder of the complexities and treacheries within the political arena. Elon Musk, for all his achievements, may have underestimated the cunning and resilience of the Washington establishment. His venture into the heart of government efficiency, guided by the controversial blueprint of Project 2025, exposes the multifaceted challenges of enacting change within a system resistant to disruption. As the story unfolds, one can't help but wonder if Musk's legacy will be marred by this venture, or if it will merely be a footnote in an otherwise illustrious career. What remains clear is the enduring truth that in the murky waters of politics, even titans can be outmaneuvered.
