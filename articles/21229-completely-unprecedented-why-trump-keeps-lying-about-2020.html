'Completely unprecedented': Why Trump keeps lying about 2020

Based on an article by Amy Goodman

Paulo SantosAugust 14, 20263 min read

The Election Is the Target, Not the Excuse

A federal assault dressed up as election concern

The reporting here is not about some abstract dispute over voting rules. It is about a sitting president using federal institutions to reopen a settled election, intimidate election workers, and build a pretext for intervening in the next one. The Justice Department pursued Fulton County election workers years after the 2020 vote, the FBI fired officials who objected to the political nature of the probe, and the White House forced out the last members of the Election Assistance Commission. That is not administrative housekeeping. It is power being bent toward control.

Power sits with Trump and the agencies he is weaponizing

The actual institutional power is obvious: Trump, the DOJ, the FBI, and the White House. Those are the actors making moves. The judge in Atlanta blocked one overreach. States still hold the core authority over elections, as Ari Berman notes. But that constitutional reality is not stopping the attempt. The point is not that Trump can lawfully seize total control tomorrow. The point is that he is trying to normalize federal intimidation until the illegal starts to feel procedural.

The fraud story is the cover story

Trump’s lie about 2020 is doing political work. According to the reporting, the administration is planning to allege foreign interference, reviving claims already debunked, so it can claim a mandate to seize voting machines, restrict mail voting, and impose proof-of-citizenship barriers. That is the architecture. The lie is not incidental misinformation; it is the predicate for a larger intervention. Calling this confusion or concern about election integrity would be a deliberate softening of what is plainly propaganda.

Election workers are the pressure point

The Fulton County episode matters because it shows who gets targeted when authoritarians want leverage: local election workers, not the people actually manufacturing the lies. Seizing ballots, seeking personal information, threatening arrests, and probing officials years later all serve one purpose. They make ordinary election administration expensive, dangerous, and politically radioactive. That is how intimidation works in practice. You do not need to overturn every result if you can terrorize the people who run the process.

Courts are a brake, not a cure

The judge’s ruling and the repeated court blocks are real constraints, and the Constitution still gives states primary authority over elections. But treating court intervention as proof that the system is fine would be naïve. Trump is already testing how far he can go before a court stops him, then moving to the next lever. The Supreme Court’s recent expansion of presidential firing power makes that game easier, not harder. The institutions are not failing by accident; they are being forced to absorb a deliberate stress test.

What this reveals

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics in the United States does not begin with tanks in the street. It begins with falsehoods, bureaucratic purges, and federal agencies used as intimidation machines against the people who keep elections functioning. Democrats are not the symmetry-dodging foil here; they are part of the institutional barrier against a presidency trying to turn administrative power into electoral control. The Democratic response has to match the scale of the attack, because this is not a messaging problem. It is an attempt to convert government into a tool for entrenching rule.
