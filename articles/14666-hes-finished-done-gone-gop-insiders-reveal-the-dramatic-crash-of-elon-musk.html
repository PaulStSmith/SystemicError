'He’s finished, done, gone': GOP insiders reveal the dramatic crash of Elon Musk

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 20, 20255 min read

Elon Musk speaks during the first cabinet meeting hosted by U.S. President Donald Trump, at the White House in Washington, DC, U.S., February 26, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

“People hate" Elon Musk — and the GOP is getting the message, according to a Politico report.

“He’s finished, done, gone. He polls terrible. People hate him,” said a GOP operative that Politico granted anonymity. “He’d go to Wisconsin thinking he can buy people’s votes, wear the cheese hat, act like a 9-year-old. ... It doesn’t work. It’s offensive to people.”

The operative is not the only GOP member now turning on the billionaire who was President Donald Trump's right-hand man — and who, as the head of the Department of Government Efficiency, oversaw the firing of 200,000 government workers and a mass slashing of government spending.

GOP pollster Frank Luntz said, “The public supported the effort to end wasteful Washington spending, but they did not support the way that it was done.”

He later added, “His mission to cut the waste from Washington was certainly helpful, but the language he used wasn’t.”

Trump ally Sen. Jim Justice (R-WV) put it a little more gently. He called Musk a “patriot” and said the work he did was “good,” however, “We got too close to the fence. We mowed too far,” he said. “We just adjust. That’s the process that’s going on.”

Trump and his administration have also “shifted away from Musk,” according to the outlet.

Advisers to Trump, as well as official White House social media accounts, have stopped posting about Musk.

Even the president has failed to mention him since April, “In February and March, Trump posted about the Tesla CEO an average of roughly four times per week; since the beginning of April, the president hasn’t mentioned Musk once on Truth Social,” they wrote.

Politico asked the White House representatives about Trump’s declining mentions of Musk, and they didn't use Musk’s name in their response.

“The mission of DOGE — to cut waste, fraud, and abuse — will surely continue,” Trump press secretary Karoline Leavitt said in a statement. “DOGE employees who onboarded at their respective agencies will continue to work with President Trump’s cabinet to make our government more efficient.”

Democratic strategist Jesse Ferguson believes it's not about Musk himself, instead, it's about “Musk-ism.”

“He wrote their playbook,” Ferguson said, “and it’s not about theoretical blame, it’s about real-world damage that he and Trump have caused that will be litigated all through the midterms.”

Our Analysis:

The GOP's Musk Conundrum: A Tale of Dissent and Damage

"People hate Elon Musk" — this blunt assessment from a GOP operative, as reported by Politico, slices through the air with the precision of a scalpel, laying bare a festering wound within the Republican Party. The narrative emerging is not just about a billionaire's fall from grace but a broader reflection on the party's flirtation with divisive figures and policies.

The Billionaire Who Flew Too Close to the Sun

Elon Musk, once heralded as President Donald Trump's "right-hand man" and the czar of government efficiency, now finds himself ostracized, a figure of public disdain. The Politico report paints a vivid picture of a man who believed he could charm the electorate with theatrics, akin to donning a "cheese hat" in Wisconsin, only to realize that gimmicks are no substitute for genuine connection. His plummeting popularity is a testament to the electorate's rejection of his approach, summarized with brutal clarity: "He’s finished, done, gone. He polls terrible. People hate him."

The Misguided Crusade Against Government Spending

Musk's tenure as the head of the Department of Government Efficiency is marked by an aggressive downsizing campaign, slashing 200,000 government jobs and cutting deeply into government spending. While the intention to eliminate "wasteful Washington spending" might resonate with fiscal conservatives, the execution has been anything but commendable. GOP pollster Frank Luntz's observation that "the public supported the effort to end wasteful spending, but they did not support the way that it was done" underscores a fundamental misalignment between policy objectives and public sentiment. The language and methodology employed by Musk and his department have evidently alienated many, turning a potentially commendable goal into a source of widespread discontent.

A Party at a Crossroads

The distancing of Trump and his administration from Musk is a telling sign of the shifting sands within the GOP. The cessation of mentions and the absence of Musk from the president's social media narrative since April indicate a strategic retreat, an attempt to disassociate from a figure who has become a liability. Trump press secretary Karoline Leavitt's statement, carefully avoiding Musk's name and focusing instead on the mission of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), is a clear attempt to salvage the department's objectives while abandoning its controversial figurehead.

The Ghost of Musk-ism

Democratic strategist Jesse Ferguson's remarks about "Musk-ism" being the real adversary encapsulate the broader issue at hand. It's not just Elon Musk as an individual but the ideology and methods he represents that have become a point of contention. The "real-world damage" wrought by the Musk-Trump partnership, according to Ferguson, is set to be a focal point in the upcoming midterms, suggesting that the ramifications of Musk's actions and the policies he championed will linger long after his departure from the political scene.

Conclusion: A Lesson in Accountability

The GOP's growing disillusionment with Elon Musk serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of unchecked ambition and the perils of prioritizing efficiency over empathy. As the party grapples with the fallout, the lesson is clear: governance cannot be reduced to mere cost-benefit analysis, and the electorate demands a more nuanced, considerate approach to policy-making. The saga of Musk's rise and fall within the GOP not only highlights the transient nature of political alliances but also underscores the importance of aligning actions with the values and expectations of the public. In the end, the GOP's challenge will be to navigate these turbulent waters, finding a path that reconciles fiscal responsibility with the broader, more compassionate demands of governance.
