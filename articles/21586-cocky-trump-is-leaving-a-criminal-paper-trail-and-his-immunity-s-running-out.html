Cocky Trump is leaving a criminal paper trail — and his immunity's running out

Based on an article by Ray Richmond

Trump’s Presidency Is Being Monetized in Real Time

A Presidency With a Ticker Tape

The reporting here is straightforward enough: Trump’s social media company floated a premium “Trump API” that would give Wall Street traders faster access to his posts, while his own financial disclosures and reported trades show repeated overlaps between his public praise and private market positions. Separately, the White House dumped blame on a teleprompter operator accused of using speech information to bet on Kalshi. That is the surface. The political meaning is worse.

This is not random corruption around the edges. It is an effort to turn the presidency into a price signal, a speculation engine, and a private monetization machine.

Who Actually Holds Power

The institutional power sits with Trump and the office he occupies. He is the one who can move markets with a post, shape permits, reward firms, and decide which interests get proximity. His company is the one trying to sell faster access to his speech output. His staff are the ones describing “strict ethics guidelines” while the president himself is the central breach.

That matters because the article’s core fact pattern is not about one rogue employee or one suspicious trade. It is about a presidency that appears to function as a pipeline from public authority to private enrichment. The money is not incidental to the power. It is the point.

The Useful Scapegoat

The administration’s instinct was to sacrifice Gabriel Perez, the teleprompter operator, as if punishing a staffer erases the structure that made the scheme possible. It does not. Perez may have behaved badly, but the White House response is the familiar move of a corrupt hierarchy: isolate the smallest player, dramatize outrage, and leave the boss untouched.

That is the misdirection. The article briefly notes that Trump did not immediately defend Perez, which is presented almost as a departure from habit. But the deeper pattern is that the president gets to define what counts as scandal, who absorbs blame, and when “ethics” becomes a weapon against expendable subordinates instead of a standard applied upward.

Ethics for the Help

The White House spokesman’s line about “strict ethics guidelines” is the kind of institutional theater that collapses under its own timing. Those rules are apparently very strict when a working staffer can be cut loose. They are much less visible when the president is allegedly moving between holdings, positive posts, and policy promises that overlap in ways any honest ethics system would flag.

The source is right to mock the word “ethics” here, because the administration is not invoking a principle. It is performing one. That performance is meant to create the impression that the system polices itself. In reality, the system is policed selectively, downward, and only when public embarrassment forces the issue.

Market Manipulation as Governance

The Trump API story is especially revealing because it strips away the last scraps of camouflage. The pitch is not merely that people want to read Trump faster. It is that speed itself has value when a president’s posts can shift assets, and that firms are willing to pay for a closer temporal advantage over the public. That is not ordinary media distribution. That is the institutionalization of informational privilege around executive speech.

Combine that with the reported stock purchases and pro-Trump posts tied to companies like Nvidia, and the pattern sharpens: public communication becomes a lever, private positions become the beneficiary, and government power starts to resemble a market-making service for insiders. The presidency is being treated less like a constitutional office than like proprietary infrastructure.

The Pattern Beneath the Scandal

The larger pattern is not just corruption. It is authoritarian privatization of state power. The office is used to enrich the family, flatter capital, punish weak targets, and normalize the idea that public authority exists to generate private gain for those closest to it. The White House can condemn a teleprompter operator. It cannot credibly pretend the rot stops there.

That is the real story. Not one bad bet, not one unethical subscription plan, not one “disgrace” conveniently attached to a low-level employee. The story is that Trump’s circle keeps converting the presidency into a cash register and daring the country to notice.
