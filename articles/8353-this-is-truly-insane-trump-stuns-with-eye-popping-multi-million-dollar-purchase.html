‘This is truly insane’: Trump stuns with eye-popping multi-million-dollar purchase

Buried within a mountain of financial disclosures made public recently is a potentially multi-million-dollar purchase made by President Donald Trump between August and October, a purchase that has left some critics stunned by its lack of news coverage.

“In a normal administration, this would be a huge scandal,” wrote journalist and political commentator Molly Jong-Fast in a social media post on Tuesday on X.

The purchase was, according to a report Tuesday from The New York Times, for between $1 million and $5 million worth of corporate debt of the technology company Intel. The disclosure was published amid the Trump administration’s decision to secure a more than $11 billion stake in the company, making the U.S. government hold 10% of the company’s stock.

“This is truly insane,” wrote journalist Ryan Grim in a social media post on X Tuesday, responding to the news.

Intel is not the only company Trump has personally invested in. Last week, reporting showed that Trump had also purchased as much as $6 million in corporate bonds for the weapons manufacturer Boeing, a purchase made close to the company being awarded a $877 million contract from the Defense Department.

Back in September, Trump also purchased between $500,000 and $1 million worth of Boeing bonds. Since January, Trump has purchased, at a minimum, $185 million worth of bonds.

These personal investments of the president continue to run in tandem with the Trump administration’s unprecedented purchases of company stocks and bonds, which officials say are being carried out in the interest of national security.

“It is an unusual new strategy that has already committed more than $10 billion in taxpayer funds and shows little sign of slowing,” wrote the Times reporter Ana Swanson in a report on Tuesday.

“The government’s growing portfolio of corporate ownership involves minority stakes, or the option to take them in the future, in at least nine companies involved in steel, minerals, nuclear energy, and semiconductors, a New York Times analysis found. The deals were all struck in the past six months, with the bulk made in October and November.

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Intersection of Politics and Personal Gain

In a recent revelation that barely skimmed the surface of mainstream news, President Donald Trump’s financial maneuvers have stirred a pot of ethical questions, casting long shadows over the integrity of his administration. The details, buried in a mountain of financial disclosures, reveal a potentially multi-million-dollar purchase by Trump—specifically, between $1 million and $5 million worth of corporate debt of Intel. This transaction is not just a blip on the financial radar but a glaring beacon of conflict of interest, especially given the Trump administration’s decision to secure a more than $11 billion stake in the company, equating to 10% of Intel’s stock.

The Silence is Deafening

Journalist Molly Jong-Fast and Ryan Grim have raised their voices against this alarming development, pointing out the stark absence of uproar it has generated. In what Jong-Fast describes as a move that would have sparked “a huge scandal” in any other administration, the muted response from the public and media alike is deeply concerning. Grim echoes this sentiment, labeling the situation as “truly insane.”

A Pattern of Questionable Investments

Trump’s financial entanglements do not end with Intel. Reports have surfaced of his investments in Boeing, a company that not only manufactures airplanes but also produces military weapons. Trump’s purchases of Boeing’s corporate bonds, totaling as much as $6 million, coincidentally or not, precede the company being awarded a significant $877 million contract from the Defense Department. Since the beginning of the year, Trump has amassed at least $185 million in bonds across various sectors.

National Security or Personal Security?

The Trump administration has justified these unprecedented purchases of company stocks and bonds as actions taken in the interest of national security. However, this “unusual new strategy,” which has already funneled more than $10 billion in taxpayer funds into the coffers of at least nine companies involved in critical industries, begs the question: Whose interest is truly being served?

Analysis: A Troubling Trend

The pattern emerging from these transactions is not just troubling—it’s unprecedented in its audacity. The intertwining of personal investments with governmental decisions raises profound ethical and legal questions. The government’s growing portfolio of corporate ownership, particularly in companies where the President has personal financial stakes, blurs the lines between national interests and personal enrichment.

In a democratic system where checks and balances are designed to prevent exactly this type of conflict of interest, the lack of significant backlash or investigation into Trump’s financial activities is both alarming and indicative of a broader systemic failure. The silence from major news outlets and the tepid response from political leaders on both sides of the aisle suggest a normalization of behavior that should be anything but normal.

The Verdict

In any other scenario, the revelation that a sitting president has potentially profited from decisions made by his administration would be a scandal of monumental proportions. Yet, in the era of Trump, it seems to be just another day at the office. This normalization of conflicts of interest and the apparent indifference of the public to such egregious acts of self-dealing underscore a dangerous shift in political ethics and accountability.

As we navigate through these murky waters, it is imperative that journalists, political commentators, and the public at large remain vigilant. The integrity of our democratic institutions depends on our collective refusal to accept such blatant conflicts of interest as the new normal.
