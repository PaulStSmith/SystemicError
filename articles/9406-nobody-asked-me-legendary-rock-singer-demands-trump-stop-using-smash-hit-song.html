'Nobody asked me!' Legendary rock singer demands Trump stop using smash hit song

Singer Kenny Loggins has demanded the immediate removal of his greatest hit “Danger Zone” from President Donald Trump’s AI video depicting him dropping fecal matter on “No Kings“ protesters, according to Variety.

Following the massive protests on Saturday, which saw an estimated 7 to 10 million protesters, the president shared a video depicting him in a fighter jet dropping what appeared to be feces on protesters to the tune of one of Loggins’ greatest hits.

“This is an unauthorized use of my performance of ‘Danger Zone.’ Nobody asked me for my permission, which I would have denied, and I request that my recording on this video is removed immediately,” Loggins said in a statement shared with Variety on Monday.

The singer’s “Danger Zone” was used in the 1986 Tom Cruise film “Top Gun,” which is what Trump’s vulgar AI video seems to be paying homage to, Variety says.

“I can’t imagine why anybody would want their music used or associated with something created with the sole purpose of dividing us. Too many people are trying to tear us apart, and we need to find new ways to come together,” Loggins said.

“We’re all Americans, and we’re all patriotic. There is no ‘us and them’ — that’s not who we are, nor is it what we should be. It’s all of us. We’re in this together, and it is my hope that we can embrace music as a way of celebrating and uniting each and every one of us.”

Our Analysis:

The “Danger Zone” of Political Decency: Trump’s Latest Misstep

In what can only be described as a crass and juvenile attempt to mock his detractors, President Donald Trump has once again crossed the boundaries of decorum and good taste. Utilizing an AI-generated video that depicts him as a fighter jet pilot dropping fecal matter on protesters, Trump has chosen Kenny Loggins’ iconic “Danger Zone” as the soundtrack to this puerile spectacle. The imagery, as reported by Variety, is as distasteful as it is bizarre, setting a new low for presidential conduct. It’s a stark illustration of how far the office of the presidency has fallen from the dignified and respectful institution it once was.

Kenny Loggins, the artist behind the unwittingly co-opted anthem, has issued a forthright demand for the immediate removal of his song from this grotesque display. His response, encapsulated in a statement to Variety, underscores a fundamental respect for decency and unity that seems lost on the current administration. Loggins articulates a sentiment many Americans share: “This is an unauthorized use of my performance of ‘Danger Zone.’ Nobody asked me for my permission, which I would have denied, and I request that my recording on this video is removed immediately.” The indignation in his words is palpable and justified.

Unauthorized and Unethical: Crossing the Line

The unauthorized use of “Danger Zone” is not merely a faux pas; it’s a flagrant disregard for intellectual property rights and basic ethical standards. It’s indicative of a broader pattern of behavior that seeks to deride, divide, and demean. Loggins’ masterpiece, forever immortalized in the 1986 film Top Gun, is a song of exhilaration and daring. Yet, its legacy is now tarnished by association with a video created, as Loggins puts it, “with the sole purpose of dividing us.”

Loggins’ Plea for Unity

Kenny Loggins’ response goes beyond mere outrage over unauthorized use. He seizes this moment to remind us of a higher calling: “We’re all Americans, and we’re all patriotic. There is no ‘us and them’ — that’s not who we are, nor is it what we should be. It’s all of us. We’re in this together.” In a political climate rife with divisiveness, Loggins champions the idea that music—and, by extension, all forms of art—should serve to unite us, not be weaponized to deepen our divisions.

A Reflection on Presidential Decorum

The use of “Danger Zone” in such an offensive context is symptomatic of a broader decay in political discourse and presidential decorum. When the leader of the free world resorts to juvenile tactics and tasteless mockery, it not only diminishes the office he holds but also emboldens others to follow suit, perpetuating a cycle of divisiveness and hostility. It’s a stark reminder of the importance of leadership that inspires, unites, and uplifts, rather than degrades and divides.

In a world desperately in need of unity and understanding, the misuse of “Danger Zone” is a call to action for all Americans. It’s a reminder to demand better from our leaders and ourselves. As Kenny Loggins so eloquently puts it, “it is my hope that we can embrace music as a way of celebrating and uniting each and every one of us.” Let’s heed his call and strive for a future where respect, decency, and unity are the cornerstones of our society.
