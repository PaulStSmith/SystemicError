The 14 massive Trump scandals Democrats are totally ignoring

Based on an article by Ralph Nader

Paulo SantosAugust 19, 20263 min read

Trump's Amnesia Machine, Fed by Democratic Cowardice

The source is making a straightforward claim: Trump’s record is a warehouse of abuses, and Democratic leaders keep walking past the fire alarm because they would rather sound refined than effective. The specifics are familiar enough to matter anyway: bankruptcy games, wage theft, sexual abuse allegations, COVID negligence, tax giveaways, climate sabotage, authoritarian election lies, racist cruelty, corporate deregulation, and the DOGE wrecking operation are all presented as evidence of the same political project.

Who Actually Has Power

Trump, not the public, is the central holder of power in this story. He shapes the agenda, sets the lie cycle, rewards loyalists, punishes dissenters, and keeps the GOP running on obedience. The Republican candidates described here are not independent actors with principles. They are ballast. They absorb the blast radius and return it as loyalty theater.

Democratic leadership still has power, too, but it is the power they refuse to use: the power to name the damage plainly, repeat it relentlessly, and force opponents to defend the indefensible. Instead, the source says they keep behaving as if truth is uncouth and memory is optional.

The Source Is Right About the Silence

The basic accusation lands because the political problem is not a shortage of evidence. It is selective recall. Trump’s pattern is not hidden; it is normalized by repetition and then sanitized by opposition timidity. When Democrats decline to say what Trump did, they do not preserve civility. They create room for impunity.

That is the real scandal here. Not that Trump lies. That is his operating system. The scandal is that his opponents often act as if their job is to wait out the cycle instead of prosecuting the case.

What Gets Misdirected

The source correctly blames Democratic leaders for passivity, but the deeper misdirection in American politics is broader: elite institutions keep treating deliberate harm as confusion. Trump’s cruelty is not chaos. It is method. His attacks on FEMA, labor, climate policy, voting systems, public health, children, and federal agencies are not random tantrums. They are a governing style built around punishment, extraction, and selective collapse.

The language of “amnesia” can be useful, but it can also flatter the idea that voters simply forgot. That is too gentle. Memory is being actively managed by money, media habits, partisan discipline, and elite caution. Forgetting is not the whole story. Manufactured forgetting is.

ICE, Brutality, and the State

The source’s discussion of “stormtrooping ICE” points to a harder truth: ICE is not a neutral enforcement body that occasionally needs better leadership. It is an instrument of state terror against immigrants and, by extension, against anyone who can be made vulnerable through bureaucracy and fear. The answer is not reform theater. ICE should be abolished.

Trump’s racism, deportation regime, and selective cruelty are inseparable from that machinery. The point is not just that he is vile. The point is that the state gives his vileness a uniform, a budget, and administrative force.

Democrats Cannot Win By Whispering

The source is most persuasive where it says Democrats should stop acting like Trump’s record is beneath them to repeat. That posture is political malpractice. If a party will not say the words “sexual abuse,” “bankruptcy fraud,” “COVID deaths,” “election theft lie,” “racism,” “corporate corruption,” and “authoritarian punishment” in the same breath as the man’s name, then it is volunteering for defeat.

And there is a harder internal critique here: Democrats do not merely fail to attack Trump. They often fail to build an affirmative democratic case sturdy enough to survive the attack. Memory matters, but so do material commitments. If voters are supposed to choose democracy, they need to see what democratic power would actually defend: wages, agencies that work, child welfare, climate safety, public health, voting rights, and accountable government.

The Pattern

This story is bigger than one man’s lies or one party’s cowardice. It is about how authoritarian politics survives by flooding the zone, then relying on institutional weakness to turn outrage into background noise. Trump does not need everyone to believe him. He only needs enough people, and enough opponents, to stop insisting on what is true.

That is the systemic error: brutality becomes governable when the people with the most reason to name it choose hesitation over confrontation.
