'I'm literally flabbergasted': Rage as Trump deploys sacred Honor Guards at UFC event

Based on an article by David McAfee

Uniforms for the Spectacle

The real power

The story is not that people online got offended. The story is that the state’s ceremonial machinery showed up as set dressing for a private fight promotion staged around Donald Trump’s birthday and his chosen political pageant at the Lincoln Memorial and White House grounds. The people with actual power here were the Trump operation, the Pentagon leadership that allowed military branding to be folded into the event, and the officials who decided that ceremonial service members could be deployed as visual props. That is the decision. The outrage is just the afterimage.

How the stunt worked

The source describes U.S. military honor guards flanking the Lincoln Memorial steps as UFC fighters entered, and separately reports that troops were offered free tickets to the White House event if they met body-composition standards. National Guard members were also stationed at the memorial during construction. None of this looks accidental. It reads like a carefully managed fusion of state symbolism, celebrity combat sports, and presidential self-advertising.

That is the point: not governance, but theatrical capture.

Who gets blamed, and who should

The weakest actors in the frame are the easiest ones to spot: the service members in uniform, the guards stationed where they were ordered, the viewers posting outrage after the fact. The source shows none of them made the decision. Trump’s team did. Pentagon and defense officials enabled it. The event was built to convert military presence into legitimacy, and then to hide that conversion behind procedural language about standards, access, and “events.”

The “no fattys” line is not a side detail. It exposes the logic. The military was not being honored; it was being curated to look camera-ready for Trump’s optics. That is institutional humiliation dressed up as discipline.

Sacrifice turned into branding

The most revealing reaction in the source comes from the military veterans and commentators who called the display a disgrace. They understood the insult immediately: honor guards that are normally associated with fallen heroes and heads of state were repurposed to escort UFC fighters past Lincoln’s statue like VIPs at a red carpet spectacle. That is not reverence. It is theft of meaning.

And the target matters. Lincoln is not background decor. Using his memorial as a backdrop for this promotion is a deliberate act of symbolic appropriation: national mourning, civic memory, and military honor all bent toward a president’s personal performance.

The framing problem

The article mostly treats the episode as an online backlash to a shocking visual. That framing is too soft. The controversy is not that people noticed disrespect. The controversy is that a political and military apparatus allowed disrespect to be operationalized. When institutions cooperate in this kind of stunt, the harm is not confusion. It is intention made palatable through ceremony.

The usual political dodge is to call this “optics” or “controversy.” Those words sanitize agency. What happened here was a coordinated display of authority using the armed forces as a prestige prop for a president’s cult of spectacle.

The pattern underneath

This is the larger pattern: authoritarian aesthetics with civilian costumes. Trump’s politics repeatedly turns institutions into stagecraft, then relies on public exhaustion to make the abuse seem ordinary. Military honor becomes branding. National ceremony becomes backdrop. Public resources become private atmosphere.

That is how democratic institutions decay in public view: not always through open seizure, but through repeated permission. One stunt at a time, officials teach the country that power can use the state’s symbols without respecting the state’s obligations.
