'Undisciplined' Trump is 'on a highwire without a net' after Iran attack: diplomat

According to the former president of the Council on Foreign Relations, reporting that Donald Trump is now fishing about for suggestions on what to do in Iran after he launched a war on the country and killed its leader is yet another example of his “undisciplined” leadership.

And that puts him in a precarious position politically.

Appearing on MS NOW’s “Morning Joe,” longtime diplomat Richard Haas expressed concern that the war on Iran will become open-ended due to lack of planning from the Trump administration and one slip-up could put the president in a no-win situation.

“A word comes to mind for me to describe what’s going on now, it’s undisciplined,” he told co-host Jonathan Lemire. “As disciplined as the United States has been on the military side of this war, we have been undisciplined on the foreign policy and political side. We have yet to make the case to the country, in the world as to why this war had to happen.”

“Now, the president’s been all over the place as to U.S. objectives,” he elaborated. “And in the clip you showed at the beginning of the show, if the complete realization of all U.S. objectives is going to happen before this war ends, this war is not going to end until literally the Iranian regime falls. That’s not something under our control. That might not just be a question of days or weeks, that could be months or years.”

He warned, “And that’s the danger; this could be open-ended. And by not consulting with Congress or the American people, the president has put himself on a high wire without a net below. And likely one of two things are going to happen, Jonathan –– Either he’s going to have to walk back [his] ambitious war aims, or he’s going to lose support. I don’t think history suggests you can sustain support for a war that is costly, and doesn’t seem to be on a trajectory of success.”

Our Analysis:

A Masterclass in Mismanagement: Trump’s Iranian Fiasco

The recent comments made by the former president of the Council on Foreign Relations, as recounted in an article detailing Donald Trump’s haphazard approach towards Iran, underscore a stark and alarming reality of his presidency: a flagrant display of “undisciplined” leadership. This critique not only slices through the facade of strategic decision-making often attributed to administrations but also exposes the perilous implications of Trump’s erratic governance.

The Unraveling of a Coherent Strategy

Richard Haas’s observations reveal a presidency in turmoil, particularly in its approach to foreign policy. The assertion that the Trump administration launched a war and killed Iran’s leader without a clear plan encapsulates a dangerous ad-hoc approach to international relations. Trump’s fishing for post-action suggestions is not only a startling admission of a lack of foresight but also a testament to an administration guided by impulse rather than strategy.

The Fallout of “Undisciplined” Leadership

Haas’s use of the word “undisciplined” is both apt and telling. It reflects a broader pattern of governance characterized by a disregard for established protocols, a tendency to bypass critical consultative processes (notably with Congress and the American public), and a failure to articulate clear objectives. This lack of discipline has not only muddied the waters of U.S. foreign policy but has also positioned the Trump administration on a precarious political high wire, without the safety net of public or legislative support.

The Perils of Open-Ended Conflict

The potential for the conflict with Iran to become open-ended due to the administration’s lack of planning is a grim prospect. Haas rightly points out the danger inherent in such a scenario: the possibility of the war dragging on until the Iranian regime falls—a goal neither feasible on a short timeline nor guaranteed to align with U.S. interests or capabilities. This underscores a reckless gamble with international stability and American lives, driven by objectives that seem to shift with the wind.

A No-Win Situation

The dichotomy Haas presents—between walking back ambitious war aims or losing support—highlights a lose-lose situation for Trump. The historical difficulty of sustaining public support for costly, seemingly unending conflicts is well-documented. Trump’s disregard for this historical precedent not only jeopardizes his political standing but also risks entrenching the U.S. in a conflict with no clear exit strategy.

Conclusion: A Crisis of Leadership

The dissection of Trump’s approach to Iran lays bare a crisis of leadership at the highest levels of the U.S. government. The presidency, under Trump’s stewardship, appears to be marked by impulsivity, a lack of strategic planning, and a dangerous penchant for unilateral decision-making. Such traits are not only incompatible with the responsibilities of the office but also pose significant risks to both American interests abroad and democratic norms at home.

In the final analysis, Trump’s handling of the Iran situation is emblematic of a broader pattern of governance that flouts the principles of accountability, prudence, and foresight—principles that are indispensable to effective leadership on the global stage.
