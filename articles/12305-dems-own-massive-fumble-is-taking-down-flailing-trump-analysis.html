Dems' own massive fumble is taking down 'flailing' Trump: analysis

A "flailing" President Donald Trump has tried to use every tool in his kit to distract his supporters from their demands for the investigation and court documents known as the Jeffrey Epstein "files," a new analysis noted. He's accused former President Bill Clinton and Hillary Clinton of committing crimes. He's alleged that former President Barack Obama should be indicted or arrested. He has called the Epstein matter a "hoax" and a "witch hunt."

Nothing is working.

Alex Shephard wrote in The New Republic that one major reason is that the story isn't coming from Democrats or even from the Democratic Party. These calls are coming from inside his house.

"Unlike with Russiagate, Trump is caught in a web that he and his allies helped spin. The fact that it is a thinly disguised attempt to distract from the Epstein story only damages its credibility further," wrote Shepard. The conspiracy about the elites drinking the blood of children while trafficking them was started by the right-wing fringe, and, like many conspiracy theories, there was a kernel of truth in that elites who knew Epstein were taking advantage of his efforts to find teenage girls he and his friends could rape.

Another problem is that during Trump's first term, numerous individuals worked from within to undermine him. That's not the case this time around.

Today, "the party is clearly and unmistakably marching in lockstep behind him," wrote Shephard. "And Trump’s Cabinet is stacked with arch loyalists who are using their powers to remake the federal government in his image; you won’t find anyone fitting the profile of, say, Rex Tillerson or Jim Mattis. Trump could credibly claim seven years ago that there were forces in the government using their powers to undermine him. He cannot do so now."

Meanwhile, the people Trump is desperately trying to turn into a foil no longer work. Obama is still incredibly popular. While Hillary Clinton is still there, she's largely quiet. Meanwhile, ex-President Clinton is nearly 80 years old, Shephard said.

Even in the case of former President Joe Biden, Shephard described as being in "exile" while he fights a stage-four cancer diagnosis. Trump also spent the last five years accusing Biden of being senile, so accusing him of masterminding some conspiracy to link him to the Epstein files doesn't add up.

The void in Democratic leadership leaves Trump without a target to attack.

"Put another way, the Democrats’ utter lack of leadership is redounding to their benefit in this rare instance: Trump simply doesn’t have a prominent Democratic foil to redirect attention to, so he’s playing old hits that a diminishing number of Americans want to hear," the analysis said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Desperation Dance: A Futile Attempt to Escape the Epstein Quagmire

In a recent piece by Alex Shephard in The New Republic, a vivid picture is painted of a President Donald Trump ensnared in his own web of deceit and distraction tactics. Trump's frantic efforts to divert attention from the Jeffrey Epstein scandal reveal not only the desperation of his actions but also the crumbling facade of his political strategies. Let's dissect this analysis with the precision of a scalpel, exposing the festering wounds of Trump's tactics.

The Inescapable Web: Trump's attempts to shift focus from the Epstein debacle to other political figures is nothing short of a circus act gone awry. Accusing Bill Clinton, Hillary Clinton, and Barack Obama of various crimes, Trump aims to conjure a smokescreen thick enough to obscure his own vulnerabilities. Yet, this strategy is fundamentally flawed. The Epstein saga, with its dark undertones of elite exploitation and abuse, is not a narrative spun by Democrats or their allies. It is a monster of the right-wing's own making, birthed from the very fringe elements that have long propped up Trump's political edifice. The irony is as thick as the walls of a prison cell that Trump might find too real for comfort.

Self-Inflicted Wounds: Shephard astutely observes that Trump's current predicament is markedly different from his past battles. Gone are the days when he could plausibly claim to be the victim of internal sabotage. His Cabinet, now a collection of unwavering loyalists, reflects not a government of the people, but a government molded in Trump's own image—a mirror reflecting back his own distortions. This transformation strips away any veneer of Trump being an outsider, battling the forces of the deep state. Instead, it reveals a figure who has ensconced himself within the very fabric of the government he once claimed to oppose.

The Futility of Finding Foils: Trump's desperate search for a Democratic bogeyman to serve as a distraction highlights his strategic bankruptcy. Obama's enduring popularity, Hillary Clinton's retreat from the public eye, and Bill Clinton's age render them ineffective targets. The attempt to paint Joe Biden, currently battling stage-four cancer, as a mastermind of conspiracy is so ludicrous that it would be comedic if it weren't so tragic. Trump's arsenal of attacks is not just outdated; it's irrelevant—a record player stuck repeating the same track to an audience that has long since left the building.

A Leadership Vacuum to Trump's Dismay: Perhaps the most telling aspect of Shephard's analysis is the identification of the current void in Democratic leadership not as a weakness, but as an unexpected strength. Without a prominent figure to vilify, Trump flails against the shadows, his punches landing on nothing but air. This scenario, as Shephard notes, leaves Trump playing "old hits" to a dwindling audience, a testament to the diminishing returns of his once potent weapon of distraction.

In conclusion, Trump's attempts to navigate the stormy seas of the Epstein scandal with his usual tactics of misdirection and vilification are failing spectacularly. The reasons are manifold, but they converge on a singular truth: you cannot escape the gravity of truth with lies and deflections. The Epstein saga, with its roots deeply entangled in the right-wing ecosystem, represents not just a scandal, but a systemic failure—a failure that Trump, no matter how hard he tries, cannot simply wish away or redirect. It's a harsh lesson in the limits of power and the inevitable reckoning that comes with the pursuit of deception as a political strategy.
