A former GOP strategist urged Deputy U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche on Saturday to get a “pardon from Trump sooner than later” because the president may soon die.

The disagreement began with a report that Dem. Rep. Ro Khanna had mistakenly read the names of innocent men on the floor of the House, as he asked why their names were redacted from the Epstein files released by the DOJ.

“Reps Khanna and Massie pushed the DOJ to unredact a file in the Epstein files. Then Khanna read four men’s names from it in a House floor speech, calling them ‘wealthy and powerful men that the DOJ hid for no apparent reason.’ The only problem is that file is a SDNY photo lineup, and the 16 people on it have no known ties to Epstein, the DOJ confirmed,” Jacqueline Sweet reported. “We identified 12 of the people on the list, and spoke to two of the men Khanna named, who denied any connection to Epstein and were seeking answers as to why their names were in the news. The lineup simply included people apparently arrested for various unrelated crimes over two decades in NYC, who resembled Maxwell and Epstein.”

Khanna responded, saying, “The problem is DOJ illegally redacted names without explanation and then refused to give context for the names once they redacted. This is why Rep. Thomas Massie and I have been pushing for the full release of the files with context and protecting survivors. Thanks for your reporting.”

That’s when Blanche chimed in, saying, “The ‘problem’ is that you didn’t come to us, but immediately ran to X and the House floor and made false accusations about four men, while we were checking the facts.”

X’s community-sourced fact checker said such a lineup “may be redacted with explanation per the law,” but noted that “DOJ failed to provide that context in violation of EFTA.”

Enter conservative activist Rick Wilson, who recently predicted that “accountability is coming in hot” for fellow Trump appointee Pam Bondi. This time, he took aim at Blanche.

“Congress owes you nothing, Todd. DOJ from top to bottom is clearly engaged in a massive coverup and violations of the law,” Wilson wrote. “You should get that pardon from Trump sooner than later. You never know when the actuarial tables will catch up to him.”

Our Analysis:

A Tangled Web of Accountability, Accusations, and Astonishing Assertions

In the latest circus act that doubles as our current political landscape, we’ve been treated to a saga that intertwines the Department of Justice (DOJ), the Epstein scandal, and the ever-controversial figure of Donald Trump. This narrative isn’t just complex; it’s a masterclass in the art of obfuscation and the perilous dance of power and accountability.

The Heart of the Matter: Misreading and Misleading

At the core of this convoluted tale is Democratic Representative Ro Khanna’s blunder on the House floor, where he mistakenly identified four individuals as being part of the Jeffrey Epstein scandal. This error stemmed from a DOJ-released Epstein file, a photo lineup from the Southern District of New York (SDNY), that Khanna misinterpreted. The individuals, as it turns out, had no known ties to Epstein, leading to a whirlwind of confusion and demands for clarity.

Khanna’s defense hinges on the claim that the DOJ’s illegal redaction of names without explanation led to this debacle. His stance, supported by Representative Thomas Massie, calls for transparency and the protection of survivors, a noble cause that, unfortunately, got mired in misinformation.

The DOJ’s Stance: A Questionable Counter

Deputy U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche’s response to Khanna’s accusations was dismissive and defensive. Blanche criticized Khanna for not seeking clarification from the DOJ before airing grievances publicly. This retort, however, skirts around the more significant issue of the DOJ’s alleged failure to provide necessary context for the redactions, a point underscored by X’s community-sourced fact checker. The checker highlighted the DOJ’s violation of the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA) by not furnishing explanations for the redactions, a detail that Blanche conveniently ignored.

Enter Rick Wilson: The Prognosticator of Pardons

Rick Wilson, a conservative activist, wades into this murky water with a rather sensational assertion directed at Blanche. He suggests Blanche secure a pardon from Trump, insinuating that Trump’s demise might be imminent and, with it, Blanche’s chance at absolution. This statement, while eye-catching, epitomizes the current state of political discourse: it’s bombastic, speculative, and distracts from the underlying issues.

The Real Issue at Hand

The swirling accusations, defenses, and counter-accusations divert attention from the critical concerns here: the integrity of the DOJ and the transparency of its operations. Whether or not Khanna’s mistake was due to a lack of diligence on his part or a systemic issue within the DOJ, the public’s trust in these institutions erodes further with each passing scandal.

Moreover, Wilson’s comment about Trump’s pardon power highlights another disturbing trend: the politicization of legal accountability. Instead of focusing on the legality and ethics of the DOJ’s actions, the conversation shifts to Trump’s controversial pardon history, further muddying the waters of justice.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error Indeed

This episode is a microcosm of the broader systemic failures plaguing our political and justice systems. It reveals a landscape where misinformation can lead to misjudgment, where accountability is elusive, and where political figures wield their power in ways that challenge the very foundation of democracy.

As we wade through this morass, one thing becomes clear: the need for a thorough examination and reform of the processes that allowed this situation to unfold. Until then, we remain spectators in a theater of the absurd, where truth is stranger than fiction, and justice is, all too often, just a word.
