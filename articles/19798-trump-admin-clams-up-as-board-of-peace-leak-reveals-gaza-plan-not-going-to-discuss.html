Trump admin clams up as Board of Peace leak reveals Gaza plan: 'Not going to discuss'

U.S President Donald Trump attends the inaugural Board of Peace meeting at the U.S. Institute of Peace in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 19, 2026. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

As President Donald Trump on Thursday held the first meeting of the so-called Board of Peace, the international organization ostensibly set up to oversee the Gaza ceasefire plan, contracting documents leaked to the Guardian provide the latest evidence that the board aims to permanently occupy the area that’s been under Israeli bombardment for more than two years.

The documents detail plans for a sprawling military base with capacity for 5,000 people that’s set to be built over 350 acres in southern Gaza, which is currently under Israeli control as stipulated by the ceasefire deal reached in October.

The base would serve as the headquarters for the International Stabilization Force (ISF), a future military force composed of troops from more than 20 countries that have signed on to the Board of Peace—an effort that has not secured support from a number of major US allies including the United Kingdom, the European Union, and key EU members such as France, Germany, and Spain.

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said at the Munich Security Conference last week that plans for the Board of Peace do not match the original United Nations mandate, which she said “provided for it to be limited in time until 2027, it provided for the Palestinians to have a say, and it referred to Gaza, whereas the statute of the Board of Peace makes no reference to any of these things.”

In Davos last month, Trump’s son-in-law and adviser Jared Kushner unveiled the board’s plans for a “New Gaza,” including areas for “coastal tourism” and residential towers.

The ISF, which has been approved by the UN Security Council, would be tasked with securing Gaza’s border and protecting civilians while training and overseeing Palestinian police forces.

But Diana Buttu, a Palestinian-Canadian former peace negotiator, told the Guardian that the establishment of a massive military base by the Board of Peace—of which Trump has been named as the permanent leader, according to its UN-approved charter—can only be seen as an act of US occupation.

“Whose permission did they get to build that military base?” Buttu asked.

A Trump administration official denied that US military forces will be stationed at the military base and declined to discuss the contracting documents when asked about them by the Guardian.

“As the president has said, no US boots will be on the ground,” the official told the outlet. “We’re not going to discuss leaked documents.”

The plans viewed by the Guardian detail 26 armored watchtowers, a network of bunkers for troops to go for protection, a small-arms range, and a barbed-wire perimeter.

The documents also describe plans to conduct a “geophysical survey” of the site planned for the military base to identify “subterranean voids, tunnels, or large cavities”—likely a reference to tunnels used by Hamas—and includes a “Human Remains Protocol.”

“If suspected human remains or cultural artifacts are discovered, all work in the immediate area must cease immediately, the area must be secured, and the contracting officer must be notified immediately for direction,” reads the document.

The bodies of about 10,000 Palestinians are believed to be buried under rubble across Gaza, according to the exclave’s civil defense agency. Israel’s bombardment of Gaza—which has continued with attacks since the ceasefire was reached last October—has killed more than 75,000 Palestinians and damaged approximately 81 of all structures in the exclave, according to the UN Satellite Center. The agency said in October that 123,464 buildings were classified as “destroyed.”

On Thursday, Trump spoke at the inaugural meeting of the Board of Peace about plans for the body to “strengthen up the United Nations” and “almost be looking over the United Nations and making sure it runs properly.”

He also said the US would be paying $10 billion into the Board of Peace but did not explain how that investment would be funded or whether Congress, which would have to approve the funds, had been consulted.

The meeting, said one organizer with the grassroots group People’s Forum, was a “petrifying projection of imperialist dystopia.”

“Trump and the Board of Peace’s plan,” they said, “is to enact neo-colonialism in Gaza and across occupied Palestine through an Israeli and international police force, and nothing more.”

Our Analysis:

Unmasking the Board of Peace: A Cloak for Occupation

In an audacious move that blurs the lines between peacekeeping and imperial occupation, President Donald Trump’s latest venture, the so-called Board of Peace, emerges not as a harbinger of harmony but as a facade for a more sinister agenda in Gaza. The Guardian’s recent exposé on leaked contracting documents reveals plans that starkly contrast with the board’s ostensibly noble mission to oversee the Gaza ceasefire plan. Instead, what we have is a blueprint for a sprawling military base set to permanently alter the geopolitical landscape under the guise of stabilization.

Military Expansion under the Guise of Peace

The documents detail a military base of vast proportions, capable of housing 5,000 personnel over 350 acres in southern Gaza, a region already ravaged by over two years of Israeli bombardment. This move, sanctioned by the ceasefire deal reached last October, blatantly contradicts the very essence of peacekeeping. It’s a clear indicator of a long-term military presence rather than a temporary peace enforcement mission.

What’s more troubling is the composition of the International Stabilization Force (ISF) which is touted to be a conglomerate of troops from more than 20 countries. However, this initiative has notably failed to secure support from major US allies, including the United Kingdom, the European Union, and key EU members such as France, Germany, and Spain. This lack of consensus raises questions about the legitimacy and unilateral nature of the Board of Peace’s objectives.

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas’s remarks at the Munich Security Conference underscored a critical deviation from the original United Nations mandate, highlighting the absence of a limited timeframe, Palestinian involvement, and reference to Gaza in the Board of Peace’s statute. This deviation not only strips the Palestinian people of their agency but also signals a departure from internationally agreed-upon principles for conflict resolution.

A Neo-Colonial Agenda?

The unveiling of plans for a “New Gaza,” complete with coastal tourism and residential towers by Trump’s son-in-law and adviser Jared Kushner, smacks of neo-colonial ambition. It paints a picture of economic exploitation and cultural erasure, masquerading as development. The establishment of the ISF, tasked with securing Gaza’s border and overseeing Palestinian police forces, further entrenches this neo-colonial narrative, raising alarms about the sovereignty and self-determination of the Palestinian people.

Diana Buttu’s question, “Whose permission did they get to build that military base?” hits the nail on the head, exposing the imperialist undertones of this endeavor. The Trump administration’s denial of deploying US military forces at the base does little to assuage fears of occupation, especially when the leaked documents describe extensive military infrastructure, including armored watchtowers and a network of bunkers.

Ignoring the Humanitarian Crisis

The plans for the military base also disturbingly include a “Human Remains Protocol,” acknowledging the grim reality of the bodies buried under rubble across Gaza. The UN Satellite Center’s statistics on the destruction wrought by Israel’s bombardment, with over 75,000 Palestinians killed and a significant portion of Gaza’s infrastructure decimated, lay bare the humanitarian crisis at hand—a crisis that the Board of Peace seems to blatantly ignore in its imperial quest.

Funding Questions and Congressional Oversight

Trump’s announcement of a $10 billion investment into the Board of Peace, without detailing the source of this funding or confirming Congressional approval, raises significant questions about the transparency and accountability of this operation. This lack of clarity is a glaring oversight, highlighting a potential overreach of executive power.

Conclusion: A Veiled Imperialist Venture

The inaugural meeting of the Board of Peace and Trump’s vision to “strengthen up the United Nations” while essentially bypassing its framework, is nothing short of a petrifying projection of imperialist dystopia, as noted by an organizer with the grassroots group People’s Forum. The plan to enact neo-colonialism in Gaza under the guise of an Israeli and international police force is a stark reminder of the age-old adage: all that glitters is not gold.

In sum, the Board of Peace is not a beacon of hope for the war-torn region of Gaza but a Trojan horse for imperialist ambitions, masquerading as a peacemaker while laying the groundwork for occupation and control. The international community must scrutinize and challenge this endeavor, ensuring that genuine peace, sovereignty, and self-determination for the Palestinian people are not sacrificed on the altar of geopolitical interests.
