According to the New York Times in an article called “What to Know About Trump’s Plan for a Triumphal Arch in Washington,” President Trump “has proposed construction of an arch in the style of the Arc de Triomphe in Washington in the lead-up to the nation’s 250th anniversary.”

His niece, trained psychologist Mary Trump, had something to say about it in a video on Substack.

“Are we still shocked? Really? Are we still shocked that a man as despicable and craven and greedy and grifty and destructive as Donald Trump would be all of the things he is? Are we still shocked that he would have the audacity and the complete lack of self-awareness to announce that he is ordering a triumphal arch to be built and placed at the entrance of the Memorial Bridge that leads from Arlington National Cemetery to the Lincoln Memorial, in honor of the 250th anniversary of the founding of this country?” she asked. “No, no--in honor of him. Are we shocked that he calls it the ‘Arc de Trump?’ That makes me want to die of shame, quite frankly. Although, potential bright side—someday, if we survive, we might be able to repurpose it, so that’ll be fun.”

Mary Trump goes on to argue that, because we know what her uncle will do, it is best to focus on those around him instead.

“We have to shift our focus. Donald is going to keep getting away with anything he can get away with. This is his MO from time immemorial. Push the envelope; see how far he can continue to push the envelope. If he gets away with it, push it further,” she said. “Instead, we have to turn our attention to the people who are allowing all of this to happen. We can be deeply, deeply embarrassed that Donald stood in front of the world with his tiny arches and claimed that the arch was being built for him and named after him right and will be placed right near Arlington National Cemetery.”

Mary Trump further asked, “Who is allowing all of this to happen?”

She also tied in the government shutdown to help make her point.

“Because who can be bothered with the fact that people have been furloughed and aren’t getting their paychecks? who can be bothered that essential workers are being expected to show up to work without pay. Who can be bothered? Donald has a White House to destroy and a ballroom to build and a triumphal arch to put up. He’s busy,” she said.

Our Analysis:

The Audacity of Arrogance: Donald Trump’s ‘Arc de Triumph’

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of egotism, Donald Trump has floated a plan so outlandish, it could only emerge from the depths of his own self-aggrandizement. The proposal? A “triumphal arch” named after himself, the ‘Arc de Trump’, to be erected in Washington in the lead-up to America’s 250th anniversary. This narcissistic architectural endeavor has, unsurprisingly, ruffled feathers, not least of which belong to his niece, Mary Trump, a trained psychologist who has never shied away from criticizing her uncle with surgical precision.

Mary Trump’s Scathing Rebuttal

Mary Trump’s response to her uncle’s latest vanity project is nothing short of a verbal evisceration. “Are we still shocked?” she asks, pointing out the glaringly obvious flaws in her uncle’s character with the kind of weary exasperation that many Americans have come to share. Her words paint a picture of a man “despicable and craven and greedy and grifty and destructive,” a man for whom the concept of self-awareness is as foreign as humility. The ‘Arc de Trump’, as she rightly points out, is not a monument for the nation but a monument to the man himself, a concept so cringe-worthy it threatens to induce mass embarrassment.

Her critique doesn’t stop at the proposed arch. Mary Trump astutely turns the spotlight on the enablers, the silent accomplices in the Trump saga. It’s here that her analysis cuts deepest, challenging the complacency and complicity of those who stand by, allowing Trump’s whims to manifest into reality. The government shutdown and its impact on workers serve as a grim backdrop to her commentary, highlighting the real, tangible consequences of prioritizing ego and legacy over governance and responsibility.

The Enablers: A Focus Shift

Mary Trump’s call to shift focus onto those who allow this behavior is both urgent and necessary. The ‘Arc de Trump’ saga is but a symptom of a larger disease - a political and cultural environment that not only tolerates but sometimes celebrates, the kind of narcissism on display. It’s a potent reminder that the problem isn’t just the man who proposes such self-serving monuments but also the system that doesn’t immediately laugh such propositions out of the room.

The ‘Arc de Trump’: A Monument to Hubris

To propose an arch, especially in the style of the Arc de Triomphe, near sacred grounds like Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial, is to misunderstand (or, more likely, disregard) the profound weight these sites hold. They are places of reflection, mourning, and respect, dedicated to those who have served and sacrificed for the country. To insert oneself into this narrative, to demand recognition alongside true heroes, is an act of audacity that borders on sacrilege.

Conclusion: The Danger of Unchecked Ego

As Mary Trump rightly points out, the danger isn’t just in what Trump proposes but in what it signifies: an unchecked ego, a relentless pursuit of self-aggrandizement at the expense of all else. The ‘Arc de Trump’ may never come to fruition, but its proposal is a stark reminder of the systemic failure to curb such impulses. It’s not just about one man’s vanity project; it’s about a collective failure to prioritize the nation’s soul over individual legacy.

In this light, Mary Trump’s commentary is not just a critique but a clarion call—a call to recognize the deeper rot within our political and social systems that allows such ideas to take root. The ‘Arc de Trump’ isn’t just a proposed monument; it’s a metaphor for the systemic error that pervades American politics and culture, an error that we must urgently correct.
