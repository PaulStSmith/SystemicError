Analyst flustered by ex-Trump official's galling admission: 'Ludicrous on its face'

By Paulo Santos

Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Administrator Lee Zeldin speaks accompanied by U.S. President Donald Trump, at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., February 12, 2026. REUTERS/Jonathan Ernst

A galling admission from a former official in President Donald Trump’s first administration on Sunday left one analyst flustered.

On February 9, The New York Times published a report that included multiple quotes from Trump administration officials who deny that climate change is real. Russ Baker, founder and editor-in-chief of WhoWhatWhy, wrote in a new Substack essay that a quote from a former Trump official at the Environmental Protection Agency revealed that the Trump administration is continuing its efforts to ensure polluters can keep polluting.

Baker pointed to one quote included in the report from Steven Milloy, a former official in Trump’s EPA during his first term.

“We’ve kept the skepticism alive,” Milloy said, adding, “I hope we don’t blow it. I don’t know when or if this opportunity will come around again.”

Baker argued that the inclusion of Milloy’s quote is a sign that the legacy media is not holding Trump officials accountable.

“If that’s what these Trump officials actually believe, it’s ludicrous on its face,” Baker wrote. “But even if that analysis had validity, would that justify ignoring the scientific evidence, or claiming that 99 percent of scientists are wrong or stupid or deluded into participating in a massive fraud?”

“To be clear, he’s not saying this may be our last opportunity to save humanity,” he added. “He’s saying this may be our last opportunity to destroy it. By stopping those who are trying to save it.”

Our Analysis:

A Brazen Legacy of Denial: The Trump Administration’s Environmental Farce

In a revelation that should shock no one yet somehow still manages to appall, a former official from President Donald Trump’s administration has unabashedly admitted to the ongoing efforts to undermine environmental protection in favor of pollution. This galling admission, as reported by The New York Times and further analyzed by Russ Baker of WhoWhatWhy, lays bare the contemptible legacy of environmental policy under Trump’s reign.

Steven Milloy, a name synonymous with climate change denial and corporate interests, nonchalantly boasts, “We’ve kept the skepticism alive,” with a hope that their regressive agenda doesn’t falter. This isn’t just an admission; it’s a brazen declaration of an anti-environment crusade. Milloy’s words are not just a reflection of a misguided belief system; they represent a calculated effort to derail any progress on confronting the existential threat of climate change.

Baker’s commentary cuts to the heart of the issue, highlighting a fundamental and disturbing truth: the Trump administration, and its enablers like Milloy, are not merely skeptical of climate change; they are actively working to ensure that efforts to combat it are thwarted. This isn’t a matter of differing opinions on scientific data; it’s an orchestrated attempt to prioritize short-term profits over the planet’s future.

The audacity of Milloy’s statement – hoping not to “blow it” – speaks volumes. It’s as if the Earth is nothing more than a casino, and Milloy and his ilk are on a hot streak, gambling with the future of humanity. The rhetoric used here is not just irresponsible; it’s a grotesque display of hubris and a profound disrespect for scientific consensus.

Moreover, Baker’s critique of the “legacy media” for not holding Trump officials accountable is a point well made. The fact that such statements can be made without immediate and overwhelming condemnation reflects a troubling normalization of anti-science sentiment within certain political circles. It’s not enough to simply report these statements; they must be challenged and debunked with the full force of factual evidence and moral clarity.

The Real Ludicrousness: To suggest, as these Trump officials implicitly do, that 99% of scientists could be “wrong or stupid or deluded” is to inhabit a fantasy world. The real delusion is thinking that such a monumental consensus could be part of a “massive fraud.” It’s a conspiracy theory unworthy of serious discussion, yet here we are, forced to confront it because of its dangerous implications.

In concluding, Baker’s assertion that Milloy’s comment is essentially a desire to continue destroying the planet is spot-on. This is not hyperbole; it’s an interpretation grounded in the reality of the policies and attitudes espoused by these officials. Their actions and words are not just an attack on environmentalism; they are an assault on reason, science, and the very idea of a sustainable future.

It’s time to recognize that the fight against climate change is not just about carbon emissions and renewable energy. It’s also a battle against a deeply entrenched ideology that values profit over the well-being of the planet and its inhabitants. The Trump administration may have left office, but the toxic legacy of its environmental policies – and the mindset that enabled them – remains a clear and present danger.
