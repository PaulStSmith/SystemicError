Bipartisan giggles over GOP Convention price tags: 'Grist to the mill of the grift'

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A Government Running on Access Fees

The Joke Is the Evidence

The source is brief: a Sunday TV segment mocked the Republican convention in Dallas, where attendees could reportedly pay $25,000 just to attend an event and $35,000 for a photo with Vice President JD Vance. The panel treated it as absurd, and it is absurd. But the real story is not the spectacle. It is the conversion of public office into a premium subscription model while the Trump operation still controls the machinery of government.

Who Holds Power

The people with actual institutional power here are not the pundits laughing about it on television. They are Trump, Vance, and the White House operation that can turn access into a cash product. That matters. This is not some fringe grift on the political margins. It is a governing faction monetizing proximity to power while it still has the state in hand.

That is the part the segment only half-says out loud. The price tags are not merely tacky. They are evidence of who gets to extract value from public office and who does not.

Grift Masquerading as Culture

Calloway’s line that Trump and his White House are “monetizing their public position for personal gain” gets closer to the point than the segment’s joking tone does. The event is not just fundraising. It is a message: loyalty buys access, and access buys profit. The convention becomes a storefront for a political machine that treats official power as inventory.

The money is the point. So is the humiliation. Charging absurd sums to see a vice president or a speaker of the House does not just signal corruption; it normalizes the idea that governing is a VIP lounge for donors and insiders.

Del Percio’s Convenient Fog

Susan Del Percio’s explanation that Republicans are being “roped into attending” because Trump “holds a grudge” is the familiar language of coercion without accountability. It frames participation as reluctant compliance, as if party elites are passive victims of personality rather than active enablers of a system they continue to feed.

That is misdirection. Yes, Trump’s intimidation matters. But fear is not a substitute for innocence. People who keep showing up to protect their standing are not spectators. They are maintaining the apparatus that makes the extortion possible.

The Framing Is Too Small

The segment’s mockery is not wrong, but it is too shallow. Calling the whole thing “nuts” gets the vibe right and the politics wrong. This is not eccentricity. It is institutionalized extraction. A ruling faction is selling access to itself at luxury rates while using the authority of office as the asset being marketed.

The article treats the price tags as a punchline. They are actually a corruption report in plain sight.

What This Reveals

The larger pattern is simple: authoritarian politics does not only seize power; it monetizes it. It turns public office into a reward structure for loyalty, a punishment system for dissent, and a revenue stream for insiders. The people laughing on cable are noticing the vulgarity. The more important fact is the structure underneath it.

A democratic politics would treat this as a scandal of power, not a funny anecdote about bad taste. The Trump operation is not accidentally embarrassing itself. It is showing exactly what it is built to do.
