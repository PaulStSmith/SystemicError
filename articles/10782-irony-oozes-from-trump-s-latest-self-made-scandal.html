Irony oozes from Trump's latest self-made scandal

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20256 min read

Bill Pulte is the head of Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), the agency that oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. He has been in the news recently over his allegations that prominent opponents of President Donald Trump committed mortgage fraud. Most recently, Pulte has put Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook in his crosshairs, claiming that she listed two homes as principal residences on mortgage applications.

Trump immediately used this allegation as a basis for trying to fire Cook, even though the Fed is supposed to be an independent agency outside of the president’s control. Governor Cook sued Trump over his firing effort, and the courts will ultimately decide whether this is within his power.

At this point, it is important to remember that Cook has not even been indicted for anything, much less convicted. We only have an allegation from Mr. Pulte.

It is also worth noting the irony of Trump, who was convicted in a civil trial for putting false information on loan forms, trying to fire someone for listing two homes as principal residences. Among the items that Trump put on his loan form was the claim that his 10,000 square foot condo was actually 32,000 square feet. Perhaps President Trump is offended by the pettiness of Cook’s alleged crime.

While the validity of Pulte’s allegations will have to be determined by the courts, the real scandal is Pulte himself. He is supposed to be running the agency that oversees the processing of tens of millions of mortgages by two huge quasi-public agencies. We are not supposed to be paying him to rifle through mortgage documents to find and disclose dirt that Trump can use against his political opponents.

The media really need to be directing some serious questions in Pulte’s direction.

First and foremost, how did he happen to discover the mortgage abuses that he alleges were committed by NY Attorney General Letitia James, Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA), and now Governor Lisa Cook? Were these “discoveries” the result of random inspections done by agency staff?

Furthermore, was he looking through non-public mortgage files to gather this information?

Also, why did he make this information public when he uncovered it, instead of going through normal channels. If he had followed established procedures, he would have turned over the information to the agency’s inspector general, who would then turn if over to the Justice Department, if they determined it was appropriate. The first time the public would hear about it was when an indictment was issued.

What reason does Pulte have for not following normal procedures?

Pulte really needs to come clean on this.

He should also come clean on his holdings of Pulte Group stock, the huge housing construction company started by his grandfather. It may be the case that conflicts of interest are almost a job requirement in the Trump administration, but many of us still think that government officials should be working for the public, not trying to fatten their pocketbook.

If Pulte helps Trump get his wish and a Trump-controlled Fed lowers interest rates, it would provide a big boost to the Pulte Group’s profits. That hope would give Pulte a strong motivation to try to hasten the day when Trump appointees dominate the Fed’s Open Market Committee that sets interest rates.

Anyhow, there is definitely a big scandal here — but it involves Bill Pulte, not Lisa Cook. The media really need to take notice.

Our Analysis:

Pulte's Political Ploys: A Distraction from Duty

In a dramatic twist that feels ripped straight from a poorly scripted political drama, Bill Pulte, the head of the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA), has taken it upon himself to become the torchbearer of mortgage fraud allegations against opponents of President Donald Trump. The latest target? Federal Reserve Board Governor Lisa Cook. Pulte's allegations have thrown him into a spotlight that reveals more about the political machinations within the Trump administration than any legitimate concerns over mortgage fraud.

The Irony of Trump's Indignation

The sheer irony of Trump, convicted in a civil trial for inflating the size of his condo on loan forms, attempting to fire Cook over allegations of listing two homes as principal residences is palpably thick. One cannot help but ponder if Trump's motivations are less about justice and more about political retribution. The allegations against Cook, thrust into the public eye without any formal indictment, reek of a desperate attempt to discredit and dismantle perceived opposition.

Pulte's Misguided Mission

What we're witnessing with Pulte is a stark departure from the responsibilities his position entails. The head of the FHFA is charged with overseeing the processing of millions of mortgages, a critical role in maintaining the stability and integrity of the housing market. Yet, here we have Pulte, seemingly more interested in combing through mortgage documents for political dirt than in fulfilling his duties. The real question is, how and why did Pulte unearth these allegations? Was this a targeted political witch hunt rather than a routine inspection? The deviation from standard procedure — bypassing the agency’s inspector general and the Justice Department — only adds to the suspicion that Pulte's actions are politically motivated.

Conflict of Interest: The Pulte Group Connection

Perhaps the most glaring issue here is the potential conflict of interest with Pulte’s holdings in the Pulte Group, a massive housing construction company. It's no secret that lower interest rates could significantly benefit the Pulte Group, and one can't help but speculate that Pulte's eagerness to serve Trump's agenda might be fueled by more than just political loyalty. This potential conflict of interest raises serious concerns about the motivations behind Pulte's actions and whether he is acting in the public's best interest or his financial gain.

The Real Scandal

The true scandal here is not Governor Lisa Cook’s unproven and unindicted allegations but rather Bill Pulte’s politicization of his role at the FHFA. In a time when the integrity of public institutions is more important than ever, Pulte's actions serve as a distressing reminder of the Trump administration's penchant for blurring the lines between governance and political vendettas. The media, and indeed the public, must demand accountability and transparency from Pulte, ensuring that government officials are held to the highest standards of integrity and impartiality.

In conclusion, while the courts will determine the validity of Pulte's allegations against Cook, the court of public opinion must scrutinize Pulte's motivations, actions, and potential conflicts of interest. The real story isn't about mortgage fraud; it's about the misuse of power for political ends. It's high time the spotlight is turned back on Pulte, not as a crusader against mortgage fraud, but as a potential architect of a scandal far more concerning and damaging to the fabric of public trust.
