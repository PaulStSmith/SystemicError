'Deep red rural America' hurts most as Trump attacks on liberal programs backfire: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosApril 22, 20255 min read

President Donald Trump's efforts to eradicate federally funded arts and humanities programs may look like an attack on liberal ideals, but a new article argues that "deep red rural America" is being hurt the most — and pushing back the hardest.

That's because the arts and humanities programs Trump is cutting are "vital to the economic revitalization and wellbeing of many rural communities which leverage them in their local economic development initiatives, ranging from tourism to automotive investment," wrote sociology professors Michèle Lamont and Daniel B. Cornfield for Salon.

The professors argued that the arts and humanities organizations "do much more than organizing cultural events: they play a crucial role in fostering social bonds, as well as community and local identity and pride."

Trump's desire to dismantle humanities councils in underserved rural areas "will also have chilling effects on future generations of art-based change agents, who are being trained and educated as budding 'cultural entrepreneurs' in programs that are being shut down," they wrote.

These are the programs, the authors argue, that "have played a vital role in fostering cultural vibrancy in underserved areas."

But rural America isn't taking the attack on arts and humanities programs lying down; the authors wrote that the threat to these vital programs has "galvanized a resistance movement in local communities throughout the nation."

This mass mobilization against the administration's cuts "signal disaffection toward the powers that be and contribute to eroding its legitimacy," they wrote. Academic organizations in red states are pushing back by filing lawsuits and petitioning Congress to protect these "vital independent" federal agencies. And citizens in local communities are writing letters to their local papers, confronting politicians, and staging demonstrations like the massive 'Hands Off Protests' of April 5th.

The authors concluded that the mobilization "is urgent as the National Endowment for the Arts may be next on the chopping block — its budget has been redirected away toward projects that celebrate 'America’s greatness.'”

Our Analysis:

Trump's Attack on Arts: A Misguided Assault on Rural America

In the latest bewildering chapter of President Donald Trump's administration, his efforts to obliterate federally funded arts and humanities programs have been unveiled not just as an affront to what many perceive as liberal ideals but, more strikingly, as a profound betrayal of "deep red rural America" — the very heartland that forms a significant part of his base. This paradoxical situation, where those who might have championed Trump's broader agenda find themselves at the sharp end of his policies, is dissected with compelling clarity by sociology professors Michèle Lamont and Daniel B. Cornfield in a Salon article that cuts through the political noise to reveal an unsettling truth.

Economic Lifelines Cut

At first glance, Trump's crusade against the arts might seem like standard conservative fare — a trimming of what are perceived as non-essential government expenditures. However, as Lamont and Cornfield painstakingly illustrate, this is no mere pruning but a reckless hacking away at programs that are "vital to the economic revitalization and wellbeing of many rural communities." These communities, often sidelined in the broader economic narrative, have found in arts and humanities programs not just a source of cultural enrichment but a critical engine for local economic development initiatives, from boosting tourism to attracting automotive investments.

It’s an irony as rich as it is tragic — that the very initiatives helping to breathe life into rural economies, fostering social bonds, and building a sense of community and local pride, are the ones under siege. Trump's disregard for the humanities councils in underserved rural areas is not just a blow to cultural events but threatens to unravel the social fabric of these communities, stifling the emergence of 'cultural entrepreneurs' who stand at the forefront of art-based community revitalization efforts.

A Groundswell of Resistance

Yet, if Trump's administration believed rural America would silently acquiesce to these cuts, they were sorely mistaken. The article highlights a burgeoning resistance movement, a testament to the deep-seated value these programs hold in local communities. From lawsuits and petitions to letters and demonstrations, the response has been swift and powerful, signaling a clear disaffection towards the administration's disregard for the arts. The 'Hands Off Protests' of April 5th emerge in the narrative not just as a rallying cry but as a potent symbol of this widespread mobilization against policy decisions that threaten to impoverish the cultural and economic life of rural America.

What is particularly galling, and which Lamont and Cornfield underscore with due emphasis, is the redirection of the National Endowment for the Arts' budget toward projects celebrating 'America’s greatness.' This pivot not only underscores a deeply flawed understanding of what truly makes America great but also betrays a dismissive attitude towards the nuanced roles art and humanities play in reflecting and shaping the national character, especially in areas most in need of their enriching influence.

Conclusion: A Misguided Policy with Real Victims

The Salon article, through its incisive analysis, lays bare the stark implications of Trump's policy maneuvers. It's a narrative of unintended consequences, where efforts to dismantle what are perceived as liberal bastions in the arts and humanities inadvertently strike at the heart of rural communities, undermining their economic and social wellbeing. This policy, far from bolstering the prospects of "deep red rural America," serves only to alienate and impoverish, cutting off vital lifelines and dimming the prospects of cultural and economic revitalization.

In the final reckoning, Trump's attack on federally funded arts and humanities programs emerges not just as a misguided assault on liberal ideals but as a profound misstep that undermines the administration's own base, eroding the cultural and economic vitality of rural communities that would have otherwise stood to benefit most. The resistance movement, as chronicled by Lamont and Cornfield, stands as a beacon of hope and a reminder that the value of arts and humanities transcends political divides, embodying the very essence of America's greatness.
