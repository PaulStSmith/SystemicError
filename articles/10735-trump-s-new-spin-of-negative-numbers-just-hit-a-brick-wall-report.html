Trump's new spin of negative numbers just 'hit a brick wall': report

CNN analyst David Goldman writes that President Donald Trump and his administration spent the last month trying to sell his July bad jobs numbers as a big lie planted by Biden sympathizers to undermine his administration.

The president called the July jobs report “rigged,” and fired Erika McEntarfer from her role as Bureau of Labor Statistics commissioner for serving them up. Afterward, officials bent themselves into pretzels trying to explain why Trump was justified in removing McEntarfer for allegedly sabotaging his administration — despite offering no proof of McEntarfer’s claimed villainy.

“That effort hit a brick wall Friday after the government produced updated jobs numbers that painted an even more concerning portrait of the U.S. economy,” wrote Goldman.

McEntarfer is gone, but Friday’s jobs report showed hiring continued to stall in Trump’s economy.

“The number of jobs actually fell in June for the first time since 2020 — all while the revisions the Trump administration so vociferously complained about were significantly less dramatic than in prior months,” Goldman said. “… In other words: The jobs data the Trump administration used to signal a scandal was neither historic nor evidence of corruption.”

The Bureau of Labor Statistics collects jobs data from two surveys, one from old-fashioned door knocking and the other from telephone, internet surveys, and automated data transfer from large corporations. The bureau follows up some information with callbacks to assure accuracy. It revises some numbers for seasonal changes and for low survey responses.

Faced with the undeniable trend, CNN reports the Trump administration has not claimed the August jobs numbers were rigged just yet. Instead Trump is trying to blame Fed Chair Jerome Powell for keeping interest rates high.

“Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer echoed that stance,” reports Goldman. “National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett conceded that the jobs report was a ‘disappointment,’ blaming the BLS’ inability to track summer hiring. He also, without evidence, attributed some of the hiring slump to Trump’s immigration policy, a conclusion that the jobs report does not capture.”

Meanwhile, Trump’s Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick insists, without evidence, that “McEntarfer was rooting against Trump and America’s success, which led to skewed jobs numbers,” Goldman said.

Our Analysis:

Trump's Desperate Spin on Disappointing Jobs Numbers

In a recent spectacle that could only unfold in the Trump administration, we've witnessed an astonishing blend of denial, scapegoating, and outright fabrication in response to the latest underwhelming jobs report. CNN analyst David Goldman provides a detailed account of how President Donald Trump and his team have twisted themselves into knots trying to deflect blame for the apparent economic slowdown under their watch.

The Facts: The July jobs report, which Trump labeled as "rigged," showed a concerning trend in the U.S. economy, with hiring stalling and the number of jobs actually falling in June for the first time since 2020. Trump's reaction? Fire Bureau of Labor Statistics Commissioner Erika McEntarfer, accusing her, without a shred of proof, of sabotaging his administration by presenting these numbers.

The Spin Cycle in Overdrive: Post-McEntarfer's dismissal, Trump and his officials embarked on a clumsy crusade to justify their actions. They claimed, again without evidence, that McEntarfer was "rooting against Trump and America’s success," leading to "skewed jobs numbers." This narrative quickly crumbled when updated jobs numbers painted an even grimmer picture of the economy, making it clear that the data was not the result of some grand conspiracy.

Blame Game: Unwilling to accept responsibility, Trump shifted the blame to Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell for keeping interest rates high, a move that reeks of desperation. Labor Secretary Lori Chavez-DeRemer and National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett joined the fray, with Hassett ludicrously blaming the BLS' inability to track summer hiring and, astonishingly, Trump’s immigration policy for the hiring slump—again, without any supporting evidence.

A Pattern of Deflection: This isn't the first time the Trump administration has attempted to manipulate or discredit unfavorable economic data. However, the audacity to fire a high-ranking statistician and concoct a baseless narrative to explain away poor job numbers is a troubling escalation. It's an attempt to undermine the integrity of independent statistical analysis for political gain.

The Real Losers: Beyond the political theater, the real victims here are the American people, who rely on accurate and impartial economic data to understand the health of their economy. By casting doubt on these numbers and scapegoating career officials, the Trump administration not only damages its credibility but also erodes trust in essential institutions.

In Conclusion: The Trump administration's handling of the July jobs report is a glaring example of its tendency to deny, deflect, and distort reality to fit its narrative. This approach does a disservice to the public and undermines the foundational principles of transparency and accountability in government. It's a dangerous game with real consequences, and Americans deserve better.
