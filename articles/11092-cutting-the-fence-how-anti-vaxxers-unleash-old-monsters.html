Cutting the Fence: How Anti-Vaxxers Unleash Old Monsters

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

This cartoon lays bare the sheer lunacy of the anti-vaccine movement with sharp imagery. A grotesque, slobbering monster labeled “Preventable Diseases” is caged in by a fortress of syringes. The message is simple: science, medicine, and collective action have chained deadly diseases and kept them at bay. Yet, lurking at the edges of this protective barrier is an anti-vaxxer, gleefully cutting through the defenses with scissors. It’s not bravery, not independence, not “questioning the system” — it’s sabotage.

Vaccines are one of the most successful public health tools in human history. Smallpox? Eradicated. Polio? On the verge of extinction. Measles? Once considered a relic of the past in developed countries. These victories weren’t achieved through “natural immunity” or sheer luck. They came through organized, large-scale immunization programs that required trust in science and trust in community. But the anti-vax movement undermines this trust with the same precision as the figure in the cartoon cutting down a crucial defense post.

The consequences are predictable. As vaccine skepticism grows, so do outbreaks of diseases that had been consigned to the medical dustbin. Measles outbreaks in Europe, the United States, and beyond are direct consequences of falling vaccination rates. Who suffers most? Children. Those too young or too vulnerable to be vaccinated rely on herd immunity — the idea that if enough people are immunized, the disease cannot spread widely. Anti-vaxxers dismantle this collective shield, putting not only their own children at risk but everyone else’s as well.

The cartoon exaggerates the monster for dramatic effect, but the truth is not far off. Preventable diseases are monsters. They maim, they kill, they disable. Polio leaves paralysis. Measles can lead to brain damage. Whooping cough suffocates infants. These are not benign childhood rites of passage; they are horrors that previous generations fought tooth and nail to overcome. That someone would willingly cut open the cage and let these beasts roam free again is not just foolish, it’s cruel.

The anti-vax movement thrives on misinformation and mistrust. Social media algorithms feed paranoia, amplifying fringe claims about microchips, government plots, or pharmaceutical conspiracies. But peel back the layers of conspiracy, and what you find is fear weaponized into ideology. Anti-vaxxers see themselves as “rebels” against an oppressive system. In reality, they’re pawns in a deadly game, doing the work of disease by breaking down collective defenses.

Public health has always required a balance between individual freedom and community responsibility. You can smoke, but not in a crowded theater. You can drive, but not drunk. Vaccines are no different: your personal choice has consequences for everyone around you. The cartoon illustrates that truth perfectly — when one person cuts the line of syringes, they create a breach in the wall that diseases can exploit. The monster doesn’t care about your politics, your conspiracies, or your “research.” It only cares about the opening.

The tragedy here is that the tools are at our fingertips. Vaccines are safe, rigorously tested, and continuously monitored. The supposed link between vaccines and autism — the movement’s founding myth — has been debunked countless times. The scientist who fabricated the claim lost his license. Yet the myth persists, stubborn as the diseases themselves. Why? Because fear spreads faster than facts. A viral Facebook post carries more emotional weight than a dry medical journal. And so, the scissors keep cutting.

But history has a way of punishing arrogance. When polio paralyzes a child, no amount of Facebook likes will undo the damage. When measles spreads through a school, no conspiracy theory will explain away the fevers, the rashes, the complications. Reality doesn’t bend to ideology. And the monster in the cartoon doesn’t play politics — it just devours.

This is why the fight against anti-vaxx rhetoric is so urgent. Public health campaigns must counter disinformation with clarity, compassion, and sometimes blunt force truth. Doctors must be supported in educating patients. Platforms that profit from viral lies must be held accountable. And society at large must treat anti-vax ideology for what it is: a reckless act of sabotage against the common good.

The cartoon is satirical, but its stakes are deadly serious. The wall of syringes is fragile if enough people decide their personal “freedom” matters more than collective survival. The anti-vaxxer with scissors doesn’t just endanger herself; she endangers her neighbors, her community, her society. Vaccination is not just a personal choice — it is a civic duty, a moral responsibility, a collective shield against ancient monsters. Cut the chain, and the beast returns.

The world has been here before. We buried children from measles, we feared summer polio outbreaks, we braced ourselves for smallpox. Vaccines gave us freedom from those fears. To dismantle that hard-won progress in the name of pseudoscience and paranoia is nothing short of self-destruction. The cartoonist captures it in one frame: civilization built a wall; the anti-vaxxers tear it down.

The question isn’t whether preventable diseases will surge if the wall falls. They will. The question is whether society will allow the fringe, the misled, and the malicious to cut us back into a darker age.

This isn’t a glitch.
It’s the design.

Written and published by Paulo Santos for Systemic Error.
