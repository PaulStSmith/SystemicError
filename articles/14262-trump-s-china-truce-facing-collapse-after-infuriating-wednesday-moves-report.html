Trump's China truce facing collapse after 'infuriating' Wednesday moves: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:Trump's Trade War Gambit: A Reckoning

Donald Trump (Photo via Reuters)

According to an overnight CNN report, Donald Trump's truce with China in the trade war he created could fall apart after his administration made two unforeseen "bombshell" announcements.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced on Wednesday that the U.S. will “aggressively revoke visas for Chinese students” studying in critical fields or if they have connections to the Chinese Communist Party, an announcement which was met with a wave of anger by families in China, many of whom have saved for years to educate their children abroad.

The second blow came after the Financial Times reported that "moves by US President Donald Trump had effectively cut off some American companies from selling software used to design semiconductors to China."

That one-two punch has reportedly "infuriated" Chinese authorities who felt they had made progress with the volatile Trump as the clock ticks on a 90-day pause on increased tariffs.

According to CNN analysts Steven Jiang and Jessie Yeung, the student visa issue will now become a key sticking point with Chinese authorities and families alike.

"It’s hard to overstate the impact. There were more than 270,000 Chinese students in the US in 2024, and even more before the pandemic. While some hail from China’s political and business elites, many also come from middle-class families," they wrote before adding, "The path to the US is attractive, but arduous. Chinese families save for years and spend exorbitant amounts of money to send their kids abroad, with students attending cram schools or hiring tutors to polish their applications. Rubio’s announcement jeopardizes all of that – with students now facing potential deportation in the middle of their hard-won education."

Our Analysis:

Trump's Trade War Gambit: A Reckoning

In a move that can only be described as both recklessly impulsive and strategically myopic, the Trump administration delivers what can only be termed as a double whammy to its already tenuous truce with China in the ongoing trade war—a war of Trump's own making. The recent "bombshell" announcements, as per a CNN report, threaten to unravel any semblance of progress, highlighting a pattern of erratic decision-making that prioritizes short-term gains over long-term strategy.

Firstly, Secretary of State Marco Rubio's declaration to "aggressively revoke visas for Chinese students" studying in critical fields or those with ties to the Chinese Communist Party is not just a diplomatic misfire; it's a blunder of colossal proportions. This decision, met with understandable fury from affected families in China, not only undermines the cultural and educational exchange between the two powers but also strikes a blow to the United States' reputation as a beacon of higher education. The implication that over 270,000 Chinese students—many from middle-class backgrounds who have invested their family's life savings for a shot at American education—could see their dreams dashed is both cruel and counterproductive.

The second "punch" reported by the Financial Times—that Trump's policies have cut off American companies from selling crucial semiconductor design software to China—adds an economic self-sabotage to the mix. This move not only hurts Chinese enterprises but also cripples American companies that have built their business models around global supply chains and markets.

These actions, described as having "infuriated" Chinese authorities, underscore a gross misjudgment of China's strategic patience and its capacity for retaliation. With a 90-day pause on increased tariffs hanging in the balance, these provocations could very well lead to a renewed escalation in the trade war, with global economic repercussions.

The CNN analysts Steven Jiang and Jessie Yeung aptly highlight the human cost of these decisions, especially the visa revocation for Chinese students. The narrative that the U.S. is an attractive yet arduous path for Chinese families seeking a better future for their children is now tainted with uncertainty and fear. Rubio's announcement not only jeopardizes the aspirations of these students but also signifies a blatant disregard for the mutual benefits of cultural and intellectual exchange.

In summary, these moves by the Trump administration are emblematic of a larger pattern of erratic governance, where impulsive decisions are made without a clear understanding of their long-term impacts. The trade war with China, exacerbated by these recent announcements, serves as a stark reminder of the need for a coherent and strategic approach to international relations—one that values diplomacy and the economic interdependence of the global community. In the absence of such an approach, the U.S. risks isolating itself, not just from China, but from the principles of global cooperation and mutual respect.
