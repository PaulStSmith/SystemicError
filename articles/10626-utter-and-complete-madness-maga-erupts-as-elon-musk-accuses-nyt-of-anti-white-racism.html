'Utter and complete madness': MAGA erupts as Elon Musk accuses NYT of 'anti-white racism'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 11, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Musk Melodrama: A Masterclass in Missing the Point

Elon Musk is seen with a bruised eye that Musk claimed he received at the hands of his son, X Æ A-12, as he attends a press conference with U.S. President Donald Trump in the Oval Office at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., May 30, 2025. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

Elon Musk caused the MAGA movement to erupt on Tuesday after he accused The New York Times of "deliberate" and "comprehensive" anti-white racism.

Musk retweeted a post by Douglass Mackey where Mackey noted that the term "white" is not capitalized, but the term "Black" is capitalized in a NYT story about a high-profile murder case. Mackey argued that the style choice showed that "you are second-class citizens," referring to white people.

"Why is 'white' always in lowercase, but Black is uppercase, @nytimes?" Musk wrote on X. "The NY Times is deliberate, precise and comprehensive in its anti-White racism."

The Associated Press explains that capitalizing the word Black in a cultural or ethnic context refers to the "strong historical and cultural commonalities" shared between people who are Black, such as being discriminated against because of the color of their skin.

"White people generally do not share the same history and culture, or the experience of being discriminated against because of skin color," the AP says.

MAGA commentators and internet personalities piled on after Musk posted.

"Did you know that the AP has an actual “explanation” for this? It’s everything you’d expect…" MAGA internet personality Alex Lorusso posted on X.

"This is why. Check the date," Eric Daugherty, news director for Florida Voice News, posted on X. "BLM and George Floyd is why. Utter and complete madness."

"AP literally says that’s the rule," political consultant Joey Mannarino posted on X. "It’s so racist."

"For the same reason that @nytimes won’t report that 5% of the population commits over 50% of the murders," citizen journalist Nick Sortor posted on X.

"Grok does this too fyi," MAGA influencer Liz Wheeler posted on X.

Our Analysis:

The Musk Melodrama: A Masterclass in Missing the Point

In a recent spectacle that could only emerge from the depths of the internet's most polarizing echo chambers, Elon Musk has once again positioned himself at the epicenter of controversy. This time, Musk accused The New York Times of "deliberate" and "comprehensive" anti-white racism, a claim as absurd as it is misleading. The basis for this accusation? The capitalization of the word "Black" but not "white" in a NYT article.

The Capitalization Controversy

Musk's grievance, echoed and amplified by a chorus of MAGA commentators, hinges on a typographical choice that has a well-documented rationale. The Associated Press (AP), a gold standard in journalistic style guides, explains that capitalizing "Black" in a cultural or ethnic context acknowledges the shared histories, cultures, and the systemic discrimination faced by people who are Black. This explanation, however, seems to have flown over the heads of Musk and his cohort, who interpret this as a slight against white people.

A Deliberate Distraction?

It's hard not to view Musk's latest tirade as anything but a deliberate distraction. By focusing on the capitalization of a word, Musk and his followers are ignoring—or, perhaps, intentionally obscuring—the real issues at play. The AP's decision to capitalize "Black" is an attempt to recognize and respect the unique experiences of Black individuals, a concept that should not be controversial.

The Irony of the Accusation

Musk's accusation of "anti-White racism" against The New York Times is, in itself, a masterclass in missing the point. It's ironic that Musk, a figure who has fashioned himself as a champion of "free speech," is now criticizing a stylistic choice meant to foster inclusivity and respect. Moreover, the claim of anti-white racism is a dangerous and baseless assertion that only serves to stoke the fires of division and misunderstanding.

The MAGA Echo Chamber

The response from MAGA commentators is predictably disheartening. Figures like Alex Lorusso and Joey Mannarino have taken Musk's bait, diving headfirst into a manufactured controversy that distracts from meaningful discourse. The reference to "utter and complete madness" by Eric Daugherty and the misguided correlation drawn by Nick Sortor between crime statistics and stylistic choices in journalism are examples of how facts are twisted to fit a preconceived narrative.

A Missed Opportunity for Meaningful Dialogue

What's most tragic about this entire ordeal is the missed opportunity for meaningful dialogue. Instead of engaging in a reasoned discussion about the nuances of racial identity and representation in media, Musk and his followers have chosen to wage a war against a perceived slight that, in reality, is an effort to acknowledge and rectify historical injustices.

In conclusion, Musk's latest foray into the culture wars is not just a tempest in a teapot; it's a deliberate distraction from the systemic issues that continue to plague society. The insistence on framing the capitalization of "Black" as anti-white racism is not only misguided but also harmful. It's time for Musk and his ilk to abandon these petty crusades and focus on addressing the real challenges facing our world today.
