Miller, who is said to have received a "direct" message from the Supreme Court in its latest ruling against Trump, recently told reporters that the U.S. Constitution allowed for legal liberties to be halted in times of "rebellion or invasion."

"A lot of it depends on whether the courts do the right thing or not," Miller said at the time.

Responding to that news, former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance reminded readers that "Trump doesn’t always win."

"So, when you see stories like the one about Stephen Miller saying that suspending habeas corpus is under serious consideration, don’t accept it as a done deal. It’s a ridiculous, anti-constitutional suggestion from someone who isn’t a lawyer," Vance wrote. "His idea that the writ can be 'suspended in a time of invasion' skips a couple of steps, including who is doing the invading and precisely who can no longer seek the writ—even if there was an invasion of gang members running across the border on Trump’s watch, that would hardly justify suspending habeas for the people who use it the most, prisoners in custody in federal and state prisons."

Vance continued:

"Trump’s plan's success isn’t inevitable, and while taking Trump’s intent to damage if not destroy the rule of law seriously, we shouldn’t hesitate to dismiss some of the ideas his people float for what they are—ridiculous."

Our Analysis:

Stephen Miller's Constitutional Contortion: A Deep Dive into Absurdity

In a recent flurry of bewildering statements, Stephen Miller, Donald Trump's White House Deputy Chief of Staff, has ignited a storm of controversy with his claim that the U.S. Constitution permits the suspension of legal liberties in times of "rebellion or invasion." This assertion, as wild as it is alarming, is a stark reminder of the lengths to which Trump's administration is willing to stretch the bounds of legality and constitutional interpretation to consolidate power and suppress dissent.

A Premise Built on Quicksand

Miller's proposition hinges on a dangerously broad interpretation of the Constitution, suggesting that crises—defined loosely as invasion or rebellion—could justify the suspension of fundamental rights. This is not only a misreading of constitutional safeguards but a leap into authoritarianism dressed in the guise of legal reasoning. His statement, that "a lot of it depends on whether the courts do the right thing or not," exposes an expectation for the judiciary to align with this skewed view, undermining the very foundation of judicial independence.

The Voice of Reason in the Wilderness

Former federal prosecutor Joyce Vance steps in as the voice of reason, cutting through Miller's rhetoric with surgical precision. Vance labels Miller's suggestion as "ridiculous" and "anti-constitutional," a stance that anchors us back to the principles of law and order that the Trump administration frequently seems to float away from. She rightly points out that Trump’s track record in the courts is far from infallible, reminding us that the judiciary has often served as a bulwark against the administration’s more radical impulses.

Vance's critique goes further, dissecting the flawed logic at the heart of Miller's argument. She questions the very premise of what constitutes an invasion, implicitly challenging the administration's portrayal of immigration as a security threat. Moreover, Vance highlights the absurdity of suspending habeas corpus rights for prisoners, who are often the primary beneficiaries of such legal protections, noting that even in the hypothetical scenario of a border invasion by gang members, such a measure would be disproportionate and unjustified.

The Dangerous Game of Legal Jenga

What we're witnessing is a dangerous game of legal Jenga, where Trump and his allies, like Miller, attempt to remove the foundational blocks of constitutional rights and protections to reshape the legal landscape to their advantage. This strategy not only threatens the rule of law but also erodes public trust in the institutions designed to protect our liberties.

Conclusion: The Path Forward

As we navigate these turbulent waters, the importance of vigilance and advocacy for the preservation of constitutional norms cannot be overstated. Joyce Vance’s rebuttal to Miller’s claims serves not just as a critique, but as a clarion call for citizens and the judiciary alike to stand firm against attempts to undermine our legal foundations.

While Stephen Miller's comments may be dismissed by some as the fringe ramblings of a Trump loyalist, they underscore a broader, more concerning effort to test the limits of executive power. As such, they should not be simply brushed aside but recognized for what they are: a blatant affront to constitutional principles and a clear and present danger to the rule of law.

The fight against such anti-constitutional suggestions is not just about countering baseless claims, but about safeguarding the very essence of our democratic system. It's a battle that requires the collective effort of every defender of democracy, underscored by a commitment to fact, reason, and the unwavering belief in the principles upon which the United States was founded.
