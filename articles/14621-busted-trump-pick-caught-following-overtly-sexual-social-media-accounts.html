Trump nominated Billy Long for the IRS role, sparking debate over Long's past endorsement of tax-avoidance schemes, his ties to firms involved in tax credit controversies, and concerns about his qualifications to lead the agency. His nomination is pending Senate confirmation, and he is expected to appear before the Senate Finance Committee on Tuesday.

But a new Rolling Stone report about his X account could complicate things for Long. The report uncovered that his publicly visible list of accounts he follows includes names such as Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, EPA head Lee Zeldin, Elon Musk's Department of Government Efficiency.

But that's not all.

His account "has also recently followed many purveyors of adult-themed content," the report said. "Some post content that is mildly sexually provocative. Other accounts post porn."

The report noted the former congressman's recent account activity "points to broader interest in pregnant women."

"One eye-catching account recently followed by Long’s account goes by the handle 'Pregnant Redhead.' This is not a porn account; quite the opposite. The creator produces content in the genre known as 'trad wife,' which celebrates pastoral stereotypes of strong men and passive women, and posts images with SFW allure," the report said.

Other accounts, however, post explicitly sexual content — including of pregnant women — but do so with account handles that disguise their true purpose. One such account followed by Long has two posts, one of which is a repost of a provocative image from the account “SpicyMoms2.”

A senior Trump senior official told Rolling Stone Long’s account was hacked.

“This is absolute bulls--- and not something the congressman would ever do,” the official said. “Clearly there has been unauthorized access to the account in the 11th hour before his hearing, and we are currently investigating the matter, including reporting it to X.”

Our Analysis:

The Sordid Saga of Billy Long: Trump's IRS Nominee Entangled in Digital Disgrace

In a move that reads less like a strategic appointment and more like a plot twist in a political farce, President Donald Trump's nomination of Billy Long for the role of IRS Commissioner has devolved from questionable to scandalous. This development isn't just a blip on the political radar; it's a glaring spotlight on the systemic rot festering within the nomination processes and the individuals chosen to lead crucial institutions.

A Tangled Web of Controversies

Billy Long, whose past is already marred by endorsements of tax-avoidance schemes and dubious ties to firms embroiled in tax credit controversies, seemed an unlikely choice for the IRS helm from the get-go. However, in a political landscape where qualifications often take a backseat to loyalty and ideological alignment, Long's nomination, though pending Senate confirmation, was not entirely unexpected.

A Digital Debacle Unfolds

Adding fuel to the fire is a recent expose by Rolling Stone, revealing Long's social media follies. His account, far from being a benign digital footprint, has been caught in the act of following providers of mature and sexually explicit content. This isn't just a minor oversight or a harmless personal preference; it's a glaring red flag for someone nominated to lead a federal agency as critical as the IRS.

Explicit Endeavors and Dubious Defenses

The content in question ranges from the mildly sexually provocative to outright porn, with a peculiar emphasis on pregnant women. Among the accounts followed are those disguised under innocuous handles, misleading the casual observer about their explicit content. This discovery not only raises questions about Long's judgment but also about the vetting process for such significant nominations.

In a desperate attempt to salvage what's left of Long's crumbling facade of respectability, a senior Trump official has cried foul, claiming Long's account was hacked. This defense, as convenient as it is implausible, reeks of desperation. The timing of this so-called hacking, conveniently just before Long's Senate hearing, is suspect at best, and at worst, a poorly concocted excuse to divert attention from the real issue at hand.

The Bigger Picture

This saga transcends the personal failings of one man; it's symptomatic of a broader systemic issue. The nomination of individuals with questionable ethics and controversial pasts to positions of power is not new. However, the audacity with which these nominations are defended, even in the face of damning evidence, underscores a troubling disregard for accountability and integrity in public service.

Conclusion: A Systemic Error in Need of Correction

Billy Long's nomination saga is a stark reminder of the importance of due diligence, transparency, and ethical integrity in public office nominations. It's high time we demand better from our leaders and the processes that place them in positions of authority. The IRS, an agency critical to the nation's financial health, deserves a commissioner whose reputation is as spotless as their commitment to upholding the tax laws of the land is unwavering.

The unfolding of these events will be a litmus test for the Senate and the administration's commitment to these principles. Will they prioritize the integrity of the IRS and the public's trust, or will they continue to endorse a nominee embroiled in controversy and scandal? The answer to this question will speak volumes about the state of our political and ethical standards.
