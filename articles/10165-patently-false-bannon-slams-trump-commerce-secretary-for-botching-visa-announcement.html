'Patently false!' Bannon slams Trump commerce secretary for botching visa announcement

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 24, 20254 min read

Steve Bannon. (Gage Skidmore)

Longtime Donald Trump ally Steve Bannon raged over what he said was Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s massive blunder during the administration’s announcement of controversial changes to the H-1B visa program, the system of work permits for high-skilled workers commonly used by the tech industry.

According to The Daily Beast, Bannon saw it as particularly infuriating that Lutnick incorrectly said that the new $100,000 fee for H-1B visas will be an annual charge, forcing other administration officials to come out and clarify it’s in fact a one-time fee.

“These are not tiny details in the document,” said Bannon on his “War Room” program. “These are not in the footnotes. This is the deal. You’re supposed to be a deal guy. You’ve got to understand your own deal that you put before the president.” He added that, “All I’m saying is the Secretary Commerce sat up there and gave not just erroneous information, but patently false information. During the show, in a hot take, I can sit and go, ‘that makes no sense,’ because, if that’s true, then, hey, we kind of won—the whole program, just shut the program down.”

Bannon, who has previously called Lutnick an “unmitigated disaster“ and urged Trump to pull him off of TV, has previously called for the H-1B program to be eliminated entirely — but he is on board with the Trump administration’s change to make it far more expensive.

The H-1B announcement has sent tech companies scrambling, with some prominent CEOs like OpenAI’s Sam Altman singing Trump’s praises in what one observer theorized was a plea to give him input in minimizing the impact of the change.

Lutnick, who has been at the forefront of selling Trump’s trade war, has been at the center of extensive drama behind the scenes at the White House, with reports saying he used Trump’s housing finance administrator to try to dig up dirt on Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, whose job Lutnick had been initially angling for.

Our Analysis:

The Bannon Blowup: A Dissection of Rage and Misinformation

In the latest episode of political theater that could only unfold in the tumultuous wings of the Trump administration, Steve Bannon, the controversial figure known for his fiery rhetoric and political maneuvering, has once again taken center stage. This time, his ire is directed towards Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over an alleged blunder concerning the H-1B visa program. Let’s dissect this with the precision of a scalpel, cutting through the drama to the core facts and implications.

The Heart of the Matter

At the heart of Bannon’s rage is a statement made by Howard Lutnick regarding the H-1B visa program, a critical mechanism for the tech industry to recruit high-skilled workers globally. Lutnick’s faux pas? Incorrectly stating that the new fee for H-1B visas would be an annual charge of $100,000, instead of a one-time fee. While mistakes happen, in the high-stakes arena of political communication, this isn’t just an error; it’s a blunder of monumental proportions, according to Bannon.

“These are not tiny details in the document,” Bannon fumed on his “War Room” program. His frustration underscores a fundamental expectation of competency and accuracy from those in the highest echelons of government. After all, if officials can’t get the details right on their own policies, how can they be trusted to competently manage the country’s complex bureaucratic machinery?

Bannon’s Broader Battle

Bannon’s outburst isn’t just about a single mistake. It’s a manifestation of his broader agenda against the H-1B visa program and, by extension, what he perceives as its facilitation of a system that disadvantages American workers in favor of cheaper foreign labor. Calling for the program’s elimination entirely, Bannon sees the increased fee not as a deterrent but as a potential death knell for the H-1B visa as we know it. His approval of the Trump administration’s move to make the program “far more expensive” aligns with his protectionist views, aimed at preserving American jobs for American workers.

The Political Chess Game

Beyond the surface-level drama, the incident sheds light on the intricate power dynamics and infighting within the Trump administration. Lutnick, who has been a key figure in pushing Trump’s trade war agenda, finds himself embroiled in controversy, not just with Bannon but in an alleged scheme to undermine Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. This isn’t merely a spat between high-ranking officials; it’s a glimpse into the cutthroat nature of political survival within the Trump administration.

The Tech Industry’s Quandary

The fallout from the H-1B announcement has sent shockwaves through the tech industry, a sector heavily reliant on high-skilled immigrant labor. CEOs like OpenAI’s Sam Altman have been put in a precarious position, forced to navigate the treacherous waters of political appeasement while advocating for policies that won’t hamstring their ability to innovate and compete globally.

Concluding Thoughts

Bannon’s tirade against Lutnick over the H-1B visa fee debacle is more than just a moment of political controversy; it’s a microcosm of the broader challenges facing the tech industry, the internal conflicts within the Trump administration, and the ongoing debate over immigration and labor in the United States. As always, the devil is in the details—or in this case, the lack thereof.
