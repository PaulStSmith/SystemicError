At what point are Trump voters finally going to get it?

Almost 100 days into the most loathsome authoritarian regime in American history, and there’s still uncertainty about if and when we call it a dictatorship.

Most Republicans prefer to believe that Trump is merely fulfilling campaign pledges that a majority of voters endorsed. Many Democrats and independents are outraged but prefer to believe we still live in a democracy, albeit one that Trump is testing.

The question I keep asking politicians, political scientists, pundits, and pollsters is what event will trigger their view that we are no longer living in a democracy. To put the question another way: What’s their definition of a dictatorship?

Let me list below the major responses I’ve received. All appear to be growing more probable by the day. Please register your own view in the poll below.

Which of these triggering events would most clearly turn America into a dictatorship?

1. Trump openly and directly defies the Supreme Court. He does what JD Vance has suggested — tells the court that although it has made its decision, it cannot enforce it. This may occur if and when the court pushes the regime to have Kilmar Abrego Garcia returned from the harsh prison in El Salvador to which he was illegally and unconstitutionally sent. Or it could be triggered by a court decision on birthright citizenship with which Trump disagrees.

2. The Trump regime openly abducts American citizens whom the regime alleges are dangerous, without any hearing. ICE has already done this on several occasions, but it has released them once their citizenship has been established. Yet we seem to be getting close to the point when allegedly “dangerous” American citizens are not released, at least not for months or years.

3. The Trump regime launches criminal investigations of Americans against whom Trump seeks vengeance — people such as former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi, former special counsel for the Department of Justice Jack Smith, former President Joe Biden, or anyone else who tried to hold Trump accountable. Trump has threatened such criminal investigations, but none has occurred as yet.

4. The Trump regime directly penalizes individuals or organizations that have criticized it, or explicitly urges Trump supporters to punish any such critic. Here again, Trump and his regime have come close to doing both — consider the penalties imposed on law firms and media corporations that have crossed Trump, or the provocative tweets Trump, Vance, and Musk have posted against perceived enemies — but so far, their efforts have been indirect or made to appear justified by their target’s other alleged malfeasance.

It seems highly likely that at least one of these will tip America into a Trumpian dictatorship. Although some observers believe we’re already in it, most say we’re not there yet but easily could be.

Hence, today’s Office Hours question: What event tips us into a dictatorship?

Our Analysis:

The Slippery Slope to a Trumpian Dictatorship: A Critical Analysis

In an era where the boundaries of democracy are being tested, the article under scrutiny paints a grim picture of a United States teetering on the edge of authoritarianism under the guise of Donald Trump's presidency. It's important, however, to dissect this narrative with a critical eye, not to dismiss the concerns raised, but to understand the complexity and implications of the scenarios presented.

Defying the Supreme Court: A Bold Leap Towards Authoritarianism?

The notion that a Trump presidency could openly defy the Supreme Court is not just alarming; it's a direct assault on the separation of powers that underpins American democracy. The example provided, where Trump might ignore a court decision on a matter as significant as birthright citizenship or the case of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, pushes the boundaries of executive power into dangerous territory. However, the leap from potential to action is a significant one, and while the threat is real, the transition to dictatorship hinges not just on defiance but on the systemic failure to hold such actions accountable.

Abductions and the Erosion of Civil Liberties

The article's second trigger point, involving the abduction of American citizens under dubious pretenses, outlines a scenario where ICE oversteps its bounds, transforming from an immigration enforcement agency into a tool of political repression. This scenario is particularly chilling because it suggests a breakdown of legal protections for citizens. Yet, the distinction between isolated incidents and a systematic policy of repression is crucial. While the fear is justified, the leap to labeling this as a step towards dictatorship requires evidence of widespread, unchecked government sanction of such actions.

Political Vengeance and the Misuse of the Justice System

The threat of launching criminal investigations against political adversaries is a hallmark of authoritarian regimes. The article rightly points out that threats have been made, but actions speak louder than threats. The initiation of such investigations without merit would represent a significant abuse of power, yet as of now, these remain hypothetical. The critical issue here is the independence of the judiciary and law enforcement - so long as these institutions maintain their integrity, the slide into dictatorship can be arrested.

Penalizing Dissent: The Ultimate Red Flag

The final scenario - directly penalizing critics of the regime - is perhaps the most tangible indicator of a drift towards authoritarianism. The article notes instances where Trump and his allies have targeted law firms, media corporations, and individuals, yet it acknowledges that these efforts have been indirect. The distinction between criticism and retaliation is foundational to a free society. Direct, systematic, and unapologetic punishment of dissent would not just tip the balance towards dictatorship; it would signal its arrival.

Conclusion: Vigilance is Key

While the scenarios outlined in the article present a dystopian vision of a future under Trump, it's essential to differentiate between potential threats and actual steps towards dictatorship. The strength of American democracy lies in its institutions and the checks and balances they provide. The true test is not in the threats posed, but in the resilience of these institutions and the public's willingness to defend democratic principles.

The call to action, then, is not just to recognize the signs of authoritarianism but to actively engage in safeguarding democracy through participation, critique, and holding power to account. The scenarios presented are concerning, but the response to these threats will define the future of American democracy.
