GOP crisis deepens as Trump throws party under the bus for his failures

Based on an article by Sarah K. Burris

Paulo SantosAugust 29, 20262 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The SetupThe Real PowerBlame as StrategyThe Mask SlipsDemocrats Should Not Confuse This With Safety

Trump Blames the Party He Controls

The Setup

Trump is not being described here as a passive beneficiary of Republican mistakes. The source makes the power map plain: he is the central decision-maker, and the Republican Party is the structure that has spent years obeying him. The immediate dispute is not over ideology. It is over who gets blamed when the bill comes due.

The reporting centers on Trump reposting an anti-GOP screed, then moving to a self-promotional ad push. That is the whole trick in miniature: set the terms, generate the damage, and then redirect the fallout toward subordinates.

The Real Power

Congressional Republicans did not accidentally wander into this. They followed Trump’s agenda, absorbed his priorities, and treated loyalty as governance. That is why the attempt to pin failure on the party is so revealing. Trump is not attacking an independent governing coalition. He is disciplining a dependent one.

The analysts quoted in the source point to the actual drivers of political pain: tariffs that raised prices, a war no one wanted, debt, and the wreckage across federal agencies. Those are not communication failures. They are policy choices with consequences. Trump owns the choices.

Blame as Strategy

Trump’s move is not confusion; it is scapegoating before the loss. First he invents fraud when elections go badly. Then he blames Republicans for not being loyal enough. At no point does he admit that his own agenda produced the conditions voters are reacting to.

That matters because the blame shift is not just rhetorical. It is operational. If MAGA voters are told Republicans betrayed Trump, then turnout can collapse where Republicans most need it. The source is right to note the danger in that maneuver. Trump may be trying to save himself by weakening the very candidates who still carry his brand.

The Mask Slips

The “golden age” talk collapses the moment it meets the actual record described here. Trump wants credit for prosperity while advertising the political costs of tariffs, chaos, and instability. That is not a contradiction by accident. It is the defining method of reactionary power: celebrate the fantasy, outsource the damage, and punish anyone who names the damage plainly.

The Republican Party helped build this arrangement. It traded institutional judgment for obedience, then discovered that obedience did not buy protection. Now Trump is treating his own coalition like a liability ledger. That is what authoritarian politics does to parties: it empties them of agency until they become disposable.

Democrats Should Not Confuse This With Safety

The source is careful not to pretend Republicans are the only force in play, and that caution is warranted. Even with Trump undercutting his own side, close Senate races, gerrymandering, and suppressive voting confusion still shape the battlefield. Republican dysfunction is not the same thing as democratic security.

But the larger lesson is sharper than a simple midterm forecast. Trump’s politics runs on domination without accountability: he makes the calls, others absorb the blame, and institutions are expected to absorb the wreckage. The Republican Party consented to that model. Now it is discovering the basic rule of strongman politics: loyalty is demanded endlessly and rewarded conditionally, if at all.
