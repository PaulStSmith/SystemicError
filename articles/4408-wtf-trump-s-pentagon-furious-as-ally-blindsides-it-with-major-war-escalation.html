'WTF?' Trump's Pentagon furious as ally blindsides it with major war escalation

By Paulo Santos

Pentagon war planners were blindsided by the scope of Israeli strikes on Iran, sparking the first significant rift between the U.S. and Israeli militaries since the conflict began eight days ago.

According to Axios, the Israeli Defense Forces destroyed an estimated 30 fuel depots during the offensive Saturday — far more extensive than what U.S. military officials expected when Israel provided advance notice of the strikes.

A senior U.S. official bluntly stated, “We don’t think it was a good idea.” An Israeli official characterized the American reaction as: “WTF?”

Pentagon strategists are concerned that destroying infrastructure serving ordinary Iranians could backfire strategically, potentially unifying Iranian society behind the regime while simultaneously driving global oil prices higher — a political liability Trump desperately wants to avoid.

“The president doesn’t like the attack. He wants to save the oil. He doesn’t want to burn it. And it reminds people of higher gas prices,” a Trump adviser told Axios.

The scale of the destruction has alarmed even Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC), a consistent war supporter. Graham posted on X: “Please be cautious about what targets you select. Our goal is to liberate the Iranian people in a fashion that does not cripple their chance to start a new and better life when this regime collapses. The oil economy of Iran will be essential to that endeavor.”

The disagreement between the allies is expected to be addressed at senior political levels as both nations confront the unintended consequences of the escalating air campaign.

Our Analysis:

Analysis: Blindsided Allies and the Shadow of Strategic Misfires

In a revelation that should surprise precisely no one paying attention to the volatile dynamics of international politics, the Pentagon found itself with its proverbial pants down as Israel’s latest military offensive in Iran exceeded U.S. expectations in both scope and intensity. The incident has laid bare the first significant fissure between the U.S. and Israeli militaries since the onset of hostilities eight days ago, according to Axios. This discord underscores a critical miscalculation in the delicate dance of diplomacy and military strategy that binds these two nations together.

The Scope of Surprises

Israeli Defense Forces’ decision to obliterate an estimated 30 fuel depots not only exceeded the anticipations set by previous briefings but also ventured into the realm of strategic folly. A senior U.S. official’s response, “We don’t think it was a good idea,” drips with the understatement often characteristic of diplomatic speak. Meanwhile, an Israeli official’s alleged reaction, “WTF?” encapsulates the bewilderment and frustration brewing between the allies. This exchange of sentiments is emblematic of a deeper discord, potentially fracturing the iron-clad facade of U.S.-Israeli military coordination.

Strategic Backfires and Political Liabilities

Pentagon planners’ concerns that the strikes could unify Iranian society against external aggression, while simultaneously inflating global oil prices, are not just valid but glaringly obvious to even the most amateur of strategists. The political ramifications of such outcomes cannot be understated, especially within the context of the Trump administration’s priorities. A Trump adviser’s comment to Axios, highlighting the president’s aversion to actions that might remind voters of higher gas prices, reveals the crux of the issue: the intersection of military action and domestic political repercussions.

Sen. Lindsey Graham’s Cautious Chorus

Sen. Lindsey Graham’s (R-SC) cautionary stance, conveyed through a post on X, strikes a rare note of prudence from a figure known for his hawkish views. Graham’s plea for restraint underscores the precarious balancing act of achieving regime change without demolishing the very infrastructure necessary for Iran’s post-regime recovery. His warning not only speaks to the strategic considerations at play but also to the humanitarian implications of decimating a nation’s economy under the guise of liberation.

The Path Forward

As the allies grapple with the “unintended consequences” of their escalating air campaign, the resolution of this disagreement at senior political levels becomes imperative. The crux of the matter isn’t merely about a single incident of overreach but rather the broader implications of such actions on the delicate tapestry of international relations, regional stability, and global oil markets.

The unfolding saga between the U.S. and Israel serves as a stark reminder of the complexities inherent in military alliances, particularly when they are tested by the fog of war. It also raises critical questions about the endgame of such conflicts and the means by which allies communicate, coordinate, and, ultimately, execute their military strategies.

In conclusion, this incident is not just a blip on the radar of U.S.-Israeli relations but a symptom of a larger, systemic issue that demands a reassessment of how military actions are conceptualized, communicated, and carried out on the international stage. The repercussions of failing to do so could extend far beyond the current conflict, shaping the geopolitical landscape for years to come.
