Questions raised as Biden impeachment witness goes missing from prison after lying to FBI

An FBI informant tied to a fake bribery scheme against the Biden family has been released from prison, raising concerns that President Donald Trump could pardon him.

Alexander Smirnov pleaded guilty to making up a story that former President Joe Biden and his son Hunter had gotten millions of dollars in bribes from a Ukrainian energy company, but journalist Jacqueline Sweet reported on her Disaster Girl Substack page that he’s apparently no longer serving a six-year prison term imposed days before Trump returned to office.

“Despite Smirnov being judged a flight risk, with ties to Russian intelligence and foreign assets, the U.S.-Israeli citizen has been mysteriously missing from prison for at least the past four months,” Sweet reported. “Following reports in April that the Trump administration was reviewing the case, concerns have now arisen that the president is considering an imminent pardon.”

“Smirnov had been serving his sentence at FCI Terminal Island, a low-security prison in Los Angeles,” the report added. “He is still listed at FCI Terminal Island on the Bureau of Prisons website, with a release date of February 2029. But while no filings on the docket for his appeal indicate how or why his release was ordered, he has not been there since at least July.”

A process server found Smirnov was no longer at the prison when he attempted to serve papers for a civil lawsuit related to his time working as a confidential FBI informant on a securities fraud case involving a California man named Andrew Hackett, and the prison confirmed he was on “furlough, according to emails and court filings Sweet reviewed.”

“I was advised to call back in approximately 15 days, as [Smirnov] may or may not return to the facility by that time,” the process server told Hackett in an email. “The representative was notably guarded and provided minimal information beyond that.”

A local sheriff eventually told Hackett that Smirnov had been furloughed, but no additional information about his new location was provided, and a spokesperson at the Department of Justice replied “no comment” to a list of questions about the furlough or a possible pardon.

Smirnov, who reportedly has ties to multiple Trump associates, pleaded guilty to making a false statement and tax evasion, and the Biden Justice Department deemed him too great a flight risk to be granted bail because of his extensive ties to Russian intelligence operatives.

His claims to the FBI about the Bidens and Burisma formed the basis for the House Republican impeachment hearings against the then-president in 2023, but the Department of Justice determined last year his testimony contained fabrications and impossible claims.

Our Analysis:

The Curious Case of Alexander Smirnov: A Dance of Deception and Potential Presidential Pardons

In a twist that reads like a script from a political thriller, the saga of Alexander Smirnov, an FBI informant entangled in a concocted bribery narrative involving the Biden family, takes a new turn with his sudden and mysterious release from prison. This development not only stirs the pot of political intrigue but also raises serious questions about the integrity of the justice system and the potentially insidious power of presidential pardons.

Alexander Smirnov, a character who could be straight out of a Cold War novel, pleaded guilty to fabricating a story so sensational it could have derailed a presidency. He claimed that former President Joe Biden and his son Hunter were embroiled in a multimillion-dollar bribery scandal with a Ukrainian energy company. The implications were explosive, the reality, however, starkly different. Smirnov’s allegations were found to be baseless, leading to his conviction. Yet, as journalist Jacqueline Sweet reports, Smirnov’s six-year sentence has been cut short under mysterious circumstances, leading to rampant speculation and concern.

The Plot Thickens

Smirnov, despite being deemed a flight risk with deep ties to Russian intelligence, has vanished from the low-security confines of FCI Terminal Island. This disappearance, shrouded in secrecy and guarded responses from officials, begs the question: How does a man with such a profile simply walk away? The notion that he might not return from a so-called “furlough” is as alarming as it is absurd.

The timing of Smirnov’s release is particularly suspect, coinciding with whispers of a potential pardon by President Donald Trump, a maneuver that would not only reek of political cronyism but also undermine the very foundations of the American justice system. Trump’s history of controversial pardons and his connection to Smirnov through multiple associates thickens the plot, suggesting a quid pro quo that tarnishes the office of the presidency.

A Dangerous Precedent

Smirnov’s role as an informant and his subsequent conviction on charges of making a false statement and tax evasion highlight a dangerous game of misinformation and manipulation. His fabricated claims against the Bidens played a significant role in the House Republican impeachment hearings against the then-president in 2023, showcasing the potential for profound political consequences based on deceit.

The Department of Justice’s determination that Smirnov’s testimony was riddled with “fabrications and impossible claims” serves as a stark reminder of the vulnerability of political systems to manipulation. The eagerness of some to exploit these fabrications for political gain is a glaring indictment of the current state of political discourse.

The Shadow of Russian Intelligence

The involvement of Russian intelligence ties in this saga adds a layer of international intrigue and underscores the ongoing challenges facing American democracy. The fact that Smirnov, with his extensive connections to Russian operatives, was initially utilized by the FBI raises critical questions about the judgment and vulnerabilities within federal law enforcement agencies.

Conclusion: A System Under Siege

The mysterious release and potential pardon of Alexander Smirnov represent more than just a single case of legal and ethical ambiguity. They symbolize the systemic flaws within both the justice system and the political arena. When individuals who engage in deceit and have ties to foreign adversaries can escape accountability through political connections, it erodes public trust in government institutions.

In this era of heightened political polarization and international tensions, the Smirnov debacle serves as a cautionary tale of the dangers of political expediency over truth and justice. As the facts continue to unfold, one thing remains clear: the integrity of the justice system and the sanctity of democratic principles are at stake.
