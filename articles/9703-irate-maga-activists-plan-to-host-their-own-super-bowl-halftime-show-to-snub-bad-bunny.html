Irate MAGA activists plan to host their own Super Bowl halftime show to snub Bad Bunny

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 10, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

A notorious far-right youth organization is planning to host its own “Super Bowl halftime show” in protest of the real one being given to Puerto Rican singer/rapper Bad Bunny.

According to Axios, Turning Point USA’s event “capitalizes on MAGA outrage over the NFL’s selection,” as Bad Bunny is critical of President Donald Trump and has skipped certain shows in response to Immigration and Customs Enforcement raids. In addition to uproar from Trump voters, Republican lawmakers have jumped in to complain as well, with Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA) calling the performer “demonic“ and complaining that the performance will be in Spanish.

More broadly, the territory of Puerto Rico’s status has been a decades-long debate, and while a number of people in both parties have advocated for statehood, Trump and his supporters have often disparaged the island, with a comedian at Trump’s Madison Square Garden campaign event last year referring to it as a “floating island of garbage.”

For the Turning Point USA “All American Halftime Show,” which is advertised as a tribute to “faith, family and freedom” on February 8, a form has been launched online “for people to express interest in music genres, with one of the choices being ‘anything in English.’ The other options included Americana, classic rock, country, hip hop, pop and worship.”

Turning Point USA is the brainchild of activist Charlie Kirk, the controversial youth organizer who was assassinated in September, kicking off a political firestorm across the country.

The group has long faced accusations of racism, and while it began as a mostly secular movement, it has more recently forged ties with Christian nationalism, an extremist movement that believes a generally right-wing interpretation of Biblical law should rule supreme over the U.S. government, culture, and national identity.

Our Analysis:

The “Patriotic” Protest: A Deep Dive into Turning Point USA’s Halftime Spectacle

In an audacious display of cultural warfare, Turning Point USA, a far-right youth organization infamous for its polarizing activities, has announced its intention to host an alternative Super Bowl halftime show. This move is not merely a diversion from mainstream entertainment but a calculated political statement, capitalizing on the MAGA base’s discontent with the NFL’s decision to spotlight Puerto Rican singer/rapper Bad Bunny—a vocal critic of former President Donald Trump.

The Manufactured Outrage

At first glance, the outrage appears to be about the NFL’s halftime show selection, but scratching the surface reveals a deeper, more insidious narrative. Bad Bunny’s criticism of Trump and his decision to skip shows in protest of ICE raids have made him a target for the far-right. The backlash, as reported by Axios, is less about the artist’s music and more about his political stance and heritage. This scenario is a classic example of how cultural expressions and entertainers become pawns in the political chess game, with Turning Point USA leading the charge.

Republican Echo Chamber

The involvement of Republican lawmakers, such as Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene (R-GA), who labeled Bad Bunny as “demonic” and lamented the inclusion of Spanish in his performance, underscores the xenophobic undertones of this controversy. The criticism from GOP figures is not just a critique of musical taste but a manifestation of the broader political debate over Puerto Rico’s status and identity. The reference to Puerto Rico as a “floating island of garbage” at a Trump campaign event epitomizes the disdain held by certain political factions toward the territory and its people.

The “All American Halftime Show”

Turning Point USA’s alternative, branded as a tribute to “faith, family, and freedom,” is a strategic move to appeal to conservative values. The choice to include “anything in English” as a music genre option is a thinly veiled swipe at Bad Bunny’s performance and a nod to the organization’s base, which often champions English-only initiatives. This event is not just a concert; it’s a political rally masquerading as entertainment, aiming to galvanize a base riled up by cultural grievances.

Charlie Kirk’s Controversial Legacy

The late Charlie Kirk, founder of Turning Point USA and a figure mired in controversy, leaves behind a legacy that continues to influence the organization. The mention of Kirk’s assassination and the subsequent political firestorm highlights the volatile environment in which these cultural battles are fought. Turning Point USA’s evolution from a secular movement to one that aligns with Christian nationalism signals a dangerous shift towards an extremist ideology that seeks to impose a “right-wing interpretation of Biblical law” over American governance and culture.

Conclusion: The Real Battle

Turning Point USA’s “All American Halftime Show” is more than a simple protest against the NFL’s halftime show—it’s a microcosm of the broader cultural and political battles raging in America. By framing their event as a stand for “faith, family, and freedom,” the organization is weaponizing patriotism to serve an agenda that seeks to divide rather than unite. This spectacle is a stark reminder of the power of cultural events to reflect and amplify the nation’s deepest divides. As we dissect the motivations and implications of such actions, it’s crucial to recognize the underlying currents of racism, xenophobia, and extremism that fuel these controversies.
