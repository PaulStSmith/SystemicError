Trump invents reason to avoid questions as DOJ defies Epstein law: 'Don't want to soil it'

President Donald Trump declined to take questions just hours after an attorney with the Department of Justice said that the administration would defy a law requiring all of the files on Jeffrey Epstein to be released by the end of the day.

Trump ended a Friday meeting with pharmaceutical CEOs by announcing that he had suddenly had an idea to meet with health insurance companies. The president insisted that he didn’t want to “soil” his idea by taking questions from the media.

“I said to myself, you know, look at what they’ve done. They’ve done something that, and as you said, something that you never thought was possible to get done,” Trump explained after the pharma CEOs spoke about lowering prices. “And I said to myself, standing watching these great leaders ... I said, you know... I think that the insurance companies, we should have a meeting or we should talk to them. Because I would say that maybe with one talk, they would be willing to cut their prices by 50, 60, or 70%.”

“So we’re going to be calling a meeting,” he continued. “And I’d prefer not talking and asking questions only for the reason that this is such a big announcement, meaning what these people have said and what the other four companies are going to say, which is exactly the same thing that I really don’t want to soil it up by asking questions, even questions that are very fair questions, that I’d love to answer.”

Earlier on Friday, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche confirmed to Fox News that not all of the Epstein files would be released by the deadline.

“I expect that we’re going to release more documents over the next couple of weeks. So today, several hundred thousand. And then over the next couple weeks, I expect several hundred thousand more,” he said.

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Evasion: A Masterclass in Deflection

In a move that seems ripped straight from the playbook of political evasion, President Donald Trump has once again showcased his unparalleled ability to dodge accountability and avoid uncomfortable questions. The latest episode involves a somewhat bewildering pivot from a critical issue involving the Department of Justice (DOJ) and the Jeffrey Epstein files to an impromptu meeting about slashing health insurance prices. This maneuver, while audacious, is hardly surprising for those familiar with Trump’s rhetorical strategies.

The Art of the Dodge

Trump’s refusal to take questions came just hours after an attorney with the DOJ revealed a blatant defiance of the law, stating that all files on Jeffrey Epstein would not be released by the mandated deadline. Instead of addressing this significant legal and ethical quagmire, Trump diverted attention towards a sudden and seemingly unrelated idea to meet with health insurance companies.

It’s a classic Trump move: when faced with a challenging or damning topic, shift the conversation to something more favorable or less controversial. But let’s not be fooled by the smokescreen. The refusal to release the Epstein files is a grave matter, implicating potential misconduct or, at the very least, a concerning lack of transparency within his administration.

Unrealistic Promises and Unanswered Questions

During the meeting with pharmaceutical CEOs, Trump boasted about an epiphany to dramatically reduce health insurance prices, claiming that with just “one talk,” prices could be cut by “50, 60, or 70%.” This assertion is as grandiose as it is dubious, demonstrating Trump’s penchant for making bold promises without a clear plan or understanding of the complexities involved.

By saying he didn’t want to “soil” his announcement with questions, Trump effectively placed his own narrative above scrutiny, sidestepping the essential role of the press in a democracy. His statement that he would “love to answer” fair questions is belied by his actions, leaving the public in the dark about both the Epstein files and the feasibility of his health insurance proposal.

The Epstein Files: A Lingering Shadow

The refusal to fully release the Epstein files remains a shadow over Trump’s presidency, raising questions about what might be contained within those documents. Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche’s vague promise to release more documents “over the next couple weeks” does little to reassure those concerned about transparency and justice.

In this context, Trump’s abrupt pivot appears even more cynical. It is a maneuver designed not only to deflect from the immediate issue of the Epstein files but also to paint himself as a crusader against high health insurance prices. This tactic, however, is transparent in its intention and questionable in its potential effectiveness.

Conclusion

Trump’s latest actions are a stark reminder of his approach to governance: avoid difficult questions, deflect accountability, and promise the moon without a ladder. As we dissect these maneuvers, it’s crucial to remain vigilant, question the feasibility of grandiose claims, and demand transparency and accountability, especially concerning issues as serious as the Epstein files. The public deserves answers, not deflections.
