Trump induces nausea with 'ghoulish' rant against Rob Reiner: 'Fundamentally evil'

President Donald Trump suggested Rob Reiner had it coming after the Hollywood legend was found dead with his wife in their home — and onlookers were sickened.

Los Angeles police are investigating the deaths of the 78-year-old Reiner and his wife, producer Michele Singer Reiner, as an apparent homicide, but the president lashed out the “Princess Bride” and “A Few Good Men” director — and suggested that the star’s vocal criticism could be linked to his killing.

“A very sad thing happened last night in Hollywood,” Trump posted Monday morning. “Rob Reiner, a tortured and struggling, but once very talented movie director and comedy star, has passed away, together with his wife, Michele, reportedly due to the anger he caused others through his massive, unyielding, and incurable affliction with a mind crippling disease known as TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME, sometimes referred to as TDS.”

“He was known to have driven people CRAZY by his raging obsession of President Donald J. Trump, with his obvious paranoia reaching new heights as the Trump Administration surpassed all goals and expectations of greatness, and with the Golden Age of America upon us, perhaps like never before,” Trump added. “May Rob and Michele rest in peace!”

Other social users were appalled by the post and seemed to agree that it might be the president’s worst ever.

“This is one of the most psychotic things Trump has ever posted,” declared journalist Aaron Rupar.

“Christ. Trump says Rob Reiner and his wife were murdered ‘due to the anger he caused others’ through his ‘TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME,’” posted Vanity Fair’s Aidan McLaughlin.

“Because you know it’s ALWAYS about Trump,” said architect and blogger Gerald Weinand.

“Holy ..., Trump is saying Rob Reiner had it coming,” gasped Bluesky user Brosemite Sam.

“If you read this properly, he’s saying that Reiner was killed bc he made a Trump supporter too angry, and this is a good thing,” added Bluesky user No True Bill. “(Note this is not what happened in objective reality).”

“So Trump’s saying his supporters are going around killing his critics now?” agreed Bluesky user Molotov Cats.

“Utterly incapable of grace,” sighed journalist Philip Bump.

“Trump is just a fundamentally evil person,” stated Gizmodo’s Matt Novak.

“The ghoulish, disgusting, unhinged, horrible Donald Trump makes the tragic murder of Rob Reiner about himself. Anyone surprised?” posted Mike Sington, a senior executive at NBC Universal.

“How this vile, disgusting, and immoral behavior has become normalized in the United States is something our descendants will study in school, to the shame of our generation,” wrote author and podcast host Russell Moore.

“Reiner’s body isn’t cold yet but here’s the American president pissing on it,” seethed political analyst John Stoeher. “Trump has brought misery and hardship to millions but no matter how bad it is, it won’t ever match the joy and laughter that Rob Reiner brought to the world. Americans will be quoting The Princess Bride etc long after they have forgotten Trump.”

“Here’s Trump saying Rob Reiner is dead because he made people mad with his ‘Trump Derangement Syndrome,’” posted journalist Mark Jacob. “Absolutely insane. It’s a real post by the president of the United States, who has the nuclear codes.”

“I had to check this was real, because when I saw it originally it looked so disgusting that I thought no-one would actually go that low, even Trump,” said Daniel Sugarman, deputy editor of Jewish News. “But no, it’s real, of course. Sickening.”

“My God. F--- this despicable waste of space,” added Bluesky user Rachel Sexton. “I will never forgive the people who voted for him. What an inhuman monster. Live your life so you can be remembered like Rob Reiner. He is being sincerely mourned and Trump never will be.”

Our Analysis:

Trump’s Reaction to Rob Reiner’s Death: A Dissection of Disgrace

In an appalling display of callousness and self-absorption, President Donald Trump has once again crossed the boundaries of decency and respect. Following the tragic deaths of Hollywood icon Rob Reiner and his wife, Michele Singer Reiner, Trump’s response was not one of sympathy or condolence but rather an astonishingly self-centered and vindictive commentary that has rightly sickened observers.

Trump’s Unhinged Rant: A Closer Look

Trump’s statement, suggesting that Reiner’s death was a result of his “TRUMP DERANGEMENT SYNDROME,” is not only insensitive but borders on the perverse. The notion that Reiner’s vocal criticism of Trump somehow contributed to his and his wife’s untimely deaths is a disturbing leap into the realm of vindictive fantasy. Trump describes Reiner as “a tortured and struggling, but once very talented movie director and comedy star,” a backhanded compliment that serves only to belittle the achievements of a man whose work has brought joy and laughter to millions.

The self-aggrandizement present in Trump’s statement is equally appalling. His reference to the “Golden Age of America” under his administration, in the context of mourning, is a glaring example of his incapacity to separate personal grievance from presidential duty. It is a stark reminder of his tendency to view every event, no matter how tragic, through the lens of his ego.

Public Outrage: A Unified Voice

The reaction from the public and journalists alike has been one of unified disgust. Aaron Rupar’s labeling of Trump’s post as “one of the most psychotic things Trump has ever posted” captures the sentiment of many. This isn’t merely about politics; it’s about the basic human decency that Trump seems incapable of exhibiting.

Journalist Philip Bump’s remark on Trump’s incapacity for grace, and Gizmodo’s Matt Novak’s blunt assessment of Trump as “a fundamentally evil person,” reflect a broader consensus on the former president’s character—or the lack thereof. The expressions of outrage are not just reactions to a single insensitive statement but a cumulative response to a pattern of behavior that consistently undermines the dignity of the office he once held.

The Legacy of Rob Reiner vs. The Infamy of Donald Trump

Political analyst John Stoeher’s poignant observation highlights a critical contrast: while Rob Reiner’s legacy is one of joy, laughter, and cultural contributions, Trump’s legacy is marred by division, animosity, and self-obsession. The idea that Americans will cherish Reiner’s contributions long after Trump is forgotten is not just wishful thinking; it’s a testament to the enduring power of positive contributions over divisive rhetoric.

The Normalization of the Abnormal

What’s perhaps most alarming is the normalization of such behavior from Trump. As Russell Moore suggests, the acceptance of such vile and disgusting conduct in the public sphere will be a stain on this generation’s legacy. The question of how this has become normalized in the United States is one that future scholars will undoubtedly grapple with, seeking to understand how a leader could so blatantly disregard the norms of decency and respect.

In Conclusion: A Call for Decency

Trump’s reaction to the tragic deaths of Rob and Michele Reiner is not just an isolated incident of insensitivity; it’s a glaring example of his broader incapacity for empathy and decency. As the public reels from his latest display of callousness, one can only hope for a return to a political discourse that values compassion over cruelty, unity over division, and respect over disdain.

In a world where leaders are remembered for their contributions to humanity and decency, Trump’s legacy will likely stand as a cautionary tale of what happens when those values are abandoned. In stark contrast, Rob Reiner’s legacy, as a man who brought laughter and thoughtfulness to the world, will continue to be celebrated. The choice between these legacies is clear, and history will undoubtedly remember the difference.
