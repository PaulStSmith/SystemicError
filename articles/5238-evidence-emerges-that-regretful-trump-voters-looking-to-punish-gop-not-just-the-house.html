Evidence emerges that regretful Trump voters looking to punish GOP: 'Not just the House'

New evidence is emerging that some Donald Trump voters now regret their choice and wish they could get a do-over in the 2024 election.

CNN’s Harry Enten showed fresh polling that indicates some Trump voters are so unhappy with the president that they plan to take out their frustrations on the Republican Party in November’s midterm.

“Yeah, regrets? Some folks had a few,” Enten said, quoting from Frank Sinatra’s signature song, “My Way.” “What are we talking about here? Well, let’s just take a look here, okay, choice for 2024 presidential election. The actual [result] was Donald Trump winning by about a point and a half, it rounds to a point. But take a look here in a polled redo between Donald Trump and Kamala Harris in April of 2025, it was within the margin of error, right, Kamala Harris by a point.”

“But look at where we are now: According to an NBC News/Survey Monkey poll, Kamala Harris wins in a redo, asking folks, essentially, if you could redo the 2024 election, how would you vote?” Enten added. “She wins it by, get this, eight points, a massive shift from what we saw back in November of 2024, when Donald Trump won by a point, and I will note that this sample was weighted to the 2024 result in which Donald Trump won by a point.”

That might be a consolation prize for Harris, but it should also be a warning sign to Republican congressional candidates, he said.

“The fact is the 2024 election is gone goodbye, but it has a massive impact on voter sentiment on what may happen later this year,” Enten said. “Why do I say that? Because let’s take a look at the vote for Congress, and this is very important, all right, 2024 Harris voters. They vote for the Democrats on average by, get this, 89 points. The Trump voters mostly stick by the Republicans, but by a significantly smaller margin, by 83 points.”

“This means that the Democratic base that voted for Kamala Harris is sticking with those congressional Democratic candidates to a much greater degree than those Trump voters are sticking with the Republican candidates for Congress,” Enten added. “And that is why what you’re seeing on that generic congressional ballot is Democrats leaping ahead by about five points, because. At least at this point, the Trump voters are not sticking by the Republicans as much as the Harris voters are sticking by the Democrats.”

The same phenomenon seems to playing out among voters who did cast ballots in 2024, he said.

“Okay, so part of the equation, right, is that the Harris voters are really sticking with those Democratic candidates, more so than the Trump voters are sticking with the Republican candidates,” Enten said. “But it’s more than that. It’s also people coming off of the sideline, okay, voters choice for election for the 2026 congressional elections if they didn’t vote in 2024. Look at this: Democrats are winning that vote by a significant margin, by 16 percentage points. So you add on to that, the Harris voters sticking with the Democratic candidates, all of a sudden, you see hey, this is the way that Democrats are going to get back their congressional majority, and more than that, I will note, that in 2024, of course, the Trump voters, or the people who voted for Trump, he actually won a larger share of those non-2020 voters.”

“So it’s not just what we’re seeing here, historically speaking, that, hey, non-voters vote Democrats,” he added. “That was not the case in 2024, and when you put it all together, you put all of this together, what do we get? It’s not just the House, it’s the Senate as well. Democrats’ chance to win the Senate a year ago was 19 percent, according to Kalshi, 30 percent six months ago, and now Democrats have a 40 percent chance of taking back the U.S. Senate.”

Our Analysis:

A Closer Look at Voter’s Remorse and Its Implications for the GOP

In a political era punctuated by polarizing figures and decisions, the phenomenon of voter’s remorse is hardly a revelation. Yet, the recent CNN report shedding light on Donald Trump supporters expressing regret over their 2024 electoral choice unveils layers of political dynamics that merit a scathing analysis. The narrative is not just about a shift in voter sentiment; it’s a glaring testimonial to the fleeting loyalty in American politics and a potential harbinger of trouble for the Republican Party.

The Regretful Trump Voter: A Reality Check

The CNN report, courtesy of Harry Enten, brings to the forefront polling data that underscores a significant swing in voter sentiment. A hypothetical rematch between Donald Trump and Kamala Harris in 2025 displays Harris outperforming Trump by eight points—a drastic departure from the slim margin that marked Trump’s victory. This is not merely a statistical anomaly but a potent indication of voter dissatisfaction that seems to have burgeoned within a relatively short span.

The GOP’s Congressional Conundrum

But the repercussions of this voter remorse extend beyond the presidency. The NBC News/Survey Monkey poll cited by Enten reveals a more profound dilemma for Republican congressional candidates. The data suggests that while Harris voters remain staunchly loyal to Democratic congressional candidates, Trump voters exhibit a markedly less fervent allegiance to their Republican counterparts. This divergence is starkly evident in the projection that Democrats lead by about five points on a generic congressional ballot.

This discrepancy in party loyalty could not be more alarming for the GOP. It underscores a fundamental flaw in the party’s strategy: an overreliance on Trump’s cult of personality which, while formidable, is proving insufficient to sustain voter support across the broader Republican slate.

The Democratic Advantage and the Senate Battle

Interestingly, the dynamic further tilts in favor of the Democrats when considering the voting inclinations of those who abstained in the 2024 election. Democrats are poised to capture this contingent by a significant margin, thereby bolstering their prospects of regaining congressional control. This trend, juxtaposed against the backdrop of historical voting patterns, signifies a potential shift in the political landscape, one that could realign the parties’ fortunes moving forward.

Moreover, the evolving probability of Democrats seizing the Senate—surging from a mere 19% to a more optimistic 40% chance—embodies the tangible impact of voter sentiment on electoral outcomes. This shift not only reflects the volatility of political allegiances but also underscores the strategic miscalculations that could spell doom for Republican hopes of legislative dominance.

Concluding Thoughts

The narrative of Trump voter regret, as illuminated by recent polling data, is more than a fleeting headline. It encapsulates the broader challenges facing the Republican Party as it grapples with the consequences of its unwavering fealty to a singular, polarizing figure. The data presented beckons a moment of introspection for the GOP, urging a recalibration of strategies that transcend the allure of Trump’s base.

Meanwhile, Democrats appear poised to capitalize on this wavering Republican loyalty, leveraging it not only to fortify their congressional ranks but also to possibly reclaim the Senate. As the political tides ebb and flow, one thing remains clear: in the ever-unpredictable arena of American politics, today’s triumphs can swiftly become tomorrow’s regrets.
