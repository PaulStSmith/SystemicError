'Going to say this slowly': Wall St. execs lectured on Trump's tariff 'off-ramp'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 30, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Illusion of an Off-Ramp in Trump's Tariff Saga

U.S. President Donald Trump delivers remarks on tariffs in the Rose Garden at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., April 2, 2025. REUTERS/Carlos Barria

A ruling by the U.S. Court of International Trade on Wednesday that Donald Trump overstepped his authority by instituting reciprocal tariffs on U.S. trading partners has given the president a chance to back off his unpopular trade war policies –– but he won't.

Reacting to the ruling where the court stated the use of tariffs was "impermissible not because it is unwise or ineffective, but because [federal law] does not allow it,” MSNBC "Morning Joe" co-host Jonathan Lemire suggested there are those who believe Trump would take advantage of the ruling as an "off-ramp."

"I know there was some speculation last night, some wishful thinking, perhaps from some other Republicans I heard from that this may be an off-ramp for President Trump, that you could blame the courts, but then actually back away from some of the tariffs because of the impact on the markets, because of the impact potentially down the road on the economy," Lemire told the panel. "But this is, Joe [Scarborough], as you well know, the president has believed in for a very long time, and at least now I'm told, he doesn't plan to alter course."

That led co-host Scarborough to state, "So I'm going to say this slowly so our billionaire friends on Wall Street and our multi-millionaire friends on Wall Street who trade every day can understand it: if you thought that Donald Trump was just talking when he kept campaigning and promising to provide tariffs –– he wasn't."

"He's been saying that since 1987, and I have it on very good authority that the people that are saying Donald Trump is backing down on tariffs and there's an off-ramp, that Donald Trump does not believe there is an off-ramp for tariffs," he continued.

"And people running around saying that he always backs down or he chickens out, why don't you just invite him to put more tariffs on more countries," he joked. "He's not backing down on tariffs. Our Analysis:

The Illusion of an Off-Ramp in Trump's Tariff Saga

In a recent ruling that should have sent shockwaves through the corridors of power, the U.S. Court of International Trade unequivocally declared that Donald Trump overstepped his authority by imposing reciprocal tariffs on U.S. trading partners. This landmark decision, rather than being a moment of reckoning, appears to be nothing more than a minor speed bump in the relentless pursuit of a misguided trade war policy that Trump has championed since the dawn of his political era.

Overstepping Authority: The Court's Verdict

The court's ruling is a clear indictment of Trump's cavalier use of tariffs, stating that the imposition was "impermissible not because it is unwise or ineffective, but because [federal law] does not allow it.” This is not merely a slap on the wrist; it's a fundamental questioning of the legal basis for Trump's trade war. Yet, the reaction—or lack thereof—from the Trump camp speaks volumes about the administration's disregard for legal constraints and judicial oversight.

The Mirage of an Off-Ramp

Enter the wishful thinking of some Republicans who believed this ruling could serve as a convenient exit strategy for Trump to retreat from his unpopular tariff policies. According to Jonathan Lemire of MSNBC's "Morning Joe," there was speculation that Trump could use the court's decision as an "off-ramp," attributing the rollback of tariffs to judicial intervention rather than admitting policy failure. However, this speculation quickly dissipated in the face of Trump's unwavering commitment to tariffs, a stance he has maintained with almost religious fervor since 1987.

No Retreat, No Surrender

Joe Scarborough, co-host of "Morning Joe," underscored Trump's steadfast belief in tariffs, dismissing any notion of a strategic withdrawal. He emphasized that for Trump, there is no off-ramp, no concession, and certainly no admission of miscalculation. This rhetoric is not just stubbornness; it's a dangerous gamble with the American economy and global trade relations.

The Real Victims of Trump's Tariff War

What's conspicuously absent in this entire discourse is a recognition of the real casualties of Trump's trade policies: the American consumers, farmers, and businesses who bear the brunt of retaliatory tariffs and increased costs. The speculative bubble of Wall Street might be insulated from the immediate effects, but the broader economic implications are dire and far-reaching.

Conclusion: A Road to Nowhere

The court's ruling offered a moment for introspection and recalibration of a failed trade policy. Instead, it's been brushed aside in a continued march towards economic isolationism. Trump's refusal to consider an off-ramp from his tariff war is not a sign of strength; it's a testament to a profound misunderstanding of global economics and a disregard for the rule of law. As Trump doubles down on his tariff strategy, one can't help but wonder: at what point does the cost of winning become too great to bear?
