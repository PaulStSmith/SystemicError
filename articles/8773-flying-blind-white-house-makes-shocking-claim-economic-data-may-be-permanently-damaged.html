'Flying blind': White House makes shocking claim economic data may be permanently damaged

By Paulo Santos

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt speaks during a briefing at the White House in Washington, D.C. on Nov. 12, 2025. REUTERS/Kevin Lamarque

The White House warned Wednesday that the federal statistical system could be “permanently damaged” in the fallout over the government shutdown.

The October jobs and inflation data might not be released at all — a major concern for Wall Street as the shutdown dragged more than 40 days, CNBC reports.

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters during a news briefing that although the longest stalemate in U.S. government history is nearing an end, it is doubtful that the data will be available.

“The Democrats may have permanently damaged the Federal Statistical system with October CPI and jobs reports likely never being released,” Leavitt said. “All of that economic data released will be permanently impaired, leaving our policymakers at the Fed, flying blind at a critical period.”

The data release from the Labor Department’s Bureau of Labor Statistics focuses on monthly nonfarm payroll count and the consumer price index. The delay also pushes back data on U.S. retail sales, imports and exports, consumer spending and income.

Our Analysis:

Analyzing the Fallout: A Critical Look at the White House’s Warning and the Shutdown’s Impact on Federal Statistics

The White House’s recent warning that the federal statistical system could be “permanently damaged” due to the government shutdown is a stark reminder of the fragility of our nation’s data infrastructure. According to reports from CNBC, the protracted shutdown, stretching over 40 days, has jeopardized the release of critical economic data, including the October jobs and inflation figures. This situation presents a grim picture not only for Wall Street but also for the broader economic landscape of the United States.

The Blame Game: A Convenient Scapegoat?

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt’s statement that “The Democrats may have permanently damaged the Federal Statistical system” is a classic example of political finger-pointing. While it’s crucial to acknowledge the ramifications of the shutdown, attributing the potential long-term damage to the federal statistical system solely to one party oversimplifies the issue and diverts attention from the collective failure of government to fulfill its responsibilities.

The Real Victims: Policy and Decision-Making

Leavitt’s assertion that “All of that economic data released will be permanently impaired, leaving our policymakers at the Fed, flying blind at a critical period” highlights the true stakes of this debacle. The absence of reliable jobs and inflation data cripples policymakers and the Federal Reserve’s ability to make informed decisions. At a time when economic indicators are more vital than ever, this blind spot could have unforeseen consequences on monetary policy, interest rates, and overall economic stability.

The Delayed Data Dilemma

The significance of the delayed data cannot be overstated. The Labor Department’s Bureau of Labor Statistics plays a pivotal role in painting a comprehensive picture of the nation’s economic health through its monthly nonfarm payroll count and the consumer price index. Furthermore, the ripple effect of this delay affects insights into U.S. retail sales, imports and exports, consumer spending, and income. In essence, the shutdown has not just paused the flow of information; it has obstructed our vision of the economic horizon.

Conclusion: A Call for Accountability and Resilience

The potential permanent damage to the federal statistical system as a result of the government shutdown is a sobering reminder of the collateral damage political standoffs can inflict on the nation’s informational infrastructure. While the White House’s warnings underscore the severity of the situation, it’s imperative to move beyond partisan blame games and focus on solutions that strengthen the resilience of our federal statistical system against future disruptions.

The aftermath of this shutdown should serve as a catalyst for bipartisan efforts to safeguard the integrity and continuity of critical economic data. Ensuring the robustness of our data systems is not just about preventing inconvenience; it’s about maintaining the tools necessary for informed policy-making that impacts every American. As we navigate the consequences of this unprecedented delay, let the lessons learned pave the way for a more resilient and reliable statistical framework.
