The Rule of Law Is Endangered — By the Very Hands Claiming to Protect It

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosMay 19, 20251 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

When Chief Justice John Roberts penned the decision on July 1, 2024, granting Donald J. Trump immunity for “official acts,” he wasn’t merely rewriting the legal boundaries of executive power — he was lighting the Constitution on fire and handing the matches to a man infamous for his willingness to burn it all down.

Fast forward less than a year, and Roberts emerges from the marble shadows to intone gravely: “The rule of law is endangered.” No kidding, John. Who do you think helped endanger it?

This is the same man who looked at four years of unhinged abuse of power, two impeachments, a bloody insurrection, and a tsunami of criminal investigations — and decided, “Yes, this man should be shielded from consequences if he committed his crimes while wearing the title ‘Mr. President.’” That’s not jurisprudence. That’s enabling.

The decision Roberts authored gave the green light for a future autocrat — Trump or anyone with delusions of authoritarian grandeur — to weaponize the presidency with impunity. The ruling didn't just chisel away at checks and balances; it took a sledgehammer to the very idea that no one is above the law.

And now, with a straight face, Roberts laments the erosion of the rule of law?

Spare us the performative hand-wringing.

This is not irony. It’s complicity.

Let’s be clear: the danger to the rule of law didn’t come from protest signs or angry tweets. It came from a Supreme Court that wrapped presidential impunity in legal robes and called it constitutional order. It came from a judiciary that thought the best way to protect democracy was to hobble its ability to defend itself from demagogues.

John Roberts may want to play the role of institutionalist savior — but history is already casting him as the man who polished the brass on the Titanic while helping plot its course straight into the iceberg.
