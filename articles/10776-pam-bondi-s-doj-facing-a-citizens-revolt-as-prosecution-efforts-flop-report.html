Pam Bondi's DOJ facing a 'citizens’ revolt' as prosecution efforts flop: report

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosSeptember 7, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Pam Bondi (Mandel Ngan/AFP)

Attempts by the Department of Justice to prosecute Americans who are balking at Donald Trump’s authoritarian impulses are not finding a friendly audience when cases are being presented before grand juries long considered to be prosecutor-friendly.

According to Alan Feuer, writing for The New York Times, the U.S. Attorneys appointed by Trump and working under the supervision of Attorney General Pam Bondi are finding it rough sledding getting grand juries to return a true bill that would set the stage to proceed to the courtroom –– and there is a reason for that.

As Feur is reporting, “In what could be read as a citizens’ revolt, ordinary people serving on grand juries have repeatedly refused in recent days to indict their fellow residents who became entangled in either the president’s immigration crackdown or his more recent show of force.”

Noting that seven prominent cases have already been stymied by grand juries, he admitted there is no way to know what transpired in grand jury deliberations; nonetheless, a trend is becoming very apparent.

According to former U.S. S Attorney Barbara McQuade, what is happening during Bondi’s tenure — both the aggressiveness of the prosecutions as well as the frequency of failures to get a true bill –- is extraordinary.

“First of all, it is exceedingly rare for any grand jury to reject a proposed indictment because ordinarily prosecutors use discretion in only bringing cases that are strong and advance the interests of justice. I have seen this maybe once or twice in my career of 20 years, but this is something different,” the popular cable TV legal analyst offered.

“My guess is that these grand jurors are seeing prosecutorial overreach and they don’t want to be part of it,” she suggested.

The Trump administration’s efforts are also being stymied by judges who are growing weary of being led astray by the DOJ prosecutors.

“The erosion of this trust — known in legal parlance as the presumption of regularity — has been widespread in the many civil cases challenging Mr. Trump’s political agenda, where judges have repeatedly accused Justice Department lawyers of misleading them or violating their orders,” Feuer wrote before adding, “But now the phenomenon has started to crop up in criminal cases, too.”

According to federal Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui, who is in a pitched battle with controversial U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro over multiple cases that have been shot down by grand juries, “Blind deference to the government. That is no longer a thing. Trust that has been earned over generations has been lost in weeks.”

Our Analysis:

The Grand Jury Rebellion: A Symptom of a Deeper Malaise

The recent article detailing the Department of Justice's (DOJ) challenges under the Trump administration paints a stark picture of a legal system at odds with its leadership. The narrative, as sketched by Alan Feuer of The New York Times, captures a tumultuous saga where grand juries, historically the rubber stamp of prosecutorial ambition, are now bucking against attempts by the DOJ to indict individuals caught in the crosshairs of Donald Trump's draconian policies. This isn't just a series of legal roadblocks; it's a vivid illustration of a systemic revolt against authoritarian impulses.

The Unprecedented Grand Jury Pushback

At the heart of this legal quagmire is a series of decisions by grand juries that have refused to indict individuals ensnared by Trump's immigration crackdown and other contentious policies. This phenomenon isn't merely a hiccup in the prosecutorial process; it's a resounding rebuke of the governance style and directives emanating from the Trump White House. According to Barbara McQuade, a former U.S. Attorney and now cable TV legal analyst, the frequency and aggressiveness of these prosecutorial failures are extraordinary. McQuade's observations underscore a critical point: grand juries, typically seen as a procedural formality, are actively resisting what they perceive as prosecutorial overreach.

Judicial Distrust and the Erosion of Presumption

The article further highlights a growing skepticism among judges towards DOJ prosecutors, a skepticism fueled by accusations of misleading information and disregard for judicial orders. This erosion of trust, as Feuer articulates, signifies a profound shift in the legal landscape. Federal Magistrate Judge Zia M. Faruqui's comments about the loss of "blind deference" to the government encapsulate a broader disillusionment with the Trump administration's legal strategies. The speed at which this trust has evaporated speaks volumes about the systemic shockwaves emanating from these prosecutorial attempts.

Analysis: The Root Cause and Implications

What we're witnessing is not just a series of isolated incidents but a systemic pushback against an administration that has sought to weaponize the legal system against its perceived enemies. This grand jury rebellion is symptomatic of a deeper malaise: a crisis of confidence in the institutions tasked with upholding justice and democracy.

The implications of this development are both broad and significant. First, it signals a critical check on presidential power, serving as a reminder of the resilience of America's democratic structures. Second, it underscores the importance of prosecutorial discretion and the ethical responsibilities that come with it. The refusal of grand juries to serve as a mere extension of political will is a powerful affirmation of the principle that justice must be administered impartially and without undue influence.

However, this situation also highlights a troubling polarization within the American legal system, reflecting broader societal divisions. The fact that DOJ's efforts are being stymied by both grand juries and judges suggests a profound mistrust in the government's motives and actions. This mistrust, if left unaddressed, could have long-lasting repercussions for the integrity of the U.S. legal system and the rule of law.

Conclusion: A Call for Reflection and Reform

The grand jury rebellion against the Trump administration's prosecutorial efforts is a clarion call for reflection and reform. It's a stark reminder that the justice system should not be manipulated as a tool for political agendas. Instead, it should embody the highest principles of fairness, impartiality, and integrity. As we move forward, it's imperative that we rebuild the trust that has been eroded and ensure that our legal institutions serve the interests of justice, not the whims of those in power.
