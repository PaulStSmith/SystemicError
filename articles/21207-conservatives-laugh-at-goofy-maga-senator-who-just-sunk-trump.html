Conservatives laugh at 'goofy' MAGA senator who just sunk Trump

Trump Wants a Personal Attorney General. Republicans Are Helping.

The Real Story

The news here is not a clever exchange in a Senate hearing. It is that Donald Trump is trying to turn the Justice Department into a private legal instrument, and Todd Blanche is presenting himself accordingly. Blanche said he sees himself as “Trump’s lawyer,” even though the attorney general is supposed to represent the American people, not the president’s personal interests.

Power Is the Point

That distinction is the whole scandal. The person with institutional power is the president, and the machinery he is trying to commandeer is federal law enforcement. Blanche did not accidentally drift into this role. By the account here, he made the decision not to defend the IRS and the Justice Department, then settled Trump’s case with a $1.8 billion agreement that the court later voided as self-dealing.

Republican Theater, Democratic Resistance

Sen. John Kennedy’s awkward question may have exposed Blanche more effectively than a polished legal objection, but the underlying blame does not sit with Kennedy’s tone or with the hearing’s jokes. It sits with the party that keeps normalizing this arrangement. Republicans have enough votes to move Blanche forward, and the only serious barrier described here is a Democratic filibuster. That is not institutional balance. It is one party trying to stop the conversion of public office into personal protection.

The Sham Behind the Settlement

The IRS episode is the clearest example of how power gets laundered into procedure. Trump claimed his rights were violated after a leak about his taxes and business failures. Blanche declined to defend the agency, settled the case, and locked in terms that spared Trump and his family from future scrutiny. Judge Kathleen M. Williams later voided the deal and called out the self-dealing plainly: a sitting president sued an agency he controls and then settled with himself.

What Blanche Represents

Schiff’s questioning got to the heart of it: what happened to a prosecutor who was supposed to care about rule of law? The answer is not confusion or drift. It is capture. Blanche is not being evaluated as a neutral lawyer who stumbled into politics. He is acting as the legal face of a system in which Trump’s enemies are prosecuted over trivialities while Trump’s own liabilities are neutralized through engineered settlements.

The Pattern

This is bigger than one nominee or one hearing. It is the steady conversion of public institutions into loyalty services for a presidential patronage network. Republicans are not merely failing to resist that project; many are actively smoothing its path. The consequence is simple: when power is treated as personal property, the law stops functioning as a constraint and starts functioning as a cover.
