'Act of bigotry': Outrage as Trump admin alters trans admiral's official portrait

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Unsettling Politicization of Public Health FiguresIn Conclusion

A MAGA hat is seen at the Ellipse, in front of the White House. REUTERS/Jon Cherry

President Donald Trump’s Department of Health and Human Services took Admiral Rachel Levine’s name off her portrait in the Humphrey Building, according to National Public Radio.

Levine, a former director of the Public Health Corps under former President Joe Biden who became the first transgender person to serve in a Senate-confirmed executive role, has a distinguished career in medicine and public health policy. A pediatrician, she also headed the Pennsylvania Department of Health, where she combated the opioid crisis by enacting new policy authorizing law enforcement officers to carry naloxone to rescue overdose victims.

However, she has been a frequent target of attack by the right, with lawmakers and MAGA activists constantly making fun of both her gender identity and physical appearance — an act that appears to be legitimized by Trump’s HHS.

According to NPR, “Levine’s official portrait was recently altered, a spokesperson for HHS confirmed to NPR. A digital photograph of the portrait in the hallway obtained by NPR shows that Levine’s previous name is now typed below the portrait, under the glass of the frame.”

This move triggered immediate outrage, with former deputy assistant secretary for health policy Adrian Shanker saying, “During the federal shutdown, the current leadership of the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health changed Admiral Levine’s photo to remove her current legal name and use a prior name.” He added that the change is an act “of bigotry against her.”

Levine, asked for comment by NPR, simply said she was honored to have headed the Public Health Corps, “and I’m not going to comment on this type of petty action.”

Our Analysis:

The Unsettling Politicization of Public Health Figures

In a move that’s as petty as it is politically charged, the Trump administration’s Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) has taken a bold step into the realm of personal attacks masked as bureaucratic adjustments. Admiral Rachel Levine, a figure celebrated for her groundbreaking role as the first transgender person to serve in a Senate-confirmed executive position, and for her extensive contributions to public health, has been undermined in a manner both subtle and deeply significant.

The Act of Erasure: According to reports from National Public Radio (NPR), a seemingly innocuous alteration to Levine’s portrait in the Humphrey Building—a removal of her current legal name—signals a blatant act of disrespect. This isn’t just a matter of rearranging office decor; it’s a calculated move to delegitimize and demean an individual based on gender identity. By reverting to Levine’s previous name, the Trump HHS has not only disrespected her personal journey and identity but also trampled on the respect and recognition she has earned through her professional achievements.

Levine’s Legacy: Before this incident, Levine was known for her distinguished career in medicine and public health policy. As a pediatrician and the former head of the Pennsylvania Department of Health, she made significant strides in combating the opioid crisis—an effort that saved lives by empowering law enforcement officers to carry naloxone for overdose emergencies. Her role as a director of the Public Health Corps under former President Joe Biden further cemented her status as a dedicated public servant. Yet, the deliberate misnaming by Trump’s HHS seeks to overshadow these accomplishments with a focus on her identity as a transgender woman.

A Reflection of Broader Attitudes: This isn’t an isolated incident but rather a reflection of the ongoing attacks Levine has faced from the right, where her gender identity and appearance have been frequent targets. Such actions by government officials legitimize and embolden public harassment, contributing to a culture of intolerance and discrimination.

The Response: Levine’s response to the alteration of her portrait—choosing not to comment on what she describes as a “petty action”—demonstrates her commitment to rise above the bigotry. However, the outcry from others, including former deputy assistant secretary for health policy Adrian Shanker, underscores the gravity of the situation. Describing the act as one of “bigotry against her,” Shanker highlights the harmful implications of such a seemingly bureaucratic decision.

In Conclusion

What we witness here is a stark example of how political vendettas can seep into the fabric of public administration, targeting individuals for their identity rather than their competency or achievements. The alteration of Admiral Rachel Levine’s portrait by Trump’s HHS is not just a matter of misnaming; it’s a deliberate act of erasure and an assault on dignity. It’s a reminder that the fight for equality and recognition in the face of bigotry is far from over, and it’s a call to action for those who stand for justice to speak out against such petty yet profoundly harmful actions.

The politicization of public health figures, especially those belonging to marginalized communities, is a dangerous precedent that undermines the integrity of our public institutions and erodes the societal fabric of respect and equality. It’s high time we recognize and call out these actions for what they are: not just systemic errors, but deeply ingrained systemic failures.
