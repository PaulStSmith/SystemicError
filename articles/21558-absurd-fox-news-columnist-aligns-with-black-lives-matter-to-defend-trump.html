'Absurd': Fox News columnist aligns with Black Lives Matter to defend Trump

Based on an article by Kathleen Culliton

Paulo SantosAugust 16, 20263 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Selective Outrage Is the Point

A Convenient Pivot

Mike Davis used a Fox News audience to perform a curious little trick: he invoked police brutality in the abstract, just long enough to pivot away from a man killed by police in Madison and toward Jack Smith, the special counsel who prosecuted Trump. The source material gives the outline: Corey Ruiz was tackled, tased, kicked, and fatally shot; protesters said the force was excessive; Davis then recast the language of accountability as a weapon against a prosecutor.

Power Wears a Badge

The first question is always who actually holds institutional power. In Madison, it was the police. They had the weapons, the authority, and the ability to end a life in seconds. That is not a rhetorical flourish. It is the state’s coercive power doing what it is built to do, then asking the public to argue over whether the violence was “excessive” or merely fatal in the approved way.

That power is not comparable to the power of protest signs, chants, or a grieving mother’s testimony. Those are responses to violence, not sources of it.

The Fox News Move

Davis did not defend police accountability because he had suddenly discovered civil rights. He used the language of accountability as a rhetorical shell game. The minute that language pointed upward, toward officers with badges and state authority, he redirected it toward Jack Smith.

That is the pattern. Accountability is treated as noble when it can be aimed at enemies. It becomes corruption, lawfare, or a menace to “law enforcement” when it points at allies. The moral vocabulary stays the same. The target changes.

Jack Smith as Decoy

The source makes Davis’s real interest plain: not public safety, not legal principle, but retaliation against the prosecutor who brought charges against Trump over classified documents and the effort to overturn the 2020 election. Smith is the figure Davis wants punished, not because he represents unchecked force, but because he represents a rare institutional answer to elite impunity.

That is why the comparison is so revealing. Davis wants readers to believe that holding a violent officer accountable and holding a prosecutor accountable are parallel acts. They are not. One is a check on state violence. The other is revenge against the machinery that tried to impose legal consequences on a powerful politician.

The Frame Is Rotten

The article’s framing is weak because it pretends this is a story about principle instead of loyalty. It treats a right-wing attack on Smith as if it were a coherent civil-liberties argument, when it is really an attempt to collapse accountability into grievance. The result is political laundering: police brutality becomes background noise, while a Trump-aligned prosecutor becomes the supposed authoritarian threat.

That inversion matters. It is how reactionary politics works now. Real harm gets blurred into atmosphere. Actual violence is recoded as confusion. And the people with the most institutional force insist that they are the injured party.

The Bigger Pattern

This story is not mainly about one columnist making a bad argument. It is about a political ecosystem that can only tolerate accountability when it is aimed downward. Police violence is excused, minimized, or buried under procedure. Elite misconduct is recast as persecution. Democratic institutions are acceptable only when they protect power from consequences.

That is the system at work: punish the weak, excuse the armed, and call it law.
