‘Always stupid and nasty!’ Trump snaps at CNN host when pressed on rising ballroom costs

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 7, 20254 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:The Paradox of Trump’s White House Ballroom Extravaganza

U.S. President Donald Trump attends a cabinet meeting at the White House in Washington, D.C., U.S., December 2, 2025. REUTERS/Brian Snyder

President Donald Trump snapped at CNN’s Kaitlan Collins Saturday morning over being asked about the price tag of his White House ballroom, the cost of which has increased by at least $100 million since first announced.

“Caitlin Collin’s of Fake News CNN, always Stupid and Nasty, asked me why the new Ballroom was costing more money than originally thought one year ago,” Trump wrote on his social media platform Truth Social, spelling Collins’ name incorrectly.

“I said because it is going to be double the size, and the quality of finishes and interiors has been brought to the highest level. Also, the column SPAN has been substantially increased for purposes of viewing. It is actually under budget and ahead of schedule, as my jobs always are. It’s just much bigger and more beautiful than originally planned.”

Trump’s White House ballroom – a 90,000 square-foot luxury event space, was reportedly designed in a “hurried process,” with some design documents apparently showing a staircase leading to nowhere and colliding windows. And, despite Trump’s claim to the contrary, the project is behind schedule, having originally been slated to break ground in September.

Trump has boasted that the project is entirely funded by private donors, some of which had been omitted from the Trump administration’s reports on who and which entities are funding the project, such as the investment company BlackRock – which donated $2.5 million toward the project – or a major investor in TikTok’s parent company ByteDance.

“Interestingly, and seldom reported, there are no taxpayer dollars involved,” Trump continued. “It is being fully paid for by private donations. FAKE NEWS CNN, and the guy who runs the whole corrupt operation that owns it, is one of the worst in the business. Their ratings are so low that they’re not even counted or relevant anymore. MAGA!!!”

Our Analysis:

The Paradox of Trump’s White House Ballroom Extravaganza

In a characteristic display of deflection and bombast, President Donald Trump snapped back at CNN’s Kaitlan Collins over inquiries regarding the ballooning costs of his White House ballroom project. This incident sheds light not just on the project’s financial ambiguities but also on Trump’s perennial war against the media, particularly those he dubs as “Fake News.”

Firstly, Trump’s reaction to legitimate journalistic questioning—labelling Collins as “always Stupid and Nasty”—is a textbook example of his strategy to discredit media scrutiny. The misspelling of Collins’ name, whether intentional or not, adds a layer of disrespect and carelessness to his response, further undermining the professionalism expected from someone of his stature.

Trump’s defense that the ballroom’s cost has escalated due to an expansion in size and enhancements in quality fails to mask the project’s glaring issues. For a venture that was initially pitched as a testament to efficiency, being “under budget and ahead of schedule,” the reality seems starkly different. Reports of a “hurried process” leading to architectural mishaps, such as a staircase leading to nowhere and colliding windows, are emblematic of poor planning and oversight. These issues are not merely aesthetic; they symbolize the chaos and lack of accountability that often marred the Trump administration’s projects.

The claim that the project is entirely funded by private donations, while intended to assuage concerns about taxpayer burden, raises its own set of red flags. The omission of significant contributors like BlackRock and an investor in TikTok’s parent company ByteDance from official reports is troubling. It not only questions the transparency and ethics of the fundraising process but also hints at potential conflicts of interest, given the substantial influence these entities wield in global finance and technology.

Furthermore, Trump’s boast about the lack of taxpayer dollars involved, followed by a tirade against CNN and its ratings, is a deflection that misses the point entirely. The issue at hand isn’t solely about who foots the bill; it’s about transparency, fiscal responsibility, and the ethical implications of accepting large sums from powerful donors without full disclosure.

In conclusion, Trump’s response to inquiries about the White House ballroom project is a microcosm of his broader approach to governance and public discourse. It’s marked by defensiveness, opacity, and a penchant for attacking critics rather than engaging in substantive discussion. The luxury and size of the ballroom might indeed be “bigger and more beautiful than originally planned,” but the shadows of secrecy and potential impropriety loom large, casting a pall over what should have been a straightforward project. In Trump’s world, the spectacle often obscures the substance, leaving the public in the dark about the real costs—financial and ethical—of such undertakings.
