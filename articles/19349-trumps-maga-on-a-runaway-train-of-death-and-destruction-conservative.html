President Donald Trump’s “delusions of grandeur” inevitably overwhelm the Constitution, decency, political good sense and Americans’ distress.

That’s the opinion of Jennifer Rubin, who writes in The Contrarian that beyond the misery wrought by the Iran war, Trump and his administration almost seem to delight in inflicting suffering on the most vulnerable.

Her list of examples is extensive. Leading off are the more than 150 direct murders at sea, a violation of international law. The video game-like bombings were done “without effort to interdict the boats or seek definitive proof that the victims are ‘narco-terrorists.’”

Then there is the general cutback in Medicaid and the refusal to extend Affordable Care Act subsidies. Add to that the suspension of SNAP benefits during last year’s government shutdown, “depriving kids, seniors, the disabled, and the working poor of food,” Rubin writes. That’s a choice prior administrations chose not to take.

DOGE actions also caused pain.

“The MAGA crowd was delighted when DOGE minions cut vital government services, slashed jobs, and consigned children and adults overseas to death,” Rubin writes. “Trump applauds when government workers lose their jobs.”

The Department of Homeland Security shutdown that has been underway since mid-February is also punching down, Rubin contends. “He and his MAGA toadies refused to separately fund TSA, FEMA, and the Coast Guard.”

Meanwhile, Trump continually “tries to cut vital social services in blue states, holding their residents hostage to his partisan power plays.”

The hidden tax on purchases imposed via tariffs is “forcing already strapped families and small businesses to pay more. The Trump regime tells Americans either they won’t pay for it, or if they do, it’s worth a recession.”

Why is this happening? Rubin claims Trump shows all the classic symptoms of narcissistic personality disorder. But it’s clear, even without an official diagnosis, “he inarguably cares nothing about (if not outright celebrates) deaths, illnesses, suffering, economic distress and any other harm he inflicts on those who refuse to worship him.”

The solution is simple, Rubin concludes. In eight months, “The voters still have the power to stop the MAGA runaway train of death and destruction.”

Our Analysis:

Analyzing Jennifer Rubin’s Scathing Indictment of Donald Trump’s Policies

Jennifer Rubin’s piece in The Contrarian paints a damning portrait of President Donald Trump’s administration, one characterized by what she describes as “delusions of grandeur” that trample over the Constitution, decency, political sensibility, and the distress of American citizens. Rubin’s critique, while sharp, dives into a litany of policy decisions and actions under Trump that she claims not only exacerbate suffering among the most vulnerable but also serve as a testament to a broader, more malicious agenda.

Direct Murders at Sea: A Violation of International Law

Rubin pulls no punches in highlighting the more than 150 direct murders at sea, directly violating international law. This action, likened to “video game-like bombings”, is criticized for its lack of effort to interdict the boats or to seek definitive proof of the victims’ affiliations. This, if true, is not just a policy failure but a gross moral and legal wrongdoing.

Cutbacks in Social Services: A Pattern of Neglect

The general cutback in Medicaid and the refusal to extend Affordable Care Act subsidies are pointed out as measures that deliberately inflict suffering. The suspension of SNAP benefits during a government shutdown is described as a choice—a cruel one at that—which previous administrations did not take. This pattern of cutting vital services, especially during times of crisis, is not just a political misstep; it’s a calculated move to deprive the needy.

DOGE’s Destructive Policies

The Department of Homeland Security’s shutdown and the subsequent refusal to fund essential agencies like TSA, FEMA, and the Coast Guard are emblematic of a broader trend under Trump’s leadership. Rubin argues that these actions are not just administrative decisions but deliberate attempts to inflict pain on the populace. The critique here is clear: Trump’s administration is accused of prioritizing political gains over human life and safety.

Partisan Power Plays and Economic Distress

Rubin also touches on the “hidden tax” imposed via tariffs, which disproportionately impacts families and small businesses. The narrative pushed by the Trump administration—that these tariffs are either inconsequential or a worthy sacrifice—is criticized for its blatant disregard for the economic distress it causes. This policy is portrayed as not just economically unsound but deeply insensitive to the struggles of average Americans.

A Diagnosis of Narcissism?

The article suggests that Trump’s actions and policies could be symptomatic of narcissistic personality disorder, emphasizing a lack of empathy and a celebration of suffering. While Rubin stops short of an official diagnosis, the implication is clear: Trump’s behavior and policy decisions are seen as fundamentally driven by ego and a disregard for the well-being of others.

The Call to Action

Rubin concludes with a call to action, reminding voters of their power to halt what she describes as the “MAGA runaway train of death and destruction”. This conclusion, while politically charged, emphasizes the role of democratic accountability in addressing what she views as systemic abuses of power.

Conclusion: A Fierce Rebuke Grounded in Policy Critique

Jennifer Rubin’s article is a scathing rebuke of Donald Trump’s presidency, focusing on specific policy decisions and actions that she argues have caused significant harm to vulnerable populations. While the tone is undeniably sharp and the critiques are biting, the underlying concern is grounded in a detailed examination of policy impacts. Rubin’s piece serves as a fierce reminder of the potential consequences of political actions on human lives and the importance of holding those in power accountable for their decisions.
