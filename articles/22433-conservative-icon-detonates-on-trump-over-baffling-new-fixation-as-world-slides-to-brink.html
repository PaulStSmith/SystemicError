Conservative icon detonates on Trump over baffling new fixation as world slides to brink

Frivolity Is Not the Crime. Power Is.

What the source shows

George Will used a Washington Post column to call Donald Trump “frivolous” and “oblivious” as tensions with Iran and South Korea deepen. The reporting also notes a more revealing detail: a Defense Department intelligence chief was fired after concluding the June strikes on Iran did not destroy its nuclear program. That is the real center of gravity here.

The Presidency, Not the Commentary

Will is not the actor with institutional power. Trump is. The presidency controls the military, the intelligence bureaucracy, and the public narrative that justifies both. A columnist can denounce the wreckage after the fact. A president can launch military operations, proclaim success, and punish the professionals who contradict him.

That distinction matters because too much coverage treats Trump’s behavior as temperament gone wrong. It is not just temperament. It is state power used irresponsibly, then defended through repetition, loyalty tests, and retaliatory discipline.

The War Claim Was the Point

The source says Trump declared the first strike had “obliterated” Iran’s nuclear program, then launched a second military operation against Tehran months later. That is not confusion. That is a political decision wrapped in triumphal language. The purpose of the claim is not accuracy; it is permission.

Once the administration speaks in absolutes, dissent becomes an obstacle. Once dissent becomes an obstacle, people get fired. The intelligence chief’s removal is not a side note. It is the mechanism that keeps the lie operational.

South Korea Becomes a Hostage

The article also mentions Trump’s feud with South Korea over what he considered insufficient help with the second Iran operation. That is how imperial arrogance travels: one administration’s private grievance becomes another country’s diplomatic problem. Seoul is not the cause of the escalation. It is being dragged into the fallout from Trump’s own choices.

This is a familiar move. Demand alignment, insult allies, then treat their hesitation as disloyalty. The framing shifts responsibility away from the decision-maker and onto whoever refuses to applaud in time.

Will Sees the Damage, Not the System

Will’s attack on Trump is sharp, but it still risks narrowing the story to one man’s vulgarity. Trump is not an exception floating above the system. He is what happens when the presidency is fused to grievance, secrecy, and unchecked force. His frivolity is dangerous because the office gives it teeth.

The deeper problem is that institutions meant to constrain this kind of behavior have already shown how easily they bend. Intelligence can be ignored. Dissent can be purged. Allies can be pressured. Then the damage gets narrated as a personality flaw instead of a governing method.

The Pattern

The pattern is not merely presidential absurdity. It is authoritarian governance by declaration, backed by punishment, laundered through elite commentary after the fact. A dangerous lie is announced as strategy. Professionals contradict it. The professionals are removed. Then the press is left to debate the tone.

That is how reckless power protects itself: by turning consequences into drama and accountability into opinion.
