GOP insiders warn huge debate threatens to tear ranks apart: 'We don't really care'

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosDecember 10, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

Our Analysis:GOP Healthcare Havoc: A Symphony of DysfunctionConclusion: A Call for Cohesion and Compassion

Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO) attends a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing. REUTERS/Nathan Howard

The Republican Party is pulling itself apart over a looming healthcare deadline as a clear plan for the future is yet to appear.

GOP representatives are still figuring out a plan for millions of Americans, though expanded subsidies for the Affordable Care Act will end at the end of this year whether a new plan is presented or not. One Republican representative, speaking to The Hill, called the situation a “recipe for disaster” — as few in the party are on the same page.

Republican Senator Josh Hawley said, “If they can’t figure out a plan, then maybe you got to do some kind of short-term extension until we figure out a plan. They should get together here and hammer something out... sitting back and saying, ‘There’s nothing we can do, we’re just going to let it all expire, we don’t have a substitute, we don’t have an alternative,’ that I think is really a recipe for disaster for people trying to buy health care.”

He went on, “What signal would that send if Republicans say, ‘Yeah, we’re going to say no to the Democrats’ plan, but we’re not going to offer anything? The message that will send is, good luck to the American people, and we don’t really care.”

An unnamed Senate Republican said there is an “ongoing discussion” over what to do with the healthcare proposals but that, as of now, there is no definite route for the party. They said, “We’re having an ongoing discussion. We need a side-by-side. A lot of in-cycle members would like a side-by-side.”

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer has since suggested the GOP is at an impasse over what to do with the looming health care deadline. He said, “They’re stuck. They can’t get their caucus to agree on a bill.”

The in-fighting and unclear route through to a new healthcare plan to replace the ACA has even prompted some Democrats to offer their help to their opponents to find a suitable plan.

Democratic whip Dick Durbin says a bipartisan deal could be struck with the foundations of the Moreno-Collins plan, which calls for a two-year extension of subsidies. Durbin said, “I want to sit down with them. I don’t think that’s a bad place to start. I’m encouraged by the fact that they want to talk.”

Republican Senators Bernie Moreno and Susan Collins shared their plan to extend the enhanced ACA rates for a further two years. A separate plan from Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee Chair Bill Cassidy and Senate Finance Committee Chair Mike Crapo would see federal funding converted into advanced subsidies.

Our Analysis:

GOP Healthcare Havoc: A Symphony of Dysfunction

In what can only be described as a spectacular display of political paralysis, the Republican Party finds itself ensnared in a self-made quagmire over the future of healthcare in America. With the clock ticking towards a critical deadline, the party’s lack of direction not only showcases a breathtaking disregard for the well-being of millions but also highlights a deeper crisis within its ranks.

The Republican Dilemma: A “Recipe for Disaster”

As expanded subsidies for the Affordable Care Act (ACA) dangle by a thread, set to expire at the end of the year, the GOP’s inability to coalesce around a viable alternative is nothing short of alarming. One Republican representative’s candid admission to The Hill that the situation is a “recipe for disaster” is an understatement. The party, fractured and directionless, is playing a dangerous game with the healthcare coverage of millions of Americans.

Senator Josh Hawley’s suggestion of a “short-term extension” is a band-aid solution to a hemorrhaging problem. Hawley’s critique of his party’s inaction—”sitting back and saying, ‘There’s nothing we can do’... that I think is really a recipe for disaster for people trying to buy health care”—is spot-on. Yet, it begs the question: Why is this realization dawning so late in the game?

The Message to Americans: “Good Luck and We Don’t Care”

What’s deeply troubling is the implicit message the GOP’s indecision sends to the American public. By failing to propose a comprehensive plan or accepting the ACA’s framework, the Republican stance essentially boils down to a cold, indifferent shrug towards the healthcare needs of the populace. As Hawley rightly points out, the lack of an alternative plan essentially communicates, “good luck to the American people, and we don’t really care.”

Ongoing Discussions: A Euphemism for Inaction

An unnamed Senate Republican’s mention of an “ongoing discussion” is a euphemism that voters have grown weary of. The quest for a “side-by-side” comparison of proposals, as mentioned by this senator, is yet another stalling tactic in a long line of procrastinations. This indecision is not a victimless crime; it directly impacts the lives, health, and financial stability of millions.

Democratic Intervention: A Lifeline or a Trap?

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer’s observation that the GOP is “stuck” is an understatement. The party’s inability to unify around a healthcare strategy has become so dire that Democrats, led by the likes of Dick Durbin, are extending an olive branch. While Durbin’s willingness to discuss the Moreno-Collins plan, which proposes a two-year extension of subsidies, is commendable, it’s a sad state of affairs when the opposition must step in to mitigate the fallout from the GOP’s internal discord.

The plans put forth by Republican Senators Bernie Moreno, Susan Collins, Bill Cassidy, and Mike Crapo offer glimpses of potential pathways forward. Yet, these proposals seem more like disparate cries in the dark rather than a cohesive strategy.

Conclusion: A Call for Cohesion and Compassion

The GOP’s healthcare debacle is not just a failure of policy but a failure of leadership. The party’s apparent willingness to let the ACA subsidies expire without a robust alternative in place is a gamble with American lives. This is not just a “recipe for disaster”; it’s a disaster already unfolding.

The Republican Party must find its way out of this self-inflicted maze. The American people deserve a healthcare plan that is thought out, comprehensive, and, above all, compassionate. The time for “ongoing discussions” has passed. The moment for decisive action is now.
