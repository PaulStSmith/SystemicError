Inside NSPM-7: The Blueprint for Authoritarian America

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosOctober 15, 20255 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

When Donald Trump signed National Security Presidential Memorandum-7 (NSPM-7), he didn’t just issue another obscure directive buried in the federal register. He set fire to the line separating dissent from sedition. What Ben Meiselas calls “chilling” is, in truth, a systematic restructuring of the American security apparatus to target ideological enemies under the guise of counterterrorism.

NSPM-7 takes the vast machinery built for the War on Terror and retools it for domestic use. Its language is bureaucratic—always the camouflage of tyranny—but the intent is plain: to surveil, intimidate, and suppress anyone not conforming to the Trumpist orthodoxy of “traditional American values.”

A Directive to Crush Dissent

Unlike executive orders, which are public and often symbolic, National Security Presidential Memoranda are policy backbones—classified coordination documents that bind intelligence, military, and law enforcement agencies to a unified mission. In this case, that mission is the criminalization of dissent.

The memo’s text, as confirmed by multiple legal analysts, lumps together concepts like anti-Americanism, anti-capitalism, and anti-Christianity as “indicators of potential radicalization.” These are not crimes—they are beliefs, opinions, critiques of power. Yet the directive tells agencies to treat such views as early warning signs of terrorism.

Translated from bureaucratese: if you protest the privatization of healthcare, challenge evangelical overreach in politics, or demand fair wages, congratulations—you may now be on the radar of Trump’s domestic counterterror machine.

ICE and the Paramilitary Pipeline

If there’s one agency that illustrates the rot at the heart of NSPM-7, it’s Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). ICE has long functioned as Trumpism’s blunt instrument—a rogue force steeped in racism, cruelty, and political theater. Under this directive, its mission is being rebranded from immigration enforcement to national security defense.

Attorney General Pam Bondi’s creation of an “anti-ICE crimes task force” is more than political pandering. It signals the federal government’s willingness to treat peaceful opposition to ICE’s atrocities as terrorism. The same ICE that detained children in cages and conducted raids at churches now gets to cloak itself in counterterror language. It’s a grotesque inversion: the perpetrators of systemic abuse posing as the victims of ideological persecution.

ICE should not be reformed—it should be abolished. No democratic society can justify a secretive internal police force that functions as both judge and executioner for the vulnerable. NSPM-7 simply expands its playground.

The Rebirth of McCarthyism in Digital Form

The most dangerous element of NSPM-7 lies in its modernization of old authoritarian tactics. Where McCarthy’s House Un-American Activities Committee once subpoenaed Hollywood writers, Trump’s new “Threat Screening Center” now combs through tweets, podcasts, and livestreams for ideological deviations. Technology has made repression frictionless.

According to investigative journalist Ken Klippenstein, this new center—housed in a nondescript Virginia office park—is compiling watchlists of Americans based not on violence, but on belief. The staff scour social media for “anti-capitalist” language, “anti-Christian” remarks, or signs of “hostility toward traditional morality.” In other words, they are criminalizing thought.

This weaponization of data and algorithms to enforce ideological conformity is not hypothetical—it’s operational. The Treasury Department has already begun auditing nonprofits accused of “subversive activity,” while banks are being urged to flag transactions associated with “extremist” movements. Translation: donate to a bail fund or protest network, and you may soon find your account under review.

The Silence of the Fourth Estate

And where is the so-called free press? Sleeping, mostly. The corporate media, fatigued by scandal and terrified of losing access, has treated NSPM-7 like another footnote in Trump’s endless deluge of chaos. But this memo is not a curiosity—it’s a declaration of intent.

Trump’s regime thrives on bureaucratic camouflage. Its most dangerous actions are wrapped in procedural normalcy. “Restoring order,” “ensuring stability,” “defending values”—each phrase is a velvet glove hiding the iron fist of surveillance and repression.

When Speaker Mike Johnson and DHS Secretary Kristi Noem equate peaceful protests with Hamas or ISIS, they are not freelancing; they are harmonizing with NSPM-7’s ideological script. The repetition of “anti-American,” “anti-Christian,” and “pro-Hamas” is deliberate rhetorical engineering, priming the public to accept that opposition equals treason.

A Legal Framework for Fascism

Every autocrat learns the same lesson: brute force is effective, but legality is enduring. NSPM-7 provides that legal scaffolding. It allows Trump’s Justice Department to pursue activists, journalists, and NGOs under the same frameworks once reserved for foreign terrorists. It normalizes ideological vetting for financial institutions and law enforcement agencies. It creates the infrastructure of a permanent internal enemy.

Civil liberties organizations, from the ACLU to the Brennan Center, are sounding the alarm. Their analyses confirm that this directive enables unprecedented inter-agency data sharing and monitoring, blurring the line between intelligence and policing. The FBI, DHS, and Treasury are now united under a single umbrella goal: to identify and disrupt “radical threats”—a category defined not by violence, but by worldview.

This is the domestic equivalent of the Patriot Act on steroids. Where that earlier law exploited fear of external terrorism, NSPM-7 turns that machinery inward, toward Americans who reject Trump’s ideological purity tests.

A Mirror of History’s Darkest Lessons

We have seen this movie before. Every authoritarian regime, from Pinochet’s Chile to McCarthy’s America, has justified its repression as defense of the homeland. Trump’s version merely updates the script for the digital age. Instead of dossiers compiled by hand, we now have social media analytics and AI-assisted sentiment scoring. Instead of public blacklists, we have opaque watchlists buried in databases.

It’s tempting to dismiss this as paranoia. But as history teaches, paranoia is often realism whispered too soon. When civil liberties vanish, they do not vanish in a blaze; they erode, clause by clause, memorandum by memorandum, until one morning dissent is a felony and silence a virtue.

Resistance in an Age of Surveillance

The response to NSPM-7 cannot be tepid. This is not a time for cautious editorial disapproval—it is a time for defiance. Civil society must challenge the memorandum’s legality in court, demand transparency, and protect whistleblowers who expose its abuses. Law firms advising clients to “self-censor” should instead be preparing constitutional challenges. Nonprofits must refuse to let financial intimidation dictate their advocacy.

The broader public must reject the false binary of “patriot or traitor.” True patriotism lies in holding power accountable, not in worshiping it. A government that fears its citizens’ ideas has already betrayed its founding principles.

And ICE—the blunt, bloody instrument of this domestic war—must be dismantled. Its existence is an affront to democracy and human rights. To abolish it is not radical; it is necessary.

The Stakes

NSPM-7 is more than a memo—it’s a manifesto. It codifies Trump’s vision of an obedient, surveilled nation where ideology determines innocence. Left unchecked, it will outlive its author, becoming the legal foundation for decades of repression.

If democracy is to survive, it must name the threat clearly: this is not governance, it is fascism wrapped in flag and scripture. It is the criminalization of thought, the sanctification of cruelty, and the bureaucratization of fear.

Ben Meiselas was right to sound the alarm. But alarm is not enough. We must act, organize, litigate, and resist. Because when dissent is defined as terrorism, resistance becomes the last act of patriotism.
