They Lost Their Humanity On Purpose

By Paulo Santos

Paulo SantosJanuary 12, 20264 min read

IN THIS ARTICLE

The most corrosive lie still circulating in liberal and technocratic circles is the idea that the modern authoritarian right can be reasoned with if we just “appeal to their better angels.” The Godless Gardener shreds that illusion with brutal clarity. His point is not rhetorical flourish or online snark—it is a diagnosis. These people are not temporarily confused. They are not “misinformed.” They are not victims of bad messaging. They have made an affirmative moral choice to abandon empathy, and they are pleased with the result.

This matters because an entire generation of political strategy has been built on the assumption that cruelty is an accidental byproduct of ignorance. If we just explain the data better, the thinking goes, if we soften our tone, if we show them the human cost, then surely conscience will kick in. But conscience only works if it exists in the first place. The Godless Gardener is pointing at something far darker: not the erosion of empathy, but its deliberate rejection.

When confronted directly—just admit you’re happy about this—many on the right don’t deny it. They deflect, they joke, they smirk, or they pivot to technicalities. But the affect gives them away. There is pleasure here. Pleasure in watching others suffer. Pleasure in punishment. Pleasure in hierarchy being violently enforced. That pleasure is not incidental; it is the fuel.

For years, liberals have clung to the fantasy that cruelty is an embarrassment conservatives would prefer to hide. That era is over. Cruelty is now the selling point. It is openly celebrated, meme‑ified, and defended as “telling it like it is.” The policies are not designed to work; they are designed to hurt the correct people. Any benefit beyond that is optional.

This is why appeals to “national unity” ring hollow. Unity requires shared moral premises. You cannot unify with a movement that defines morality as domination. You cannot reconcile with people who experience empathy as weakness and compassion as betrayal. To attempt it is not virtue—it is self‑delusion.

The right’s favorite counterattack is to accuse critics of exaggeration. Stop calling us fascists, they say, you’re losing credibility. But credibility with whom? With the very people who have spent a decade signaling, in word and deed, that they admire authoritarian power? The ones who cheer mass deportations, applaud police violence, and fantasize about civil war? The label is not an insult—it is a description, arrived at through observation.

What the Godless Gardener captures so well is the emotional asymmetry of this moment. One side is still arguing as if shame works. The other side has burned shame as a political resource and replaced it with grievance and glee. You cannot shame people who feel righteous joy at the suffering of their enemies.

This also explains the persistent liberal confusion about why “facts don’t work anymore.” Facts never worked on people whose worldview is not built on truth-seeking. For authoritarians, facts are tools, not commitments. They are adopted when useful and discarded when inconvenient. Expecting factual rebuttal to change behavior in that context is like bringing a spreadsheet to a knife fight.

There is an additional, uncomfortable truth embedded in this transcription: the cruelty is not merely top‑down. It is not just politicians or media figures whipping up hatred. The base wants this. They demand it. Leaders who fail to deliver sufficient brutality are punished. This is not manipulation—it is representation.

That realization forces a strategic reckoning. If the opposition has abandoned humanity, then politics becomes less about persuasion and more about containment. Less about compromise and more about preventing harm. Democracies do not survive by endlessly negotiating with movements that seek their destruction.

Calling this out is not “divisive.” It is clarifying. The division already exists; pretending otherwise only benefits those exploiting it. The Godless Gardener is not advocating despair—he is advocating accuracy. You cannot fight what you refuse to name.

So stop begging for empathy from people who have proudly amputated it. Stop mistaking cruelty for confusion. Stop framing sadism as economic anxiety. The mask is off, and has been for years. They are happy about this. They want this. And any political strategy that refuses to start from that reality is doomed to fail.

History is unforgiving to societies that mistake moral collapse for mere disagreement. The warning signs are not subtle anymore. They are being broadcast, laughed about, and defended in real time. The only remaining question is whether those who still value humanity are willing to defend it with the seriousness the moment demands.
